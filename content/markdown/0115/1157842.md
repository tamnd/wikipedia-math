# Hasse–Weil zeta function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hasse–Weil zeta function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hasse–Weil zeta function** attached to an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *V* defined over an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) *K* is a [meromorphic function](wikipedia://en/80857) on the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) defined in terms of the number of points on the variety after reducing modulo each prime number *p*. It is a global [*L*-function](wikipedia://en/245283) defined as an [Euler product](wikipedia://en/398540) of [local zeta functions](wikipedia://en/396014).

:::

Hasse–Weil *L*-functions form one of the two major classes of global *L*-functions, alongside the *L*-functions associated to [automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representations). Conjecturally, these two types of global *L*-functions are actually two descriptions of the same type of global *L*-function; this would be a vast generalisation of the [Taniyama-Weil conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama-Weil_conjecture), itself an important result in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

For an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) over a number field *K*, the Hasse–Weil zeta function is conjecturally related to the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200) of the elliptic curve over *K* by the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The description of the Hasse–Weil zeta function *up to finitely many factors of its Euler product* is relatively simple. This follows the initial suggestions of [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118) and [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019), motivated by the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809), which results from the case when *V* is a single point.[^o4]

Taking the case of *K* the [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) field $\mathbb{Q}$, and *V* a [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469), for [almost all](wikipedia://en/1309) [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) *p* the reduction of *V* modulo *p*, an algebraic variety *V*<sub>*p*</sub> over the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) $\mathbb{F}_{p}$ with *p* elements (defined by reducing modulo *p* equations for *V*). Again for almost all *p* it will be projective and non-singular. We define a [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) of the [complex variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variable) *s*,

$$Z_{V\!,\mathbb{Q}}(s) = \prod_{p} Z_{V\!,\,p}(p^{-s}),$$

which is the [infinite product](wikipedia://en/26382006) of the [local zeta functions](wikipedia://en/396014)

$$Z_{V\!,\,p}(p^{-s}) = \exp\left(\sum_{k = 1}^\infty \frac{N_k}{k} (p^{-s})^k\right)$$

where *N<sub>k</sub>* is the number of points of *V* defined over the finite field extension $\mathbb{F}_{p^k}$ of $\mathbb{F}_{p}$.

This $Z_{V\!,\mathbb{Q}}(s)$ is [well-defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-defined) only up to multiplication by [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) in $p^{-s}$ for finitely many primes *p*.

Since the indeterminacy is relatively harmless, and has [meromorphic continuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meromorphic_continuation) everywhere, there is a sense in which the properties of *Z(s)* do not essentially depend on it. In particular, while the exact form of the [functional equation](wikipedia://en/595708) for *Z*(*s*), reflecting in a vertical line in the complex plane, will definitely depend on the 'missing' factors, the existence of some such functional equation does not.

A more refined definition became possible with the development of [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231); this neatly explains what to do about the missing, 'bad reduction' factors. According to general principles visible in [ramification theory](wikipedia://en/384644), 'bad' primes carry good information (theory of the *conductor*). This manifests itself in the étale theory in the [Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion](wikipedia://en/35128259) for [good reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_reduction); namely that there is good reduction, in a definite sense, at all primes *p* for which the [Galois representation](wikipedia://en/486929) ρ on the étale cohomology groups of *V* is *unramified*. For those, the definition of local zeta function can be recovered in terms of the [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of

$$\rho(\operatorname{Frob}(p)),$$

Frob(*p*) being a [Frobenius element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_element) for *p*. What happens at the ramified *p* is that ρ is non-trivial on the [inertia group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertia_group) *I*(*p*) for *p*. At those primes the definition must be 'corrected', taking the largest quotient of the representation ρ on which the inertia group acts by the [trivial representation](wikipedia://en/450573). With this refinement, the definition of *Z*(*s*) can be upgraded successfully from 'almost all' *p* to *all* *p* participating in the Euler product. The consequences for the functional equation were worked out by [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) and [Deligne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deligne) in the later 1960s; the functional equation itself has not been proved in general.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Hasse–Weil conjecture"}

## Hasse–Weil conjecture {#Hasse–Weil_conjecture}

The Hasse–Weil conjecture states that the Hasse–Weil zeta function should extend to a meromorphic function for all complex *s*, and should satisfy a functional equation similar to that of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809). For elliptic curves over the rational numbers, the Hasse–Weil conjecture follows[^o7] from the [modularity theorem](wikipedia://en/174475): each elliptic curve $E$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is modular.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture"}

## Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture {#Birch_and_Swinnerton-Dyer_conjecture}

The [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161) states that the [rank](wikipedia://en/244541) of the [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) *E*(*K*) of points of an elliptic curve *E* is the order of the zero of the Hasse–Weil *L*-function *L*(*E*, *s*) at *s* = 1, and that the first non-zero coefficient in the [Taylor expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_expansion) of *L*(*E*, *s*) at *s* = 1 is given by more refined arithmetic data attached to *E* over *K*.[^o10] The conjecture is one of the seven [Millennium Prize Problems](wikipedia://en/27599234) listed by the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655), which has offered a \$1,000,000 prize for the first correct proof.[^o11]

:::

## Elliptic curves over Q {#Elliptic_curves_over_Q}

An elliptic curve is a specific type of variety. Let *E* be an [elliptic curve over **Q**](wikipedia://en/10225#Elliptic_curves_over_the_rational_numbers) of [conductor](wikipedia://en/18847878) *N*. Then, *E* has good reduction at all primes *p* not dividing *N*, it has [multiplicative reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semistable_elliptic_curve) at the primes *p* that *exactly* divide *N* (i.e. such that *p* divides *N*, but *p*<sup>2</sup> does not; this is written *p* || *N*), and it has [additive reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additive_reduction) elsewhere (i.e. at the primes where *p*<sup>2</sup> divides *N*). The Hasse–Weil zeta function of *E* then takes the form

$$Z_{V\!,\mathbb{Q}}(s)= \frac{\zeta(s)\zeta(s-1)}{L(E,s)}. \,$$

Here, ζ(*s*) is the usual [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) and *L*(*E*, *s*) is called the *L*-function of *E*/**Q**, which takes the form[^o13]

$$L(E,s)=\prod_pL_p(E,s)^{-1} \,$$

where, for a given prime *p*,

$$L_p(E,s)=\begin{cases} (1-a_pp^{-s}+p^{1-2s}), & \text{if } p\nmid N \\ (1-a_pp^{-s}), & \text{if }p\mid N \text{ and } p^2 \nmid N \\ 1, & \text{if }p^2\mid N \end{cases}$$

where in the case of good reduction *a*<sub>*p*</sub> is *p* + 1 − (number of points of *E* mod *p*), and in the case of multiplicative reduction *a*<sub>*p*</sub> is ±1 depending on whether *E* has split (plus sign) or non-split (minus sign) multiplicative reduction at *p*. A multiplicative reduction of curve *E* by the prime *p* is said to be split if -c<sub>6</sub> is a square in the finite field with p elements.[^o14]

There is a useful relation not using the conductor:

1. If *p* doesn't divide $\Delta$ (where $\Delta$ is the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/10225#Elliptic_curves_over_the_real_numbers) of the elliptic curve) then *E* has good reduction at *p*.
1. If *p* divides $\Delta$ but not $c_4$ then *E* has multiplicative bad reduction at *p*.
1. If *p* divides both $\Delta$ and $c_4$ then *E* has additive bad reduction at *p*.
