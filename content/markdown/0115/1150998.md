# Nicolaus II Bernoulli

*Image: Bernoulli Nicolaus(II).jpeg.* Nicolaus II Bernoulli, portrait by [Johann Rudolf Huber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Rudolf_Huber) (1723).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicolaus II Bernoulli"}

**Nicolaus II Bernoulli** (also **Niklaus** or **Nikolaus**; 6 February 1695 in [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel) – 9 August 1726 in [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg)) was a Swiss [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) as were his father [Johann Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/268886) and one of his brothers, [Daniel Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/46784). He was one of the many prominent mathematicians in the [Bernoulli family](wikipedia://en/199226).

:::

## Work {#Work}

Nicolaus worked mostly on [curves](wikipedia://en/89246), [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), and [probability](wikipedia://en/22934). He was a friend and contemporary of [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902), who studied under Nicolaus' father. He also contributed to [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034).

He was the older brother of [Daniel Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/46784), to whom he also taught mathematics. Even in his youth he had learned several languages. From the age of 13, he studied mathematics and law at the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel). In 1711 he received his Master's of Philosophy; in 1715 he received a Doctorate in Law. In 1716–1717 he was a private tutor in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice). From 1719 he had the Chair in Mathematics at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua), as the successor of [Giovanni Poleni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Poleni). He served as an assistant to his father, among other areas, in the correspondence over the priority dispute between [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) and [Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281), and also in the priority dispute between his father and the English mathematician [Brook Taylor](wikipedia://en/210429). In 1720 he posed the problem of reciprocal [orthogonal trajectories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_trajectories), which was intended as a challenge for the English Newtonians. From 1723 he was a law professor at the Berner Oberen Schule. In 1725 he together with his brother Daniel, with whom he was touring Italy and France at this time, was invited by [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) to the newly founded [St. Petersburg Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Academy). Eight months after his appointment he came down with a fever and died. His professorship was succeeded in 1727 by [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902), whom the Bernoulli brothers had recommended. His early death cut short a promising career.
