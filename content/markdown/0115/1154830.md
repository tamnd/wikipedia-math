# Planimetrics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Planimetrics"}

**Planimetrics** is the study of [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) measurements, including [angles](wikipedia://en/1196), [distances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distances), and [areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areas).

:::

## History {#History}

To measure planimetrics a [planimeter](wikipedia://en/1103376) or [dot planimeter](wikipedia://en/67446631) is used. This rather advanced analog technology is being taken over by simple [image measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_measurement) software tools like, [ImageJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ImageJ), [Adobe Acrobat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Acrobat), [Google Earth Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Earth_Pro), [Gimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIMP), [Photoshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoshop) and [KLONK Image Measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLONK_Image_Measurement) which can help do this kind of work from digitized images.

## In geography {#In_geography}

Planimetric elements in geography are those features that are independent of elevation, such as roads, building footprints, and rivers and lakes. They are represented on two-dimensional maps as they are seen from the air, or in aerial photography.[^o4] These features are often digitized from [orthorectified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthorectified) [aerial photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_photography) into data layers that can be used in analysis and cartographic outputs.[^o5]

A *planimetric map* is one that does not include [relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartographic_relief_depiction) data.[^o6]
