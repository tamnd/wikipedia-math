# Jayadeva (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jayadeva"}

**Jayadeva** (c. 1000 CE) was an Indian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who further developed the cyclic method ([Chakravala method](wikipedia://en/4050658))[^o2] that was called by [Hermann Hankel](wikipedia://en/932515) "the finest thing achieved in the theory of numbers before [Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_Lagrange) (18th century)".[^o3] He also made significant contributions to [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).[^o4]

:::

Jayadeva's works are [lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_literary_work), and he is known only from a 20-verse quotation in Udaya-divakara *Sundari* (c. 1073), a commentary on [Bhaskara I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaskara_I)'s *Laghu-bhaskariya*. This means that Jayadeva must have lived sometime before 1073,[^o5] possibly around 1000 CE.[^o2]
