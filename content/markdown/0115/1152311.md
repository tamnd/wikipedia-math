# Signature (topology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Signature"}

In the field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), the **signature** is an integer [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) which is defined for an oriented [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* of dimension [divisible by four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisible_by_four).

:::

This invariant of a manifold has been studied in detail, starting with [Rokhlin's theorem](wikipedia://en/7031816) for 4-manifolds, and [Hirzebruch signature theorem](wikipedia://en/7192444).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) and [oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriented) manifold *M* of dimension 4*k*, the [cup product](wikipedia://en/848684) gives rise to a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) *Q* on the 'middle' real [cohomology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group)

$$H^{2k}(M,\mathbf{R}).$$

The basic identity for the cup product

$$\alpha^p \smile \beta^q = (-1)^{pq}(\beta^q \smile \alpha^p)$$

shows that with *p* = *q* = 2*k* the product is [symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric). It takes values in

$$H^{4k}(M,\mathbf{R}).$$

If we assume also that *M* is [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_manifold), [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) identifies this with

$$H_{0}(M,\mathbf{R})$$

which can be identified with $\mathbf{R}$. Therefore, the cup product, under these hypotheses, does give rise to a [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375) on *H*<sup>2*k*</sup>(*M*,*R*); and therefore to a quadratic form *Q*. The form *Q* is [non-degenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-degenerate) due to Poincaré duality, as it pairs non-degenerately with itself.[^o4] More generally, the signature can be defined in this way for any general compact [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) with *4n*-dimensional Poincaré duality.

The **signature** $\sigma(M)$ of *M* is by definition the [signature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_%28quadratic_form%29) of *Q*, that is, $\sigma(M) = n_+ - n_-$ where any diagonal matrix defining *Q* has $n_+$ positive entries and $n_-$ negative entries.[^o5] If *M* is not connected, its signature is defined to be the sum of the signatures of its connected components.

:::

## Other dimensions {#Other_dimensions}

If *M* has dimension not divisible by 4, its signature is usually defined to be 0. There are alternative generalization in [L-theory](wikipedia://en/696568): the signature can be interpreted as the 4*k*-dimensional (simply connected) symmetric L-group $L^{4k},$ or as the 4*k*-dimensional quadratic L-group $L_{4k},$ and these invariants do not always vanish for other dimensions. The [Kervaire invariant](wikipedia://en/13816037) is a mod 2 (i.e., an element of $\mathbf{Z}/2$) for framed manifolds of dimension 4*k*+2 (the quadratic L-group $L_{4k+2}$), while the [de Rham invariant](wikipedia://en/25901631) is a mod 2 invariant of manifolds of dimension 4*k*+1 (the symmetric L-group $L^{4k+1}$); the other dimensional L-groups vanish.

### Kervaire invariant {#Kervaire_invariant}

When $d=4k+2=2(2k+1)$ is twice an odd integer ([singly even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singly_even)), the same construction gives rise to an [antisymmetric bilinear form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisymmetric_bilinear_form). Such forms do not have a signature invariant; if they are non-degenerate, any two such forms are equivalent. However, if one takes a [quadratic refinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_refinement) of the form, which occurs if one has a [framed manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framed_manifold), then the resulting [ε-quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/13979549) need not be equivalent, being distinguished by the [Arf invariant](wikipedia://en/6950659). The resulting invariant of a manifold is called the [Kervaire invariant](wikipedia://en/13816037).

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Compact oriented manifolds *M* and *N* satisfy $\sigma(M \sqcup N) = \sigma(M) + \sigma(N)$ by definition, and satisfy $\sigma(M\times N) = \sigma(M)\sigma(N)$ by a [Künneth formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Künneth_formula).
- If *M* is an oriented boundary, then $\sigma(M)=0$.
- [René Thom](wikipedia://en/61633) (1954) showed that the signature of a manifold is a cobordism invariant, and in particular is given by some linear combination of its [Pontryagin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Semenovich_Pontryagin) [numbers](wikipedia://en/385944).[^o10] For example, in four dimensions, it is given by $\frac{p_1}{3}$. [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) (1954) found an explicit expression for this linear combination as the [L genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_genus) of the manifold.
- [William Browder](wikipedia://en/6646431) (1962) proved that a simply connected compact [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) with 4*n*-dimensional [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) is homotopy equivalent to a manifold if and only if its signature satisfies the expression of the [Hirzebruch signature theorem](wikipedia://en/7192444).
- [Rokhlin's theorem](wikipedia://en/7031816) says that the signature of a 4-dimensional simply connected manifold with a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303) is divisible by 16.
