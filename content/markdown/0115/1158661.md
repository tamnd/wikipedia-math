# Parameshvara Nambudiri

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Parameshvara Nambudiri"}

**Vatasseri Parameshvara Nambudiri** (c. 1380–1460)[^o3] was a major [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) of the [Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics](wikipedia://en/3099132) founded by [Madhava of Sangamagrama](wikipedia://en/2793007). He was also an [astrologer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrologer). Parameshvara was a proponent of [observational astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observational_astronomy) in [medieval India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_India) and he himself had made a series of [eclipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse) observations to verify the accuracy of the computational methods then in use. Based on his eclipse observations, Parameshvara proposed several corrections to the astronomical parameters which had been in use since the times of [Aryabhata](wikipedia://en/297204). The computational scheme based on the revised set of parameters has come to be known as the *Drgganita* or [Drig system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drig_system). Parameshvara was also a prolific writer on matters relating to astronomy. At least 25 manuscripts have been identified as being authored by Parameshvara.[^o3]

:::

## Biographical details {#Biographical_details}

Parameshvara was a [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) of Bhrgu[gotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotra) following the [Ashvalayanasutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashvalayana) of the [Rigveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda). Parameshvara's family name (*Illam*) was Vatasseri and his family resided in the village of Alathiyur (Sanskritised as *Asvatthagrama*) in [Tirur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirur), [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala). Alathiyur is situated on the northern bank of the river [Nila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nila_River) (river [Bharathappuzha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharathappuzha)) at its mouth in Kerala. He was a grandson of a disciple of [Govinda Bhattathiri](wikipedia://en/27043777) (1237–1295 CE), a legendary figure in the astrological traditions of [Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala).

Parameshvara studied under teachers Rudra and Narayana, and also under [Madhava of Sangamagrama](wikipedia://en/2793007) (c. 1350 – c. 1425) the founder of the [Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics](wikipedia://en/3099132). [Damodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damodara), another prominent member of the [Kerala school](wikipedia://en/3099132), was his son and also his pupil. Parameshvara was also a teacher of [Nilakantha Somayaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilakantha_Somayaji) (1444–1544) the author of the celebrated [Tantrasamgraha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantrasamgraha).

## Work {#Work}

Parameshvara wrote commentaries on many mathematical and astronomical works such as those by [Bhāskara I](wikipedia://en/1158626) and [Aryabhata](wikipedia://en/297204). He made a series of eclipse observations over a 55-year period. Constantly attempted to compare these with the theoretically computed positions of the planets. He revised planetary parameters based on his observations.

One of Parameshvara's more significant contributions was his [mean value type formula](wikipedia://en/19662) for the inverse interpolation of the sine.[^o6]

He was the first mathematician to give a formula for the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) circumscribing a cyclic quadrilateral.[^o7] The expression is sometimes attributed to [Lhuilier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhuilier) \[1782\], 350 years later. With the sides of the [cyclic quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/194131) being *a, b, c,* and *d*, the radius *R* of the circumscribed circle is:

$$R = \sqrt {\frac{(ab + cd)(ac + bd)(ad + bc)}{(- a + b + c + d)(a - b + c + d)(a + b - c + d)(a + b + c - d)}}.$$

## Works by Parameshvara {#Works_by_Parameshvara}

The following works of Parameshvara are well-known.[^o9] A complete list of all manuscripts attributed to Parameshvara is available in Pingree.[^o3]

- *Bhatadipika* – Commentary on [Āryabhaṭīya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Āryabhaṭīya) of [Āryabhaṭa I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Āryabhaṭa_I)
- *Karmadipika* – Commentary on *Mahabhaskariya* of Bhaskara I
- *Paramesvari* – Commentary on *Laghubhaskariya* of Bhaskara I
- *Sidhantadipika* – Commentary on *Mahabhaskariyabhashya* of [Govindasvāmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govindasvāmi)
- *Vivarana* – Commentary on [Surya Siddhanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surya_Siddhanta) and [Lilāvati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilāvati)
- *Drgganita* – Description of the [Drig system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drig_system) (composed in 1431 CE)
- *Goladipika* – Spherical geometry and astronomy (composed in 1443 CE)
- *Grahanamandana* – Computation of eclipses (Its epoch is 15 July 1411 CE.)
- *Grahanavyakhyadipika* – On the rationale of the theory of eclipses
- *Vakyakarana* – Methods for the derivation of several astronomical tables
