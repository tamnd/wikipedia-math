# Dorothy Edgington

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Dorothy Edgington"}

**Dorothy Margaret Doig Edgington**[^o3] [FBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_British_Academy) (née **Milne**, born 29 April 1941) is a British philosopher active in [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) and [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939).[^o4] She is particularly known for her work on the logic of [conditionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_sentences) and [vagueness](wikipedia://en/32369).[^o8]

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Dorothy Edgington was born on 29 April 1941 to Edward Milne and his wife Rhoda née Blair. She attended St Leonards School before going to [St Hilda's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Hilda's_College,_Oxford) to read PPE. She obtained her BA in 1964, followed in 1967 by a BPhil at [Nuffield College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuffield_College,_Oxford).[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Most of Edgington's career was spent at [Birkbeck College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkbeck_College). In 1968, her first academic post was as a Lecturer in Philosophy at Birkbeck, remaining there until 1996. From 1996 until 2001, she was appointed Fellow of [University College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Oxford). This was followed by a professorship at Birkbeck from 2001 to 2003. She was then [Waynflete Professor of Metaphysical Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynflete_Professor_of_Metaphysical_Philosophy) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford)[^o8] from 2003[^o9] until 2006. Currently, she is a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) and a Fellow of [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford) who teaches at Birkbeck again[^o9] part-time.

[Birkbeck College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkbeck,_University_of_London) hosts a lecture series named after Edgington. The lectures were given by [John McDowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McDowell) in 2012, given by [Rae Langton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rae_Helen_Langton) in 2014, and the Edgington Lectures were given by [Kit Fine](wikipedia://en/3853559) in 2016.[^o10]

From 2004 to 2005, she was President of the [Mind Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_Association). Later, she became President of the [Aristotelian Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelian_Society) from 2007 to 2008. She is a Fellow of the [British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy).[^o11]

In 2023, she was elected a member of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea).[^o12]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- 'The Paradox of Knowability' (1985), *Mind* 94:557–568. Presents a resolution of [Fitch's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitch's_paradox) based on [situation semantics](wikipedia://en/13279065).
- 'On Conditionals' (1995), *Mind* 104:235–329. Defends an epistemic theory of conditionals against a truth-functional one, as part of the Mind's state of the art series.
- 'Vagueness by Degrees'. In Rosanna Keefe & Peter Smith (eds.), *Vagueness: A Reader*. MIT Press (1997)
- 'Counterfactuals and the Benefit of Hindsight'. In Phil Dowe & Paul Noordhof (eds.), *Cause and Chance: Causation in an Indeterministic World*. Routledge (2004)
- *[Conditionals](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/conditionals/)* (2006), *The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*.
