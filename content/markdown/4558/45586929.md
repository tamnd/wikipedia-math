# Artin–Tate lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Artin–Tate lemma"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the **Artin–Tate lemma**, named after [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) and his former advisor [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351), states:[^o2]

:::

Let *A* be a [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346) [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) and $B \subset C$ [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29) [algebras over *A*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_over_a_ring). If *C* is of finite type over *A* and if *C* is finite over *B*, then *B* is of finite type over *A*.

(Here, "of finite type" means "[finitely generated algebra](wikipedia://en/11985661)" and "finite" means "[finitely generated module](wikipedia://en/326430)".) The lemma was introduced by E. Artin and J. Tate in 1951[^o3] to give a [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) of [Hilbert's Nullstellensatz](wikipedia://en/149215).

The lemma is similar to the [Eakin–Nagata theorem](wikipedia://en/62674651), which says: if *C* is finite over *B* and *C* is a Noetherian ring, then *B* is a Noetherian ring.

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The following proof can be found in Atiyah–MacDonald.[^o6] Let $x_1,\ldots, x_m$ generate $C$ as an $A$-algebra and let $y_1, \ldots, y_n$ generate $C$ as a $B$-module. Then we can write

$$x_i = \sum_j b_{ij}y_j \quad \text{and} \quad y_iy_j = \sum_{k}b_{ijk}y_k$$

with $b_{ij},b_{ijk} \in B$. Then $C$ is finite over the $A$-algebra $B_0$ generated by the $b_{ij},b_{ijk}$. Using that $A$ and hence $B_0$ is Noetherian, also $B$ is finite over $B_0$. Since $B_0$ is a finitely generated $A$-algebra, also $B$ is a finitely generated $A$-algebra.

:::

## Noetherian necessary {#Noetherian_necessary}

Without the assumption that *A* is Noetherian, the statement of the Artin–Tate lemma is no longer true. Indeed, for any non-Noetherian ring *A* we can define an *A*-algebra structure on $C = A\oplus A$ by declaring $(a,x)(b,y) = (ab,bx+ay)$. Then for any [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) $I \subset A$ which is not finitely generated, $B = A \oplus I \subset C$ is not of finite type over *A*, but all conditions as in the lemma are satisfied.
