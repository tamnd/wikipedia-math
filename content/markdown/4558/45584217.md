# Dot product representation of a graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dot product representation of a graph"}

A **dot product representation of a simple graph** is a method of representing a [graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_%28graph_theory%29) using vector spaces and the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) from [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422). Every graph has a dot product representation.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *G* be a graph with vertex set *V*. Let *F* be a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), and *f* a function from *V* to *F*<sup>*k*</sup> such that *xy* is an edge of *G* if and only if *f*(*x*)·*f*(*y*) ≥ *t*. This is the dot product representation of *G*. The number *t* is called the **dot product threshold**, and the smallest possible value of *k* is called the **dot product dimension.**[^o2]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A [threshold graph](wikipedia://en/11503485) is a dot product graph with positive t and dot product dimension 1.[^o2]
- Every [interval graph](wikipedia://en/363423) has dot product dimension at most 2.[^o2]
- Every [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) has dot product dimension at most 4.[^o9]

:::
