# Chan–Karolyi–Longstaff–Sanders process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chan–Karolyi–Longstaff–Sanders process"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Chan–Karolyi–Longstaff–Sanders process** (abbreviated as **CKLS process**) is a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) with applications to [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance). In particular it has been used to model the [term structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_structure) of [interest rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rates). The CKLS process can also be viewed as a generalization of the [Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process](wikipedia://en/2183186). It is named after K. C. Chan, G. Andrew Karolyi, Francis A. Longstaff, and Anthony B. Sanders, with their paper published in 1992.[^o2][^n1]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The CKLS process $X_t$ is defined by the following [stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/1361454):

> $$dX_t = (\alpha + \beta X_t) dt + \sigma X_t^{\gamma}dW_t$$

where $W_t$ denotes the [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984). The CKLS process has the following equivalent definition:[^o6]

> $$dX_t = -k(X_t - a) dt + \sigma X_t^{\gamma}dW_t$$

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- CKLS is an example of a [mean-reverting process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean-reverting_process)[^o6]
- The [moment-generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment-generating_function) (MGF) of $X_t^{2(1-\gamma)}$ has a [singularity](wikipedia://en/54240) at a critical moment independent of $\gamma$. Moreover, the MGF can be written as the MGF of the CIR model plus a term that is a solution to a [Nonlinear partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/16678376).
- The CKLS equation has a unique pathwise solution.[^o9]
- Cai and Wang (2015) have derived a [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406) and deviation principle for the CKLS model while studying its asymptotic behavior.[^o9]
- CKLS has been referred to as a time-homogeneous model as usually the parameters $\alpha, \beta, \sigma, \gamma$ are taken to be time-independent.[^o10]
- The CKLS has also been referred to as a [one-factor model](wikipedia://en/52100819) (also see [Factor analysis](wikipedia://en/253492)).[^o11][^o12]

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

Many [interest rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate) models and [short-rate models](wikipedia://en/1219048) are special cases of the CKLS process which can be obtained by setting the CKLS model parameters to specific values.[^o2][^o12] In all cases, $\sigma$ is assumed to be positive.

| CIR VR | 0 | 0 | 3/2 |
| [CEV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constant_elasticity_of_variance_model) | 0 | Any | Any |

## Financial applications {#Financial_applications}

The CKLS process is often used to model interest rate dynamics and pricing of [bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_%28finance%29), [bond options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_option),[^o22] [currency exchange rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_exchange_rate),[^o23] [securities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_%28finance%29),[^o24] and other [options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_%28finance%29), [derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_%28finance%29), and [contingent claims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contingent_claim).[^o25][^o10] It has also been used in the pricing of [fixed income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_income) and [credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_risk) and has been combined with other [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) methods such as [GARCH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GARCH)-class models.[^o26]

One question studied in the literature is how to set the model parameters, in particular the elasticity parameter $\gamma$.[^o27][^o28] [Robust statistics](wikipedia://en/2885691) and [nonparametric estimation techniques](wikipedia://en/223369) have been used to measure CKLS model parameters.[^o11][^o10]

In their original paper, CKLS argued that the elasticity of interest rate volatility is 1.5 based on historical data, a result that has been widely cited. Also, they showed that models with $\gamma \ge 1$ can model [short-term interest rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-term_interest_rates_%28disambiguation%29) more accurately than models with $\gamma < 1$.[^o2]

Later empirical studies by Bliss and Smith have shown the reverse: sometimes lower $\gamma$ values (like 0.5) in the CKLS model can capture volatility dependence more accurately compared to higher $\gamma$ values. Moreover, by redefining the regime period, Bliss and Smith have shown that there is evidence for regime shift in the [Federal Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve) between 1979 and 1982. They have found evidence supporting the square root Cox-Ingersoll-Ross model (CIR SR), a special case of the CKLS model with $\gamma = 1/2$.[^o29]

The period of 1979-1982 marked a change in [monetary policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_policy) of the [Federal Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve), and this regime change has often been studied in the context of CKLS models.[^o11]
