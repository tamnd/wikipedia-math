# Frank Yates

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Frank Yates"}

**Frank Yates** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (12 May 1902 – 17 June 1994) was an English statistician who was one of the pioneers of 20th-century [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685).[^o3][^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Yates was born in [Manchester, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester), the eldest of five children (and only son) of seed merchant and [botanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany) [Percy Yates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Gates_Morgan) and his wife Edith. He attended [Wadham House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadham_House), a [private school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_school), before gaining a scholarship to [Clifton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifton_College) in 1916. In 1920, he obtained a scholarship at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), and four years later graduated with a [First Class Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_honours) degree.[^o3][^o4]

He spent two years teaching [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) to [secondary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_School_Mathematics_Curriculum_Improvement_Study) pupils at [Malvern College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvern_College) before heading to [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa), where he was mathematical advisor on the [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_%28British_colony%29) Survey. He returned to [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), due to ill health, and met and married a chemist, Margaret Forsythe Marsden, the daughter of a [civil servant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_servant). This marriage was dissolved in 1933, and he later married Prascovie (Pauline) Tchitchkine, previously the partner of Alexis Tchitchkine. After her death in 1976, he married Ruth Hunt, his long-time secretary.[^o3][^o4]

In 1931, Yates was appointed assistant statistician at [Rothamsted Experimental Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Experimental_Station) by [R.A. Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824). In 1933, he became head of statistics when Fisher went to [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London). At Rothamsted he worked on the [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541), including contributions to the theory of [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634), as well as developing [Yates's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yates's_algorithm) and the balanced incomplete [block design](wikipedia://en/1188184).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he worked on what would later be called [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476).[^o3][^o5]

After WWII, he worked on [sample survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_survey) design and analysis. He became an enthusiast of electronic [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer), in 1954 obtaining an [Elliott 401](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Brothers_%28computer_company%29) for Rothamsted and contributing to the initial development of [statistical computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_computing).[^o3][^o4][^o5] During 1960–61, he was President of the [British Computer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Computer_Society), succeeding the founding president and computer pioneer, [Maurice Wilkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Wilkes). In 1960, he was awarded the [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Gold of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) and, in 1966, he was awarded the [Royal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Medal) of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society).[^o3][^o4] He retired from Rothamsted to become a senior research fellow at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London).[^o3][^o4] He died in 1994, aged 92, in [Harpenden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpenden).[^o3]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- *The design and analysis of factorial experiments*, Technical Communication no. 35 of the Commonwealth Bureau of Soils (1937) (alternatively attributed to the Imperial Bureau of Soil Science).
- *Statistical tables for biological, agricultural and medical research* (1938, coauthor [R.A. Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824)): [sixth edition, 1963](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/dspace/bitstream/2440/10701/1/stat_tab.pdf)
- *Sampling methods for censuses and surveys* (1949)
- Computer programs GENFAC, RGSP, Fitquan.
