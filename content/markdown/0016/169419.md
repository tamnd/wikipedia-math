# Metamagical Themas

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Metamagical Themas"}

***Metamagical Themas*** is an eclectic collection of articles that [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758) wrote for the [popular science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_science) magazine *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* during the early 1980s. The anthology was published in 1985 by [Basic Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Books).

:::

The volume is substantial in size and contains extensive notes concerning responses to the articles and other information relevant to their content. (One of the notes—page 65—suggested [memetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memetics) for the study of [memes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memes).)

## Contents {#Contents}

Major themes of the columns include self-reference in [memes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meme), language, art and logic; discussions of philosophical issues important in cognitive science/AI; analogies and what makes something similar to something else (specifically what makes, for example, an uppercase letter 'A' recognizable as such) [^o4]; and lengthy discussions of the work of [Robert Axelrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Axelrod_%28political_scientist%29) on the [prisoner's dilemma](wikipedia://en/43717), as well as the idea of [superrationality](wikipedia://en/277125).[^o5]

The concept of superrationality, and its relevance to the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War), environmental issues and such, is accompanied by notes on experiments conducted by the author at the time[^o6]. Another notable feature is the inclusion of two dialogues in the style of those appearing in *[Gödel, Escher, Bach](wikipedia://en/48103)*. [Ambigrams](wikipedia://en/440995) are mentioned.

There are three articles centered on the [Lisp programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_programming_language), in which Hofstadter first details the language itself, and then shows how it relates to [Gödel's incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_incompleteness_theorem). Two articles are devoted to [Rubik's Cube](wikipedia://en/25971) and similar puzzles. Many chapters open with an illustration of an extremely abstract alphabet, yet one which is still [gestaltly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestalt_psychology) recognizable as such.

The game of [Nomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomic) was first introduced to the public in this column, in June 1982, when excerpts from a book (still unpublished at the time) by the game's creator [Peter Suber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Suber) were printed and discussed.

The index of the book mentions Hofstadter's recurring alter ego, [Egbert B. Gebstadter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egbert_B._Gebstadter), whose "book" is also referenced in the Bibliography.

## List of Hofstadter's "Metamagical Themas" Chapters {#List_of_Hofstadter's_"Metamagical_Themas"_Chapters}

From January 1957 through December 1980, [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435)'s "[Mathematical Games](wikipedia://en/44051448)" column was a monthly feature in *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* magazine. In 1981, Gardner's column alternated with a new column by Hofstadter called "Metamagical Themas" (an [anagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagram) of "Mathematical Games"). Then Hofstadter's column appeared monthly from January 1982 through July 1983.[^o8]

The Chapters were re-ordered so that they could be grouped more cohesively into seven Sections. A *Post Scriptum* was added to each chapter often detailing correspondence with readers after the publication of the column.

## French edition {#French_edition}

*Metamagical Themas* was also published in French, under the title *Ma Thémagie* (InterEditions, 1988), the translators being Jean-Baptiste Berthelin, Jean-Luc Bonnetain, and Lise Rosenbaum.

Hofstadter talks of the challenges of translating this book in his subsequent work on translation, Le Ton Beau de Marot. [^o15]

## Reception {#Reception}

[Dave Langford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Langford) reviewed *Metamagical Themas* for *[White Dwarf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Dwarf_%28magazine%29)* #88, and stated that "a heady mixture of computers, art, mathematics, philosophy, jokes and above all games."[^o17]

[Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435), the creator of the Mathematical column in Scientific American said “The incredible Dr. Hofstadter is back, in golden-braid form, with a collection of wide-ranging essays that swarm with extraordinary ideas, brilliant fables, deep philosophical questions and Carrollian word play.” [^o18]
