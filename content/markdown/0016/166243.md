# Ralph Abraham (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ralph Abraham"}

**Ralph Herman Abraham** (July 4, 1936 – September 19, 2024) was an American mathematician. In 1968 he became a member of the faculty of the [University of California, Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Cruz) (UCSC), and later stayed on as a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) of mathematics.

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Abraham earned his [BSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Engineering) (1956), [MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) (1958), and [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) (1960) from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). His PhD thesis, titled *Discontinuities in General Relativity*, was written under the direction of Nathaniel Coburn. Prior to joining UCSC, he held positions at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) (research lecturer in mathematics; 1960–1962), [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) (postdoctoral fellow and assistant professor of mathematics; 1962–1964) and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) (assistant professor of mathematics; 1964–1968). He has also held visiting positions in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam), [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), [Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick), [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona), [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel), and [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence).

He founded the Visual Math Institute at UCSC[^o5] in 1975; at that time, it was called the "Visual Mathematics Project".[^o6] He was editor of *World Futures* and for the *International Journal of Bifurcations and Chaos*. Abraham was a member of cultural historian [William Irwin Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Irwin_Thompson)'s [Lindisfarne Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindisfarne_Association).[^o7]

Abraham has been involved in the development of [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632) since the 1960s and 1970s. He has been a consultant on [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295) and its applications in numerous fields, such as medical physiology, ecology, mathematical economics, and psychotherapy.[^o8]

Another interest of Abraham's concerns alternative ways of expressing mathematics, for example visually or aurally. He has staged performances in which mathematics, visual arts and [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music) are combined into one presentation. Abraham developed an interest in "Hip" activities in Santa Cruz in the 1960s and had a website gathering information on the topic.[^o9] He credited his use of the [psychedelic drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_drug) [DMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimethyltryptamine) with "swerv\[ing his\] career toward a search for the connections between mathematics and the experience of the Logos".[^o10]

Abraham died at his home in Santa Cruz County, at the age of 88.[^o11]
