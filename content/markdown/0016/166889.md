# Wiener equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wiener equation"}

A simple mathematical representation of [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436), the **Wiener equation**, named after [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185),[^o2] assumes the current [velocity](wikipedia://en/20491903) of a [fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid) particle fluctuates [randomly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random):

:::

$$\mathbf{v} = \frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt} = g(t),$$

where **v** is [velocity](wikipedia://en/20491903), **x** is position, *d/dt* is the time [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921), and *g(t)* may for instance be [white noise](wikipedia://en/46182).

Since velocity changes instantly in this formalism, the *Wiener equation* is not suitable for short time scales. In those cases, the [Langevin equation](wikipedia://en/166890), which looks at particle [acceleration](wikipedia://en/2443), must be used.
