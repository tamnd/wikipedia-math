# Sides of an equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sides of an equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **LHS** is informal shorthand for the **left-hand side** of an [equation](wikipedia://en/9284). Similarly, **RHS** is the **right-hand side**. The two sides have the same value, expressed differently, since [equality](wikipedia://en/90446) is [symmetric](wikipedia://en/9259).[^o2]

:::

More generally, these terms may apply to an [inequation](wikipedia://en/89486) or [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489); the right-hand side is everything on the right side of a [test operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_operator) in an [expression](wikipedia://en/609125), with LHS defined similarly.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The expression on the right side of the "=" sign is the right side of the equation and the expression on the left of the "=" is the left side of the equation.

For example, in

$$x + 5 = y + 8$$

$x + 5$ is the **left-hand side** (LHS) and $y + 8$ is the **right-hand side** (RHS).

:::

## Homogeneous and inhomogeneous equations {#Homogeneous_and_inhomogeneous_equations}

In solving mathematical equations, particularly [linear simultaneous equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_simultaneous_equations), [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) and [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234), the terminology *homogeneous* is often used for equations with some [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) *L* on the LHS and 0 on the RHS. In contrast, an equation with a non-zero RHS is called *inhomogeneous* or *non-homogeneous*, as exemplified by

*Lf* = *g*,

with *g* a fixed function, which equation is to be solved for *f*. Then any solution of the inhomogeneous equation may have a solution of the homogeneous equation added to it, and still remain a solution.

For example in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), the homogeneous equation may correspond to a physical theory formulated in [empty space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_space), while the inhomogeneous equation asks for more 'realistic' solutions with some matter, or charged particles.

## Syntax {#Syntax}

More abstractly, when using [infix notation](wikipedia://en/199014)

*T* \* *U*

the term *T* stands as the **left-hand side** and *U* as the **right-hand side** of the [operator](wikipedia://en/4140245) \*. This usage is less common, though.
