# Name

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Name"}

*Image: Balinese ground zero.JPG.* Names of the [2002 Bali bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Bali_bombings) victims in [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia)

A **name** is a [term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminology) used for identification by an external observer. A name can identify a class or category of things, or a single thing, either uniquely, or within a given context. The entity identified by a name is called its [referent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Referent). A [personal name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_name) identifies, not necessarily uniquely, a *specific* individual human. The name of a specific entity is sometimes called a [proper name](wikipedia://en/23484) (although that term has a philosophical meaning as well) and is, when consisting of only one word, a [proper noun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_noun). Other nouns are sometimes called "**common names**" or ([obsolete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsolete)) "**general names**". A name can be given to a person, place, or thing; for example, parents can give their child a name or a scientist can give an element a name.

:::

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word *name* comes from [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) *nama*; cognate with [Old High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_High_German) (OHG) *namo*, [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) नामन् (*nāman*), [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *[nomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_naming_conventions)*, [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) ὄνομα (*onoma*), and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) نام (*nâm*), from the [Proto-Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European) (PIE) *\*h₁nómn̥*.[^o3] Outside Indo-European, it can be connected to [Proto-Uralic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Uralic_language) *\*nime*.

## Naming conventions {#Naming_conventions}

A [naming convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_convention) is a set of agreed, stipulated, or generally accepted [standards, norms, social norms, or criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_%28norm%29) for naming things.

Parents may follow a naming convention when selecting names for their children. Some have chosen alphabetical names by birth order. In some East Asian cultures it is common for one syllable in a two-syllable [given name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Given_name) to be a [generation name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_name) which is the same for immediate siblings. In many cultures it is common for the son to be named after the father or a grandfather. In certain African cultures, such as in Cameroon, the eldest son gets the family name for his given name. In other cultures, the name may include the place of residence, or the place of birth.

Major naming conventions include:

- In [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), [astronomical naming conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_naming_conventions)
- In [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), [binomial nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_nomenclature)
- In [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), [chemical nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_nomenclature)
- In [classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics), [Roman naming conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_naming_conventions)
- In [computer programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programming), [identifier naming conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identifier_naming_convention)
- In [computer networking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_networking), [computer naming schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_naming_scheme)
- In [planetary science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_science), [planetary nomenclature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_nomenclature)
- In [sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science) generally, [systematic names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systematic_name) for a variety of things

Products may follow a naming convention. [Automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) typically have a binomial name, a "make" (manufacturer) and a "model", in addition to a model year, such as a 2007 [Chevrolet Corvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Corvette). Sometimes there is a name for the car's "decoration level" or "trim line" as well: e.g., [Cadillac Escalade EXT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac_Escalade_EXT) *[Platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum)*, after the precious metal. [Computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computers) often have increasing numbers in their names to signify the next generation.

Courses at schools typically follow a naming convention: an abbreviation for the subject area and then a number ordered by increasing level of difficulty.

Many numbers (e.g., bank accounts, government IDs, credit cards, etc.) are not random but have an internal structure and convention. Virtually all organizations that assign names or numbers will follow some convention in generating these identifiers. Airline flight numbers, [Space Shuttle flight numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Space_Shuttle_missions), even [phone numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_number) all have an internal convention.

## Personal name {#Personal_name}

*Image: A History of Barrington, Rhode Island - Autographs.jpg.* A [signature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature) is a person's own handwritten name

A [personal name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_name) is an identifying word or words by which an individual is intimately known or designated.[^o6] In many countries, it is traditional for individuals to have a personal name (also called a [given name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Given_name) or first name) and a [surname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surname) (also called a last name or [family name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_name) because it is shared by members of the same family).[^o7] Some people have two surnames, one inherited from each parent. In most of Europe and the Americas, the given name typically comes before the surname, whereas in parts of Asia and [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) the surname comes before the given name ([Eastern name order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_name_order)). In some cultures it is traditional for a woman to take her husband's surname when she gets married.

A common practice in many countries is [patronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronym) which means that a component of a personal name is based on the given name of one's father. A less common practice in countries is [matronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matronym) which means that a component of a personal name is based on the given name of one's mother. In some [East Asian cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_culture), it is traditional for given names to include a [generation name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_name), a syllable shared between siblings and cousins of the same generation.

[Middle names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_names) are also used by many people as a third identifier, and can be chosen for personal reasons including signifying relationships, preserving pre-marital/maiden names (a popular practice in the United States), and to perpetuate family names. The practice of using [middle names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_name) dates back to ancient Rome, where it was common for members of the elite to have a *[praenomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praenomen)* (a personal name), a *[nomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_naming_conventions)* (a family name, not exactly used the way middle names are used today), and a *[cognomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognomen)* (a name representing an individual attribute or the specific branch of a person's family).[^o8] Middle names eventually fell out of use, but regained popularity in Europe during the nineteenth century.[^o8]

Besides first, middle, and last names, individuals may also have [nicknames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicknames), [aliases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliases), or [titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titles). Nicknames are informal names used by friends or family to refer to a person ("Chris" may be used as a short form of the personal name "Christopher"). A person may choose to use an alias, or a fake name, instead of their real name, possibly to protect or obscure their identity. People may also have titles designating their role in an institution or profession (members of royal families may use various terms such as [king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King), [queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_regnant), [duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke), or [duchess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchess) to signify their positions of authority or their relation to the throne).[^o7]

## Names of names {#Names_of_names}

In [onomastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onomastic) terminology, personal names of men are called *andronyms* (from [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) ἀνήρ / man, and ὄνομα / name),[^n1] while personal names of women are called *gynonyms* (from [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) γυνή / woman, and ὄνομα / name).[^n2]

## Brand names {#Brand_names}

Developing a name for a brand or product is heavily influenced by [marketing research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing_research) and [strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing_strategy) to be appealing and marketable. The brand name is often a [neologism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neologism) or [pseudoword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoword), such as [Kodak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodak) or [Sony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony).

## Religious names {#Religious_names}

section

*Image: secret of secrets a.jpg.* Two charts from an Arabic copy of the *Secretum Secretorum* for determining whether a person will live or die based on the numerical value of the patient's name.

In the ancient world, particularly in the ancient near-east ([Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel), [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt), [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia)) names were thought to be extremely powerful and act, in some ways, as a separate manifestation of a person or deity.[^o16] This viewpoint is responsible both for the [reluctance to use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_God_in_Judaism) the [proper name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YHWH) of [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God) in [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) writing or speech, as well as the common understanding in ancient [magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_%28paranormal%29) that magical rituals had to be carried out "in \[someone's\] name". By invoking a [god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deity) or spirit by name, one was thought to be able to summon that spirit's power for some kind of miracle or magic.

### Biblical names {#Biblical_names}

In the [Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament), the names of individuals are meaningful, and a change of name indicates a change of status. For example, the patriarch [Abram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abram) and his wife Sarai were renamed "[Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham)" and "[Sarah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah)" at the institution of the [Abrahamic covenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_covenant) ([Genesis 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genesis_17):4, 17:15). Simon was renamed Peter when he was given the [Keys to Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keys_to_Heaven).

Throughout the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible), characters are given names at birth that reflect something of significance or describe the course of their lives. For example: [Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon) meant [peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace),[^o18] and the king with that name was the first whose reign was without [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War).[^o19] Likewise, [Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_%28Hebrew_Bible%29) named his firstborn son [Manasseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manasseh_%28tribal_patriarch%29) (Hebrew: "causing to forget")(Genesis 41:51); when Joseph also said, "God has made me forget all my troubles and everyone in my father's family." Biblical Jewish people did not have surnames which were passed from generation to generation. However, they were typically known as the child of their father. For example: דוד בן ישי (David ben Yishay) meaning, [David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David), son of [Jesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_%28biblical_figure%29) (1 Samuel 17:12,58). Today, this style of name is still used in [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious) rites.

### Indian name {#Indian_name}

Indian names are based on a variety of systems and [naming conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_conventions), which vary from region to region. Names are also influenced by religion and [caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste) and may come from [epics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_epic_poetry). [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India)'s population speaks a [wide variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_India) of languages and nearly every major [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religions_of_the_world) in the world has a following in India. This variety makes for differences in names and naming styles. Due to [historical Indian cultural influences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India), several names across [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia) and [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asia) are influenced by or adapted from Indian names or words.

For some Indians, their [birth name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name) is different from their official name; the birth name starts with a randomly selected name from the [person's horoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_astrology) (based on the *[nakshatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakshatra)* or lunar mansion corresponding to the person's birth).

Many children are given three names, sometimes as a part of religious teaching.

### Quranic names {#Quranic_names}

Most names in the Quran are Arabic, such as Allah, Muhammad, Khwaja, Ismail, Mehboob, Suhelahmed, Shoheb Ameena, Aaisha, Sameena, Rumana, and Swaleha.

## Name use by animals and plants {#Name_use_by_animals_and_plants}

The use of personal names is not unique to humans. [Dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin)[^o23] and [green-rumped parrotlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green-rumped_parrotlet)[^o24] also use symbolic names to address [contact calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_call) to specific individuals. Individual dolphins have distinctive signature whistles, to which they will respond even when there is no other information to clarify which dolphin is being referred to.
