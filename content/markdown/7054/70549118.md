# Miriam D. Mann

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Miriam D. Mann"}

**Miriam Daniel Mann** (1907–1967) was one of the first Black [female computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_%28occupation%29) for the [National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Advisory_Committee_for_Aeronautics) (NACA).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Mann was born in 1907, in Covington, Georgia.[^o4] She attended [Talladega College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talladega_College). She was married to Bill Mann,[^o8] with whom she had three children.[^o5] In 1943, in the wake of labor shortages caused by [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Mann responded to a recruitment drive for Black female mathematicians by the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA).[^o5] She subsequently attended a 10-week training course at [Hampton Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Institute) and was accepted for a position as a "human computer".[^o8][^o6] At the time she was hired the state of Virginia was [segregated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States), as was the NACA campus at [Langley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langley_Air_Force_Base), Virginia. Mann repeatedly removed the "COLORED COMPUTERS" sign segregating the cafeteria. The sign was replaced each time until Mann removed it a final time and it was never replaced.[^o7]

Mann worked for NACA (which became [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA) in 1958) until her retirement in 1966.[^o8] She died in 1967.[^o4]

In 2017 Mann's granddaughter [Duchess Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchess_Harris) co-authored a book with Sue Bradford Edwards entitled *Hidden Human Computers: The Black Women of NASA*.[^o9] In 2018 the Virginia Capitol Foundation announced that Mann's name would be on the [Virginia Women's Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Women's_Monument)'s glass Wall of Honor.[^o10]
