# Hans Föllmer

*Image: Hans\_Föllmer\_2006.jpg.* Föllmer in Madrid, 2006

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hans Föllmer"}

**Hans Föllmer** (20 May 1941 in [Heiligenstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilbad_Heiligenstadt), Thuringia, Germany) is a German mathematician, currently [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) at the [Humboldt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) of [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin),[^o2][^o3] visiting professor at the [National University of Singapore](wikipedia://en/286880), and [Andrew D. White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_D._White) Professor-at-Large at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). He was awarded the [Cantor medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor_medal) in 2006.[^o4] In 2007 he became *doctor [honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa)* at the [Paris Dauphine University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University).[^o5]

:::

Föllmer is widely known for his contributions to [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), stochastic analysis[^o6] and [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680).

In [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255), he made early contributions to the mathematical modeling of social interactions.[^o7]

In mathematical finance, he made fundamental contributions to the theory of risk measures[^o8] and the hedging of contingent claims.

The Föllmer process, a stochastic process first considered by [Erwin Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger) but formulated in the language of [stochastic differential equations](wikipedia://en/1361454) by Föllmer, is named after him.[^o9] In mathematical finance, the Föllmer-Schweizer decomposition is named after him and Martin Schweizer.[^o10]
