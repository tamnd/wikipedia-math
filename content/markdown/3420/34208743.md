# Iacopo Barsotti

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Iacopo Barsotti"}

**Iacopo Barsotti**, or **Jacopo Barsotti** (Turin, 28 April 1921 – Padua, 27 October 1987) was an Italian mathematician who introduced [Barsotti–Tate groups](wikipedia://en/29750969).

:::

In 1942 he graduated from the [Scuola Normale Superiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore) in [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa), and became assistant professor Francesco Severi at the [University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome) in 1946. In 1948 he emigrated to the US, first as a guest professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), then as a full professor at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) and at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University). In 1961 he was recalled to Pisa as a teacher first of [Geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), then of Algebra. From 1968 to his death he taught Geometry at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua). Iacopo was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 1982.[^o2]

His research work mainly concerned [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). In particular, in the field of group theory, he dealt with abelian varieties, theorizing what are now called [Barsotti–Tate groups](wikipedia://en/29750969) and which are at the basis of [crystalline cohomology](wikipedia://en/10220067). He also dealt with [theta functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theta_functions), generalizing them with the introduction of the "theta-like class of functions."

In [*p*-adic Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/26086853), so-called [period rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_ring) are denoted by the letter B, a nod to Barsotti's work.[^o3]
