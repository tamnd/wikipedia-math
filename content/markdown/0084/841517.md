# Ajima Naonobu

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ajima Naonobu"}

Ajima **Ajima Naonobu**, also known as **Ajima Manzō Chokuyen**, was a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) of the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period).[^o3]

:::

His [Dharma name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma_name) was (祖眞院智算量空居士).[^o4]

## Work {#Work}

Ajima is credited with introducing [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) into Japanese mathematics. The significance of this innovation is diminished by a likelihood that he had access to European writings on the subject.[^o6] Ajima also posed the question of inscribing three mutually tangent circles in a triangle;[^o7] these circles are now known as [Malfatti circles](wikipedia://en/1475894) after the later work of [Gian Francesco Malfatti](wikipedia://en/1475987), but two [triangle centers](wikipedia://en/12401488) derived from them, the Ajima–Malfatti points, are named after Ajima.[^o8][^o9]

Ajima was an astronomer at the Shogun's Observatory (*Bakufu Temmongaki*).[^o10]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

In 1976, the [International Astronomical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union) (IAU) honored Ajima by identifying a crater on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) with his name. [Naonobu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naonobu_%28crater%29) is a small [lunar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) [impact crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_crater) located on the eastern [Mare Fecunditatis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_Fecunditatis), to the northwest of the prominent crater [Langrenus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langrenus_%28crater%29).[^o12][^o13]
