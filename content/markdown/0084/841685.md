# Tractrix

*Image: Tractrixtry.gif.* Tractrix created by the end of a pole (lying flat on the ground). Its other end is first pushed then dragged by a finger as it spins out to one side.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tractrix"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **tractrix** (la; plural: **tractrices**) is the [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) along which an object moves, under the influence of [friction](wikipedia://en/11062), when pulled on a [horizontal plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_plane) by a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) attached to a pulling point (the *tractor*) that moves at a [right angle](wikipedia://en/76956) to the initial line between the object and the puller at an [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) speed. It is therefore a [curve of pursuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curve_of_pursuit). It was first introduced by [Claude Perrault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Perrault) in 1670, and later studied by [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) (1676) and [Christiaan Huygens](wikipedia://en/42127) (1693).[^o2]

:::

## Mathematical derivation {#Mathematical_derivation}

*Image: Tractrix.svg.* Tractrix with object initially at $(4, 0)$

Suppose the object is placed at $(a, 0)$ and the puller at the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432), so that $a$ is the length of the pulling thread. (In the example shown to the right, the value of $a$ is 4.) Suppose the puller starts to move along the $y$ axis in the positive direction. At every moment, the thread will be [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to the curve described by the object, so that it becomes completely determined by the movement of the puller. Mathematically, if the coordinates of the object are $(x, y)$, then by the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034) the $y$-coordinate of the puller is $y + \sqrt{a^2 - x^2}$ . Writing that the slope of thread equals that of the tangent to the curve leads to the [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309)

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}{x}$$

with the initial condition $y(a) = 0$. Its solution is

$$y = \int_x^a \frac{\sqrt{a^2-t^2}}{t}\,dt = \! a\ln{\frac{a+\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}{x}}-\sqrt{a^2-x^2} .$$

If instead the puller moves downward from the origin, then the sign should be removed from the differential equation and therefore inserted into the solution. Each of the two solutions defines a branch of the tractrix; they meet at the [cusp](wikipedia://en/6851758) point $(a, 0)$.

The first term of this solution can also be written

$$a \operatorname{arsech}\frac{x}{a},$$

where $\operatorname{arsech}$ is the [inverse hyperbolic secant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_hyperbolic_secant) function.

## Basis of the tractrix {#Basis_of_the_tractrix}

The essential property of the tractrix is constancy of the distance between a point $P$ on the curve and the intersection of the [tangent line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_line) at $P$ with the [asymptote](wikipedia://en/3107) of the curve.

The tractrix might be regarded in a multitude of ways:

1. It is the [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of the center of a [hyperbolic spiral](wikipedia://en/183045) rolling (without skidding) on a straight line.
1. It is the [involute](wikipedia://en/840704) of the [catenary](wikipedia://en/7163) function, which describes a fully flexible, [inelastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastomer), homogeneous string attached to two points that is subjected to a [gravitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity) field. The catenary has the equation $y(x) = a \cosh\,\tfrac{x}{a}$.
1. The trajectory determined by the middle of the back axle of a car pulled by a rope at a constant speed and with a constant direction (initially perpendicular to the vehicle).
1. It is a (non-linear) curve which a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) of radius $a$ rolling on a straight line, with its center at the $x$ axis, intersects perpendicularly at all times.

The function admits a horizontal asymptote. The curve is symmetrical with respect to the $y$-axis. The curvature radius is $r = a \cot\,\tfrac{x}{y}$.

A great implication that the tractrix had was the study of its [surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549) about its asymptote: the [pseudosphere](wikipedia://en/60766). Studied by [Eugenio Beltrami](wikipedia://en/543760) in 1868,[^o5] as a surface of constant negative [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623), the pseudosphere is a local model of [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291). The idea was carried further by Kasner and Newman in their book *[Mathematics and the Imagination](wikipedia://en/31052235)*, where they show a toy train dragging a [pocket watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_watch) to generate the tractrix.[^o6]

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Evolute2.gif.* [Catenary](wikipedia://en/7163) as [evolute](wikipedia://en/842387) of a tractrix

- The curve can be parameterised by the equation $x = t - \tanh(t), y= 1/{\cosh(t)}$.
- Due to the geometrical way it was defined, the tractrix has the property that the segment of its [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482), between the asymptote and the point of tangency, has constant length $a$.
- The [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of one branch between $x = x_{1}$ and $x = x_{2}$ is $a\,\ln\,\tfrac{y_{1}}{y_{2}}$.
- The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) between the tractrix and its asymptote is $\tfrac{\pi\,a^{2}}{2}$, which can be found using [integration](wikipedia://en/15532) or [Mamikon's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamikon's_theorem).
- The [envelope](wikipedia://en/880235) of the [normals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_normal) of the tractrix (that is, the [evolute](wikipedia://en/842387) of the tractrix) is the [catenary](wikipedia://en/7163) (or *chain curve*) given by $y = a \cosh\,\tfrac{x}{a}$.
- The surface of revolution created by revolving a tractrix about its asymptote is a [pseudosphere](wikipedia://en/60766).
- The tractrix is a [transcendental curve](wikipedia://en/519554); it cannot be defined by a polynomial equation.

:::

## Practical application {#Practical_application}

In 1927, P. G. A. H. Voigt patented a [horn loudspeaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_loudspeaker) design based on the assumption that a wave front traveling through the horn is spherical of a constant radius. The idea is to minimize [distortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distortion#Audio_distortion) caused by internal reflection of sound within the horn. The resulting shape is the surface of revolution of a tractrix.[^o11] Voigt's design removed the annoying "honk" characteristic from previous horn designs, especially conical horns, and thus revitalized interest in the horn loudspeaker.[^o12] [Klipsch Audio Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klipsch_Audio_Technologies) has used the tractrix design for the great majority of their loudspeakers, and many loudspeaker designers returned to the tractrix in the 21st century, creating an [audiophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audiophile) market segment. The tractrix horn differs from the more common exponential horn in that it provides for a wider spread of high frequency energy, and it supports the lower frequencies more strongly.[^o13]

An important application is in the forming technology for [sheet metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheet_metal). In particular a tractrix profile is used for the corner of the die on which the sheet metal is bent during deep drawing.[^o14]

A [toothed belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toothed_belt)-pulley design provides improved efficiency for mechanical power transmission using a tractrix catenary shape for its teeth.[^o15] This shape minimizes the friction of the belt teeth engaging the pulley, because the moving teeth engage and disengage with minimal sliding contact. Original timing belt designs used simpler [trapezoidal](wikipedia://en/201826) or circular tooth shapes, which cause significant sliding and friction.

## Drawing machines {#Drawing_machines}

- In October–November 1692, Christiaan Huygens described three tractrix-drawing machines.[^o20]
- In 1693 [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281) devised a "universal tractional machine" which, in theory, could integrate any [first order differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753#Definitions).[^o17] The concept was an analog computing mechanism implementing the tractional principle. The device was impractical to build with the technology of Leibniz's time, and was never realized.
- In 1706 [John Perks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Perks) built a tractional machine in order to realise the [hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_function) quadrature.[^o18]
- In 1729 [Giovanni Poleni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Poleni) built a tractional device that enabled [logarithmic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_function) to be drawn.[^o19]

A history of all these machines can be seen in an article by [H. J. M. Bos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._J._M._Bos).[^o20]
