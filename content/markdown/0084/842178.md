# Shafi Goldwasser

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shafi Goldwasser"}

**Shafrira Goldwasser** (שפרירה גולדווסר; born November 14, 1959[^o7][^o8]) is an Israeli-American computer scientist. She is the RSA Professor of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology),[^o9] a professor of mathematical sciences at the [Weizmann Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute_of_Science), the former director of the [Simons Institute for the Theory of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Institute_for_the_Theory_of_Computing), and co-founder and chief scientist of Duality Technologies.[^o10][^o11][^o14] In 2012, she and [Silvio Micali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Micali) won the ACM [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award).

:::

## Education and early life {#Education_and_early_life}

Goldwasser was born in New York City and grew up in Tel Aviv.[^o8] She later returned to the US and obtained her bachelor's degree in 1979 in mathematics and science from [Carnegie Mellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University). She received a master's degree in 1981 and PhD in 1984 from [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) advised by [Manuel Blum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Blum).[^o3]

While at Berkeley, Goldwasser worked on cryptography and algorithmic number theory.[^o16] She and Blum proposed the [Blum-Goldwasser cryptosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blum-Goldwasser_cryptosystem).[^o3] Along with [Silvio Micali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Micali), also a student at Berkeley at the time, she introduced the notion of [probabilistic encryption](wikipedia://en/1051442) where one message can be encrypted probabilistically to many different ciphertexts, which is more resistant to [chosen-plaintext attacks](wikipedia://en/63852).[^o16]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Goldwasser joined [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1983, and in 1997 became the first holder of the RSA Professorship. She became a professor at the [Weizmann Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute_of_Science), concurrent to her professorship at MIT, in 1993. She is a member of the [theory of computation](wikipedia://en/30402) group at [MIT Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Computer_Science_and_Artificial_Intelligence_Laboratory).[^o18]

In November 2016, along with several colleagues including [Vinod Vaikuntanathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinod_Vaikuntanathan), Goldwasser co-founded Duality Technologies in order to commercialize [fully homomorphic encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fully_homomorphic_encryption).[^o19] She is also a scientific advisor for several technology startups, including QED-it, specializing in the Zero Knowledge Blockchain, and [Algorand](wikipedia://en/67291815), a pure proof-of-stake [blockchain](wikipedia://en/44065971) founded by her collaborator [Silvio Micali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Micali).[^o20]

On January 1, 2018, she became the director of [Simons Institute for the Theory of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Institute_for_the_Theory_of_Computing), a position she held until August 2024.[^o21][^o22]

### Research {#Research}

Goldwasser's research areas include [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) and [computational number theory](wikipedia://en/511466). In 1984, along with Silvio Micali, she introduced [probabilistic encryption](wikipedia://en/1051442), which has become the basis for most public-key cryptographic schemes.[^o24][^o16]

In 1985, Goldwasser, Silvio Micali, and [Charles Rackoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Rackoff) introduced [zero-knowledge proofs](wikipedia://en/450541), which became a fundamental cryptographic primitive used to probabilistically and interactively demonstrate the validity of an assertion without conveying any additional knowledge.[^o26] They began by studying [interactive proofs](wikipedia://en/160255) more broadly, where a proof is developed interactively by answering a series of questions about a problem.[^o16] In the late 1980s, Micali's group and the duo of [László Babai](wikipedia://en/3461604) and [Shlomo Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo_Moran) separately published papers introducing the concept of interactive proofs: they all later shared a Gödel prize for their contributions.[^o25]

In complexity theory, she has worked on [hardness of approximation](wikipedia://en/20677277) and its connections to interactive proofs and the [PCP theorem](wikipedia://en/3001241).[^o26] She has also developed protocols for delegating computations to untrusted servers.[^o27] With Joe Kilian, she developed a [primality test](wikipedia://en/183751) using elliptic curves.[^o28] Goldwasser is also a lead on [Project CETI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_CETI), an interdisciplinary initiative for translating the communication of sperm whales.[^o29]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Goldwasser was awarded the 2012 [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award) along with [Silvio Micali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Micali) for having "pioneered the field of provable security, which laid the mathematical foundations that made modern cryptography possible."[^o31][^o32]

Goldwasser has twice won the [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize) in [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392): first in 1993 along with [László Babai](wikipedia://en/3461604), Silvio Micali, [Shlomo Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo_Moran), and [Charles Rackoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Rackoff) (for *"The knowledge complexity of interactive proof systems"*),[^o33] and again in 2001 along with [Sanjeev Arora](wikipedia://en/21513331), [Uriel Feige](wikipedia://en/24734804), [Carsten Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carsten_Lund), [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938), [Rajeev Motwani](wikipedia://en/16989288), [Shmuel Safra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shmuel_Safra), [Madhu Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhu_Sudan), and [Mario Szegedy](wikipedia://en/4291061) (for *Interactive Proofs and the Hardness of Approximating Cliques*).[^o34] She also received the [ACM Grace Murray Hopper Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Grace_Murray_Hopper_Award) in 1996 and the [RSA Award for Excellence in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_Award_for_Excellence_in_Mathematics) in 1998.[^o35]

In 2001 she was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and in 2002 she gave a plenary lecture at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Beijing.[^o36] In 2004 she was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o39] and in 2005 to the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) for contributions to cryptography, number theory, and complexity theory, and their applications to privacy and security.[^o37] In 2006, [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) awarded her its Computer Science Distinguished Alumni Award.[^o38] She was selected as an [IACR](wikipedia://en/87043) Fellow in 2007. Goldwasser received the 2008–2009 Athena Lecturer Award of the Association for Computing Machinery's Committee on Women in Computing.[^o39] She is the recipient of [The Franklin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Franklin_Institute)'s [2010 Benjamin Franklin Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Franklin_Institute_Awards) in Computer and Cognitive Science.[^o40] She received the [IEEE Emanuel R. Piore Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Emanuel_R._Piore_Award) in 2011.[^o41]

Goldwasser was elected as an [ACM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Fellow) in 2017.[^o42] In July 2017, she was a plenary lecturer in the Mathematical Congress of the Americas.[^o43] She received the 2018 Frontier of Knowledge award together with Micali, Rivest and Shamir.[^o44]

In 2018, Goldwasser was awarded an honorary degree by her alma mater, [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University).[^o45] In June 2019 Goldwasser was awarded an honorary doctorate of science by the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o46] She was elected as a fellow of the UK's [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 2023.[^o47]

Goldwasser is featured in the Notable Women in Computing cards.[^o48] She won the [Suffrage Science award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffrage_Science_award) in 2016.[^o6] She was on the Mathematical Sciences jury for the [Infosys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys_Prize) in 2020.[^o49] She was awarded the 2021 [L'Oréal-UNESCO for Women in Science Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Oréal-UNESCO_For_Women_in_Science_Awards) in Computer Science.[^o50]
