# Joseph Moxon

*Image: Mechanick Exercises by Joseph Moxon 1694.jpg.* Frontispiece and title page of Joseph Moxon's *Mechanick Exercises*, 1694.

*Image: Moxon A Map of the Earth 1681 Cornell CUL PJM 1012 01.jpg.* Moxon's Map with a view of the world as known in 1681. The seven days of creation are illustrated in the panels at the top of the map.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph Moxon"}

**Joseph Moxon** (8 August 1627 – February 1691),[^o3] [hydrographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrographer) to [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England), was an [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) printer specialising in mathematical books and maps, a maker of globes and [mathematical instruments](wikipedia://en/26722913), and mathematical [lexicographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexicographer). He produced the first English-language dictionary devoted to mathematics, the first detailed instructional manual for printers, and the first English-language how-to books for tradesmen. In November 1678, he became the first tradesman to be elected as a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Joseph Moxon was born on 8 August 1627 in [Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield), [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire). Around 1638, at an age between 9 and 11, he accompanied his father, [James Moxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Moxon), to [Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft) and [Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam) where James was printing English Bibles. It was at this time that Moxon learned the basics of printing.

## Printer {#Printer}

After the [First English Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_English_Civil_War) the family returned to [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) and Moxon and his older brother, James, started a printing business which specialized in the publication of [Puritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puritan) texts, with the notable exception of *A Book of Drawing, Limning, Washing or Colouring of Mapps and Prints* of 1647 which was produced for [Thomas Jenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jenner_%28publisher%29), a seller of maps.

In 1652, Moxon visited [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam) and commissioned the engraving of [globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe)-printing plates, and by the end of the year was selling large celestial and terrestrial globes in a new business venture. He specialised in the printing of maps and charts, and in the production of globes, and mathematical instruments made of paper.

Moxon's *Mechanick exercises* was published in parts between 1677 and 1684. It was completed in two volumes: the first giving instructions on metalworking, woodworking, brick-laying and sundial-construction; and the second (issued 1683–1684) providing a detailed series of instructions for printers, including [typefounding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typefounding), composition, press-work etc., which have given printing historians much (probably idealised) information on the working practices of hand-press period printing-houses.[^o6]

## Hydrographer {#Hydrographer}

In January 1662, he was appointed hydrographer to the King, despite his Puritan background. His shop at this time was on [Ludgate Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludgate_Hill); afterwards, in 1683, it was "on the west side of [Fleet Ditch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Ditch)", but always "at the sign of [Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_%28mythology%29)".[^n1]

Moxon theorized that the [Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean) was ice free, and warmed by twenty-four hours of sunlight in the summer. He also speculated that Arctic ice was created near land, and that if one sailed far enough northwards, one would be free of northern land masses and, subsequently, ice.

These views led him to believe that the [Northwest Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Passage) would be found by sailing near the [North Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pole). These views later influenced [Daines Barrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daines_Barrington) and [Samuel Engel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Engel), whose refinement of Moxon's ideas would influence [Captain Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Cook)'s Third Voyage in search of the Northwest Passage.

## Death {#Death}

When Moxon died in 1691 his estate and business was carried on by his son, mapmaker, engraver and instrument-maker James Moxon.[^o10]
