# Exterior covariant derivative

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exterior covariant derivative"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **exterior covariant derivative** is an extension of the notion of [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) to the setting of a differentiable [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) or [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) with a [connection](wikipedia://en/470632).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *G* be a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) and *P* → *M* be a [principal *G*-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) on a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) *M*. Suppose there is a [connection](wikipedia://en/470632) on *P*; this yields a natural direct sum decomposition $T_u P = H_u \oplus V_u$ of each tangent space into the [horizontal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_subspace) and [vertical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_subspace) subspaces. Let $h: T_u P \to H_u$ be the projection to the horizontal subspace.

If *ϕ* is a [*k*-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector-valued_form) on *P* with values in a vector space *V*, then its exterior covariant derivative *Dϕ* is a form defined by

$$D\phi(v_0, v_1,\dots, v_k)= d \phi(h v_0 ,h v_1,\dots, h v_k)$$

where *v*<sub>*i*</sub> are tangent vectors to *P* at *u*.

Suppose that *ρ* : *G* → GL(*V*) is a [representation](wikipedia://en/292786) of *G* on a vector space *V*. If *ϕ* is [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) in the sense that

$$R_g^* \phi = \rho(g)^{-1}\phi$$

where $R_g(u) = ug$, then *Dϕ* is a [tensorial (*k* + 1)-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensorial_form) on *P* of the type *ρ*: it is equivariant and horizontal (a form *ψ* is horizontal if *ψ*(*v*<sub>0</sub>, ..., *v*<sub>k</sub>) = *ψ*(*hv*<sub>0</sub>, ..., *hv*<sub>*k*</sub>).)

By [abuse of notation](wikipedia://en/1698066), the differential of *ρ* at the identity element may again be denoted by *ρ*:

$$\rho: \mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{gl}(V).$$

Let $\omega$ be the [connection one-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connection_one-form) and $\rho(\omega)$ the representation of the connection in $\mathfrak{gl}(V).$ That is, $\rho(\omega)$ is a [$\mathfrak{gl}(V)$-valued form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebra-valued_differential_form#operations), vanishing on the horizontal subspace. If *ϕ* is a tensorial *k*-form of type *ρ*, then

$D \phi = d \phi + \rho(\omega) \cdot \phi,$[^o4]

where, following the notation in *Lie algebra-valued differential form#Operations*, we wrote

$$(\rho(\omega) \cdot \phi)(v_1, \dots, v_{k+1}) = {1 \over (1+k)!} \sum_{\sigma} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\rho(\omega(v_{\sigma(1)})) \phi(v_{\sigma(2)}, \dots, v_{\sigma(k+1)}).$$

Unlike the usual [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782), which squares to 0, the exterior covariant derivative does not. In general, one has, for a tensorial zero-form *ϕ*,

$D^2\phi=F \cdot \phi.$[^o5]

where *F* = *ρ*(Ω) is the representation in $\mathfrak{gl}(V)$ of the [curvature two-form](wikipedia://en/489440) Ω. The form F is sometimes referred to as the [field strength tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_strength_tensor), in analogy to the role it plays in [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism). Note that *D*<sup>2</sup> vanishes for a [flat connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_connection) (i.e. when Ω = 0).

If *ρ* : *G* → GL(***R***<sup>*n*</sup>), then one can write

$$\rho(\Omega) = F = \sum {F^i}_j {e^j}_i$$

where ${e^i}_j$ is the matrix with 1 at the (*i*, *j*)-th entry and zero on the other entries. The matrix ${F^i}_j$ whose entries are 2-forms on *P* is called the **curvature matrix**.

:::

## For vector bundles {#For_vector_bundles}

Given a smooth real vector bundle $E \to M$ with a [connection](wikipedia://en/391816) $\nabla$ and rank $r$, the **exterior covariant derivative** is a real-linear map on the [vector-valued differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector-valued_differential_forms) that are valued in $E$:

$$d^\nabla:\Omega^k(M,E)\to\Omega^{k+1}(M,E).$$

The covariant derivative is such a map for $k = 0$. The exterior covariant derivatives extends this map to general $k$. There are several equivalent ways to define this object:

- Suppose that a vector-valued differential 2-form is regarded as assigning to each $p$ a multilinear map $s_{p}: T_{p}M \times T_{p}M \to E_{p}$ which is completely anti-symmetric. Then the exterior covariant derivative $d^{\nabla} s$ assigns to each $p$ a multilinear map $T_{p}M \times T_{p}M \times T_{p}M \to E_{p}$ given by the formula

$$\begin{aligned}\nabla_{x_1}(s(X_2,X_3))&-\nabla_{x_2}(s(X_1,X_3))+\nabla_{x_3}(s(X_1,X_2))\\ &-s([X_1,X_2],x_3)+s([X_1,X_3],x_2)-s([X_2,X_3],x_1).\end{aligned}$$

where $x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}$ are arbitrary tangent vectors at $p$ which are extended to smooth locally-defined vector fields $X_{1}, X_{2} X_{3}$. The legitimacy of this definition depends on the fact that the above expression depends only on $x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}$, and not on the choice of extension. This can be verified by the Leibniz rule for covariant differentiation and for the [Lie bracket of vector fields](wikipedia://en/10282799). The pattern established in the above formula in the case $k = 2$ can be directly extended to define the exterior covariant derivative for arbitrary $k$.

- The exterior covariant derivative may be characterized by the axiomatic property of defining for each $k$ a real-linear map $\Omega^{k}(M, E) \to \Omega^{k + 1}(M, E)$ which for $k = 0$ is the covariant derivative and in general satisfies the Leibniz rule

$$d^\nabla(\omega \wedge s) = (d\omega) \wedge s + (-1)^k \omega \wedge (d^\nabla s)$$

for any differential $k$-form $\omega$ and any vector-valued form $s$. This may also be viewed as a direct inductive definition. For instance, for any vector-valued differential 1-form $s$ and any local frame $e_{1}, ..., e_{r}$ of the vector bundle, the coordinates of $s$ are locally-defined differential 1-forms $\omega^{1}, ..., \omega^{r}$. The above inductive formula then says that

$$\begin{aligned} d^\nabla s&=d^\nabla(\omega^1 e_1+\cdots+\omega^r e_r)\\ &=(d\omega^1) e_1+\cdots+(d\omega^r) e_r-\omega^1 \nabla e_1-\cdots-\omega^r\nabla e_r.\end{aligned}$$

In order for this to be a legitimate definition of $d^{\nabla}s$, it must be verified that the choice of local frame is irrelevant. This can be checked by considering a second local frame obtained by an arbitrary change-of-basis matrix; the [inverse matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_matrix) provides the change-of-basis matrix for the 1-forms $\omega_{1}, ..., \omega_{r}$. When substituted into the above formula, the Leibniz rule as applied for the standard exterior derivative and for the covariant derivative $\nabla$ cancel out the arbitrary choice.

- A vector-valued differential 2-form $s$ may be regarded as a certain collection of functions $s^{\alpha}_{ij}$ assigned to an arbitrary local frame of $E$ over a local coordinate chart of $M$. The exterior covariant derivative is then defined as being given by the functions

$$(d^\nabla s)^\alpha{}_{ijk}=\nabla_is^\alpha{}_{jk}-\nabla_js^\alpha{}_{ik}+\nabla_ks^\alpha{}_{ij}.$$

The fact that this defines a tensor field valued in $E$ is a direct consequence of the same fact for the covariant derivative. The further fact that it is a differential 3-form valued in $E$ asserts the full anti-symmetry in $i, j, k$ and is directly verified from the above formula and the contextual assumption that $s$ is a vector-valued differential 2-form, so that $s^{\alpha}_{ij} = -s^{\alpha}_{ji}$. The pattern in this definition of the exterior covariant derivative for $k = 2$ can be directly extended to larger values of $k$. This definition may alternatively be expressed in terms of an arbitrary local frame of $E$ but without considering coordinates on $M$. Then a vector-valued differential 2-form is expressed by differential 2-forms $s^{1}, ..., s^{r}$ and the connection is expressed by the connection 1-forms, a skew-symmetric $r \times r$ matrix of differential 1-forms $\theta_{\alpha}^{\beta}$. The exterior covariant derivative of $s$, as a vector-valued differential 3-form, is expressed relative to the local frame by $r$ many differential 3-forms, defined by

$$(d^\nabla s)^\alpha=d(s^\alpha)+\theta_\beta{}^\alpha\wedge s^\beta.$$

In the case of the trivial real line bundle $\mathbb{R} \times M \to M$ with its standard connection, vector-valued differential forms and differential forms can be naturally identified with one another, and each of the above definitions coincides with the standard [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782).

Given a principal bundle, any [linear representation](wikipedia://en/292786) of the structure group defines an [associated bundle](wikipedia://en/398874), and any connection on the principal bundle induces a connection on the associated vector bundle. Differential forms valued in the vector bundle may be naturally identified with fully anti-symmetric [tensorial forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector-valued_differential_forms#Basic_or_tensorial_forms_on_principal_bundles) on the total space of the principal bundle. Under this identification, the notions of exterior covariant derivative for the principal bundle and for the vector bundle coincide with one another.

The [curvature](wikipedia://en/489440) of a connection on a vector bundle may be defined as the composition of the two exterior covariant derivatives $\Omega^{0}(M, E) \to \Omega^{1}(M, E)$ and $\Omega^{1}(M, E) \to \Omega^{2}(M, E)$, so that it is defined as a real-linear map $F: \Omega^{0}(M, E) \to \Omega^{2}(M, E)$. It is a fundamental but not immediately apparent fact that $F(s)_{p}: T_{p}M \times T_{p}M \to E_{p}$ only depends on $s(p)$, and does so linearly. As such, the curvature may be regarded as an element of $\Omega^{2}(M, \operatorname{End}(E))$. Depending on how the exterior covariant derivative is formulated, various alternative but equivalent definitions of curvature (some without the language of exterior differentiation) can be obtained.

It is a well-known fact that the composition of the standard exterior derivative with itself is zero: $d(d\omega) = 0$. In the present context, this can be regarded as saying that the standard connection on the trivial line bundle $\mathbb{R} \times M \to M$ has zero curvature.

Some equations involving covariant derivative can be locally solved using Chen's iterated integrals[^o7] or using approach based on linear homotopy operator.[^o8]

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

- [Bianchi's second identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bianchi's_second_identity), which says that the exterior covariant derivative of Ω is zero (that is, *D*Ω = 0) can be stated as: $d\Omega + \operatorname{ad}(\omega) \cdot \Omega = d\Omega + [\omega \wedge \Omega] = 0$.

:::
