# John Fauvel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Fauvel"}

**John Fauvel** (21 July 1947 – 12 May 2001) was a British mathematician and historian of mathematics.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Fauvel was from [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland), where his father was a [principal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_%28education%29). [Ian Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Blair) was a cousin.

John attended [Trinity College, Glenalmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Glenalmond). He then studied mathematics at the [University of Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Essex), where he graduated in 1970, and at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick), where he obtained his master's degree in 1973. In 1977, he obtained his [MPhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Philosophy) from the University of Warwick under the supervision of David Orme Tall, with a thesis entitled *Fuzzy Theory*.

## Career {#Career}

From 1974, Fauvel worked at the [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University). From 1991 to 1994, he was President of the [British Society for the History of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/7795793) and he also edited its newsletter. From 1992 to 1996, he directed an international study group on the relations between history and mathematics pedagogy, which is affiliated with the [International Commission for Mathematical Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Commission_for_Mathematical_Education) (ICMI), and in 2000 he co-directed a major study of ICMI. In 1998, he was invited lecturer by the [New Zealand Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Mathematical_Society).[^o3] Fauvel was a visiting scholar in the Mathematics Department at [Colorado College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_College) on several occasions, including one on a [Fulbright Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Fellowship) during the Winter and Spring of 1999.

Fauvel died on 12 May 2001 of complications from liver disease.[^o3]
