# Fay's trisecant identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fay's trisecant identity"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **Fay's trisecant identity** is an identity between [theta functions](wikipedia://en/449745) of [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) introduced by John Fay.[^n1] Fay's identity holds for theta functions of Jacobians of curves, but not for theta functions of general [abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152).

:::

The name "trisecant identity" refers to the geometric interpretation given by [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594),[^n2] who used it to show that the [Kummer variety](wikipedia://en/23431597) of a genus $g$ [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181), given by the image of the map from the Jacobian to [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) of dimension $2^g-1$ induced by theta functions of order 2, has a 4-dimensional space of trisecants.

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose that

- $C$ is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) Riemann surface,
- $g$ is the [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) of $C$,
- $\theta:\mathbb{C}^g\to \mathbb{C}$ is the Riemann theta function of $C$,
- $E$ is a [prime form](wikipedia://en/7404483) on $C\times C$,
- $u$, $v$, $x$, $y$ are points of $C$,
- $z$ is an element of $\mathbb{C}^g$, and
- $\omega$ is a 1-form on $C$ with values in $\mathbb{C}^g$.

Then Fay's identity states that

$$\begin{aligned} E(x,v)E(u,y) \theta \bigg(z+\int_u^x\omega\bigg) \theta\bigg(z+\int_v^y\omega\bigg) &- E(x,u)E(v,y) \theta \bigg(z+\int_v^x\omega\bigg)\theta \bigg(z+\int_u^y\omega\bigg)\\[5pt] &= E(x,y)E(u,v)\theta(z)\theta\bigg(z+\int_{u+v}^{x+y}\omega\bigg) \end{aligned}$$

with

$$\int_{u+v}^{x+y}\omega=\int_u^x\omega+\int_v^y\omega=\int_u^y\omega+\int_v^x\omega.$$

:::
