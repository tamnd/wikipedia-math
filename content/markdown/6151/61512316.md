# Bailout embedding

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bailout embedding"}

In the theory of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), a **bailout embedding** is a system defined as[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

$$\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dt} (u-f(x)) = -k(x) (u-f(x)), \\[8pt] &\frac{dx}{dt} = u. \end{aligned}$$

Here the function *k*(*x*) \< 0 on a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of unwanted [orbits](wikipedia://en/252250); otherwise *k*(*x*) > 0. The [trajectories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajectory) of the full system of a bailout embedding *bail out*—that is, detach—from the [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119), into a larger space, in which they move around. If, after some time these orbits arrive at a [stable](wikipedia://en/5493795) neighbourhood of the embedding, *k*(*x*) > 0, they collapse once more onto the embedding; that is, onto the original [dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamics_%28mechanics%29). The bailout embedding forms in this way an enlarged version of the [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087), one in which particular sets of orbits are cut from the asymptotic or [limit set](wikipedia://en/5492198), while maintaining the dynamics of a different set of orbits—the wanted set—as [attractors](wikipedia://en/191094) of the larger dynamical system. With a choice of *k*(*x*) = −(*γ* + ∇*f*), these dynamics are seen to detach from unstable regions such as [saddle points](wikipedia://en/640249) in [conservative systems](wikipedia://en/8918323).

One important application of the bailout embedding concept is to [divergence-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergence-free) [flows](wikipedia://en/2619735); the most important class of these are [Hamiltonian systems](wikipedia://en/1197531).
