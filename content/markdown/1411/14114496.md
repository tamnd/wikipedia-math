# Metric space aimed at its subspace

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Metric space aimed at its subspace"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **metric space aimed at its subspace** is a [categorical](wikipedia://en/5869) construction that has a direct geometric meaning. It is also a useful step toward the construction of the *metric envelope*, or [tight span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tight_span), which are basic (injective) objects of the category of [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018).

:::

Following (Holsztyński 1966), a notion of a metric space *Y* aimed at its subspace *X* is defined.

## Informal introduction {#Informal_introduction}

Informally, imagine terrain *Y*, and its part *X*, such that wherever in *Y* you place a sharpshooter, and an apple at another place in *Y*, and then let the sharpshooter fire, the bullet will go through the apple and will always hit a point of *X*, or at least it will fly arbitrarily close to points of *X* – then we say that *Y* is aimed at *X*.

A priori, it may seem plausible that for a given *X* the superspaces *Y* that aim at *X* can be arbitrarily large or at least huge. We will see that this is not the case. Among the spaces which aim at a subspace isometric to *X*, there is a unique ([up to](wikipedia://en/44787) [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777)) [universal](wikipedia://en/32245) one, Aim(*X*), which in a sense of canonical [isometric embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometric_embedding) contains any other space aimed at (an isometric image of) *X*. And in the special case of an arbitrary [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) metric space *X* every bounded subspace of an arbitrary metric space *Y* aimed at *X* is [totally bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_bounded) (i.e. its [metric completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_completion) is compact).

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let $(Y, d)$ be a metric space. Let $X$ be a subset of $Y$, so that $(X,d |_X)$ (the set $X$ with the metric from $Y$ restricted to $X$) is a metric subspace of $(Y,d)$. Then

::: {#o6 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition"}

**Definition**. Space $Y$ aims at $X$ if and only if, for all points $y, z$ of $Y$, and for every real $\epsilon > 0$, there exists a point $p$ of $X$ such that

$$|d(p,y) - d(p,z)| > d(y,z) - \epsilon.$$

Let $\text{Met}(X)$ be the space of all real valued [metric maps](wikipedia://en/469760) (non-[contractive](wikipedia://en/6239)) of $X$. Define

$$\text{Aim}(X) := \{f \in \operatorname{Met}(X) : f(p) + f(q) \ge d(p,q) \text{ for all } p,q\in X\}.$$

Then

$$d(f,g) := \sup_{x\in X} |f(x)-g(x)| < \infty$$

for every $f, g\in \text{Aim}(X)$ is a metric on $\text{Aim}(X)$. Furthermore, $\delta_X\colon x\mapsto d_x$, where $d_x(p) := d(x,p)\,$, is an isometric embedding of $X$ into $\operatorname{Aim}(X)$; this is essentially a generalisation of the [Kuratowski-Wojdysławski embedding](wikipedia://en/21014764) of bounded metric spaces $X$ into $C(X)$, where we here consider arbitrary metric spaces (bounded or unbounded). It is clear that the space $\operatorname{Aim}(X)$ is aimed at $\delta_X(X)$.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Let $i\colon X \to Y$ be an isometric embedding. Then there exists a natural metric map $j\colon Y \to \operatorname{Aim}(X)$ such that $j \circ i = \delta_X$:

$$(j(y))(x) := d(x,y)\,$$

for every $x\in X\,$ and $y\in Y\,$.

**Theorem** The space *Y* above is aimed at subspace *X* if and only if the natural mapping $j\colon Y \to \operatorname{Aim}(X)$ is an isometric embedding.

Thus it follows that every space aimed at *X* can be isometrically mapped into Aim(*X*), with some additional (essential) categorical requirements satisfied.

The space Aim(*X*) is [injective](wikipedia://en/7840768) (hyperconvex in the sense of [Aronszajn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aronszajn)-Panitchpakdi) – given a metric space *M,* which contains Aim(*X*) as a metric subspace, there is a canonical (and explicit) metric retraction of *M* onto Aim(*X*) (Holsztyński 1966).

:::
