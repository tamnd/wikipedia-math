# L. Gustave du Pasquier

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="L. Gustave du Pasquier"}

**Louis-Gustave du Pasquier** (18 August 1876, [Auvernier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auvernier) – 31 January 1957, [Cornaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornaux)) was a Swiss mathematician and historian of mathematics and mathematical sciences.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Du Pasquier studied at l'[École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique), the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich), [La Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France), and the Collège Libre des Sciences Sociales. He received his doctorate in 1906 from the University of Zurich with dissertation *Zahlentheorie der Tettarionen* under the supervision of [Adolf Hurwitz](wikipedia://en/771690).[^o4] Du Pasquier then taught at La Chaux-de-Fonds, Kusnacht, Frauenfeld, Winterthur, and Zurich, before he became a professor at the [University of Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Neuchâtel) in 1911. Du Pasquier wrote more than 60 articles published in scientific journals. He did research on number theory, probability theory, relativity theory, astronomy, and actuarial science.[^o5][^o6] He edited the 7th volume of the collected works of [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902).

Du Pasquier was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1920 at Strasbourg, in 1924 at Toronto,[^o7] in 1928 at Bologna, and in 1932 at Zurich.
