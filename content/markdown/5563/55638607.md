# David Bevan (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Bevan"}

**David Bevan** is an English mathematician, computer scientist and software developer. He is known for Bevan's theorem, which gives the asymptotic enumeration of grid classes of permutations[^o4][^o5] and for his work on [enumerating the class of permutations](wikipedia://en/25446917) avoiding the pattern 1324.[^o5][^o6] He is also known for devising [weighted reference counting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_counting#Weighted_reference_counting), an approach to computer [memory management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_management) that is suitable for use in [distributed systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_systems).[^o7][^o8]

:::

## Work and research {#Work_and_research}

Bevan was a lecturer in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) in the department of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at the [University of Strathclyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strathclyde).[^o10][^o11][^o12] He has degrees in mathematics and computer science from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) and a degree in theology from the [London School of Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Theology).[^o13] He received his PhD in mathematics from [The Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Open_University) in 2015; his thesis, *On the growth of permutation classes*, was supervised by Robert Brignall.[^o3]

In 1987, as a research scientist at [GEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_Company)'s [Hirst Research Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirst_Research_Centre) in Wembley, he developed an approach to computer [memory management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_management), called [weighted reference counting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_counting#Weighted_reference_counting), that is suitable for use in [distributed systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_systems).[^o7][^o8] During the 1990s, while working for the [Summer Institute of Linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Institute_of_Linguistics) in [Papua New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea), he developed a computer program, called *FindPhone*, that was widely used by field linguists to analyse phonetic data in order to understand the [phonology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonology) of [minority languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_languages).[^o14][^o15][^o16] While employed by [Pitney Bowes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitney_Bowes), he was a major contributor to the development of the [FreeType](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FreeType) text rendering library.[^o17]

Bevan's mathematical research has concerned areas of [enumerative combinatorics](wikipedia://en/3925533), particularly in relation to [permutation classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_patterns#Closed_classes).[^o5] He established that the growth rate of a monotone grid class of permutations is equal to the square of the [spectral radius](wikipedia://en/386169) of a related [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431).[^o4][^o5] He has also determined bounds on the growth rate of the class of permutations avoiding the pattern 1324.[^o5][^o6] In the Acknowledgements sections of his journal articles, he often includes the Latin phrase [Soli Deo gloria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soli_Deo_gloria).[^o18][^o19][^o20]
