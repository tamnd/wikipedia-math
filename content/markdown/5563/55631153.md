# Tensor representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tensor representation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **tensor representations** of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) are those that are obtained by taking finitely many [tensor products](wikipedia://en/30844) of the [fundamental representation](wikipedia://en/649743) and its dual. The irreducible factors of such a representation are also called tensor representations, and can be obtained by applying [Schur functors](wikipedia://en/11597641) (associated to [Young tableaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_tableaux)). These coincide with the [rational representations](wikipedia://en/5784135) of the general linear group.

:::

More generally, a [matrix group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_group) is any subgroup of the general linear group. A tensor representation of a matrix group is any representation that is contained in a tensor representation of the general linear group. For example, the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) O(*n*) admits a tensor representation on the space of all trace-free symmetric tensors of order two. For orthogonal groups, the tensor representations are contrasted with the [spin representations](wikipedia://en/10160091).
