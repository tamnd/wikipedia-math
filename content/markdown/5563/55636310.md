# Elizabeth H. Slate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elizabeth H. Slate"}

**Elizabeth H. Slate** is an American statistician, interested in the [Bayesian statistics](wikipedia://en/404412) of [longitudinal data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitudinal_data) and applications to health. She is the Duncan McLean and Pearl Levine Fairweather Professor of Statistics at [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University). Some of Slate's most heavily cited work concerns the effects of [selenium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selenium) on [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer). Slate's research has also included work on the early detection of [osteoarthritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteoarthritis).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Slate majored in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) (CMU), graduating in 1986. After earning a master's degree at CMU in statistics in 1988, she completed her Ph.D. there in 1991, under the supervision of Robert E. Kass; her dissertation was *Reparameterization of Statistical Models*.

She joined the School of [Operations Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_Research) and [Industrial Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Engineering) at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1992, and moved to the department of [Biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878), [Bioinformatics](wikipedia://en/4214) and [Epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997) of the [Medical University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_University_of_South_Carolina) in 2000. In 2011 she moved again, to Florida State University, as Fairweather Professor.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Slate became a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 2007, and a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 2023.
