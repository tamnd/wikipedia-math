# Donna Brogan

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Donna Brogan"}

**Donna Jean Brogan** (born July 12, 1939) is an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University). Brogan has worked in biostatistical research in the areas of women's health, mental health and [psychosocial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosocial) health statistics, statistics on breast cancer, and analysis of complex survey data.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Brogan was born July 12, 1939, and grew up in a working-class neighborhood in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), Maryland, and she was the first in her family to go to college.[^o4][^o5] She earned a B.A. in mathematics from [Gettysburg College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_College) in 1960, an M.S. in statistics from [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) in 1962.[^o6] She earned a PhD in statistics from [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University) in 1967,[^o5] under the supervision of Joseph Sendransk.

## Work {#Work}

In 1971, Brogan founded the [Caucus for Women in Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucus_for_Women_in_Statistics), and helped to establish the standing ASA Committee on Women in Statistics.[^o8]

For four years, she was an assistant professor in the [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina) in the School of Public Health, with a specialization in sample survey design and analysis. Then in 1970, she joined the [Emory University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University_School_of_Medicine) as associate professor, later professor in the Department of Statistics and Biometry.[^o9] Between 1991 and 1994, she was the division director of biostatistics at Emory.[^o6] She retired in 2004 from Emory University.[^o9]

Since 1975, she has worked in freelance and active as a biostatistician, primarily in the specialty area of design and analysis of complex sample surveys.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

As a woman in mathematics in the 1950s and 1960s, she suffered from many incidents of sex discrimination, including issues with unequal compensation from the [University of North Carolina Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_Chapel_Hill) and [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University), as well as a legal battle with the [DeKalb County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeKalb_County,_Georgia) voter registrar, which involved the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union).[^o4]

She was married to Charles Ruhl, and had two children, although their son died in infancy.[^o4] Brogan and Ruhl later divorced, and Brogan lived as a single mother for several years.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

She is a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_American_Statistical_Association#1985) of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), received the Emory University Thomas Jefferson Award from Emory University in 1993,[^o12] and was awarded the Iowa State University Distinguished Alumni Award in 2002.[^o13] In 1995, Iowa State University engraved her name on its *Plaza of Heroines*, which honors outstanding women graduates and faculty.[^o5][^o6]

The "Donna J. Brogan Lecture in Biostatistics" was established in 2004 at Emory University, to honor Brogan's work at the school.[^o9]
