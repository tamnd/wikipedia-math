# Sullivan conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sullivan conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Sullivan conjecture** or **Sullivan's conjecture on maps from classifying spaces** can refer to any of several results and conjectures prompted by [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) work of [Dennis Sullivan](wikipedia://en/7349608). A basic theme and motivation concerns the [fixed point](wikipedia://en/449738) set in [group actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) $G$. The most elementary formulation, however, is in terms of the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) $BG$ of such a group. Roughly speaking, it is difficult to map such a space $BG$ continuously into a finite [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) $X$ in a non-trivial manner. Such a version of the Sullivan conjecture was first proved by [Haynes Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haynes_Miller).[^o2] Specifically, in 1984, Miller proved that the [function space](wikipedia://en/340630), carrying the [compact-open topology](wikipedia://en/726335), of [base point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_point)-preserving mappings from $BG$ to $X$ is [weakly contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_contractible).

:::

This is equivalent to the statement that the map $X$ → $F(BG, X)$ from X to the function space of maps $BG$ → $X$, not necessarily preserving the base point, given by sending a point $x$ of $X$ to the constant map whose image is $x$ is a [weak equivalence](wikipedia://en/34615176). The mapping space $F(BG, X)$ is an example of a homotopy fixed point set. Specifically, $F(BG, X)$ is the homotopy fixed point set of the group $G$ acting by the trivial action on $X$. In general, for a group $G$ acting on a space $X$, the homotopy fixed points are the fixed points $F(EG, X)^G$ of the mapping space $F(EG, X)$ of maps from the [universal cover](wikipedia://en/250323) $EG$ of $BG$ to $X$ under the $G$-action on $F(EG, X)$ given by $g$ in $G$ acts on a map $f$ in $F(EG, X)$ by sending it to $gfg^{-1}$. The $G$-equivariant map from $EG$ to a single point $*$ induces a natural map η: $X^G = F(*,X)^G$→$F(EG, X)^G$ from the fixed points to the homotopy fixed points of $G$ acting on $X$. Miller's theorem is that η is a weak equivalence for trivial $G$-actions on finite-dimensional CW complexes. An important ingredient and motivation for his proof is a result of [Gunnar Carlsson](wikipedia://en/35054518) on the [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) of $BZ/2$ as an unstable module over the [Steenrod algebra](wikipedia://en/2146220).[^o3]

Miller's theorem generalizes to a version of Sullivan's conjecture in which the action on $X$ is allowed to be non-trivial. In 1971,[^o4] Sullivan conjectured that η is a weak equivalence after a certain p-completion procedure due to A. Bousfield and [D. Kan](wikipedia://en/2290155) for the group $G=Z/2$. This conjecture was incorrect as stated, but a correct version was given by Miller, and proven independently by Dwyer-Miller-Neisendorfer,[^o5] Carlsson,[^o6] and [Jean Lannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Lannes_%28mathematician%29),[^o7] showing that the natural map $(X^G)_p$ → $F(EG, (X)_p)^G$ is a weak equivalence when the order of $G$ is a power of a prime p, and where $(X)_p$ denotes the Bousfield-Kan p-completion of $X$. Miller's proof involves an unstable [Adams spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/4720689), Carlsson's proof uses his affirmative solution of the [Segal conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segal_conjecture) and also provides information about the homotopy fixed points $F(EG, X)^G$ before completion, and Lannes's proof involves his T-functor.[^o8]
