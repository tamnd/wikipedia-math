# Bôcher's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bôcher's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Bôcher's theorem** is either of two theorems named after the American mathematician [Maxime Bôcher](wikipedia://en/3180285).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Bôcher's theorem in complex analysis"}

## Bôcher's theorem in complex analysis {#Bôcher's_theorem_in_complex_analysis}

In [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), the theorem states that the finite [zeros](wikipedia://en/264210) of the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) $r'(z)$ of a non-[constant](wikipedia://en/320861) [rational function](wikipedia://en/361210) $r(z)$ that are not multiple zeros are also the positions of equilibrium in the field of force due to particles of positive mass at the zeros of $r(z)$ and particles of [negative mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_mass) at the [poles](wikipedia://en/81560) of $r(z)$, with masses numerically equal to the respective multiplicities, where each particle repels with a force equal to the mass times the inverse distance.

Furthermore, if *C*<sub>1</sub> and *C*<sub>2</sub> are two disjoint circular regions which contain respectively all the zeros and all the poles of $r(z)$, then *C*<sub>1</sub> and *C*<sub>2</sub> also contain all the critical points of $r(z)$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Bôcher's theorem for harmonic functions"}

## Bôcher's theorem for harmonic functions {#Bôcher's_theorem_for_harmonic_functions}

In the theory of [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762), Bôcher's theorem states that a positive harmonic function in a punctured domain (an open domain minus one point in the interior) is a linear combination of a harmonic function in the unpunctured domain with a scaled [fundamental solution](wikipedia://en/1820858) for the [Laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian) in that domain.

:::
