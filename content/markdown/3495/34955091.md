# Conley–Zehnder theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conley–Zehnder theorem"}

In mathematics, the **Conley–Zehnder theorem**, named after [Charles C. Conley](wikipedia://en/37010888) and [Eduard Zehnder](wikipedia://en/36134745), provides a lower bound for the number of [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738) of [Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/1818270) of standard symplectic [tori](wikipedia://en/74800) in terms of the topology of the underlying tori. The lower bound is one plus the [cup-length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup-length) of the torus (thus 2n+1, where 2n is the dimension of the considered torus), and it can be strengthen to the rank of the homology of the torus (which is 2<sup>2n</sup>) provided all the fixed points are non-degenerate, this latter condition being generic in the C<sup>1</sup>-topology.

:::

The theorem was conjectured by [Vladimir Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490), and it was known as the [Arnold conjecture](wikipedia://en/72203783) on fixed points of [symplectomorphisms](wikipedia://en/312304). Its validity was later extended to more general closed symplectic manifolds by [Andreas Floer](wikipedia://en/2280157) and several others.
