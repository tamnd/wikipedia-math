# Michel Enock

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michel Enock"}

**Michel Enock-Levi** (1 June 1947 – 5 June 2025) was a French mathematician and a Directeur de recherche au CNRS,[^o3] credited with the early development of [Pontryagin-style dualities for non-commutative topological groups](wikipedia://en/366136#Dualities_for_non-commutative_topological_groups). Enock died in June 2025, at the age of 78.[^o4]

:::

## Scientific contributions {#Scientific_contributions}

Enock is known for the 1992 book co-authored with Jean-Marie Schwartz on the subject of Kac algebras,[^o6] (not to be confused with [Kac-Moody algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kac-Moody_algebras)). According to the French mathematician [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340),[^o7] the book develops the general theory to characterize [quantum groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_groups) among [Hopf algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_algebras), similar to the characterization of [Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_groups) among [locally compact groups](wikipedia://en/1651204), "with emphasis on the analytical aspects of the subject instead of the purely algebraic ones". Further, Connes writes: The theory of Kac algebras and their duality, \[was\] elaborated independently by M. Enock and J. -M. Schwartz, and by G. I. Kac and [L. I. Vainermann](wikipedia://en/74794076) in the seventies. The subject has now reached a state of maturity Specifically, Enock co-developed a general [Pontryagin duality](wikipedia://en/366136) theory for all [locally compact groups](wikipedia://en/1651204).

## Scientific career {#Scientific_career}

Enock completed his postgraduate thesis (1971) and state thesis (1976) at [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University). In 1971, he became an assistant professor at the University of Paris 13 ([Sorbonne Paris North University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_Paris_North_University)). From 1978, he was a Researcher at the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) (CNRS). He received accreditation to direct research at [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) in 1985. He supervised two doctorate students.[^o9]

Enock became a Director of Research at CNRS in 2000 and a First class Research Director in 2011. From 2012, he was emeritus Research Director at CNRS.[^o3]
