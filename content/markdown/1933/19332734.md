# Tammes problem

*Image: Tammes cropped.png.* 12 points in an optimal arrangement around a sphere, which also are the [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) of a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tammes problem"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Tammes problem** is a problem in [packing a given number of points](wikipedia://en/16879665) on the surface of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) such that the minimum distance between points is maximized. It is named after the Dutch botanist Pieter Merkus Lambertus Tammes (the nephew of pioneering botanist [Jantina Tammes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jantina_Tammes)) who posed the problem in his 1930 doctoral dissertation on the distribution of pores on [pollen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollen) grains.[^o2] If circles with diameter equal to the minimum distance are drawn around each point, they will not cross. Another equivalent way of phrasing the problem is to ask for the largest radius such that the given number of circles of that radius can be packed disjointly on the sphere.

:::

::: {#o3 .problem cue=unsolved}

What is the optimal packing of circles on the surface of a sphere for every possible amount of circles?

:::

It can be viewed as a particular special case of the [generalized Thomson problem](wikipedia://en/3104018#Generalizations) of minimizing the total [Coulomb energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulomb_force) of [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron) in a spherical arrangement.[^o4] Thus far, solutions have been [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) only for small numbers of circles: 3 through 14, and 24.[^o5] There are [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) solutions for many other cases, including those in higher dimensions.[^o6][^o7]
