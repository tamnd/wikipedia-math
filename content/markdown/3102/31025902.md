# Kobayashi metric

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kobayashi metric"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and especially [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), the **Kobayashi metric** is a [pseudometric](wikipedia://en/68503) intrinsically associated to any [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742). It was introduced by [Shoshichi Kobayashi](wikipedia://en/20695112) in 1967. **Kobayashi hyperbolic** manifolds are an important class of complex manifolds, defined by the property that the Kobayashi pseudometric is a metric. Kobayashi hyperbolicity of a complex manifold *X* implies that every [holomorphic map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_map) from the complex line **C** to *X* is constant.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The origins of the concept lie in [Schwarz's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarz's_lemma) in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759). Namely, if *f* is a [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110) on the [open unit disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_unit_disc) *D* in the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) **C** such that *f*(0) = 0 and |*f*(*z*)| \< 1 for all *z* in *D*, then the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) *f* '(0) has absolute value at most 1. More generally, for any holomorphic map *f* from *D* to itself (not necessarily sending 0 to 0), there is a more complicated upper bound for the derivative of *f* at any point of *D*. However, the bound has a simple formulation in terms of the [Poincaré metric](wikipedia://en/1487868), which is a [complete](wikipedia://en/144652#Geodesic_completeness) [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on *D* with [curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) −1 (isometric to the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane)). Namely: every holomorphic map from *D* to itself is distance-decreasing with respect to the Poincaré metric on *D*.

This is the beginning of a strong connection between complex analysis and the geometry of negative curvature. For any [complex space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_analytic_space) *X* (for example a complex manifold), the **Kobayashi pseudometric** *d*<sub>*X*</sub> is defined as the largest pseudometric on *X* such that

$$d_X(f(x),f(y)) \le \rho(x,y),$$

for all holomorphic maps *f* from the unit disc *D* to *X*, where $\rho(x,y)$ denotes distance in the Poincaré metric on *D*.[^o4] In a sense, this formula generalizes Schwarz's lemma to all complex spaces; but it may be vacuous in the sense that the Kobayashi pseudometric *d*<sub>*X*</sub> may be identically zero. For example, it is identically zero when *X* is the complex line **C**. (This occurs because **C** contains arbitrarily big discs, the images of the holomorphic maps *f*<sub>*a*</sub>: *D* → **C** given by *f*(*z*) = *az* for arbitrarily big positive numbers *a*.)

A complex space *X* is said to be **Kobayashi hyperbolic** if the Kobayashi pseudometric *d*<sub>*X*</sub> is a metric, meaning that *d*<sub>*X*</sub>(*x*,*y*) > 0 for all *x* ≠ *y* in *X*. Informally, this means that there is a genuine bound on the size of discs mapping holomorphically into *X*. In these terms, Schwarz's lemma says that the unit disc *D* is Kobayashi hyperbolic, and more precisely that the Kobayashi metric on *D* is exactly the Poincaré metric. The theory becomes more interesting as more examples of Kobayashi hyperbolic manifolds are found. (For a Kobayashi hyperbolic manifold *X*, the Kobayashi metric is a metric intrinsically determined by the complex structure of *X*; it is not at all clear that this should ever happen. A real manifold of positive dimension never has an intrinsic metric in this sense, because its [diffeomorphism group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphism_group) is too big to allow that.)

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

1. Every holomorphic map *f*: *X* → *Y* of complex spaces is distance-decreasing with respect to the Kobayashi pseudometrics of *X* and *Y*. It follows that if two points *p* and *q* in a complex space *Y* can be connected by a chain of holomorphic maps **C** → *Y*, then *d*<sub>*Y*</sub>(*p*,*q*) = 0, using that *d*<sub>**C**</sub> is identically zero. This gives many examples of complex manifolds for which the Kobayashi pseudometric is identically zero: the [complex projective line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_projective_line) **CP**<sup>1</sup> or more generally [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977) **CP**<sup>*n*</sup>, **C**−{0} (using the [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) **C** → **C**−{0}), an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225), or more generally a [compact complex torus](wikipedia://en/3055042).
1. Kobayashi hyperbolicity is preserved under passage to [open subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_subset) or to [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_subset) complex subspaces. It follows, for example, that any bounded [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) in **C**<sup>*n*</sup> is hyperbolic.
1. A complex space is Kobayashi hyperbolic if and only if its [universal covering space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_covering_space) is Kobayashi hyperbolic.[^o7] This gives many examples of hyperbolic complex curves, since the [uniformization theorem](wikipedia://en/288270) shows that most complex curves (also called [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181)) have universal cover isomorphic to the disc *D*. In particular, every compact complex curve of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) at least 2 is hyperbolic, as is the complement of 2 or more points in **C**.

:::

## Basic results {#Basic_results}

For a Kobayashi hyperbolic space *X*, every holomorphic map **C** → *X* is constant, by the distance-decreasing property of the Kobayashi pseudometric. This is often the most important consequence of hyperbolicity. For example, the fact that **C** minus 2 points is hyperbolic implies [Picard's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard's_theorem) that the image of any nonconstant [entire function](wikipedia://en/10372) **C** → **C** misses at most one point of **C**. [Nevanlinna theory](wikipedia://en/393135) is a more quantitative descendant of Picard's theorem.

**Brody's theorem** says that a *compact* complex space *X* is Kobayashi hyperbolic if and only if every holomorphic map **C** → *X* is constant.[^o9] An application is that hyperbolicity is an open condition (in the Euclidean topology) for families of compact complex spaces.[^o10] [Mark Green](wikipedia://en/56936978) used Brody's argument to characterize hyperbolicity for closed complex subspaces *X* of a compact complex torus: *X* is hyperbolic if and only if it contains no translate of a positive-dimensional subtorus.[^o11]

If a complex manifold *X* has a [Hermitian metric](wikipedia://en/2640459) with [holomorphic sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/390538#Holomorphic_sectional_curvature) bounded above by a negative constant, then *X* is Kobayashi hyperbolic.[^o12] In dimension 1, this is called the [Ahlfors](wikipedia://en/61636)–Schwarz lemma.

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="The Green–Griffiths–Lang conjecture"}

## The Green–Griffiths–Lang conjecture {#The_Green–Griffiths–Lang_conjecture}

The results above give a complete description of which complex manifolds are Kobayashi hyperbolic in complex dimension 1. The picture is less clear in higher dimensions. A central open problem is the **Green–[Griffiths](wikipedia://en/3660414)–[Lang](wikipedia://en/872991)** conjecture: if *X* is a complex [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) of [general type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_type), then there should be a closed algebraic subset *Y* not equal to *X* such that every nonconstant holomorphic map **C** → *X* maps into *Y*.[^o15]

[Clemens](wikipedia://en/48430466) and [Voisin](wikipedia://en/5980475) showed that for *n* at least 2, a very general [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) *X* in **CP**<sup>*n*+1</sup> of degree *d* at least 2*n*+1 has the property that every closed subvariety of *X* is of general type.[^o16] ("Very general" means that the property holds for all hypersurfaces of degree *d* outside a [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) union of lower-dimensional algebraic subsets of the projective space of all such hypersurfaces.) As a result, the Green–Griffiths–Lang conjecture would imply that a very general hypersurface of degree at least 2*n*+1 is Kobayashi hyperbolic. Note that one cannot expect all [smooth](wikipedia://en/35033819) hypersurfaces of a given degree to be hyperbolic, for example because some hypersurfaces contain lines (isomorphic to **CP**<sup>1</sup>). Such examples show the need for the subset *Y* in the Green–Griffiths–Lang conjecture.

The conjecture on hyperbolicity is known for hypersurfaces of high enough degree, thanks to a series of advances by [Siu](wikipedia://en/6085925), [Demailly](wikipedia://en/53350424) and others, using the technique of [jet](wikipedia://en/1521924) differentials. For example, Diverio, Merker and Rousseau showed that a general hypersurface in **CP**<sup>*n*+1</sup> of degree at least 2<sup>*n*<sup>5</sup></sup> satisfies the Green-Griffiths-Lang conjecture.[^o17] ("General" means that this holds for all hypersurfaces of given degree outside a *finite* union of lower-dimensional algebraic subsets of the projective space of all such hypersurfaces.) In 2016, Brotbek [^o18] gave a proof of the Kobayashi conjecture for the hyperbolicity of general hypersurfaces of high degree, based on a use of Wronskian differential equations; explicit degree bounds have then been obtained in arbitrary dimension by Ya Deng and Demailly, e.g. \[*(en)<sup>2n+2</sup>/3*\] by the latter.[^o19] Better bounds for the degree are known in low dimensions.

[McQuillan](wikipedia://en/39463065) proved the Green–Griffiths–Lang conjecture for every complex projective surface of general type whose [Chern numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern_number) satisfy *c*<sub>1</sub><sup>2</sup> > *c*<sub>2</sub>.[^o20] For an arbitrary variety *X* of general type, Demailly showed that every holomorphic map **C**→ *X* satisfies some (in fact, many) [algebraic differential equations](wikipedia://en/9501827).[^o21]

In the opposite direction, Kobayashi conjectured that the Kobayashi pseudometric is identically zero for [Calabi–Yau manifolds](wikipedia://en/188932). This is true in the case of [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116), using that every projective K3 surface is covered by a family of elliptic curves.[^o22] More generally, Campana gave a precise conjecture about which complex projective varieties *X* have Kobayashi pseudometric equal to zero. Namely, this should be equivalent to *X* being **special** in the sense that *X* has no rational fibration over a positive-dimensional [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) of general type.[^o23]

:::

## Analogy with number theory {#Analogy_with_number_theory}

For a projective variety *X*, the study of holomorphic maps **C** → *X* has some analogy with the study of [rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200) of *X*, a central topic of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). There are several conjectures on the relation between these two subjects. In particular, let *X* be a projective variety over a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) *k*. Fix an embedding of *k* into **C**. Then Lang conjectured that the complex manifold *X*(**C**) is Kobayashi hyperbolic if and only if *X* has only finitely many *F*-rational points for every finite extension field *F* of *k*. This is consistent with the known results on rational points, notably [Faltings' theorem](wikipedia://en/172199) on subvarieties of [abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties).

More precisely, let *X* be a projective variety of general type over a number field *k*. Let the **exceptional set** *Y* be the [Zariski closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski_closure) of the union of the images of all nonconstant holomorphic maps **C** → *X*. According to the Green–Griffiths–Lang conjecture, *Y* should be a proper closed subset of *X* (and, in particular, not be equal to *X*). The **strong Lang conjecture** predicts that *Y* is defined over *k* and that *X* − *Y* has only finitely many *F*-rational points for every finite extension field *F* of *k*.[^o25]

In the same spirit, for a projective variety *X* over a number field *k* (or, more generally, a finitely generated field *k* of characteristic zero), Campana conjectured that the Kobayashi pseudometric of *X*(**C**) is identically zero if and only if *X* has **potentially dense** rational points, meaning that there is a finite extension field *F* of *k* such that the set *X*(*F*) of *F*-rational points is Zariski dense in *X*.[^o26]

## Variants {#Variants}

The [Carathéodory metric](wikipedia://en/11543702) is another intrinsic pseudometric on complex manifolds, based on holomorphic maps to the unit disc rather than from the unit disc. The Kobayashi infinitesimal pseudometric is a [Finsler pseudometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finsler_metric) whose associated distance function is the Kobayashi pseudometric as defined above.[^o29] The Kobayashi–Eisenman pseudo-volume form is an intrinsic [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) on a complex *n*-fold, based on holomorphic maps from the *n*-dimensional [polydisc](wikipedia://en/1599249) to *X*. It is understood better than the Kobayashi pseudometric. In particular, every projective variety of general type is **measure-hyperbolic**, meaning that the Kobayashi–Eisenman pseudo-volume form is positive outside a lower-dimensional algebraic subset.[^o30]

Analogous pseudometrics have been considered for flat [affine](wikipedia://en/9996788) and projective structures, as well as for more general [projective connections](wikipedia://en/5776733) and [conformal connections](wikipedia://en/5804750).[^o31]

:::
