# Weil–Petersson metric

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weil–Petersson metric"}

In mathematics, the **Weil–Petersson metric** is a [Kähler metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_metric) on the [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661) *T*<sub>*g*,*n*</sub> of genus *g* [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) with *n* marked points. It was introduced by (txt) using the [Petersson inner product](wikipedia://en/4028987) on forms on a Riemann surface (introduced by [Hans Petersson](wikipedia://en/9079432)).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If a point of Teichmüller space is represented by a Riemann surface *R*, then the cotangent space at that point can be identified with the space of [quadratic differentials](wikipedia://en/2610010) at *R*. Since the Riemann surface has a natural [hyperbolic metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_metric), at least if it has negative [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420), one can define a [Hermitian inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_form#Hermitian_form) on the space of quadratic differentials by integrating over the Riemann surface. This induces a Hermitian inner product on the tangent space to each point of Teichmüller space, and hence a Riemannian metric.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

(Weil 1958) stated, and (Ahlfors 1961) proved, that the Weil–Petersson metric is a [Kähler metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_metric). (Ahlfors 1961b) proved that it has negative holomorphic [sectional](wikipedia://en/285618), [scalar](wikipedia://en/285622), and [Ricci curvatures](wikipedia://en/276582). The Weil–Petersson metric is usually not complete.

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The Weil–Petersson metric can be defined in a similar way for some [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) of higher-dimensional varieties.

:::
