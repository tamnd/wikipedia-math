# Half-transitive graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Half-transitive graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **half-transitive graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) that is both [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581175) and [edge-transitive](wikipedia://en/584406), but not [symmetric](wikipedia://en/1577896).[^o2] In other words, a graph is half-transitive if its [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) acts [transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) upon both its vertices and its edges, but not on ordered pairs of linked vertices.

:::

*Image: Holt graph.svg.* The [Holt graph](wikipedia://en/24240406) is the smallest half-transitive graph. The lack of reflectional symmetry in this drawing highlights the fact that edges are not equivalent to their inverse.

Every connected [symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/1577896) must be [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581175) and [edge-transitive](wikipedia://en/584406), and the converse is true for graphs of odd degree,[^o3] so that half-transitive graphs of odd degree do not exist. However, there do exist half-transitive graphs of even degree.[^o4] The smallest half-transitive graph is the [Holt graph](wikipedia://en/24240406), with degree 4 and 27 vertices.[^o5][^o6]
