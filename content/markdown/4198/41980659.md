# Nalini Anantharaman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nalini Anantharaman"}

**Nalini Florence Anantharaman** (born 26 February 1976) is an [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_people)-[French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for her work in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) and [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). She is currently a professor at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg). She has won major prizes for her work, including the [Henri Poincaré Prize](wikipedia://en/4890712) in 2012.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Nalini Florence Anantharaman was born in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) in 1976 to two mathematicians of [Indian-origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_people). Her father and her mother are Professors at the [University of Orléans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Orléans). She entered *[Ecole Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecole_Normale_Supérieure)* in 1994. She completed her [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in Paris under the supervision of [François Ledrappier](wikipedia://en/62286155) in 2000 at [Université Pierre et Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University) (Paris 6).[^o5][^o9]

She became a full Professor, at the [Paris-Sud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_University), in 2009 following time out at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in the year before as a Visiting Miller professor. From January to June 2013 at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). She is now a Professor at [Université de Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) and holder of the [Spectral Geometry](wikipedia://en/20635289) chair at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France).[^o5]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2012 she was one of four recipients of the [Henri Poincaré Prize](wikipedia://en/4890712) for [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), along with [Freeman Dyson](wikipedia://en/11397), [Barry Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Simon) and fellow Frenchwoman [Sylvia Serfaty](wikipedia://en/28587610).[^o7] Anantharaman was included for her work in "[quantum chaos](wikipedia://en/313779), [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_system_%28definition%29) and [Schrödinger equations](wikipedia://en/59874), including a remarkable advance in the problem of quantum unique [ergodicity](wikipedia://en/5456824)".[^o8] In 2011 she won the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize) which is awarded for work associated with [Fourier Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_Series). She also received the Grand Prix Jacques Herbrand from the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences#Medals,_awards_and_prizes) in 2011.[^o9][^o10] In 2015, Anantharaman was elected to be a member of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea).[^o11] She was an invited plenary speaker at the 2018 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842).[^o12]

In 2018, for her work related to “Quantum Chaos”, Anantharaman won the [Infosys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys_Prize) (in Mathematical Sciences category), one of the highest monetary awards in India that recognize excellence in science and research.[^o13] In 2020 she received the [Nemmers Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1252473).[^o14]
