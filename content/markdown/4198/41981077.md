# Matrix-exponential distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Matrix-exponential distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **matrix-exponential distribution** is an [absolutely continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_continuous) distribution with rational [Laplace–Stieltjes transform](wikipedia://en/365929).[^o2] They were introduced by [David Cox](wikipedia://en/1990771) in 1955 as **distributions with rational Laplace–Stieltjes transforms**.[^o3]

:::

The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) is $f(x) = \mathbf{\alpha} e^{x\,T} \mathbf{s} \text{ for }x\ge 0$ (and 0 when *x* \< 0), and the [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) is $F(t) = 1 - \alpha e^{\textbf{A}t} \textbf{1}$[^o4] where **1** is a vector of 1s and

$$\begin{aligned} \alpha & \in \mathbb R^{1\times n}, \\ T & \in \mathbb R^{n\times n}, \\ s & \in \mathbb R^{n\times 1}. \end{aligned}$$

There are no restrictions on the parameters **α**, **T**, **s** other than that they correspond to a probability distribution.[^o5] There is no straightforward way to ascertain if a particular set of parameters form such a distribution.[^o3] The dimension of the matrix **T** is the order of the matrix-exponential representation.[^o2]

The distribution is a generalisation of the [phase-type distribution](wikipedia://en/3513151).

## Moments {#Moments}

If *X* has a matrix-exponential distribution then the *k*th [moment](wikipedia://en/368684) is given by[^o3]

$$\operatorname E(X^k) = (-1)^{k+1}k! \mathbf{\alpha} T^{-(k+1)}\mathbf{s}.$$

## Fitting {#Fitting}

Matrix exponential distributions can be fitted using [maximum likelihood estimation](wikipedia://en/140806).[^o8]
