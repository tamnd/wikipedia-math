# Clique-width

*Image: Clique-width construction.svg.* Construction of a [distance-hereditary graph](wikipedia://en/17315337) of clique-width 3 by disjoint unions, relabelings, and label-joins. Vertex labels are shown as colors.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clique-width"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **clique-width** of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) $G$ is a parameter that describes the structural complexity of the graph; it is closely related to [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086), but unlike treewidth it can be small for [dense graphs](wikipedia://en/2777098). It is defined as the minimum number of [labels](wikipedia://en/1609861) needed to construct $G$ by means of the following 4 operations :

:::

1. Creation of a new vertex $v$ with label $i$ (denoted by $i(v)$)
1. [Disjoint union](wikipedia://en/9938459) of two labeled graphs $G$ and $H$ (denoted by $G \oplus H$)
1. Joining by an edge every vertex labeled $i$ to every vertex labeled $j$ (denoted by $\eta(i,j)$), where $i \ne j$
1. Renaming label $i$ to label $j$ (denoted by $\rho(i,j)$)

Graphs of bounded clique-width include the [cographs](wikipedia://en/1175666) and [distance-hereditary graphs](wikipedia://en/17315337). Although it is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) to compute the clique-width when it is unbounded, and unknown whether it can be computed in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) when it is bounded, efficient [approximation algorithms](wikipedia://en/563105) for clique-width are known. Based on these algorithms and on [Courcelle's theorem](wikipedia://en/35810608), many graph optimization problems that are NP-hard for arbitrary graphs can be solved or approximated quickly on the graphs of bounded clique-width.

The construction sequences underlying the concept of clique-width were formulated by [Courcelle](wikipedia://en/43140009), Engelfriet, and Rozenberg in 1990[^n1] and by (Wanke 1994). The name "clique-width" was used for a different concept by (Chlebíková 1992). By 1993, the term already had its present meaning.[^n2]

## Special classes of graphs {#Special_classes_of_graphs}

[Cographs](wikipedia://en/1175666) are exactly the graphs with clique-width at most 2.[^n3] Every [distance-hereditary graph](wikipedia://en/17315337) has clique-width at most 3. However, the clique-width of unit interval graphs is unbounded (based on their grid structure).[^n4] Similarly, the clique-width of bipartite permutation graphs is unbounded (based on similar grid structure).[^n5] Based on the characterization of cographs as the graphs with no induced subgraph isomorphic to a path with four vertices, the clique-width of many graph classes defined by forbidden induced subgraphs has been classified.[^o10]

Other graphs with bounded clique-width include the [$k$-leaf powers](wikipedia://en/49639194) for bounded values of $k$; these are the [induced subgraphs](wikipedia://en/1655188) of the leaves of a tree $T$ in the [graph power](wikipedia://en/38618878) $T^{k}$. However, leaf powers with unbounded exponents do not have bounded clique-width.[^o11]

## Bounds {#Bounds}

(Courcelle Olariu 2000) and (Corneil Rotics 2005) proved the following bounds on the clique-width of certain graphs:

- If a graph has clique-width at most $k$, then so does every [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) of the graph.[^o15]
- The [complement graph](wikipedia://en/1175649) of a graph of clique-width $k$ has clique-width at most $2k$.[^o16]
- The graphs of [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) $w$ have clique-width at most $3 \cdot 2^{w - 1}$. The exponential dependence in this bound is necessary: there exist graphs whose clique-width is exponentially larger than their treewidth.[^o17] In the other direction, graphs of bounded clique-width can have unbounded treewidth; for instance, $n$-vertex [complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816) have clique-width 2 but treewidth $n - 1$. However, graphs of clique-width $k$ that [have no complete bipartite graph $K_{t,t}$ as a subgraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biclique-free_graph) have treewidth at most $3k(t - 1) - 1$. Therefore, for every family of [sparse graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparse_graph), having bounded treewidth is equivalent to having bounded clique-width.[^n6]
- Another graph parameter, the [rank-width](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rank-width), is bounded in both directions by the clique-width: rank-width ≤ clique-width ≤ 2<sup>rank-width + 1</sup>.[^n7]

Additionally, if a graph $G$ has clique-width $k$, then the [graph power](wikipedia://en/38618878) $G^{c}$ has clique-width at most $2kc^{k}$.[^n8] Although there is an exponential gap in both the bound for clique-width from treewidth and the bound for clique-width of graph powers, these bounds do not compound each other: if a graph $G$ has treewidth $w$, then $G^{c}$ has clique-width at most $2(c + 1)^{w + 1} - 2$, only singly exponential in the treewidth.[^n9]

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

::: {#o23 .problem cue=unsolved}

Can graphs of bounded clique-width be recognized in polynomial time?

:::

Many optimization problems that are [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) for more general classes of graphs may be solved efficiently by [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297) on graphs of bounded clique-width, when a construction sequence for these graphs is known.[^n10][^n11] In particular, every [graph property](wikipedia://en/1655142) that can be expressed in MSO<sub>1</sub> [monadic second-order logic](wikipedia://en/14650395) (a form of logic allowing quantification over sets of vertices) has a linear-time algorithm for graphs of bounded clique-width, by a form of [Courcelle's theorem](wikipedia://en/35810608).[^n11]

It is also possible to find optimal [graph colorings](wikipedia://en/426743) or [Hamiltonian cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle) for graphs of bounded clique-width in polynomial time, when a construction sequence is known, but the exponent of the polynomial increases with the clique-width, and evidence from computational complexity theory shows that this dependence is likely to be necessary.[^n12] The graphs of bounded clique-width are [$\chi$-bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Χ-bounded), meaning that their chromatic number is at most a function of the size of their largest clique.[^n13]

The graphs of clique-width three can be recognized, and a construction sequence found for them, in polynomial time using an algorithm based on [split decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_decomposition).[^n14] For graphs of unbounded clique-width, it is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) to compute the clique-width exactly, and also NP-hard to obtain an approximation with sublinear additive error.[^n15] However, when the clique-width is bounded, it is possible to obtain a construction sequence of bounded width (exponentially larger than the actual clique-width) in polynomial time,[^o31] in particular in quadratic time in the number of vertices.[^n16] It remains open whether the exact clique-width, or a tighter approximation to it, can be calculated in [fixed-parameter tractable time](wikipedia://en/603026), whether it can be calculated in polynomial time for every fixed bound on the clique-width, or even whether the graphs of clique-width four can be recognized in polynomial time.[^n15]

## Related width parameters {#Related_width_parameters}

The theory of graphs of bounded clique-width resembles that for graphs of bounded [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086), but unlike treewidth allows for [dense graphs](wikipedia://en/2777098). If a family of graphs has bounded clique-width, then either it has bounded treewidth or every [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) is a subgraph of a graph in the family.[^n6] Treewidth and clique-width are also connected through the theory of [line graphs](wikipedia://en/675231): a family of graphs has bounded treewidth if and only if their line graphs have bounded clique-width.[^n17]

The graphs of bounded clique-width also have bounded [twin-width](wikipedia://en/71380095).[^n18]
