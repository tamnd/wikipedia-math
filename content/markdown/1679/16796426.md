# Area theorem (conformal mapping)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Area theorem"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) theory of [conformal mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_mapping), the **area theorem** gives an [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) satisfied by the [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) of certain conformal mappings. The theorem is called by that name, not because of its implications, but rather because the proof uses the notion of [area](wikipedia://en/1209).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose that $f$ is [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478) and [injective](wikipedia://en/45196) in the punctured [open](wikipedia://en/39358) [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) $\mathbb D\setminus\{0\}$ and has the power series representation

$$f(z)= \frac 1z + \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n z^n,\qquad z\in \mathbb D\setminus\{0\},$$

then the coefficients $a_n$ satisfy

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty n|a_n|^2\le 1.$$

:::

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The idea of the proof is to look at the area uncovered by the image of $f$. Define for $r\in(0,1)$

$$\gamma_r(\theta):=f(r\,e^{-i\theta}),\qquad \theta\in[0,2\pi].$$

Then $\gamma_r$ is a simple closed curve in the plane. Let $D_r$ denote the unique bounded connected component of $\mathbb C\setminus\gamma_r([0,2\pi])$. The existence and uniqueness of $D_r$ follows from [Jordan's curve theorem](wikipedia://en/267444).

If $D$ is a domain in the plane whose boundary is a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function#Smoothness) simple closed curve $\gamma$, then

$$\mathrm{area}(D)=\int_\gamma x\,dy=-\int_\gamma y\,dx\,,$$

provided that $\gamma$ is positively [oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation_%28topology%29) around $D$. This follows easily, for example, from [Green's theorem](wikipedia://en/243334). As we will soon see, $\gamma_r$ is positively oriented around $D_r$ (and that is the reason for the minus sign in the definition of $\gamma_r$). After applying the [chain rule](wikipedia://en/6113) and the formula for $\gamma_r$, the above expressions for the area give

$$\mathrm{area}(D_r)= \int_0^{2\pi} \Re\bigl(f(r e^{-i\theta})\bigr)\,\Im\bigl(-i\,r\,e^{-i\theta}\,f'(r e^{-i\theta})\bigr)\,d\theta = -\int_0^{2\pi} \Im\bigl(f(r e^{-i\theta})\bigr)\,\Re\bigl(-i\,r\,e^{-i\theta}\,f'(r e^{-i\theta})\bigr)d\theta.$$

Therefore, the area of $D_r$ also equals to the average of the two expressions on the right hand side. After simplification, this yields

$$\mathrm{area}(D_r) = -\frac 12\, \Re\int_0^{2\pi}f(r\,e^{-i\theta})\,\overline{r\,e^{-i\theta}\,f'(r\,e^{-i\theta})}\,d\theta,$$

where $\overline z$ denotes [complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation). We set $a_{-1}=1$ and use the power series expansion for $f$, to get

$$\mathrm{area}(D_r) = -\frac 12\, \Re\int_0^{2\pi} \sum_{n=-1}^\infty \sum_{m=-1}^\infty m\,r^{n+m}\,a_n\,\overline{a_m}\,e^{i\,(m-n)\,\theta}\,d\theta\,.$$

(Since $\int_0^{2\pi} \sum_{n=-1}^\infty\sum_{m=-1}^\infty m\,r^{n+m}\,|a_n|\,|a_m|\,d\theta<\infty\,,$ the rearrangement of the terms is justified.) Now note that $\int_0^{2\pi} e^{i\,(m-n)\,\theta}\,d\theta$ is $2\pi$ if $n= m$ and is zero otherwise. Therefore, we get

$$\mathrm{area}(D_r)= -\pi\sum_{n=-1}^\infty n\,r^{2n}\,|a_n|^2.$$

The area of $D_r$ is clearly positive. Therefore, the right hand side is positive. Since $a_{-1}=1$, by letting $r\to1$, the theorem now follows.

It only remains to justify the claim that $\gamma_r$ is positively oriented around $D_r$. Let $r'$ satisfy $r<r'<1$, and set $z_0=f(r')$, say. For very small $s>0$, we may write the expression for the [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113) of $\gamma_s$ around $z_0$, and verify that it is equal to $1$. Since, $\gamma_t$ does not pass through $z_0$ when $t\ne r'$ (as $f$ is injective), the invariance of the winding number under homotopy in the complement of $z_0$ implies that the winding number of $\gamma_r$ around $z_0$ is also $1$. This implies that $z_0\in D_r$ and that $\gamma_r$ is positively oriented around $D_r$, as required.

:::

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

The inequalities satisfied by power series coefficients of conformal mappings were of considerable interest to mathematicians prior to the solution of the [Bieberbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieberbach_conjecture). The area theorem is a central tool in this context. Moreover, the area theorem is often used in order to prove the [Koebe 1/4 theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koebe_1/4_theorem), which is very useful in the study of the geometry of conformal mappings.

:::
