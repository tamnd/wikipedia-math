# Boyd Crumrine Patterson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Boyd Crumrine Patterson"}

**Boyd Crumrine Patterson** was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and the ninth president of [Washington & Jefferson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_&_Jefferson_College).[^o3]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Patterson was born in [McKeesport, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKeesport,_Pennsylvania), on April 23, 1902, and graduated from Washington and Jefferson College in 1923, completing his studies in three years.[^o3] He was a member of the well-known Crumrine family of [Washington County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Pennsylvania) and a third-generation W&J graduate.[^o3] His father, John P. Patterson, was a member of W&J's class of 1885; his grandfather, Boyd Crumrine, a noted local historian, was in Jefferson College's class of 1860.[^o3] He was also a member of the [Phi Kappa Psi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Kappa_Psi) fraternity.[^o5]

For graduate study, Boyd went to [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) where he studied [inversive geometry](wikipedia://en/295844) with [Frank Morley](wikipedia://en/589167). In 1926, he wrote a dissertation "Differential Invariants of Inversive Geometry" for his doctoral degree.

Patterson returned to Washington & Jefferson College as a member of the faculty from 1926 to 1927 before taking a mathematics professorship at [Hamilton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_College_%28New_York%29). Continuing to collaborate with Morley, they co-wrote a paper on algebraic inversive invariants in 1930. In 1943, Patterson became the chair of the mathematics department at Hamilton.[^o3]

In 1950, he returned to W&J to assume its presidency.[^o3] In that position, he oversaw curriculum revisions, updated admissions standards, and generally enhanced Washington and Jefferson's reputation.[^o3] All told, 17 buildings were constructed during Patterson's tenure, including the [Phi Gamma Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Gamma_Delta) fraternity House, the [Wilbur F. Henry Memorial Physical Education Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilbur_F._Henry_Memorial_Physical_Education_Center), ten [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraternities_and_sororities) housing units in the center of campus, the [U. Grant Miller Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U._Grant_Miller_Library), the Student Center, the Commons, and two new dormitories.[^o3] The athletic fields also were improved. In 1952, the college's two war surplus barracks, Washington Hall and Jefferson Hall, were dismantled.[^o3] During his presidency, the college's endowment expanded from \$2.3 million to nearly \$11 million.[^o6]

On December 12, 1969, the Board of Trustees authorized the admission of women as undergraduate students, to be effective in September 1970.[^o3] Dr. Patterson retired on June 30, 1970.[^o3] He died of a stroke on July 12, 1988, in his home in [Clinton, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton,_Oneida_County,_New_York).[^o6]
