# Catalan solid

*Image: Catalan-18.jpg.* Set of Catalan solids

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Catalan solid"}

The **Catalan solids** are the [dual polyhedra](wikipedia://en/8815) of [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847). The Archimedean solids are thirteen highly-symmetric polyhedra with regular faces and symmetric vertices.[^n1] The faces of the Catalan solids correspond by duality to the vertices of Archimedean solids, and vice versa.[^n2]

:::

## Description {#Description}

The Catalan solids are [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive) or *isohedral*, meaning that their faces are symmetric to one another, but they are not [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) because their vertices are not symmetric. Their duals, the Archimedean solids, are vertex-transitive but not face-transitive. Each Catalan solid has constant [dihedral angles](wikipedia://en/338046), meaning the angle between any two adjacent faces is the same.[^n1] Additionally, two Catalan solids, the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) and [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025), are [edge-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-transitive), meaning their edges are symmetric to each other. Some Catalan solids were discovered by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) during his study of [zonohedra](wikipedia://en/669402), and [Eugène Charles Catalan](wikipedia://en/639553) completed the list of the thirteen solids in 1865.[^o6]

*Image: DormanLuke.svg.* The [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631)'s construction, the dual polyhedron of a [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280), by [Dorman Luke construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorman_Luke_construction)

In general, each face of a [dual uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/51069784) (including the Catalan solid) can be constructed by using the [Dorman Luke construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorman_Luke_construction).[^o7] Some of the Catalan solids can be constructed by adding pyramids to the faces of Platonic solids. These examples are [Kleetopes](wikipedia://en/31165912) of Platonic solids: [triakis tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/722548), [tetrakis hexahedron](wikipedia://en/722554), [triakis octahedron](wikipedia://en/722549), [triakis icosahedron](wikipedia://en/722873), and [pentakis dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/723196).[^o8]

Two Catalan solids, the [pentagonal icositetrahedron](wikipedia://en/725126) and the [pentagonal hexecontahedron](wikipedia://en/727271), are [chiral](wikipedia://en/1170160), meaning that these two solids are not their own mirror images. They are dual to the [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249) and [snub dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/287137), respectively, which are also chiral.

Eleven of the thirteen Catalan solids are known to have the [Rupert property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_property), which means that a copy of the same solid can be passed through a hole in the solid.[^n3]
