# Gell-Mann matrices

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gell-Mann matrices"}

The **Gell-Mann matrices**, developed by [Murray Gell-Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Gell-Mann), are a set of eight [linearly independent](wikipedia://en/101863) 3×3 [traceless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_trace) [Hermitian matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_matrices) used in the study of the [strong interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_interaction) in [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics). They span the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17945#The_Lie_algebra_associated_with_a_Lie_group) of the [SU(3)](wikipedia://en/173997#SU%283%29) group in the defining representation, up to a prefactor of $i$.

:::

## Matrices {#Matrices}

{| border="0" cellpadding="8" cellspacing="0"

\|$\lambda_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ |$\lambda_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -i & 0 \\ i & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ |$\lambda_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ |- |$\lambda_4 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ |$\lambda_5 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & -i \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ i & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ | |- |$\lambda_6 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ |$\lambda_7 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -i \\ 0 & i & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ |$\lambda_8 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -2 \end{pmatrix} .$ |}

## Properties {#Properties}

These matrices are [traceless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traceless), [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/189682), and obey the extra trace orthonormality relation, so they can generate [unitary matrix](wikipedia://en/129222) group elements of [SU(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%283%29) through [exponentiation](wikipedia://en/672731).[^o5] These properties were chosen by Gell-Mann because they then naturally generalize the [Pauli matrices](wikipedia://en/24868) for [SU(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%282%29) to SU(3), which formed the basis for Gell-Mann's [quark model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark_model).[^o6] Gell-Mann's generalization further [extends to general SU(*n*)](wikipedia://en/5277589#Construction). For their connection to the [standard basis](wikipedia://en/277087) of Lie algebras, see the [Weyl–Cartan basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clebsch–Gordan_coefficients_for_SU%283%29#Standard_basis).

### Trace orthonormality {#Trace_orthonormality}

In mathematics, orthonormality typically implies a norm which has a value of unity (1). Gell-Mann matrices, however, are normalized to a value of 2. Thus, the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of the pairwise product results in the ortho-normalization condition

$$\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_i \lambda_j) = 2\delta_{ij},$$

where $\delta_{ij}$ is the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890).

This is so the embedded Pauli matrices corresponding to the three embedded subalgebras of *SU*(2) are conventionally normalized. In this three-dimensional matrix representation, the [Cartan subalgebra](wikipedia://en/1336000) is the set of linear combinations (with real coefficients) of the two matrices $\lambda_3$ and $\lambda_8$, which commute with each other.

There are three [significant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clebsch–Gordan_coefficients_for_SU%283%29#Standard_basis) [SU(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%282%29) subalgebras:

- $$\{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3\}$$
- $\{\lambda_4, \lambda_5, x\},$ and
- $$\{\lambda_6, \lambda_7, y\},$$

where the $x$ and $y$ are linear combinations of $\lambda_3$ and $\lambda_8$. The SU(2) [Casimirs](wikipedia://en/513672#Casimir_elements_and_representation_theory) of these subalgebras mutually commute.

However, any unitary similarity transformation of these subalgebras will yield SU(2) subalgebras. There is an uncountable number of such transformations.

### Commutation relations {#Commutation_relations}

The 8 generators of SU(3) satisfy the [commutation and anti-commutation relations](wikipedia://en/7193)[^o9]

$$\begin{aligned} \left[ \lambda_a, \lambda_b \right] &= 2 i \sum_c f_{abc} \lambda_c, \\ \{ \lambda_a, \lambda_b \} &= \frac{4}{3} \delta_{ab} I + 2 \sum_c d_{abc} \lambda_c, \end{aligned}$$

with the [structure constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_constant)

$$\begin{aligned} f_{abc} &= -\frac{1}{4} i \operatorname{tr}([\lambda_a , \lambda_b] \lambda_c ), \\ d_{abc} &= \frac{1}{4} \operatorname{tr}(\{\lambda_a , \lambda_b\} \lambda_c ). \end{aligned}$$

The [structure constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_constant) $d_{abc}$ are completely symmetric in the three indices. The [structure constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_constant) $f_{abc}$ are completely antisymmetric in the three indices, generalizing the antisymmetry of the [Levi-Civita symbol](wikipedia://en/195982) $\epsilon_{jkl}$ of $SU(2)$. For the present order of Gell-Mann matrices they take the values

$$f_{123} = 1 \ , \quad f_{147} = f_{165} = f_{246} = f_{257} = f_{345} = f_{376} = \frac{1}{2} \ , \quad f_{458} = f_{678} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \ .$$

In general, they evaluate to zero, unless they contain an odd count of indices from the set {2,5,7}, corresponding to the antisymmetric (imaginary) $\lambda$s.

Using these commutation relations, the product of Gell-Mann matrices can be written as

$$\lambda_a \lambda_b = \frac{1}{2} ([\lambda_a,\lambda_b] + \{\lambda_a,\lambda_b\}) = \frac{2}{3} \delta_{ab} I + \sum_c \left(d_{abc} + i f_{abc}\right) \lambda_c ,$$

where $I$ is the 3×3 identity matrix.

### Fierz completeness relations {#Fierz_completeness_relations}

Since the eight matrices and the identity are a complete trace-orthogonal set spanning all 3×3 matrices, it is straightforward to find two Fierz ***completeness relations***, (Li & Cheng, 4.134), analogous to that [satisfied by the Pauli matrices](wikipedia://en/24868#Completeness_relation_2). Namely, using the dot to sum over the eight matrices and using Greek indices for their row/column indices, the following identities hold,

$$\delta^\alpha _\beta \delta^\gamma _\delta = \frac{1}{3} \delta^\alpha_\delta \delta^\gamma _\beta +\frac{1}{2} \lambda^\alpha _\delta \cdot \lambda^\gamma _\beta$$

and

$$\lambda^\alpha _\beta \cdot \lambda^\gamma _\delta = 2 \delta^\alpha_\delta \delta^\gamma _\beta -\frac{2}{3} \delta^\alpha_\beta \delta^\gamma _\delta ~.$$

:::

## Representation theory {#Representation_theory}

A particular choice of matrices is called a [group representation](wikipedia://en/12695), because any element of SU(3) can be written in the form $\mathrm{exp}(i \theta^j g_j)$ using the [Einstein notation](wikipedia://en/195407), where the eight $\theta^j$ are real numbers and a sum over the index $j$ is implied. Given one representation, an equivalent one may be obtained by an arbitrary unitary similarity transformation, since that leaves the commutator unchanged.

The matrices can be realized as a representation of the [infinitesimal generators](wikipedia://en/17945#The_Lie_algebra_associated_with_a_Lie_group) of the [special unitary group](wikipedia://en/173997) called [SU(3)](wikipedia://en/173997#The_group_SU%283%29). The [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of this group (a real Lie algebra in fact) has dimension eight and therefore it has some set with eight [linearly independent](wikipedia://en/101863) generators, which can be written as $g_i$, with *i* taking values from 1 to 8.[^o5]

### Casimir operators and invariants {#Casimir_operators_and_invariants}

The squared sum of the Gell-Mann matrices gives the quadratic [Casimir operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_operator), a group invariant,

$$C = \sum_{i=1}^8 \lambda_i \lambda_i = \frac{16} 3 I$$

where $I\,$is 3×3 identity matrix. There is another, independent, [cubic Casimir operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clebsch–Gordan_coefficients_for_SU%283%29#Casimir_operators), as well.

## Application to quantum chromodynamics {#Application_to_quantum_chromodynamics}

These matrices serve to study the internal (color) rotations of the [gluon fields](wikipedia://en/40812493) associated with the coloured quarks of [quantum chromodynamics](wikipedia://en/25264) (cf. [colours of the gluon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluon#Eight_gluon_colours)). A gauge colour rotation is a spacetime-dependent SU(3) group element $\; U = \exp \left(\frac{\ i\ }{2}\ \theta^k({\mathbf r},t)\ \lambda_k\right) \;,$ where summation over the eight indices $k$ is implied.
