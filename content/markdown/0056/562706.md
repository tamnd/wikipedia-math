# Cleve Moler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Cleve Moler"}

**Cleve Barry Moler** (August 17, 1939 – May 20, 2026) was an American mathematician and [computer programmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programming) who specialized in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506). In the mid to late 1970s, he was one of the authors of [LINPACK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LINPACK) and [EISPACK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EISPACK), [Fortran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran) libraries for numerical computing. He created [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412), a numerical computing package, to give his students at the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico) easy access to these libraries without writing Fortran. In 1984, he co-founded [MathWorks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MathWorks) with [Jack Little](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_N._Little) to commercialize this program.[^o3]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Cleve Barry Moler was born on August 17, 1939, in [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City), Utah.[^o16] He received his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) from [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1961, and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1965 from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), both in mathematics.[^o5] He worked for Charles Lawson at the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory) in 1961 and 1962.

He was a professor of mathematics and computer science for almost 20 years at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico).[^o6] Before joining [MathWorks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MathWorks) full-time in 1989, he also worked for [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel) Hypercube, where he coined the term "[embarrassingly parallel](wikipedia://en/1738712)", and [Ardent Computer Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardent_Computer_Corporation). He is also co-author of four textbooks on numerical methods and was a member of the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery). He was president of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) 2007–2008.[^o7]

Moler was elected a member of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) on February 14, 1997, for conceiving and developing widely used mathematical software. He received an honorary degree from [Linköping University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linköping_University), Sweden. He received an honorary degree of Doctor of Mathematics from the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) in 2001.[^o8] On April 30, 2004, he was named Honorary Doctor (doctor technices, honoris causa) by the [Technical University of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Denmark).[^o9] In 2009, he was recognized by [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) as a SIAM Fellow for his outstanding contributions to numerical analysis and software, including the invention of MATLAB.[^o10] In April 2012, the [IEEE Computer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Computer_Society) named Moler the recipient of the 2012 Computer Pioneer Award.[^o11] In February 2014, IEEE awarded Moler the 2014 [IEEE John von Neumann Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_John_von_Neumann_Medal).[^o12] In April 2017, he was made Fellow of the [Computer History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_History_Museum).[^o13][^o14]

Moler died on May 20, 2026, at his home in [Saint Michaels, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Michaels,_Maryland), of complications from [Parkinson’s disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson’s_disease) at the age of 86.[^o15][^o16]
