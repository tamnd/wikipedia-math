# Yasumasa Kanada

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yasumasa Kanada"}

**Yasumasa Kanada** was a Japanese computer scientist most known for his numerous world records over three decades for calculating digits of [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601). He set the record 11 of the past 21 times.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Kanada was a professor in the Department of Information Science at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan) until 2015.

## Pi records {#Pi_records}

From 2002 until 2009, Kanada held the world record calculating the number of digits in the decimal expansion of pi – exactly 1.2411 trillion digits.[^o4] The calculation took more than 600 hours on 64 nodes of a [HITACHI SR8000/MPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HITACHI_SR8000/MPP) [supercomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputer). Some of his competitors in recent years include [Jonathan](wikipedia://en/1145010) and [Peter Borwein](wikipedia://en/1339736) and the [Chudnovsky brothers](wikipedia://en/1705790).
