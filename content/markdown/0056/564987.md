# Robert Woodhouse

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Woodhouse"}

**Robert Woodhouse** (28 April 1773 – 23 December 1827) was a British mathematician and astronomer.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Robert Woodhouse was born on 28 April 1773 in [Norwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwich), [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk), the son of Robert Woodhouse, [linen draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloth_merchant), and Judith Alderson, the daughter of a [Unitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarianism) minister from [Lowestoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowestoft).[^o6] Robert junior was baptised at [St George's Church, Colegate, Norwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_George's_Church,_Colegate,_Norwich), on 19 May, 1773.[^o5] A younger son, John Thomas Woodhouse, was born in 1780. The brothers were educated at the [Paston School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paston_School) in [North Walsham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Walsham), 24 km north of Norwich.[^o6]

In May 1790 Woodhouse was admitted to [Gonville and Caius College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge), [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University),[^o6] the college where Paston pupils were traditionally sent.[^o7] In 1795 he graduated as the [Senior Wrangler](wikipedia://en/19355609) (ranked first among the mathematics undergraduates at the university), and took the First [Smith's Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize).[^o6] He obtained his [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) at Cambridge in 1798.[^o9]

### Marriage and career at Cambridge {#Marriage_and_career_at_Cambridge}

*Image: Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge Wellcome M0012088.jpg.* [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge) by [David Loggan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Loggan) (1688)

*Image: Robert Woodhouse Anal Calc.png.* The frontispiece of Woodhouse's *Principles of Analytical Calculation* (1803)

Woodhouse was a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the college from 1798 to 1823, after which he resigned so as to be able to marry Harriet, the daughter of William Wilkin, a Norwich architect.[^o6] They were married on 20 February 1823; the marriage produced a son, also named Robert.[^o6][^o9] Harriet Woodhouse died at Cambridge on 31 March 1826.[^o10]

Woodhouse was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) on 16 December 1802.[^o11] His earliest work, entitled the *Principles of Analytical Calculation*, was published at Cambridge in 1803.[^n1] In this he explained the [differential notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_notation) and strongly pressed the employment of it; but he severely criticised the methods used by continental writers, and their constant assumption of non-evident principles.[^n2]

In 1809 Woodhouse published a textbook covering [planar trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261) and [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405) and the next year a historical [treatise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatise) on the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) and [isoperimetrical problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoperimetry). He next produced an [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy); of which the first book (usually bound in two volumes), on practical and descriptive astronomy, was issued in 1812, and the second book, containing an account of the treatment of physical astronomy by [Pierre-Simon Laplace](wikipedia://en/344783) and other continental writers, was issued in 1818.[^n1]

Woodhouse became the [Lucasian Professor of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/147921) in 1820, but the small income caused him to resign the professorship in 1822 and instead accept the better paid post as the [Plumian professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plumian_Professor_of_Astronomy_and_Experimental_Philosophy) in the university.[^o6][^n3] As Plumian Professor he was responsible for installing and adjusting the transit instruments and clocks at the [Cambridge Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Observatory).[^n4]

Woodhouse did not exercise much influence on the majority of his contemporaries, and the movement might have died away for the time being if it had not been for the advocacy of [George Peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Peacock_%28mathematician%29), [Charles Babbage](wikipedia://en/5698), and [John Herschel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Herschel), who formed the [Analytical Society](wikipedia://en/206234), with the object of advocating the general use in the university of analytical methods and of the differential notation.[^n5] Woodhouse was the first director of the newly built [observatory at Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Observatory), a post he held until his death in 1827.[^o6]

On his death in Cambridge he was buried in [Caius College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge) chapel.[^o11]
