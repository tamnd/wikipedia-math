# Fourier–Bessel series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fourier–Bessel series"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Fourier–Bessel series** is a particular kind of [generalized Fourier series](wikipedia://en/245530) (an [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) expansion on a finite interval) based on [Bessel functions](wikipedia://en/4700).

:::

Fourier–Bessel series are used in the solution to [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), particularly in [cylindrical coordinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindrical_coordinate) systems.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Fourier–Bessel series of a function $f(x)$ with a [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) of 0, *b* satisfying $f(b) = 0$

*Image: Basisweki.jpg.* Bessel function for (i) $\alpha=0$ and (ii) $\alpha=1$.

$$f: [0,b] \to \mathbb{R}$$

is the representation of that function as a [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of many [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) versions of the same [Bessel function of the first kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel_functions_of_the_first_kind) *J*<sub>*α*</sub>, where the argument to each version *n* is differently scaled, according to [^o4][^o5]

$$(J_\alpha )_n (x) := J_\alpha \left( \frac{u_{\alpha,n}}b x \right)$$

where *u*<sub>*α*,*n*</sub> is a [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function), numbered *n* associated with the Bessel function *J*<sub>*α*</sub> and *c*<sub>*n*</sub> are the assigned coefficients:[^o6]

$$f(x) \sim \sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n J_\alpha \left( \frac{u_{\alpha,n}}b x \right).$$

:::

## Interpretation {#Interpretation}

The Fourier–Bessel series may be thought of as a Fourier expansion in the ρ coordinate of [cylindrical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindrical_coordinates). Just as the [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) is defined for a finite interval and has a counterpart, the [continuous Fourier transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_Fourier_transform) over an infinite interval, so the Fourier–Bessel series has a counterpart over an infinite interval, namely the [Hankel transform](wikipedia://en/1452960).

## Calculating the coefficients {#Calculating_the_coefficients}

As said, differently scaled Bessel Functions are orthogonal with respect to the [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product)

*Image: Fbsecofspeech.jpg.* (i) Speech signal (mtlb.mat from Matlab toolbox), (ii) FBSE coefficients of speech signal, and (iii) magnitude of FBSE coefficients of speech signal.

$$\langle f,g \rangle = \int_0^b x f(x) g(x) \, dx$$

according to

$$\int_0^b x J_\alpha\left(\frac{x u_{\alpha,n}}{b}\right)\,J_\alpha\left(\frac{x u_{\alpha,m}}{b}\right)\,dx = \frac{b^2}{2} \delta_{mn}[J_{\alpha+1}(u_{\alpha,n})]^2,$$

(where: $\delta_{mn}$ is the Kronecker delta). The coefficients can be obtained from [projecting](wikipedia://en/1508507) the function $f(x)$ onto the respective Bessel functions:

$$c_n = \frac{ \langle f,(J_\alpha)_n \rangle }{ \langle (J_\alpha)_n,(J_\alpha)_n \rangle } = \frac{ \int_0^b x f(x) (J_\alpha)_n(x) \, dx }{ \frac12 (b J_{\alpha\pm1}(u_{\alpha,n}))^2}$$

where the plus or minus sign is equally valid.

For the inverse transform, one makes use of the following representation of the [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021)[^o9]

$$\frac{2 x^\alpha y^{1-\alpha}}{b^2} \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{J_\alpha\left(\frac{x u_{\alpha,k}}{b}\right)\,J_\alpha\left(\frac{y u_{\alpha,k}}{b}\right)}{J_{\alpha+1}^2(u_{\alpha,k})} = \delta(x-y).$$

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Fourier–Bessel series expansion employs aperiodic and decaying Bessel functions as the basis. The Fourier–Bessel series expansion has been successfully applied in diversified areas such as Gear fault diagnosis,[^o12] discrimination of odorants in a turbulent ambient,[^o13] postural stability analysis, detection of voice onset time, glottal closure instants (epoch) detection, separation of speech formants, speech enhancement,[^o14] and speaker identification.[^o15] The Fourier–Bessel series expansion has also been used to reduce cross terms in the Wigner–Ville distribution.

:::

::: {#o17 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Dini series"}

## Dini series {#Dini_series}

A second Fourier–Bessel series, also known as *Dini series*, is associated with the [Robin boundary condition](wikipedia://en/9945920)

$$b f'(b) + c f(b) = 0 ,$$

where $c$ is an arbitrary constant. The Dini series can be defined by

$$f(x) \sim \sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n J_\alpha(\gamma_n x/b),$$

where $\gamma_n$ is the *n*-th zero of $x J'_\alpha(x) + c J_\alpha(x)$.

The coefficients $b_n$ are given by

$$b_n = \frac{2 \gamma_n^2}{ b^2(c^2+\gamma_n^2-\alpha^2)J_\alpha^2(\gamma_n)} \int_0^b J_\alpha(\gamma_n x/b)\,f(x) \,x \,dx.$$
