# Poisson random measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Poisson random measure"}

Let $(E, \mathcal A, \mu)$ be some [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) with $\sigma$-[finite measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_finite_measure) $\mu$. The **Poisson random measure** with intensity [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) $\mu$ is a family of [random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variables) $\{N_A\}_{A\in\mathcal{A}}$ defined on some [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) $(\Omega, \mathcal F, \mathrm{P})$ such that

:::

i) $\forall A\in\mathcal{A},\quad N_A$ is a [Poisson random variable](wikipedia://en/23009144) with rate $\mu(A)$.

ii) If sets $A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_n\in\mathcal{A}$ don't intersect then the corresponding [random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variables) from i) are mutually [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_independence).

iii) $\forall\omega\in\Omega\;N_{\bullet}(\omega)$ is a measure on $(E, \mathcal {A})$

## Existence {#Existence}

If $\mu\equiv 0$ then $N\equiv 0$ satisfies the conditions i)–iii). Otherwise, in the case of [finite measure](wikipedia://en/4316185) $\mu$, given $Z$, a [Poisson random variable](wikipedia://en/23009144) with rate $\mu(E)$, and $X_{1}, X_{2},\ldots$, mutually [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_independence) [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) with [distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) $\frac{\mu}{\mu(E)}$, define $N_{\cdot}(\omega) = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{Z(\omega)} \delta_{X_i(\omega)}(\cdot)$ where $\delta_{c}(A)$ is a [degenerate measure](wikipedia://en/101843) located in $c$. Then $N$ will be a Poisson random measure. In the case $\mu$ is not finite the [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) $N$ can be obtained from the measures constructed above on parts of $E$ where $\mu$ is finite.

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

This kind of [random measure](wikipedia://en/10040846) is often used when describing jumps of [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895), in particular in [Lévy–Itō decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lévy–Itō_decomposition) of the [Lévy processes](wikipedia://en/1025748).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The Poisson random measure generalizes to the [Poisson-type random measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson-type_random_measures), where members of the PT family are invariant under restriction to a subspace.

:::
