# Subdirectly irreducible algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Subdirectly irreducible algebra"}

In the branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) known as [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) (and in its applications), a **subdirectly irreducible algebra** is an [algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) that cannot be factored as a [subdirect product](wikipedia://en/17395221) of "simpler" algebras. Subdirectly irreducible algebras play a somewhat analogous role in algebra to [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) *A* is said to be subdirectly irreducible when *A* has more than one element, and when any [subdirect representation](wikipedia://en/17395221) of *A* includes (as a factor) an algebra [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to *A*, with the isomorphism being given by the projection map.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The two-element [chain](wikipedia://en/30330#Chains), as either a [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959), a [Heyting algebra](wikipedia://en/309343), a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304)p. 56, or a [semilattice](wikipedia://en/755647), is subdirectly irreducible. In fact, the two-element chain is the only subdirectly irreducible [distributive lattice](wikipedia://en/310883).p. 56
- Any finite chain with two or more elements, as a [Heyting algebra](wikipedia://en/309343), is subdirectly irreducible. (This is not the case for chains of three or more elements as either lattices or semilattices, which are subdirectly reducible to the two-element chain. The difference with Heyting algebras is that *a* → *b* need not be comparable with *a* under the lattice order even when *b* is.)
- Any [finite](wikipedia://en/323707) [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order a power of a prime (i.e. any cyclic [*p*-group](wikipedia://en/24664)) is subdirectly irreducible.p. 56 (One weakness of the analogy between subdirect irreducibles and prime numbers is that the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) are subdirectly representable by any infinite family of nonisomorphic prime-power cyclic groups, e.g. just those of order a [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908) assuming there are infinitely many.) In fact, an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) is subdirectly irreducible [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to a cyclic *p*-group or isomorphic to a [Prüfer group](wikipedia://en/4885031) (an infinite but [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite) *p*-group, which is the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of its finite *p*-subgroups).p. 61
- A [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) is subdirectly irreducible if and only if it has [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) one.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The **subdirect representation theorem** of universal algebra states that every algebra is subdirectly representable by its subdirectly irreducible [quotients](wikipedia://en/332640). An equivalent definition of "subdirect irreducible" therefore is any algebra *A* that is not subdirectly representable by those of its quotients not isomorphic to *A*. (This is not quite the same thing as "by its proper quotients" because a proper quotient of *A* may be isomorphic to *A*, for example the quotient of the semilattice (**Z**, $\min$) obtained by identifying just the two elements 3 and 4.)

An immediate [corollary](wikipedia://en/168865) is that any [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753), as a class closed under [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564), [subalgebras](wikipedia://en/45239), and [direct products](wikipedia://en/8887), is determined by its subdirectly irreducible members, since every algebra *A* in the variety can be constructed as a subalgebra of a suitable direct product of the subdirectly irreducible quotients of *A*, all of which belong to the variety because *A* does. For this reason one often studies not the variety itself but just its subdirect irreducibles.

An algebra *A* is subdirectly irreducible if and only if it contains two elements that are identified by every proper quotient, equivalently, if and only if its lattice **Con** *A* of [congruences](wikipedia://en/48167) has a least nonidentity element. That is, any subdirect irreducible must contain a specific pair of elements witnessing its irreducibility in this way. Given such a witness (*a*, *b*) to subdirect irreducibility we say that the subdirect irreducible is (*a*, *b*)-irreducible.

Given any class *C* of similar algebras, **Jónsson's lemma** (due to [Bjarni Jónsson](wikipedia://en/3893573)) states that if the variety HSP(*C*) generated by *C* is [congruence-distributive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congruence-distributive_variety), its subdirect irreducibles are in HSP<sub>U</sub>(*C*), that is, they are quotients of subalgebras of [ultraproducts](wikipedia://en/273642) of members of *C*. (If *C* is a finite set of finite algebras, the ultraproduct operation is redundant.)

## Applications {#Applications}

A necessary and sufficient condition for a Heyting algebra to be subdirectly irreducible is for there to be a greatest element strictly below 1. The witnessing pair is that element and 1, and identifying any other pair *a*, *b* of elements identifies both *a*→*b* and *b*→*a* with 1 thereby collapsing everything above those two implications to 1. Hence every finite chain of two or more elements as a Heyting algebra is subdirectly irreducible.

By Jónsson's Lemma, subdirectly irreducible algebras of a congruence-distributive variety generated by a finite set of finite algebras are no larger than the generating algebras, since the quotients and subalgebras of an algebra *A* are never larger than *A* itself. For example, the subdirect irreducibles in the variety generated by a finite [linearly ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_ordered) Heyting algebra *H* must be just the nondegenerate quotients of *H*, namely all smaller linearly ordered nondegenerate Heyting algebras. The conditions cannot be dropped in general: for example, the variety of all Heyting algebras is generated by the set of its finite subdirectly irreducible algebras, but there exist subdirectly irreducible Heyting algebras of arbitrary (infinite) [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174). There also exists a single finite algebra generating a (non-congruence-distributive) variety with arbitrarily large subdirect irreducibles.[^o10]
