# Mikhail Kadets

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mikhail Kadets"}

**Mikhail Iosiphovich Kadets** (Михаил Иосифович Кадец, Михайло Йосипович Кадець, sometimes transliterated as **Kadec**, 30 November 1923 – 7 March 2011) was a Soviet-born Jewish mathematician working in [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and the theory of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Kadets was born in [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv). In 1943, he was drafted into the army. After demobilisation in 1946, he studied at [Kharkov University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkov_University), graduating in 1950. After several years in [Makeevka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makeevka) he returned to Kharkov in 1957, where he spent the remainder of his life working at various institutes. He defended his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1955 (under the supervision of [Boris Levin](wikipedia://en/49586896)), and his doctoral dissertation in 1963. He was awarded the State Prize of Ukraine in 2005.

After reading the Ukrainian translation of [Banach](wikipedia://en/29646)'s monograph *[Théorie des Opérations Linéaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Théorie_des_Opérations_Linéaires)*,[^o7] he became interested in the theory of Banach spaces.[^o8] In 1966, Kadets solved in the affirmative the [Banach](wikipedia://en/29646)–[Fréchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_René_Fréchet) problem, asking whether every two [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) infinite-dimensional Banach spaces are [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660). He developed the method of equivalent norms, which has found numerous applications. For example, he showed that every separable Banach space admits an equivalent [Fréchet differentiable](wikipedia://en/2735727) norm if and only if the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) is separable.[^o9]

Together with [Aleksander Pełczyński](wikipedia://en/56285756), he obtained important results on the topological structure of [Lp spaces](wikipedia://en/45194).[^o10]

Kadets also made several contributions to the theory of finite-dimensional normed spaces. Together with M. G. Snobar (1971), he showed that every $n$-dimensional subspace of a Banach space is the image of a projection of norm at most $\sqrt{n}.$[^o11] Together with [V. I. Gurarii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Gurariy) and V. I. Matsaev, he found the exact order of magnitude of the [Banach–Mazur distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Mazur_distance) between the $n$-dimensional spaces $\ell^n_p$ and $\ell^n_q.$[^o12]

In [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), Kadets proved (1964) what is now called the Kadets $1/4$ theorem, which states that, if $|\lambda_n - n| \leq C < 1/4$ for all integers $n,$ then the sequence $(\exp(i \lambda_n x))_{n \in \mathbb{Z}}$ is a [Riesz basis](wikipedia://en/4795751) in $L_2[-\pi, \pi]$[^o13]

Kadets was the founder of the [Kharkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv) school of Banach spaces.[^o9]

Together with his son Vladimir Kadets, he authored two books about series in Banach spaces.[^o14]
