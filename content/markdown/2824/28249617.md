# Franciszek Indan Pykna

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Franciszek Indan Pykna"}

**Franciszek Indan Pykna** (12 July 1913 – 29 January 1992) was a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) mathematician, philosopher, and linguist.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Pykna was born in [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), to Polish parents Kazimierz Indan Pykno and Benigna Norwillo. He spent his early childhood travelling with his parents through Russia; they returned to Poland ([Kunow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunow) and Odrowaz). They finally settled in [Kovel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kovel), where he went to Juliusz Slowacki High School. After graduating he moved to [Vilnius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius) in order to study at [Vilnius University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_University).

## Academic work {#Academic_work}

Pykna achieved a master's degree in 1937 with his thesis "The concept of ethical philosophy of Descartes".[^o4] He became an assistant in the Philosophy department of Stefan Batory University while working on his PhD in the subject of ethics[^o5] He was nominated as senior assistant to the department of Philosophy starting on 1 September 1939,[^o6] which was the day [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) started; the university was evacuated to Poland. After the war, most of the staff settled in [Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toruń), Poland, creating [Nicolaus Copernicus University in Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus_University_in_Toruń). In 1945 he worked in the department of logic studies at the university as a senior assistant to professor Tadeusz Czezowski. Between 1947 and 1948 he headed the mathematical department in the Fourth College in Torun.[^o7]

He then returned to Nicolaus Copernicus University, working with professor [Roman Ingarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Ingarden) and Tadeusz Czezowski. He started publishing his works: "Revue Philosophique de la France et de l'Etranger", "Pozytywizm etyczny Emila Durkheima" ([Emile Durkheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emile_Durkheim)'s ethical positivism; 1960),[^o8] "Set of tasks of probability and mathematical statistics" (1962)[^o9] "Cohorts method applied to arrays of elimination" (1967), and "Mathematics Theory and a set of tasks" (1973).[^o10]

His correspondence with Tadeusz Kotarbinski (philosopher and professor of [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw)) can be found in the archives of Archives of Janina and Tadeusz Kotarbinski, 1962.[^o11] Franciszek Indan was a board member of the Polish Society of Mathematics from 1971 to 1981.[^o12] His works are available on line from Twardowski Digital Archives.[^o13]

## Private life {#Private_life}

He was married to Zofia Siemaszko, with whom he had five children.
