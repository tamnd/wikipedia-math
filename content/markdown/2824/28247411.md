# Pseudomanifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pseudomanifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **pseudomanifold** is a special type of [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). It looks like a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) at most of its points, but it may contain [singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity). For example, the [cone](wikipedia://en/782427) of solutions of $z^2=x^2+y^2$ forms a pseudomanifold.

:::

*Image: Pinched torus.jpg.* Figure 1: A pinched torus

A pseudomanifold can be regarded as a [combinatorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial) realisation of the general idea of a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) with singularities. The concepts of [orientability](wikipedia://en/187446), orientation and [degree of a mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_of_a_mapping) make sense for pseudomanifolds and moreover, within the combinatorial approach, pseudomanifolds form the natural domain of definition for these concepts.[^o2][^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A topological space *X* endowed with a [triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440976) *K* is an *n*-dimensional pseudomanifold if the following conditions hold:[^o6]

1. (*pure*) *X* = |*K*| is the [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of all *n*-[simplices](wikipedia://en/39781).
1. Every (*n*–1)-simplex is a [face](wikipedia://en/39781#Elements) of exactly one or two *n*-simplices for *n > 1*.
1. For every pair of *n*-simplices σ and σ' in *K*, there is a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of *n*-simplices σ = σ<sub>0</sub>, σ<sub>1</sub>, ..., σ<sub>*k*</sub> = σ' such that the [intersection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_%28mathematics%29) σ<sub>*i*</sub> ∩ σ<sub>*i*+1</sub> is an (*n*−1)-simplex for all *i* = 0, ..., *k*−1.

### Implications of the definition {#Implications_of_the_definition}

- Condition 2 means that *X* is a **non-branching** [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686).
- Condition 3 means that *X* is a **strongly connected** simplicial complex.[^o8]
- If we require Condition 2 to hold only for (*n*−1)-simplexes in sequences of *n*-simplexes in Condition 3, we obtain an equivalent definition only for n=2. For n≥3 there are examples of combinatorial non-pseudomanifolds that are strongly connected through sequences of *n*-simplexes satisfying Condition 2.[^o9]

### Decomposition {#Decomposition}

Strongly connected n-complexes can always be assembled from *n*-simplexes gluing just two of them at (*n*−1)-simplexes. However, in general, construction by gluing can lead to non-pseudomanifoldness (see Figure 2).

*Image: Nonpmnonclok3.svg.* Figure 2: Gluing a manifold along manifold edges (in green) may create non-pseudomanifold edges (in red). A decomposition is possible cutting (in blue) at a singular edge

Nevertheless it is always possible to decompose a non-pseudomanifold surface into manifold parts cutting only at singular edges and vertices (see Figure 2 in blue). For some surfaces several non-equivalent options are possible (see Figure 3).

*Image: M3foldok.svg.* Figure 3: The non pseudomanifold surface on the left can be decomposed into an orientable manifold (central) or into a non-orientable one (on the right).

On the other hand, in higher dimension, for n>2, the situation becomes rather tricky.

- In general, for n≥3, n-pseudomanifolds cannot be decomposed into manifold parts only by cutting at singularities (see Figure 4).

*Image: Pinchedpieaok.svg.* Figure 4: Two 3-pseudomanifolds with singularities (in red) that cannot be broken into manifold parts only by cutting at singularities.

- For n≥3, there are n-complexes that cannot be decomposed, even into pseudomanifold parts, only by cutting at singularities.[^o9]

:::

## Related definitions {#Related_definitions}

- A pseudomanifold is called *normal* if the link of each simplex with [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149) ≥ 2 is a pseudomanifold.

::: {#o13 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A [pinched torus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinched_torus) (see Figure 1) is an example of an orientable, [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_surface) 2-dimensional pseudomanifold.[^o6]

(Note that a pinched torus is not a normal pseudomanifold, since the link of a vertex is not connected.)

- Complex [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties) (even with singularities) are examples of pseudomanifolds.[^o8]

(Note that real algebraic varieties aren't always pseudomanifolds, since their singularities can be of codimension 1, take xy=0 for example.)

- [Thom spaces](wikipedia://en/1243451) of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) over triangulable [compact manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_manifold) are examples of pseudomanifolds.[^o8]
- Triangulable, compact, [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), [homology manifolds](wikipedia://en/7338342) over **Z** are examples of pseudomanifolds.[^o8]
- Complexes obtained gluing two 4-simplices at a common tetrahedron are a proper superset of 4-pseudomanifolds used in [spin foam](wikipedia://en/644488) formulation of [loop quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/152664).[^o14]
- Combinatorial n-complexes defined by gluing two *n*-simplexes at a *(n-1)*-face are not always n-pseudomanifolds. Gluing can induce non-pseudomanifoldness.[^o9]

:::
