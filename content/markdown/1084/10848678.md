# Mandeville's paradox

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mandeville's paradox"}

**Mandeville's paradox** is named after [Bernard Mandeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Mandeville) (1670–1733), who posits that actions which may be qualified as vicious with regard to individuals have benefits for society as a whole. This is alluded to in the subtitle of his most famous work, *[The Fable of The Bees: or, Private Vices, Public Benefits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fable_of_The_Bees:_or,_Private_Vices,_Public_Benefits)*. He states that "Fraud, Luxury, and Pride must live; Whilst we the Benefits receive."[^o2]

:::

The [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist) [Adam Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Smith) opposes this (although he defends a moderated version of this line of thought in his theory of the [invisible hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invisible_hand)), since Mandeville fails, in his opinion, to distinguish between vice and virtue.[^o3]
