# Typology (social science research method)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Typology"}

**Typology** is a [composite measure](wikipedia://en/2988563) that involves the classification of observations in terms of their [attributes](wikipedia://en/30423282) on multiple [variables](wikipedia://en/30423282).[^o2] Such classification is usually done on a [nominal scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominal_scale).[^o2] Typologies are used in both [qualitative](wikipedia://en/371299) and [quantitative research](wikipedia://en/389564).

:::

An example of a typology would be classification such as by age and health: young-healthy, young-sick, old-healthy, old-sick.

Typological theorizing is the development of theories about configurations of variables that constitute theoretical types.[^o3] According to Andrew Bennett and Alexander George, typological theories are useful "to address complex phenomena without oversimplifying, clarify similarities and differences among cases to facilitate comparisons, provide a comprehensive inventory of all possible kinds of cases, incorporate interactions effects, and draw attention to... kinds of cases that have not occurred."[^o3]
