# Minkowski–Steiner formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minkowski–Steiner formula"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Minkowski–Steiner formula** is a formula relating the [surface area](wikipedia://en/1209) and [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). More precisely, it defines the surface area as the "derivative" of enclosed volume in an appropriate sense.

:::

The Minkowski–Steiner formula is used, together with the [Brunn–Minkowski theorem](wikipedia://en/7500545), to prove the [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182). It is named after [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537) and [Jakob Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445).

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Statement of the Minkowski-Steiner formula"}

## Statement of the Minkowski-Steiner formula {#Statement_of_the_Minkowski-Steiner_formula}

Let $n \geq 2$, and let $A \subsetneq \mathbb{R}^{n}$ be a compact set. Let $\mu (A)$ denote the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) (volume) of $A$. Define the quantity $\lambda (\partial A)$ by the **Minkowski–Steiner formula**

$$\lambda (\partial A) := \liminf_{\delta \to 0} \frac{\mu \left( A + \overline{B_{\delta}} \right) - \mu (A)}{\delta},$$

where

$$\overline{B_{\delta}} := \left\{ x = (x_{1}, \dots, x_{n}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n} \left| | x | := \sqrt{x_{1}^{2} + \dots + x_{n}^{2}} \leq \delta \right. \right\}$$

denotes the [closed ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_ball) of [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) $\delta > 0$, and

$$A + \overline{B_{\delta}} := \left\{ a + b \in \mathbb{R}^{n} \left| a \in A, b \in \overline{B_{\delta}} \right. \right\}$$

is the [Minkowski sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_sum) of $A$ and $\overline{B_{\delta}}$, so that

$$A + \overline{B_{\delta}} = \left\{ x \in \mathbb{R}^{n} \mathrel|\ \mathopen| x - a \mathclose| \leq \delta \mbox{ for some } a \in A \right\}.$$

:::

## Remarks {#Remarks}

### Surface measure {#Surface_measure}

For "sufficiently regular" sets $A$, the quantity $\lambda (\partial A)$ does indeed correspond with the $(n - 1)$-dimensional measure of the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) $\partial A$ of $A$. See Federer (1969) for a full treatment of this problem.

### Convex sets {#Convex_sets}

When the set $A$ is a [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292), the [lim-inf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_inferior) above is a true [limit](wikipedia://en/285773), and one can show that

$$\mu \left( A + \overline{B_{\delta}} \right) = \mu (A) + \lambda (\partial A) \delta + \sum_{i = 2}^{n - 1} \lambda_{i} (A) \delta^{i} + \omega_{n} \delta^{n},$$

where the $\lambda_{i}$ are some [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) of $A$ (see [quermassintegrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quermassintegral)) and $\omega_{n}$ denotes the measure (volume) of the [unit ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ball) in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$:

$$\omega_{n} = \frac{2 \pi^{n / 2}}{n \Gamma (n / 2)},$$

where $\Gamma$ denotes the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316).

## Example: volume and surface area of a ball {#Example:_volume_and_surface_area_of_a_ball}

Taking $A = \overline{B_{R}}$ gives the following well-known formula for the surface area of the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) of radius $R$, $S_{R} := \partial B_{R}$:

$$\lambda (S_{R}) = \lim_{\delta \to 0} \frac{\mu \left( \overline{B_{R}} + \overline{B_{\delta}} \right) - \mu \left( \overline{B_{R}} \right)}{\delta}$$

$$= \lim_{\delta \to 0} \frac{[ (R + \delta)^{n} - R^{n} ] \omega_{n}}{\delta}$$

$$= n R^{n - 1} \omega_{n},$$

where $\omega_{n}$ is as above.
