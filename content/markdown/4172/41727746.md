# Octahedral pyramid

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Octahedral pyramid"}

In 4-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **octahedral pyramid** is bounded by one [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) on the base and 8 [triangular pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_pyramid) [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29) which meet at the apex. Since an octahedron has a circumradius divided by edge length less than one,[^o3] the triangular pyramids can be made with regular faces (as regular [tetrahedrons](wikipedia://en/30606)) by computing the appropriate height.

:::

Having all regular cells, it is a [Blind polytope](wikipedia://en/72252379). Two copies can be augmented to make an [octahedral bipyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedral_bipyramid) which is also a Blind polytope.

## Occurrences of the octahedral pyramid {#Occurrences_of_the_octahedral_pyramid}

The regular [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) has *octahedral pyramids* around every vertex, with the [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) passing through the center of the 16-cell. Therefore placing two regular octahedral pyramids base to base constructs a 16-cell. The 16-cell tessellates 4-dimensional space as the [16-cell honeycomb](wikipedia://en/9409435).

Exactly 24 regular octahedral pyramids will fit together around a vertex in four-dimensional space (the apex of each pyramid). This construction yields a [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485) with octahedral bounding cells, surrounding a central vertex with 24 edge-length long radii. The 4-dimensional content of a unit-edge-length 24-cell is 2, so the content of the regular octahedral pyramid is 1/12. The 24-cell tessellates 4-dimensional space as the [24-cell honeycomb](wikipedia://en/9481422).

The octahedral pyramid is the [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) for a [truncated 5-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_5-orthoplex), .

*Image: Truncated pentacross.png.*

The graph of the octahedral pyramid is the only possible minimal counterexample to [Negami's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negami's_conjecture), that the connected graphs with [planar covers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_cover) are themselves projective-planar.[^o5]

## Other polytopes {#Other_polytopes}

### Cubic pyramid {#Cubic_pyramid}

The dual to the octahedral pyramid is a [cubic pyramid](wikipedia://en/36537625), seen as a cubic base and 6 [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455) meeting at an [apex](wikipedia://en/6022079).

*Image: Cubic pyramid.png.*

Example 4-dimensional coordinates, 8 points in first 3 coordinates for cube and 4th dimension for the apex.

$$\begin{array}{lllr} (\pm 1, & \pm 1, & \pm 1; & 0) \\ (0, & 0, & 0; & 1) \end{array}$$

### Square-pyramidal pyramid {#Square-pyramidal_pyramid}

*Image: Square pyramid pyramid edgecenter.png.* Edge-centered view

The **square-pyramidal pyramid**, ( ) ∨ \[( ) ∨ {4}\], is a bisected octahedral pyramid. It has a [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455) base, and 4 [tetrahedrons](wikipedia://en/30606) along with another one more square pyramid meeting at the apex. It can also be seen in an edge-centered projection as a [square bipyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_bipyramid) with four tetrahedra wrapped around the common edge.
