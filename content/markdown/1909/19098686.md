# Data Desk

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Data Desk"}

**Data Desk** was a [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) program for visual [data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954), visual data exploration, and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685). It carried out [Exploratory Data Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploratory_Data_Analysis) (EDA) and standard statistical analyses by means of dynamically linked graphic data displays that instantly updated to reflect any change in the data.

:::

## History {#History}

**Data Desk** was developed in 1985 by [Paul F. Velleman](wikipedia://en/20485920), a statistics professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) who had studied [exploratory data analysis](wikipedia://en/416589) with [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974). Data Desk was released in 1986 for the [Macintosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macintosh). It provided most standard statistical methods accessed through its own desktop interface.

In 1997, Data Desk was released for [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows), and included a [General Linear Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Linear_Model) (GLM), [multivariate statistics](wikipedia://en/19384), and nonlinear curve fitting. DD/XL is an add-in for [Microsoft Excel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Excel) that adds Data Desk Functionality directly to the [Spreadsheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spreadsheet)

Data Desk's developer, [Data Description](http://www.datadesk.com/), pioneered linked graphic displays including a 3-D rotating plot and graphical slider control of parameters. It has also developed proprietary technology for computer-based multimedia instruction and currently provides contract data analysis services.

In an undated statement on their webside, Data Description announced that the company was being dissolved and all sales, trial downloads and upgrades had ceased.

## Reviews {#Reviews}

*[MacUser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacUser)* in November 1988 rated Data Desk Professional 4.5 mice out of 5. Approving of its features, user interface, and "rarely seen better" documentation, the magazine concluded "You can find less expensive packages, but ... you'll get what you pay for".[^o5]

[Macworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macworld) reviewed DD/XL on December 1, 2000 with a 4.5 out of 5.[^o6]

[InfoWorld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InfoWorld) reviewed Data Desk 6.0 and said "DataDesk Plus is by far the best Windows package for in-depth data exploration". Also, DataDesk Plus is easily the best Windows statistics package for teaching statistics" [^o7]

[Macworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macworld) reviewed Data Desk in October 1997, and gave it 9.1 out of 10, and a 5 star rating.
