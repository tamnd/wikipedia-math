# Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem"}

**Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem.**

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem**, named after [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495), [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980), and [Gustav Roch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Roch), is Hirzebruch's 1954 result generalizing the classical [Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/247261) on [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) to all complex [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties) of higher dimensions. The result paved the way for the [Grothendieck–Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck–Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch_theorem) proved about three years later.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem"}

## Statement of Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem {#Statement_of_Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch_theorem}

The Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem applies to any holomorphic [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) *E* on a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) *X*, to calculate the [holomorphic Euler characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_Euler_characteristic) of *E* in [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357), namely the alternating sum[^o5]

$$\chi(X,E) = \sum_{i=0}^{n} (-1)^{i} \dim_{\mathbb{C}} H^{i}(X,E)$$

of the dimensions as complex vector spaces, where *n* is the complex dimension of *X*.

Hirzebruch's theorem states that χ(*X*, *E*) is computable in terms of the [Chern classes](wikipedia://en/294349) *c<sub>k</sub>*(*E*) of *E*, and the [Todd classes](wikipedia://en/1568489) $\operatorname{td}_{j}(X)$ of the holomorphic [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of *X*. These all lie in the [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) of *X*; by use of the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) (or, in other words, integration over *X*) we can obtain numbers from classes in $H^{2n}(X).$ The Hirzebruch formula asserts that

$$\chi(X,E) = \sum_{j=0}^{n} \operatorname{ch}_{n-j}(E) \operatorname{td}_{j}(X),$$

using the [Chern character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern_character) ch(*E*) in cohomology. In other words, the products are formed in the cohomology ring of all the 'matching' degrees that add up to 2*n*. Formulated differently, it gives the equality[^o6]

$$\chi(X,E) = \int_X \operatorname{ch}(E) \operatorname{td}(X)$$

where $\operatorname{td}(X)$ is the [Todd class](wikipedia://en/1568489) of the tangent bundle of *X*.

Significant special cases are when *E* is a complex [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366), and when *X* is an [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311) (**Noether's formula**). Weil's Riemann–Roch theorem for vector bundles on curves, and the Riemann–Roch theorem for algebraic surfaces (see below), are included in its scope. The formula also expresses in a precise way the vague notion that the [Todd classes](wikipedia://en/1568489) are in some sense reciprocals of the [Chern Character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern_character).

:::

## Riemann Roch theorem for curves {#Riemann_Roch_theorem_for_curves}

For curves, the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem is essentially the classical [Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/247261). To see this, recall that for each [divisor](wikipedia://en/1693055) *D* on a curve there is an [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859) O(*D*) (which corresponds to a line bundle) such that the [linear system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_systems_of_divisors) of *D* is more or less the space of sections of O(*D*). For curves the Todd class is $1+c_1(T(X))/2,$ and the Chern character of a sheaf O(*D*) is just 1+*c*<sub>1</sub>(O(*D*)), so the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem states that

$h^0(\mathcal{O}(D)) - h^1(\mathcal{O}(D)) = c_1(\mathcal{O}(D)) +c_1(T(X))/2\ \ \$ (integrated over *X*).

But *h*<sup>0</sup>(O(*D*)) is just *l*(*D*), the dimension of the linear system of *D*, and by [Serre duality](wikipedia://en/359175) *h*<sup>1</sup>(O(*D*)) = *h*<sup>0</sup>(O(*K* − *D*)) = *l*(*K* − *D*) where *K* is the [canonical divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_divisor). Moreover, *c*<sub>1</sub>(O(*D*)) integrated over *X* is the degree of *D*, and *c*<sub>1</sub>(*T*(*X*)) integrated over *X* is the Euler class 2 − 2*g* of the curve *X*, where *g* is the genus. So we get the classical Riemann Roch theorem

$$\ell(D)-\ell(K-D) = \text{deg}(D)+1-g.$$

For vector bundles *V*, the Chern character is rank(*V*) + *c*<sub>1</sub>(*V*), so we get Weil's Riemann Roch theorem for vector bundles over curves:

$$h^0(V) - h^1(V) = c_1(V) + \operatorname{rank}(V)(1-g).$$

:::

## Riemann Roch theorem for surfaces {#Riemann_Roch_theorem_for_surfaces}

For surfaces, the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem is essentially the [Riemann–Roch theorem for surfaces](wikipedia://en/3144703)

$$\chi(D) = \chi(\mathcal{O}) + ((D.D)-(D.K))/2.$$

combined with the Noether formula.

If we want, we can use Serre duality to express *h*<sup>2</sup>(O(*D*)) as *h*<sup>0</sup>(O(*K* − *D*)), but unlike the case of curves there is in general no easy way to write the *h*<sup>1</sup>(O(*D*)) term in a form not involving sheaf cohomology (although in practice it often vanishes).

:::

## Asymptotic Riemann–Roch {#Asymptotic_Riemann–Roch}

Let *D* be a [Cartier divisor](wikipedia://en/1693055#Cartier_divisors) on an irreducible projective variety *X* of dimension *n*. Then

$$\chi \left (X,\mathcal O_X(mD) \right )=\frac{(D^n)}{n!}.m^n+O(m^{n-1}).$$

More generally, if $\mathcal F$ is any coherent sheaf on *X* then[^o12]

$$\chi \left (X,\mathcal F\otimes \mathcal O_X(mD) \right )=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal F)\frac{(D^n)}{n!}.m^n+O(m^{n-1}).$$

Moreover, if *D* is [ample](wikipedia://en/1186896), then [Serre vanishing](wikipedia://en/23535337#Vanishing_theorems) implies that[^o13]

$$h^0 \left (X,\mathcal F\otimes \mathcal O_X(mD) \right )=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal F)\frac{(D^n)}{n!}.m^n+O(m^{n-1}).$$
