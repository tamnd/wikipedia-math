# Simplicial commutative ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simplicial commutative ring"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), a **simplicial commutative ring** is a [commutative monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid_object) in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of [simplicial abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_abelian_group), or, equivalently, a [simplicial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_object) in the [category of commutative rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_commutative_rings). If *A* is a simplicial commutative ring, then it can be shown that $\pi_0 A$ is a [ring](wikipedia://en/61346) and $\pi_i A$ are [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over that ring (in fact, $\pi_* A$ is a [graded ring](wikipedia://en/103094) over $\pi_0 A$.)

:::

A [topology](wikipedia://en/29954)-counterpart of this notion is a [commutative ring spectrum](wikipedia://en/40777156).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The ring of [polynomial differential forms](wikipedia://en/54505172) on simplexes.

:::

## Graded ring structure {#Graded_ring_structure}

Let *A* be a simplicial commutative ring. Then the ring structure of *A* gives $\pi_* A = \oplus_{i \ge 0} \pi_i A$ the structure of a graded-commutative graded ring as follows.

By the [Dold–Kan correspondence](wikipedia://en/39431504), $\pi_* A$ is the homology of the [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) corresponding to *A*; in particular, it is a graded abelian group. Next, to multiply two elements, writing $S^1$ for the [simplicial circle](wikipedia://en/22359636), let $x:(S^1)^{\wedge i} \to A, \, \, y:(S^1)^{\wedge j} \to A$ be two maps. Then the composition

$$(S^1)^{\wedge i} \times (S^1)^{\wedge j} \to A \times A \to A,$$

the second map the multiplication of *A*, induces $(S^1)^{\wedge i} \wedge (S^1)^{\wedge j} \to A$. This in turn gives an element in $\pi_{i + j} A$. We have thus defined the graded multiplication $\pi_i A \times \pi_j A \to \pi_{i + j} A$. It is [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative) because the smash product is. It is [graded-commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded-commutative) (i.e., $xy = (-1)^{|x||y|} yx$) since the involution $S^1 \wedge S^1 \to S^1 \wedge S^1$ introduces a minus sign.

If *M* is a [simplicial module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_module) over *A* (that is, *M* is a [simplicial abelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_abelian_group) with an action of *A*), then the similar argument shows that $\pi_* M$ has the structure of a graded module over $\pi_* A$ (cf. [Module spectrum](wikipedia://en/41276205)).

## Spec {#Spec}

By definition, the category of affine [derived schemes](wikipedia://en/40776948) is the [opposite category](wikipedia://en/372399) of the category of simplicial commutative rings; an object corresponding to *A* will be denoted by $\operatorname{Spec} A$.
