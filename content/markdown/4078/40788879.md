# Kenji Fukaya

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kenji Fukaya"}

**Kenji Fukaya** (Japanese: 深谷賢治, *Fukaya Kenji*, born in 1959[^o3]) is a Japanese mathematician known for his work in [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298) and [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243).[^o4] His many fundamental contributions to mathematics include the discovery of the [Fukaya category](wikipedia://en/31598730). He was a permanent faculty member at the [Simons Center for Geometry and Physics](wikipedia://en/25568388) and a professor of mathematics at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University) before leaving for [Tsinghua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsinghua_University).[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: Fukaya seidel.jpg.* Kenji Fukaya (left) with [Paul Seidel](wikipedia://en/27678785), [Oberwolfach](wikipedia://en/1368669) 2002

Fukaya was both an undergraduate and a graduate student in mathematics at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo), receiving his BA in 1981, and his PhD in 1986. In 1987, he joined the University of Tokyo faculty as an associate professor. He then moved to [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University) as a full professor in 1994. In 2013, he then moved to the United States in order to join the faculty of the [Simons Center for Geometry and Physics](wikipedia://en/25568388) at Stony Brook.

The [Fukaya category](wikipedia://en/31598730), meaning the $A_\infty$ category of whose objects are Lagrangian submanifolds of a given [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356), is named after him, and is intimately related to [Floer homology](wikipedia://en/2672856). Other contributions to symplectic geometry include his proof (with [Kaoru Ono](wikipedia://en/58024023)) of a weak version of the [Arnold conjecture](wikipedia://en/72203783) and a construction of general [Gromov–Witten invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gromov–Witten_invariants). His many other mathematical contributions include important theorems in Riemannian geometry and work on physics-related topics such as gauge theory and mirror symmetry.

Fukaya was awarded the [Japanese Mathematical Society's](wikipedia://en/18376786) [Geometry Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_Prize) in 1989[^o7] and [Spring Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_Prize) in 1994.[^o8] He also received the [Inoue Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inoue_Prize) in 2002, the [Japan Academy Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy_Prize_%28academics%29) in 2003,[^o9] the [Asahi Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahi_Prize) in 2009,[^o10] and the [Fujihara Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujihara_Award) in 2012.[^o11] He has served on the governing board of the Japanese Mathematical Society and on the Mathematical Committee of the Science Council of Japan. In 2025 he was awarded the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) in Mathematics.[^o12]

Fukaya was an invited speaker at the 1990 International Congress of Mathematicians in Kyoto, where he gave a talk entitled, *Collapsing Riemannian Manifolds and its Applications.*[^o13]
