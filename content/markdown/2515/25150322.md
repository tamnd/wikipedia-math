# Orthogonal diagonalization

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthogonal diagonalization"}

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), an **orthogonal diagonalization** of a [normal matrix](wikipedia://en/170366) (e.g. a [symmetric matrix](wikipedia://en/126474)) is a [diagonalization](wikipedia://en/246325) by means of an [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/105620) change of coordinates.[^o2]

:::

The following is an orthogonal diagonalization algorithm that diagonalizes a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) *q*(*x*) on **R**<sup>*n*</sup> by means of an orthogonal change of coordinates *X* = *PY*.[^o3]

- Step 1: Find the symmetric matrix *A* that represents *q* and find its [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) Δ().
- Step 2: Find the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of *A*, which are the [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_polynomial) of Δ().
- Step 3: For each eigenvalue *λ* of *A* from step 2, find an [orthogonal basis](wikipedia://en/755400) of its [eigenspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenspace).
- Step 4: Normalize all eigenvectors in step 3, which then form an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) of **R**<sup>*n*</sup>.
- Step 5: Let *P* be the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) whose columns are the normalized [eigenvectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector) in step 4.

Then *X* = *PY* is the required orthogonal change of coordinates, and the diagonal entries of *P*<sup>T</sup>*A**P* will be the eigenvalues *λ*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *λ*<sub>*n*</sub> that correspond to the columns of *P*.

Such decomposition exists by the [spectral theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_Theorem#Normal_matrices).
