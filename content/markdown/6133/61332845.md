# Melody Chan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Melody Chan"}

**Melody Tung Chan** is an American mathematician and violinist who works as Associate Professor of Mathematics at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University). She is a winner of the [Alice T. Schafer Prize](wikipedia://en/57181252) and of the [AWM–Microsoft Research Prize in Algebra and Number Theory](wikipedia://en/59776675). Her research involves [combinatorial commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/11503412), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), and [tropical geometry](wikipedia://en/867041).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Chan was inspired to become a violinist as a pre-schooler, seeing [Yo-Yo Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yo-Yo_Ma) on [Sesame Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_Street). As a freshman at [Scarsdale High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarsdale_High_School) in [Scarsdale, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarsdale,_New_York), she became the youngest first place winner of the 1997 Young Artists Competition of the [Sarah Lawrence College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Lawrence_College) chamber orchestra. She studied violin at [Juilliard School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juilliard_School) with [Itzhak Perlman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itzhak_Perlman) and [Dorothy DeLay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_DeLay) from 2000 to 2001. In 2002, she played a [Vivaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Vivaldi) concerto for four violins alongside Perlman in a performance at the [Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts) that was broadcast on [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS).

She then majored in computer science and mathematics at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). At Yale she played violin in the Yale Symphony Orchestra, won a [Barry M. Goldwater Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_M._Goldwater_Scholarship) and the university's Hart Lyman Prize for best junior student, and became vice president of the local chapter of [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa). She graduated *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honors)* from Yale in 2005.

After studying for the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) from 2005 to 2006, Chan worked with [Paul Seymour](wikipedia://en/1575708) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), completing a master's degree there in 2008. She completed her doctorate in 2012 at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). Her dissertation, *Tropical curves and metric graphs*, was supervised by [Bernd Sturmfels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernd_Sturmfels).

Chan conducted postdoctoral research at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) from 2012 to 2015, and then joined Brown as Manning Assistant Professor in 2015.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

As an undergraduate at Yale, Chan won the 2005 [Alice T. Schafer Prize](wikipedia://en/57181252) for her undergraduate research, which resulted in three published papers on [distinguishing colorings](wikipedia://en/47480525) of [Cayley graphs](wikipedia://en/358277).

Chan was given a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) in 2018. She is the 2020 winner of the [AWM–Microsoft Research Prize in Algebra and Number Theory](wikipedia://en/59776675), "in recognition of Chan’s advances at the interface between algebraic geometry and combinatorics", including "an astounding result" on the [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609), "foundational work on the moduli of metric graphs and tropical curves", and "beautiful new results on the expected number of turns in a random Young tableau".

She was named a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), in the 2022 class of fellows, "for contributions to research at the interface of algebraic geometry and combinatorics, and to mentorship and mathematical exposition".
