# Jean Écalle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean Écalle"}

**Jean Écalle** (born 1947) is a French mathematician, specializing in dynamic systems, perturbation theory, and analysis.

:::

Écalle received, in 1974 from the [University of Paris-Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris-Saclay) in [Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orsay), a doctorate under the supervision of Hubert Delange with Thèse d'État entitled *La théorie des invariants holomorphes*. He is a *directeur de recherche* (senior researcher) of the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) (CNRS) and is a professor at the University of Paris-Saclay.

He developed a theory of so-called "[resurgent functions](wikipedia://en/74683745)", analytic functions with isolated singularities, which have a special algebra of derivatives (*Alien calculus*, *Calcul différentiel étranger*). "Resurgent functions" are divergent [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) whose Borel transforms converge in a neighborhood of the origin and give rise, by means of analytic continuation, to (usually) multi-valued functions, but these multi-valued functions have merely isolated singularities without singularities that form cuts with dimension one or greater.[^o3][^o4][^o5] Écalle's theory has important applications to solutions of generalizations of [Abel's integral equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel's_integral_equation); the method of resurgent functions provides for such solutions a (Borel) resummation method for dealing with divergent series arising from semiclassical asymptotic developments in quantum theory.[^o6]

He applied his theory to dynamic systems [^o7] and to the interplay between diophantine small denominators and resonance involved in problems of [germs](wikipedia://en/352714) of [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641).[^o8]

Independently of [Yulij Ilyashenko](wikipedia://en/56631455) he proved that the number of [limit cycles](wikipedia://en/452950) of polynomial vector fields in the plane is finite, which [Henri Dulac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Dulac) had already tried to prove in 1923. This result is related to [Hilbert's sixteenth problem](wikipedia://en/381750).

In 1988 Écalle was the inaugural recipient of the Prix Mergier-Bourdeix of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences). He was in 1990 an Invited Speaker at [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto).[^o9]
