# Rita Zemach

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rita Zemach"}

**Rita B. Zemach** (née Dresner; April 3, 1926 – June 8, 2015) was an American statistician who worked for the [Michigan Department of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Department_of_Health_and_Human_Services), and helped promote women in statistics.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Rita Dresner started her undergraduate studies at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), but transferred to [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College) in her junior year. At Barnard, she became a member of the editorial staff of the *Barnard Bulletin*, the school newspaper. She graduated from Barnard in 1947.

## Statistics career {#Statistics_career}

As Rita Zemach, she became an elected member of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1961, and earned her Ph.D. in statistics in 1965 from [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University). Her dissertation, supervised by [Esther Seiden](wikipedia://en/55791412), was *On Orthogonal Arrays of Strength Four and Their Applications*. She later published this work, "the first significant progress on [orthogonal arrays](wikipedia://en/17887653) of strength 4", with Seiden in the *[Annals of Mathematical Statistics](wikipedia://en/23772805)*.

Her later research concerned more applied areas of statistics, in health and [resource allocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_allocation). By 1979, she had become chief of statistics research and education for the Michigan Department of Public Health.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Zemach was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 1987.
