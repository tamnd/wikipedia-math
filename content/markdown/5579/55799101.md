# Glossary of representation theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glossary of representation theory"}

This is a **glossary of [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200)** in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).

:::

The term "module" is often used synonymously for a representation; for the module-theoretic terminology, see also [glossary of module theory](wikipedia://en/25334192).

See also [Glossary of Lie groups and Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/62157107), [list of representation theory topics](wikipedia://en/19397596) and .

**Notations**: We write $\mathbb{G}_m = GL_1$. Thus, for example, a one-representation (i.e., a character) of a group *G* is of the form $\chi: G \to \mathbb{G}_m$.

## A {#A}

::: {#o3 .entry cue=term name="Adams"}

**Adams**

[Adams operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams_operations).

:::

::: {#o4 .entry cue=term name="adjoint"}

**adjoint**

The [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) of a Lie group *G* is the representation given by the adjoint action of *G* on the Lie algebra of *G* (an adjoint action is obtained, roughly, by differentiating a conjugation action.)

:::

::: {#o5 .entry cue=term name="admissible"}

**admissible**

A representation of a real reductive group is called [admissible](wikipedia://en/11055227) if (1) a maximal compact subgroup *K* acts as unitary operators and (2) each irreducible representation of *K* has finite multiplicity.

:::

::: {#o6 .entry cue=term name="alternating"}

**alternating**

The [alternating square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_and_alternating_squares) of a representation *V* is a subrepresentation $\operatorname{Alt}^2(V)$ of the second [tensor power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_power) $V^{\otimes 2}=V\otimes V$.

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=term name="Artin"}

**Artin**

[Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351).

[Artin's theorem on induced characters](wikipedia://en/63001204) states that a character on a finite group is a rational linear combination of characters induced from cyclic subgroups.

[Artin representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_representation) is used in the definition of the [Artin conductor](wikipedia://en/34170016).

:::

::: {#o8 .entry cue=term name="automorphic"}

**automorphic**

[automorphic representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation)

:::

## B {#B}

::: {#o10 .entry cue=term name="Borel–Weil–Bott theorem"}

**Borel–Weil–Bott theorem**

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, the [Borel–Weil–Bott theorem](wikipedia://en/725331) realizes an irreducible representation of a [reductive algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_algebraic_group) as the space of the global sections of a line bundle on a flag variety. (In the positive characteristic case, the construction only produces [Weyl modules](wikipedia://en/32084687), which may not be irreducible.)

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=term name="branching"}

**branching**

[branching rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branching_rule)

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=term name="Brauer"}

**Brauer**

[Brauer's theorem on induced characters](wikipedia://en/3053436) states that a character on a finite group is a linear combination with integer coefficients of characters induced from elementary subgroups.

:::

## C {#C}

::: {#o14 .entry cue=term name="Cartan–Weyl theory"}

**Cartan–Weyl theory**

Another name for the [representation theory of semisimple Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/62881966).

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=term name="Casimir element"}

**Casimir element**

A [Casimir element](wikipedia://en/513672) is a distinguished element of the center of the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra.

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=term name="category of representations"}

**category of representations**

Representations and equivariant maps between them form a [category of representations](wikipedia://en/55872661).

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=term name="character"}

**character**

A [character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_%28representation_theory%29) is a one-dimensional representation.

The character of a finite-dimensional representation π is the function $g \mapsto \operatorname{tr} \pi(g)$. In other words, it is the composition $G \overset{\pi}\to GL(V) \overset{\operatorname{tr}}\to \mathbb{G}_m$.

An [irreducible character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_character) (resp. a [trivial character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_character)) is the character of an irreducible representation (resp. a trivial representation).

The [character group](wikipedia://en/1384568) of a group *G* is the group of all characters on *G*; namely, $\operatorname{Hom}(G, \mathbb{G}_m)$.

The [character ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_ring) is the group ring (over the integers) of the character group of *G*.

A virtual character is an element of a character ring.

A [distributional character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributional_character) may be defined for an infinite-dimensional representation.

An [infinitesimal character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_character).

:::

::: {#o18 .entry cue=term name="Chevalley"}

**Chevalley**

Chevalley

[Chevalley generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalley_generators)

[Chevalley group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalley_group).

[Chevalley's restriction theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalley's_restriction_theorem).

:::

::: {#o19 .entry cue=term name="class function"}

**class function**

A [class function](wikipedia://en/1001329) *f* on a group *G* is a function such that $f(g) = f(h g h^{-1})$; it is a function on conjugacy classes.

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=term name="cluster algebra"}

**cluster algebra**

A [cluster algebra](wikipedia://en/27038423) is an integral domain with some combinatorial structure on the generators, introduced in an attempt to systematize the notion of a [dual canonical basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_canonical_basis).

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=term name="coadjoint"}

**coadjoint**

A [coadjoint representation](wikipedia://en/3226011) is the dual representation of an adjoint representation.

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=term name="complete"}

**complete**

“completely reducible" is another term for "semisimple".

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=term name="complex"}

**complex**

A [complex representation](wikipedia://en/695060) is a representation of *G* on a complex vector space. Many authors refer complex representations simply as representations.

The [complex-conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex-conjugate) $\overline{V}$ of a complex representation *V* is the representation with the same underlying additive group *V* with the linear action of *G* but with the action of a complex number through complex conjugation.

A complex representation is self-conjugate if it is isomorphic to its complex conjugate.

:::

::: {#o24 .entry cue=term name="complementary"}

**complementary**

A complementary representation to a subrepresentation *W* of a representation *V* is a representation *W'* such that *V* is the direct sum of *W* and *W'*.

:::

::: {#o25 .entry cue=term name="cuspidal"}

**cuspidal**

[cuspidal representation](wikipedia://en/6043458)

:::

::: {#o26 .entry cue=term name="crystal"}

**crystal**

[crystal basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_basis)

:::

::: {#o27 .entry cue=term name="cyclic"}

**cyclic**

A cyclic *G*-module is a *G*-module generated by a single vector. For example, an irreducible representation is necessarily cyclic.

:::

## D {#D}

::: {#o29 .entry cue=term name="Dedekind"}

**Dedekind**

[Dedekind's theorem on linear independence of characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind's_theorem_on_linear_independence_of_characters).

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=term name="defined over"}

**defined over**

Given a field extension $K/F$, a representation *V* of a group *G* over *K* is said to be [defined over *F*](wikipedia://en/9213667) if $V \simeq V_0 \otimes_F K$ for some representation $V_0$ over *F* such that $g: V \to V$ is induced by $g: V_0 \to V_0$; i.e., $g \cdot (v \otimes \lambda) = g v \otimes \lambda$. Here, $V_0$ is called an *F*-form of *V* (and is not necessarily unique).

:::

::: {#o31 .entry cue=term name="Demazure"}

**Demazure**

[Demazure's character formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demazure's_character_formula)

:::

::: {#o32 .entry cue=term name="direct sum"}

**direct sum**

The [direct sum of representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_representations) *V*, *W* is a representation that is the direct sum $V \oplus W$ of the vector spaces together with the linear group action $\pi_{V \oplus W}(g)(v + w) = \pi_V(g)v + \pi_W(g)w$.

:::

::: {#o33 .entry cue=term name="discrete"}

**discrete**

An irreducible representation of a Lie group *G* is said to be in the [discrete series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_series) if the matrix coefficients of it are all square integrable. For example, if *G* is compact, then every irreducible representation of it is in the discrete series.

:::

::: {#o34 .entry cue=term name="dominant"}

**dominant**

The irreducible representations of a simply-connected compact Lie group are indexed by their highest weight. These *dominant weights* form the lattice points in an orthant in the weight lattice of the Lie group.

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=term name="dual"}

**dual**

The [dual representation](wikipedia://en/1442471) (or the contragredient representation) of a representation *V* is a representation that is the dual vector space $V^* = \operatorname{Hom}(V, k)$ together with the linear group action that preserves the natural pairing $V^* \times V \to k$

A [dual canonical basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_canonical_basis) is a dual of [Lusztig's canonical basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusztig's_canonical_basis).

:::

## E {#E}

::: {#o37 .entry cue=term name="Eisenstein"}

**Eisenstein**

[Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/1270458)

:::

::: {#o38 .entry cue=term name="equivariant"}

**equivariant**

The term “*G*-equivariant” is another term for “*G*-linear”.

:::

::: {#o39 .entry cue=term name="exterior"}

**exterior**

An [exterior power of a representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_power_of_a_representation) *V* is a representation $\wedge^n(V)$ with the group action induced by $V^{\otimes n} \to \wedge^n(V)$.

:::

## F {#F}

::: {#o41 .entry cue=term name="faithful"}

**faithful**

A [faithful representation](wikipedia://en/2617872) $\pi: G \to GL(V)$ is a representation such that $\pi$ is [injective](wikipedia://en/45196) as a function.

:::

::: {#o42 .entry cue=term name="fiber functor"}

**fiber functor**

[fiber functor](wikipedia://en/41040671).

:::

::: {#o43 .entry cue=term name="Frobenius reciprocity"}

**Frobenius reciprocity**

The [Frobenius reciprocity](wikipedia://en/55690071) states that for each representation $\sigma$ of *H* and representation $\pi$ of *G* there is a bijection

$\operatorname{Hom}_G(\pi, \operatorname{Ind}_H^G \sigma) = \operatorname{Hom}_H(\pi|_H, \sigma)$ that is natural in the sense that $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G$ is the right [adjoint functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor) to the restriction functor $\pi \mapsto \pi|_H$.

:::

::: {#o44 .entry cue=term name="fundamental"}

**fundamental**

[Fundamental representation](wikipedia://en/649743): For the irreducible representations of a simply-connected [compact Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Lie_group) there exists a set of *fundamental weights*, indexed by the vertices of the [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109) of G, such that *dominant weights* are simply non-negative integer linear combinations of the fundamental weights.

The corresponding irreducible representations are the *fundamental representations* of the Lie group. In particular, from the expansion of a dominant weight in terms of the fundamental weights, one can take a corresponding [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of the fundamental representations and extract one copy of the irreducible representation corresponding to that dominant weight.

In the case of the [special unitary group](wikipedia://en/173997) *SU*(*n*), the *n* − 1 fundamental representations are the wedge products

$$Alt^k\ {\mathbb C}^n$$

consisting of [alternating tensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_tensor), for k=1,2,...,n-1.

:::

## G {#G}

::: {#o46 .entry cue=term name="G-linear"}

**G*-linear**

A [*G*-linear map](wikipedia://en/422711) $f: V \to W$ between representations is a linear transformation that commutes with the *G*-actions; i.e., $f \circ \pi_V(g) = \pi_W(g) \circ f$ for every *g* in *G*.

:::

::: {#o47 .entry cue=term name="G-module"}

**G*-module**

Another name for a representation. It allows for the module-theoretic terminology: e.g., trivial *G*-module, *G*-submodules, etc.

:::

::: {#o48 .entry cue=term name="G-equivariant vector bundle"}

**G*-equivariant vector bundle**

A [*G*-equivariant vector bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant_vector_bundle) is a vector bundle $p: E \to X$ on a *G*-space *X* together with a *G*-action on *E* (say right) such that $g: p^{-1}(x) \to p^{-1}(xg)$ is a well-defined linear map.

:::

::: {#o49 .entry cue=term name="Galois"}

**Galois**

[Galois representation](wikipedia://en/486929).

:::

::: {#o50 .entry cue=term name="good"}

**good**

A [good filtration](wikipedia://en/37479627) of a representation of a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) *G* is a filtration such that the quotients are isomorphic to $H^0(\lambda) = \Gamma(G/B, L_\lambda)$ where $L_\lambda$ are the line bundles on the flag variety $G/B$.

:::

## H {#H}

::: {#o52 .entry cue=term name="Harish-Chandra"}

**Harish-Chandra**

[Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190) (11 October 1923 – 16 October 1983), an Indian American mathematician.

The [Harish-Chandra Plancherel theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harish-Chandra_Plancherel_theorem).

:::

::: {#o53 .entry cue=term name="highest weight"}

**highest weight**

Given a complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ and a choice of a [positive Weyl chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_Weyl_chamber), the [highest weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight) of a representation of $\mathfrak{g}$ is the weight of an $\mathfrak{h}$-weight vector *v* such that $E_{\alpha} v = 0$ for every positive root $\alpha$ (*v* is called the highest weight vector).

The [theorem of the highest weight](wikipedia://en/58092933) states (1) two finite-dimensional irreducible representations of $\mathfrak{g}$ are isomorphic if and only if they have the same highest weight and (2) for each dominant integral $\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*$, there is a finite-dimensional irreducible representation having $\lambda$ as its highest weight.

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=term name="Hom"}

**Hom**

The [Hom representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hom_representation) $\operatorname{Hom}(V, W)$ of representations *V*, *W* is a representation with the group action obtained by the vector space identification $\operatorname{Hom}(V, W) = V^* \otimes W$.

:::

## I {#I}

::: {#o56 .entry cue=term name="indecomposable"}

**indecomposable**

An [indecomposable representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indecomposable_representation) is a representation that is not a direct sum of at least two proper subrepresebtations.

:::

::: {#o57 .entry cue=term name="induction"}

**induction**

Given a representation $(\sigma, W)$ of a subgroup *H* of a group *G*, the [induced representation](wikipedia://en/357452)

$\operatorname{Ind}_H^G(\sigma) = \{ f: G \to W | f(hg) = \sigma(h) f(g) \}$ is a representation of *G* that is induced on the *H*-linear functions $f: G \to W$; cf. #Frobenius reciprocity.

Depending on applications, it is common to impose further conditions on the functions $f: G \to W$; for example, if the functions are required to be compactly supported, then the resulting induction is called the [compact induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_induction).

:::

::: {#o58 .entry cue=term name="infinitesimally"}

**infinitesimally**

Two admissible representations of a real reductive group are said to be [infinitesimally equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimally_equivalent) if their associated Lie algebra representations on the space of *K*-finite vectors are isomorphic.

:::

::: {#o59 .entry cue=term name="integrable"}

**integrable**

A representation of a [Kac–Moody algebra](wikipedia://en/1146292) is said to be [integrable](wikipedia://en/64905081) if (1) it is a sum of weight spaces and (2) Chevalley generators $e_i, f_i$ are [locally nilpotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_nilpotent_endomorphism).

:::

::: {#o60 .entry cue=term name="intertwining"}

**intertwining**

The term "[intertwining operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertwining_operator)" is an old name for a *G*-linear map between representations.

:::

::: {#o61 .entry cue=term name="involution"}

**involution**

An [involution representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Involution_representation) is a representation of a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) on a Hilbert space that preserves involution.

:::

::: {#o62 .entry cue=term name="irreducible"}

**irreducible**

An [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982) is a representation whose only subrepresentations are zero and itself. The term "irreducible" is synonymous with "simple".

:::

::: {#o63 .entry cue=term name="isomorphism"}

**isomorphism**

An isomorphism between representations of a group *G* is an invertible *G*-linear map between the representations.

:::

::: {#o64 .entry cue=term name="isotypic"}

**isotypic**

Given a representation *V* and a simple representation *W* (subrepresebtation or otherwise), the [isotypic component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotypic_component) of *V* of type *W* is the direct sum of all subrepresentations of *V* that are isomorphic to *W*. For example, let *A* be a ring and *G* a group acting on it as automorphisms. If *A* is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/55823265) as a *G*-module, then the [ring of invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_invariants) $A^G$ is the isotypic component of *A* of trivial type.

The [isotypic decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotypic_decomposition) of a semisimple representation is the decomposition into the isotypic components.

:::

## L {#L}

::: {#o72 .entry cue=term name="lattice"}

**lattice**

The [root lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_lattice) is the free abelian group generated by the roots.

The [weight lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_lattice) is the group of all linear functionals $\chi \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ on a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ that are integral: $\chi(H_{\alpha})$ is an integer for every root $\alpha$.

## M {#M}

::: {#o75 .entry cue=term name="Maschke's theorem"}

**Maschke's theorem**

[Maschke's theorem](wikipedia://en/30876007) states that a finite-dimensional representation over a field *F* of a finite group *G* is a [semisimple representation](wikipedia://en/55823265) if the characteristic of *F* does not divide the order of *G*.

:::

::: {#o76 .entry cue=term name="Mackey theory"}

**Mackey theory**

The [Mackey theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackey_theory) may be thought of a tool to answer the question: given a representation *W* of a subgroup *H* of a group *G*, when is the induced representation $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W$ an irreducible representation of *G*?[^o77]

:::

::: {#o78 .entry cue=term name="Maass–Selberg"}

**Maass–Selberg**

[Maass–Selberg relations](wikipedia://en/33188525).

:::

::: {#o79 .entry cue=term name="matrix coefficient"}

**matrix coefficient**

A [matrix coefficient](wikipedia://en/11017946) of a representation $\pi: G \to GL(V)$ is a linear combination of functions on *G* of the form $g \mapsto \langle \pi(g) v, \alpha \rangle$ for *v* in *V* and $\alpha$ in the dual space $V^*$. Note the notion makes sense for any group: if *G* is a topological group and $\pi$ is continuous, then a matrix matrix coefficient would be a continuous function on *G*. If *G* and $\pi$ are algebraic, it would be a [regular function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_function) on *G*.

:::

::: {#o80 .entry cue=term name="modular"}

**modular**

The [modular representation theory](wikipedia://en/1110685).

:::

::: {#o81 .entry cue=term name="Molien"}

**Molien**

Given a finite-dimensional complex representation *V* of a finite group *G*, [Molien's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molien's_theorem) says that the series $\sum_{n = 0}^{\infty} \dim (\mathbb{C}[V]^G)_n t^n$, where $(\mathbb{C}[V]^G)_n$ denotes the space of $G$-invariant homogeneous polynomials on *V* of degree *n*, coincides with $(\# G)^{-1} \sum_{g \in G} \det(1 - t g |V)^{-1}$. The theorem is also valid for a reductive group by replacing $(\# G)^{-1} \sum_{g \in G}$ by integration over a maximal compact subgroup.

:::

## P {#P}

::: {#o86 .entry cue=term name="Peter–Weyl"}

**Peter–Weyl**

The [Peter–Weyl theorem](wikipedia://en/294474) states that the linear span of the [matrix coefficients](wikipedia://en/11017946) on a compact group *G* is dense in $L^2(G)$.

:::

::: {#o87 .entry cue=term name="permutation"}

**permutation**

Given a group *G*, a *G*-set *X* and *V* the vector space of functions from *X* to a fixed field, a [permutation representation](wikipedia://en/59487764) $\pi$ of *G* on *V* is a representation given by the induced action of *G* on *V*; i.e., $(\pi(g)v)(x) = v(g^{-1} x)$. For example, if *X* is a finite set and *V* is viewed as a vector space with a basis parameteized by *X*, then the symmetric group $G = \operatorname{Sym}(X)$ permutates the elements of the basis and its linear extension is precisely the permutation representation.

:::

::: {#o88 .entry cue=term name="Plancherel"}

**Plancherel**

[Plancherel formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plancherel_formula)

:::

::: {#o89 .entry cue=term name="positive-energy representation"}

**positive-energy representation**

[positive-energy representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-energy_representation).

:::

::: {#o90 .entry cue=term name="primitive"}

**primitive**

The term "primitive element" (or a vector) is an old term for a Borel-weight vector.

:::

::: {#o91 .entry cue=term name="projective"}

**projective**

A [projective representation](wikipedia://en/348860) of a group *G* is a group homomorphism $\pi: G \to PGL(V) = GL(V)/\mathbb{G}_m$. Since $PGL(V) = \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}(V))$, a projective representation is precisely a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of *G* on $\mathbb{P}(V)$ as automorphisms.

:::

::: {#o92 .entry cue=term name="proper"}

**proper**

A proper subrepresentation of a representation *V* is a subrepresentstion that is not *V*.

:::

## Q {#Q}

::: {#o94 .entry cue=term name="quotient"}

**quotient**

Given a representation *V* and a subrepresentation $W \subset V$, the quotient representation is the representation $(\pi_{V/W}, V/W)$ given by $\pi_{V/W}(g): V/W \to V/W, \, v + W \mapsto gv + W$.

:::

::: {#o95 .entry cue=term name="quaternionic"}

**quaternionic**

A [quaternionic representation](wikipedia://en/695046) of a group *G* is a [complex representation](wikipedia://en/695060) equipped with a *G*-invariant [quaternionic structure](wikipedia://en/39227817).

:::

::: {#o96 .entry cue=term name="quiver"}

**quiver**

A [quiver](wikipedia://en/355100), by definition, is a directed graph. But one typically studies representations of a quiver.

:::

## R {#R}

::: {#o98 .entry cue=term name="rational"}

**rational**

A representation *V* is [rational](wikipedia://en/5784135) if each vector *v* in *V* is contained in some finite-dimensional subrepresentation (depending on *v*.)

:::

::: {#o99 .entry cue=term name="real"}

**real**

A [real representation](wikipedia://en/695101) of a vector space is a representation on a real vector space.

A real character is a character $\chi$ of a group $G$ such that $\chi(g)\in\mathbb{R}$ for all $g$ in $G$.[^o100]

:::

::: {#o101 .entry cue=term name="regular"}

**regular**

A [regular representation](wikipedia://en/360011) of a finite group *G* is the induced representation of *G* on the [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) over a field of *G*.

A regular representation of a [linear algebraic group](wikipedia://en/395885) *G* is the induced representation on the coordinate ring of *G*. See also: [representation on coordinate rings](wikipedia://en/43165864).

::: {#o102 .entry cue=term name="representation"}

**representation**

Representation theory is simple to define: it is the study of the ways in which a given group may act on vector spaces. It is almost certainly unique, however, among such clearly delineated subjects, in the breadth of its interest to mathematicians. This is not surprising: group actions are ubiquitous in 20th century mathematics, and where the object on which a group acts is not a vector space, we have learned to replace it by one that is (e.g., a cohomology group, tangent space, etc.). As a consequence, many mathematicians other than specialists in the field (or even those who think they might want to be) come in contact with the subject in various ways.

A [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) of a group *G* is a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) $\pi: G \to GL(V)$ from *G* to the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) $GL(V)$. Depending on the group *G*, the homomorphism $\pi$ is often implicitly required to be a morphishm in a category to which *G* belongs; e.g., if *G* is a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315), then $\pi$ must be continuous. The adjective “linear” is often omitted.

Equivalently, a linear representation is a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of *G* on a vector space *V* that is linear: the action $\sigma: G \times V \to V$ such that for each *g* in *G*, $\sigma_g: V \to V, v \mapsto \sigma(g, v)$ is a linear transformation.

A [virtual representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_representation) is an element of the Grothendieck ring of the category of representations.

:::

## S {#S}

**Schur**

*Image: Schur.jpg.* Issai Schur

[Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522)

[Schur's lemma](wikipedia://en/521696) states that a *G*-linear map between irreducible representations must be either bijective or zero.

The [Schur orthogonality relations](wikipedia://en/7947411) on a compact group says the characters of non-isomorphic irreducible representations are orthogonal to each other.

The [Schur functor](wikipedia://en/11597641) $V \mapsto S^{\lambda}(V)$ constructs representations such as symmetric powers or exterior powers according to a partition $\lambda$. The characters of $S^\lambda(V)$ are [Schur polynomials](wikipedia://en/3147062).

The [Schur–Weyl duality](wikipedia://en/15291970) computes the irreducible representations occurring in tensor powers of $GL(V)$-modules.

A [Schur polynomial](wikipedia://en/3147062) is a [symmetric function](wikipedia://en/22510359), of a type occurring in the Weyl character formula applied to unitary groups.

[Schur index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur_index).

A [Schur complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur_complex).

**smooth**

A [smooth representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_representation) of a [locally profinite group](wikipedia://en/32286640) *G* is a complex representation such that, for each *v* in *V*, there is some compact open subgroup *K* of *G* that fixes *v*; i.e., $g \cdot v = v$ for every *g* in *K*.

A [smooth vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_vector_%28representation_theory%29) in a representation space of a Lie group is a vector *v* such that $g \mapsto g \cdot v$ is a smooth function.

**symmetric**

A [symmetric power of a representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_power_of_a_representation) *V* is a representation $\operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$ with the group action induced by $V^{\otimes n} \to \operatorname{Sym}^n(V)$.

In particular, the [symmetric square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_and_alternating_squares) of a representation *V* is a representation $\operatorname{Sym}^2(V)$ with the group action induced by $V^{\otimes 2} \to \operatorname{Sym}^2(V)$.

## U {#U}

::: {#o122 .entry cue=term name="uniformly bounded"}

**uniformly bounded**

A [uniformly bounded representation](wikipedia://en/36186626) of a [locally compact group](wikipedia://en/1651204) is a representation in the algebra of bounded operators that is continuous in [strong operator topology](wikipedia://en/458699) and that is such that the norm of the operator given by each group element is uniformly bounded.

::: {#o123 .entry cue=term name="unitary"}

**unitary**

A [unitary representation](wikipedia://en/292788) of a group *G* is a representation π such that π(*g*) is a [unitary operator](wikipedia://en/225999) for every *g* in *G*.

A [unitarizable representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarizable_representation) is a representation equivalent to a unitary representation.

## V {#V}

::: {#o125 .entry cue=term name="Verma module"}

**Verma module**

Given a complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ and a choice of a [positive Weyl chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_Weyl_chamber), the [Verma module](wikipedia://en/2975234) $M_{\chi}$ associated to a linear functional $\chi: \mathfrak{h} \to \mathbb{C}$ is the quotient of the enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak{g})$ by the left ideal generated by $E_{\alpha}$ for all positive roots $\alpha$ as well as $H - \chi(H) 1$ for all $H \in \mathfrak{h}$.[^o126]

## W {#W}

::: {#o128 .entry cue=term name="weight"}

**weight**

The term "weight" is another name for a character.

The [weight subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_subspace) of a representation *V* of a weight $\chi: G \to \mathbb{G}_m$ is the subspace $V_{\chi} = \{ v \in V | g \cdot v = \chi(g) v \}$ that has positive dimension.

Similarly, for a linear functional $\chi: \mathfrak{h} \to \mathbb{C}$ of a complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak{h}$, $\chi$ is a weight of an $\mathfrak{h}$-module *V* if $V_{\chi} = \{ v \in V | H \cdot v = \chi(H)v \}$ has positive dimension; cf. #highest weight.

weight lattice

dominant weight: a weight \\lambda is dominant if $<\lambda, \alpha> \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ for some $\alpha \in \Phi$

fundamental dominant weight: : Given a set of simple roots $\Delta = \{ \alpha_1 , \alpha_2 , ... , \alpha_n \}$, it is a basis of $E$. $\alpha_1^v , \alpha_2^v , ... , \alpha_n^v \in \Phi^v$ is a basis of $E$ too; the dual basis $\lambda_1 , \lambda_2 , ..., \lambda_n$ defined by $(\lambda_i, \alpha_j^v) = \delta_{ij}$ , is called the fundamental dominant weights.

::: {#o129 .entry cue=term name="Weyl"}

**Weyl**

[Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544)

The [Weyl character formula](wikipedia://en/5011916) expresses the character of an irreducible representations of a complex [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587) in terms of highest weights.

The [Weyl integration formula](wikipedia://en/63509442) says: given a compact connected Lie group *G* with a maximal torus *T*, there exists a real continuous function *u* on *T* such that for every continuous function *f* on *G*,

$\int_G f(g) dg = \int_T f(t) u(t) dt.$ (Explicitly, $u$ is 1 over the cardinality of the Weyl group times the product of $|e^{\alpha(t)} - e^{-\alpha(t)}|^2$ over the roots $\alpha$.)

[Weyl module](wikipedia://en/32084687).

A [Weyl filtration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_filtration) is a filtration of a representation of a reductive group such that the quotients are isomorphic to [Weyl modules](wikipedia://en/32084687).

::: {#o131 .entry cue=term name="Young"}

**Young**

[Alfred Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Young_%28mathematician%29)

The [Young symmetrizer](wikipedia://en/1585226) is the *G*-linear endomorphism $c_{\lambda}: V^{\otimes n} \to V^{\otimes n}$ of a tensor power of a *G*-module *V* defined according to a given partition $\lambda$. By definition, the [Schur functor](wikipedia://en/11597641) of a representation *V* assigns to *V* the image of $c_{\lambda}$.
