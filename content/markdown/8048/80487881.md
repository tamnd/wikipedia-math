# Thomas Storer (American mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Storer"}

**Thomas Frederick Storer** (1938 – November 9, 2006) was a [Navajo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo) American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who studied [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [cyclotomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotomy). He was a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), and was one of the first Native Americans to become a PhD mathematician. In addition to his mathematical research, teaching, and mentoring, Storer also did notable work on [string figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_figures).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Storer, a citizen of the [Navajo Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo_Nation), was born in 1938.[^o4] He attended the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) for his bachelors degree, with the help of a [football scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_scholarship).[^o5][^o11] He went on to the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) for his graduate work, receiving an MA in 1962, and a PhD in 1964.[^o5] His thesis was titled *A Family of Generalized Difference Sets*, and was advised by Albert Whiteman.[^o6] Storer was one of the first [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans) to complete a PhD in mathematics; [Native Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiian) [Edwin Mookini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Mookini) completed his PhD in the same year.[^o4][^o7][^o8]

In 1965, after a short period in residence in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179),[^o9] Storer went to the University of Michigan, where he remained for the rest of his career.[^o10] He was promoted to full professor in 1979, and retired to [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) status in 2001.[^o11][^o12]

At the University of Michigan, Storer was known as a teacher and mentor. In 1985, he received the Amoco Foundation Good Teaching Award.[^o12] Storer was an advocate for Native American causes on the University of Michigan campus, and learned the [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe_language) spoken by the [Ojibwe people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe_people) in Michigan.[^o11][^o12] Ten doctoral students completed their PhDs with Storer as their thesis advisor, including [Peyton Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_Young) (co-advised with [Jack Edmonds](wikipedia://en/3625459)).[^o6]

## String figures {#String_figures}

Storer also wrote several works on string figures, the designs formed by manipulating string around one's fingers. His bibliography on the topic went through three editions.[^o11] He also wrote a monograph on string figures, which appeared as a two-volume special issue of the *[Bulletin of String Figures Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_String_Figure_Association)*.[^o4]

In his monograph, he devised a [notation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notation_system) for compactly and precisely describing string figures.[^o4] The International String Figure Association began recommending that contributors use either the older notation of [Haddon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Cort_Haddon) and [Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._H._R._Rivers), or else a modified, more verbose version of Storer's notation.[^o14]

*Image: Osage Two Diamonds Jayne Fig 56.jpg.* Osage 2-Diamonds string figure

For example, Storer's monograph encodes the sequence of steps for making the Osage 2-Diamonds string figure (pictured) as follows:[^o15]

$\underline{O}.A : \square1 \vert$ $\star \overrightarrow{1} (\underline{5f}) \# \vert$ $\overrightarrow{1}(2n) \# N1 \vert$ $\overleftarrow{2} \downarrow (1-\text{⧌}) : <2 (\#) : \square 5 \vert$ $(\text{palms away)}.$

Here, the fingers are encoded with the numerals 1 through 5, with 1 representing the thumb and 5 representing the little finger. The notation describes a sequence of movements of a loop held by one finger (a "functor", written before a set of parentheses), relative to a loop held by another finger (an "object", written inside parentheses). The symbol $\square$ denotes releasing a loop of string, while the vertical line $\vert$ denotes moving hands apart so that the string is not [slack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tension_%28physics%29).[^o4][^o15]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Storer was married to Karen Storer for a period of over 30 years. They had two daughters.[^o11][^o12] Storer died on November 9, 2006.[^o5][^o12]
