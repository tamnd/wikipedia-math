# Morio Obata

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Morio Obata"}

**Morio Obata** (小畠 守生, *Obata Morio*; June 5, 1926 – December 21, 2006) was a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who contributed to [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Obata was born on June 5, 1926, in [Busan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busan), [Korea, Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_under_Japanese_rule).[^o4]

Obata obtained a [bachelor of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_science) degree from the Fifth College at [Kumamoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumamoto_University) in 1947[^o4] and subsequently studied under the guidance of [Kentaro Yano](wikipedia://en/34951366) at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo), who was frequently away working at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) and other institutions. A chance meeting with [Shizuo Kakutani](wikipedia://en/2572542) in 1947 influenced Obata's decision to pursue [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) as his field of research, as Kakutani remarked that if he were younger and just starting his mathematical career, he would be studying it. Obata obtained a [master of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_science) degree from the University of Tokyo in 1950.[^o4] In 1951, he joined the [Tokyo Metropolitan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_University) and published joint papers with his friend Shigeru Ishihara on [affine connections](wikipedia://en/607690) and [conformal transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_transformation) from 1953 to 1955.[^o5] In 1956, he established that an irreducible noncompact [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) admits [isometries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometries) which are not [holomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic) only if the [holonomy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holonomy_group) is the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) $\text{Sp}(n)$.[^o5][^o6] Obata's [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation) was on affine connections on [manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifolds) with [almost complex](wikipedia://en/514258), [almost quaternion](wikipedia://en/56425908#Almost_quaternionic_structure), and [almost Hermitian structure](wikipedia://en/2640459).[^o7] He earned his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in 1958.[^o4]

From 1958 to 1961, he worked as a visiting mathematician and research [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign)[^o8][^o4][^o5] until he joined Tokyo Metropolitan University staff as an [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor).[^o4] In 1963, he participated in [fundraising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundraising) efforts for mathematical research and worked with [Takeo Fujisawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeo_Fujisawa) (then vice-president of [Honda Motor Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda)) to secure funding for the mathematical community through the Sakkokai foundation.[^o5] Obata was made a [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_professor) of mathematics in 1966.[^o4] After leaving Tokyo Metropolitan University in 1978, Obata moved to [Keio University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keio_University), where he supervised a number of geometry graduate students. In 1987, Obata established the [Geometry Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_Prize) of the [Mathematical Society of Japan](wikipedia://en/18376786), which is awarded to geometers for achievements, contributions, or guidance to younger researchers. By 1991, the prize had been awarded to ten geometers.[^o5] He retired from Keio University in 1991 at the age of 65; a [workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_conference) celebrating his contributions to geometry and global analysis was held in his honor from November 19 to 21 of the same year.[^o5]

Obata died on December 21, 2006, at the age of 80. He was a member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for 47 years.[^o9]
