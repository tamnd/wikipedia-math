# Washington Mio

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Washington Mio"}

**Washington Mio** is a mathematician specializing in [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732) and [shape analysis](wikipedia://en/16576796). He is a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and served as the chair of [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University)'s mathematics department.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Mio earned his bachelor's degree in mathematics from [State University of Campinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_Campinas), Brazil in 1978. Two years later he finished his M.S. in mathematics from the [Instituto de Matemática Pura e Aplicada](wikipedia://en/2334708).[^o4] Mio completed his Ph.D. at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) in 1984 with [Sylvain Cappell](wikipedia://en/17173914) as his advisor.[^o5] His dissertation was published in the [Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937).[^o6]

In 1996 Mio, along with John Bryant, Steven Ferry, and [Shmuel Weinberger](wikipedia://en/19159322), disproved [James Cannon](wikipedia://en/19404886)'s influential *Resolution Conjecture* using [surgery theory](wikipedia://en/5961115).[^o7]

In 2004 Mio, together with Eric Klassen, Anuj Srivastava, and Shantanu H. Joshi, introduced a widely used method for analyzing and automatically classifying shapes based on [geodesic paths](wikipedia://en/91096).[^o8]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 2015, Mio was inducted as a [fellow of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society) for "contributions to topology as well as to the mathematics, statistics, and applications of shape analysis."[^o10]

[Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University) awarded Mio the title of Distinguished Research Professor in 2023[^o11] and made him the inaugural Roger W. Roberts Professor of Mathematics in 2024.[^o12]
