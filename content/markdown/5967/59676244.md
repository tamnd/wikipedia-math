# QM–AM–GM–HM inequalities

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="QM–AM–GM–HM inequalities"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **QM–AM–GM–HM inequalities**, also known as the **mean inequality chain**, state the relationship between the [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463) (HM), [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) (GM), [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) (AM), and [quadratic mean](wikipedia://en/66819) (QM; also known as root mean square). Suppose that $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ are positive [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438). Then[^o2]

:::

$$\frac{n}{\frac{1}{x_1}+\frac{1}{x_2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{x_n}}\leq\sqrt[n]{x_1x_2\cdots x_n}\leq\frac{x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n}{n} \leq\sqrt{\frac{x_1^2+x_2^2+\cdots+x_n^2}{n}}.$$

In other words, HM ≤ GM ≤ AM ≤ QM. These inequalities often appear in mathematical competitions and have applications in many fields of science.

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

There are three inequalities between means to prove. There are various methods to prove the inequalities, including [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881), the [Cauchy–Schwarz inequality](wikipedia://en/38128), [Lagrange multipliers](wikipedia://en/159974), and [Jensen's inequality](wikipedia://en/297811). For several proofs that GM ≤ AM, see [Inequality of arithmetic and geometric means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inequality_of_arithmetic_and_geometric_means).

::: {#o6 .statement cue=named-heading name="AM–QM inequality"}

### AM–QM inequality {#AM–QM_inequality}

From the [Cauchy–Schwarz inequality on real numbers](wikipedia://en/38128#real_number_proof), setting one vector to $(1, 1, ...)$:

$\left( \sum_{i=1}^n x_i \cdot 1 \right)^{\! 2} \leq \left( \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 \right) \left( \sum_{i=1}^n 1^2 \right) = n \,\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2,$ hence $\left( \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i}{n} \right)^{\! 2} \leq \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2}{n}$. For positive $x_i$ the square root of this gives the inequality.

### HM–GM inequality {#HM–GM_inequality}

The reciprocal of the harmonic mean is the arithmetic mean of the reciprocals $1/x_1 , \dots, 1/x_n$, and it exceeds $1/\sqrt[n]{x_1 \dots x_n}$ by the AM-GM inequality. $x_i > 0$ implies the inequality:

$\frac{n}{\frac{1}{x_1} + \dots + \frac{1}{x_n}} \leq \sqrt[n]{x_1\dots x_n}.$[^o11]

## The *n* = 2 case {#The_n_=_2_case}

*Image: HM-GM-AM-QM inequality n=2 case visualization.svg.* Visualization of the inequalities inside of a semi-circle, with supporting equalities in grey, *a* = *x*<sub>1</sub>, *b* = *x*<sub>2</sub>.

When *n* = 2, the inequalities become

$\frac {2x_1 x_2}{x_1+x_2} \leq \sqrt{x_1 x_2} \leq \frac{x_1+x_2}{2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{x_1^2+x_2^2}{2}}$ for all $x_1, x_2 > 0,$ [^o13]

which can be visualized in a semi-circle whose diameter is *x*<sub>1</sub>+*x*<sub>2</sub>.

Suppose *C* is a point on \[*AB*\] and let *AC* = *x*<sub>1</sub> and *BC* = *x*<sub>2</sub>. [Find the midpoint](wikipedia://en/437979#Construction) of \[*AB*\] as *D* and use as the center for the semi-circle from *A* to *B*. Construct perpendiculars to \[*AB*\] at *D* and *C* respectively, intersecting the circle at *E* and *F* respectively. Join \[*CE*\] and \[*DF*\] and further construct a perpendicular \[*CG*\] to \[*DF*\] at *G*. The length of *DE* is the arithmetic mean by the virtue of being the ray of the circle. *CE* can be calculated to be the quadratic mean from the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034), *CF* to be the geometric mean from a combination of [Thales's theorem](wikipedia://en/267471) (establishing that $\triangle ABF$ is a [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420)) and [Geometric mean theorem](wikipedia://en/37981515), *GF* to be the harmonic mean from the [similarity](wikipedia://en/39377#Similar_triangles) of triangle $\triangle CGF$ and $\triangle DCF$ (whose edge \[*DF*\]'s length can be calculated using the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034) and the two other known edges).
