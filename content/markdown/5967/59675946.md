# Trilateration

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trilateration"}

*Image: 3D Trilateration.jpg.* Trilateration in three-dimensional geometry

*Image: GPS-pseudorange.svg.* Intersection point of three pseudo-ranges

**Trilateration** is the use of [distances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distances) (or "ranges") for determining the unknown [position coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Position_coordinate) of a [point](wikipedia://en/593693) of interest.[^o2] When more than three distances are involved, it may also be called **multilateration**, for emphasis. The point of interest is often around Earth ([geopositioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopositioning)).

:::

The distances or ranges might be ordinary [Euclidean distances](wikipedia://en/53932) ([slant ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_range)) or [spherical distances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_distance) (scaled [central angles](wikipedia://en/462705)), as in *[true-range multilateration](wikipedia://en/702847)*; or biased distances ([pseudo-ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-range)), as in *[pseudo-range multilateration](wikipedia://en/3516652)*.

Trilateration or multilateration should not be confused with *[triangulation](wikipedia://en/207174)*, which uses [angles](wikipedia://en/1196) for positioning; and *[direction finding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direction_finding)*, which determines the line of sight [direction](wikipedia://en/54516626) to a target without determining the [radial distance](wikipedia://en/25120).

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Terminology"}

## Terminology {#Terminology}

Multiple, sometimes overlapping and conflicting terms are employed for similar concepts – e.g., *multilateration* without modification has been used for aviation systems employing both true-ranges and pseudo-ranges.[^o5][^o6] Moreover, different fields of endeavor may employ different terms. In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), *trilateration* is defined as the process of determining absolute or relative locations of points by measurement of distances, using the geometry of [circles](wikipedia://en/6220), [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) or [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654). In surveying, *trilateration* is a specific technique.[^o7][^o8][^o9]
