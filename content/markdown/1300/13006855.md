# Erdős–Mordell inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erdős–Mordell inequality"}

*Image: Ungleichung erdos mordell2.svg.* The Erdős–Mordell inequality illustrated

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **Erdős–Mordell inequality** states that for any triangle $ABC$ and point $P$ inside $ABC$, the sum of the distances from $P$ to the sides is less than or equal to half of the sum of the distances from $P$ to the vertices. It is named after [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Louis Mordell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Mordell). (Erdős 1935) posed the problem of proving the inequality; a proof was provided two years later by (txt).[^n1] This solution was however not very elementary. Subsequent simpler proofs were then found by (Kazarinoff 1957), (Bankoff 1958), and (Alsina Nelsen 2007).[^n2][^n3][^n4]

:::

[Barrow's inequality](wikipedia://en/7333103) is a strengthened version of the Erdős–Mordell inequality in which the distances from $P$ to the sides are replaced by the distances from *P* to the points where the [angle bisectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_bisector) of $\angle APB$, $\angle BPC$, and $\angle CPA$ cross the sides. Although the replaced distances are longer, their sum is still less than or equal to half the sum of the distances to the vertices.

## Statement and proof {#Statement_and_proof}

Let $P$ be an arbitrary point inside a given triangle $ABC$, and let $PL$, $PM$, and $PN$ be the perpendiculars from $P$ to the sides of the triangles. (If the triangle is obtuse, one of these perpendiculars may cross through a different side of the triangle and end on the line supporting one of the sides.) Then the inequality states that[^n4]

$$PA+PB+PC\geq 2(PL+PM+PN).$$

The proof is as follows:

- Let the sides of $ABC$ be $a$, $b$, and $c$, which are opposite to the vertices $A$, $B$, and $C$. Let us denote the distance between internal point $P$ to all such three vertices as $PA = p$, $PB = q$, and $PC = r$, and the distance between internal point $P$ to all three sides of a triangle $ABC$, namely the distance from $P$ to $BC$ is $x$, to $CA$ is $y$, and to $AB$ is $z$. First, we prove that $$cr\geq ax+by.$$ This is equivalent to $$\frac{c(r+z)}2\geq \frac{ax+by+cz}2.$$
- The right side is the area of triangle ABC, but on the left side, $r + z$ is at least the height of the triangle; consequently, the left side cannot be smaller than the right side. Now reflect $P$ on the angle bisector at $C$. We find that $cr \ge ay + bx$ for the reflection of $P$. Similarly, $bq \ge az + cx$ and $ap \ge bz + cy$. We solve these inequalities for $r$, $q$, and $p$: $$\begin{aligned} r &\geq (a/c)y+(b/c)x, \\ q &\geq (a/b)z+(c/b)x, \\ p &\geq (b/a)z+(c/a)y.\end{aligned}$$
- Adding the three up, we get $$p + q + r \geq \left( \frac{b}{c} + \frac{c}{b} \right) x + \left( \frac{a}{c} + \frac{c}{a} \right) y + \left( \frac{a}{b} + \frac{b}{a} \right) z.$$
- Since the sum of a positive number and its reciprocal is at least 2 by [AM–GM inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inequality_of_arithmetic_and_geometric_means), we are finished. Equality holds only for the [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285), where $P$ is its centroid.

## Another strengthened version {#Another_strengthened_version}

Let $ABC$ be a triangle inscribed into a circle $O$, and $P$ be a point inside of $ABC$. Let $D$, $E$, and $F$ be the orthogonal projections of $P$ onto $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$. Points $M$, $N$, and $Q$ be the orthogonal projections of $P$ onto tangents to $O$ at points $A$, $B$, and $C$, respectively. Then,

$$PM+PN+PQ \ge 2(PD+PE+PF)$$

Equality hold if and only if triangle $ABC$ is equilateral.[^n5][^n6]

## A generalization {#A_generalization}

Let $A_1A_2...A_n$ be a [convex polygon](wikipedia://en/660019), and $P$ be an interior point of $A_1A_2...A_n$. Let $R_i$ be the distance from $P$ to the vertex $A_i$, $r_i$ the distance from $P$ to the side $A_iA_{i+1}$, $w_i$ the segment of the bisector of the angle $A_iPA_{i+1}$ from $P$ to its intersection with the side $A_iA_{i+1}$ then[^n7]

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_i \ge \left(\sec{\frac{\pi}{n}}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \ge \left(\sec{\frac{\pi}{n}}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n} r_i$$

:::

## In absolute geometry {#In_absolute_geometry}

In [absolute geometry](wikipedia://en/699294) the Erdős–Mordell inequality is equivalent, as proved in (Pambuccian 2008), to the statement that the sum of the angles of a triangle is less than or equal to two right angles.[^n8]
