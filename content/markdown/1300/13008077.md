# Imre Csiszár

Csiszár Imre

*Image: ImreCsiszar1976.jpg.* Imre Csiszár in 1976

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Imre Csiszár"}

**Imre Csiszár** (born 7 February 1938) is a Hungarian mathematician known for his contributions to [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). In 1996, he received the [Claude E. Shannon Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_E._Shannon_Award), one of the highest honours in information theory.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Csiszár was born in [Miskolc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskolc), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary). He developed an interest in mathematics during middle school, influenced in part by his father, a forest engineer who applied mathematical methods in his work.

He studied mathematics at [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest), receiving his diploma in 1961. He earned his PhD in 1967 and the Doctor of Mathematical Sciences degree in 1977.

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Csiszár was influenced by mathematician [Alfréd Rényi](wikipedia://en/614988), particularly in the field of probability theory. He has been associated with the [Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31714670) of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) since 1961, where he has held several positions, including head of the Information Theory Group and head of the Stochastics Department.

He has also served as a professor of mathematics at [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University). Throughout his career, he has held visiting positions at institutions including [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University), the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia), the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo), and [Nippon Telegraph and Telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Telegraph_and_Telephone).

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

Csiszár is a Fellow of the [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE) and a member of several scientific societies, including the [Bernoulli Society for Mathematical Statistics and Probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_Society_for_Mathematical_Statistics_and_Probability).

His awards include:

- [Claude E. Shannon Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_E._Shannon_Award) (1996)
- [IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Richard_W._Hamming_Medal) (2015)
- IEEE Information Theory Society Paper Award (1988)
- Academy Award for Interdisciplinary Research of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences (1989)

## Publications {#Publications}

- With [János Körner](wikipedia://en/63058245): *Information Theory: Coding Theorems for Discrete Memoryless Systems*. Academic Press, 1981; 2nd edition, Cambridge University Press, 2011.
- With Paul C. Shields: *Information Theory and Statistics: A Tutorial*. Now Publishers, 2004.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Csiszár is married and has four children.
