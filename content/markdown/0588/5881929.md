# Robert Leslie Ellis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Leslie Ellis"}

**Robert Leslie Ellis** (25 August 1817 – 12 May 1859) was an English polymath, remembered principally as a mathematician and editor of the works of [Francis Bacon](wikipedia://en/11319).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Robert Leslie Ellis was the youngest of six children of Francis Ellis (1772–1842) of [Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_England) and his wife Mary née Kilbee (1777–1847). He was named after his paternal grandfather Robert Ellis and his wife Penelope née Leslie with ‘Leslie’ being a second surname not a forename.[^o4]

Educated privately, he entered [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), in 1836, graduating as [Senior Wrangler](wikipedia://en/19355609) in 1840 and elected Fellow of Trinity shortly afterwards. Although he had also entered the [Inner Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Temple) in 1838, was called to the bar in 1840, and later helped [William Whewell](wikipedia://en/205488) (his eventual brother-in-law via sister 'Fanny')[^o4] with jurisprudence, Ellis never practised law. He hoped unsuccessfully for the [Cambridge chair of civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regius_Professor_of_Civil_Law_%28Cambridge%29).

Inheriting substantial [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland) estates when his father died, estates that had been inherited by his father from his uncle [Henry Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ellis_%28governor%29),[^o4] Robert Leslie Ellis contemplated entering Parliament as a [Whig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_%28British_political_party%29) under [Sir William Napier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Francis_Patrick_Napier)'s patronage. Yet his courtship of one of Napier's daughters ended in some confusion: Ellis never married, and never stood for Parliament.

As a mathematician, Ellis founded the *Cambridge Mathematical Journal* with [D. F. Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Farquharson_Gregory) in 1837. He corresponded with [Augustus De Morgan](wikipedia://en/18949289) on the conjectured [four color theorem](wikipedia://en/10949).

Continental travel failed to restore Ellis' health. An attack of rheumatic fever at [Sanremo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanremo) in 1849 left him an invalid, and he returned to Cambridge, living at Anstey Hall, [Trumpington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpington,_Cambridgeshire), next to his friend [John Grote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Grote), vicar of Trumpington. From his sickbed Ellis kept up contact with the young Trinity mathematician William Walton, and dictated his thoughts on a wide range of topics, including etymology, bees' cells, Roman money, the principles of a projected Chinese dictionary, and Boole's *[The Laws of Thought](wikipedia://en/1993328)* (1854).

## Works {#Works}

Ellis took on the editing of [Francis Bacon](wikipedia://en/11319)'s works with two other Trinity fellows, [Douglas Denon Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Denon_Heath) and [James Spedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Spedding). Dramatic deterioration of Ellis's health from 1847 left his work on the general prefaces to Bacon's philosophy unfinished. Spedding and Heath completed the *Works* in seven volumes, published 1857–1859.

Ellis's own major mathematical contributions were on functional and differential equations, and the theory of probability (*On the foundations of the theory of probabilities*, read to the Cambridge Philosophical Society on 14 February 1842; published in the fourth volume of the *Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society* of 1844). Philosophically, Ellis, like [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298) and later [John Venn](wikipedia://en/251482), defended an objective rather than subjective theory of probability.

William Walton edited a posthumous collection of both published and unpublished writings, in *The mathematical and other writings of R. L. Ellis* (1863): this was prefaced by a biographical memoir by [Harvey Goodwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Goodwin). Correspondence and notebooks of Ellis are amongst the Mayor Papers and Whewell Papers at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge).[^o7]

Leslie translated [Dante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante), [Roman law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_law) texts and Danish ballads; a gentle melancholia suffuses the lines of his own poetry which he left in manuscript.
