# Robert Alexander Rankin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Alexander Rankin"}

**Robert Alexander Rankin** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) FRSAMD (27 October 1915 – 27 January 2001) was a [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Rankin was born in [Garlieston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlieston) in [Wigtownshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigtownshire) the son of Rev Oliver Rankin (1885–1954), minister of [Sorbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbie)[^o4] and his wife, Olivia Theresa Shaw. His father took the name [Oliver Shaw Rankin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Shaw_Rankin) on marriage and became Professor of [Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament) Language, Literature and Theology in the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh).[^o5]

Rankin was educated at [Fettes College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fettes_College) then studied mathematics at [Clare College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge), graduating in 1937. At [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) he was particularly influenced by [J.E. Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371) and [A.E. Ingham](wikipedia://en/5380138).[^o4]

Rankin was elected a Fellow of Clare College in 1939, but his career was interrupted by the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), during which he worked first for the Ministry of Supply then on [rocketry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket) research at [Fort Halstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Halstead). In 1945 he returned to Cambridge as an assistant lecturer, and then moved to the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham) in 1951 as Mason professor of mathematics. In 1954 he became [Professor of Mathematics, Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_of_Mathematics,_Glasgow) [University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow), retiring in 1982.[^o4]

In 1954 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were [William M. Smart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_M._Smart), [Robert Garry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Garry), [James Norman Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Norman_Davidson) and [Robert Pollock Gillespie](wikipedia://en/51040007). He served as Vice President 1960 to 1963 and won the Society's Keith Prize for the period 1961–63.[^o5]

Rankin had a continuing interest in [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717), working initially with [G.H. Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.H._Hardy) on Ramanujan's unpublished notes. His research interests lay in the distribution of [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_numbers) and in [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000). In 1939 he developed what is now known as the [Rankin–Selberg method](wikipedia://en/27989406). In 1977 [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press) published Rankin's *Modular Forms and Functions*. In his review, [Marvin Knopp](wikipedia://en/34193073) wrote:

For, as much as any recent exposition of [modular functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_function), this book succeeds in getting near the research frontier, and in some instances even reaches it – no small feat in this theory. Only someone of Rankin's stature as a research mathematician and experience in the classroom could aspire to such an accomplishment in a self-contained work – beginning with first principles.[^o6]

In 1987 Rankin received the [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize) from the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o7]

Rankin died in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow) on 27 January 2001.[^o4]
