# Cocountability

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cocountability"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **cocountable** [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of a set $X$ is a subset $Y$ whose [complement](wikipedia://en/54347) in $X$ is a [countable set](wikipedia://en/6026). In other words, $Y$ contains all but countably many elements of $X$. Since the rational numbers are a countable subset of the reals, for example, the irrational numbers are a cocountable subset of the reals. If the complement is finite, then one says $Y$ is [cofinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofinite).

:::

## σ-algebras {#σ-algebras}

The set of all subsets of $X$ that are either countable or cocountable forms a [σ-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra), i.e., it is closed under the operations of countable unions, countable intersections, and complementation. This σ-algebra is the *countable-cocountable algebra* on $X$. It is the smallest σ-algebra containing every [singleton set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singleton_set).

## Topology {#Topology}

The [cocountable topology](wikipedia://en/801246) (also called the "countable complement topology") on any set $X$ consists of the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) and all cocountable subsets of $X$.
