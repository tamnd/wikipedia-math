# John Charles Fields

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="John Charles Fields"}

**John Charles Fields**, [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society),[^o3] [FRSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) (May 14, 1863 – August 9, 1932) was a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and the founder of the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) for outstanding achievement in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Born in [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), [Canada West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_West), to a leather shop owner, Fields graduated from Hamilton Collegiate Institute in 1880 and [University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Toronto), [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 1884 before leaving for the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) to study at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore,_Maryland), [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland). Fields received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1887.[^o5] His [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis), entitled *Symbolic Finite Solutions and Solutions by Definite Integrals of the Equation d<sup>n</sup>y/dx<sup>n</sup> = x<sup>m</sup>y*,[^o6] was published in the *[American Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Mathematics)* in 1886.

Fields taught for two years at Johns Hopkins before joining the faculty of [Allegheny College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_College) in [Meadville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadville,_Pennsylvania), [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania). Disillusioned with the state of mathematical research in [North America](wikipedia://en/21139) at the time, he left for [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) in 1891, locating primarily in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin,_Germany), [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) and [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris,_France), where he associated with some of the greatest mathematical minds of the time, including [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459), [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887), [Ferdinand Georg Frobenius](wikipedia://en/341048) and [Max Planck](wikipedia://en/19848). Fields also began a lifelong friendship with [Gösta Mittag-Leffler](wikipedia://en/1438792). He began publishing papers on a new topic, [algebraic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_functions), which would prove to be the most fruitful research field of his career.

Fields returned to Canada in 1902 to lecture at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto). Back in the country of his birth, he worked tirelessly to raise the status of mathematics within academic and public circles. He successfully lobbied the [Ontario Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Ontario) for an annual research grant of \$75,000 for the university and helped establish the [National Research Council of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_of_Canada), and the Ontario Research Foundation. Fields served as president of the [Royal Canadian Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Institute) from 1919 until 1925, during which time he aspired to mold the institute into a leading centre of scientific research, although with mixed success. His efforts, however, were pivotal in making [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto) the location of the 1924 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM).[^o7] He was an Invited Speaker of the ICM in 1912 at Cambridge,[^o8] in 1924 at Toronto,[^o9] and in 1928 at Bologna.[^o10]

Fields is best known for his development of the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859), which is considered by some to be the [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize) of mathematics, although there are differences between the awards. First awarded in 1936, the medal was reintroduced in 1950 and has been awarded every four years since. It is awarded to two, three or four mathematicians under the age of 40 who have made important contributions to the field.

Fields began planning the award in the late 1920s but, due to deteriorating health, never saw the implementation of the medal in his lifetime. He died on August 9, 1932, after a three-month illness; in his will, he left \$47,000 for the Fields Medal fund. His plan for the Fields medal was completed by [J. L. Synge](wikipedia://en/1252509).[^o11]

## Honours {#Honours}

Fields was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) in 1907 and of the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London) in 1913.

The [Fields Institute](wikipedia://en/2495768) at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) was named in his honour.
