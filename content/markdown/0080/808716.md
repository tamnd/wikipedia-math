# Prouhet–Thue–Morse constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prouhet–Thue–Morse constant"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Prouhet–Thue–Morse constant**, named for Eugène Prouhet, [Axel Thue](wikipedia://en/635533), and [Marston Morse](wikipedia://en/856523), is the number—denoted by $\tau$—whose [binary expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_expansion) 0.01101001100101101001011001101001... is given by the [Prouhet–Thue–Morse sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prouhet–Thue–Morse_sequence). That is,

:::

$$\tau = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{t_n}{2^{n+1}} = 0.412454033640 \ldots$$

where $t_{n}$ is the $n^{\operatorname{th}}$ element of the Prouhet–Thue–Morse sequence.

## Other representations {#Other_representations}

The Prouhet–Thue–Morse constant can also be expressed, without using $t_{n}$ , as an infinite product,

$$\tau = \frac{1}{4}\left[2-\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{2^n}}\right)\right]$$

This formula is obtained by substituting *x* = 1/2 into generating series for $t_{n}$

$$F(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (-1)^{t_n} x^n = \prod_{n=0}^{\infty} ( 1 - x^{2^n} )$$

The [continued fraction expansion](wikipedia://en/46802) of the constant is \[0; 2, 2, 2, 1, 4, 3, 5, 2, 1, 4, 2, 1, 5, 44, 1, 4, 1, 2, 4, 1, …\]

Yann Bugeaud and Martine Queffélec showed that infinitely many partial quotients of this continued fraction are 4 or 5, and infinitely many partial quotients are greater than or equal to 50.[^o4]

## Transcendence {#Transcendence}

The Prouhet–Thue–Morse constant was shown to be [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325) by [Kurt Mahler](wikipedia://en/4421496) in 1929.[^o6]

He also showed that the number

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} t_n \, \alpha^n$$

is also transcendental for any [algebraic number](wikipedia://en/1158) α, where 0 \< |*α*| \< 1.

Yann Bugaeud proved that the Prouhet–Thue–Morse constant has an [irrationality measure](wikipedia://en/5968488) of 2.[^o7]

## Appearances {#Appearances}

The Prouhet–Thue–Morse constant appears in [probability](wikipedia://en/22934). If a [language](wikipedia://en/10939) *L* over {0, 1} is chosen at random, by flipping a [fair coin](wikipedia://en/9597100) to decide whether each word *w* is in *L*, the probability that it contains at least one word for each possible length is [^o9]

$$p = \prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{2^n}}\right) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{t_n}}{2^{n+1}} = 2 - 4 \tau = 0.35018386544\ldots$$
