# Category of groups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Category of groups"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the [category](wikipedia://en/5869) $\mathbf{Grp}$ (or $\mathbf{Gp}$[^o2]) has the [class](wikipedia://en/20696) of all [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) for objects and [group homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/12396) for [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891). As such, it is a [concrete category](wikipedia://en/167001). [Group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) may be thought of as the study of this category.

:::

## Relation to other categories {#Relation_to_other_categories}

There are two [forgetful functors](wikipedia://en/844783) from $\mathbf{Grp}$, $\mathrm{M}:\mathbf{Grp}\to\mathbf{Mon}$ from groups to [monoids](wikipedia://en/19652) and $\mathrm{U}:\mathbf{Grp}\to\mathbf{Set}$ from groups to [sets](wikipedia://en/26829). $\mathrm{M}$ has two [adjoints](wikipedia://en/53991): one right, and one left. $\mathrm{I}:\mathbf{Mon}\to\mathbf{Grp}$ is the right adjoint [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) sending every monoid to the submonoid of invertible elements and $\mathrm{K}:\mathbf{Mon}\to\mathbf{Grp}$ the left adjoint functor sending every monoid to the [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790) of that monoid. The forgetful functor $\mathrm{U}:\mathbf{Grp}\to\mathbf{Set}$ has a left adjoint given by the composite $\mathrm{KF}:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Mon}\to\mathbf{Grp}$, where $\mathrm{F}$ is the [free functor](wikipedia://en/333835#Free_functor); this functor assigns to every set $S$ the [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) on $S$.

## Categorical properties {#Categorical_properties}

The [monomorphisms](wikipedia://en/59538) in $\mathbf{Grp}$ are precisely the [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) homomorphisms, the [epimorphisms](wikipedia://en/59539) are precisely the [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) homomorphisms, and the [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828) are precisely the [bijective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijective) homomorphisms.

The category $\mathbf{Grp}$ is both [complete and co-complete](wikipedia://en/62781). The [category-theoretical product](wikipedia://en/237868) in $\mathbf{Grp}$ is just the [direct product of groups](wikipedia://en/3706015) while the [category-theoretical coproduct](wikipedia://en/310889) in $\mathbf{Grp}$ is the [free product](wikipedia://en/360036) of groups. The [zero objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object) in $\mathbf{Grp}$ are the [trivial groups](wikipedia://en/193059) (consisting of just an identity element).

Every morphism $f:G\to H$ in $\mathbf{Grp}$ has a [category-theoretic kernel](wikipedia://en/142616) (given by the ordinary [kernel of algebra](wikipedia://en/45240) $\operatorname{ker}f=\{x\in G|f(x)=e\}$), and also a [category-theoretic cokernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cokernel_%28category_theory%29) (given by the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) of $H$ by the [normal closure](wikipedia://en/153099) of $f(G)$ in $H$). Unlike in abelian categories, it is not true that every monomorphism in $\mathbf{Grp}$ is the kernel of its cokernel.

### Not additive and therefore not abelian {#Not_additive_and_therefore_not_abelian}

The [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748), $\mathbf{Ab}$, is a [full subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_subcategory) of $\mathbf{Grp}$. $\mathbf{Ab}$ is an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063), but $\mathbf{Grp}$ is not. Indeed, $\mathbf{Grp}$ isn't even an [additive category](wikipedia://en/60854), because there is no natural way to define the "sum" of two group homomorphisms. A proof of this is as follows: The set of morphisms from the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_3$ of order three to itself, $E=\operatorname{Hom}(S_3,S_3)$, has ten elements: an element $z$ whose product on either side with every element of $E$ is $z$ (the homomorphism sending every element to the identity), three elements such that their product on one fixed side is always itself (the projections onto the three subgroups of order two), and six automorphisms. If $\mathbf{Grp}$ were an additive category, then this set $E$ of ten elements would be a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404). In any ring, the zero element is singled out by the property that $0x=x0=0$ for all $x$ in the ring, and so $z$ would have to be the zero of $E$. However, there are no two nonzero elements of $E$ whose product is $z$, so this finite ring would have no [zero divisors](wikipedia://en/51441). A [finite ring](wikipedia://en/23706953) with no zero divisors is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) by [Wedderburn's little theorem](wikipedia://en/7132002), but there is no field with ten elements because every [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) has for its order, the power of a prime.

### Exact sequences {#Exact_sequences}

The notion of [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) is meaningful in $\mathbf{Grp}$, and some results from the theory of abelian categories, such as the [nine lemma](wikipedia://en/454673), the [five lemma](wikipedia://en/138214), and their consequences hold true in $\mathbf{Grp}$.

$\mathbf{Grp}$ is a [regular category](wikipedia://en/9790950).
