# Antecedent (logic)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Antecedent"}

An **antecedent** is the first half of a [hypothetical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothetical) [proposition](wikipedia://en/81094), whenever the if-clause precedes the then-clause. In some contexts the antecedent is called the ***protasis***.[^o2]

:::

Examples:

- If $P$, then $Q$.

This is a nonlogical formulation of a hypothetical proposition. In this case, the antecedent is **P**, and the [consequent](wikipedia://en/804778) is **Q**. In the [implication](wikipedia://en/658808) "$\phi$ implies $\psi$", $\phi$ is called the **antecedent** and $\psi$ is called the consequent.[^o3] Antecedent and [consequent](wikipedia://en/804778) are connected via [logical connective](wikipedia://en/18153) to form a [proposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_%28logic%29).

- If $X$ is a man, then $X$ is mortal.

"$X$ is a man" is the antecedent for this proposition while "$X$ is mortal" is the consequent of the proposition.

- If men have walked on the Moon, then I am the king of France.

Here, "men have walked on the Moon" is the antecedent and "I am the king of France" is the consequent.

Let $y=x+1$.

- If $x=1$ then $y=2$.

"$x=1$" is the antecedent and "$y=2$" is the consequent of this hypothetical proposition.
