# Victor Kolyvagin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Victor Kolyvagin"}

**Victor Alexandrovich Kolyvagin** (Виктор Александрович Колывагин, born 11 March, 1955) is a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) mathematician who wrote a series of papers on [Euler systems](wikipedia://en/1052176), leading to breakthroughs on the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161), and [Iwasawa's conjecture](wikipedia://en/396150) for [cyclotomic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotomic_fields).[^o3] His work also influenced [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027)'s work on [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953).[^o6][^o4]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Kolyvagin received his Ph.D. in Mathematics in 1981 from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University),[^o8] where his advisor was [Yuri I. Manin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_I._Manin). He then worked at [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494) in Moscow[^o6] until 1994. Since 1994 he has been a professor of mathematics in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). He was a professor at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) until 2002 when he became the first person to hold the [Mina Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mina_Rees) Chair in mathematics at the [Graduate Center of the City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_Center_of_the_City_University_of_New_York).[^o7][^o8]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 1990 he received the Chebyshev Prize of the [USSR Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o8]
