# Richard Swineshead

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Swineshead"}

**Richard Swineshead** (also **Suisset**, **Suiseth**, etc.; fl. c. 1340 – 1354) was an English mathematician, [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), and [natural philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosopher). He was perhaps the greatest of the [Oxford Calculators](wikipedia://en/4555383) of [Merton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College), where he was a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) certainly by 1344 and possibly by 1340. His magnum opus was a series of treatises known as the *Liber calculationum* ("Book of Calculations"), written c. 1350, which earned him the nickname of **The Calculator**.[^o2]

*Image: Swineshead, Richard – Calculator, 1520 – BEIC 143141.jpg.* *Calculator*, 1520

:::

[Robert Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Burton_%28scholar%29) (d. 1640) wrote in *[The Anatomy of Melancholy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Anatomy_of_Melancholy)* that "[Scaliger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar_Scaliger) and [Cardan](wikipedia://en/13145) admire Suisset the calculator, *qui pene modum excessit humani ingenii* \[whose talents were almost superhuman\]".[^o3]

[Gottfried Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Leibniz) wrote in a letter of 1714: "Il y a eu autrefois un Suisse, qui avoit mathématisé dans la Scholastique: ses Ouvrages sont peu connus; mais ce que j'en ai vu m'a paru profond et considérable." ("There was once a Suisse, who did mathematics belonging to [scholasticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism); his works are little known, but what I have seen of them seemed to me profound and relevant.")[^o4][^o5] Leibniz had a copy of one of Swineshead's treatises made from an edition in the [Bibliothèque du Roi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliothèque_du_Roi) in Paris[^o6] and credited Swineshead as "the man 'who introduced mathematics into scholastic philosophy.'"[^o7]
