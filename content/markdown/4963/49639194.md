# Leaf power

*Image: 3-leaf power.svg.* A tree (top) and its corresponding 3-leaf power (bottom)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leaf power"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) area of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **$k$-leaf power** of a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) $T$ is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) $G$ whose [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) are the leaves of $T$ and whose edges connect pairs of leaves whose [distance](wikipedia://en/1020021) in $T$ is at most $k$. That is, $G$ is an [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) of the [graph power](wikipedia://en/38618878) $T^k$, induced by the leaves of $T$. For a graph $G$ constructed in this way, $T$ is called a **$k$-leaf root** of $G$.

:::

A graph is a **leaf power** if it is a $k$-leaf power for some $k$.[^n1] These graphs have applications in [phylogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogeny), the problem of reconstructing evolutionary trees.

## Related classes of graphs {#Related_classes_of_graphs}

Since powers of [strongly chordal graphs](wikipedia://en/24809733) are strongly chordal and trees are strongly chordal, it follows that leaf powers are strongly chordal graphs.[^o4] Actually, leaf powers form a proper subclass of strongly chordal graphs; a graph is a leaf power if and only if it is a fixed tolerance NeST graph[^o5] and such graphs are a proper subclass of strongly chordal graphs.[^o6]

In (Brandstädt Hundt Mancini Wagner 2010) it is shown that [interval graphs](wikipedia://en/363423) and the larger class of rooted directed path graphs are leaf powers. The [indifference graphs](wikipedia://en/42535190) are exactly the leaf powers whose underlying trees are [caterpillar trees](wikipedia://en/27258886).

The $k$-leaf powers for bounded values of $k$ have bounded [clique-width](wikipedia://en/16795502), but this is not true of leaf powers with unbounded exponents.[^o8]

## Structure and recognition {#Structure_and_recognition}

A graph is a 3-leaf power if and only if it is a (bull, dart, gem)-free [chordal graph](wikipedia://en/744165).[^o10] Based on this characterization and similar ones, 3-leaf powers can be recognized in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time).[^n2]

Characterizations of 4-leaf powers are given by (Rautenbach 2006) and (Brandstädt Le Sritharan 2008), which also enable linear time recognition. Recognition of the 5-leaf and 6-leaf power graphs are also solved in linear time by Chang and Ko (2007)[^o14] and Ducoffe (2018),[^o15] respectively.

For $k \ge 7$ the recognition problem of $k$-leaf powers was unsolved for a long time, but (Lafond 2021) showed that $k$-leaf powers can be recognized in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) for any fixed $k$. However, the high dependency on the parameter $k$ makes this algorithm unsuitable for practical use.

Also, it has been proved that recognizing $k$-leaf powers is [fixed-parameter tractable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-parameter_tractable) when parameterized by $k$ and the [degeneracy](wikipedia://en/27545816) of the input graph.[^o17]
