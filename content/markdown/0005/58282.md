# Thermal diffusivity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thermal diffusivity"}

In [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/29952), **thermal diffusivity** is the [thermal conductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conductivity) divided by [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) and [specific heat capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_heat_capacity) at constant pressure. It is a measure of the rate of [heat transfer](wikipedia://en/184726) inside a material and has [SI units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI) of m<sup>2</sup>/s. It is an [intensive property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intensive_property). Thermal diffusivity is usually denoted by lowercase [alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha) ($\alpha$), but $a$, $h$, $\kappa$ ([kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kappa)),[^o3] $K$,[^o4] $D$, $D_T$ are also used.

:::

The formula is[^o5]

$$\alpha = \frac{k}{\rho c_p},$$

where

$k$ is [thermal conductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conductivity) (W/(m·K)),

$c_{p}$ is [specific heat capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_heat_capacity) (J/(kg·K)),

$\rho$ is [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) (kg/m<sup>3</sup>).

Together, $\rho c_{p}$ can be considered the [volumetric heat capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volumetric_heat_capacity) (J/(m<sup>3</sup>·K)).

Thermal diffusivity is a positive [coefficient](wikipedia://en/51203) in the [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415):[^o6]

$$\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \alpha \nabla^2 T.$$

One way to view thermal diffusivity is as the ratio of the [time derivative](wikipedia://en/1783069) of [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050) to its [curvature](wikipedia://en/3224219#Generalization_to_higher_dimensions), quantifying the rate at which temperature concavity is "smoothed out". In a substance with high thermal diffusivity, heat moves rapidly through it because the substance conducts heat quickly relative to its energy storage capacity or "thermal bulk".

Thermal diffusivity and [thermal effusivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_effusivity) are related concepts and quantities used to simulate [non-equilibrium thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/472429). Diffusivity is the more fundamental concept and describes the [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) of heat spread throughout some *[local](wikipedia://en/1462457) volume* of a substance. Effusivity describes the corresponding transient process of heat flow through some *local area* of interest. Upon reaching a [steady state](wikipedia://en/1326107), where the stored energy distribution stabilizes, the thermal conductivity ($k$) may be sufficient to describe heat transfers inside solid or rigid bodies by applying [Fourier's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier's_law).[^o7][^o8]

Thermal diffusivity is often measured with the [flash method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_flash_analysis).[^o9][^o10] It involves heating a strip or cylindrical sample with a short energy pulse at one end and analyzing the temperature change (reduction in amplitude and phase shift of the pulse) a short distance away.[^o11][^o12]
