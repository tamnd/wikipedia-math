# Sedenion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sedenion"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the **sedenions** form a 16-[dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_of_a_vector_space) [noncommutative](wikipedia://en/294390) and [nonassociative](wikipedia://en/1335) [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) over the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), usually represented by the capital letter S, boldface $\mathbf{S}$ or [blackboard bold](wikipedia://en/40276) $\mathbb S$.

:::

The sedenions are obtained by applying the [Cayley–Dickson construction](wikipedia://en/175609) to the [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436), which can be mathematically expressed as $\mathbb{S}=\mathcal{CD}(\mathbb{O},1)$.[^o3] As such, the octonions are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to a [subalgebra](wikipedia://en/45239#Subalgebras_for_algebras_over_a_ring_or_field) of the sedenions. Unlike the octonions, the sedenions are not an [alternative algebra](wikipedia://en/3160). Applying the Cayley–Dickson construction to the sedenions yields a 32-dimensional algebra, called the [trigintaduonions](wikipedia://en/25474499) or sometimes the 32-nions.[^o4]

The term *sedenion* is also used for other 16-dimensional algebraic structures, such as a tensor product of two copies of the [biquaternions](wikipedia://en/1207070), or the algebra of 4 × 4 [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) over the real numbers, or that studied by (Smith 1995).

## History {#History}

The term *sedenion* was first mentioned by English mathematician [James Joseph Sylvester](wikipedia://en/234861) in an 1884 paper titled *On quaternions, nonions, sedenions, etc.*[^o7][^o8] In 1919, sedenions were elaborated on by [Leonard Eugene Dickson](wikipedia://en/1790247).[^o9]

## Arithmetic {#Arithmetic}

*Image: Sedenion-Fano Tesseract.gif.* A visualization of a 4D extension to the cubic [octonion](wikipedia://en/51436#Fano_plane_mnemonic),[^o11] showing the 35 triads as [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) through the real $(e_0)$ vertex of the sedenion example given

Every sedenion is a [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of the unit sedenions $e_0$, $e_1$, $e_2$, $e_3$, ..., $e_{15}$, which form a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) of the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of sedenions. Every sedenion can be represented in the form

$$x = x_0 e_0 + x_1 e_1 + x_2 e_2 + \cdots + x_{14} e_{14} + x_{15} e_{15}.$$

Addition and subtraction are defined by the addition and subtraction of corresponding coefficients and multiplication is [distributive](wikipedia://en/103118) over addition.

Like other algebras based on the [Cayley–Dickson construction](wikipedia://en/175609), the sedenions contain the algebra they were constructed from. So they contain the octonions (generated by $e_0$ to $e_7$ in the table below), and therefore also the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) (generated by $e_0$ to $e_3$), [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) (generated by $e_0$ and $e_1$) and real numbers (generated by $e_0$).

### Multiplication {#Multiplication}

Like [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436), [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) of sedenions is neither [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative) nor [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative). However, in contrast to the octonions, the sedenions do not even have the property of being [alternative](wikipedia://en/3160). They do, however, have the property of [power associativity](wikipedia://en/25054), that is, for any element $x$ of $\mathbb{S}$, $x^n$ is well defined. They are also [flexible](wikipedia://en/36035264).

The sedenions have a multiplicative [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) $e_0$ and multiplicative inverses, but they are not a [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625) because they have [zero divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_divisors): two nonzero sedenions can be multiplied to obtain zero, for example $(e_3 + e_{10})(e_6 - e_{15})$. All [hypercomplex number](wikipedia://en/51438) systems after sedenions that are based on the Cayley–Dickson construction also contain zero divisors.

### Sedenion properties {#Sedenion_properties}

*Image: PG(3,2) g005.png.* An illustration of the structure of [PG(3,2)](wikipedia://en/59836267) that provides the multiplication law for sedenions, as shown by (Saniga Holweck Pracna 2015). Any three points (representing three sedenion imaginary units) lying on the same line are such that the product of two of them yields the third one, sign disregarded.

From the above table, we can see that:

$$e_0e_i = e_ie_0 = e_i \, \text{for all} \, i,$$

$e_ie_i = -e_0 \,\, \text{for}\,\, i \neq 0,$ and

$$e_ie_j = -e_je_i \,\, \text{for}\,\, i \neq j \,\,\text{with}\,\, i,j \neq 0.$$

#### Anti-associative {#Anti-associative}

The sedenions are not fully anti-associative. Choose any four generators, $i,j,k$ and $l$. The following 5-cycle shows that these five relations cannot all be anti-associative.

$$(ij)(kl) = -((ij)k)l = (i(jk))l = -i((jk)l) = i(j(kl)) = -(ij)(kl)$$

In particular, in the table above, using $e_1,e_2,e_4$ and $e_8$ the last expression associates. $(e_1e_2)e_{12} = e_1(e_2e_{12}) = -e_{15}$

:::

### Quaternionic subalgebras {#Quaternionic_subalgebras}

### Space of zero divisors {#Space_of_zero_divisors}

It has been shown that the pairs of zero divisors in the unit sedenions form a manifold isomorphic to the Lie group [G<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/292864) in the space $\mathbb{S}^2$.[^o21]

::: {#o23 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

(Moreno 1998) showed that the space of pairs of norm-one sedenions that multiply to zero is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to the compact form of the exceptional [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) [G<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/292864). (Note that in his paper, a "zero divisor" means a *pair* of elements that multiply to zero.)

(Guillard Gresnigt 2019) demonstrated that the three generations of [leptons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepton) and [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark) that are associated with unbroken [gauge symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_symmetry) $\mathrm {SU(3)_{c} \times U(1)_{em}}$ can be represented using the algebra of the complexified sedenions $\mathbb {C \otimes S}$. A primitive [idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent) [projector](wikipedia://en/519182) $\rho_{+} = 1/2(1+ie_{15})$ – where $e_{15}$ is chosen as an [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358) akin to $e_{7}$ for $\mathbb {O}$ in the [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404) – that [acts](wikipedia://en/12781) on the [standard basis](wikipedia://en/462005) of the sedenions uniquely divides the algebra into three sets of [split basis](wikipedia://en/893559) elements for $\mathbb {C \otimes O}$, whose adjoint [left actions](wikipedia://en/12781#Left_group_action) *on themselves* generate three copies of the [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305) $\mathrm{Cl}(6)$ which in turn contain [minimal left ideals](wikipedia://en/25977#Types_of_ideals) that describe a single generation of [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion) with unbroken $\mathrm {SU(3)_{c} \times U(1)_{em}}$ gauge symmetry. In particular, they note that [tensor products](wikipedia://en/30844) between normed division algebras generate zero divisors akin to those inside $\mathbb {S}$, where for $\mathbb {C \otimes O}$ the lack of alternativity and associativity does not affect the construction of minimal left ideals since their underlying split basis requires only two basis elements to be multiplied together, in-which associativity or alternativity are uninvolved. Still, these ideals constructed from an adjoint algebra of left actions of the algebra on itself remain associative, alternative, and [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a Clifford algebra. Altogether, this permits three copies of $(\mathbb {C \otimes O})_{L} \cong \mathrm {Cl(6)}$ to exist inside $\mathbb {(C \otimes S)}_{L}$. Furthermore, these three complexified octonion subalgebras are not independent; they share a common $\mathrm{Cl}(2)$ subalgebra, which the authors note could form a theoretical basis for [CKM](wikipedia://en/1083721) and [PMNS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontecorvo–Maki–Nakagawa–Sakata_matrix) matrices that, respectively, describe [quark mixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark_mixing) and [neutrino oscillations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino_oscillation).

Sedenion neural networks provide a means of efficient and compact expression in machine learning applications and have been used in solving multiple time-series and traffic forecasting problems[^o26][^o27] as well as [computer chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_chess).[^o28]
