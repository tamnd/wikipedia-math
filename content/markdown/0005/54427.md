# Computer algebra system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Computer algebra system"}

A **computer algebra system** (**CAS**) or **symbolic algebra system** (**SAS**) is any [mathematical software](wikipedia://en/1727027) with the ability to manipulate [mathematical expressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_expressions) in a way similar to the traditional manual computations of [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) and [scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist). The development of the computer algebra systems in the second half of the 20th century is part of the discipline of "[computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805)" or "symbolic computation", which has spurred work in [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) over [mathematical objects](wikipedia://en/19453961) such as [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000).

:::

Computer algebra systems may be divided into two classes: specialized and general-purpose. The specialized ones are devoted to a specific part of mathematics, such as [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), or teaching of [elementary mathematics](wikipedia://en/1775329).

General-purpose computer algebra systems aim to be useful to a user working in any scientific field that requires manipulation of mathematical expressions. To be useful, a general-purpose computer algebra system must include various features such as:

- a [user interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User_interface) allowing a user to enter and display mathematical formulas, typically from a keyboard, menu selections, mouse or stylus.
- a [programming language](wikipedia://en/23015) and an [interpreter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpreter_%28computing%29) (the result of a computation commonly has an unpredictable form and an unpredictable size; therefore user intervention is frequently needed),
- a [simplifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_computation#Simplification), which is a [rewrite system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewrite_system) for simplifying mathematics formulas,
- a [memory manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_management), including a [garbage collector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbage_collector_%28computing%29), needed by the huge size of the intermediate data, which may appear during a computation,
- an [arbitrary-precision arithmetic](wikipedia://en/600892) implementation, needed by the huge size of the integers that may occur,
- a large library of mathematical [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) and [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110).

The library must not only provide for the needs of the users, but also the needs of the simplifier. For example, the computation of [polynomial greatest common divisors](wikipedia://en/9927028) is systematically used for the simplification of expressions involving fractions.

This large amount of required computer capabilities explains the small number of general-purpose computer algebra systems. Significant systems include [Axiom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_%28computer_algebra_system%29), [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_%28computer_algebra_system%29), [Maxima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_%28software%29), [Magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma_%28computer_algebra_system%29), [Maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_%28software%29), [Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica), [SageMath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SageMath), and [SymPy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SymPy).

## History {#History}

*Image: TI-nspire CX CAS.jpg.* A Texas Instruments [TI-Nspire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TI-Nspire) calculator that contains a computer algebra system

In the 1950s, while computers were mainly used for numerical computations, there were some research projects into using them for symbolic manipulation. Computer algebra systems began to appear in the 1960s and evolved out of two quite different sources—the requirements of theoretical physicists and research into [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence).[^o3]

A prime example of the first development was the pioneering work conducted by the later Nobel Prize laureate in physics [Martinus Veltman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinus_Veltman), who designed a program for symbolic mathematics, especially high-energy physics, called [Schoonschip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schoonschip) (Dutch for "clean ship") in 1963. Other early systems include [FORMAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FORMAC_%28programming_language%29).[^o4]

Using [Lisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_%28programming_language%29) as the programming basis, [Carl Engelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Engelman) created [MATHLAB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MATHLAB) in 1964 at [MITRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MITRE) within an artificial-intelligence research environment.[^o5] MATHLAB was later made available to users on PDP-6 and PDP-10 systems running TOPS-10 or TENEX in universities. Today it can still be used on [SIMH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIMH) emulations of the PDP-10. MATHLAB ("**math**ematical **lab**oratory") should not be confused with [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412) ("**mat**rix **lab**oratory"), which is a system for numerical computation built 15 years later at the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico).[^o6]

There were in the mid-sixties several groups working on symbolic systems and algorithms. [Jean Sammet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Sammet) of IBM brought them together in a conference, called SYMSAM, that she organized in 1966 ; Sammet also founded SICSAM, the Special Interest Committee on Symbolic and Algebraic Manipulation. SICSAM later became SIGSAM.[^o7]

Building on the success of MATHLAB and other early CAS, back at MIT, W. A. Martin began deliberations regarding a new symbolic mathematics system, Macsyma, that would combine all the work of himself, of Carl Engelman and of Joel Moses, and would use the latest algorithms that they heard about at the 1966 SYMSAM. [Macsyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macsyma) was made public at the 1971 SIGSAM Symposium.[^o7]

In 1987, [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard) introduced the first hand-held calculator CAS with the [HP-28 series](wikipedia://en/5791181).[^o8] Other early handheld calculators with symbolic algebra capabilities included the [Texas Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Instruments) [TI-89 series](wikipedia://en/54399) and [TI-92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TI-92) calculator, and the [Casio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casio) [CFX-9970G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFX-9970G).[^o9]

The follow-on popular computer algebra systems include [muMATH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MuMATH), [Reduce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduce_computer_algebra_system), [Derive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derive_%28computer_algebra_system%29) (based on muMATH), and [Macsyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macsyma); a [copyleft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyleft) version of Macsyma is called [Maxima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_%28software%29). [Reduce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduce_%28computer_algebra_system%29) became free software in 2008.[^o10] Commercial systems include [Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica)[^o11] and [Maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_%28software%29), which are commonly used by research mathematicians, scientists, and engineers. Freely available alternatives include [SageMath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SageMath) (which can act as a [front-end](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_and_back_ends) to several other free and nonfree CAS). Other significant systems include [Axiom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_%28computer_algebra_system%29), [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_%28computer_algebra_system%29), Maxima and [Magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma_%28computer_algebra_system%29).

The movement to web-based applications in the early 2000s saw the release of [WolframAlpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WolframAlpha), an online search engine and CAS which includes the capabilities of [Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica).[^o12]

More recently, computer algebra systems have been implemented using [artificial neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_neural_networks).[^o13]

## Symbolic manipulations {#Symbolic_manipulations}

At its inception around 1968, **Macsyma** (an acronym for [Project MAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_MAC)'s SYmbolic MAnipulator), was written in [LISP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_%28programming_language%29) and designed to utilize multiple data representations. Its general expression representation was modeled after [FORMAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FORMAC), though adapted to use LISP [list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_%28abstract_data_type%29) structures. While this allowed the software to represent any arbitrary mathematical expression, its built-in simplifier had limited capabilities.[^o7]

To address specific mathematical operations, Macsyma included a [rational function](wikipedia://en/361210) representation—similar to ALPAK—which processed ratios of multivariable [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) with [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) coefficients. This representation relied on a [greatest common divisor](wikipedia://en/12354) (GCD) algorithm to automatically maintain these functions in simplified forms. As the software evolved, its developers expanded its architecture to include additional representations, notably for [power series](wikipedia://en/59958).[^o7]

A Computer Algebra System typically includes symbolic manipulations such as:

- simplification to a smaller expression or some [standard form](wikipedia://en/515096), including automatic simplification with assumptions and simplification with constraints
- [substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution_%28algebra%29) of symbols or numeric values for certain expressions
- change of form of expressions: expanding products and powers, partial and full [factorization](wikipedia://en/82341), rewriting as [partial fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_fraction), [constraint satisfaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constraint_satisfaction), rewriting [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367) as exponentials, transforming logic expressions, etc.
- [partial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differentiation) and [total differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_differentiation)
- some [indefinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antidifferentiation) and [definite integration](wikipedia://en/15532) (see [symbolic integration](wikipedia://en/1652672)), including multidimensional integrals
- symbolic constrained and unconstrained global optimization
- [solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed-form_solution) of linear and some non-linear equations over various domains
- solution of some [differential](wikipedia://en/1424309) and [difference equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_equation)
- taking some [limits](wikipedia://en/285759)
- integral transforms
- [series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_series) operations such as expansion, summation and products
- matrix operations including [products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_product_%28Matrix%29), [inverses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_inversion), etc.
- [statistical computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_statistical_packages)
- [theorem proving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theorem_proving) and [verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_verification) which is very useful in the area of [experimental mathematics](wikipedia://en/256363)
- [optimized code generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_programming#Implementations)

In the above, the word *some* indicates that the operation cannot always be performed.

## Additional capabilities {#Additional_capabilities}

Many also include:

- a [programming language](wikipedia://en/23015), allowing users to implement their own algorithms
- [arbitrary-precision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitrary-precision) numeric operations
- exact integer arithmetic and number theory functionality
- [Editing of mathematical expressions](wikipedia://en/486362) in two-dimensional form
- plotting [graphs and parametric plots](wikipedia://en/87352) of functions in two and three dimensions, and animating them
- drawing charts and diagrams
- [APIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application_programming_interface) for linking it on an external program such as a database, or using in a programming language to use the computer algebra system
- [string manipulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_manipulation) such as [matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_matching) and [searching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_searching)
- add-ons for use in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) such as physics, [bioinformatics](wikipedia://en/4214), [computational chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_chemistry) and packages for [physical computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_physics)[^o16]
- solvers for [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309)[^o17][^o18][^o19][^o20]
- [pretty-printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretty-printing) output to conform to standard mathematical notation conventions

Some include:

- [graphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics) production and editing such as [computer-generated imagery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-generated_imagery) and [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) as [image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_processing)
- [sound synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_synthesis)

Some computer algebra systems focus on specialized disciplines; these are typically developed in academia and are free. They can be inefficient for numeric operations as compared to numeric systems.

## Types of expressions {#Types_of_expressions}

The expressions manipulated by a CAS typically include multivariable [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000); standard functions of expressions ([sine](wikipedia://en/30367), [exponential](wikipedia://en/9678), etc.); various special functions ([Γ](wikipedia://en/12316), [ζ](wikipedia://en/25809), [erf](wikipedia://en/177111), [Bessel functions](wikipedia://en/4700), etc.); arbitrary functions of expressions; optimization; derivatives, integrals, simplifications, sums, and products of expressions; truncated [series](wikipedia://en/15287) with expressions as coefficients, [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) of expressions, and so on.[^o22][^o23] Numeric domains supported typically include [floating-point representation of real numbers](wikipedia://en/11376), [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) (of unbounded size), [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) (floating-point representation), [interval representation of reals](wikipedia://en/2176160), [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) (exact representation) and [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158).

## Use in education {#Use_in_education}

There have been many advocates for increasing the use of computer algebra systems in primary and secondary-school classrooms. The primary reason for such advocacy is that computer algebra systems represent real-world math more than do paper-and-pencil or hand calculator based mathematics.[^o25] This push for increasing computer usage in mathematics classrooms has been supported by some boards of education. It has even been mandated in the curriculum of some regions.[^o26]

Computer algebra systems have been extensively used in higher education.[^o27][^o28] Many universities offer either specific courses on developing their use, or they implicitly expect students to use them for their course work. The companies that develop computer algebra systems have pushed to increase their prevalence among university and college programs.[^o29][^o30]

CAS-equipped calculators are not permitted on the [ACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_%28test%29), the [PLAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLAN_%28test%29), the [SAT](wikipedia://en/144716), and in some classrooms[^o31][^o32] though it may be permitted on [College Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Board)'s calculator-permitted [AP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Placement) tests, including the [AP Calculus](wikipedia://en/4234894), [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_Chemistry), [Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_Physics), and [Statistics](wikipedia://en/4973089) exams.[^o32]

## Mathematics used in computer algebra systems {#Mathematics_used_in_computer_algebra_systems}

- [Knuth–Bendix completion algorithm](wikipedia://en/614147)[^o34]
- [Root-finding algorithms](wikipedia://en/153299)[^o34]
- [Symbolic integration](wikipedia://en/1652672) via e.g. [Risch algorithm](wikipedia://en/289450) or [Risch–Norman algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risch–Norman_algorithm)
- [Hypergeometric summation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_summation) via e.g. [Gosper's algorithm](wikipedia://en/19590493)
- [Limit computation](wikipedia://en/26551602) via e.g. Gruntz's algorithm
- [Polynomial factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_factorization) via e.g., over finite fields,[^o35] [Berlekamp's algorithm](wikipedia://en/6057100) or [Cantor–Zassenhaus algorithm](wikipedia://en/6059689).
- [Greatest common divisor](wikipedia://en/12354) via e.g. [Euclidean algorithm](wikipedia://en/10377)
- [Gaussian elimination](wikipedia://en/13035)[^o36]
- [Gröbner basis](wikipedia://en/357328) via e.g. [Buchberger's algorithm](wikipedia://en/696317); generalization of Euclidean algorithm and Gaussian elimination
- [Padé approximant](wikipedia://en/3090820)
- [Schwartz–Zippel lemma](wikipedia://en/2789076) and testing polynomial identities
- [Chinese remainder theorem](wikipedia://en/7713)
- [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109)
- [Landau's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau's_algorithm) (nested radicals)
- Derivatives of [elementary functions](wikipedia://en/10412) and [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110). (e.g. See [derivatives of the incomplete gamma function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivatives_of_the_incomplete_gamma_function).)
- [Cylindrical algebraic decomposition](wikipedia://en/12218154)
- [Quantifier elimination](wikipedia://en/2150441) over real numbers via cylindrical algebraic decomposition
