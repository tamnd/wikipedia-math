# Greibach normal form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Greibach normal form"}

In [formal language](wikipedia://en/10939) theory, a [context-free grammar](wikipedia://en/6759) is in **Greibach normal form** (**GNF**) if the right-hand sides of all [production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_%28computer_science%29) rules start with a [terminal symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_symbol), optionally followed by some non-terminals. A non-strict form allows one exception to this format restriction for allowing the [empty word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_word) (epsilon, ε) to be a member of the described language. The normal form was established by [Sheila Greibach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheila_Greibach) and it bears her name.

:::

More precisely, a context-free grammar is in Greibach normal form, if all production rules are of the form:

$$A \to a A_1 A_2 \cdots A_n$$

where $A$ is a [nonterminal symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonterminal_symbol), $a$ is a terminal symbol, and $A_1 A_2 \ldots A_n$ is a (possibly empty) sequence of nonterminal symbols.

Observe that the grammar does not have [left recursions](wikipedia://en/1418498).

Every context-free grammar can be transformed into an equivalent grammar in Greibach normal form.[^o2] Various constructions exist. Some do not permit the second form of rule and cannot transform context-free grammars that can generate the empty word. For one such construction the size of the constructed grammar is O($n$<sup>4</sup>) in the general case and O($n$<sup>3</sup>) if no derivation of the original grammar consists of a single nonterminal symbol, where $n$ is the size of the original grammar.[^o3] This conversion can be used to prove that every [context-free language](wikipedia://en/6867) can be accepted by a real-time (non-deterministic) [pushdown automaton](wikipedia://en/24510), i.e., the automaton reads a letter from its input every step.

Given a grammar in GNF and a derivable string in the grammar with length $n$, any [top-down parser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top-down_parsing) will halt at depth $n$.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variants of Greibach normal form"}

## Variants of Greibach normal form {#Variants_of_Greibach_normal_form}

It is even possible to convert a grammar into Greibach normal form in a way such that in every production, at most two nonterminal symbols occur on the right-hand side; this is referred to as **quadratic Greibach normal form**.

A context-free grammar is in **double Greibach normal form**, if all production rules take one of the two forms:

$$A \to a A_1 A_2 \cdots A_nb$$

$$A \to a$$

where $A$ is a [nonterminal symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonterminal_symbol), $a,b$ are terminal symbols (not necessarily distinct), and $A_1 A_2 \ldots A_n$ is a (possibly empty) sequence of nonterminal symbols. Similarly as above, a grammar is in quadratic double Greibach normal form, if at most two nonterminal symbols occur in the right-hand side of each production. A classic result by Hotz (1978) states that every context-free grammar that does not generate the empty word can be effectively converted into an equivalent grammar in quadratic double Greibach normal form.
