# Baire category theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Baire category theorem"}

The **Baire category theorem** (**BCT**) is an important result in [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). The theorem has two forms, each of which gives [sufficient conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficient_condition) for a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) to be a [Baire space](wikipedia://en/200550) (a topological space such that the [intersection](wikipedia://en/41780044) of [countably](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably) many [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358) is still dense). It is used in the proof of results in many areas of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), including some of the fundamental theorems of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180).

:::

Versions of the Baire category theorem were first proved independently in 1897 by [Osgood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Fogg_Osgood) for the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) $\mathbb{R}$ and in 1899 by [Baire](wikipedia://en/1550892)[^o2] for [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$.[^n1] The more general statement for [completely metrizable spaces](wikipedia://en/7975189) was first shown by [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/10989)[^n2] in 1914.

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

A [Baire space](wikipedia://en/200550) is a topological space $X$ in which every [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) intersection of [open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_%28topology%29) [dense sets](wikipedia://en/23523594) is dense in $X.$ See the corresponding article for a list of equivalent characterizations, as some are more useful than others depending on the application.

- (**BCT1**) Every [completely metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_metrizable) topological space is a Baire space.[^n3] More generally, every [complete](wikipedia://en/7832) [pseudometric space](wikipedia://en/68503) is a Baire space.[^n4][^n5][^n6]
- (**BCT2**) Every [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) is a Baire space.[^n7][^n3] More generally, every [locally compact regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_regular) space is a Baire space.[^n4][^n8]

Neither of these statements directly implies the other, since there are complete metric spaces that are not locally compact (the [irrational numbers](wikipedia://en/20647689) with the metric defined below; also, any [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) of [infinite dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_of_a_vector_space)), and there are locally compact Hausdorff spaces that are not [metrizable](wikipedia://en/19738) (for instance, any uncountable product of non-trivial compact Hausdorff spaces; also, several function spaces used in functional analysis; the uncountable [Fort space](wikipedia://en/10105571)). See [Steen and Seebach](wikipedia://en/915449) in the references below.

:::

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Relation to the axiom of choice"}

## Relation to the axiom of choice {#Relation_to_the_axiom_of_choice}

The proof of **BCT1** for arbitrary complete metric spaces requires some form of the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840); and in fact BCT1 is equivalent over [ZF](wikipedia://en/152214) to the [axiom of dependent choice](wikipedia://en/441950), a weak form of the axiom of choice.[^o17]

A restricted form of the Baire category theorem, in which the complete metric space is also assumed to be [separable](wikipedia://en/27855), is provable in ZF with no additional choice principles.[^n9] This restricted form applies in particular to the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line), the [Baire space](wikipedia://en/2078245) $\omega^{\omega},$ the [Cantor space](wikipedia://en/352376) $2^{\omega},$ and a separable [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) such as the [$L^p$-space](wikipedia://en/45194) $L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$.

:::

::: {#o20 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), **BCT1** can be used to prove the [open mapping theorem](wikipedia://en/17395276), the [closed graph theorem](wikipedia://en/64620077) and the [uniform boundedness principle](wikipedia://en/247392).

**BCT1** also shows that every nonempty complete metric space with no [isolated point](wikipedia://en/390557) is [uncountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable). (If $X$ is a nonempty countable metric space with no isolated point, then each [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798) $\{x\}$ in $X$ is [nowhere dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowhere_dense), and $X$ is [meagre](wikipedia://en/226975) in itself.) In particular, this proves that the set of all [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) is uncountable.

**BCT1** shows that each of the following is a Baire space:

- The space $\mathbb{R}$ of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438)
- The [irrational numbers](wikipedia://en/20647689), with the metric defined by $d(x, y) = \tfrac{1}{n+1},$ where $n$ is the first index for which the [continued fraction](wikipedia://en/739199) expansions of $x$ and $y$ differ (this is a complete metric space)
- The [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172)

By **BCT2**, every finite-dimensional Hausdorff [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) is a Baire space, since it is locally compact and Hausdorff. This is so even for non-[paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact) (hence nonmetrizable) manifolds such as the [long line](wikipedia://en/338403).

**BCT** is used to prove [Hartogs's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartogs's_theorem), a fundamental result in the theory of several complex variables.

**BCT1** is used to prove that a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) cannot have countably infinite dimension.

:::

::: {#o22 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

(**BCT1**) The following is a standard proof that a complete pseudometric space $X$ is a Baire space.[^n6]

Let $U_1, U_2, \ldots$ be a countable collection of open dense subsets. We want to show that the intersection $U_1 \cap U_2 \cap \ldots$ is dense. A subset is dense if and only if every nonempty open subset intersects it. Thus to show that the intersection is dense, it suffices to show that any nonempty open subset $W$ of $X$ has some point $x$ in common with all of the $U_n$. Because $U_1$ is dense, $W$ intersects $U_1;$ consequently, there exists a point $x_1$ and a number $0 < r_1 < 1$ such that:

$$\overline{B}\left(x_1, r_1\right) \subseteq W \cap U_1$$

where $B(x, r)$ and $\overline{B}(x, r)$ denote an open and closed ball, respectively, centered at $x$ with radius $r.$ Since each $U_n$ is dense, this construction can be continued recursively to find a pair of sequences $x_n$ and $0 < r_n < \tfrac{1}{n}$ such that:

$$\overline{B}\left(x_n, r_n\right) \subseteq B\left(x_{n-1}, r_{n-1}\right) \cap U_n.$$

(This step relies on the axiom of choice and the fact that a finite intersection of open sets is open and hence an open ball can be found inside it centered at $x_n$.) The sequence $\left(x_n\right)$ is [Cauchy](wikipedia://en/6085) because $x_n \in B\left(x_m, r_m\right)$ whenever $n > m,$ and hence $\left(x_n\right)$ converges to some limit $x$ by completeness. If $n$ is a positive integer then $x \in \overline{B}\left(x_n, r_n\right)$ (because this set is closed). Thus $x \in W$ and $x \in U_n$ for all $n.$ $\blacksquare$

There is an alternative proof using [Choquet's game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choquet's_game).[^o24]

(**BCT2**) The proof that a [locally compact regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_regular) space $X$ is a Baire space is similar.[^n8] It uses the facts that (1) in such a space every point has a [local base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_base) of [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_%28topology%29) [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_%28topology%29) neighborhoods; and (2) in a compact space any collection of closed sets with the [finite intersection property](wikipedia://en/365026) has nonempty intersection. The result for [locally compact Hausdorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_Hausdorff) spaces is a special case, as such spaces are regular.

:::
