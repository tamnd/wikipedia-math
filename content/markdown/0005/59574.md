# Biproduct

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Biproduct"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) and its applications to [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **biproduct** of a finite collection of [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29), in a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) with [zero objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object), is both a [product](wikipedia://en/237868) and a [coproduct](wikipedia://en/310889). In a [preadditive category](wikipedia://en/59521) the notions of product and coproduct coincide for finite collections of objects.[^o2] The biproduct is a generalization of finite [direct sums of modules](wikipedia://en/58899).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let **C** be a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) with [zero morphisms](wikipedia://en/320638). Given a finite (possibly empty) collection of objects *A*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *A*<sub>*n*</sub> in **C**, their *biproduct* is an [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) $A_1 \oplus \dots \oplus A_n$ in **C** together with [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891)

- $p_k \!: A_1 \oplus \dots \oplus A_n \to A_k$ in **C** (the *[projection](wikipedia://en/3909097) morphisms*)
- $i_k \!: A_k \to A_1 \oplus \dots \oplus A_n$ (the *[embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) morphisms*)

satisfying

- $p_k \circ i_k = 1_{A_k}$, the identity morphism of $A_k,$ and
- $p_l \circ i_k = 0$, the [zero morphism](wikipedia://en/320638) $A_k \to A_l,$ for $k \neq l,$

and such that

- $\left( A_1 \oplus \dots \oplus A_n, p_k \right)$ is a [product](wikipedia://en/237868) for the $A_k,$ and
- $\left( A_1 \oplus \dots \oplus A_n, i_k \right)$ is a [coproduct](wikipedia://en/310889) for the $A_k.$

If **C** is preadditive and the first two conditions hold, then each of the last two conditions is equivalent to $i_1 \circ p_1 + \dots + i_n\circ p_n = 1_{A_1 \oplus \dots \oplus A_n}$ when *n* > 0.[^o5] An empty, or [nullary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullary), product is always a [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object) in the category, and the empty coproduct is always an [initial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_object) in the category. Thus an empty, or nullary, biproduct is always a [zero object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object).

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

In the category of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974), biproducts always exist and are given by the [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_abelian_groups).[^o8] The zero object is the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059).

Similarly, biproducts exist in the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603). The biproduct is again the direct sum, and the zero object is the [trivial vector space](wikipedia://en/1463006#Trivial_or_zero_vector_space).

More generally, biproducts exist in the [category of modules](wikipedia://en/11231812) over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404).

On the other hand, biproducts do not exist in the [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092).[^o9] Here, the product is the [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015), but the coproduct is the [free product](wikipedia://en/360036).

Also, biproducts do not exist in the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829). For, the product is given by the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095), whereas the coproduct is given by the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946). This category does not have a zero object.

[Block matrix](wikipedia://en/457464) algebra relies upon biproducts in categories of [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859).[^o10]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

If the biproduct $A \oplus B$ exists for all pairs of objects *A* and *B* in the category **C**, and **C** has a zero object, then all finite biproducts exist, making **C** both a [Cartesian monoidal category](wikipedia://en/43676277) and a co-Cartesian monoidal category.

If **C** is a [preadditive category](wikipedia://en/59521), then every finite product is a biproduct, and every finite coproduct is a biproduct. For example, if $A_1 \times A_2$ exists, then there are unique morphisms $i_k: A_k \to A_1 \times A_2$ such that

- $$p_k \circ i_k = 1_{A_k},\ (k = 1, 2)$$
- $p_l \circ i_k = 0$ for $k \neq l.$

To see that $A_1 \times A_2$ is now also a coproduct, and hence a biproduct, suppose we have morphisms $f_k: A_k \to X,\ k=1,2$ for some object $X$. Define $f := f_1 \circ p_1 + f_2 \circ p_2.$ Then $f$ is a morphism from $A_1 \times A_2$ to $X$, and $f \circ i_k = f_k$ for $k = 1, 2$.

In this case, $i_1 \circ p_1 + i_2 \circ p_2 = 1_{A_1 \times A_2}$.

An [additive category](wikipedia://en/60854) is a [preadditive category](wikipedia://en/59521) in which all finite biproducts exist. In particular, biproducts always exist in [abelian categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_categories).
