# Edward Witten

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Edward Witten"}

**Edward Witten** (born August 26, 1951) is an American [theoretical physicist](wikipedia://en/19594028) known for his contributions to [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305), [topological quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/443757), [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) and various areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). He is a professor emeritus in the school of [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science) at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey).[^o5] Witten is a researcher in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305), [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312), [supersymmetric quantum field theories](wikipedia://en/224636), and other areas of mathematical physics. Witten's work has also significantly impacted pure mathematics.[^o6] In 1990, he became the first physicist to be awarded a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) by the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868), for his mathematical insights in physics, such as his 1981 proof of the [positive energy theorem](wikipedia://en/2608111) in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), and his interpretation of the [Jones](wikipedia://en/1227628) invariants of knots as [Feynman integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_integral).[^o7] He is considered the practical founder of [M-theory](wikipedia://en/20406).[^o8]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Witten was born on August 26, 1951, in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), Maryland, to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family,[^o10] as the eldest of four children. His brother [Matt Witten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Witten) became a writer, and his brother Jesse Amnon Witten became a law partner in the firm [Faegre Drinker Biddle & Reath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faegre_Drinker_Biddle_&_Reath).[^o11] Their sister Celia M. Witten earned a Ph.D. in mathematics from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and then an M.D. from the [University of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Miami).[^o13] Edward Witten is the son of Lorraine (born Wollach) Witten[^o14] and [Louis Witten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Witten), a [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physicist) specializing in [gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity) and [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024).[^o15]

Witten attended the [Park School of Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_School_of_Baltimore) (class of 1968), and received his [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree with a major in [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History) and minor in [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) from [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) in 1971.[^o16]

He had aspirations in journalism and politics and published articles in both *[The New Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Republic)* and *[The Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nation)* in the late 1960s.[^o17][^o18] In 1972, he worked for six months on [George McGovern's presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_McGovern_1972_presidential_campaign).[^o19]

Witten attended the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) for one semester as an economics graduate student before dropping out.[^o20] He returned to academia, enrolling in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1973, then shifting departments and receiving a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in physics in 1976 and completing a dissertation, "Some problems in the short distance analysis of gauge theories", under the supervision of [David Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gross).[^o21] He was a [National Radio Astronomy Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Radio_Astronomy_Observatory) summer student (1974),[^o22] held a fellowship at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) (1976–77), visited [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) (1977–78),[^o23][^o24] was a junior fellow in the Harvard Society of Fellows (1977–1980), and held a [MacArthur Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program) fellowship (1982).[^o5]

## Research {#Research}

### Fields medal work {#Fields_medal_work}

Witten was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) by the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) in 1990.[^o27]

In a written address to the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842), [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) said of Witten:[^o6]

*Image: Widden Mori.jpg.* Edward Witten (left) with mathematician [Shigefumi Mori](wikipedia://en/794811), probably at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1990, where they received the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859)

As an example of Witten's work in pure mathematics, Atiyah cites his application of techniques from [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) to the mathematical subject of [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774). In the late 1980s, Witten coined the term *[topological quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/443757)* for a certain type of physical theory in which the [expectation values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expectation_value) of observable quantities encode information about the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758).[^o28] In particular, Witten realized that a physical theory now called [Chern–Simons theory](wikipedia://en/391908) could provide a framework for understanding the mathematical theory of [knots](wikipedia://en/366808) and [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257).[^o29] Although Witten's work was based on the mathematically ill-defined notion of a [Feynman path integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_path_integral) and therefore not [mathematically rigorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_rigor), mathematicians were able to systematically develop Witten's ideas, leading to the theory of [Reshetikhin–Turaev invariants](wikipedia://en/60958267).[^o30]

Another result for which Witten was awarded the Fields Medal was his proof in 1981 of the [positive energy theorem](wikipedia://en/2608111) in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024).[^o31] This theorem asserts that (under appropriate assumptions) the total [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) of a gravitating system is always positive and can be zero only if the geometry of [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) is that of flat [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space). It establishes Minkowski space as a stable ground state of the [gravitational field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_field). While the original proof of this result due to [Richard Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen) and [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137) used [variational methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_methods),[^o32][^o33] Witten's proof used ideas from [supergravity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergravity_theory) to simplify the argument.[^o34]

A third area mentioned in Atiyah's address is Witten's work relating [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636) and [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560),[^o35] a branch of mathematics that studies the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of [manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifolds) using the concept of a [differentiable function](wikipedia://en/330206). Witten's work gave a physical proof of a classical result, the [Morse inequalities](wikipedia://en/348560#Morse_inequalities), by interpreting the theory in terms of [supersymmetric quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersymmetric_quantum_mechanics).[^o35]

### M-theory {#M-theory}

By the mid 1990s, physicists working on [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) had developed five different consistent versions of the theory. These versions are known as [type I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_I_string), [type IIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_IIA_string), [type IIB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_IIB_string), and the two flavors of [heterotic string](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotic_string) theory ([SO(32)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) and [E<sub>8</sub>×E<sub>8</sub>](wikipedia://en/647994)). The thinking was that of these five candidate theories, only one was the actual correct [theory of everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_everything), and that theory was the one whose low-energy limit matched the physics observed in our world today.[^o37]

Speaking at [Strings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strings_%28conference%29) '95 conference at [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California), Witten made the surprising suggestion that these five string theories were in fact not distinct theories, but different limits of a single theory, which he called [M-theory](wikipedia://en/20406).[^o38][^o39] Witten's proposal was based on the observation that the five string theories can be mapped to one another by certain rules called [dualities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_duality) and are identified by these dualities. It led to a flurry of work now known as the [second superstring revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_superstring_revolution).[^o37]

### Other work {#Other_work}

*Image: Gross Witten Hawking TIFR 2001.jpg.* Edward Witten (center) with [David Gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gross) and [Stephen Hawking](wikipedia://en/19376148) at [Strings 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strings_%28conference%29) at TIFR in Mumbai, India

Another of Witten's contributions to physics was to the result of gauge/gravity duality. In 1997, [Juan Maldacena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Maldacena) formulated a result known as the [AdS/CFT correspondence](wikipedia://en/644443), which establishes a relationship between certain [quantum field theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field_theories) and theories of [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312).[^o41] Maldacena's discovery has dominated high-energy theoretical physics for the past 15 years because of its applications to theoretical problems in quantum gravity and quantum field theory. Witten's foundational work following Maldacena's result has shed light on this relationship.[^o42]

In collaboration with [Nathan Seiberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Seiberg), Witten established several powerful results in quantum field theories. In their paper on string theory and [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917), Seiberg and Witten studied certain [noncommutative quantum field theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_quantum_field_theory) that arise as limits of string theory.[^o43] In another well-known paper, they studied aspects of [supersymmetric gauge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersymmetric_gauge_theory).[^o44] The latter paper, combined with Witten's earlier work on topological quantum field theory,[^o28] led to developments in the topology of [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) [4-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1362795), in particular the notion of [Seiberg–Witten invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiberg–Witten_invariant).[^o45]

With [Anton Kapustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Kapustin), Witten has made deep mathematical connections between S-duality of gauge theories and the [geometric Langlands correspondence](wikipedia://en/10500178).[^o46] Partly in collaboration with Seiberg, one of his recent interests includes aspects of field theoretical description of topological phases in condensed matter and non-supersymmetric dualities in field theories that, among other things, are of high relevance in condensed matter theory. In 2016, he has also brought tensor models to the relevance of holographic and quantum gravity theories, by using them as a generalization of the [Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev model](wikipedia://en/62223541).[^o47]

Witten has published influential and insightful work in many aspects of quantum field theories and mathematical physics, including the physics and mathematics of anomalies, integrability, dualities, localization, and homologies. Many of his results have deeply influenced areas in theoretical physics (often well beyond the original context of his results), including string theory, quantum gravity and topological condensed matter.[^o48] In particular, Witten is known for collaborating with [Ruth Britto](wikipedia://en/67093393) on a method calculating scattering amplitudes known as the [BCFW recursion relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCFW_recursion).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Witten has been honored with numerous awards including a [MacArthur Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellowship) (1982), the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) (1990), the Golden Plate Award of the [American Academy of Achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Achievement) (1997),[^o50] the [Nemmers Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1252473) (2000), the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science)[^o51] (2002), Pythagoras Award[^o52] (2005), the [Henri Poincaré Prize](wikipedia://en/4890712) (2006), the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize) (2008), the [Lorentz Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_Medal) (2010) the [Isaac Newton Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton_Medal) (2010) and the [Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Fundamental_Physics) (2012). Since 1999, he has been a Foreign Member of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) (London), and in March 2016 was elected an Honorary Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh).[^o53][^o54] [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) appointed Witten as a member of the [Pontifical Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Academy_of_Sciences) (2006). He also appeared in the list of *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_%28magazine%29)* magazine's [100 most influential people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100) of 2004. In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o55] Witten was elected as a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1984, a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1988, and a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1993.[^o56][^o57][^o58] In May 2022 he was awarded an honorary Doctor of Sciences from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania).[^o59]

In an informal poll at a 1990 cosmology conference, Witten received the largest number of mentions as "the smartest living physicist".[^o60]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Witten has been married to [Chiara Nappi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiara_Nappi), a professor of physics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), since 1979.[^o62] They have two daughters and a son. Their daughter [Ilana B. Witten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilana_B._Witten) is a neuroscientist at Princeton University,[^o63] and daughter [Daniela Witten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniela_Witten) is a biostatistician at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington).[^o64]

Witten sits on the board of directors of [Americans for Peace Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans_for_Peace_Now) and on the advisory council of [J Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_Street_%28advocacy_group%29).[^o65] He supports the [two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution) and advocates a boycott of Israeli institutions and economic activity beyond its 1967 borders, though not of Israel itself.[^o66] Witten lived in Israel for a year in the 1960s.[^o67]
