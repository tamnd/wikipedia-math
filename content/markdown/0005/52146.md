# Julius Plücker

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Julius Plücker"}

**Julius Plücker** (16 July 1801 – 22 May 1868) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist). He made fundamental contributions to the field of [analytical geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_geometry) and was a pioneer in the investigations of [cathode rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode_ray) that led eventually to the [discovery of the electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_of_the_electron). He also vastly extended the study of [Lamé curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamé_curve).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early years {#Early_years}

Plücker was born at [Elberfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elberfeld) (now part of [Wuppertal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuppertal)) on July 16th, 1801.[^o6] After being educated at [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Düsseldorf) and at the universities of [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg) and [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) he went to [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) in 1823,[^o7] where he came under the influence of the great school of French geometers, whose founder, [Gaspard Monge](wikipedia://en/77840), had only recently died.

In 1825 he returned to Bonn, and in 1828 was made professor of mathematics.[^o7]

In the same year he published the first volume of his *Analytisch-geometrische Entwicklungen*, which introduced the method of "abridged notation".

In 1831 he published the second volume, in which he clearly established on a firm and independent basis [projective duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_duality).

### Career {#Career}

In 1836, Plücker was made professor of physics at [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn). In 1858, after a year of working with vacuum tubes of his Bonn colleague [Heinrich Geißler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Geißler),[^o9] he published his first classical researches on the action of the magnet on the electric discharge in rarefied gases. He found that the discharge caused a fluorescent glow to form on the glass walls of the vacuum tube, and that the glow could be made to shift by applying an electromagnet to the tube, thus creating a magnetic field.[^o10] It was later shown that the glow was produced by cathode rays.

Plücker, first by himself and afterwards in conjunction with [Johann Hittorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Wilhelm_Hittorf), made many important discoveries in the spectroscopy of gases. He was the first to use the vacuum tube with the capillary part now called a [Geissler tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geissler_tube), by means of which the luminous intensity of feeble electric discharges was raised sufficiently to allow of spectroscopic investigation. He anticipated [Robert Wilhelm Bunsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wilhelm_Bunsen) and [Gustav Kirchhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Kirchhoff) in announcing that the lines of the spectrum were characteristic of the chemical substance which emitted them, and in indicating the value of this discovery in chemical analysis. According to Hittorf, he was the first who saw the three lines of the hydrogen spectrum, which a few months after his death, were recognized in the spectrum of the solar protuberances.

In 1865, Plücker returned to the field of geometry and invented what was known as *[line geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_geometry)* in the nineteenth century. In [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), [Plücker coordinates](wikipedia://en/1168486) refer to a set of [homogeneous co-ordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_co-ordinates) introduced initially to embed the space of lines in projective space $\mathbf{P}^3$ as the [Klein quadric](wikipedia://en/2933091) in $\mathbf{P}^5$. The construction uses 2×2 [minor determinants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_determinant), or equivalently the second [exterior power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_power) of the underlying [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of dimension 4. It is now part of the theory of [Grassmannians](wikipedia://en/373810) $\mathbf{Gr}(k, V)$ ($k$-dimensional subspaces of an $n$-dimensional vector space $V$), to which the generalization of these co-ordinates to $k \times k$ minors of the $n \times k$ matrix of homogeneous coordinates, also known as [Plücker coordinates](wikipedia://en/1168486), apply. The embedding of the Grassmannian $\mathbf{Gr}(k, V)$ into the projectivization $\mathbf{P}(\Lambda^k(V))$ of the $k$th exterior power of $V$ is known as the [Plücker embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plucker_embedding).
