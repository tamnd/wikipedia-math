# Péclet number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Péclet number"}

In [continuum mechanics](wikipedia://en/5918), the **Péclet number** ($\mathbf{Pe}$, after [Jean Claude Eugène Péclet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Claude_Eugène_Péclet)) is a class of [dimensionless numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_number) relevant in the study of [transport phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_phenomena) in a continuous environment. It is defined to be the ratio of the rate of [advection](wikipedia://en/166377) of a [physical quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_quantity) by the flow to the rate of [diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion) of the same quantity driven by an appropriate [gradient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_gradient). In the context of species or [mass transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transfer), the Péclet number is the product of the [Reynolds number](wikipedia://en/23868856) and the [Schmidt number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmidt_number) ($\operatorname{Re} \times \operatorname{Sc}$). In the context of the [thermal fluids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_fluids), the thermal Péclet number is equivalent to the product of the Reynolds number and the [Prandtl number](wikipedia://en/58283) ($\operatorname{Re} \times \Pr$).

:::

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Pe less 1.gif.* Plan view: For $Pe_L \to 0$, advection is negligible, and diffusion dominates mass transport.

The Péclet number is defined as

$$\mathrm{Pe} = \dfrac{\text{advective transport rate}}{\text{diffusive transport rate}}.$$

*Image: Pe equal 1.png.* Plan view: For $Pe_L = 1$, diffusion and advection occur over equal times, and both have a non-negligible influence on mass transport.

:::

## Usages {#Usages}

### Mass transfer {#Mass_transfer}

For mass transfer, it is defined as

$$\mathrm{Pe}_L = \frac{L u}{D} = \mathrm{Re}_L \, \mathrm{Sc},$$

where $L$ is the [characteristic length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_length), $u$ the local [flow velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_velocity), $D$ the [mass diffusion coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fick's_law), $\operatorname{Re}$ the Reynolds number, $\operatorname{Sc}$ the Schmidt number.

Such ratio can also be re-written in terms of times, as a ratio between the characteristic temporal intervals of the system:

$$\mathrm{Pe}_L = \frac{u/L}{D/L^2} = \frac{L^2/D}{L/u} = \frac{\text{diffusion time}}{\text{advection time}}.$$

For $\mathrm{Pe_L} \gg 1$ the diffusion happens in a much longer time compared to the advection, and therefore the latter of the two phenomena predominates in the mass transport.

### Heat transfer {#Heat_transfer}

*Image: Pe greater 1.png.* Plan view: For $Pe_L \rightarrow \infty$, diffusion is negligible, and advection dominates mass transport.

For [heat transfer](wikipedia://en/184726), the Péclet number is defined as

$$\mathrm{Pe}_L = \frac{L u}{\alpha} = \mathrm{Re}_L \, \mathrm{Pr},$$

where $\Pr$ the Prandtl number, and $\alpha$ the [thermal diffusivity](wikipedia://en/58282),

$$\alpha = \frac{k}{\rho c_p},$$

where $k$ is the [thermal conductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_conductivity), $\rho$ the [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density), and $c_{p}$ the [specific heat capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_heat_capacity).

## Engineering applications {#Engineering_applications}

In engineering applications, the Péclet number is often very large. In such situations, the dependency of the flow upon *downstream* locations is diminished, and variables in the flow tend to become "one-way" properties. Thus, when modelling certain situations with high Péclet numbers, simpler computational models can be adopted.[^o8]

A flow will often have different Péclet numbers for heat and mass. This can lead to the phenomenon of [double diffusive convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_diffusive_convection).

In the context of particulate motion, the Péclet number has also been called **Brenner number**, with symbol $\mathbf{Br}$, in honour of [Howard Brenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Brenner).[^o9]

## Other usages {#Other_usages}

The Péclet number also finds applications beyond transport phenomena, as a general measure for the relative importance of the random fluctuations and of the systematic average behavior in mesoscopic systems.[^o11]
