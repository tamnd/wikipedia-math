# Figure-eight knot (mathematics)

*Image: Figure8knot-mathematical-knot-theory.svg.* Figure-eight knot of practical knot-tying, with ends joined

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Figure-eight knot"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), a **figure-eight knot** (also called **Listing's knot**[^o3]) is the unique knot with a [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298598) of four. This makes it the knot with the third-smallest possible crossing number, after the [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075) and the [trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773). The figure-eight knot is a [prime knot](wikipedia://en/315030).

:::

## Origin of name {#Origin_of_name}

The name is given because tying a normal [figure-eight knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure-eight_knot) in a rope and then joining the ends together, in the most natural way, gives a model of the mathematical knot.

## Description {#Description}

A simple parametric representation of the figure-eight knot is as the set of all points (*x*,*y*,*z*) where

$$\begin{aligned} x & = 2 \cos{(3t)} + \cos{(t)}, \\ y & = 2 \sin{(3t)} + \sin{(t)}, \\ z & = \sin{(4t)}, \end{aligned}$$

for *t* varying over the real numbers (see 2D visual realization at bottom right).

The figure-eight knot is [prime](wikipedia://en/315030), [alternating](wikipedia://en/1236143), [rational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_knot) with an associated value of 5/3,[^o6] and is [achiral](wikipedia://en/4333700). The figure-eight knot is also a [fibered knot](wikipedia://en/1358592). This follows from other, less simple (but very interesting) representations of the knot:

(1) It is a *homogeneous*[^o7] [closed braid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_braid) (namely, the closure of the 3-string braid σ<sub>1</sub>σ<sub>2</sub><sup>−1</sup>σ<sub>1</sub>σ<sub>2</sub><sup>−1</sup>), and a theorem of [John Stallings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stallings) shows that any closed homogeneous braid is fibered.

(2) It is the link at (0,0,0,0) of an [isolated critical point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolated_critical_point) of a real-polynomial map F: **R**<sup>4</sup>→**R**<sup>2</sup>, so (according to a theorem of [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632)) the [Milnor map](wikipedia://en/2696842) of F is actually a fibration. [Bernard Perron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Perron) found the first such F for this knot, namely,

$$F(x, y, z, t)=G(x, y, z^2-t^2, 2zt),\,\!$$

where

$$\begin{aligned} G(x,y,z,t)=\ & (z(x^2+y^2+z^2+t^2)+x (6x^2-2y^2-2z^2-2t^2), \\ & \ t x \sqrt{2}+y (6x^2-2y^2-2z^2-2t^2)). \end{aligned}$$

## Mathematical properties {#Mathematical_properties}

The figure-eight knot has played an important role historically (and continues to do so) in the theory of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257). Sometime in the mid-to-late 1970s, [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190) showed that the figure-eight was [hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_knot), by [decomposing](wikipedia://en/1227329) its [complement](wikipedia://en/1350120) into two [ideal](wikipedia://en/4995922) [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/241291) [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra). ([Robert Riley](wikipedia://en/64550014) and [Troels Jørgensen](wikipedia://en/31074567), working independently of each other, had earlier shown that the figure-eight knot was hyperbolic by other means.) This construction, new at the time, led him to many powerful results and methods. For example, he was able to show that all but ten [Dehn surgeries](wikipedia://en/1240699) on the figure-eight knot resulted in non-[Haken](wikipedia://en/236567), non-[Seifert-fibered](wikipedia://en/1631931) [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_decomposition_%283-manifold%29) 3-manifolds; these were the first such examples. Many more have been discovered by generalizing Thurston's construction to other knots and links.

The figure-eight knot is also the hyperbolic knot whose complement has the smallest possible [volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_volume_%28knot%29), $6\Lambda(\pi/3) \approx 2.02988...$ , where $\Lambda$ is the [Lobachevsky function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobachevsky_function).[^o9] From this perspective, the figure-eight knot can be considered the simplest hyperbolic knot. The figure eight knot complement is a [double-cover](wikipedia://en/250323) of the [Gieseking manifold](wikipedia://en/23010120), which has the smallest volume among non-compact hyperbolic 3-manifolds.

The figure-eight knot and the [(−2,3,7) pretzel knot](wikipedia://en/9911144) are the only two hyperbolic knots known to have more than 6 *exceptional surgeries*, Dehn surgeries resulting in a non-hyperbolic 3-manifold; they have 10 and 7, respectively. A theorem of [Lackenby](wikipedia://en/49182345) and Meyerhoff, whose proof relies on the [geometrization conjecture](wikipedia://en/220642) and [computer assistance](wikipedia://en/2840305), holds that 10 is the largest possible number of exceptional surgeries of any hyperbolic knot. However, it is not currently known whether the figure-eight knot is the only one that achieves the bound of 10. A well-known conjecture is that the bound (except for the two knots mentioned) is 6.

The figure-eight knot has genus 1 and is fibered. Therefore its complement fibers over the circle, the fibers being [Seifert surfaces](wikipedia://en/1343951) which are 2-dimensional tori with one boundary component. The [monodromy map](wikipedia://en/1358592) is then a homeomorphism of the 2-torus, which can be represented in this case by the matrix $(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix})$.

## Invariants {#Invariants}

The [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) of the figure-eight knot is

$$\Delta(t) = -t + 3 - t^{-1},\$$

since $\begin{pmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 0 & -1\end{pmatrix}$ is a possible [Seifert matrix](wikipedia://en/1343951), or because of its [Conway polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647#Alexander–Conway_polynomial), which is

$\nabla(z) = 1-z^2,\$[^o11]

and the [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642) is

$$V(q) = q^2 - q + 1 - q^{-1} + q^{-2}.\$$

The symmetry between $q$ and $q^{-1}$ in the Jones polynomial reflects the fact that the figure-eight knot is achiral.
