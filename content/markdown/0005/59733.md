# Hexagon

Regular polygon stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hexagon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **hexagon** (from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) ἕξ, hex, meaning "six", and γωνία, gonía, meaning "corner, angle") is a six-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621).[^o2] The total of the internal angles of any [simple](wikipedia://en/1059530) (non-self-intersecting) hexagon is 720°.

:::

## Regular hexagon {#Regular_hexagon}

A regular hexagon is defined as a hexagon that is both [equilateral](wikipedia://en/214547) and [equiangular](wikipedia://en/2476494). Its [internal angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) is one-third of a circle, equal to 120°. The [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) denotes this polygon as $\{6\}$.[^o4] However, the regular hexagon can also be considered as cutting off the vertices of an equilateral triangle, which can also be denoted as $\mathrm{t}\{3\}$.

A regular hexagon is [bicentric](wikipedia://en/13290844), meaning that it is both [cyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_polygon) (has a circumscribed circle) and [tangential](wikipedia://en/35520654) (has an inscribed circle). The common length of the sides equals the radius of the [circumscribed circle](wikipedia://en/161262) or [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432), which equals $\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$ times the [apothem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apothem) (radius of the [inscribed circle](wikipedia://en/4470203)).

### Measurement {#Measurement}

The longest diagonals of a regular hexagon, connecting diametrically opposite vertices, are twice the length of one side. From this it can be seen that a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) with a vertex at the center of the regular hexagon and sharing one side with the hexagon is [equilateral](wikipedia://en/173285), and that the regular hexagon can be partitioned into six equilateral triangles.

*Image: Regular hexagon 1.svg.* *R* = [Circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius); *r* = [Inradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inradius); *t* = side length

The maximal [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007#Polygons) (which corresponds to the long [diagonal](wikipedia://en/349251) of the hexagon), *D*, is twice the maximal radius or [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius), *R*, which equals the side length, *t*. The minimal diameter or the diameter of the [inscribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscribed) circle (separation of parallel sides, flat-to-flat distance, short diagonal or height when resting on a flat base), *d*, is twice the minimal radius or [inradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inradius), *r*. The maxima and minima are related by the same factor:

$$\begin{aligned} r &= \frac{d}{2} = \cos(30^\circ) R = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} R = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} t\\ d &= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} D\\ \end{aligned}$$

The area of a regular hexagon

$$\begin{aligned} A &= \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}R^2 &&= 3Rr = 2\sqrt{3} r^2 \\ &\approx 2.598 R^2 &&\approx 3.464 r^2\\ &= \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{8}D^2 &&= \frac{3}{4}Dd = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} d^2 \\ &\approx 0.6495 D^2 &&\approx 0.866 d^2. \end{aligned}$$

For any regular [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621), the area can also be expressed in terms of the [apothem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apothem) *a* and the perimeter *p*. For the regular hexagon these are given by *a* = *r*, and *p*${} = 6R = 4r\sqrt{3}$, so

$$\begin{aligned} A &= \frac{ap}{2} \\ &= \frac{r \cdot 4r\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2r^2\sqrt{3} \\ &\approx 3.464 r^2. \end{aligned}$$

The regular hexagon fills the fraction $\tfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2\pi} \approx 0.8270$ of its [circumscribed circle](wikipedia://en/161262).

If a regular hexagon has successive vertices A, B, C, D, E, F and if P is any point on the circumcircle between B and C, then PE + PF = PA + PB + PC + PD.

It follows from the ratio of [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) to [inradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inradius) that the height-to-width ratio of a regular hexagon is 1:1.1547005; that is, a hexagon with a long [diagonal](wikipedia://en/349251) of 1.0000000 will have a distance of 0.8660254 or cos(30°) between parallel sides.

### Point in plane {#Point_in_plane}

For an arbitrary point in the plane of a regular hexagon with circumradius $R$, whose distances to the centroid of the regular hexagon and its six vertices are $L$ and $d_i$ respectively, we have[^o7]

$$d_1^2 + d_4^2 = d_2^2 + d_5^2 = d_3^2+ d_6^2= 2\left(R^2 + L^2\right),$$

$$d_1^2 + d_3^2+ d_5^2 = d_2^2 + d_4^2+ d_6^2 = 3\left(R^2 + L^2\right),$$

$$d_1^4 + d_3^4+ d_5^4 = d_2^4 + d_4^4+ d_6^4 = 3\left(\left(R^2 + L^2\right)^2 + 2 R^2 L^2\right).$$

If $d_i$ are the distances from the vertices of a regular hexagon to any point on its circumcircle, then [^o7]

$$\left(\sum_{i=1}^6 d_i^2\right)^2 = 4 \sum_{i=1}^6 d_i^4 .$$

### Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Hexagon reflections.svg.* The six lines of [reflection](wikipedia://en/1390873) of a regular hexagon, with Dih<sub>6</sub> or **r12** symmetry, order 12.

*Image: Regular hexagon symmetries.svg.* The dihedral symmetries are divided depending on whether they pass through vertices (**d** for diagonal) or edges (**p** for perpendiculars) Cyclic symmetries in the middle column are labeled as **g** for their central gyration orders. Full symmetry of the regular form is **r12** and no symmetry is labeled **a1**.

A regular hexagon has six [rotational symmetries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_symmetries) (*rotational symmetry of order six*) and six [reflection symmetries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_symmetries) (*six lines of symmetry*), making up the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) D<sub>6</sub>.[^o12] There are 16 subgroups. There are 8 up to isomorphism: itself (D<sub>6</sub>), 2 dihedral: (D<sub>3,</sub> D<sub>2</sub>), 4 [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327): (Z<sub>6</sub>, Z<sub>3</sub>, Z<sub>2</sub>, Z<sub>1</sub>) and the trivial (e)

These symmetries express nine distinct symmetries of a regular hexagon. [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) labels these by a letter and group order.[^o13] **r12** is full symmetry, and **a1** is no symmetry. **p6**, an [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560) hexagon constructed by three mirrors can alternate long and short edges, and **d6**, an [isotoxal](wikipedia://en/6289189) hexagon constructed with equal edge lengths, but vertices alternating two different internal angles. These two forms are [duals](wikipedia://en/19865549) of each other and have half the symmetry order of the regular hexagon. The **i4** forms are regular hexagons flattened or stretched along one symmetry direction. It can be seen as an [elongated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongation_%28geometry%29) [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884), while **d2** and **p2** can be seen as horizontally and vertically elongated [kites](wikipedia://en/84840). **g2** hexagons, with opposite sides parallel are also called hexagonal [parallelogons](wikipedia://en/15302229).

Each subgroup symmetry allows one or more degrees of freedom for irregular forms. Only the **g6** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

Hexagons of symmetry **g2**, **i4**, and **r12**, as [parallelogons](wikipedia://en/15302229) can tessellate the Euclidean plane by translation. Other [hexagon shapes can tile the plane](wikipedia://en/2863719#Topologically_equivalent_tilings) with different orientations.

### Tessellations {#Tessellations}

Like [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) and [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285), regular hexagons fit together without any gaps to *tile the plane* (three hexagons meeting at every vertex), and so are useful for constructing [tessellations](wikipedia://en/321671).[^o17] The cells of a [beehive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beehive_%28beekeeping%29) [honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeycomb) are hexagonal for this reason and because the shape makes efficient use of space and building materials. The [Voronoi diagram](wikipedia://en/177668) of a regular triangular lattice is the honeycomb tessellation of hexagons.

## Related polygons and tilings {#Related_polygons_and_tilings}

A regular hexagon has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {6}. A regular hexagon is a part of the regular [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719), {6,3}, with three hexagonal faces around each vertex.

A regular hexagon can also be created as a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285), with Schläfli symbol t{3}. Seen with two types (colors) of edges, this form only has D<sub>3</sub> symmetry.

A [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) hexagon, t{6}, is a [dodecagon](wikipedia://en/508486), {12}, alternating two types (colors) of edges. An [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) hexagon, h{6}, is an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285), {3}. A regular hexagon can be [stellated](wikipedia://en/180253) with equilateral triangles on its edges, creating a [hexagram](wikipedia://en/302441). A regular hexagon can be dissected into six [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) by adding a center point. This pattern repeats within the regular [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858).

A regular hexagon can be extended into a regular [dodecagon](wikipedia://en/508486) by adding alternating [squares](wikipedia://en/659939) and [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) around it. This pattern repeats within the [rhombitrihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864311).

## Hexagonal structures {#Hexagonal_structures}

*Image: Giant's Causeway (13).JPG.* Giant's Causeway closeup

From bees' [honeycombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeycomb) to the [Giant's Causeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant's_Causeway), hexagonal patterns are prevalent in nature due to their efficiency. In a [hexagonal grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagonal_grid) each line is as short as it can possibly be if a large area is to be filled with the fewest hexagons. This means that honeycombs require less [wax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wax) to construct and gain much strength under [compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_%28physics%29).

Irregular hexagons with parallel opposite edges are called [parallelogons](wikipedia://en/15302229) and can also tile the plane by translation. In three dimensions, [hexagonal prisms](wikipedia://en/2859668) with parallel opposite faces are called [parallelohedrons](wikipedia://en/25207334) and these can tessellate 3-space by translation.

## Tesselations by hexagons {#Tesselations_by_hexagons}

In addition to the regular hexagon, which determines a unique tessellation of the plane, any irregular hexagon which satisfies the [Conway criterion](wikipedia://en/42806211) will tile the plane.

## Hexagon inscribed in a conic section {#Hexagon_inscribed_in_a_conic_section}

[Pascal's theorem](wikipedia://en/699966) (also known as the "Hexagrammum Mysticum Theorem") states that if an arbitrary hexagon is inscribed in any [conic section](wikipedia://en/19008673), and pairs of opposite [sides are extended](wikipedia://en/43958459) until they meet, the three intersection points will lie on a straight line, the "Pascal line" of that configuration.

::: {#o33 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Cyclic hexagon"}

### Cyclic hexagon {#Cyclic_hexagon}

The [Lemoine hexagon](wikipedia://en/3322811) is a [cyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_polygon) hexagon (one inscribed in a circle) with vertices given by the six intersections of the edges of a triangle and the three lines that are parallel to the edges that pass through its [symmedian point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmedian_point).

If the successive sides of a cyclic hexagon are *a*, *b*, *c*, *d*, *e*, *f*, then the three main diagonals intersect in a single point if and only if *ace* = *bdf*.[^o34]

If, for each side of a cyclic hexagon, the adjacent sides are extended to their intersection, forming a triangle exterior to the given side, then the segments connecting the circumcenters of opposite triangles are [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157).[^o35]

If a hexagon has vertices on the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) of an [acute triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_triangle) at the six points (including three triangle vertices) where the extended altitudes of the triangle meet the circumcircle, then the area of the hexagon is twice the area of the triangle.[^o36]

:::

## Hexagon tangential to a conic section {#Hexagon_tangential_to_a_conic_section}

Let ABCDEF be a hexagon formed by six [tangent lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_line) of a conic section. Then [Brianchon's theorem](wikipedia://en/1554065) states that the three main diagonals AD, BE, and CF intersect at a single point.

In a hexagon that is [tangential to a circle](wikipedia://en/35520654) and that has consecutive sides *a*, *b*, *c*, *d*, *e*, and *f*,[^o38]

$$a + c + e = b + d + f.$$

## Equilateral triangles on the sides of an arbitrary hexagon {#Equilateral_triangles_on_the_sides_of_an_arbitrary_hexagon}

*Image: Equilateral in hexagon.svg.* Equilateral triangles on the sides of an arbitrary hexagon

If an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) is constructed externally on each side of any hexagon, then the midpoints of the segments connecting the [centroids](wikipedia://en/187926) of opposite triangles form another equilateral triangle.[^o40]

## Skew hexagon {#Skew_hexagon}

*Image: Skew polygon in triangular antiprism.svg.* A regular skew hexagon seen as edges (black) of a [triangular antiprism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_antiprism), symmetry D<sub>3d</sub>, \[2<sup>\+</sup>,6\], (2\*3), order 12.

A **skew hexagon** is a [skew polygon](wikipedia://en/6545461) with six vertices and edges but not existing on the same plane. The interior of such a hexagon is not generally defined. A *skew zig-zag hexagon* has vertices alternating between two parallel planes.

A **regular skew hexagon** is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) with equal edge lengths. In three dimensions it will be a zig-zag skew hexagon and can be seen in the vertices and side edges of a [triangular antiprism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_antiprism) with the same D<sub>3d</sub>, \[2<sup>\+</sup>,6\] symmetry, order 12.

The [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) and [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) (same as triangular antiprism) have regular skew hexagons as petrie polygons.

## Convex equilateral hexagon {#Convex_equilateral_hexagon}

A *principal diagonal* of a hexagon is a diagonal which divides the hexagon into quadrilaterals. In any convex [equilateral](wikipedia://en/214547) hexagon (one with all sides equal) with common side *a*, there exists[^o48] a principal diagonal *d*<sub>1</sub> such that

$$\frac{d_1}{a} \leq 2$$

and a principal diagonal *d*<sub>2</sub> such that

$$\frac{d_2}{a} > \sqrt{3}.$$

## Hexagon versus Sexagon {#Hexagon_versus_Sexagon}

The debate over whether hexagons should be referred to as "sexagons" has its roots in the etymology of the term. The prefix "hex-" originates from the Greek word "hex," meaning six, while "sex-" comes from the Latin "sex," also signifying six. Some linguists and mathematicians argue that since many English mathematical terms derive from Latin, the use of "sexagon" would align with this tradition. Historical discussions date back to the 19th century, when mathematicians began to standardize terminology in geometry. However, the term "hexagon" has prevailed in common usage and academic literature, solidifying its place in mathematical terminology despite the historical argument for "sexagon." The consensus remains that "hexagon" is the appropriate term, reflecting its Greek origins and established usage in mathematics. (see [Numeral\_prefix#Occurrences](wikipedia://en/1705121#Occurrences)).
