# Demographics of Portugal

::: {#o5 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Portugal"}

[Demographic](wikipedia://en/45642) features of the population of [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal) include [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density), [ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group), education level, health of the populace, economic status, [religious affiliations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Portugal) and other aspects of the population. As of 2025, Portugal had an estimated population of 11,424,031 inhabitants.[^o6] Its population density, at \123.9 PD/km2, is higher than that of most [EU countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_state_of_the_European_Union), moderately surpassing the EU average of \105.4 PD/km2.[^o7][^o8]

:::

High fertility and birth rates persisted until the 1980s, after which they started to dramatically decline, leading to rapid population aging. At the end of the first decade of the 21st century, almost one in five [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) was over 65 years old.[^o9] Lately, Portugal has been experiencing a short growth in birth rates. The total fertility rate has climbed from an all-time low of 1.21 children per woman in 2013 to 1.43 in 2022, still well below [replacement level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-replacement_fertility).

Portugal is a fairly linguistically and religiously homogeneous country. Ethnically, the Portuguese people form a big majority of the total population in Portugal. The [Portuguese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) are mainly a combination of ancient paleolithic populations, and the [proto-Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Celtic), [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts), and the para-Celtic [Lusitanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusitanians). Some other groups, like the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) ([Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths), [Suevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suevi), [Buri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buri_tribe), [Alans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alans) and [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals)) and later the [Moorish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moors) ([Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs) and [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers)), [Sephardic Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jewish), and the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people) also passed through the country.

Today, [Brazilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilians_in_Portugal), Britons, [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_Portugal), Italians, French, [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_Portugal), [Nepalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalis_in_Portugal) and [countries members of PALOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Portuguese_people) ([Portuguese-speaking African countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese-speaking_African_countries)) are the main immigrants and form the major foreign communities in the country.[^o10][^o11]

Portuguese is spoken throughout the country, with only some villages near the northern municipality of [Miranda do Douro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miranda_do_Douro) speaking [Mirandese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirandese_language), locally recognised as a co-official language.

In the 2020s, Portugal reached 11 million people due to extensive immigration from other countries, most notably the former Portuguese colonies.[^o12][^o17]

## Population {#Population}

Due to a decrease in [emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_diaspora) and an increase in [immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Portugal) during the late 1990s and early 2000s, Portugal's population grew steadily, reaching a peak of 10,573,479 in December 2009. However, following the 2008 financial crisis, emigration surged and immigration declined, and these trends, combined with persistently low birth rates and an aging population, led to a gradual population decline. By 2018, the population had decreased by about 2.27% from its 2009 peak. From then onwards, however, net migration became positive again, supported by a renewed influx of immigrants and a slight rise in birth rates.[^o14][^o15] As a result, Portugal's population resumed growth and, by 2021, had already surpassed the 2009 peak. In 2025, Portugal's statistic service revealed that 10,74 million people lived in Portugal, using the pre-2026 methodology.[^o16]

In 2026, it became known that Portugal's population exceeded the milestone of 11 million people for the first time.[^o17] The revelation came after [INE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Nacional_de_Estatística_%28Portugal%29) revised upwards its population estimates for Portugal in the period 2021-26, as the INE adopted a new methodology to estimate the population of Portugal.[^o18] The new methodology used from 2026 onwards is aiming to better capture the realities of migratory movements in Portugal, increase the consistency of the population statistics with other official statistics and produce more analytical and detailed population statistics as required by the newer [European Union directives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directive_%28European_Union%29).[^o19]

The distribution of the population is widely uneven; the most densely populated areas are the [Lisbon metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_metropolitan_area) (which contains well above a quarter of the country's population), the metropolitan areas of [Porto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_metropolitan_area) and the [Atlantic coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Portugal), while other vast areas are very sparsely populated, like the plateaus of [Alentejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alentejo), the [Trás-os-Montes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trás-os-Montes) and [Serra da Estrela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_da_Estrela) highlands, and the lesser islands of the [Azores archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores).

Note: Crude migration change (per 1000) is a trend analysis, an extrapolation based average population change (current year minus previous) minus natural change of the current year (see table vital statistics). As average population is an estimate of the population in the middle of the year and not end of the year.

The population of the country almost doubled during the twentieth century (+91%), but the pattern of growth was extremely uneven due to large-scale internal migration from the rural areas to the industrial cities of Lisbon and Porto, a phenomenon which happened as a consequence of the [robust economic growth and structural modernisation, owing to a liberalisation of the economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_Portugal) of the 1960s.[^o20]

### Immigration {#Immigration}

*Image: Portuguese and foreign born population pyramid in 2021.svg.* Portuguese and foreign born population pyramid in 2021

In 1992, 1.3% of the population was foreign, and by 2025 the number had grown to 13.9% or 1,585,854 people.[^o130]

Since the independence of the former [African colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language_in_Africa), Portugal saw a steady immigration from Africa, most notably [Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde), [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola) and [Guinea-Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea-Bissau), but also [São Tomé and Príncipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/São_Tomé_and_Príncipe), [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique) and former [Portuguese India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India) in [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia).

Portugal saw migration waves due to labor shortages since 1999, first from Eastern Europe (1999–2002), in two distinctive groups, a *Slav* ([Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) and [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria)) and an *East Latin* ([Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania) and [Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova)), that stopped and started declining as the labour market became saturated.

Since 2003, most of the immigrants came from [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) and the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent). Family reunification was seen as important for a successful integration in the country, thus the government eased it, and in 2006, more than 6 in 10 new immigrants were family members of legal foreign residents in the country.[^o131]

There is also a significant number of elderly Western European residents in search of quality of life, namely from the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), [the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands) and [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium). More recently, there is significant migration from the former Portuguese colony of [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil), as since 2017 more than 30,000 Brazilians immigrate to Portugal annually.

### Ethnic minorities and persons with disabilities {#Ethnic_minorities_and_persons_with_disabilities}

Portugal does not collect ethnicity or racial data of its population.[^o142]

[Anti-racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-racism) laws prohibit and penalize racial discrimination in [housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House), business, and health services.

Discrimination against persons with disabilities in employment, education, access to health care, or the provision of other [state services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_services) is illegal. The law mandates access to public buildings and to newly built private buildings for such persons.

## Languages {#Languages}

*Image: Portugal portuguese dialects.png.* Map of Portuguese language dialects in Portugal.

The main language spoken as first language by the overwhelming majority of the population is [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language).[^o144] Other autochthonous languages spoken include:

- *Caló* (*see also [Caló language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caló_language)*), the language of the [Portuguese-Romani community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Portugal). There are about 52,000 Romani people in Portugal.[^o145]
- *Mirandês* (*see also [Mirandese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirandese_language)*), officially recognised as an official language.[^o146] It enjoys special protection in the area of [Miranda do Douro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miranda_do_Douro). As of today, there are about 15,000 people who speak the language (0.14%).[^o147][^o148][^o149]
- *Barranquenhu (see also [Barranquenho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquenho))*, spoken in the town of [Barrancos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrancos) (in the border between [Extremadura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremadura), [Andalusia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusia) and [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal)). As of today, there are about 3,000 speakers of the language (0.03%).[^o150]
- [Minderico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minderico) – a [sociolect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociolect) or [argot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argot) spoken in [Minde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minde,_Alcanena), practically extinct. There are about 150 speakers left[^o151][^o152]
- [Portuguese Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Sign_Language), the official language for the deaf community in Portugal. There are about 30,000 deaf people (0.29%) in Portugal who use the language.[^o153]

## Religion {#Religion}

The great majority of the Portuguese population belongs to the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Portugal). Religious observance remains strong in northern areas, while the population of Lisbon and southern areas are generally less devout.

Religious minorities include a little over 400,000 [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) and [Mormons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints)[^o156][^o157][^o158] (3.84% of the total population).

There are also about 100,000 [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims)[^o159] (1%) and 45,000 [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu)[^o160][^o161][^o162] (0.43%), most of whom came from [Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa), a former [Portuguese colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) on the west coast of India (Some Muslims also came from former two Portuguese African colonies with important Muslim minorities: [Guinea-Bissau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea-Bissau) and [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique)).

There are also about 1,500 [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew),[^o163] 2,000 Baha'i[^o164] and 35,000 [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs)[^o160] (0.34%).

Portugal is also home to about 17,000 [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism),[^o165] mostly [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people_in_Portugal) from [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau) and a few [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_Portugal) from Goa.

, Portugal is one of the most religious countries in Europe; most Portuguese believe with certainty in the [existence of God](wikipedia://en/621169), and religion plays an [important role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Importance_of_religion_by_country) in the life of most Portuguese.[^o166][^o167] According to the Pew Research Center, 40% of Portuguese Catholics pray daily.[^o168]
