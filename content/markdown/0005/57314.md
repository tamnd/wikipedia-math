# Adi Shamir

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adi Shamir"}

**Adi Shamir** (עדי שמיר; born July 6, 1952) is an Israeli [cryptographer](wikipedia://en/18934432) and inventor. He is a co-inventor of the [Rivest–Shamir–Adleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29) (RSA) [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) (along with [Ron Rivest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Rivest) and [Len Adleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Len_Adleman)), a co-inventor of the [Feige–Fiat–Shamir identification scheme](wikipedia://en/7116196) (along with [Uriel Feige](wikipedia://en/24734804) and [Amos Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Fiat)), one of the inventors of [differential cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/8536) and has made numerous contributions to the fields of [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). In 2002, [Ron Rivest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Rivest), [Len Adleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Len_Adleman), and Shamir won the ACM [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award).[^o32]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Adi Shamir was born in [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv). He received a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) (BSc) degree in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University) in 1973 and obtained an [MSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) and [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) from the [Weizmann Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute) in 1975 and 1977 respectively. He spent a year as a [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) and did research at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT) from 1977 to 1980.

## Scientific career {#Scientific_career}

In 1980, he returned to Israel, joining the faculty of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Science) at the [Weizmann Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute).[^o8] Starting from 2006, he is also an invited professor at [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in Paris.[^o8]

In addition to [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29), Shamir's other numerous inventions and contributions to cryptography include the [Shamir secret sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamir's_Secret_Sharing) scheme, the breaking of the [Merkle-Hellman knapsack cryptosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkle-Hellman_knapsack_cryptosystem), [visual cryptography](wikipedia://en/441935), and the [TWIRL](wikipedia://en/727814) and [TWINKLE](wikipedia://en/839471) [factoring](wikipedia://en/15491) devices. Together with [Eli Biham](wikipedia://en/348920), he discovered [differential cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/8536) in the late 1980s, a general method for attacking [block ciphers](wikipedia://en/4594). It later emerged that differential cryptanalysis was already known — and kept a secret — by both [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM)[^o9] and the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) (NSA).[^o10]

In 1984, Shamir introduced the concept of [identity-based cryptography](wikipedia://en/24158304), in which a user's public key can be derived from a unique identifier such as an email address.[^o11]

In 1986, together with [Amos Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Fiat), he devised the [Fiat–Shamir heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat–Shamir_heuristic), a widely used method for converting interactive [identification protocols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_protocol) into [digital signature](wikipedia://en/59644) schemes.[^o12]

Together with Rivest and Adleman, Shamir co-founded [RSA Security](wikipedia://en/301240), the company behind the annual [RSA Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_Conference) held since 1991.[^o13][^o32]

With [Alex Biryukov](wikipedia://en/9189491) and David Wagner, he presented in 2000 a real-time cryptanalysis of [A5/1](wikipedia://en/294149), the [stream cipher](wikipedia://en/49007) used to encrypt [GSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSM) mobile phone communications.[^o14]

In 2001, with [Ron Rivest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Rivest) and Yael Tauman, he introduced the concept of [ring signatures](wikipedia://en/5376829), which allow a member of a group to sign a message without revealing which member produced the signature.[^o15][^o16] The same year, together with Scott Fluhrer and Itsik Mantin, he published a key-recovery attack on the [RC4](wikipedia://en/25831) stream cipher, known as the [Fluhrer–Mantin–Shamir attack](wikipedia://en/10461532), which demonstrated a practical weakness in the [Wired Equivalent Privacy](wikipedia://en/422014) (WEP) protocol then used to secure [Wi-Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi) networks.[^o17]

Shamir has also developed a range of [side-channel attacks](wikipedia://en/667678) on cryptographic implementations, including cache attacks and [acoustic cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/1561321); in 2014, together with Daniel Genkin and [Eran Tromer](wikipedia://en/78318716), he demonstrated the extraction of full 4096-bit RSA keys from a laptop using the sound it emits during decryption.[^o18][^o19]

Since the late 2010s, Shamir has also studied the security of [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) systems, co-authoring work on the theory of adversarial examples, including the "Dimpled Manifold Model" of how neural network decision boundaries evolve during training,[^o20] and on cryptanalytic techniques for extracting the parameters of [neural network models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_network_models).[^o21]

In March 2019, Shamir was unable to attend the [RSA Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_Conference) in San Francisco, where he traditionally appeared on the Cryptographers' Panel, after his application for a US visa went unanswered for two months; addressing the conference by video, he suggested that the research community reconsider where it holds its scientific conferences.[^o22][^o13]

Beyond cryptography, Shamir has made contributions to computer science more broadly, such as finding the first [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time) algorithm for [2-satisfiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-satisfiability)[^o23] and proving, building on work of [Carsten Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carsten_Lund), [Lance Fortnow](wikipedia://en/25303350), Howard Karloff, and [Noam Nisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Nisan), that the [complexity classes](wikipedia://en/7543) [PSPACE](wikipedia://en/39351) and [IP](wikipedia://en/2197070) are equal.[^o24][^o25]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

- 1983 – [Erdős Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős_Prize) of the Israel Mathematical Society.[^o27][^o32]
- 1986 – [IEEE W.R.G. Baker Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_W.R.G._Baker_Award).[^o28]
- 1990 – UAP Scientific Prize (le Prix scientifique UAP ([Union des Assurances de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_des_Assurances_de_Paris))).
- 1992 – [Pontifical Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Academy_of_Sciences) (Vaticans [PIUS XI Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_XI_Gold_Medal)).[^o29]
- 1996 – [Paris Kanellakis Theory and Practice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Kanellakis_Theory_and_Practice_Award).[^o30]
- 1998 – Elected to the [Israel Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities).[^o32]
- 2000 – [IEEE Koji Kobayashi Computers and Communications Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Koji_Kobayashi_Computers_and_Communications_Award).[^o31]
- 2002 – [ACM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award), together with [Rivest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Rivest) and [Adleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Adleman), in recognition of his contributions to cryptography.[^o32]
- 2004 – Fellow of the [International Association for Cryptologic Research](wikipedia://en/87043).[^o32]
- 2005 – Elected to the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o32]
- 2008 – [Israel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Prize) for computer sciences.[^o33][^o34]
- 2009 – Honorary DMath (Doctor of Mathematics) degree from the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo)[^o35]
- 2017 – (33rd) [Japan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Prize) in the field of Electronics, Information and Communication for his contribution to [information security](wikipedia://en/15036) through pioneering research on cryptography.[^o36]
- 2018 – [Foreign Member of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Member_of_the_Royal_Society) (ForMemRS) for substantial contribution to the improvement of natural knowledge.[^o37]
- 2019 – Elected a Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society).[^o38]
- 2022 – Elected an International Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o16][^o39]
- 2024 – [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) for his fundamental contributions to Mathematical Cryptography.
- 2025 – [Levchin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levchin_Prize) for his contributions to Real World Cryptography.[^o40]
