# Hypersonic speed

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hypersonic speed"}

*Image: X-43A (Hyper - X) Mach 7 computational fluid dynamic (CFD).jpg.* [CFD](wikipedia://en/305924) image of the [NASA X-43A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_X-43) at Mach 7

In [aerodynamics](wikipedia://en/2819), **hypersonic speed** refers to speeds much faster than the [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853), usually more than approximately [Mach](wikipedia://en/20051) 5.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

The precise Mach number at which a craft can be said to be flying at hypersonic speed varies, since individual physical changes in the airflow (like molecular [dissociation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissociation_%28chemistry%29) and [ionization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionization)) occur at different speeds; these effects collectively become important around Mach 5–10. The hypersonic regime can also be alternatively defined as speeds where [specific heat capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_heat_capacity) changes with the temperature of the flow as the [kinetic energy](wikipedia://en/17327) of the moving object is converted into heat.[^o5]

[Hypersonic weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_weapon) are typically [boost-glide vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boostglide_vehicle) or [cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_cruise_missile) designed for aerodynamic flight and maneuvering above Mach 5.

High hypersonic speeds are experienced during [atmospheric entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_entry). [Spaceplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceplane) are designed to be capable of flight in this regime. The [North American X-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_X-15) and the [Space Shuttle orbiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_orbiter) are the only crewed spaceplanes to fly faster than Mach 5.

## Characteristics of flow {#Characteristics_of_flow}

*Image: Dopplereffectsourcemovingrightatmach5.0.gif.* Simulation of hypersonic speed (Mach 5)

While the definition of hypersonic flow can be quite vague [^o2] a hypersonic flow may be characterized by certain physical phenomena at very fast supersonic flow.[^o7]

The peculiarities in hypersonic flows are as follows:

1. [Shock](wikipedia://en/146253) layer [^o2]
1. Shock interaction - aerothermal:[^o8] [aerodynamic heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodynamic_heating)[^o2] of the fuselage [^o9]
1. Entropy layer
1. Real gas effects
1. Low density effects
1. Independence of aerodynamic coefficients with Mach number.

### Small shock stand-off distance {#Small_shock_stand-off_distance}

As a body's Mach number increases, the density behind a [bow shock](wikipedia://en/146253#Bow_shocks) generated by the body also increases, which corresponds to a decrease in volume behind the shock due to [conservation of mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_mass). Consequently, the distance between the bow shock and the body decreases at higher Mach numbers.[^o11]

### Entropy layer {#Entropy_layer}

As Mach numbers increase, the [entropy](wikipedia://en/9891) change across the shock also increases, which results in a strong [entropy gradient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_gradient) and highly [vortical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vortical) flow that mixes with the [boundary layer](wikipedia://en/224301).

### Viscous interaction {#Viscous_interaction}

A portion of the large [kinetic energy](wikipedia://en/17327) associated with flow at high Mach numbers transforms into [internal energy](wikipedia://en/340757) in the fluid due to viscous effects. The increase in internal energy is realized as an increase in temperature. Since the pressure gradient normal to the flow within a boundary layer is approximately zero for low to moderate hypersonic Mach numbers, the increase of temperature through the boundary layer coincides with a decrease in density. This causes the bottom of the boundary layer to expand, so that the boundary layer over the body grows thicker and can often merge with the shock wave near the body leading edge.

### High-temperature flow {#High-temperature_flow}

High temperatures due to a manifestation of viscous dissipation cause non-equilibrium chemical flow properties such as vibrational excitation and [dissociation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissociation_%28chemistry%29) and [ionization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionization) of molecules resulting in [convective](wikipedia://en/47526) and [radiative heat-flux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_reentry#Real_%28non-equilibrium%29_gas_model).

## Classification of Mach regimes {#Classification_of_Mach_regimes}

Although "subsonic" and "supersonic" usually refer to speeds below and above the local [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853) respectively, aerodynamicists often use these terms to refer to particular ranges of Mach values. When an aircraft approaches [transonic](wikipedia://en/247737) speeds (around [Mach](wikipedia://en/20051) 1), it enters a special regime. The usual approximations based on the [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395), which work well for subsonic designs, start to break down because, even in the freestream, some parts of the flow locally exceed Mach 1. So, more sophisticated methods are needed to handle this complex behavior.[^o16]

The "supersonic regime" usually refers to the set of Mach numbers for which linearized theory may be used; for example, where the ([air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air)) flow is not chemically reacting and where [heat transfer](wikipedia://en/184726) between air and vehicle may be reasonably neglected in calculations. Generally, [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA) defines "high" hypersonic as any Mach number from 10 to 25, and re-entry speeds as anything greater than Mach 25. Among the spacecraft operating in these regimes are returning [Soyuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_%28spacecraft%29) and [Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon) [space capsules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_capsule); the previously-operated [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle); various reusable spacecraft in development such as [SpaceX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX) [Starship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starship) and [Rocket Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Lab) [Electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_%28rocket%29); and (theoretical) [spaceplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceplane).

## Similarity parameters {#Similarity_parameters}

The categorization of airflow relies on a number of [similarity parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_number), which allow the simplification of a nearly infinite number of test cases into groups of similarity. For transonic and [compressible flow](wikipedia://en/219144), the [Mach](wikipedia://en/20051) and [Reynolds numbers](wikipedia://en/23868856) alone allow good categorization of many flow cases.

Hypersonic flows, however, require other similarity parameters. First, the [analytic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_equation) for the [oblique shock angle](wikipedia://en/146253) become nearly independent of Mach number at high (~>10) Mach numbers. Second, the formation of strong shocks around aerodynamic bodies means that the freestream [Reynolds number](wikipedia://en/23868856) is less useful as an estimate of the behavior of the [boundary layer](wikipedia://en/224301) over a body (although it is still important). Finally, the increased temperature of hypersonic flow mean that [real gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_gas) effects become important. Research in hypersonics is therefore often called [aerothermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerothermodynamics), rather than [aerodynamics](wikipedia://en/2819).[^o23]

The introduction of real gas effects means that more variables are required to describe the full state of a gas. Whereas a stationary gas can be described by three variables ([pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure), [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050), [adiabatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adiabatic_index)), and a moving gas by four ([flow velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_velocity)), a hot gas in chemical equilibrium also requires state equations for the chemical components of the gas, and a gas in nonequilibrium solves those state equations using time as an extra variable. This means that for nonequilibrium flow, something between 10 and 100 variables may be required to describe the state of the gas at any given time. Additionally, rarefied hypersonic flows (usually defined as those with a [Knudsen number](wikipedia://en/401314) above 0.1) do not follow the [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395).

Hypersonic flows are typically categorized by their total energy, expressed as total [enthalpy](wikipedia://en/10274) (MJ/kg), total pressure (kPa-MPa), stagnation pressure (kPa-MPa), [stagnation temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagnation_temperature) (K), or flow velocity (km/s).

[Wallace D. Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_D._Hayes) developed a similarity parameter, similar to the Whitcomb [area rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_rule), which allowed similar configurations to be compared. In the study of hypersonic flow over slender bodies, the product of the freestream Mach number $M_{\infty}$ and the flow deflection angle $\theta$, known as the hypersonic similarity parameter:

$$K = M_{\infty}\theta$$

is considered to be an important governing parameter.[^o23] The slenderness ratio of a vehicle $\tau = d/l$, where $d$ is the diameter and $l$ is the length, is often substituted for $\theta$.

## Regimes {#Regimes}

Hypersonic flow can be approximately separated into a number of regimes. The selection of these regimes is rough, due to the blurring of the boundaries where a particular effect can be found.

### Perfect gas {#Perfect_gas}

In this regime, the gas can be regarded as an [ideal gas](wikipedia://en/65905). Flow in this regime is still Mach number dependent. Simulations start to depend on the use of a constant-temperature wall, rather than the adiabatic wall typically used at lower speeds. The lower border of this region is around Mach 5, where [ramjets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramjet) become inefficient, and the upper border around Mach 10–12.

### Two-temperature ideal gas {#Two-temperature_ideal_gas}

This is a subset of the perfect gas regime, where the gas can be considered chemically perfect, but the rotational and vibrational temperatures of the gas must be considered separately, leading to two temperature models. See particularly the modeling of supersonic nozzles, where vibrational freezing becomes important.

### Dissociated gas {#Dissociated_gas}

In this regime, diatomic or polyatomic gases (the gases found in most atmospheres) begin to [dissociate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissociation_%28chemistry%29) as they come into contact with the [bow shock](wikipedia://en/146253) generated by the body. [Surface catalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalysis) plays a role in the calculation of surface heating, meaning that the type of surface material also has an effect on the flow. The lower border of this regime is where any component of a gas mixture first begins to dissociate in the stagnation point of a flow (which for nitrogen is around 2000 K). At the upper border of this regime, the effects of [ionization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionization) start to have an effect on the flow.

### Ionized gas {#Ionized_gas}

In this regime the [ionized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionization) electron population of the stagnated flow becomes significant, and the electrons must be modeled separately. Often the electron temperature is handled separately from the temperature of the remaining gas components. This region occurs for freestream flow velocities around 3–4 km/s. Gases in this region are modeled as non-radiating [plasmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_%28physics%29).

### Radiation-dominated regime {#Radiation-dominated_regime}

Above around 12 km/s, the heat transfer to a vehicle changes from being conductively dominated to radiatively dominated. The modeling of gases in this regime is split into two classes:

1. [Optically thin](wikipedia://en/38993): where the gas does not re-absorb radiation emitted from other parts of the gas
1. Optically thick: where the radiation must be considered a separate source of energy.

The modeling of optically thick gases is extremely difficult, since, due to the calculation of the radiation at each point, the computation load theoretically expands exponentially as the number of points considered increases.
