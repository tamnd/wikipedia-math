# Grigori Mints

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Grigori Mints"}

**Grigori Mints** (June 7, 1939 – May 29, 2014) was a Russian [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). He was born in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad), in the Soviet Union (now [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg), Russia), and received his Ph.D. in 1965 from [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_State_University) under [Nikolai Aleksandrovich Shanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Aleksandrovich_Shanin) with his thesis *On Predicate and Operator Variants for Building Theories of Constructive Mathematics*. In 1990 he received his D.Sc. from [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_State_University) with the thesis *Proof Transformations and Synthesis of Programs*. He was a [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor).[^o4] Since 1991, Grigori "Grisha" Mints was a professor of philosophy and, by courtesy, of mathematics and of computer science at Stanford University. Before joining Stanford, Mints held research positions at the [Steklov Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Mathematical_Institute), [Leningrad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_University), and the [Estonian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_Academy_of_Sciences).

:::

Considered one of the most distinguished logicians in the world, Mints was passionate about the applications of logic to philosophy. His expertise was in [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478) – the analysis of the structure of mathematical reasoning. Mints was elected to the [Estonian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2008 and to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2010.

Mints was a very active member of the steering committee of the [WoLLIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WoLLIC) series of workshops on logic and language, after having been a member of the community in several capacities such as invited speaker, PC member, PC chair, Organising Committee chair, guest editor of proceedings and special issue, and steering committee member.
