# Furry's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Furry's theorem"}

*Image: Triangle diagram.svg.* This triangle diagram vanishes by Furry's theorem in quantum electrodynamics.

In [quantum electrodynamics](wikipedia://en/25268), **Furry's theorem** states that if a [Feynman diagram](wikipedia://en/11617) consists of a closed loop of [fermion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion) lines with an odd number of vertices, its contribution to the [amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_amplitude) vanishes. As a [corollary](wikipedia://en/168865), a single [photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon) cannot arise from the [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_vacuum_state) or be absorbed by it. The theorem was first derived by [Wendell H. Furry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_H._Furry) in 1937,[^o2] as a direct consequence of the [conservation of energy](wikipedia://en/67088) and [charge conjugation symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-symmetry).

:::

## Theory {#Theory}

Quantum electrodynamics has a number of [symmetries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_%28physics%29), one of them being the [discrete symmetry](wikipedia://en/1372580) of charge conjugation. This acts on fields through a [unitary](wikipedia://en/225999) charge conjugation operator $C$ which anticommutes with the photon field $A_\mu(x)$ as $CA^\mu(x) C^\dagger = -A^\mu(x)$, while leaving the vacuum state invariant $C|\Omega\rangle = |\Omega\rangle$. Considering the simplest case of the [correlation function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_function_%28quantum_field_theory%29) of a single photon operator gives

$$\langle \Omega|A^\mu(x)|\Omega\rangle = \langle \Omega|C^\dagger C A^\mu(x) C^\dagger C|\Omega\rangle = - \langle \Omega|A^\mu(x)|\Omega\rangle,$$

so this correlation function must vanish.[^o4] For $n$ photon operators, this argument shows that under charge conjugation this picks up a factor of $(-1)^n$ and thus vanishes when $n$ is odd. More generally, since the charge conjugation operator also anticommutes with the vector current $j^\mu(x)$, Furry's theorem states that the correlation function of any odd number of [on-shell or off-shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_shell_and_off_shell) photon fields and/or currents must vanish in quantum electrodynamics.

Since the theorem holds at the [non-perturbative](wikipedia://en/1719992) level, it must also hold at each order in [perturbation theory](wikipedia://en/297069).[^o5] At leading order this means that any [fermion loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-loop_Feynman_diagram) with an odd number of vertices must have a vanishing contribution to the amplitude. An explicit calculation of these diagrams reveals that this is because the diagram with a fermion going clockwise around the loop cancels with the second diagram where the fermion goes anticlockwise. The vanishing of the three vertex loop can also be seen as a consequence of the [renormalizability](wikipedia://en/291453) of quantum electrodynamics since the bare [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/40752010) does not have any counterterms involving three photons.[^o6]

## Applications and limitations {#Applications_and_limitations}

Furry's theorem allows for the simplification of a number of amplitude calculations in quantum electrodynamics.[^o8] In particular, since the result also holds when photons are off-shell, all Feynman diagrams which have at least one internal fermion loops with an odd number of vertices have a vanishing contribution to the amplitude and can be ignored. Historically the theorem was important in showing that the scattering of photons by an external field, known as [Delbrück scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delbrück_scattering), does not proceed via a triangle diagram and must instead proceed through a box diagram.[^o2]

In the presence of a background charge density or a nonzero [chemical potential](wikipedia://en/218628), Furry's theorem is broken, although if both these vanish then it does hold at [nonzero temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_quantum_field_theory) as well as at zero temperatures.[^o9] It also does not apply in the presence of a strong background [magnetic field](wikipedia://en/36563) where photon splitting interactions $\gamma \rightarrow \gamma \gamma$ are allowed, a process that may be detected in [astrophysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics) settings such as around [neutron stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_star).[^o10] The theorem also does not hold when [Weyl fermions](wikipedia://en/33162584) are involved in the loops rather than [Dirac fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_fermion), resulting in non-vanishing odd vertex number diagrams. In particular, the non-vanishing of the triangle diagram with Weyl fermions gives rise to the [chiral anomaly](wikipedia://en/312229), with the sum of these having to cancel for a quantum theory to be consistent.

While the theorem has been formulated in quantum electrodynamics, a version of it holds more generally. For example, while the [Standard Model](wikipedia://en/47641) is not charge conjugation invariant due to [weak interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_interaction), the fermion loop diagrams with an odd number of photons attached will still vanish since these are equivalent to a purely quantum electrodynamical diagram. Similarly, any diagram involving such loops as sub-diagrams will also vanish. It is however no longer true that all odd number photon diagrams need to vanish. For example, relaxing the requirement of charge conjugation and [parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_%28physics%29) invariance of quantum electrodynamics, as occurs when weak interactions are included, allows for a three-photon vertex term.[^o11] While this term does give rise to $\gamma \rightarrow \gamma \gamma$ interactions, they only occur if two of the photons are [virtual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_particle); searching for such interactions must be done indirectly, such as through [bremsstrahlung](wikipedia://en/54147) experiments from electron-positron collisions.[^o12]

In non-Abelian [Yang–Mills theories](wikipedia://en/672202), Furry's theorem does not hold since these involve noncommuting [color charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge). For example, the quark triangle diagrams with three external [gluons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluon) are proportional to two different [generator](wikipedia://en/17944#Generators_and_dimension) [traces](wikipedia://en/43270) $\text{tr}[T^aT^bT^c] \neq \text{tr}[T^aT^cT^b]$ and so they do not cancel.[^o13][^o14] However, charge conjugation arguments can still be applied in limited cases such as to deduce that the triangle diagram $gg \rightarrow X$ for a color neutral [spin](wikipedia://en/19593829) $1^-$ [boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson) vanishes.[^o15]
