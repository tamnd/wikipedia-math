# Grokking (machine learning)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Grokking"}

*Image: Grokking modular addition.jpg.* The blue loss curves represent early memorization of the training set ([overfitting](wikipedia://en/173332)), and the red curves show late generalization, with the learning of a modular addition algorithm that works with unseen inputs.[^o2]

In [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), **grokking**, or **delayed generalization**, is a phenomenon observed in some settings where a model abruptly transitions from [overfitting](wikipedia://en/173332) (performing well only on [training data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_data)) to [generalizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalization_%28learning%29) (performing well on both training and test data), after many training iterations with little or no improvement on the held-out data.[^n1]p. 2 This contrasts with what is typically observed in machine learning, where generalization occurs gradually alongside improved performance on training data.[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Origin {#Origin}

The term *grokking* was introduced by [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI) researcher Alethea Power and colleagues in the January 2022 paper "Grokking: Generalization Beyond Overfitting on Small Algorithmic Datasets".[^n1] It is derived from the word *[grok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok)* coined by [Robert Heinlein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Heinlein) in his novel *[Stranger in a Strange Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranger_in_a_Strange_Land)*.[^o2] In ML research, "grokking" is *not* used as a synonym for "generalization"; rather, it names a sometimes-observed delayed‑generalization training phenomenon in which training and held‑out performance do not improve in tandem, and in which held‑out performance rises abruptly after a long period in which test performance has reached what appears to be a maximum value. Authors also analyze the "grokking time", the [epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_artificial_intelligence#epoch) or step at which this transition occurs in those scenarios.[^n1]p. 10

## Interpretations {#Interpretations}

Grokking can be understood as a [phase transition](wikipedia://en/54423) during the training process.[^o10] In particular, recent work has shown that grokking may be due to a [complexity](wikipedia://en/1635) phase transition in the model during training.[^o11] While grokking has been thought of as largely a phenomenon of relatively shallow models, grokking has been observed in deep neural networks and non-neural models and is the subject of active research.[^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15]

One potential explanation is that the [weight decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_decay) (a component of the loss function that penalizes higher values of the neural network parameters, also called [regularization](wikipedia://en/2009061)) slightly favors the general solution that is simpler (in that it involves lower weight values) but that is also harder to find. According to Neel Nanda, the process of learning the general solution may be gradual, even though the transition to the general solution occurs more suddenly later.[^o2]

Recent theories[^o16][^o17] have hypothesized that grokking occurs when neural networks transition from a "lazy training"[^o18] regime where the weights do not deviate far from initialization, to a "rich" regime where weights abruptly begin to move in task-relevant directions. Follow-up empirical and theoretical work[^o19] has accumulated evidence in support of this perspective, and it offers a unifying view of earlier work as the transition from lazy to rich training dynamics is known to arise from properties of adaptive optimizers,[^o20] weight decay,[^o21] initial parameter weight norm,[^o14] and more. This perspective is complementary to a unifying "pattern learning speeds" framework that links grokking and [double descent](wikipedia://en/3257325); within this view, delayed generalization can arise across training time ("epoch‑wise") or across model size ("model‑wise"), and the authors report "model‑wise grokking".[^o22]
