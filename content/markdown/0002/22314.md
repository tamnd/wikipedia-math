# O

O

o

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="O"}

**O** ([minuscule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_case): **o**) is the fifteenth [letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_%28alphabet%29) and the fourth [vowel letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vowel_letter) of the [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet), used in the [modern English alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_alphabet), the alphabets of other western European languages and others worldwide. Its name in English is [*o*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_alphabet#Letter_names) (pronounced ), plural [*oes*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_alphabet#Letter_names).[^o3]

:::

## Name {#Name}

In English, the name of the letter is the "long O" sound, pronounced . In most other languages, its name matches the letter's pronunciation in [open syllables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_syllable).

Its graphic form has remained fairly constant from [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenician_alphabet) times until today. The name of the Phoenician letter was *[ʿeyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayin)*, meaning "eye", and its shape originates simply as a drawing of a human eye (possibly inspired by the corresponding [Egyptian hieroglyph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyphs), [Proto-Sinaitic script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Sinaitic_script)). Its original sound value was that of a consonant, probably ʕ, the sound represented by the cognate [Arabic letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_alphabet) [ع *ʿayn*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ع).[^o7]

The use of this Phoenician letter for a vowel sound is due to the early [Greek alphabets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet), which adopted the letter "[omicron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omicron)" to represent the vowel . The letter was adopted with the value in the [Old Italic alphabets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Italic_alphabets), including the [early Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Latin_alphabet). In Greek, a variation of the form later came to differentiate this long sound ([omega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega), meaning "large O") from the short o (Omicron, meaning "small o"). The Greek omicron gave rise to the corresponding [Cyrillic letter O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_%28Cyrillic%29).[^o7]

### English {#English}

The letter $\langle o\rangle$ is the fourth [most common letter](wikipedia://en/6767165) in the [English alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_alphabet).[^o30] Like the other English vowel letters, it has associated "long" and "short" pronunciations. The "long" $\langle o\rangle$ as in *boat* is actually most often a [diphthong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diphthong) (realized dialectically anywhere from to ). In English, there is also a "short" $\langle o\rangle$ as in *fox*, , which sounds slightly different in different dialects. In most dialects of [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English), it is either an [open-mid back rounded vowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-mid_back_rounded_vowel) or an [open back rounded vowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_back_rounded_vowel) ; in [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English), it is most commonly an unrounded back to a central vowel .[^o31]

Common [digraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digraph_%28orthography%29) include $\langle \operatorname{oo}\rangle$, which represents either , or ; $\langle \operatorname{oi}\rangle$ or $\langle \operatorname{oy}\rangle$, which typically represents the diphthong , and $\langle \operatorname{ao}\rangle$, $\langle \operatorname{oe}\rangle$, and $\langle \operatorname{ou}\rangle$ which represent a variety of pronunciations depending on context and etymology.[^o31]

In other contexts, especially before a letter with a [minim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minim_%28palaeography%29), $\langle o\rangle$ may represent the sound , as in 'son' or 'love'. It can also represent the [semivowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semivowel) , as in *choir* or *quinoa.*[^o32]

"O" in isolation is a word, also spelled "oh" and pronounced . Before a noun, usually capitalized, it indicates a [noun of address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noun_of_address), as in the titles "[O Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Canada)" or "[O Captain! My Captain!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Captain!_My_Captain!)" or in certain verses of the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible).[^o33]

### Other languages {#Other_languages}

$\langle o\rangle$ is commonly associated with the [open-mid back rounded vowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-mid_back_rounded_vowel) , [mid back rounded vowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid_back_rounded_vowel) or [close-mid back rounded vowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close-mid_back_rounded_vowel) in many languages. Other languages use $\langle o\rangle$ for various values, usually back vowels which are at least partly open. Derived letters such as $\langle \text{ö}\rangle$ and $\langle \text{ø}\rangle$ have been created for the alphabets of some languages to distinguish values that were not present in Latin and Greek, particularly rounded front vowels.[^o35]

### Other systems {#Other_systems}

In the [International Phonetic Alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet), o represents the [close-mid back rounded vowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close-mid_back_rounded_vowel).[^o37]
