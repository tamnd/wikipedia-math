# Symplectic manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Symplectic manifold"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **symplectic manifold** is a [smooth manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219#Definition), $M$, equipped with a [closed](wikipedia://en/404181) [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate_form) [differential 2-form](wikipedia://en/221519), $\omega$, called the symplectic form. The study of symplectic manifolds is called [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298) or symplectic topology. Symplectic manifolds arise naturally in abstract formulations of [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586) and [analytical mechanics](wikipedia://en/454450) as the [cotangent bundles](wikipedia://en/221600) of manifolds. For example, in the [Hamiltonian formulation](wikipedia://en/198319) of classical mechanics, which provides one of the major motivations for the field, the set of all possible configurations of a system is modeled as a manifold, and this manifold's cotangent bundle describes the [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) of the system.

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

Symplectic manifolds arise from [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586); in particular, they are a generalization of the [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) of a closed system.[^o3][^o4] In the same way the [Hamilton equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_equations) allow one to derive the time evolution of a system from a set of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), the symplectic form should allow one to obtain a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) describing the flow of the system from the differential $dH$ of a Hamiltonian function $H$.[^o5] So we require a linear map $TM \rightarrow T^*M$ from the [tangent manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_manifold) $TM$ to the [cotangent manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotangent_manifold) $T^* M$, or equivalently, an element of $T^*M \otimes T^*M$. Letting $\omega$ denote a [section](wikipedia://en/367621) of $T^*M \otimes T^* M$, the requirement that $\omega$ be [non-degenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degenerate_form) ensures that for every differential $dH$ there is a unique corresponding vector field $V_H$ such that $dH = \omega (V_H, \cdot)$. Since one desires the Hamiltonian to be constant along flow lines, one should have $\omega(V_H, V_H) = dH(V_H) = 0$, which implies that $\omega$ is [alternating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_form) and hence a 2-form. Finally, one makes the requirement that $\omega$ should not change under flow lines, i.e. that the [Lie derivative](wikipedia://en/294437) of $\omega$ along $V_H$ vanishes. Applying [Cartan's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan_homotopy_formula), this amounts to (here $\iota_X$ is the [interior product](wikipedia://en/2154963)):

$$\mathcal{L}_{V_H}(\omega) = 0\;\Leftrightarrow\;\mathrm d (\iota_{V_H} \omega) + \iota_{V_H} \mathrm d\omega= \mathrm d (\mathrm d\,H) + \mathrm d\omega(V_H) = \mathrm d\omega(V_H)=0$$

so that, on repeating this argument for different smooth functions $H$ such that the corresponding $V_H$ span the tangent space at each point the argument is applied at, we see that the requirement for the vanishing Lie derivative along flows of $V_H$ corresponding to arbitrary smooth $H$ is equivalent to the requirement that *ω* should be [closed](wikipedia://en/404181).

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $M$ be a smooth [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). A **symplectic form** on $M$ is a closed non-degenerate differential [2-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-form) $\omega$.[^o8][^o9] Here, non-degenerate means that for every point $p \in M$, the skew-symmetric pairing on the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) $T_p M$ defined by $\omega$ is non-degenerate. That is to say, if there exists an $X \in T_p M$ such that $\omega( X, Y ) = 0$ for all $Y \in T_p M$, then $X = 0$. The closed condition means that the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) of $\omega$ vanishes.[^o8][^o9]

A **symplectic manifold** is a pair $(M, \omega)$ where $M$ is a smooth manifold and $\omega$ is a symplectic form. Assigning a symplectic form to $M$ is referred to as giving $M$ a **symplectic structure**. Since in odd dimensions, [skew-symmetric matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skew-symmetric_matrices) are always singular, nondegeneracy implies that $\dim M$ is even.[^o10]

By nondegeneracy, $\omega$ can be used to define a pair of [**musical isomorphisms**](wikipedia://en/3762980) $\omega^\flat: T M \rightarrow T^* M, \omega^\sharp : T^* M \rightarrow T M$, such that $\omega(X, Y) = \omega^\flat(X) (Y)$ for any two vector fields $X, Y$, and $\omega^\sharp \circ\omega^\flat = \operatorname{Id}$.

A symplectic manifold $(M, \omega)$ is **exact** iff the symplectic form $\omega$ is [exact](wikipedia://en/404181), i.e. equal to $\omega = -d\theta$ for some 1-form $\theta$. The symplectic form on any compact symplectic manifold without boundary is inexact, by [Stokes' theorem](wikipedia://en/25441497).[^o11]

By [Darboux's theorem](wikipedia://en/1543358), around any point $p$ there exists a local coordinate system, in which $\omega = \Sigma_i dp_i \wedge dq^i$, where d denotes the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) and ∧ denotes the [exterior product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_product).[^o12] This form is called the [Poincaré two-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_two-form) or the **canonical two-form**. Thus, we can locally think of *M* as being the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) $T^*\mathbb{R}^n$ and generated by the corresponding [tautological 1-form](wikipedia://en/1855974) $\theta = \Sigma_i p_i dq^i, \;\omega = d\theta$.

A (local) **Liouville form** is any (locally defined) $\lambda$ such that $\omega = d\lambda$. A vector field $X$ is (locally) **Liouville** iff $\mathcal L_X \omega = \omega$. By [Cartan's magic formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan's_magic_formula), this is equivalent to $d(\omega(X, \cdot)) = \omega$. A Liouville vector field can thus be interpreted as a way to recover a (local) Liouville form. By Darboux's theorem, around any point there exists a local Liouville form, though it might not exist globally.

On a symplectic manifold, every smooth function $H:M\to\mathbb R$ determines a [Hamiltonian vector field](wikipedia://en/1818270) $X_H$ by $\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH$, up to sign convention.[^o13] The integral curves of $X_H$ are the Hamiltonian flow of $H$. In classical mechanics, $H$ is the energy function and the symplectic form encodes Hamilton's equations. The set of all Hamiltonian vector fields make up a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944), and is written as $(\operatorname{Ham}(M), [\cdot, \cdot])$ where $[\cdot, \cdot]$ is the [Lie bracket](wikipedia://en/10282799).

Given any two smooth functions $f, g : M \to \mathbb{R}$, their **[Poisson bracket](wikipedia://en/294340)** is defined by $\{f,g\} = \omega (X_g,X_f)$.[^o14] This makes any symplectic manifold into a [Poisson manifold](wikipedia://en/882902).[^o15] The **Poisson bivector** is a [bivector](wikipedia://en/884040) field $\pi$ defined by $\{ f,g \} = \pi(df \wedge dg)$, or equivalently, by $\pi := \omega^{-1}$. The Poisson bracket and Lie bracket are related by $X_{\{f,g\}} = [X_f,X_g]$.

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

If $(M,\omega)$ is a symplectic manifold of dimension $2n$, then $\omega^n$ is a nowhere-vanishing top-degree form. Thus every symplectic manifold is orientable and has a natural volume form, called the symplectic volume form.[^o10]

Unlike a Riemannian metric, a symplectic form does not define lengths or angles. By [Darboux's theorem](wikipedia://en/1543358), all symplectic manifolds of the same dimension are locally symplectomorphic. Consequently, symplectic geometry has no local curvature invariant analogous to the Riemannian curvature tensor; many of its main questions are global.[^o4][^o12]

:::

## Submanifolds {#Submanifolds}

There are several natural geometric notions of [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) of a symplectic manifold $(M, \omega)$. Let $N \subset M$ be a submanifold. It is[^o19][^o11]

- **symplectic** iff $\omega|_N$ is a symplectic form on $N$;
- **isotropic** iff $\omega|_N = 0$, equivalently, iff $T_p N \subset T_p N^\omega$ for any $p \in N$;
- **coisotropic** iff $T_p N^\omega \subset T_p N$ for any $p \in N$;
- **Lagrangian** iff it is both **isotropic** and **coisotropic**, i.e. $\omega|_N=0$ and $\text{dim }N=\tfrac{1}{2}\dim M$. By the nondegeneracy of $\omega$, Lagrangian submanifolds are the maximal isotropic submanifolds and minimal coisotropic submanifolds.

## Lagrangian submanifolds {#Lagrangian_submanifolds}

Lagrangian submanifolds are the most important submanifolds. [Weinstein](wikipedia://en/7344764) proposed the "symplectic creed": *Everything is a Lagrangian submanifold.* By that, he means that everything in symplectic geometry is most naturally expressed in terms of Lagrangian submanifolds.[^o21]

A **Lagrangian fibration** of a symplectic manifold *M* is a [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826) where all of the [fibers](wikipedia://en/276526#Formal_definition) are Lagrangian submanifolds.

Given a submanifold $N \subset M$ of codimension 1, the **characteristic line distribution** on it is the duals to its tangent spaces: $T_p N^\omega$. If there also exists a Liouville vector field $X$ in a neighborhood of it that is [transverse](wikipedia://en/3299423) to it. In this case, let $\alpha := \omega(X, \cdot)|_N$, then $(N, \alpha)$ is a [contact manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_manifold), and we say it is a **contact type** submanifold. In this case, the [Reeb vector field](wikipedia://en/10761147) is tangent to the characteristic line distribution.

An *n*-submanifold is locally specified by a smooth function $u: \mathbb{R}^n \to M$. It is a Lagrangian submanifold if $\omega(\partial_i , \partial_j) = 0$ for all $i, j \in 1:n$. If locally there is a canonical coordinate system $(q, p)$, then the condition is equivalent to

$$[ u, v ]_{p,q} = \sum_{i=1}^n \left(\frac{\partial q_i}{\partial u} \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial v} - \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial u} \frac{\partial q_i}{\partial v} \right) = 0, \quad \forall i, j \in 1:n$$

where $[\cdot, \cdot]_{p, q}$ is the [Lagrange bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_bracket) in this coordinate system.

The graph of a closed 1-form on $M$ is a Lagrangian submanifold of $T^*M$. In particular, the graph of $df$ is Lagrangian. Conversely, if a Lagrangian submanifold $L\subset T^*M$ projects diffeomorphically to $M$, then it is the graph of a closed 1-form.[^o19] It is globally the graph of $df$ only when that closed 1-form is exact.

### Lagrangian mapping {#Lagrangian_mapping}

*Image: TIKZ PICT FBN.svg.*

Let *L* be a Lagrangian submanifold of a symplectic manifold (*K*,ω) given by an [immersion](wikipedia://en/6993953) *i* : *L* ↪ *K* (*i* is called a **Lagrangian immersion**). Let *π* : *K* ↠ *B* give a Lagrangian fibration of *K*. The composite (*π* ∘ *i*) : *L* ↪ *K* ↠ *B* is a **Lagrangian mapping**. The **critical value set** of *π* ∘ *i* is called a [caustic](wikipedia://en/880406).[^o24]

Two Lagrangian maps (*π*<sub>1</sub> ∘ *i*<sub>1</sub>) : *L*<sub>1</sub> ↪ *K*<sub>1</sub> ↠ *B*<sub>1</sub> and (*π*<sub>2</sub> ∘ *i*<sub>2</sub>) : *L*<sub>2</sub> ↪ *K*<sub>2</sub> ↠ *B*<sub>2</sub> are called **Lagrangian equivalent** if there exist [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) *σ*, *τ* and *ν* such that both sides of the diagram given on the right [commute](wikipedia://en/138484), and *τ* preserves the symplectic form.[^o9] Symbolically:

$$\tau \circ i_1 = i_2 \circ \sigma, \ \nu \circ \pi_1 = \pi_2 \circ \tau, \ \tau^*\omega_2 = \omega_1 \, ,$$

where *τ*<sup>∗</sup>*ω*<sub>2</sub> denotes the [pull back](wikipedia://en/494995#Pullback_of_differential_forms) of *ω*<sub>2</sub> by *τ*.

:::

## Symmetries {#Symmetries}

A map $f: (M, \omega) \to (M', \omega')$ between symplectic manifolds is a [symplectomorphism](wikipedia://en/312304) when it preserves the symplectic structure, i.e. the [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995) is the same $f^* \omega' = \omega$. The most important symplectomorphisms are symplectic flows, i.e. ones generated by integrating a vector field on $(M, \omega)$.

Given a vector field $X$ on $(M, \omega)$, it generates a symplectic flow iff $\mathcal L_X \omega = 0$. Such vector fields are called **symplectic**. Any Hamiltonian vector field is symplectic, and conversely, any symplectic vector field is *locally* Hamiltonian.

A property that is preserved under all symplectomorphisms is a **symplectic invariant**. In the spirit of [Erlangen program](wikipedia://en/243382), symplectic geometry is the study of symplectic invariants.

## Examples {#Examples}

### The standard symplectic structure {#The_standard_symplectic_structure}

Let $\{v_1, \ldots, v_{2n}\}$ be a basis for $\mathbb{R}^{2n}.$ We define our symplectic form $\omega$ on this basis as follows:

$$\omega(v_i, v_j) = \begin{cases} 1 & j-i =n \text{ with } 1 \leqslant i \leqslant n \\ -1 & i-j =n \text{ with } 1 \leqslant j \leqslant n \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

In this case the symplectic form reduces to a simple [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477). If $I_n$ denotes the $n\times n$ [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718) then the matrix, $\Omega$, of this bilinear form is given by the $2n\times 2n$ [block matrix](wikipedia://en/457464):

$$\Omega = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & I_n \\ -I_n & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

That is,

$$\omega =\mathrm{d}x_1\wedge \mathrm{d}y_1 + \dotsb + \mathrm{d}x_n\wedge \mathrm{d}y_n.$$

It has a fibration by Lagrangian submanifolds with fixed value of $y$, i.e. $\{\mathbb{R}^n \times \{y\} : y \in \mathbb{R}^n\}$.

A Liouville form for this is $\lambda=\frac{1}{2} \sum_i\left(x_i d y_i-y_i d x_i\right)$ and $\omega=d \lambda$, the Liouville vector field is

$$Y=\frac{1}{2} \sum_i\left(x_i \partial_{x_i}+y_i \partial_{y_i}\right),$$

the radial field. Another Liouville form is $\Sigma_i x_i dy_i$, with Liouville vector field $Y=\sum_i x_i \partial_{x_i}$.

### Surfaces {#Surfaces}

Every oriented smooth surface with an area form is a symplectic manifold. In dimension two, the closedness condition is automatic for any 2-form.

### Cotangent bundles {#Cotangent_bundles}

If $Q$ is a smooth manifold, its cotangent bundle $T^*Q$ carries a [canonical 1-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_1-form) $\lambda$, also called the tautological or Liouville 1-form. The exterior derivative $\omega=d\lambda$, up to sign convention, is the [canonical symplectic form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_symplectic_form) on $T^*Q$, also called the Poincaré two-form.

The canonical 1-form is defined by the property that, for any $v\in T_{x,\alpha}T^*Q$, $\lambda(v)=\alpha(\pi_*v)$ where $\pi:T^*Q\to Q$ is the bundle projection. In local coordinates $q^i$ on $Q$, the canonical 1-form is $\lambda = \sum_{i=1}^n p_idq^i$ where $p_i$ are fiber coordinates on the cotangent bundle such that $\alpha = \sum_{i=1}^n p_i(\alpha)dq^i$. In these coordinates, the canonical symplectic form is

$$\omega = \sum_{i=1}^n dp_i \wedge dq^i$$

The tautological 1-form $\lambda = \sum_i p_i dq^i$ has Liouville vector field $Y = \sum_i p_i \partial_{p_i}$, the fiberwise radial field. Its flow dilates covectors: $(q, p) \mapsto\left(q, e^t p\right)$.

The zero section of the cotangent bundle is Lagrangian.

### Kähler manifolds {#Kähler_manifolds}

A [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) is a symplectic manifold equipped with a compatible integrable complex structure. They form a particular class of [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742). A large class of examples come from complex [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). Any smooth complex [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) $V \subset \mathbb{CP}^n$ has a symplectic form which is the restriction of the [Fubini—Study form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fubini-Study_metric) on the [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) $\mathbb{CP}^n$.

A symplectic manifold endowed with a [metric](wikipedia://en/195795) that is [compatible](wikipedia://en/514258#Compatible_triples) with the symplectic form is an [almost Kähler manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_Kähler_manifold) in the sense that the tangent bundle has an [almost complex structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_complex_structure), but this need not be [integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrability_condition). A compatible almost-complex structure is an endomorphism $J$ of the tangent space such that $J^2=-I$, $\omega(X,JY)=-\omega(JX,Y)$, and $\omega(X,JX) \ge 0$ for all $X$. For such a compatible almost complex structure, $g(X,Y)=\omega(X,JY)$ defines a Riemannian metric. When $J$ is integrable, the resulting symplectic manifold is Kähler.[^o33]

### Coadjoint orbits {#Coadjoint_orbits}

[Coadjoint orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coadjoint_orbit) of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) carry natural symplectic forms. If $\mathcal O\subset\mathfrak g^*$ is the coadjoint orbit through $\xi$, then tangent vectors at $\xi$ have the form $\operatorname{ad}^*_X\xi$, and the symplectic form is given, up to sign convention, by

$$\omega_\xi(\operatorname{ad}^*_X\xi,\operatorname{ad}^*_Y\xi)=\langle \xi,[X,Y]\rangle.$$

Coadjoint orbits also arise naturally in [moment map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_map) theory and [symplectic reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_reduction).[^o35]

### Lagrangian correspondences {#Lagrangian_correspondences}

A [symplectomorphism](wikipedia://en/312304) can be described as a Lagrangian submanifold. If $\phi:(M,\omega_M)\to (N,\omega_N)$ is a symplectomorphism, then its graph is a Lagrangian submanifold of $\overline{M}\times N$, where $\overline{M}$ denotes $M$ equipped with the symplectic form $-\omega_M$.[^o37]

More generally, a **Lagrangian correspondence** from $M$ to $N$ is a Lagrangian submanifold of $\overline{M}\times N$. Lagrangian correspondences are used in formulations of the [symplectic category](wikipedia://en/44076398) and in [Floer homology](wikipedia://en/2672856).

:::

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

- [Presymplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/40076902) generalize the symplectic manifolds by only requiring $\omega$ to be closed, but possibly degenerate. Any submanifold of a symplectic manifold inherits a presymplectic structure.
- [Poisson manifolds](wikipedia://en/882902) generalize the symplectic manifolds by preserving only the [differential-algebraic](wikipedia://en/1816587) structures of a symplectic manifold.
- [Dirac manifolds](wikipedia://en/41519162) generalize Poisson manifolds and presymplectic manifolds by preserving even less structure. The definition is designed so that any submanifold of a Poisson manifold induces a Dirac manifold. They can be called "pre-Poisson" manifolds.
- A **multisymplectic manifold** of degree *k* is a manifold equipped with a closed nondegenerate *k*-form.[^o40]
- A **polysymplectic manifold** is a Legendre bundle provided with a polysymplectic tangent-valued $(n+2)$-form; it is utilized in [Hamiltonian field theory](wikipedia://en/47392500).[^o41]
