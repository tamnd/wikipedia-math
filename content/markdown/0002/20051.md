# Mach number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mach number"}

*Image: FA-18 going transonic.JPG.* An [F/A-18F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_F/A-18E/F_Super_Hornet) Super Hornet creating a [vapor cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vapor_cone) at [transonic speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transonic_speed) just before reaching the [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853).

The **Mach number** (**M** or **Ma**), often only **Mach** (; ), is a [dimensionless quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_quantity) in [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034) representing the ratio of [flow velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_velocity) past a [boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_%28thermodynamic%29) to the local [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853).[^o2][^o3] It is named after Austrian physicist and philosopher [Ernst Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Mach).

:::

$$\mathrm{M} = \frac{u}{c},$$

where:

- M is the local Mach number,
- $u$ is the local flow velocity with respect to the boundaries (either internal, such as an object immersed in the flow, or external, like a channel), and
- $c$ is the speed of sound in the medium, which in air varies with the square root of the [thermodynamic temperature](wikipedia://en/41789).

By definition, at Mach 1, the local flow velocity $u$ is equal to the speed of sound. At Mach 0.65, $u$ is 65% of the speed of sound (subsonic), and, at Mach 1.35, $u$ is 35% faster than the speed of sound (supersonic).

The local speed of sound, and hence the Mach number, depends on the temperature of the surrounding gas. The Mach number is primarily used to determine the approximation with which a flow can be treated as an [incompressible flow](wikipedia://en/461477). The medium can be a gas or a liquid. The boundary can be travelling in the medium, or it can be stationary while the medium flows along it, or they can both be moving, with different [velocities](wikipedia://en/20491903): what matters is their relative velocity with respect to each other. The boundary can be the boundary of an object immersed in the medium, or of a channel such as a [nozzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nozzle), [diffuser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine#Air_intake) or [wind tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_tunnel) channelling the medium. As the Mach number is defined as the ratio of two speeds, it is a dimensionless quantity. If M \< 0.2–0.3 and the flow is [quasi-steady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steady_flow) and [isothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isothermal_flow), compressibility effects will be small and simplified incompressible flow equations can be used.[^o2][^o3]

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The Mach number is named after the physicist and philosopher [Ernst Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Mach),[^o5] in honour of his achievements, according to a proposal by the aeronautical engineer [Jakob Ackeret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ackeret) in 1929.[^o6] The word Mach is always capitalized since it derives from a proper name and since the Mach number is a dimensionless quantity rather than a [unit of measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units#Unit_names). This is also why the number comes after the word Mach. It was also known as *Mach's number* by Lockheed when reporting the effects of compressibility on the P-38 aircraft in 1942.[^o7]

## Overview {#Overview}

*Image: Comparison US standard atmosphere 1962.svg.* The speed of sound (blue) depends only on the temperature variation at altitude (red) and can be calculated from it since isolated density and pressure effects on the speed of sound cancel each other. The speed of sound increases with height in two regions of the stratosphere and thermosphere, due to heating effects in these regions.

Mach number is a measure of the [compressibility characteristics of fluid flow](wikipedia://en/219144): the fluid (air) behaves under the influence of compressibility in a similar manner at a given Mach number, regardless of other variables.[^o9] As modeled in the [International Standard Atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Standard_Atmosphere), dry air at [mean sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_sea_level), standard temperature of 15 C, the speed of sound is \340.3 m/s.[^o10] The speed of sound is not a constant; in a gas, it increases proportionally to the square root of the [absolute temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_temperature), and since atmospheric temperature generally decreases with increasing altitude between sea level and 11000 m, the speed of sound also decreases. For example, the standard atmosphere model lapses temperature to \-56.5 C at 11000 m altitude, with a corresponding speed of sound (Mach 1) of \295.0 m/s, 86.7% of the sea level value.

### Appearance in the continuity equation {#Appearance_in_the_continuity_equation}

The Mach number arises naturally when the [continuity equation](wikipedia://en/410793) is [nondimensionalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondimensionalization) for [compressible flows](wikipedia://en/219144). If density variations are related to pressure through the isentropic relation $dp = c^{2}\,d\rho$, the [nondimensionalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondimensionalization) continuity equation contains a [prefactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefactor) $(u/c)^{2} = M^{2}$. This shows that the Mach number directly measures the importance of compressibility effects in a flow. In the limit $M \to 0$, the equation reduces to the [incompressibility](wikipedia://en/461477) condition $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$.[^o12]

## Classification of Mach regimes {#Classification_of_Mach_regimes}

The terms *subsonic* and *supersonic* are used to refer to speeds below and above the local speed of sound, and to particular ranges of Mach values. This occurs because of the presence of a *transonic regime* around flight (free stream) M = 1 where approximations of the [Navier-Stokes equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier-Stokes_equations) used for subsonic design no longer apply; the simplest explanation is that the flow around an airframe locally begins to exceed M = 1 even though the free stream Mach number is below this value.

Meanwhile, the *supersonic regime* is usually used to talk about the set of Mach numbers for which linearised theory may be used, where for example the ([air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air)) flow is not chemically reacting, and where heat-transfer between air and vehicle may be reasonably neglected in calculations.

## High-speed flow around objects {#High-speed_flow_around_objects}

Flight can be roughly classified in six categories:

::: {#o21 .table cue=table}

| Regime | [Subsonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsonic_aircraft) | [Transonic](wikipedia://en/247737) | [Speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853) | [Supersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic) | [Hypersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic) | [Hypervelocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypervelocity) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mach | \<0.8 | \0.8–1.2 | \1.0 | \1.2–5.0 | \5.0–10.0 | \>8.8 |

:::

At transonic speeds, the flow field around the object includes both sub- and supersonic parts. The transonic period begins when first zones of M > 1 flow appear around the object. In case of an airfoil (such as an aircraft's wing), this typically happens above the wing. Supersonic flow can decelerate back to subsonic only in a normal shock; this typically happens before the trailing edge. (Fig.1a)

As the speed increases, the zone of M > 1 flow increases towards both leading and trailing edges. As M = 1 is reached and passed, the normal shock reaches the trailing edge and becomes a weak oblique shock: the flow decelerates over the shock, but remains supersonic. A normal shock is created ahead of the object, and the only subsonic zone in the flow field is a small area around the object's leading edge. (Fig.1b)

When an aircraft exceeds Mach 1 (i.e. the [sound barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_barrier)), a large pressure difference is created just in front of the [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft). This abrupt pressure difference, called a [shock wave](wikipedia://en/146253), spreads backward and outward from the aircraft in a cone shape (a so-called [Mach cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mach_cone)). It is this shock wave that causes the [sonic boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_boom) heard as a fast moving aircraft travels overhead. A person inside the aircraft will not hear this. The higher the speed, the more narrow the cone; at just over M = 1 it is hardly a cone at all, but closer to a slightly concave plane.

At fully supersonic speed, the shock wave starts to take its cone shape and flow is either completely supersonic, or (in case of a blunt object), only a very small subsonic flow area remains between the object's nose and the shock wave it creates ahead of itself. (In the case of a sharp object, there is no air between the nose and the shock wave: the shock wave starts from the nose.)

As the Mach number increases, so does the strength of the [shock wave](wikipedia://en/146253) and the Mach cone becomes increasingly narrow. As the fluid flow crosses the shock wave, its speed is reduced and temperature, pressure, and density increase. The stronger the shock, the greater the changes. At high enough Mach numbers the temperature increases so much over the shock that ionization and dissociation of gas molecules behind the shock wave begin.

## High-speed flow in a channel {#High-speed_flow_in_a_channel}

As a flow in a channel becomes supersonic, one significant change takes place. The conservation of [mass flow rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_flow_rate) leads one to expect that contracting the flow channel would increase the flow speed (i.e. making the channel narrower results in faster air flow) and at subsonic speeds this holds true. However, once the flow becomes supersonic, the relationship of flow area and speed is reversed: expanding the channel actually increases the speed.[^o23]

## Calculation {#Calculation}

When the speed of sound is known, the Mach number at which an aircraft is flying can be calculated by

$$\mathrm{M} = \frac{u}{c}$$

where:

- M is the Mach number
- *u* is [velocity](wikipedia://en/20491903) of the moving aircraft and
- *c* is the [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853) at the given altitude (more properly temperature)

and the speed of sound varies with the [thermodynamic temperature](wikipedia://en/41789) as:

$$\begin{aligned} c &= \sqrt{\gamma R_* T} &&R_* = \frac{R}{M_\text{dry air}}\\ R &= 8.31446261815324 &&M_\text{dry air} \approx 0.0289647\\ \gamma &\approx 1.4 && R_* \approx 287.055023\\ c &\approx 20.046871\sqrt{T} &&M \approx \frac{u}{20.046871\sqrt{T}} \end{aligned}$$

where:

- $\gamma\,$ is the [ratio of specific heat](wikipedia://en/1949009) of a gas at a constant pressure to heat at a constant volume (e.g. 1.4 for room temperature dry air)
- $R_*$ is the [specific gas constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_gas_constant) for dry air,
- $T,$ is the static air temperature in [Kelvins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin_%28unit%29).

If the speed of sound is not known, Mach number may be determined by measuring the various air pressures (static and dynamic) and using the following formula that is derived from [Bernoulli's equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli's_principle) for Mach numbers less than 1.0. Assuming air to be an [ideal gas](wikipedia://en/65905), the formula to compute Mach number in a subsonic compressible flow is:[^o25]

$$\mathrm{M} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma- 1 }\left[\left(\frac{q_c}{p} + 1\right)^\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma} - 1\right]}\,$$

where:

- *q<sub>c</sub>* is [impact pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_pressure) (dynamic pressure)
- *p* is [static pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_pressure)
- $\gamma\,$ is the [ratio of specific heat](wikipedia://en/1949009) of a gas at a constant pressure to heat at a constant volume (1.4 for air)

The formula to compute Mach number in a supersonic compressible flow is derived from the [Rayleigh](wikipedia://en/59656) supersonic pitot equation:

$$\frac{p_t}{p} = \left[\frac{\gamma + 1}{2}\mathrm{M}^2\right]^\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1} \cdot \left[\frac{\gamma + 1}{1 - \gamma + 2\gamma\, \mathrm{M}^2}\right]^\frac{1}{\gamma - 1}$$

### Calculating Mach number from pitot tube pressure {#Calculating_Mach_number_from_pitot_tube_pressure}

Mach number is a function of temperature and true airspeed. Aircraft [flight instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_instruments), however, operate using pressure differential to compute Mach number, not temperature.

Assuming air to be an [ideal gas](wikipedia://en/65905), the formula to compute Mach number in a subsonic compressible flow is found from Bernoulli's equation for M \< 1 (above):[^o25]

$$\mathrm{M} = \sqrt{5\left[\left(\frac{q_c}{p} + 1\right)^\frac{2}{7} - 1\right]}\,$$

The formula to compute Mach number in a supersonic compressible flow can be found from the Rayleigh supersonic pitot equation (above) using parameters for air:

$$\mathrm{M} \approx 0.88128485 \sqrt{\left(\frac{q_c}{p} + 1\right)\left(1 - \frac{1}{7\,\mathrm{M}^2}\right)^{2.5}}$$

where:

- *q<sub>c</sub>* is the dynamic pressure measured behind a normal shock.

As can be seen, M appears on both sides of the equation, and for practical purposes a [root-finding algorithm](wikipedia://en/153299) must be used for a numerical solution (the equation is a [septic equation](wikipedia://en/25451646) in M<sup>2</sup> and, though some of these may be solved explicitly, the [Abel–Ruffini theorem](wikipedia://en/152518) guarantees that there exists no general form for the roots of these polynomials). It is first determined whether M is indeed greater than 1.0 by calculating M from the subsonic equation. If M is greater than 1.0 at that point, then the value of M from the subsonic equation is used as the initial condition for [fixed point iteration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_point_iteration) of the supersonic equation, which usually converges very rapidly.[^o25] Alternatively, [Newton's method](wikipedia://en/22145) can also be used.
