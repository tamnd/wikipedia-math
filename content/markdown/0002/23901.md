# Polytope compound

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polytope compound"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **polyhedral compound** is a figure that is composed of several [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra) sharing a common [centre](wikipedia://en/187926). They are the three-dimensional analogs of [polygonal compounds](wikipedia://en/313303#Regular_compounds) such as the [hexagram](wikipedia://en/302441).

:::

The outer [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) of a compound can be connected to form a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) called its [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634). A compound is a [faceting](wikipedia://en/8613537) of its convex hull.

Another convex polyhedron is formed by the small central space common to all members of the compound. This polyhedron can be used as the core for a set of [stellations](wikipedia://en/180253).

## Regular compounds {#Regular_compounds}

A regular polyhedral compound can be defined as a compound which, like a [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299), is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive), [edge-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-transitive), and [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive). Unlike the case of polyhedra, this is not equivalent to the [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) acting transitively on its [flags](wikipedia://en/9352497); the compound of two tetrahedra is the only regular compound with that property. There are five regular compounds of polyhedra:

Best known is the regular compound of two [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606), often called the [stella octangula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula), a name given to it by [Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736). The vertices of the two tetrahedra define a [cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_%28geometry%29), and the intersection of the two define a regular [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), which shares the same face-planes as the compound. Thus the compound of two tetrahedra is a [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of the octahedron, and in fact, the only finite stellation thereof.

The regular [compound of five tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/3645795) comes in two [enantiomorphic](wikipedia://en/1170160) versions, which together make up the regular compound of ten tetrahedra.[^o9] The regular compound of ten tetrahedra can also be seen as a compound of five stellae octangulae.[^o9]

Each of the regular tetrahedral compounds is self-dual or dual to its chiral twin; the regular compound of five cubes and the regular compound of five octahedra are dual to each other.

Hence, regular polyhedral compounds can also be regarded as **dual-regular compounds**.

Coxeter's notation for regular compounds is given in the table above, incorporating [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394). The material inside the square brackets, \[*d*{*p*,*q*}\], denotes the components of the compound: *d* separate {*p*,*q*}'s. The material *before* the square brackets denotes the vertex arrangement of the compound: *c*{*m*,*n*}\[*d*{*p*,*q*}\] is a compound of *d* {*p*,*q*}'s sharing the vertices of {*m*,*n*} counted *c* times. The material *after* the square brackets denotes the facet arrangement of the compound: \[*d*{*p*,*q*}\]*e*{*s*,*t*} is a compound of *d* {*p*,*q*}'s sharing the faces of {*s*,*t*} counted *e* times. These may be combined: thus *c*{*m*,*n*}\[*d*{*p*,*q*}\]*e*{*s*,*t*} is a compound of *d* {*p*,*q*}'s sharing the vertices of {*m*,*n*} counted *c* times *and* the faces of {*s*,*t*} counted *e* times. This notation can be generalised to compounds in any number of dimensions.[^o10]

## Dual compounds {#Dual_compounds}

A **dual** compound is composed of a polyhedron and its dual, arranged reciprocally about a common [midsphere](wikipedia://en/4709281), such that the edge of one polyhedron intersects the dual edge of the dual polyhedron. There are five dual compounds of the regular polyhedra.

The core is the [rectification](wikipedia://en/2192366) of both solids. The hull is the dual of this rectification, and its rhombic faces have the intersecting edges of the two solids as diagonals (and have their four alternate vertices). For the convex solids, this is the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634).

The tetrahedron is self-dual, so the dual compound of a tetrahedron with its dual is the regular [stellated octahedron](wikipedia://en/2601866).

The octahedral and icosahedral dual compounds are the first stellations of the [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) and [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023), respectively.

The small stellated dodecahedral (or great dodecahedral) dual compound has the great dodecahedron completely interior to the small stellated dodecahedron.[^o18]

## Group theory {#Group_theory}

In terms of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), if *G* is the symmetry group of a polyhedral compound, and the group [acts transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_transitively) on the polyhedra (so that each polyhedron can be sent to any of the others, as in uniform compounds), then if *H* is the [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_subgroup) of a single chosen polyhedron, the polyhedra can be identified with the [orbit space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_space) *G*/*H* – the coset *gH* corresponds to which polyhedron *g* sends the chosen polyhedron to.

## Compounds of tilings {#Compounds_of_tilings}

There are eighteen two-parameter families of regular compound tessellations of the Euclidean plane. In the hyperbolic plane, five one-parameter families and seventeen isolated cases are known, but the completeness of this listing has not been enumerated.

The Euclidean and hyperbolic compound families 2 {*p*,*p*} (4 ≤ *p* ≤ ∞, *p* an integer) are analogous to the spherical [stella octangula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula), 2 {3,3}.

A known family of regular Euclidean compound honeycombs in any number of dimensions is an infinite family of compounds of [hypercubic honeycombs](wikipedia://en/12237982), all sharing vertices and faces with another hypercubic honeycomb. This compound can have any number of hypercubic honeycombs.

There are also *dual-regular* tiling compounds. A simple example is the E<sup>2</sup> compound of a [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719) and its dual [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858), which shares its edges with the [deltoidal trihexagonal tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deltoidal_trihexagonal_tiling). The Euclidean compounds of two hypercubic honeycombs are both regular and dual-regular.
