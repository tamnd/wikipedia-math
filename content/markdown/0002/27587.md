# Summary statistics

*Image: Michelsonmorley-boxplot.svg.* [Box plot](wikipedia://en/160960) of the [Michelson–Morley experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelson–Morley_experiment), showing several summary statistics.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Summary statistics"}

In [descriptive statistics](wikipedia://en/8187), **summary statistics** are used to summarize a set of [observations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation), in order to communicate the largest amount of information as simply as possible. Statisticians commonly try to describe the observations in

:::

- a measure of location, or [central tendency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_tendency), such as the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612)
- a measure of [statistical dispersion](wikipedia://en/23773247) like the [standard mean absolute deviation](wikipedia://en/27590)
- a measure of the shape of the distribution like [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) or [kurtosis](wikipedia://en/16848)
- if more than one variable is measured, a measure of [statistical dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_and_dependence) such as a [correlation coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_product-moment_correlation_coefficient)

A common collection of [order statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_statistics) used as summary statistics are the [five-number summary](wikipedia://en/160963), sometimes extended to a [seven-number summary](wikipedia://en/18083379), and the associated [box plot](wikipedia://en/160960).

Entries in an [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634) table can also be regarded as summary statistics.p. 378

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Location estimation"}

### Location {#Location}

Common measures of location, or [central tendency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_tendency), are the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612), [median](wikipedia://en/18837), [mode](wikipedia://en/1432127), and [interquartile mean](wikipedia://en/44145).[^o6][^o7]

:::

### Spread {#Spread}

Common measures of [statistical dispersion](wikipedia://en/23773247) are the [standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590), [variance](wikipedia://en/32344), [range](wikipedia://en/27588), [interquartile range](wikipedia://en/14812), [absolute deviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_deviation), [mean absolute difference](wikipedia://en/5730990) and the [distance standard deviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance_standard_deviation). Measures that assess spread in comparison to the typical size of data values include the [coefficient of variation](wikipedia://en/1012687).

The [Gini coefficient](wikipedia://en/12883) was originally developed to measure income inequality and is equivalent to one of the [L-moments](wikipedia://en/24350828).

A simple summary of a dataset is sometimes given by quoting particular [order statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_statistics) as approximations to selected [percentiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percentiles) of a distribution.

### Shape {#Shape}

Common measures of the shape of a distribution are [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) or [kurtosis](wikipedia://en/16848), while alternatives can be based on [L-moments](wikipedia://en/24350828). A different measure is the [distance skewness](wikipedia://en/28212#Distance_skewness), for which a value of zero implies central symmetry.

### Dependence {#Dependence}

The common measure of dependence between paired random variables is the [Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_product-moment_correlation_coefficient), while a common alternative summary statistic is [Spearman's rank correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/235623). A value of zero for the [distance correlation](wikipedia://en/28217722) implies independence.

:::

## Human perception of summary statistics {#Human_perception_of_summary_statistics}

Humans efficiently use summary statistics to quickly perceive the gist of auditory and visual information.[^o12][^o13][^o14]
