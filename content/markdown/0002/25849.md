# Ralph Merkle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ralph Merkle"}

**Ralph C. Merkle** (born February 2, 1952) is an American computer scientist and mathematician. He is one of the inventors of [public-key cryptography](wikipedia://en/24222), the inventor of [cryptographic hashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographic_hash), and more recently a researcher and speaker on [cryonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryonics).

:::

Merkle is a renowned cryptographer, known for devising [Merkle's Puzzles](wikipedia://en/791670), co-inventing the [Merkle–Hellman knapsack](wikipedia://en/59652) cryptosystem, and inventing cryptographic hashing ([Merkle–Damgård construction](wikipedia://en/4076217)) and [Merkle trees](wikipedia://en/2497388). He has worked as a manager at [Elxsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elxsi), research scientist at [Xerox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerox) [PARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PARC_%28company%29) (Palo Alto Research Center), and a [nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanotechnology) theorist at [Zyvex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zyvex). Merkle has held positions as a Distinguished Professor at [Georgia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech), senior research fellow at IMM, faculty member at [Singularity University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singularity_Group), and board member at [Alcor Life Extension Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcor_Life_Extension_Foundation). He received the [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers) [Richard W. Hamming Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Richard_W._Hamming_Medal) in 2010 and has published works on [molecular manipulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_manipulation) and [self-replicating machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-replicating_machine). Ralph Merkle is a grandnephew of baseball star [Fred Merkle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Merkle) and is married to video game designer [Carol Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Shaw). He serves on the board of directors of the [cryonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryonics_Institute) organization Alcor Life Extension Foundation and appears in the science fiction novel *[The Diamond Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Diamond_Age)*.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

While an undergraduate, Merkle devised [Merkle's Puzzles](wikipedia://en/791670), a scheme for communication over an [insecure channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insecure_channel), as part of a class project at UC Berkeley.[^o6] The scheme is now recognized to be an early example of [public key cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_key_cryptography). He co-invented the [Merkle–Hellman knapsack cryptosystem](wikipedia://en/59652), invented [cryptographic hashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographic_hashing) (now called the [Merkle–Damgård construction](wikipedia://en/4076217) based on a pair of articles published 10 years later that established the security of the scheme), and invented [Merkle trees](wikipedia://en/2497388). The Merkle–Damgård construction is at the heart of many hashing algorithms.[^o7][^o8] At [Xerox PARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerox_PARC) Merkle designed the [Khufu and Khafre](wikipedia://en/634695) [block ciphers](wikipedia://en/4594) and the [Snefru](wikipedia://en/450613) hash function.

## Career {#Career}

Merkle was the manager of [compiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiler) development at [Elxsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elxsi) from 1980. In 1988, he became a research scientist at [Xerox PARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerox_PARC). In 1999, he became a nanotechnology theorist for [Zyvex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zyvex). In 2003 he became a [Distinguished Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Professor) at [Georgia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology), where he led the [Georgia Tech Information Security Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech_Information_Security_Center).[^o10] In 2006 he returned to the San Francisco Bay Area, where he has been a senior research fellow at IMM, a faculty member at [Singularity University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singularity_University), and a board member of the [Alcor Life Extension Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcor_Life_Extension_Foundation). He was awarded the [IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Richard_W._Hamming_Medal) in 2010.[^o11] He is active in the field of molecular manipulation and self-replicating machines and has published books on the subject.[^o12]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Ralph Merkle is a grandnephew of baseball star [Fred Merkle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Merkle); son of Theodore Charles Merkle, director of [Project Pluto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Pluto); and brother of [Judith Merkle Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judith_Merkle_Riley), a historical writer.[^o14] Merkle is married to [Carol Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Shaw_%28video_game_designer%29),[^o14] the video game designer best known for the 1982 Atari 2600 game *[River Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Raid)*.

Merkle is on the board of directors of the [cryonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryonics) organization [Alcor Life Extension Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcor_Life_Extension_Foundation).[^o15]

Merkle appears in the [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction) novel *[The Diamond Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Diamond_Age)*, involving nanotechnology.

## Awards {#Awards}

- 1996 [Paris Kanellakis Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Kanellakis_Award) (from the [ACM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery)) for the Invention of [Public Key Cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Key_Cryptography).[^o17]
- 1998 [Feynman Prize in Nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_Prize_in_Nanotechnology) for computational modeling of molecular tools for atomically-precise chemical reactions[^o18]
- 1999 [IEEE Koji Kobayashi Computers and Communications Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Koji_Kobayashi_Computers_and_Communications_Award)[^o19]
- 2000 [RSA Award for Excellence in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_Award_for_Excellence_in_Mathematics) for the invention of public key cryptography.[^o20]
- 2008 International Association for Cryptographic Research (IACR) fellow for the invention of public key cryptography.[^o21]
- 2010 [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE) Hamming Medal for the invention of public key cryptography[^o22]
- 2011 [Computer History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_History_Museum) Fellow "for his work, with Whitfield Diffie and Martin Hellman, on public key cryptography."[^o23]
- 2011 [National Inventors Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Inventors_Hall_of_Fame), for the invention of public key cryptography[^o24]
- 2012 [National Cyber Security Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cyber_Security_Hall_of_Fame) inductee
- 2020 [Levchin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levchin_Prize) “for fundamental contributions to the development of public key cryptography, hash algorithms, Merkle trees, and digital signatures”[^o25]
