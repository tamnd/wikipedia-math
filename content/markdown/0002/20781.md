# Monoid ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monoid ring"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **monoid ring** is a [ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_%28algebra%29) constructed from a ring and a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652), just as a [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) is constructed from a ring and a [group](wikipedia://en/19447).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *R* be a ring and let *G* be a monoid. The monoid ring or **monoid algebra** of *G* over *R*, denoted *R*\[*G*\] or *RG*, is the set of formal sums $\sum_{g \in G} r_g g$, where $r_g \in R$ for each $g \in G$ and *r*<sub>*g*</sub> = 0 for all but finitely many *g*, equipped with coefficient-wise addition, and the multiplication in which the elements of *R* commute with the elements of *G*. More formally, *R*\[*G*\] is the free *R*-module on the set *G*, endowed with *R*-linear multiplication defined on the base elements by *g·h* := *gh*, where the left-hand side is understood as the multiplication in *R*\[*G*\] and the right-hand side is understood in *G*.

Alternatively, one can identify the element $g \in R[G]$ with the function *e<sub>g</sub>* that maps *g* to 1 and every other element of *G* to 0. This way, *R*\[*G*\] is identified with the set of functions φ: *G* → *R* such that {*g* : φ(*g*) ≠ 0} is finite. equipped with addition of functions, and with multiplication defined by

$$(\phi \psi)(g) = \sum_{k\ell=g} \phi(k) \psi(\ell).$$

If *G* is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447), then *R*\[*G*\] is also called the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) of *G* over *R*.

:::

## Universal property {#Universal_property}

Given *R* and *G*, there is a [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) α: *R* → *R*\[*G*\] sending each *r* to *r*1 (where 1 is the identity element of *G*), and a [monoid homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid_homomorphism) β: *G* → *R*\[*G*\] (where the latter is viewed as a monoid under multiplication) sending each *g* to 1*g* (where 1 is the multiplicative identity of *R*). We have that α(*r*) commutes with β(*g*) for all *r* in *R* and *g* in *G*.

The universal property of the monoid ring states that given a ring *S*, a ring homomorphism α': *R* → *S*, and a monoid homomorphism β': *G* → *S* to the multiplicative monoid of *S*, such that α'(*r*) commutes with β'(*g*) for all *r* in *R* and *g* in *G*, there is a unique ring homomorphism γ: *R*\[*G*\] → *S* such that composing α and β with γ produces α' and β '.

## Augmentation {#Augmentation}

The [augmentation](wikipedia://en/43337378) is the ring homomorphism *η*: *R*\[*G*\] → *R* defined by

$$\eta\left(\sum_{g\in G} r_g g\right) = \sum_{g\in G} r_g.$$

The [kernel](wikipedia://en/330095) of *η* is called the [augmentation ideal](wikipedia://en/2224774). It is a [free](wikipedia://en/326454) *R*-[module](wikipedia://en/276410) with basis consisting of 1 – *g* for all *g* in *G* not equal to 1.

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Given a ring *R* and the (additive) monoid of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) **N** (or {*x*<sup>*n*</sup>} viewed multiplicatively), we obtain the ring *R*\[{*x*<sup>*n*</sup>}\] =: *R*\[*x*\] of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) over *R*. The monoid **N**<sup>*n*</sup> (with the addition) gives the polynomial ring with *n* variables: *R*\[**N**<sup>*n*</sup>\] =: *R*\[*X*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *X*<sub>*n*</sub>\].

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

If *G* is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799), the same construction yields a **semigroup ring** *R*\[*G*\].
