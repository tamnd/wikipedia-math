# Recreational mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Recreational mathematics"}

*Image: Conways game of life breeder animation.gif.* [John Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Conway)'s [game of life](wikipedia://en/37035) [breeder-pattern animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breeder_%28cellular_automaton%29).

**Recreational mathematics** is [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) carried out for [recreation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreation) (entertainment) rather than as a strictly research-and-application-based professional activity or as a part of a student's formal education.

:::

The [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA) has seventeen [Special Interest Groups](wikipedia://en/198772#Special_Interest_Groups) which includes a recreational math group called SIGMAA-Rec. The SIGMAA-Rec website defines recreational mathematics as, "...a broad term that covers many different areas including [games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_games), [puzzles](wikipedia://en/366445), magic, art, and more."[^o2]

Although it is not necessarily limited to being an endeavor for [amateurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_amateur_mathematicians), many topics in this field require no knowledge of advanced mathematics.[^o3] Mathematical competitions (such as those sponsored by [mathematical associations](wikipedia://en/2742403)) are also categorized under recreational mathematics.

## Areas of mathematics {#Areas_of_mathematics}

Fields of mathematics

*Image: Knight's tour anim 2.gif.* The [Knight's tour](wikipedia://en/38501), a [mathematical chess problem](wikipedia://en/30681859), animated.

Some of the more well-known topics in recreational mathematics are [Rubik's Cubes](wikipedia://en/25971), [magic squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_squares), [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractals), [logic puzzles](wikipedia://en/101595) and [mathematical chess problems](wikipedia://en/30681859), but this area of mathematics includes the [aesthetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetics) and [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture) of mathematics, peculiar or amusing stories and [coincidences about mathematics](wikipedia://en/740817), and the personal lives of [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902).

## History {#History}

### 1200s {#1200s}

*Image: Liber abbaci magliab f124r.jpg.* A page of Fibonacci's Liber Abaci from the Biblioteca Nazionale di Firenze showing (in box on right) 13 entries of the Fibonacci sequence.

The [Liber Abaci](wikipedia://en/167699) or Liber Abbaci was written by [Leonardo of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_of_Pisa), posthumously known as [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949). Multiple math puzzles are presented with one titled, *How Many Pairs of Rabbits Are Created by One Pair in One Year.*[^o7]

### 1700s {#1700s}

[Choi Seok-jeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choi_Seok-jeong) (Korean: 최석정; Hanja: 崔錫鼎; 1646–1715) made the earliest known form of [latin squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_squares) in his book *Gusuryak* (Koo-Soo-Ryak: Korean: 구수략; Hanja: 九數略).[^o9] The mathematical-puzzle game [Sudoku](wikipedia://en/1365807) created in the 1970s is a special case of Latin squares.

### 1800s {#1800s}

[The Lady's and Gentleman's Diary](wikipedia://en/42340154) was a recreational math magazine created by [Wesley S. B. Woolhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_S._B._Woolhouse) which ran from 1704 to 1871.[^o11]

### 1900s {#1900s}

[Howard Garns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Garns) created the first Sudoku published for the [Dell Magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell_Magazines) in May 1979.[^o13]

## Forms {#Forms}

Category:Mathematics and culture

[Numeracy](wikipedia://en/397245) is a requirement for recreational activities, from knowing the [range](wikipedia://en/27588) of people who can participate at a time to using specific areas of knowledge to participate at all. Hobbiests enjoy finding the mathematical sides of the world and creating enjoyable experiences with mathematics.

### Books {#Books}

Different from a singular [math word problem](wikipedia://en/297013) [mathematical fiction](wikipedia://en/50334376) books may involve multiple problems which further build upon each other as the story progresses.

One example is a children's-book series called *[Usborne Puzzle Adventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usborne_Puzzle_Adventure_series)*.[^o16]

### Games {#Games}

*Image: Young people playing Igisoro, a traditional Rwandan Game.ogv.* Young people playing a Mancala-variant called [Igisoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igisoro), a traditional [Rwandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda) game.

[Mathematical games](wikipedia://en/20434) are [multiplayer games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplayer_game) whose rules, strategies, and outcomes can be studied and explained using [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). The players of the game may not need to use explicit mathematics in order to play mathematical games. For example, [Mancala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mancala) is studied in the mathematical field of [combinatorial game theory](wikipedia://en/292231), but no mathematics is necessary in order to play it.

### Mathemagics {#Mathemagics}

*Image: 27\_card\_trick.svg.* Mathematical explanation of the [Twenty-One Card Trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-One_Card_Trick): The cards are dealt into three piles which are accumulated with the pile containing the target card (shaded yellow and labelled with the step number) put in the middle. This is done three times, the middle card (\*) is the one in all chosen piles.

[Magic tricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_tricks) based on mathematical principles can produce self-working but surprising effects. For instance, a [mathemagician](wikipedia://en/2164767) might use the [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) properties of a deck of [playing cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_52-card_deck) to guess a volunteer's selected card, or [Hamming codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamming_codes) to identify whether a volunteer is lying.[^o19]

### Puzzles {#Puzzles}

*Image: Vagão desajeitado (IME USP).webm.* Clumsy wagon, a puzzle that has as its core a mathematical problem that seeks to verify if it is possible for a wagon with only two points of contact to pass through a winding rail, available on the collection of Matemateca IME-USP.

[Mathematical puzzles](wikipedia://en/366445) require mathematics in order to solve them. They have specific rules, as do [multiplayer games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplayer_game), but mathematical puzzles do not usually involve competition between two or more players. Instead, in order to solve such a [puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle), the solver must find a solution that satisfies the given conditions.

[Logic puzzles](wikipedia://en/101595) and [classical ciphers](wikipedia://en/705892) are common examples of mathematical puzzles. [Cellular automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_automata) and [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractals) are also considered mathematical puzzles, even though the solver only interacts with them by providing a set of initial conditions.

As they often include or require game-like features or thinking, mathematical puzzles are sometimes also called mathematical games. These games can be done through different forms of media, such as board games, books, comics, and video games.

### Other activities {#Other_activities}

*Image: One-DOF-Superimposed-Rigid-Origami-with-Multiple-States-srep36883-s7.ogv.* Origami-inspired engineering to discover how to make self-folding structures.[^o24]

Other curiosities and pastimes of non-trivial mathematical interest include:

- patterns in [juggling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juggling)
- the sometimes profound algorithmic and geometrical characteristics of [origami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origami)
- patterns and process in creating [string figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_figure) such as [Cat's cradles](wikipedia://en/491964), etc.
- [fractal-generating software](wikipedia://en/19725960)
- [Mathematics and art](wikipedia://en/22468661)

## Relationship with the sciences {#Relationship_with_the_sciences}

*Image: BonkioValleyDiagram.webm.* A video from the Wikibook, '[Physics Explained Through a Video Game](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Physics_Explained_Through_a_Video_Game).'

The academic field of mathematics is a core-component for others, such as [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939). Whenever there is recreational play in those academic fields they include recreational mathematics as well.

## Distributers {#Distributers}

Recreational mathematics is shared in similar ways as other hobbies. Individual enthusiasts and math-related organizations will make and share what they have created and/or learned.

### Publications {#Publications}

- The journal *[Eureka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_%28University_of_Cambridge_magazine%29)* published by the mathematical society of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) is one of the oldest publications in recreational mathematics. It has been published 60 times since 1939 and authors have included many famous mathematicians and scientists such as [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435), [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807), [Roger Penrose](wikipedia://en/26193), [Ian Stewart](wikipedia://en/332183), [Timothy Gowers](wikipedia://en/365715), [Stephen Hawking](wikipedia://en/19376148) and [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742).
- The *[Journal of Recreational Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1118492)* was the largest publication on this topic from its founding in 1968 until 2014 when it ceased publication.
- *[Mathematical Games](wikipedia://en/44051448)* (1956 to 1981) was the title of a long-running *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* column on recreational mathematics by [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435). He inspired several generations of mathematicians and scientists through his interest in mathematical recreations. "Mathematical Games" was succeeded by 25 "[Metamagical Themas](wikipedia://en/169419)" columns (1981–1983), a similarly distinguished, but shorter-running, column by [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758), then by 78 "Mathematical Recreations" and "Computer Recreations" columns (1984 to 1991) by [A. K. Dewdney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._K._Dewdney), then by 96 "Mathematical Recreations" columns (1991 to 2001) by [Ian Stewart](wikipedia://en/332183), and most recently "Puzzling Adventures" by [Dennis Shasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Shasha).
- The [Recreational Mathematics Magazine](http://rmm.ludus-opuscula.org/), published by the [Ludus Association](http://ludicum.org/), is electronic and semiannual, and focuses on results that provide amusing, witty but nonetheless original and scientifically profound mathematical nuggets. The issues are published in the exact moments of the equinox.
