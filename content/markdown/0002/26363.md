# RIPEMD

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="RIPEMD"}

*Image: RIPEMD 160 2.png.* A sub-block from the compression function of the RIPEMD-160 hash algorithm

**RIPEMD** (**RIPE Message Digest**) is a family of [cryptographic hash functions](wikipedia://en/439526) developed in 1992 (the original RIPEMD) and 1996 (other variants). There are five functions in the family: RIPEMD, RIPEMD-128, RIPEMD-160, RIPEMD-256, and RIPEMD-320, of which RIPEMD-160 is the most common.[^o3]

:::

The original RIPEMD, as well as RIPEMD-128, is not considered secure because 128-bit result is too small and also (for the original RIPEMD) because of design weaknesses. The 256- and 320-bit versions of RIPEMD provide the same level of security as RIPEMD-128 and RIPEMD-160, respectively; they are designed for applications where the security level is sufficient, but a longer hash result is necessary.

While RIPEMD functions are less popular than [SHA-1](wikipedia://en/26672) and [SHA-2](wikipedia://en/1638777), they are used, among others, in [Bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265) and other [cryptocurrencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrencies) based on Bitcoin.[^o4]

## History {#History}

The original RIPEMD function was designed in the framework of the [EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union) project RIPE ([RACE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RACE_%28Europe%29) Integrity Primitives Evaluation) in 1992.[^o6][^o7] Its design was based on the [MD4](wikipedia://en/578106) hash function. In 1996, in response to security weaknesses found in the original RIPEMD,[^o8] [Hans Dobbertin](wikipedia://en/2210027), [Antoon Bosselaers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoon_Bosselaers) and [Bart Preneel](wikipedia://en/1520490) at the [COSIC](wikipedia://en/8116603) research group at the [Katholieke Universiteit Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katholieke_Universiteit_Leuven) in [Leuven, Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuven) published four strengthened variants: RIPEMD-128, RIPEMD-160, RIPEMD-256, and RIPEMD-320.[^o9]

In August 2004, a collision was reported for the original RIPEMD.[^o10] This does not apply to RIPEMD-160.[^o11]

In 2019, the best collision attack for RIPEMD-160 could reach 34 rounds out of 80 rounds, which was published at CRYPTO 2019.[^o12]

In February 2023, a collision attack for RIPEMD-160 was published at EUROCRYPT 2023, which could reach 36 rounds out of 80 rounds with time complexity of 2<sup>\64.5</sup>.[^o13]

In December 2023, an improved collision attack was found based on the technique from the previous best collision attack, this improved collision attack could reach 40 rounds out of 80 round with a theoretical time complexity of 2<sup>\49.9</sup>.[^o14]

## RIPEMD-160 hashes {#RIPEMD-160_hashes}

The 160-bit RIPEMD-160 hashes (also termed RIPE *message digests*) are typically represented as 40-digit [hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263) numbers. The following demonstrates a 43-byte [ASCII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCII) input and the corresponding RIPEMD-160 hash:

RIPEMD-160("The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy #87CEEBog") = 37f332f68db77bd9d7edd4969571ad671cf9dd3b

RIPEMD-160 behaves with the desired [avalanche effect](wikipedia://en/769439) of cryptographic hash functions (small changes, e.g. changing d to c, result in a completely different hash):

RIPEMD-160("The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy #87CEEBog") = 132072df690933835eb8b6ad0b77e7b6f14acad7

The hash of a zero-length string is:

RIPEMD-160("") = 9c1185a5c5e9fc54612808977ee8f548b2258d31
