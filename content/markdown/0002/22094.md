# Nutation

*Image: Nutation.gif.* Animation of free (or Euler) nutation of a sphere

*Image: Praezession.svg.* green solid 2px blue solid 2px red solid 2px in obliquity of a planet caused by the [gravitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity) forces of other nearby bodies acting upon the planet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nutation"}

**Nutation** (la) is a rocking, swaying, or nodding motion in the [axis of rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_rotation) of a largely axially symmetric object, such as a [gyroscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroscope), [planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet), or [bullet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet) [in flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_ballistics), or as an intended behaviour of a mechanism.

:::

In an appropriate [reference frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_of_reference) it can be defined as a change in the second [Euler angle](wikipedia://en/411492#Euler_rotations). If it is not caused by forces external to the body, it is called free nutation or Euler nutation (after [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902)).[^o12]

A pure nutation is a movement of a rotational axis such that the first Euler angle (or precession) is constant. Astronomers usually make a distinction between *[precession](wikipedia://en/72576)*, which is a steady long-term change in the axis of rotation, and *nutation*, which is the combined effect of similar shorter-term variations.[^o2] Therefore it can be seen that the circular red arrow in the planetary nutation diagram indicates the combined effects of precession and nutation, while nutation in the absence of precession would only change the tilt from vertical (second Euler angle). However, in spacecraft dynamics, [precession](wikipedia://en/24714) (a change in the first Euler angle) is sometimes referred to as nutation.[^o3]

## In a rigid body {#In_a_rigid_body}

If a [top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_top) is set at a tilt on a horizontal surface and spun rapidly, its rotational axis starts precessing about the vertical. After a short interval, the top settles into a motion in which each point on its rotation axis follows a circular path. The vertical force of gravity produces a horizontal torque $\mathbf{\tau}$ about the point of contact with the surface; the top rotates in the direction of this torque with an angular velocity $\mathbf{\Omega}$ such that at any moment

$\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{\Omega} \times \mathbf{L}$, ([cross product](wikipedia://en/157092))

where $\mathbf{L}$ is the instantaneous angular momentum of the top.[^o5]

Initially, however, there is no precession, and the upper part of the top falls sideways and downward, thereby tilting. This gives rise to an imbalance in torques that starts the precession. In falling, the top overshoots the amount of tilt at which it would precess steadily and then oscillates about this level. This oscillation is called *nutation*. If the motion is damped, the oscillations will die down until the motion is a steady precession.[^o5][^o6]

The physics of nutation in tops and [gyroscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroscope) can be explored using the model of a *heavy [symmetrical top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetrical_top)* with its tip fixed. (A symmetrical top is one with rotational symmetry, or more generally one in which two of the three principal moments of inertia are equal.) Initially, the effect of friction is ignored. The motion of the top can be described by three [Euler angles](wikipedia://en/411492): the tilt angle $\theta$ between the symmetry axis of the top and the vertical (second Euler angle); the [azimuth](wikipedia://en/47487) $\varphi$ of the top about the vertical (first Euler angle); and the rotation angle $\psi$ of the top about its own axis (third Euler angle). Thus, precession is the change in $\varphi$ and nutation is the change in $\theta$.[^o7]

If the top has mass $M$ and its [center of mass](wikipedia://en/173961) is at a distance $l$ from the pivot point, its [gravitational potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_potential) relative to the plane of the support is

$$V = Mgl\cos(\theta).$$

In a coordinate system where the $z$ axis is the axis of symmetry, the top has [angular velocities](wikipedia://en/65927) $\omega_{1},\,\omega_{2},\,\omega_{3}$ and [moments of inertia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moments_of_inertia) $I_{1},\,I_{2},\,I_{3}$ about the $x, y$, and $z$ axes. Since we are taking a symmetric top, we have $I_{1}$=$I_{2}$. The [kinetic energy](wikipedia://en/17327) is

$$E_\text{r} = \frac{1}{2}I_1\left(\omega_1^2 + \omega_2^2\right) + \frac{1}{2}I_3\omega_3^2.$$

In terms of the Euler angles, this is

$$E_\text{r} = \frac{1}{2}I_1\left(\dot{\theta}^2 + \dot{\phi}^2\sin^2(\theta)\right) + \frac{1}{2}I_3\left(\dot{\psi} + \dot{\phi}\cos(\theta)\right)^2.$$

If the [Euler–Lagrange equations](wikipedia://en/23371726) are solved for this system, it is found that the motion depends on two constants $a$ and $b$ (each related to a [constant of motion](wikipedia://en/5391037)). The rate of precession is related to the tilt by

$$\dot{\phi} = \frac{b - a\cos(\theta)}{\sin^2(\theta)}.$$

The tilt is determined by a differential equation for $u = \cos(\theta)$ of the form

$$\dot{u}^2 = f(u)$$

where $f$ is a [cubic polynomial](wikipedia://en/243709) that depends on parameters $a$ and $b$ as well as constants that are related to the energy and the gravitational torque. The roots of $f$ are [cosines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine) of the angles at which the [rate of change](wikipedia://en/1783069) of $\theta$ is zero. One of these is not related to a physical angle; the other two determine the upper and lower bounds on the tilt angle, between which the gyroscope oscillates.[^o8]

## Astronomy {#Astronomy}

The nutation of a planet occurs because the gravitational effects of other bodies cause the speed of its [axial precession](wikipedia://en/72576) to vary over time, so that the speed is not constant. English astronomer [James Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bradley) discovered the nutation of [Earth's axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_rotation) in 1728.

::: {#o11 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Earth's nutation"}

### Earth {#Earth}

Earth's nutation

*Image: Trópico de Cáncer en México - Carretera 83 (Vía Corta) Zaragoza-Victoria, Km 27+800.jpg.* Yearly changes in the location of the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer) near a highway in Mexico

Nutation subtly changes the [axial tilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt) of Earth with respect to the [ecliptic](wikipedia://en/9264) plane, shifting the [major circles of latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_of_latitude#Major_circles_of_latitude) that are defined by the Earth's tilt (the [tropical circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_circle) and the [polar circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_circle)).

In the case of Earth, the principal sources of tidal force are the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun) and [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon), which continuously change location relative to each other and thus cause nutation in Earth's axis. The largest component of Earth's nutation has a period of 18.6 years, the same as that of the precession of the [Moon's orbital nodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_node).[^o12] However, there are other significant periodic terms that must be accounted for depending upon the desired accuracy of the result. A mathematical description (set of equations) that represents nutation is called a "theory of nutation". In the theory, parameters are adjusted in a more or less *ad hoc* method to obtain the best fit to data. Simple [rigid body dynamics](wikipedia://en/481856) do not give the best theory; one has to account for deformations of the Earth, including [mantle inelasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthenosphere) and changes in the [core–mantle boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core–mantle_boundary).[^o13]

The principal term of nutation is due to the regression of the Moon's [nodal line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nodal_line) and has the same period of 6798 days (18.61 years). It reaches plus or minus 17″ in [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) and 9.2″ in [obliquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt).[^o14] All other terms are much smaller; the next-largest, with a period of 183 days (0.5 year), has amplitudes 1.3″ and 0.6″ respectively. The periods of all terms larger than 0.0001″ (about as accurately as available technology can measure) lie between 5.5 and 6798 days; for some reason (as with ocean tidal periods) they seem to avoid the range from 34.8 to 91 days, so it is customary to split the nutation into long-period and short-period terms. The long-period terms are calculated and mentioned in the almanacs, while the additional correction due to the short-period terms is usually taken from a table. They can also be calculated from the [Julian day](wikipedia://en/38007) according to IAU 2000B methodology.[^o15]
