# Pavel Urysohn

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pavel Urysohn"}

**Pavel Samuilovich Urysohn** (in [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Па́вел Самуи́лович Урысо́н; 3 February 1898 – 17 August 1924) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who is best known for his contributions to the theory of topological dimension, and for developing [Urysohn's metrization theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urysohn's_metrization_theorem) and [Urysohn's lemma](wikipedia://en/49253), both of which are fundamental results in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). He also constructed what is now called the [Urysohn universal space](wikipedia://en/20983231)[^o3] and his name is also commemorated in the terms [Fréchet–Urysohn space](wikipedia://en/28480560), [Menger–Urysohn dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menger–Urysohn_dimension) and [Urysohn integral equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urysohn_integral_equation). He and [Pavel Alexandrov](wikipedia://en/576245) formulated the modern definition of [compactness](wikipedia://en/6042) in 1923.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Pavel Urysohn was born in [Odesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa) in 1898.[^o5] His mother died when he was little, and he entered the care of his father and sister. The family moved to [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) in 1912, where Urysohn completed his secondary education.[^o6] While still at school, he worked at [Shanyavsky University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanyavsky_Moscow_City_People's_University) on an experimental project on X-ray radiation and was supervised by [Petr Lazarev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Lazarev).[^o7][^o8]

At that time, Urysohn's interests lay predominantly in physics. Urysohn enrolled at the Moscow State University in 1915 and earned his Bachelor of Science in 1919.[^o5][^o6] There he attended the lectures of [Nikolai Luzin](wikipedia://en/576228) and [Dimitri Egorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Egorov), which made him turn his attention to mathematics.[^o8][^o6][^o5] Between 1919 and 1921, Urysohn completed a doctorate on integral equations under the supervision of Luzin.[^o9] He then became an assistant professor at [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), and Egorov prompted him to start working in topology.[^o5]

By 1922, Urysohn had given topological definitions to curve, surface, and dimension, and his work attracted the attention of many prominent European mathematicians.[^o5][^o8] In the summers of 1923 and 1924, Urysohn and his friend and fellow mathematician, [Pavel Aleksandrov](wikipedia://en/576245), traveled through France, Holland, and Germany, where they met [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), [Felix Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/10989), and [L. E. J. Brouwer](wikipedia://en/172896).[^o5] The three European mathematicians were impressed by Urysohn's work and expressed their hopes that he would visit them again in subsequent years.[^o10]

Urysohn and Aleksandrov were staying in a cottage in [Brittany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany), France, when Urysohn drowned at the age of 26 while swimming off the coast nearby [Batz-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batz-sur-Mer).[^o5]

Urysohn's sister, Lina Neiman, wrote a memoir about his life and childhood. Not being a mathematician, she included in the book memorial articles about his mathematical works by [Pavel Alexandrov](wikipedia://en/576245), [Vadim Efremovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadim_Yefremovich), [Andrei Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161), [Lazar Lyusternik](wikipedia://en/4201372), and [Mark Krasnosel'skii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Krasnoselsky).[^o11]
