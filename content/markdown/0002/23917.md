# Prismatoid

*Image: Prismatoid (parameters h,A₁,A₂,A₃).svg.* Prismatoid with parallel faces $A_{1}$ and $A_{3}$, midway cross-section $A_{2}$, and height $h$.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prismatoid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **prismatoid** is a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) whose [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) all lie in two parallel [planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29). Its lateral faces can be [trapezoids](wikipedia://en/201826) or [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654). If both planes have the same number of vertices, and the lateral faces are either [parallelograms](wikipedia://en/133496) or trapezoids, it is called a **prismoid**.

:::

## Volume {#Volume}

If the areas of the two parallel faces are $A_{1}$ and $A_{3}$, the cross-sectional area of the intersection of the prismatoid with a plane midway between the two parallel faces is $A_{2}$, and the height (the distance between the two parallel faces) is $h$, then the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of the prismatoid is given by

$$V = \frac{h(A_1 + 4A_2 + A_3)}{6}.$$

This formula follows immediately by [integrating](wikipedia://en/15532) the area parallel to the two planes of vertices by [Simpson's rule](wikipedia://en/219962), since that rule is exact for integration of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) of degree up to 3, and in this case the area is at most a [quadratic function](wikipedia://en/187240) in the height.

Families of prismatoids include:

- [Pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045), in which one plane contains only a single point;[^o5]
- [Wedges](wikipedia://en/2951640), in which one plane contains only two points;
- [Prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), whose polygons in each plane are congruent and joined by rectangles or parallelograms;[^o5]
- [Antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851), whose polygons in each plane are congruent and joined by an alternating strip of triangles;[^n1]
- [Star antiprisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_antiprism);
- [Cupolae](wikipedia://en/689743), in which the polygon in one plane contains twice as many points as the other and is joined to it by alternating triangles and rectangles;
- [Frusta](wikipedia://en/89405) obtained by [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429) of a pyramid or a cone;
- [Quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278)-faced [hexahedral](wikipedia://en/237209) prismatoids:
  1. [Parallelepipeds](wikipedia://en/23975) – six [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) faces
  1. [Rhombohedrons](wikipedia://en/2706406) – six [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) faces
  1. [Trigonal trapezohedra](wikipedia://en/3504388) – six congruent rhombus faces
  1. [Cuboids](wikipedia://en/156802) – six rectangular faces
  1. [Quadrilateral frusta](wikipedia://en/89405) – an [apex](wikipedia://en/6022079)-[truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455)
  1. [Cube](wikipedia://en/6285) – six square faces

## Higher dimensions {#Higher_dimensions}

*Image: 4D\_Tetrahedral\_Cupola-perspective-cuboctahedron-first.png.* A tetrahedral-cuboctahedral cupola.

In general, a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) is prismatoidal if its vertices exist in two [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862). For example, in four dimensions, two polyhedra can be placed in two parallel 3-spaces, and connected with polyhedral sides.
