# Numerology

*Image: Bongo, Pietro – Numerorum mysteria, 1591 – BEIC 58079.jpg.* *Numerorum mysteria* (1591), a treatise on numerology by [Pietro Bongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Bongo) and his most influential work in Europe[^n1]

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Numerology"}

**Numerology** (known before the 20th century as **arithmancy**) is the belief in an [occult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occult), divine or [mystical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysticism) relationship between a [number](wikipedia://en/21690) and [metaphysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) phenomena or ideas. It is also the study of the numerical value, via an alphanumeric system, of the letters in words and names (e.g. [gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria)). When numerology is applied to a person's name, it is a form of [onomancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onomancy). It is often associated with [astrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrology) and other [divinatory arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divination).[^n2]

:::

**Number symbolism** is an ancient and pervasive aspect of human thought, deeply intertwined with [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion), [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), mysticism, and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). Different cultures and traditions have assigned specific meanings to numbers, often linking them to divine principles, cosmic forces, or natural patterns.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The term en is derived from two Greek words – el (meaning number) and el (meaning [divination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divination)). "el" (el) Arithmancy is thus the study of divination through numbers.[^n3] Although the word "arithmancy" dates back to the 1570s,[^o6] the word "numerology" was not recorded in English before c. 1907.[^o7]

## History {#History}

The practice of [gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria), assigning numerical values to words and names and imputing those values with religious meaning, dates back to [antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history). An [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria) inscription from the 8th century BC, commissioned by [Sargon&nbsp;II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargon&nbsp;II) declares "the king built the wall of Khorsabad 16,283 [cubits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubit) long to correspond with the numerical value of his name".[^n4] [Rabbinic literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbinic_literature) used gematria to interpret passages in the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible).

The practice of using alphabetic letters to represent numbers developed in the Greek city of [Miletus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miletus), and is thus known as the Milesian system.[^n5] Early examples include vase graffiti dating back to the 6th century BCE.[^n6] Aristotle wrote that the [Pythagorean tradition](wikipedia://en/291170), founded in the 6th century by [Pythagoras of Samos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagoras_of_Samos), practiced [isopsephy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isopsephy),[^n7] the Greek predecessor of Hebrew [gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria). Pythagoras was a contemporary of the philosophers [Anaximander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaximander), [Anaximenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaximenes_of_Miletus), and the historian [Hecataeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecataeus_of_Miletus), all of whom lived in Miletus, across the sea from [Samos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samos).[^n8] The Milesian system was in common use by the reign of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) (336–323 BCE) and was adopted by other cultures during the subsequent [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period).[^n5] It was officially adopted in Egypt during the reign of [Ptolemy&nbsp;II Philadelphus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy&nbsp;II_Philadelphus) (284–246 BCE).[^n5]

By the late 4th century AD, following the [Edict of Thessalonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Thessalonica) in 380 AD and the subsequent enforcement of [Nicene Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicene_Christianity) under [Theodosius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I), departures from the beliefs of the [state church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_church_of_the_Roman_Empire) were classified as civil violations within the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire).[^n9] Numerology, referred to as [isopsephy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isopsephy), remained in use in conservative [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox) circles, particularly in mystical and theological contexts.[^n10]

Some [alchemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemical) theories were closely related to numerology. For example, Arab alchemist [Jabir ibn Hayyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabir_ibn_Hayyan) (died c. 806−816) framed his experiments in an elaborate numerology based on the names of substances in the [Arabic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language).[^n11]

Numerology is prominent in Sir [Thomas Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Browne)'s 1658 literary discourse *[The Garden of Cyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Garden_of_Cyrus)*. Throughout its pages, the author attempts to demonstrate that the number five and the related [quincunx](wikipedia://en/174906) pattern can be found throughout the arts, in design, and in nature, particularly [botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany).[^o19]

Some approaches to understanding the meanings of the [Qur'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur'an) (the book of Muslims) include the understanding of numerical meanings, numerical symbols and their combination with purely textual approaches.

## Methods {#Methods}

### Alphanumeric systems {#Alphanumeric_systems}

Various numerology systems assign numerical value to the letters of an [alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet). Examples include the [Abjad numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abjad_numerals) in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), [Hebrew numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_numerals), [Armenian numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_numerals), and [Greek numerals](wikipedia://en/166697). The traditional [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) practice of assigning mystical meaning to words based on their numerical values, and on connections between words of equal value, is known as *gematria*.[^n12]

The [Mandaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeism) number alphasyllabary is also used for numerology ([Mandaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaic_language): *gmaṭ aria*). The *[Book of the Zodiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_the_Zodiac)* is an important [Mandaean text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaean_text) on numerology.[^n13]

::: {#o26 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Pythagorean numerology"}

### Pythagorean method {#Pythagorean_method}

In the Pythagorean method (which uses a kind of place-value for number-letter attributions, as do the ancient Hebrew and Greek systems), the letters of the modern [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet) are assigned numerical values 1 through 9.[^n14]

:::

::: {#o29 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Agrippa code"}

### Agrippan method {#Agrippan_method}

*Image: Agrippan numerology table.jpg.* Agrippa's numerology table as published in *[Three Books of Occult Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Books_of_Occult_Philosophy)*

[Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Cornelius_Agrippa) applied the concept of arithmancy to the [classical Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet#Classical_Latin_alphabet) in the 16th century in *[Three Books of Occult Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Books_of_Occult_Philosophy)*. He mapped the letters as follows (in accordance with the Latin alphabet's place-value at that time):[^n15]

::: {#o31 .table cue=table}

| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H | I |
| 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 70 | 80 | 90 |
| K | L | M | N | O | P | Q | R | S |
| 100 | 200 | 300 | 400 | 500 | 600 | 700 | 800 | 900 |
| T | V | X | Y | Z | I | V | HI | HV |

:::

Note that the letters U, J, and W were not commonly considered part of the [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet) at the time.

:::

### Angel numbers {#Angel_numbers}

So-called **angel numbers**, as defined by [Doreen Virtue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doreen_Virtue) and Lynnette Brown in 2004, are numbers consisting of [repeating digits](wikipedia://en/216551), such as 111 or 444. , a number of popular media publications had published articles suggesting that these numbers have numerological significance.[^n16] After converting to Christianity, Doreen gave an interview with *[The Cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cut_%28publication%29)* in 2024 and renounced the concept of angel numbers saying: "It’s garbage. I regret it, and I'm sorry that I made them."[^n17]

## English systems {#English_systems}

There are various systems of [English Qabalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Qabalah) or numerology.[^n18] These systems interpret the letters of the [Roman script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_script) or [English alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_alphabet) via an assigned set of numerological significances.[^n19][^n20] [English Qaballa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Qaballa), on the other hand, refers specifically to a Qabalah supported by a system discovered by [James Lees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Lees_%28English_magician%29) in 1976.

The first system of English gematria was used by the poet [John Skelton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Skelton_%28poet%29) in 1523 in his poem "The Garland of Laurel".[^n21] The next reference to an English gematria found in the literature was made by Willis F. Whitehead in 1899 in his book *The Mystic Thesaurus*, in which he describes a system he called "English Cabala".[^n22]

In 1952, John P. L. Hughes published *The Hidden Numerical Significance of the English Language, or, Suggestive Gematria*, based on his lecture delivered at Holden Research Circle on July 4, 1952.[^n23] A system related to the [Spiritualist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritualism_%28movement%29) [Agasha Temple of Wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agasha_Temple_of_Wisdom) was described by William Eisen in his two volume *The English Cabalah* (1980–82).[^n24][^n25]

[William G. Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_G._Gray) proposes another system in his 1984 book *Concepts of Qabalah*,[^n26] more recently republished as *Qabalistic Concepts*.[^n27] This system includes correspondence attributions of the English letters to the positions on the [Tree of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_of_Life). [Michael Bertiaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bertiaux) described a system called *Angelic Gematria* in his *The Voudon Gnostic Workbook* (1989).[^o47] David Rankine described a system of English gematria[^n28] using [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) which he calls *Prime Qabalah* in his book *Becoming Magick* (2004).[^n29]

A system known as *Trigrammaton Qabalah* (TQ), was first published by R. Leo Gillis in 1996, including a gematria of the English alphabet based on one of the *[Holy Books of Thelema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Books_of_Thelema)* written by Aleister Crowley in 1907, called *Liber Trigrammaton*. A primary feature of this qabalah is a new understanding of the [Cube of Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_of_Space) and its 26 components of edges, faces, and vertices, which equal the number of letters in the English alphabet.[^n30]

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

In modern culture, numerology continues to influence personal and public life. For example, Australian [MP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_parliament) [Sussan Ley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussan_Ley) credibly altered the spelling of her own name according to numerological advice, believing it would bring excitement to her life, a story widely reported in 2025.[^o52]

Similarly, celebrities like [Gwyneth Paltrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwyneth_Paltrow) have marked entry into a "9-year" numerological cycle signifying transformation and closure, with symbolic gestures such as posting rain-imagery associated with personal renewal.[^o53]

The outbreak of the [Russo-Georgian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Georgian_War) on 8 August 2008 (08.08.08) fed [conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theories) due to the "obviously mystical coincidence of numbers" in the date.[^o54] (Some circles simply refer to that war using the label "the three eights" (три восьмёрки).[^o55])

## Related uses {#Related_uses}

Scientific theories are sometimes labeled "numerology" if their primary inspiration appears to be a set of patterns rather than [scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method) observations. This colloquial use of the term is quite common within the scientific community and it is mostly used to dismiss a theory as questionable science.[^o57]

The best known example of "numerology" in science involves the [coincidental resemblance of certain large numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_large_numbers_hypothesis) that intrigued mathematical physicist [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742), mathematician [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) and astronomer [Arthur Stanley Eddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Stanley_Eddington).[^n31] These numerical coincidences refer to such quantities as the ratio of the age of the universe to the [atomic unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_units) of time, the number of [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron) in the universe, and the difference in strengths between [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity) and the electric force for the electron and [proton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton).[^n32] (See also [Fine-tuned universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-tuned_universe)).

[Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli) was also fascinated by the appearance of certain numbers, including [137](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-structure_constant) (a prime number), in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939).[^n33]

British mathematician [I. J. Good](wikipedia://en/404404) wrote:

There have been a few examples of numerology that have led to theories that transformed society: see the mention of Kirchhoff and Balmer in (Good 1962, 316) \[...\] and one can well include [Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) on account of his [third law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler's_Third_Law). It would be fair enough to say that numerology was the origin of the theories of [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism), [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), gravitation. \[...\] So I intend no disparagement when I describe a formula as numerological.

When a numerological formula is proposed, then we may ask whether it is correct. \[...\] I think an appropriate definition of correctness is that the formula has a good explanation, in a [Platonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonism) sense, that is, the explanation could be based on a good theory that is not yet known but 'exists' in the universe of possible reasonable ideas.[^n34]
