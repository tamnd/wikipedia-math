# Statistical population

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Statistical population"}

Population In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **population** is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of similar items which is of interest for some question or [experiment](wikipedia://en/19621951).[^o2] A statistical population can be a group of existing objects (e.g. the set of all stars within the [Milky Way galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way_galaxy)) or a hypothetical and potentially infinite group of objects conceived as a generalization from experience (e.g. the set of all possible hands in a game of poker).

:::

In [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577), the population is [modelled](wikipedia://en/20590) by a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) with unknown parameters.[^o4] By analyzing a [subset of the population](wikipedia://en/160361), it is then possible to [estimate](wikipedia://en/1565926) the [population parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_parameter) using the appropriate [sample statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_statistics).[^o5]

::: {#o7 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Population mean"}

## Mean {#Mean}

The **population mean** is the arithmetic mean of some numerical property across the entire population. Where the property under consideration is modelled by a random variable, the population mean refers to the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of that random variable.[^o8] Not every probability distribution has a well-defined mean (see the [Cauchy distribution](wikipedia://en/7003) for an example).

The [sample mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_mean) may differ from the population mean, especially for small samples. The [law of large numbers](wikipedia://en/157055) states that the larger the size of the sample, the more likely it is that the sample mean will be close to the population mean.[^o9]

:::
