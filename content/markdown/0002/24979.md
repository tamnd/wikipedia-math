::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="4-polytope"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **4-polytope** (sometimes also called a **polychoron**,[^o5] **polycell**, or **polyhedroid**) is a [four-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-dimensional) [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471).[^o6][^o7] It is a connected and closed figure, composed of lower-dimensional polytopal elements: [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) ([polygons](wikipedia://en/23621)), and [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29) ([polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470)). Each face is shared by exactly two cells. The 4-polytopes were discovered by the Swiss mathematician [Ludwig Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408) before 1853.[^n1]

:::

The two-dimensional analogue of a 4-polytope is a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621), and the three-dimensional analogue is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470).

Topologically 4-polytopes are closely related to the [uniform honeycombs](wikipedia://en/2961), such as the [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318), which tessellate 3-space; similarly the 3D [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) is related to the infinite 2D [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809). Convex 4-polytopes can be *cut and unfolded* as [nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_net) in 3-space.

::: {#o10 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A 4-polytope is a closed [four-dimensional](wikipedia://en/1364622) figure. It comprises [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) (corner points), [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) and [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29). A cell is the three-dimensional analogue of a face, and is therefore a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470). Each face must join exactly two cells, analogous to the way in which each edge of a polyhedron joins just two faces. Like any [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471), the elements of a 4-polytope cannot be subdivided into two or more sets which are also 4-polytopes, i.e. it is not a compound.

:::

## Geometry {#Geometry}

The convex [regular 4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_4-polytopes) are the four-dimensional analogues of the [Platonic solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_solids). The most familiar 4-polytope is the [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112) or hypercube, the 4D analogue of the cube.

The convex regular 4-polytopes can be ordered by size as a measure of 4-dimensional content (hypervolume) for the same radius. Each greater polytope in the sequence is *rounder* than its predecessor, enclosing more content[^n2] within the same radius. The 4-simplex (5-cell) is the limit smallest case, and the 120-cell is the largest. Complexity (as measured by comparing [configuration matrices](wikipedia://en/18801624#As_configurations) or simply the number of vertices) follows the same ordering.

4-polytopes cannot be seen in three-dimensional space due to their extra dimension. Several techniques are used to help visualise them.

**Orthogonal projection**

[Orthogonal projections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) can be used to show various symmetry orientations of a 4-polytope. They can be drawn in 2D as vertex-edge graphs, and can be shown in 3D with solid faces as visible [projective envelopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_envelope).

**Perspective projection**

Just as a 3D shape can be projected onto a flat sheet, so a 4-D shape can be projected onto 3-space or even onto a flat sheet. One common projection is a [Schlegel diagram](wikipedia://en/7201595) which uses [stereographic projection](wikipedia://en/143431) of points on the surface of a [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) into three dimensions, connected by straight edges, faces, and cells drawn in 3-space.

**Sectioning**

Just as a slice through a polyhedron reveals a cut surface, so a slice through a 4-polytope reveals a cut "hypersurface" in three dimensions. A sequence of such sections can be used to build up an understanding of the overall shape. The extra dimension can be equated with time to produce a smooth animation of these cross sections.

**Nets**

A [net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_%28polytope%29) of a 4-polytope is composed of polyhedral [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) that are connected by their faces and all occupy the same three-dimensional space, just as the polygon faces of a [net of a polyhedron](wikipedia://en/1148129) are connected by their edges and all occupy the same plane.

## Topological characteristics {#Topological_characteristics}

*Image: Brückner Achtzelle 2.jpg.* 4-polytopes with 8 cells by [Max Brückner](wikipedia://en/48703370) (1909), including a [Schlegel diagram](wikipedia://en/7201595) of the [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112).

The topology of any given 4-polytope is defined by its [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) and [torsion coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_coefficient_%28topology%29).[^o16]

The value of the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) used to characterise polyhedra does not generalize usefully to higher dimensions, and is zero for all 4-polytopes, whatever their underlying topology. This inadequacy of the Euler characteristic to reliably distinguish between different topologies in higher dimensions led to the discovery of the more sophisticated Betti numbers.[^o16]

Similarly, the notion of orientability of a polyhedron is insufficient to characterise the surface twistings of toroidal 4-polytopes, and this led to the use of torsion coefficients.[^o16]

## Classification {#Classification}

### Criteria {#Criteria}

Like all polytopes, 4-polytopes may be classified based on properties like "[convexity](wikipedia://en/6292)" and "[symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741)".

- A 4-polytope is *[convex](wikipedia://en/1817885)* if its boundary (including its cells, faces and edges) does not intersect itself and the line segment joining any two points of the 4-polytope is contained in the 4-polytope or its interior; otherwise, it is *non-convex*. Self-intersecting 4-polytopes are also known as [star 4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_4-polytope), from analogy with the star-like shapes of the non-convex [star polygons](wikipedia://en/313303) and [Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler–Poinsot_polyhedra).
- A 4-polytope is *[regular](wikipedia://en/518693)* if it is [transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_group_action) on its [flags](wikipedia://en/9352497). This means that its cells are all [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [regular polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedra), and similarly its [vertex figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_figures) are congruent and of another kind of regular polyhedron.
- A convex 4-polytope is *[semi-regular](wikipedia://en/11684875)* if it has a [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) under which all vertices are equivalent ([vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive)) and its cells are [regular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/333299). The cells may be of two or more kinds, provided that they have the same kind of face. There are only 3 cases identified by [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) in 1900: the [rectified 5-cell](wikipedia://en/2720430), [rectified 600-cell](wikipedia://en/2720121), and [snub 24-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756).
- A 4-polytope is *[uniform](wikipedia://en/6842392)* if it has a [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) under which all vertices are equivalent, and its cells are [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223). The faces of a uniform 4-polytope must be [regular](wikipedia://en/333306).
- A 4-polytope is *[scaliform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaliform_polytope)* if it is vertex-transitive, and has all equal length edges. This allows cells which are not uniform, such as the regular-faced convex [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034).
- A regular 4-polytope which is also [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885) is said to be a [convex regular 4-polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_regular_4-polytope).
- A 4-polytope is *prismatic* if it is the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two or more lower-dimensional polytopes. A prismatic 4-polytope is uniform if its factors are uniform. The [hypercube](wikipedia://en/31112) is prismatic (product of two [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29), or of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) and [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860)), but is considered separately because it has symmetries other than those inherited from its factors.
- A *[tiling](wikipedia://en/321671) or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654) of 3-space* is the division of three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) into a repetitive [grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_%28spatial_index%29) of polyhedral cells. Such tilings or tessellations are infinite and do not bound a "4D" volume, and are examples of infinite 4-polytopes. A *uniform tiling of 3-space* is one whose vertices are congruent and related by a [space group](wikipedia://en/463721) and whose cells are [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223).

### Classes {#Classes}

The following lists the various categories of 4-polytopes classified according to the criteria above:

*Image: Schlegel half-solid truncated 120-cell.png.* The [truncated 120-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_120-cell) is one of 47 convex non-prismatic uniform 4-polytopes

**[Uniform 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/1645899)** ([vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive)):

- **Convex uniform 4-polytopes** (64, plus two infinite families)
  - 47 non-prismatic [convex uniform 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/1645899#Convex_uniform_4-polytope) including:
    - 6 [Convex regular 4-polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_regular_4-polytope)
  - [Prismatic uniform 4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/1645899#Prismatic_uniform_4-polytope):
    - {} × {p,q} : 18 [polyhedral hyperprisms](wikipedia://en/1645899#Polyhedral_hyperprisms) (including cubic hyperprism, the regular [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783))
    - Prisms built on antiprisms (infinite family)
    - {p} × {q} : [duoprisms](wikipedia://en/2114155) (infinite family)
- **Non-convex uniform 4-polytopes** (10 + unknown) *Image: Ortho solid 016-uniform polychoron p33-t0.png.* The [great grand stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604513) is the largest of 10 regular star 4-polytopes, having 600 vertices.
  - 10 (regular) [Schläfli-Hess polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schläfli-Hess_polytope)
  - 57 hyperprisms built on [nonconvex uniform polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex_uniform_polyhedron)
  - Unknown total number of nonconvex uniform 4-polytopes: [Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527) and other collaborators have identified 2191 forms (convex and star, excluding the infinite families), all constructed by [vertex figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_figures) by [Stella4D software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_%28software%29).[^o20]

**Other convex 4-polytopes**:

- [Polyhedral pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_pyramid)
- [Polyhedral bipyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_bipyramid)
- [Polyhedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_prism)

*Image: Cubic honeycomb.png.* The regular [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318) is the only infinite regular 4-polytope in Euclidean 3-dimensional space.

**Infinite uniform 4-polytopes of [Euclidean 3-space](wikipedia://en/9697)** (uniform tessellations of convex uniform cells)

- 28 [convex uniform honeycombs](wikipedia://en/2961): uniform convex polyhedral tessellations, including:
  - 1 regular tessellation, [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318): {4,3,4}

**Infinite uniform 4-polytopes of [hyperbolic 3-space](wikipedia://en/318742)** (uniform tessellations of convex uniform cells)

- 76 Wythoffian [convex uniform honeycombs in hyperbolic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_uniform_honeycombs_in_hyperbolic_space), including:
  - [4 regular tessellation of compact hyperbolic 3-space](wikipedia://en/505253#Tessellations_of_hyperbolic_3-space): {3,5,3}, {4,3,5}, {5,3,4}, {5,3,5}

**Dual [uniform 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/1645899)** ([cell-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell-transitive)):

- 41 unique dual convex uniform 4-polytopes
- 17 unique dual convex uniform polyhedral prisms
- infinite family of dual convex uniform duoprisms (irregular tetrahedral cells)
- 27 unique convex dual uniform honeycombs, including:
  - [Rhombic dodecahedral honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombic_dodecahedral_honeycomb)
  - [Disphenoid tetrahedral honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disphenoid_tetrahedral_honeycomb)

**Others:**

- [Weaire–Phelan structure](wikipedia://en/2795211) periodic space-filling honeycomb with irregular cells

*Image: Hemi-icosahedron coloured.svg.* The [11-cell](wikipedia://en/948617) is an abstract regular 4-polytope, existing in the [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325), it can be seen by presenting its 11 hemi-icosahedral vertices and cells by index and color.

**[Abstract regular 4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/1211056)**:

- [11-cell](wikipedia://en/948617)
- [57-cell](wikipedia://en/948622)

These categories include only the 4-polytopes that exhibit a high degree of symmetry. Many other 4-polytopes are possible, but they have not been studied as extensively as the ones included in these categories.
