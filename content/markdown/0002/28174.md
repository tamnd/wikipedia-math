# Data storage

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Data storage"}

**Data storage** is the recording (storing) of [information](wikipedia://en/18985062) ([data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data)) in a **storage medium**. [Handwriting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handwriting), [phonographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonograph_record) recording, [magnetic tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_tape), and [optical discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_disc) are all examples of storage media. Biological molecules such as [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA) and [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) are considered by some as data storage.[^o4] Recording may be accomplished with virtually any form of [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy). Electronic data storage requires electrical power to store and retrieve data.

:::

Data stored in a digital, machine-readable medium is called *digital data*. Computer data storage is one of the core functions of a [general-purpose computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General-purpose_computer). [Electronic documents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_document) can be stored in much less space than paper [documents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Document).[^o5] [Barcodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcode) and [magnetic ink character recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_ink_character_recognition) (MICR) are two ways of recording machine-readable data on paper.

::: {#o7 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Record medium"}

## Recording media {#Recording_media}

A recording medium is physical material that holds information. Newly created information is distributed and can be stored in four storage media–print, film, magnetic, and optical–and seen or heard in four information flows–telephone, radio, TV, and the Internet[^o8] as well as being observed directly. Digital information is stored on [electronic media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_media) in many different [recording formats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_format).

With [electronic media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_media), the data and the recording media are sometimes referred to as "software" despite the more common use of the word to describe [computer software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_software). With ([traditional art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_%28arts%29)) static media, [art materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_materials) such as [crayons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crayon) may be considered both equipment and medium as the wax, charcoal or chalk material from the equipment becomes part of the surface of the medium.

Some recording media may be temporary, either by design or by nature. [Volatile organic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatile_organic_compound) may be used to purposely make data expire over time or to reduce environmental impact. Data such as [smoke signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_signal) or [skywriting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skywriting) are temporary by nature. Depending on the volatility, a [gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas) (e.g., [atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere), [smoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke)) or a liquid surface such as a lake would be considered a temporary recording medium, if it could be considered a recording medium at all.

## Global capacity, digitization, and trends {#Global_capacity,_digitization,_and_trends}

A 2003 [UC Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Berkeley) report estimated that about five [exabytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exabyte) of new information were produced in 2002 and that 92% of this data was stored on magnetic media (primarily hard disk drives). This was about twice the data produced in 1999.[^o11] The amount of data transmitted over [telecommunications systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_system) in 2002 was nearly 18 exabytes—three and a half times more than was recorded on non-volatile storage. Telephone calls constituted 98% of the telecommunicated information in 2002. The researchers' highest estimate for the growth rate of newly stored information (uncompressed) was more than 30% per year.

In a more limited study, the [International Data Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Data_Corporation) estimated that the total amount of digital data in 2007 was 281 exabytes and that the total amount of digital data produced exceeded the global storage capacity for the first time.[^o12]

A 2011 article in *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_magazine)* estimated that the year 2002 was the beginning of the digital age for information storage: an age in which more information is stored on digital storage devices than on analog storage devices.[^o13] In 1986, approximately 1% of the world's capacity to store information was in digital format; this grew to 3% by 1993, to 25% by 2000, and to 94% by 2007. These figures correspond to less than three compressed [exabytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exabytes) in 1986, and 295 [compressed](wikipedia://en/8013) exabytes in 2007.[^o13] The quantity of digital storage doubled roughly every three to four years.[^o14]

, an increase of 60x from 2010, and that it will increase to 181 zettabytes generated in 2025.[^o15]
