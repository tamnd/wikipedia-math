# Demographics of Russia

*Image: Russia Population Density Map 2021.png.* Map of Russia's population density at municipality level, based on the results of the 2021 census.

::: {#o11 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Russia"}

[Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) has an estimated population of 146.0 million as of 1 January 2025,[^o2] down from 147.2 million recorded in the [2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census).[^o12] It is the [most populous country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_countries_by_population) in Europe, and the [ninth-most populous country](wikipedia://en/69058) in the world. Russia has a [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_density) of \8.5 /km2,[^o13] with its overall [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_life_expectancy) being 73 years (68 years for males and 79 years for females) .[^o14][^o15][^o16] The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) across Russia was estimated to be 1.37 children born per woman ,[^o17] which is in line with the [European average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_total_fertility_rate)[^o18] but below the [replacement rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replacement_rate) of 2.1.

:::

By the end of 2024, the natural decline of the Russian population amounted to 596.2 thousand people, according to published data from Rosstat. Compared to the end of 2023, the indicator increased by 20.4% (from 495.3 thousand).[^o19] However, this decline is offset by massive [immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Russia), particularly from Central Asia.

From 1992 to 2012, and again since 2016, Russia's [death rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_rate) has exceeded its [birth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_rate), which has been called a [demographic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_crisis_of_Russia) by analysts.[^o20] In 2009, Russia recorded annual population growth for the first time in fifteen years; during the mid-2010s, Russia had seen increased population growth due to declining [death rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_rates), increased [birth rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_rates) and increased immigration.[^o21] Between 2020 and 2021, Russia's population had undergone its largest peacetime decline in recorded history, due to excess deaths from the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Russia).[^o22] The [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) and subsequent [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukrainian_war_%282022–present%29) further deepened the crisis with increased emigration and military fatalities.

Russia is a [multinational state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_state),[^o140] home to over [193 ethnic groups nationwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Russia). In the 2021 Census, nearly 72% of the population were ethnic [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians) and approximately 19% of the population were ethnic minorities.[^o23] According to the United Nations, Russia's [immigrant population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Russia) is the world's third largest, numbering over 11.6 million; most of whom are from other [post-Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states).[^o155]

### Demographic crisis {#Demographic_crisis}

*Image: Deserted village at Sukhona River.jpg.* Thousands of abandoned villages are scattered across Russia.[^o28]

After having peaked at 148,689,000 in 1991, the population then decreased, falling to 142,737,196 by 2008.[^o29] Russia has become increasingly reliant on immigration to maintain its population; 2021 had the highest net immigration since 1994,[^o30] despite which there was a small overall decline from 146.1 million to 145.4 million in 2021, the largest decline in over a decade.[^o31]

The [natural population](wikipedia://en/9888599) had declined by 997,000 between October 2020 and September 2021 (the difference between the number of births and the number of deaths over a period).[^o32] The natural death rate in January 2020, 2021, and 2022 have each been nearly double the natural birth rate.[^o33]

Following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in 2022, the demographic crisis in the country has deepened,[^o34] as the country has suffered high [military fatalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casualties_of_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) while facing renewed [human capital flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_capital_flight) and [brain drain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_drain) caused by Western mass-sanctions and boycotts.[^o35] The number of Russian men which died due to the [Russo-Ukrainian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Ukrainian_war) has been censored by the [Russian 2022 war censorship laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_2022_war_censorship_laws).[^o36] In 2022, tens of thousands of tech workers left Russia.[^o37][^o38] In 2024, the website of the science journal [*Science*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_%28journal%29) indicated that Russia has seen a multi-year brain drain in the science profession and that salaries are decreasing in the Russian scientific community.[^o39] Many commentators predict that the situation will be worse than during the 1990s.[^o40] Although, a large part of the emigrants have returned home to Russia in a continuing process.[^o41]

In March 2023, *The Economist* reported that "Over the past three years the country has lost around 2 million more people than it would ordinarily have done, as a result of war \[in Ukraine\], [disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Russia) and [exodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_emigration_following_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine)."[^o42]

The UN is projecting that the decline that started in 2021 will continue, and if current demographic conditions persist, Russia's population will be 120 million in 50 years, a decline of about 17%.[^o43][^o42] In January 2024, the Russian statistics agency [Rosstat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_State_Statistics_Service_%28Russia%29) predicted that Russia's population could drop to 130 million by 2046.[^o44]

### Fertility {#Fertility}

*Image: Russia animated population pyramid.gif.* Population age pyramid of Russia from 1946 to 2023

*Image: Natural Population Growth of Russia.PNG.* Birth and death rates and natural growth, 1950–2014

*Image: Total fertility rate in Russia.png.* Total fertility rate in Russia, 1990—2024[^o46]

Between 1993 and 2008 there was a great [decrease in the country's population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_decline) from 148 to 143 million.[^o47] There was a huge 50% decrease in the number of births per year from 2.5 million in 1987 to 1.2 million since 1997, but the current 1.37 fertility rate is still higher than that of the 1990s.[^o17]

At the beginning of 2022, 320,400 babies were born between January and March, 16,600 fewer than January–March 2021. There were nearly twice as many deaths (584,700) as births.[^o47] The crude birth rate – 8.9 per 100,000 inhabitants – was the lowest since the year 2000.[^o47]

Russia has a low fertility rate with 1.37 children per woman in 2025, below 2.1 children per woman, which must be the number reached to maintain its population.[^o17][^o47] As a result of their low fertility for decades, the Russian population is one of the oldest in the world with an average of 40.3 years.[^o47] In February 2026, the [Russian Ministry of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_%28Russia%29) approved new guidelines recommending that women indicating that they do not want to have children are referred to psychotherapy to "foster a positive attitude toward motherhood". Speaking with [France24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_24) under only their first names, several Russian women criticized the plan.[^o48][^o49][^o50]

### Historical fertility rates {#Historical_fertility_rates}

*Image: Fertility rate of Russia from 1843 to 2016.svg.* TFR of Russia from 1843 to 2016

The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) is the number of children born to each woman. It is based on fairly good data for the entire period. Sources: [Our World In Data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_World_In_Data) and [Gapminder Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gapminder_Foundation).[^o52]

In many of the years from 1843 to 1917, Russia had the highest total fertility rate in the world.[^o52] These elevated fertility rates did not lead to population growth due to high mortality rate, the casualties of the Russian Revolution, the two world wars and to a lesser extent the political killings.[^o53] From 1840 to 1910 Russia's total fertility rate remained consistently elevated between 6.7 and 7.4 children per woman. ## Immigration {#Immigration}

In 2006, in a bid to compensate for the country's demographic decline, the Russian government started simplifying immigration laws.[^o99] New citizenship rules introduced in April 2014 allowing eligible citizens from former Soviet republics to obtain Russian citizenship, have gained strong interest among Russian-speaking residents of those countries (i.e. [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians), [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans), [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians) and [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians)).[^o100][^o101]

There are an estimated four million undocumented immigrants from the [ex-Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union) states in Russia.[^o102] In 2012, the [Russian Federal Security Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federal_Security_Service)'s Border Service stated there had been an increase in undocumented migration from the Middle East and Southeast Asia (Note that these were Temporary Contract Migrants)[^o103] Under legal changes made in 2012, undocumented immigrants who are caught will be banned from reentering the country for 10 years.[^o104][^o105]

Since the collapse of the USSR, most immigrants have come from [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine), [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan), [Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistan), [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan), [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia), [Kyrgyzstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyzstan), [Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova), [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan), [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmenistan), [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus), from poor areas of China, and from [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam) and [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos).[^o106]

### Worker migration {#Worker_migration}

Temporary [migrant workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migrant_workers_in_Russia) in Russia consists of about 7 million people. Most of the temporary workers come from Central Asia (mostly from [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan), [Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistan) and [Kyrgyzstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyzstan)), the [South Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Caucasus) (mostly from [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia) and [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan)), and East Asia (mostly from poor areas of China, from [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam) and [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos)). Most of them work in the construction, cleaning and in the household industries. They primarily live in cities such as Moscow and [Moscow Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Oblast), [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg), [Yekaterinburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinburg), [Novosibirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk), [Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan), [Nijniy Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijniy_Novgorod), [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok), [Samara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samara), [Krasnodar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnodar), [Ufa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ufa), [Sochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochi), [Khabarovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabarovsk), [Krasnoyarsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnoyarsk), [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk), [Rostov on Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostov_on_Don), [Volgograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgograd), [Omsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omsk), [Tyumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyumen), [Voronezh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh), [Perm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm,_Russia) and others. The mayor of Moscow said that Moscow cannot do without worker migrants. New laws are in place that require worker migrants to be fluent in Russian, know Russian history and laws.

## Emigration {#Emigration}

The [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) has led to considerable [emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_emigration_following_the_invasion_of_Ukraine_%282022–present%29), with over 300,000 [Russian citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_citizen) and residents estimated to have left Russia by mid-March 2022, at least 500,000 by the end of August 2022,[^o109] and an additional 400,000 by early October. The total number of [political refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_asylum), [economic migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_migrant), and [conscientious objectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objector)[^o110][^o111][^o112][^o113][^o114] is thought to be more than 900,000. In addition to evading criminal prosecution for opposing the invasion, and fear of being conscripted after President [Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Putin)'s 21 September 2022 announcement of [partial mobilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_mobilization), those fleeing voiced reasons such as disagreement with the war, the uselessness and cruelty of the war, sympathy for Ukraine, disagreement with the political roots of the war with Ukraine, the rejection of killing, and an assessment that Russia is no longer the place for their family.[^o115]

## Occupied and annexed regions {#Occupied_and_annexed_regions}

Russia has encouraged or even forced people in occupied or annexed regions to become Russian citizens, a procedure known as [passportization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passportization). This includes the Donetsk, Kherson, Luhansk and Zaporizhzhia oblasts and the Autonomous Republic of Crimea of Ukraine,[^o117] as well as South Ossetia and Abkhazia in Georgia.[^o118]

## Health {#Health}

*Image: Metallurg Sochi.jpg.* Metallurg, a Soviet-era [sanatorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanatorium) in [Sochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochi)[^o121]

Russia's constitution guarantees free, [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care) for all Russian citizens, through a compulsory state health insurance program.[^o122] The [Ministry of Health of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_%28Russia%29) oversees the Russian public healthcare system, and the sector employs more than two million people. Federal regions also have their own departments of health that oversee local administration. A separate private health insurance plan is needed to access private healthcare in Russia.[^o123]

Russia spent 5.32% of its GDP on healthcare in 2018.[^o124] Its healthcare expenditure is notably lower than other developed nations.[^o125] Russia has one of the world's most female-biased [sex ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sex_ratio), with 0.859 males to every female with more older females than males prevailing,[^o126] due to its high male [mortality rate](wikipedia://en/191305) in later age.[^o127] In 2019, the overall [life expectancy in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_life_expectancy) at birth was 73.2 years (68.2 years for males and 78.0 years for females),[^o128] and it had a very low [infant mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality) (5 per 1,000 [live births](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_birth_%28human%29)).[^o129]

The principal causes of death in Russia are cardiovascular diseases.[^o130] [Obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity) is a prevalent health issue in Russia; 61.1% of Russian adults were overweight or obese in 2016.[^o131] However, Russia's historically high [alcohol consumption rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_consumption_in_Russia) is the biggest health issue in the country,[^o132][^o133] as it remains [one of the world's highest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_alcohol_consumption_per_capita), despite a stark decrease in the last decade.[^o134] [Smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_consumption_by_country) is another health issue in the country.[^o135] The country's [high suicide rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_suicide_rate), although [on the decline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_in_Russia),[^o136] remains a significant social issue.[^o137]

### COVID-19 pandemic {#COVID-19_pandemic}

Russia had one of the [highest number of confirmed cases in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_by_country_and_territory). Analysis of excess deaths from official government demographic statistics, based on births and deaths and excluding migration, showed that Russia had its biggest ever annual population drop in peacetime, with the population declining by 997,000 between October 2020 and September 2021, which demographer [Alexei Raksha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Raksha) interpreted as being primarily due to the COVID-19 pandemic.[^o32]

## Ethnic groups {#Ethnic_groups}

Russia is a [multinational state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_state), with many subnational entities associated with different minorities.[^o140] There are over [193 ethnic groups nationwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Russia). In the 2021 census, nearly 71.73% of the population identified as ethnic [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians) (among those stating their ethnicity), and while approximately 19% of the total population identified with various ethnic minority groups.[^o141][^o142] The percentage of total Russian population that did not publicly indicate any ethnic identity in the census increased from 3.94% in 2010 to 11.27% in 2021.[^o143]

According to the [2021 Russian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census), the number of ethnic Russians decreased by nearly 5.43 million, from roughly 111 million people in 2010 to approximately 105.5 million in 2021.[^o144] In 2010, four-fifths of Russia's population originated from West of the [Ural Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Mountains) — of which the vast majority were [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs),[^o145] with a substantial minority of [Finno-Ugric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finno-Ugric_peoples) and [Germanic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples).[^o146][^o147] [Turkic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) form a large minority, and are spread around pockets across the vast nation.[^o148] Various distinct ethnic groups [also inhabit the North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoples_of_the_Caucasus).[^o149] Other minorities include [Mongolian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) ([Buryats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryats) and [Kalmyks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmyks)),[^o150][^o151] the [Indigenous peoples of Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Siberia),[^o152] a [historical Jewish population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Russia),[^o153] and the [Koryo-saram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryo-saram) (including [Sakhalin Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin_Koreans)).[^o154]

According to the United Nations, Russia's [immigrant population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Russia) was the [third-largest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_and_dependent_territories_by_immigrant_population) as recently as 2016, numbering over 11.6 million;[^o155] most of which are from [post-Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states), mainly from Central Asia.[^o156] Due to a decline in immigrant population and increases in other countries, Russia has the tenth largest immigrant population with 7.6 million as of 2024.[^o157] There are [22 republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_Russia) in Russia, who have their own ethnicities, cultures, and languages. ## Languages {#Languages}

Russian is the [official](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) and the predominantly spoken language in Russia. It is the most spoken [native language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) in Europe, the most geographically widespread language of Eurasia, as well as the world's most widely spoken [Slavic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages).[^o163] Russian is the fifth-most used language on the Internet,[^o161] and is one of two official languages aboard the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station),[^o162] as well as one of the six [official languages of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_languages_of_the_United_Nations).[^o163]

Russia is a [multilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingualism#Europe) nation; approximately 100–150 minority languages are spoken across the country.[^o164][^o165] According to the [Russian Census of 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Census_%282002%29), 142.6 million across the country spoke Russian, 5.3 million spoke [Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatar_language), and 1.8 million spoke [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language).[^o166] The constitution allows the country's individual republics the right to [establish their own state languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_official_languages_in_Russia) in addition to Russian, as well as guarantee its citizens the right to preserve their native language and to create conditions for its study and development.[^o167] However, various experts have claimed Russia's linguistic diversity is rapidly declining.[^o168][^o169]

## Religion {#Religion}

*Image: Кафедральный собор Благовещения Пресвятой Богородицы.jpg.* [Annunciation Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annunciation_Cathedral,_Voronezh) in [Voronezh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh)

Russia is a [secular state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular_state) by constitution, and its largest religion is [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Russia). It has the world's [largest Orthodox population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy_by_country).[^o173][^o174] As of different sociological surveys on religious adherence, between 41% and over 80% of the total population of Russia adhere to the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church).[^o175][^o176][^o177] Other branches of Christianity present in Russia include [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Russia) (approx. 1%), [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists), [Pentecostals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostals), [Lutherans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheranism) and other Protestant churches (together totalling about 0.5% of the population) and [Old Believers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Believers).[^o178][^o179] There is some presence of Judaism, [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism), and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism); pagan beliefs are also present to some extent in remote areas, sometimes [syncretized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretism) with one of the mainstream religions.

In 2017, a survey made by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) showed that 73% of Russians declared themselves as Christians—out of which 71% were [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox), 1% were [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic), and 2% were Other Christians, while 15% were [unaffiliated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion), 10% were [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims), and 1% followed other religions.[^o180] According to various reports, the proportion of [Atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism) in Russia is between 16% and 48% of the population.[^o181]

[Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Russia) is the second-largest religion in Russia, and it is the traditional religion among most [peoples of the North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoples_of_the_Caucasus), and among some [Turkic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) scattered along the [Volga-Ural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idel-Ural) region.[^o182] [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Russia) have a sizable population in three Siberian republics: [Buryatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryatia), [Tuva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuva), and [Zabaykalsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabaykalsky_Krai), and in [Kalmykia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmykia), the only region in Europe where Buddhism is the most practised religion.[^o183]

## Education {#Education}

*Image: МГУ, вид с воздуха.jpg.* [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), the most prestigious educational institution in Russia.[^o185]

Russia has an adult [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) of 100%.[^o186] It grants [free education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_education) to its citizens under its constitution.[^o187] The [Ministry of Education of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_%28Russia%29) is responsible for primary and secondary education, as well as vocational education; while the [Ministry of Education and Science of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Science_and_Higher_Education_%28Russia%29) is responsible for science and higher education.[^o188] Regional authorities regulate education within their jurisdictions within the prevailing framework of federal laws. Russia is among the world's most educated countries, and has the [third-highest proportion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_tertiary_education_attainment) of [tertiary-level graduates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education) in terms of percentage of population, at 62%.[^o189] It spent roughly 4.7% of its GDP on education in 2018.[^o190]

Russia has [compulsory education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education) for a duration of 11 years, exclusively for children aged 7 to 17–18.[^o188] Its [pre-school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-school) education system is highly developed and optional,[^o191] some four-fifths of children aged 3 to 6 attend day nurseries or kindergartens. Primary school is compulsory for 11 years, starting from age 6 to 7, and leads to a basic general education certificate.[^o188] An additional two or three years of schooling are required for the secondary-level certificate, and some seven-eighths of Russians continue their education past this level. Admission to an institute of higher education is selective and highly competitive:[^o187] first-degree courses usually take five years.[^o192] The oldest and largest [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_institutions_of_higher_education_in_Russia) in Russia are [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) and [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University).[^o193] There are ten highly prestigious federal universities across the country. Russia was the world's fifth-leading destination for [international students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_student) in 2019, hosting roughly 300,000.[^o194]

## Urbanized areas {#Urbanized_areas}

Russia is one of the world's [most urbanized countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization_by_country), with roughly 75% of its total population living in urban areas.[^o126] Moscow, the capital and largest city, has a population estimated at 12.4 million residents within the city limits,[^o196] while over 17 million residents in the urban area,[^o197] and over 20 million residents in the [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_metropolitan_area).[^o198] Moscow is among the [world's largest cities](wikipedia://en/14649921), being the [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_cities_by_population_within_city_limits) entirely within Europe, the [most populous urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_Europe) in Europe,[^o197] the [most populous metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_Europe) in Europe,[^o198] and also the largest city by land area on the European continent.[^o199] [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg), the cultural capital, is the second-largest city, with a population of roughly 5.4 million inhabitants.[^o200] Other major urban areas are [Yekaterinburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinburg), [Novosibirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk), [Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan), [Nizhny Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizhny_Novgorod), and [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk).
