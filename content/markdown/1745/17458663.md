# Nullity (graph theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nullity"}

The **nullity** of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) in the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) subject of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) can mean either of two unrelated numbers. If the graph has *n* vertices and *m* edges, then:

:::

- In the [matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_theory) of graphs, the nullity of the graph is the nullity of the [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) *A* of the graph. The nullity of *A* is given by *n* − *r* where *r* is the [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) of the adjacency matrix. This nullity equals the multiplicity of the [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) 0 in the spectrum of the adjacency matrix. See Cvetkovič and Gutman (1972), Cheng and Liu (2007), and Gutman and Borovićanin (2011).
- In the [matroid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid_theory) the nullity of the graph is the nullity of the oriented [incidence matrix](wikipedia://en/420919) *M* associated with the graph. The nullity of *M* is given by *m* − *n* + *c*, where, *c* is the number of components of the graph and *n* − *c* is the [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) of the oriented incidence matrix. This name is rarely used; the number is more commonly known as the **cycle rank**, **cyclomatic number**, or **[circuit rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_rank)** of the graph. It is equal to the rank of the co[graphic matroid](wikipedia://en/745714) of the graph. It also equals the nullity of the [Laplacian matrix](wikipedia://en/1448472) of the graph, defined as *L* = *D − A*, where *D* is the diagonal matrix of vertex degrees; the Laplacian nullity equals the cycle rank because *L* = *M* *M*<sup>T</sup> (*M* times its own transpose).
