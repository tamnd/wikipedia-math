# Rank (graph theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rank"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a branch of mathematics, the **rank** of an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) has two unrelated definitions. Let $n$ equal the number of [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) of the graph.

:::

- In the [matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_theory) of graphs the rank $r$ of an undirected graph is defined as the [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) of its [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463).

Analogously, the [nullity](wikipedia://en/17458663) of the graph is the nullity of its adjacency matrix, which equals $n - r$.

- In the [matroid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid_theory) of graphs the rank of an undirected graph is defined as the number $n - c$, where $c$ is the number of [connected components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28graph_theory%29) of the graph. Equivalently, the rank of a graph is the [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) of the oriented [incidence matrix](wikipedia://en/420919) associated with the graph.[^o2]

Analogously, the [nullity](wikipedia://en/17458663) of the graph is the [nullity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_%28matrix%29) of its oriented incidence matrix, given by the formula $m - n + c$, where *n* and *c* are as above and *m* is the number of edges in the graph. The nullity is equal to the first [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) of the graph. The sum of the rank and the nullity is the number of edges.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}
