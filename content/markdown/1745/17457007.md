# Valuation (measure theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Valuation"}

In [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), or at least in the approach to it via the [domain theory](wikipedia://en/325077), a **valuation** is a [map](wikipedia://en/516931) from the class of [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358) of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) to the set of [positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_number) [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) including [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824), with certain properties. It is a concept closely related to that of a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873), and as such, it finds applications in measure theory, [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), and [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392).

:::

## Domain/Measure theory definition {#Domain/Measure_theory_definition}

Let $\scriptstyle (X,\mathcal{T})$ be a topological space: a **valuation** is any [set function](wikipedia://en/25911217)

$$v : \mathcal{T} \to \mathbb{R}^+ \cup \{+\infty\}$$

satisfying the following three properties

$$\begin{array}{lll} v(\varnothing) = 0 & & \scriptstyle{\text{Strictness property}}\\ v(U)\leq v(V) & \mbox{if}~U\subseteq V\quad U,V\in\mathcal{T} & \scriptstyle{\text{Monotonicity property}}\\ v(U\cup V)+ v(U\cap V) = v(U)+v(V) & \forall U,V\in\mathcal{T} & \scriptstyle{\text{Modularity property}}\, \end{array}$$

The definition immediately shows the relationship between a valuation and a measure: the properties of the two mathematical object are often very similar if not identical, the only difference being that the domain of a measure is the [Borel algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_algebra) of the given topological space, while the domain of a valuation is the class of open sets. Further details and references can be found in (Alvarez-Manilla Edalat Saheb-Djahromi 2000) and (Goubault-Larrecq 2005).

### Continuous valuation {#Continuous_valuation}

A valuation (as defined in domain theory/measure theory) is said to be **continuous** if for *every directed family* $\scriptstyle \{U_i\}_{i\in I}$ *of [open sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_sets)* (i.e. an [indexed family](wikipedia://en/23624339) of open sets which is also [directed](wikipedia://en/9165) in the sense that for each pair of indexes $i$ and $j$ belonging to the [index set](wikipedia://en/393055) $I$, there exists an index $k$ such that $\scriptstyle U_i\subseteq U_k$ and $\scriptstyle U_j\subseteq U_k$) the following [equality](wikipedia://en/90446) holds:

$$v\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}U_i\right) = \sup_{i\in I} v(U_i).$$

This property is analogous to the [τ-additivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Τ-additivity) of measures.

### Simple valuation {#Simple_valuation}

A valuation (as defined in domain theory/measure theory) is said to be **simple** if it is a [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) with [non-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative_number) [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) of [Dirac valuations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caluation_%28measure_theory%29#Dirac_valuation), that is,

$$v(U)=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\delta_{x_i}(U)\quad\forall U\in\mathcal{T}$$

where $a_i$ is nonnegative for all $i$. Simple valuations are obviously continuous in the above sense. The [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) of a *directed family of simple valuations* (i.e. an indexed family of simple valuations which is also directed in the sense that for each pair of indexes $i$ and $j$ belonging to the index set $I$, there exists an index $k$ such that $\scriptstyle v_i(U)\leq v_k(U)\!$ and $\scriptstyle v_j(U)\leq v_k(U)\!$) is called **quasi-simple valuation**

$$\bar{v}(U) = \sup_{i\in I}v_i(U) \quad \forall U\in \mathcal{T}.\,$$

### See also {#See_also}

- The **extension problem** for a given valuation (in the sense of domain theory/measure theory) consists in finding under what type of conditions it can be extended to a measure on a proper topological space, which may or may not be the same space where it is defined: the papers (Alvarez-Manilla Edalat Saheb-Djahromi 2000) and (Goubault-Larrecq 2005) in the reference section are devoted to this aim and give also several historical details.
- The concepts of **valuation on [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292)** and **valuation on [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470)** are a generalization of valuation in the sense of [domain](wikipedia://en/325077)/measure theory. A valuation on convex sets is allowed to assume [complex values](wikipedia://en/5826), and the underlying topological space is the set of [non-empty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-empty_set) convex [compact subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_subset) of a [finite-dimensional vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-dimensional_vector_space): a valuation on manifolds is a complex valued [finitely additive measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_additive_measure) defined on a proper [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of the [class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_%28mathematics%29) of all [compact submanifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_submanifold) of the given [manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifolds).

## Examples {#Examples}

### Dirac valuation {#Dirac_valuation}

Let $\scriptstyle (X,\mathcal{T})$ be a topological space, and let *$x$* be a point of *$X$*: the map

$$\delta_x(U)= \begin{cases} 0 & \mbox{if}~x\notin U\\ 1 & \mbox{if}~x\in U \end{cases} \quad \text{ for all } U \in \mathcal{T}$$

is a valuation in the domain theory/measure theory, sense called **[Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742) valuation**. This concept bears its origin from [distribution theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) as it is an obvious transposition to valuation theory of [Dirac distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_distribution): as seen above, Dirac valuations are the "[bricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick)" simple valuations are made of.
