# Double layer potential

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Double layer potential"}

In [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342), an area of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **double layer potential** is a solution of [Laplace's equation](wikipedia://en/36941) corresponding to the [electrostatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrostatic_potential) or [magnetic potential](wikipedia://en/2395496) associated to a [dipole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipole) distribution on a closed surface *S* in three-dimensions. Thus a double layer potential $u(\mathbf{x})$ is a scalar-valued function of $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbf{R}^{3}$ given by

$$u(\mathbf{x}) = \frac {-1} {4\pi} \int_S \rho(\mathbf{y}) \frac{\partial}{\partial\nu} \frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|} \, d\sigma(\mathbf{y})$$

where *ρ* denotes the dipole distribution, *∂*/*∂ν* denotes the directional derivative in the direction of the outward unit normal in the *y* variable, and dσ is the surface measure on *S*.

:::

More generally, a double layer potential is associated to a [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) *S* in *n*-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) by means of

$$u(\mathbf{x}) = \int_S \rho(\mathbf{y})\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu} P(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\,d\sigma(\mathbf{y})$$

where *P*(**y**) is the [Newtonian kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtonian_kernel) in *n* dimensions.
