# Herschel's condition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Herschel's condition"}

*Image: Sine condition.svg.* Entrance and exit rays through an imaging system (grey box).

In [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics), the **Herschel's condition** is a condition for an [optical system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_system) to produce sharp images for objects over an extended axial range, i.e. for objects displaced along the optical axis. It was formulated by [John Herschel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Herschel).[^o2]

:::

## Mathematical formulation {#Mathematical_formulation}

The Herschel's condition in mathematical form is

$$\frac{1-\cos \alpha_\mathrm{o}}{1-\cos \alpha_\mathrm{i}} = \frac{1-\cos \beta_\mathrm{o}}{1-\cos \beta_\mathrm{i}} = \frac{n_i^2}{n_o^2}|M_T|^2$$

where $\alpha_o,\beta_o$ are the object side ray angles, $\alpha_i,\beta_i$ are the image side ray angle. $n_o,n_i$ are the object and image side [refractive index](wikipedia://en/25880), and $M_T$ is the transverse magnification. This condition can be derived by the [Fermat's principle](wikipedia://en/37232).[^o4]

This condition can also be expressed as[^o5][^o6]

$$M_L = \frac{n_o\sin^2 (\alpha_\mathrm{o}/2)}{n_i\sin^2 (\alpha_\mathrm{i}/2)}=\frac{n_o(1-\cos \alpha_\mathrm{o})}{n_i(1-\cos \alpha_\mathrm{i})} \text{ and } M_T = \frac{n_o\sin (\alpha_\mathrm{o}/2)}{n_i\sin (\alpha_\mathrm{i}/2)}$$

where $M_L = \frac{n_i}{n_o}M_T^2$ is the longitudinal magnification.

This condition is in general conflict with the [Abbe sine condition](wikipedia://en/1123083), which is the condition for aberration free imaging for objects displaced off-axis. They can be simultaneously satisfied only when the system has magnification equal to the ratio of refractive index $|M_T|=n_o/n_i$.[^o5]
