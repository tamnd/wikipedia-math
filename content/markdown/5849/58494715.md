# Maria-Pia Geppert

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maria-Pia Geppert"}

**Maria-Pia Geppert** (May 28, 1907 – November 18, 1997) was a German mathematician and [biostatistician](wikipedia://en/3878) who co-founded the *[Biometrical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometrical_Journal)*.

:::

Geppert was the first woman to become a full professor at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen). With [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896), [Hilda Geiringer](wikipedia://en/7553047), [Ruth Moufang](wikipedia://en/2003831), and [Hel Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hel_Braun), Geppert was one of only a handful of women to work in mathematics in Germany before World War II and later convert their degrees into research careers as full professors.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Geppert was born in [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breslau), with Italian descent through her mother. Breslau is now [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław), in Poland, but at that time it was part of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire). Her older brother, Harald Geppert also became a mathematician, and a supporter of the Nazis.

She studied mathematics in Breslau and in [Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giessen), and in 1932 completed a doctorate at the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau). Her dissertation, *Approximative Darstellungen analytischer Funktionen, die durch Dirichletsche Reihen gegeben sind*, concerned [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) and was supervised by [Guido Hoheisel](wikipedia://en/5384175). [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230), in his last publication before Hitler came to power, commented unfavorably on one of her papers.

Next, Geppert moved to Rome, where from 1933 to 1936 she studied [actuarial science](wikipedia://en/189373) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) for a second doctorate under the supervision of [Guido Castelnuovo](wikipedia://en/1558891). She completed a [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1942 at the [University of Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen). Her dissertation was *Comparison of Two Observed Frequencies*. (Seneta Phipps 2001) write that her habilitation dissertation was "important but forgotten" because of the circumstances of the war, and they adopt her title for their own.

## Career {#Career}

In 1940, Geppert became director of the Department of Epidemiology and Statistics for the William G. Kerckhoff Heart Research Institute in [Bad Nauheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Nauheim), later to become the [Max Planck Institute for Heart and Lung Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Heart_and_Lung_Research). She joined [Goethe University Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt) in 1943, as a lecturer in biostatistics.

In 1964 she became chair for medical biometry at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen). In doing so, she became the first female full professor at the University of Tübingen. She retired in 1975.

## *Biometrical Journal* {#Biometrical_Journal}

With Ottokar Heinisch, Geppert founded the *[Biometrical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometrical_Journal)* in 1959. She was co-editor-in-chief with Heinisch from its founding until 1966, and remained co-editor-in-chief with Erna Weber until 1969.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1951, Geppert became the first German elected into the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute) in the post-war period. She also became an honorary member of the [International Biometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Biometric_Society) in 1965, the first person from the German region of the society to be so honored.
