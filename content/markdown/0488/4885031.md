# Prüfer group

*Image: Prüfer.png.* The Prüfer $2$-group with presentation $\langle g_{n}: g_{n+1}^{2} = g_{n}, g_{1}^{2} = e\rangle$, illustrated as a subgroup of the unit circle in the complex plane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prüfer group"}

In mathematics, specifically in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Prüfer *p*-group** or the ***p*-quasicyclic group** or $p^\infty$-group, $\mathbb Z(p^\infty)$, for a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p* is the unique [*p*-group](wikipedia://en/24664) in which every element has *p* different *p*-th roots.

:::

The Prüfer *p*-groups are [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) that are important in the classification of infinite abelian groups: they (along with the group of [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers)) form the smallest building blocks of all [divisible groups](wikipedia://en/472147).

The groups are named after [Heinz Prüfer](wikipedia://en/1655401), a German mathematician of the early 20th century.

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Constructions"}

## Constructions {#Constructions}

The Prüfer *p*-group may be identified with the subgroup of the [circle group](wikipedia://en/508177), $\operatorname{U}(1)$, consisting of all *p*<sup>*n*</sup>-th [roots of unity](wikipedia://en/171950) as *n* ranges over all non-negative integers:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)&=\{e^{2\pi i m/p^n} \mid 0 \leq m < p^n,\,n\in \mathbb{Z}^+\} \\ &= \{z\in\mathbb{C} \mid z^{(p^n)}=1 \text{ for some } n\in \mathbb{Z}^+\}.\end{aligned}$$

The group operation here is the multiplication of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). There is a [presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_presentation)

$$\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty) = \langle\, g_1, g_2, g_3, \ldots \mid g_1^p = 1, g_2^p = g_1, g_3^p = g_2, \dots\,\rangle.$$

Here, the group operation in $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ is written as multiplication.

Alternatively and equivalently, the Prüfer *p*-group may be defined as the [Sylow *p*-subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_subgroup) of the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, consisting of those elements whose order is a power of *p*:

$$\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty) = \mathbb{Z}[1/p]/\mathbb{Z}$$

(where $\mathbb{Z}[1/p]$ denotes the group of all rational numbers whose denominator is a power of *p*, using addition of rational numbers as group operation).

For each natural number *n*, consider the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) $\mathbb{Z}/p^n \mathbb{Z}$ and the embedding $\mathbb{Z}/p^n \mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/p^{n+1} \mathbb{Z}$ induced by multiplication by *p*. The [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of this system is $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$:

$$\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty) = \varinjlim \mathbb{Z}/p^n \mathbb{Z} .$$

If we perform the direct limit in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315), then we need to impose a topology on each of the $\mathbb{Z}/p^n \mathbb{Z}$, and take the [final topology](wikipedia://en/1819736) on $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$. If we wish for $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ to be [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637), we must impose the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology) on each of the $\mathbb{Z}/p^n \mathbb{Z}$, resulting in $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ to have the discrete topology.

We can also write

$$\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)=\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$$

where $\mathbb{Q}_p$ denotes the additive group of [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423) and $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is the subgroup of *p*-adic integers.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The complete list of subgroups of the Prüfer *p*-group $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ is:

$$0 \subsetneq \left({1 \over p}\mathbb{Z}\right)/\mathbb{Z} \subsetneq \left({1 \over p^2}\mathbb{Z}\right)/\mathbb{Z} \subsetneq \left({1 \over p^3}\mathbb{Z}\right)/\mathbb{Z} \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq \mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$$

Here, each $\left({1 \over p^n}\mathbb{Z}\right)/\mathbb{Z}$ is a cyclic subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ with *p*<sup>*n*</sup> elements; it contains precisely those elements of $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ whose [order](wikipedia://en/493527) divides *p*<sup>*n*</sup> and corresponds to the set of *p<sup>n</sup>*-th roots of unity.

The Prüfer *p*-groups are the only infinite groups whose subgroups are [totally ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered) by inclusion. This sequence of inclusions expresses the Prüfer *p*-group as the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of its finite subgroups. As there is no [maximal subgroup](wikipedia://en/1472649) of a Prüfer *p*-group, it is its own [Frattini subgroup](wikipedia://en/540707).

Given this list of subgroups, it is clear that the Prüfer *p*-groups are [indecomposable](wikipedia://en/810549) (cannot be written as a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of proper subgroups). More is true: the Prüfer *p*-groups are [subdirectly irreducible](wikipedia://en/17398087). An abelian group is subdirectly irreducible if and only if it is isomorphic to a finite cyclic *p*-group or to a Prüfer group.

The Prüfer *p*-group is the unique infinite [*p*-group](wikipedia://en/24664) that is [locally cyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_cyclic_group) (every finite set of elements generates a cyclic group). As seen above, all proper subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ are finite. The Prüfer *p*-groups are the only infinite abelian groups with this property.[^n1]

The Prüfer *p*-groups are [divisible](wikipedia://en/472147). They play an important role in the classification of divisible groups; along with the rational numbers they are the simplest divisible groups. More precisely: an abelian group is divisible if and only if it is the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of a (possibly infinite) number of copies of $\mathbb{Q}$ and (possibly infinite) numbers of copies of $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ for every prime *p*. The ([cardinal](wikipedia://en/6173)) numbers of copies of $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ that are used in this direct sum determine the divisible group up to isomorphism.[^n2]

As an abelian group (that is, as a [**Z**-module](wikipedia://en/276410)), $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ is [Artinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artinian_group) but not [Noetherian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noetherian_group).[^n3] It can thus be used as a counterexample against the idea that every Artinian module is Noetherian (whereas every [Artinian *ring*](wikipedia://en/489273) is Noetherian).

The [endomorphism ring](wikipedia://en/59623) of $\mathbb{Z}(p^\infty)$ is isomorphic to the ring of [*p*-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integers) $\mathbb{Z}_p$.[^n1]

In the theory of [locally compact topological groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_topological_group) the Prüfer *p*-group (endowed with the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology)) is the [Pontryagin dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_dual) of the compact group of [*p*-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integers), and the group of *p*-adic integers is the Pontryagin dual of the Prüfer *p*-group.[^n4]
