# Absorption law

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Absorption law"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **absorption law** or **absorption identity** is an [identity](wikipedia://en/298428) linking a pair of [binary operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_operations).

:::

Two binary operations, ¤ and ⁂, are said to be connected by the absorption law if:

*a* ¤ (*a* ⁂ *b*) = *a* ⁂ (*a* ¤ *b*) = *a*.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Lattices {#Lattices}

A [set](wikipedia://en/26691) equipped with two [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative) and [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative) binary operations $\scriptstyle \lor$ ("join") and $\scriptstyle \land$ ("meet") that are connected by the absorption law is called a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304#Lattices_as_algebraic_structures); in this case, both operations are necessarily [idempotent](wikipedia://en/14972) (i.e. *a* $\scriptstyle \lor$ *a* = *a* and *a* $\scriptstyle \land$ *a* = *a*).

Examples of lattices include [Heyting algebras](wikipedia://en/309343) and [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959),[^o5] in particular sets of sets with *[union](wikipedia://en/47949)* (∪) and *[intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429)* (∩) operators, [ordered sets](wikipedia://en/203204) with [*min* and *max*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_element) operations and [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) with [least common multiple](wikipedia://en/17961) and [greatest common divisor](wikipedia://en/12354).

### Logic {#Logic}

In [classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868), and in particular [Boolean algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_algebra_%28logic%29), the operations [OR](wikipedia://en/7962) and [AND](wikipedia://en/18152), which are also denoted by $\scriptstyle \lor$ and $\scriptstyle \land$, satisfy the lattice axioms, including the absorption law. The same is true for [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262).

::: {#o8 .example kind=example/counterexample cue=heading name="Non-examples"}

### Non-examples {#Non-examples}

The absorption law does not hold in many other algebraic structures, such as [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346), *e.g.* the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), [relevance logics](wikipedia://en/185076), [linear logics](wikipedia://en/579675), and [substructural logics](wikipedia://en/412909). In the last case, there is no [one-to-one correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-to-one_correspondence) between the [free variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_variable) of the defining pair of identities.
