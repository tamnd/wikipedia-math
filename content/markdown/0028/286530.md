# Arne Beurling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arne Beurling"}

*Image: Arne Beurling Uppsala maj 2019.jpg.* Statue of Arne Beurling in [Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala).

**Arne Carl-August Beurling** (3 February 1905 – 20 November 1986) was a [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University) (1937–1954) and later at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). Beurling worked extensively in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342). The "[Beurling factorization](wikipedia://en/314780#Factorization_into_inner_and_outer_functions_%28Beurling%29)" helped mathematical scientists to understand the [Wold decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wold_decomposition), and inspired further work on the [invariant subspaces](wikipedia://en/585826) of linear operators and [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987), e.g. Håkan Hedenmalm's factorization theorem for [Bergman spaces](wikipedia://en/3205927).

:::

He is perhaps most famous for single-handedly decrypting an early version of the German cipher machine [Siemens and Halske T52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_and_Halske_T52) in a matter of two weeks during 1940, using only pen and paper. This machine's cipher is generally considered to be more complicated than that of the more famous [Enigma machine](wikipedia://en/9256). Beurling's method of decrypting military telegrams between Norway and Germany worked from June 1940 right up until 1943 when the Germans changed equipment.

## Early life {#Early_life}

Beurling was born on 3 February 1905 in [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg), Sweden and was the son of the landowner Konrad Beurling and baroness Elsa Raab.[^o4] After [graduating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studentexamen) in 1924, he was enrolled at the [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University) where he received a Bachelor of Arts degree in 1926 and two years later a Licentiate of Philosophy degree.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

### Early career {#Early_career}

Beurling was assistant teacher at Uppsala University from 1931 to 1933.[^o4] He received his doctorate in mathematics in 1933 for his dissertation *Études sur un problème de majoration*.[^o7] Beurling was a [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent) of mathematics at Uppsala University from 1933 and then professor of mathematics from 1937 to 1954.[^o4]

### World War II {#World_War_II}

In the summer of 1940 he single-handedly [deciphered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decipher) and [reverse-engineered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_engineering) an early version of the [Siemens and Halske T52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_and_Halske_T52) also known as the *Geheimfernschreiber* ("secret teletypewriter") used by [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) for sending [ciphered](wikipedia://en/5244) messages.[^o9] The T52 was one of the so-called "[Fish cyphers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_cyphers)", that, using transposition, created nearly one quintillion (893,622,318,929,520,960) different variations. It took Beurling two weeks to solve the problem using pen and paper. Beurling, who had academic knowledge of the German language, proceeded in the classic way when deciphering a code: By trying to identify high-frequency or unusually long words, and based on some hypotheses, making formulas and tables to test them against other coded messages.[^o10] Using Beurling's work, a device was created that enabled Sweden to decipher German [teleprinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleprinter) traffic passing through Sweden from [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway) on a cable. In this way, Swedish authorities knew about [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) before it occurred.[^o11] Since the Swedes would not reveal how this knowledge was attained, the Swedish warning was not treated as credible by Soviets.

This became the foundation for the Swedish [National Defence Radio Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_Radio_Establishment) (*FRA*). The cypher in the *[Geheimfernschreiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geheimfernschreiber)* is generally considered to be more complex than the cypher used in the Enigma machines.[^o12]

### Later life {#Later_life}

He was visiting professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) from 1948 to 1949.[^o14] From 1954, he was professor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton, New Jersey, [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), where he took over [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s office.[^o15]

He was the [doctoral advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor) of [Lennart Carleson](wikipedia://en/1669541) and [Carl-Gustav Esseen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl-Gustav_Esseen). Carleson, the 2006 winner of the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285), described Beurling as “one of the few persons \[of whom\] I would use the word ‘genius’,” adding that “mathematics was part of his personality.”[^o16]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

*Image: Beurlings familjegrav.jpg.* Arne Beurling´s family grave at Norra begravningsplatsen in [Solna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solna_Municipality).

Arne Beurling was first married (1936–40) to Britta Östberg (born 1907), daughter of Henrik Östberg and Gerda Nilsson. In 1950, he married Karin Lindblad (1920–2006), daughter of ironmonger Henric Lindblad and Wanja Bengtsson.[^o4] Karin was a distinguished Ph.D. student from Uppsala University. When they lived in Princeton, she worked in a [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry) lab at Princeton University.[^o18] He had two children from his first marriage — Pehr-Henrik (1936–1962) and Jane (1938–1992).[^o4]

Beurling's great-grandfather was Pehr Henrik Beurling (1758 or 1763–1806), who founded a high quality clock factory in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm) in 1783.

## Death {#Death}

Arne Beurling died in 1986 and was buried at [Norra begravningsplatsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norra_begravningsplatsen) in [Solna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solna_Municipality).[^o20]

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

Beurling's prowess as a cryptanalyst is the subject of the 2005 short opera *Krypto CEG* by [Jonas Sjöstrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Sjöstrand) and [Kimmo Eriksson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimmo_Eriksson).
