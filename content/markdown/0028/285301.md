# Unit square

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unit square"}

*Image: Unit Square.svg.* The unit square in the [real plane](wikipedia://en/9417)

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **unit square** is a [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) whose sides have length $1$. Often, *the* unit square refers specifically to the square in the [Cartesian plane](wikipedia://en/7706#Cartesian_coordinates_in_two_dimensions) with corners at the four points $(0, 0$), $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, and $(1, 1)$.[^o2]

:::

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

In a [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706) with coordinates $(x, y)$, a unit square is defined as a [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) consisting of the points where both $x$ and $y$ lie in a closed [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) from $0$ to $1$.

That is, a unit square is the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) $I \times I$, where $I$ denotes the closed unit interval.

## Complex coordinates {#Complex_coordinates}

The unit square can also be thought of as a subset of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628), the topological space formed by the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). In this view, the four corners of the unit square are at the four complex numbers $0$, $1$, $i$, and $1 + i$.

## Rational distance problem {#Rational_distance_problem}

::: {#o6 .problem cue=unsolved}

Is there a point in the plane at a rational distance from all four corners of a unit square?

:::

It is not known whether any point in the plane is a [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) distance from all four vertices of the unit square.[^o7]
