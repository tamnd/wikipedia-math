# Social statistics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Social statistics"}

**Social statistics** is the use of [statistical](wikipedia://en/26685) measurement systems to study [human behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_behavior) in a social environment. This can be accomplished through [polling](wikipedia://en/277315) a group of people, evaluating a subset of data obtained about a group of people, or by observation and statistical analysis of a set of data that relates to people and their behaviors.

:::

## Statistics in the social sciences {#Statistics_in_the_social_sciences}

### History {#History}

*Image: Statue élevée à la mémoire de Adolphe Quetelet.jpg.* [Adolph Quetelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolph_Quetelet) published data on European population.

[Adolph Quetelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolph_Quetelet) was a proponent of [social physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_physics). In his book *Physique sociale*[^o4] he presents distributions of human [heights](wikipedia://en/239972), [age of marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_marriage), time of birth and death, [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) of human marriages, births and deaths, a [survival density](wikipedia://en/30876757) for humans and curve describing [fecundity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fecundity) as a function of age. He also developed the [Quetelet Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quetelet_Index).

[Francis Ysidro Edgeworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ysidro_Edgeworth) published "On Methods of Ascertaining Variations in the Rate of Births, Deaths, and Marriages" in 1885[^o5] which uses squares of differences for studying fluctuations and [George Udny Yule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Udny_Yule) published "On the [Correlation](wikipedia://en/157057) of total [Pauperism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauperism) with Proportion of [Out-Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poor_relief)" in 1895.[^o6]

A numerical [calibration](wikipedia://en/740955) for the fertility curve was given by [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695) in 1897 in his "The Chances of Death, and Other Studies in Evolution"[^o7] In this book Pearson also uses [standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590), [correlation](wikipedia://en/157057) and [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) for studying humans.

[Vilfredo Pareto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilfredo_Pareto) published his analysis of the [distribution of income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_of_income) in [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) and [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland) in 1897,[^o8] this is now known as the [Pareto principle](wikipedia://en/24562).

[Louis Guttman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Guttman) proposed that the values of [ordinal variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_variable) can be represented by a [Guttman scale](wikipedia://en/1058531), which is useful if the number of variables is large and allows the use of techniques such as [ordinary least squares](wikipedia://en/1651906).[^o9]

[Macroeconomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomic) statistical research has provided [stylized facts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stylized_facts), which include:

- [Bowley's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowley's_law) (1937) regarding the proportion between [wages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wages) and national output [^o10]
- The [Phillips curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_curve) (1958) regarding the relation between [wages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wages) and [unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment)[^o11]

Statistics and statistical analyses have become a key feature of social science: statistics is employed in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology), [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science), [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology) and [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology).

### Usage and applications {#Usage_and_applications}

Social scientists use social statistics for many purposes, including:

- the [evaluation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Program_evaluation) of the quality of [services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_%28economics%29) available to a group or organization,
- analyzing behaviors of groups of people in their environment and special situations,
- determining the wants of people through statistical [sampling](wikipedia://en/160361)
- evaluation of wage expenditures and savings[^o19]
- preventing industrial diseases[^o19]
- prevention of industrial accidents[^o19]
- [labour disputes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_dispute), such as supporting the [Anthracite Coal Strike Commission of 1902-1903](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_strike_of_1902)[^o20]
- supporting governments in times of peace and war[^o21]

### Reliability {#Reliability}

The use of statistics has become so widespread in the social sciences that many universities such as [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard), have developed institutes focusing on "quantitative social science." Harvard's [Institute for Quantitative Social Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Quantitative_Social_Science) focuses mainly on fields like [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) that incorporate the advanced causal statistical models that [Bayesian methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_method) provide. However, some experts in causality feel that these claims of [causal statistics](wikipedia://en/26923751) are overstated.[^o23][^o24] There is a debate regarding the uses and value of statistical methods in social science, especially in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science), with some statisticians questioning practices such as [data dredging](wikipedia://en/1311951) that can lead to unreliable policy conclusions of political partisans who overestimate the interpretive power that non-robust statistical methods such as simple and multiple [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386) allow. Indeed, an important axiom that social scientists cite, but often forget, is that "[correlation does not imply causation](wikipedia://en/39834)."
