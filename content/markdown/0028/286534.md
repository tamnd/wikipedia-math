# Truncated icosidodecahedron

Semireg polyhedron stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Truncated icosidodecahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **truncated icosidodecahedron**, **rhombitruncated icosidodecahedron**,[^o2] **great rhombicosidodecahedron**,[^o3][^o4] **omnitruncated dodecahedron** or **omnitruncated icosahedron**[^o5] is an [Archimedean solid](wikipedia://en/1847), one of thirteen [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885), [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560), non-[prismatic](wikipedia://en/283007) solids constructed by two or more types of [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) polygon [faces](wikipedia://en/149926). It is the [cantitruncated](wikipedia://en/7083690#Cantitruncation) form of either a [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361) or a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968).[^o6]

:::

It has 62 faces: 30 [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29), 20 regular [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733), and 12 regular [decagons](wikipedia://en/327393). It has the most edges and vertices of all [Platonic](wikipedia://en/23905) and Archimedean solids, though the [snub dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/287137) has more faces. Of all vertex-transitive polyhedra, it occupies the largest percentage (89.80%) of the volume of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/991784) in which it is [inscribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscribed), very narrowly beating the snub dodecahedron (89.63%) and [rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/285486) (89.23%), and less narrowly beating the [truncated icosahedron](wikipedia://en/31282) (86.74%); it also has by far the greatest volume (206.8 cubic units) when its edge length equals 1. Of all [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) polyhedra that are not prisms or [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851), it has the largest sum of angles (90 + 120 + 144 = 354 degrees) at each vertex; only a prism or antiprism with more than 60 sides would have a larger sum. Since each of its faces has [point symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_symmetry) (equivalently, 180° [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096)), the truncated icosidodecahedron is a **15**-[zonohedron](wikipedia://en/669402).

## Names {#Names}

The name *truncated icosidodecahedron*, given originally by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736), is misleading. An actual [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429) of an [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023) has [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048) instead of [squares](wikipedia://en/659939). This nonuniform polyhedron is [topologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically) equivalent to the Archimedean solid.

Alternate interchangeable names are:

- *Truncated icosidodecahedron* ([Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736))
- *Rhombitruncated icosidodecahedron* ([Magnus Wenninger](wikipedia://en/8864685)[^o2])
- *Great rhombicosidodecahedron* ([Robert Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Williams_%28geometer%29),[^o3] Peter Cromwell[^o4])
- *[Omnitruncated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnitruncation_%28geometry%29) dodecahedron* or *icosahedron* ([Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527)[^o5])

The name *great rhombicosidodecahedron* refers to the relationship with the (small) [rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/285486) (compare section Dissection). There is a [nonconvex uniform polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex_uniform_polyhedron) with a similar name, the [nonconvex great rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157639).

## Area and volume {#Area_and_volume}

The surface area *A* and the volume *V* of the truncated icosidodecahedron of edge length *a* are:

$$\begin{aligned} A &= 30 \left (1 + \sqrt{3} + \sqrt{5 + 2\sqrt{5}} \right)a^2 &&\approx 174.292\,0303a^2. \\ V &= \left( 95 + 50\sqrt{5} \right) a^3 &&\approx 206.803\,399a^3. \end{aligned}$$

If a set of all 13 [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847) were constructed with all edge lengths equal, the truncated icosidodecahedron would be the largest.

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

[Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) for the vertices of a truncated icosidodecahedron with edge length 2*φ* − 2, centered at the origin, are all the [even permutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_permutation) of:

(±$\tfrac{1}{\varphi}$, ±$\tfrac{1}{\varphi}$, ±(3 + *φ*)),

(±$\tfrac{2}{\varphi}$, ±*φ*, ±(1 + 2*φ*)),

(±$\tfrac{1}{\varphi}$, ±*φ*<sup>2</sup>, ±(−1 + 3*φ*)),

(±(2*φ* − 1), ±2, ±(2 + *φ*)) and

(±*φ*, ±3, ±2*φ*),

where *φ* = $\tfrac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).

Therefore, the [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) is the common distance of these points from the origin. For unit edge length, giving

*R* = $\tfrac{\sqrt{24\varphi+19}}{2}$ = $\tfrac{\sqrt{31+12\sqrt{5}}}{2}$ ≈ 3.802.

## Dissection {#Dissection}

The truncated icosidodecahedron is the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of a [rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/285486) with [cuboids](wikipedia://en/156802) above its 30 squares, whose height to base ratio is $\varphi$. The rest of its space can be dissected into nonuniform cupolas, namely 12 [between inner pentagons and outer decagons](wikipedia://en/1131073) and 20 [between inner triangles and outer hexagons](wikipedia://en/1128465).

An alternative dissection also has a rhombicosidodecahedral core. It has 12 [pentagonal rotundae](wikipedia://en/1128491) between inner pentagons and outer decagons. The remaining part is a [toroidal polyhedron](wikipedia://en/24804975).

## Orthogonal projections {#Orthogonal_projections}

The truncated icosidodecahedron has seven special [orthogonal projections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection), centered on a vertex, on three types of edges, and three types of faces: square, hexagonal and decagonal. The last two correspond to the A<sub>2</sub> and H<sub>2</sub> [Coxeter planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane).

## Spherical tilings and Schlegel diagrams {#Spherical_tilings_and_Schlegel_diagrams}

The truncated icosidodecahedron can also be represented as a [spherical tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_tiling), and projected onto the plane via a [stereographic projection](wikipedia://en/143431). This projection is [conformal](wikipedia://en/50627), preserving angles but not areas or lengths. Straight lines on the sphere are projected as circular arcs on the plane.

[Schlegel diagrams](wikipedia://en/7201595) are similar, with a [perspective projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspective_projection) and straight edges.

## Geometric variations {#Geometric_variations}

Within [Icosahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2877844) there are unlimited geometric variations of the *truncated icosidodecahedron* with [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560) faces. The [truncated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/376538), [rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/285486), and [truncated icosahedron](wikipedia://en/31282) as degenerate limiting cases.

## Truncated icosidodecahedral graph {#Truncated_icosidodecahedral_graph}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **truncated icosidodecahedral graph** (or **great rhombicosidodecahedral graph**) is the [graph of vertices and edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-skeleton) of the truncated icosidodecahedron, one of the [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847). It has 120 [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and 180 edges, and is a [zero-symmetric](wikipedia://en/44969299) and [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) [Archimedean graph](wikipedia://en/44969442).[^o23]
