# Truncated cuboctahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Truncated cuboctahedron"}

Semireg polyhedron stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **truncated cuboctahedron** or **great rhombicuboctahedron** is an [Archimedean solid](wikipedia://en/1847), named by Kepler as a [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429) of a [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280). It has 12 [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) faces, 8 regular [hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) faces, 6 regular [octagonal](wikipedia://en/314575) faces, 48 vertices, and 72 edges. Since each of its faces has [point symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_symmetry) (equivalently, 180° [rotational](wikipedia://en/39789) symmetry), the truncated cuboctahedron is a **9-[zonohedron](wikipedia://en/669402)**. The truncated cuboctahedron can [tessellate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnitruncated_cubic_honeycomb) with the [octagonal prism](wikipedia://en/3026569). It is the [cantitruncated](wikipedia://en/7083690#Cantitruncation) form of either a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) or a [regular octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458).[^o2]

*Image: Truncated cuboctahedron.stl.* 3D model of a truncated cuboctahedron

:::

## Names {#Names}

| The name *truncated cuboctahedron*, given originally by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736), is misleading: an actual [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429) of a [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) has [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048) instead of [squares](wikipedia://en/659939); however, this nonuniform polyhedron is [topologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically) equivalent to the Archimedean solid unrigorously named truncated cuboctahedron. Alternate interchangeable names are: *Truncated cuboctahedron* ([Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736)), *Rhombitruncated cuboctahedron* ([Magnus Wenninger](wikipedia://en/8864685)[^o4]), *Great rhombicuboctahedron* ([Robert Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Williams_%28geometer%29)[^o5]), *Great rhombcuboctahedron* (Peter Cromwell[^o6]), *[Omnitruncated](wikipedia://en/7083690) cube* or *cantitruncated cube* ([Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527)), *Beveled cube* ([Conway polyhedron notation](wikipedia://en/5021705)). |  |

There is a [nonconvex uniform polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex_uniform_polyhedron) with a similar name: the [nonconvex great rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/4157120).

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

The [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) for the vertices of a truncated cuboctahedron having edge length 2 and centered at the origin are all the [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) of:

$$\Bigl(\pm 1, \quad \pm\left(1 + \sqrt 2\right), \quad \pm\left(1 + 2\sqrt 2\right) \Bigr).$$

## Area and volume {#Area_and_volume}

The area *A* and the volume *V* of the truncated cuboctahedron of edge length *a* are:

$$\begin{aligned} A &= 12\left(2+\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}\right) a^2 &&\approx 61.755\,1724~a^2, \\ V &= \left(22+14\sqrt{2}\right) a^3 &&\approx 41.798\,9899~a^3. \end{aligned}$$

## Dissection {#Dissection}

The truncated cuboctahedron is the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of a [rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213) with cubes above its 12 squares on 2-fold symmetry axes. The rest of its space can be dissected into 6 [square cupolas](wikipedia://en/1128479) below the octagons, and 8 [triangular cupolas](wikipedia://en/1128465) below the hexagons.

A dissected truncated cuboctahedron can create a genus 5, 7, or 11 [Stewart toroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_toroid) by removing the central rhombicuboctahedron, and either the 6 square cupolas, the 8 triangular cupolas, or the 12 cubes respectively. Many other lower symmetry toroids can also be constructed by removing the central rhombicuboctahedron, and a subset of the other dissection components. For example, removing 4 of the triangular cupolas creates a genus 3 toroid; if these cupolas are appropriately chosen, then this toroid has tetrahedral symmetry.[^o10][^o11]

## Uniform colorings {#Uniform_colorings}

There is only one [uniform coloring](wikipedia://en/18624814) of the faces of this polyhedron, one color for each face type.

A 2-uniform coloring, with [tetrahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2904472), exists with alternately colored hexagons.

## Orthogonal projections {#Orthogonal_projections}

## Spherical tiling {#Spherical_tiling}

The truncated cuboctahedron can also be represented as a [spherical tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_tiling), and projected onto the plane via a [stereographic projection](wikipedia://en/143431). This projection is [conformal](wikipedia://en/50627), preserving angles but not areas or lengths. Straight lines on the sphere are projected as circular arcs on the plane.

## Full octahedral group {#Full_octahedral_group}

*Image: Full octahedral group elements in truncated cuboctahedron; JF.png.*

Like many other solids the truncated octahedron has full [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805) - but its relationship with the full octahedral group is closer than that: Its 48 vertices correspond to the elements of the group, and each face of [its dual](wikipedia://en/724752) is a [fundamental domain](wikipedia://en/469365) of the group.

The image on the right shows the 48 permutations in the group applied to an example object (namely the light JF compound on the left). The 24 light elements are rotations, and the dark ones are their reflections.

The edges of the solid correspond to the 9 reflections in the group:

- Those between octagons and squares correspond to the 3 reflections between opposite octagons.
- Hexagon edges correspond to the 6 reflections between opposite squares.
- (There are no reflections between opposite hexagons.)

The subgroups correspond to solids that share the respective vertices of the truncated octahedron. E.g. the 3 subgroups with 24 elements correspond to a nonuniform [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249) with chiral octahedral symmetry, a nonuniform [rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213) with [pyritohedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyritohedral_symmetry) (the [cantic snub octahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantic_snub_octahedron)) and a nonuniform [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654) with [full tetrahedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_tetrahedral_symmetry). The unique subgroup with 12 elements is the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) A<sub>4</sub>. It corresponds to a nonuniform [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) with [chiral tetrahedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral_tetrahedral_symmetry).

The truncated cuboctahedron is one of a family of uniform polyhedra related to the cube and regular octahedron.

This polyhedron can be considered a member of a sequence of uniform patterns with [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) (4.6.2*p*) and [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) . For *p* \< 6, the members of the sequence are [omnitruncated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnitruncation_%28geometry%29) polyhedra ([zonohedrons](wikipedia://en/669402)), shown below as spherical tilings. For *p* \< 6, they are tilings of the hyperbolic plane, starting with the [truncated triheptagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/8834676).

## Truncated cuboctahedral graph {#Truncated_cuboctahedral_graph}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **truncated cuboctahedral graph** (or **great rhombcuboctahedral graph**) is the [graph of vertices and edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-skeleton) of the truncated cuboctahedron, one of the [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847). It has 48 [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and 72 edges, and is a [zero-symmetric](wikipedia://en/44969299) and [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) [Archimedean graph](wikipedia://en/44969442).[^o26]
