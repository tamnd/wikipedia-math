# Pairwise compatibility graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pairwise compatibility graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) $G$ is a **pairwise compatibility graph** (PCG) if there exists a weighted [tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_%28data_structure%29) $T$ and two non-negative real numbers $d_{min} \leq d_{max}$ such that each node $u'$ of $G$ has a one-to-one mapping with a [leaf node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaf_node) $u$ of $T$ such that two nodes $u'$ and $v'$ are adjacent in $G$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the distance between $u$ and $v$ are in the interval $[d_{min}, d_{max}]$.[^o2]

:::

The subclasses of PCG include graphs of at most seven vertices, [cycles](wikipedia://en/878962), [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_%28graph_theory%29), [complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816), [interval graphs](wikipedia://en/363423) and [ladder graphs](wikipedia://en/19657952).[^o2] However, there is a graph with eight vertices that is known not to be a PCG.[^o3]

## Relationship to phylogenetics {#Relationship_to_phylogenetics}

Pairwise compatibility graphs were first introduced by Paul Kearney, J. Ian Munro and Derek Phillips in the context of [phylogeny reconstruction](wikipedia://en/3986130). When sampling from a [phylogenetic tree](wikipedia://en/149326), the task of finding nodes whose path distance lies between given lengths $d_{min} \leq d_{max}$ is equivalent to [finding a clique](wikipedia://en/249254) in the associated PCG.[^o5]

## Complexity {#Complexity}

The [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/6511) of deciding whether an arbitrary graph is a PCG is NP-complete.[^o7] Additionally, the related problem of finding for a graph $G$ and a selection of non-edge relations $S$ a PCG containing $G$ as a subgraph and with none of the edges in $S$ is known to be NP-hard.[^o3]

The task of finding nodes in a tree whose path distances lie between $d_{min}$ and $d_{max}$ is known to be solvable in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial-time). Therefore, if the tree could be recovered from a PCG in polynomial time, then the clique problem on PCGs would be polynomial too. As of 2020, neither of these complexities is known.[^o2]
