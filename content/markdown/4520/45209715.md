# Livingstone graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Livingstone graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Livingstone graph** is a [distance-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/7768222) with 266 vertices and 1463 edges. Its [intersection array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_array) is {11,10,6,1;1,1,5,11}.[^o3] It is the largest distance-transitive graph with degree 11 and diameter ≤ 4.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Algebraic properties"}

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the Livingstone graph is the [sporadic](wikipedia://en/324806) [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600) [J<sub>1</sub>](wikipedia://en/10796779), and the stabiliser of a point is [PSL(2,11)](wikipedia://en/382667). As the stabiliser is maximal in J<sub>1</sub>, it acts primitively on the graph.

As the Livingstone graph is distance-transitive, PSL(2,11) acts transitively on the set of 11 vertices adjacent to a reference vertex *v*, and also on the set of 12 vertices at distance 4 from *v*. The second action is equivalent to the standard action of PSL(2,11) on the projective line over **F**<sub>11</sub>; the first is equivalent to an exceptional action on 11 points, related to the [Paley biplane](wikipedia://en/1188184#Paley_biplane).

:::
