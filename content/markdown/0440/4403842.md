# Profunctor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Profunctor"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **profunctors** are a generalization of [relations](wikipedia://en/3931) and also of [bimodules](wikipedia://en/492612).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **profunctor** (also named **distributor** by the French school and **module** by the Sydney school) $\,\phi$ from a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $C$ to a category $D$, written

$$\phi : C\nrightarrow D,$$

is defined to be a [functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functor_%28category_theory%29)

$$\phi : D^{\mathrm{op}}\times C\to\mathbf{Set}$$

where $D^\mathrm{op}$ denotes the [opposite category](wikipedia://en/372399) of $D$ and $\mathbf{Set}$ denotes the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829). Given morphisms $f : d\to d', g : c\to c'$ respectively in $D, C$ and an element $x\in\phi(d',c)$, we write $xf\in \phi(d,c), gx\in\phi(d',c')$ to denote the actions.

Using that the [category of small categories](wikipedia://en/2072741) $\mathbf{Cat}$ is [cartesian closed](wikipedia://en/237037), the profunctor $\phi$ can be seen as a functor

$$\hat{\phi} : C\to\hat{D}$$

where $\hat{D}$ denotes the category $\mathrm{Set}^{D^\mathrm{op}}$ of [presheaves](wikipedia://en/4840944) over $D$.

A **correspondence** from $C$ to $D$ is a profunctor $D\nrightarrow C$.

### Profunctors as categories {#Profunctors_as_categories}

An equivalent definition of a profunctor $\phi : C\nrightarrow D$ is a category whose objects are the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of the objects of $C$ and the objects of $D$, and whose morphisms are the morphisms of $C$ and the morphisms of $D$, plus zero or more additional morphisms from objects of $D$ to objects of $C$. The sets in the formal definition above are the hom-sets between objects of $D$ and objects of $C$. (These are also known as het-sets, since the corresponding morphisms can be called *heteromorphisms*.) The previous definition can be recovered by the restriction of the hom-functor $\phi^\text{op}\times \phi \to \mathbf{Set}$ to $D^\text{op}\times C$.

This also makes it clear that a profunctor can be thought of as a relation between the objects of $C$ and the objects of $D$, where each member of the relation is associated with a set of morphisms. A functor is a special case of a profunctor in the same way that a function is a special case of a relation.

### Composition of profunctors {#Composition_of_profunctors}

The composite $\psi\phi$ of two profunctors

$\phi : C\nrightarrow D$ and $\psi : D\nrightarrow E$

is given by

$$\psi\phi=\mathrm{Lan}_{Y_D}(\hat{\psi})\circ\hat\phi$$

where $\mathrm{Lan}_{Y_D}(\hat{\psi})$ is the left [Kan extension](wikipedia://en/2502518) of the functor $\hat{\psi}$ along the [Yoneda functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoneda_functor) $Y_D : D\to\hat D$ of $D$ (which to every object $d$ of $D$ associates the functor $D(-,d) : D^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Set}$).

It can be shown that

$$(\psi\phi)(e,c)=\left(\coprod_{d\in D}\psi(e,d)\times\phi(d,c)\right)\Bigg/\sim$$

where $\sim$ is the least [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) such that $(y',x')\sim(y,x)$ whenever there exists a morphism $v$ in $D$ such that

$y'=vy \in\psi(e,d')$ and $x'v=x \in\phi(d,c)$.

Equivalently, profunctor composition can be written using a [coend](wikipedia://en/3865669)

$$(\psi\phi)(e,c)=\int^{d\colon D}\psi(e,d)\times\phi(d,c)$$

### Bicategory of profunctors {#Bicategory_of_profunctors}

Composition of profunctors is associative only up to isomorphism (because the product is not strictly associative in **Set**). The best one can hope is therefore to build a [bicategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicategory) **Prof** whose

- 0-cells are [small categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_category),
- 1-cells between two small categories are the profunctors between those categories,
- 2-cells between two profunctors are the [natural transformations](wikipedia://en/45022) between those profunctors.

## Properties {#Properties}

### Lifting functors to profunctors {#Lifting_functors_to_profunctors}

A functor $F : C\to D$ can be seen as a profunctor $\phi_F : C\nrightarrow D$ by postcomposing with the Yoneda functor:

$$\phi_F=Y_D\circ F.$$

It can be shown that such a profunctor $\phi_F$ has a [right adjoint](wikipedia://en/53991). Moreover, this is a characterization: a profunctor $\phi : C\nrightarrow D$ has a right adjoint if and only if $\hat\phi : C\to\hat D$ factors through the [Cauchy completion](wikipedia://en/933541) of $D$, i.e. there exists a functor $F : C\to D$ such that $\hat\phi=Y_D\circ F$.
