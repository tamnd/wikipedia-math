# Third fundamental form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Third fundamental form"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **third fundamental form** is a surface metric denoted by $\mathrm{I\!I\!I}$. Unlike the [second fundamental form](wikipedia://en/652078), it is independent of the [surface normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_normal).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $S$ be the [shape operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_operator) and $M$ be a [smooth surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_surface). Also, let $\mathbf{u}_{p}$ and $\mathbf{v}_{p}$ be elements of the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) $T_{p}(M)$. The third fundamental form is then given by

$$\mathrm{I\!I\!I}(\mathbf{u}_p,\mathbf{v}_p)=S(\mathbf{u}_p)\cdot S(\mathbf{v}_p)\,.$$

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The third fundamental form is expressible entirely in terms of the [first fundamental form](wikipedia://en/510355) and [second fundamental form](wikipedia://en/652078). If we let $H$ be the mean curvature of the surface and $K$ be the Gaussian curvature of the surface, we have

$$\mathrm{I\!I\!I}-2H\mathrm{I\!I}+K\mathrm{I}=0\,.$$

As the shape operator is self-adjoint, for $u,v \in T_{p}(M)$, we find

$$\mathrm{I\!I\!I}(u,v)=\langle Su,Sv\rangle=\langle u,S^2v\rangle=\langle S^2u,v\rangle\,.$$

:::
