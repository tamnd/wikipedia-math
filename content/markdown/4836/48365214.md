# Nancy Hingston

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nancy Hingston"}

**Nancy Burgess Hingston** is a mathematician working in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). She is a professor emerita of mathematics at [The College of New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_College_of_New_Jersey).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Nancy Hingston's father William Hingston was superintendent of the [Central Bucks School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bucks_School_District) in Pennsylvania; her mother was a high school mathematics and computer science teacher.[^o18] She graduated from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) with a double major in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939). After a year studying physics as a graduate student, she switched to mathematics,[^o3] and completed her PhD in 1981 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) under the supervision of [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457).

## Career {#Career}

Before joining TCNJ, she taught at the University of Pennsylvania.[^o18] She has also been a frequent visitor to the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179),[^o3] and has been involved with the Program for Women and Mathematics at the Institute for Advanced Study since its founding in 1994.[^o7]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Nancy Hingston made major contributions in Riemannian geometry and Hamiltonian dynamics, and more specifically in the study of [closed geodesics](wikipedia://en/19903176) and, more generally, [periodic orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_orbits) of Hamiltonian systems. In her very first paper,[^o9] she proved that a generic [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on a closed manifold possesses infinitely many closed geodesics. In the 1990s, she proved that the growth rate of [closed geodesics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_geodesics) in Riemannian 2-spheres is at least the one of prime numbers.[^o10] In the years 2000s, she proved the long-standing [Conley conjecture](wikipedia://en/74883647) from symplectic geometry: every Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of a standard symplectic torus of any even dimension possesses infinitely many periodic points[^o11] (the result was subsequently extended by [Viktor Ginzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Ginzburg) to more general [symplectic manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_manifolds)).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Nancy Hingston was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2014.[^o13][^o14][^o15]

She is a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), for "contributions to differential geometry and the study of closed geodesics."[^o16]

## Personal {#Personal}

Her husband, Jovi Tenev, is a lawyer.[^o18] She has three children.[^o14]
