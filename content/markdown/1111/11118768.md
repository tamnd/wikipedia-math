# Parabolic induction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Parabolic induction"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **parabolic induction** is a method of constructing [representations](wikipedia://en/12695) of a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) from representations of its [parabolic subgroups](wikipedia://en/588356#Parabolic_subgroups).

:::

If *G* is a reductive algebraic group and $P=MAN$ is the [Langlands decomposition](wikipedia://en/11085278) of a parabolic subgroup *P*, then parabolic induction consists of taking a representation of $MA$, extending it to *P* by letting *N* act trivially, and [inducing](wikipedia://en/357452) the result from *P* to *G*.

There are some generalizations of parabolic induction using [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060), such as [cohomological parabolic induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomological_parabolic_induction) and [Deligne–Lusztig theory](wikipedia://en/10574794).

## Philosophy of cusp forms {#Philosophy_of_cusp_forms}

The *philosophy of [cusp forms](wikipedia://en/610165)* was a slogan of [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190), expressing his idea of a kind of reverse engineering of [automorphic form](wikipedia://en/782146) theory, from the point of view of [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).[^o4] The [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536) Γ fundamental to the classical theory disappears, superficially. What remains is the basic idea that representations in general are to be constructed by parabolic induction of [cuspidal representations](wikipedia://en/6043458).[^o5] A similar philosophy was enunciated by [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643),[^o6] and the philosophy is a precursor of the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905). A consequence for thinking about representation theory is that [cuspidal representations](wikipedia://en/6043458) are the fundamental class of objects, from which other representations may be constructed by procedures of induction.

According to [Nolan Wallach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nolan_Wallach)[^o7]

> Put in the simplest terms the "philosophy of cusp forms" says that for each Γ-conjugacy classes of Q-rational parabolic subgroups one should construct automorphic functions (from objects from spaces of lower dimensions) whose constant terms are zero for other conjugacy classes and the constant terms for \[an\] element of the given class give all constant terms for this parabolic subgroup. This is almost possible and leads to a description of all automorphic forms in terms of these constructs and cusp forms. The construction that does this is the [Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/1270458).
