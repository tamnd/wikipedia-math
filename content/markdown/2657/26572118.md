# Joseph Silverman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph Silverman"}

**Joseph Silverman** (August 25, 1860 in [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio) – July 26, 1930 in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City)), was a leading [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Judaism) [rabbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbi) and author. He was the first American born rabbi to serve in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_Emanu-El_of_New_York).

:::

Born in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati), he attended the [University of Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cincinnati) and received a [Doctor of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Divinity) from the [Hebrew Union College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Union_College) in 1887, from which he received his rabbinic ordination three years earlier.[^o4] In 1887, he married and subsequently had five children with his wife Henrietta.

He was Rabbi of [Temple Emanu-El](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Emanu-El_%28Dallas%29), [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas), [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas), September 1884 to June 1885; rabbi of [Congregation B'nai Israel, Galveston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_B'nai_Israel,_Galveston), Texas July, 1885 to February 20, 1888. While in Texas he was a circuit preacher to the Jewish communities in the vicinity of Dallas and Galveston, and aided in organizing many Sabbath schools and congregations. He was consulting editor of the *[Jewish Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Encyclopedia)* ([Funk & Wagnalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk_&_Wagnalls)). He helped organize the [Religious Congress of the World's Fair in Chicago, 1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Congress_of_the_World's_Fair_in_Chicago,_1893), where his address on this occasion was titled, "The Popular Errors About the Jews."

At the beginning of 1888, Silverman received an offer from [Temple Emanu-El](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_Emanu-El_of_New_York) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) to serve as a rabbi of the leading [Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Judaism) congregation in America. Silverman started at Temple Emanu-El on March 1, 1888, succeeding rabbi [Gustav Gottheil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Gottheil).

During the years of his career in New York, 1888-1922 he was also president (1900–1903) of the Central Conference of American Rabbis, the Eastern Council[^o2] 1918- and was founder and president of the Emanu-El Brotherhood.

Silverman published many articles and books, including *A Catechism on Judaism* (1886) and *The Renaissance of Judaism* (1918). An article from *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* on April 21, 1912, quoted Silverman at a memorial service for victims of the [RMS Titanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Titanic) disaster as saying "Not God was responsible for this great disaster but the imperfection of human knowledge and judgment."[^o3]

Silverman died on July 26, 1930, having spent 34 years as the leader of Temple Emanu-El followed by eight years as Rabbi Emeritus. *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* described him in its obituary as "one of the most prominent clergymen of his faith in America".[^o4]
