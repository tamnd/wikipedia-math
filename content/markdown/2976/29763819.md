# Ushiki's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ushiki's theorem"}

In mathematics, particularly in the study of functions of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variable), **Ushiki's theorem**, named after S. Ushiki, states that certain [well-behaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-behaved) functions cannot have certain kinds of well-behaved invariant manifolds.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="The theorem"}

## The theorem {#The_theorem}

A [biholomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biholomorphic) [mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapping_%28mathematics%29) $F:\mathbb{C}^n\to\mathbb{C}^n$ cannot have a 1-dimensional [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) [invariant manifold](wikipedia://en/29763944). In particular, such a map cannot have a [homoclinic connection](wikipedia://en/29764173) or [heteroclinic connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteroclinic_connection).

:::

## Commentary {#Commentary}

Invariant manifolds typically appear as solutions of certain asymptotic problems in [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems). The most common is the [stable manifold](wikipedia://en/5466649) or its kin, the unstable manifold.

## The publication {#The_publication}

Ushiki's theorem was published in 1980.[^o6] The theorem appeared in print again several years later, in a certain Russian journal, by an author apparently unaware of Ushiki's work.

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="An application"}

## An application {#An_application}

The [standard map](wikipedia://en/9503407) cannot have a homoclinic or heteroclinic connection. The practical consequence is that one cannot show the existence of a [Smale's horseshoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smale's_horseshoe) in this system by a perturbation method, starting from a homoclinic or heteroclinic connection. Nevertheless, one can show that Smale's horseshoe exists in the standard map for many parameter values, based on crude rigorous numerical calculations.

:::
