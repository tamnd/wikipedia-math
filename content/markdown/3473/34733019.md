# Jacobson–Morozov theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacobson–Morozov theorem"}

In mathematics, the **Jacobson–Morozov theorem** is the assertion that [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235) elements in a semi-simple [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) can be extended to [sl<sub>2</sub>-triples](wikipedia://en/14994282). The theorem is named after (Jacobson 1951), (Morozov 1942).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The statement of Jacobson–Morozov relies on the following preliminary notions: an sl<sub>2</sub>-triple in a [semi-simple Lie algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-simple_Lie_algebra) $\mathfrak g$ (throughout in this article, over a field of [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero)) is a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) of Lie algebras $\mathfrak{sl}_2 \to \mathfrak g$. Equivalently, it is a triple $e, f, h$ of elements in $\mathfrak g$ satisfying the relations

$$[h,e] = 2e, \quad [h,f] = -2f, \quad [e,f] = h.$$

An element $x \in \mathfrak g$ is called nilpotent, if the [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) $[x, -] : \mathfrak g \to \mathfrak g$ (known as the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188)) is a [nilpotent endomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilpotent_endomorphism). It is an elementary fact that for any sl<sub>2</sub>-triple $(e, f, h)$, *e* must be nilpotent. The Jacobson–Morozov theorem states that, conversely, any nilpotent non-zero element $e \in \mathfrak g$ can be extended to an sl<sub>2</sub>-triple.[^o6][^o7] For $\mathfrak g = \mathfrak{sl}_n$, the sl<sub>2</sub>-triples obtained in this way are made explicit in (Chriss Ginzburg 1997, p. 184).

The theorem can also be stated for [linear algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/395885) (again over a field *k* of characteristic zero): any morphism (of algebraic groups) from the [additive group](wikipedia://en/332229) $G_a$ to a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) *H* factors through the embedding

$$G_a \to SL_2, x \mapsto \left ( \begin{array}{cc} 1 & x \\ 0 & 1 \end{array} \right ).$$

Furthermore, any two such factorizations

$$SL_2 \to H$$

are conjugate by a *k*-point of *H*.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

A far-reaching generalization of the theorem as formulated above can be stated as follows: the inclusion of pro-reductive groups into all linear algebraic groups, where morphisms $G \to H$ in both categories are taken up to conjugation by elements in $H(k)$, admits a [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint), the so-called pro-reductive envelope. This left adjoint sends the additive group $G_a$ to $SL_2$ (which happens to be semi-simple, as opposed to pro-reductive), thereby recovering the above form of Jacobson–Morozov. This generalized Jacobson–Morozov theorem was proven by (André Kahn 2002, Theorem 19.3.1) by appealing to methods related to [Tannakian categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannakian_category) and by (O'Sullivan 2010) by more geometric methods.

:::
