# Barratt–Priddy theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Barratt–Priddy theorem"}

In [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Barratt–Priddy theorem** (also referred to as **Barratt–Priddy–Quillen theorem**) expresses a connection between the homology of the [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901) and mapping spaces of spheres. The theorem (named after Michael Barratt, Stewart Priddy, and [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519)) is also often stated as a relation between the [sphere spectrum](wikipedia://en/21118593) and the [classifying spaces](wikipedia://en/923556) of the symmetric groups via Quillen's [plus construction](wikipedia://en/581071).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

The mapping space $\operatorname{Map}_0(S^n,S^n)$ is the [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) of all continuous maps $f\colon S^n \to S^n$ from the $n$-dimensional sphere $S^n$ to itself, under the topology of [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652) (a special case of the [compact-open topology](wikipedia://en/726335)). These maps are required to fix a basepoint $x\in S^n$, satisfying $f(x)=x$, and to have [degree](wikipedia://en/3094450) 0; this guarantees that the mapping space is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233). The Barratt–Priddy theorem expresses a relation between the homology of these mapping spaces and the homology of the [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901) $\Sigma_n$.

It follows from the [Freudenthal suspension theorem](wikipedia://en/1880440) and the [Hurewicz theorem](wikipedia://en/591587) that the $k$th [homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) $H_k(\operatorname{Map}_0(S^n,S^n))$ of this mapping space is *independent* of the dimension $n$, as long as $n>k$. Similarly, (txt) proved that the $k$th [group homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_homology) $H_k(\Sigma_n)$ of the symmetric group $\Sigma_n$ on $n$ elements is independent of $n$, as long as $n \ge 2k$. This is an instance of [homological stability](wikipedia://en/51898110).

The Barratt–Priddy theorem states that these "stable homology groups" are the same: for $n \ge 2k$, there is a natural isomorphism

$$H_k(\Sigma_n)\cong H_k(\text{Map}_0(S^n,S^n)).$$

This isomorphism holds with integral coefficients (in fact with any coefficients, as is made clear in the reformulation below).

:::

## Example: first homology {#Example:_first_homology}

This isomorphism can be seen explicitly for the first homology $H_1$. The [first homology of a group](wikipedia://en/248635#H1) is the largest [commutative](wikipedia://en/2974) quotient of that group. For the permutation groups $\Sigma_n$, the only commutative quotient is given by the [sign of a permutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_of_a_permutation), taking values in $\{-1,\,1$}. This shows that $H_1(\Sigma_n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order 2, for all $n\ge 2$. (For $n= 1$, $\Sigma_1$ is the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059), so $H_1(\Sigma_1) = 0$.)

It follows from the theory of [covering spaces](wikipedia://en/250323) that the mapping space $\operatorname{Map}_0(S^1,S^1)$ of the circle $S^1$ is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414), so $H_1(\operatorname{Map}_0(S^1,S^1))=0$. For the 2-sphere $S^2$, the first [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506) and first homology group of the mapping space are [both infinite cyclic](wikipedia://en/1104697#.CF.803.28S2.29_.3D_Z):

$$\pi_1(\operatorname{Map}_0(S^2,S^2))=H_1(\operatorname{Map}_0(S^2,S^2))\cong \mathbb{Z}.$$

A generator for this group can be built from the [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384) $S^3 \to S^2$. Finally, once $n\ge 3$, both are [cyclic of order 2](wikipedia://en/1104697#Table_of_homotopy_groups):

$$\pi_1(\operatorname{Map}_0(S^n,S^n))=H_1(\operatorname{Map}_0(S^n,S^n))\cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.$$

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Reformulation of the theorem"}

## Reformulation of the theorem {#Reformulation_of_the_theorem}

The infinite symmetric group $\Sigma_{\infty}$ is the union of the finite [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901) $\Sigma_{n}$, and Nakaoka's theorem implies that the group homology of $\Sigma_{\infty}$ is the stable homology of $\Sigma_{n}$: for $n\ge 2k$,

$$H_k(\Sigma_{\infty}) \cong H_k(\Sigma_{n}).$$

The [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) of this group is denoted $B \Sigma_{\infty}$, and its homology of this space is the group homology of $\Sigma_{\infty}$:

$$H_k(B \Sigma_{\infty})\cong H_k(\Sigma_{\infty}).$$

We similarly denote by $\operatorname{Map}_0(S^{\infty},S^{\infty})$ the union of the mapping spaces $\operatorname{Map}_0(S^{n},S^{n})$ under the inclusions induced by [suspension](wikipedia://en/1251702). The homology of $\operatorname{Map}_0(S^{\infty},S^{\infty})$ is the stable homology of the previous mapping spaces: for $n>k$,

$$H_k(\operatorname{Map}_0(S^{\infty},S^{\infty})) \cong H_k(\operatorname{Map}_0(S^{n},S^{n})).$$

There is a natural map $\varphi\colon B\Sigma_{\infty} \to \operatorname{Map}_0(S^{\infty},S^{\infty})$; one way to construct this map is via the model of $B\Sigma_{\infty}$ as the space of finite subsets of $\mathbb{R}^{\infty}$ endowed with an appropriate topology. An equivalent formulation of the Barratt–Priddy theorem is that $\varphi$ is a *homology equivalence* (or *acyclic map*), meaning that $\varphi$ induces an isomorphism on all homology groups with any local coefficient system.

:::

## Relation with Quillen's plus construction {#Relation_with_Quillen's_plus_construction}

The Barratt–Priddy theorem implies that the space $B\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}$ resulting from applying Quillen's [plus construction](wikipedia://en/581071) to $B\Sigma_{\infty}$ can be identified with $\operatorname{Map}_{0}(S^{\infty},S^{\infty})$. (Since $\pi_{1}(\operatorname{Map}_{0}(S^{\infty},S^{\infty}))\cong H_{1}(\Sigma_{\infty})\cong\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}$, the map $\varphi: B\Sigma_{\infty}\to\operatorname{Map}_{0}(S^{\infty},S^{\infty})$ satisfies the universal property of the plus construction once it is known that $\varphi$ is a homology equivalence.)

The mapping spaces $\operatorname{Map}_{0}(S^{n},S^{n})$ are more commonly denoted by $\Omega^{n}_{0}S^{n}$, where $\Omega^{n}S^{n}$ is the $n$-fold [loop space](wikipedia://en/2174332) of the $n$-sphere $S^{n}$, and similarly $\operatorname{Map}_{0}(S^{\infty},S^{\infty})$ is denoted by $\Omega^{\infty}_{0}S^{\infty}$. Therefore the Barratt–Priddy theorem can also be stated as

$B\Sigma_\infty^+\simeq \Omega_0^\infty S^\infty$ or

$$\textbf{Z}\times B\Sigma_\infty^+\simeq \Omega^\infty S^\infty$$

In particular, the homotopy groups of $B\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}$ are the [stable homotopy groups of spheres](wikipedia://en/1104697):

$$\pi_i(B\Sigma_\infty^+)\cong \pi_i(\Omega^\infty S^\infty)\cong \lim_{n\rightarrow \infty} \pi_{n+i}(S^n)=\pi_i^s(S^n)$$

## "*K*-theory of **F**<sub>1</sub>" {#"K-theory_of_F1"}

The Barratt–Priddy theorem is sometimes colloquially rephrased as saying that "the *K*-groups of **F**<sub>1</sub> are the stable homotopy groups of spheres". This is not a meaningful mathematical statement, but a metaphor expressing an analogy with [algebraic *K*-theory](wikipedia://en/598500).

The "[field with one element](wikipedia://en/13798040)" **F**<sub>1</sub> is not a [mathematical object](wikipedia://en/19453961); it refers to a collection of analogies between algebra and combinatorics. One central analogy is the idea that $GL_{n}(\mathbf{F}_{1})$ should be the symmetric group $\Sigma_{n}$. The [higher *K*-groups](wikipedia://en/598500#Higher_K-theory) $K_{i}(R)$ of a ring *R* can be defined as

$$K_i(R)=\pi_i(BGL_\infty(R)^+)$$

According to this analogy, the K-groups $K_{i}(\mathbf{F}_{1})$ of $\mathbf{F}_{1}$ should be defined as $\pi_{i}(BGL_{\infty}(\mathbf{F}_{1})^{+})=\pi_{i}(B\Sigma_{\infty}^{+})$, which by the Barratt–Priddy theorem is:

$$K_i(\mathbf{F}_1)=\pi_i(BGL_\infty(\mathbf{F}_1)^+)=\pi_i(B\Sigma_\infty^+)=\pi_i^s.$$
