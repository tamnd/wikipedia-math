# Sheldon Axler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Sheldon Axler"}

**Sheldon Jay Axler** (born November 6, 1949, [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia)) is an American mathematician and textbook author. He is a professor of mathematics and the Dean of the College of Science and Engineering at [San Francisco State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_State_University).

:::

He graduated from [Miami Palmetto Senior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Palmetto_Senior_High_School) in [Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami), [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida) in 1967. He obtained his AB in mathematics with highest honors at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) (1971) and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in mathematics, under professor [Donald Sarason](wikipedia://en/53777531), from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), with the dissertation "Subalgebras of $L^{\infty}$" in 1975. As a postdoc, he was a [C. L. E. Moore instructor](wikipedia://en/21957255) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).

He taught for many years and became a full professor at [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University). In 1997, Axler moved to [San Francisco State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_State_University), where he became the chair of the Mathematics Department.

Axler received the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) for expository writing in 1996 from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for a paper titled "Down with Determinants!" in which he shows how one can teach or learn linear algebra without the use of [determinants](wikipedia://en/8468).[^o3] Axler later wrote a textbook, *Linear Algebra Done Right* (4th ed. 2024), to the same effect.

In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o4] He was an associate editor of the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)* and the editor-in-chief of the [Mathematical Intelligencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Intelligencer).

## Books {#Books}

- *Linear Algebra Done Right*, fourth edition, [Undergraduate Texts in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/8871375), Springer, 2024.
- (with John E. McCarthy, and Donald Sarason) editors. *Holomorphic Spaces*, [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press) 1998.
- (with Paul Bourdon, and Wade Ramey) [*Harmonic Function Theory*, second edition](https://books.google.com/books/about/Harmonic_Function_Theory.html?id=wATLzBfup-wC), [Graduate Texts in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/7392980), Springer, 2001.
- *Harmonic Function Theory software*, a [Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica) package for symbolic manipulation of [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762), version 7.00, released 1 January 2009 (previous versions released in 1992, 1993, 1994, 1996, 1999, 2000, 2001, 2002, 2003, and 2008).
- *Precalculus: A Prelude to Calculus*, Wiley, 2009 (third printing, 2010).
- (with [Peter Rosenthal](wikipedia://en/43370076) and Donald Sarason) editors. [*A Glimpse at Hilbert Space Operators*](https://books.google.com/books/about/A_Glimpse_at_Hilbert_Space_Operators.html?id=8rcv8Od38McC), [Birkhäuser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkhäuser), 2010.
- *College Algebra*, [John Wiley & Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wiley_&_Sons) 2011.
- *Algebra & Trigonometry*, John Wiley & Sons, January 2011.
- *Measure, Integration & Real Analysis* (open access, updated 2020), Springer, November 2019.
