# Cluster prime

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cluster prime"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **cluster prime** is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$ such that every even positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *k* ≤ p − 3 can be written as the difference between two prime numbers not exceeding $p$ (). For example, the number [23](wikipedia://en/362203) is a cluster prime because 23 − 3 = 20, and every even integer from 2 to 20, inclusive, is the difference of at least one pair of prime numbers not exceeding 23:

:::

- 5 − 3 = 2
- 7 − 3 = 4
- 11 − 5 = 6
- 11 − 3 = 8
- 13 − 3 = 10
- 17 − 5 = 12
- 17 − 3 = 14
- 19 − 3 = 16
- 23 − 5 = 18
- 23 − 3 = 20

On the other hand, [149](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/149_%28number%29) is not a cluster prime because 140 \< 146, and there is no way to write 140 as the difference of two primes that are less than or equal to 149.

By convention, 2 is not considered to be a cluster prime. The first 23 odd primes (up to 89) are all cluster primes. The first few odd primes that are not cluster primes are

[97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/97_%28number%29), [127](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/127_%28number%29), [149](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/149_%28number%29), [191](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/191_%28number%29), [211](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/211_%28number%29), [223](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/223_%28number%29), [227](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/227_%28number%29), [229](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/229_%28number%29), ...

It is not known if there are infinitely many cluster primes.

## Properties {#Properties}

- The [prime gap](wikipedia://en/5075259) preceding a cluster prime is always six or less. For any given prime number $n$, let $p_n$ denote the n-th prime number. If ${{p_{n} - p_{n - 1}}}$ ≥ 8, then $p_n$ − 9 cannot be expressed as the difference of two primes not exceeding $p_n$; thus, $p_n$ is not a cluster prime.
  - The converse is not true: the smallest non-cluster prime that is the greater of a pair of gap length six or less is [227](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/227_%28number%29), a gap of only four between 223 and 227. 229 is the first non-cluster prime that is the greater of a [twin prime](wikipedia://en/41997) pair.
- The set of cluster primes is a [small set](wikipedia://en/3152856). In 1999, Richard Blecksmith proved that the sum of the [reciprocals](wikipedia://en/229940) of the cluster primes is finite.[^o5]
- Blecksmith also proved an explicit [upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) on C(x), the number of cluster primes less than or equal to x. Specifically, for any positive integer $m$: $C(x) < {x \over ln(x)^m}$ for all [sufficiently large](wikipedia://en/10077292) x.
  - It follows from this that [almost all](wikipedia://en/1309) prime numbers are absent from the set of cluster primes.

:::
