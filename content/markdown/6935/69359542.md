# Zhuo Qun Song

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Zhuo Qun Song"}

**Zhuo Qun Song** (; born 1997), also called **Alex Song**, is a Canadian who was from 2015 to 2026, the most highly decorated [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) (IMO) contestant, with five gold medals and one bronze medal. He was surpassed by [Alex Chui](wikipedia://en/84076399) in July 2026.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Song was born in [Tianjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianjin), China in 1997.[^o5] He and his parents moved to Canada in 2002.[^o5] Song was brought up in [Waterloo, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterloo,_Ontario).[^o6][^o7]

Song was interested in mathematics at a very young age where he started participating in competitions in Grade 1. By Grade 4, Song was participating in competitions such as the [Canadian Open Mathematics Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Open_Mathematics_Challenge) and the [American Mathematics Competitions](wikipedia://en/665435). In Grade 5, Song became interested in solving Olympiad type questions and started training to solve them.[^o5]

In 2011, Song moved to the United States to attend [Phillips Exeter Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Exeter_Academy).[^o7]

## International Mathematical Olympiad {#International_Mathematical_Olympiad}

In 2010, when Song was in the seventh grade, he represented [Vincent Massey Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Massey_Secondary_School) in the [Canadian Mathematical Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Mathematical_Olympiad) where he finished first place.[^o5][^o9]

In the same year, Song represented Canada in the 2010 IMO where he won a bronze medal.[^o9] He would continue to represent Canada for 5 subsequent IMOs where he obtained a gold medal each time. He obtained a perfect score on his final run in 2015, the only contestant to do so that year.[^o6][^o7][^o10] The performances made Song the most decorated contestant of all time until 2026.[^o11][^o12] In 2015, Song was also one of the twelve top scorers of the [United States of America Mathematical Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America_Mathematical_Olympiad), representing Phillips Exeter Academy.[^o13]

## Post-IMO {#Post-IMO}

Song graduated from Phillips Exeter Academy in 2015.[^o6][^o7]

Song attended [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) where he graduated in 2019 with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in Mathematics.[^o24]

During his time at Princeton, Song was part of the team that participated in the [Putnam Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition). His team won second place in 2016[^o25] and third place in 2017.[^o26]

Song interned as a trader at [Jane Street Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Street_Capital) during the summers of 2017 and 2018. Song was previously a Quantitative Researcher at [Citadel LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel_LLC).[^o27] As of 2022, he is a graduate student at the [University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana–Champaign).[^o28] He also has been lead coach for the Canadian IMO team since 2020.[^o29]
