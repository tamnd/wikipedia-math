# Nick Day (statistician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nick Day"}

**Nicholas Edward Day**, [CBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBE), [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (born 24 September 1939)[^o2] is a retired [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [cancer epidemiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_epidemiology).[^o2]

:::

## Education {#Education}

He was educated at [Gresham's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham's_School) and the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), from 1958-1962, where he gained a B.A. in Mathematics and a Diploma in Statistics, and the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen) from 1962-1966, where he obtained a Doctorate of Philosophy.

## Career {#Career}

Day worked at the [International Agency for Research on Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Agency_for_Research_on_Cancer) in [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon) from 1969 to 1986, where he rose to become head of the Unit of [Biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) and [Field Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_study).[^o2] He was director of the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_%28UK%29) Biostatistics Unit from 1986 to 1989, and continued as honorary director until 1999.[^o2] From 1997 until his retirement in 2004 he was co-director of the [Strangeways Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangeways_Research_Laboratory) in Cambridge.[^o5] He was also professor of [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) from 1989 to 1999, and professor of [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997) from 1999 until 2004.[^o2]

Day was made a [Commander of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBE) in the [2001 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_New_Year_Honours) for services to statistics and epidemiology underpinning cancer biology.[^o6]

His hobbies include [sea fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_angling) and tree growing.[^o2]
