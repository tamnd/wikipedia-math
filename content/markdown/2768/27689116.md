# Huzihiro Araki

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Huzihiro Araki"}

**Huzihiro Araki** was a Japanese [mathematical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physicist) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked on the foundations of [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), on [quantum statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/712450), and on the theory of [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987).[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Araki is the son of the [University of Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kyoto) physics professor Gentarō Araki, with whom he studied and with whom in 1954 he published his first physics paper. He earned his diploma under [Hideki Yukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Yukawa) and in 1960 he attained his doctorate at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) with thesis advisors [Rudolf Haag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Haag) and [Arthur Wightman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Wightman).[^o7] He was a professor at the [University of Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kyoto) starting in 1966, and became the director of the Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences (RIMS).

Araki died on 16 December 2022.[^o4]

## Research {#Research}

Araki worked on [axiomatic quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/3559472), [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), and in particular on applications of [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987) like [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185) and [C\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/7184). At the beginning of the 1960s, in Princeton, he made important contributions to [local quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_quantum_physics) and to the [scattering theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_theory) of Haag and [David Ruelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ruelle). He also supplied important contributions in the mathematical theory of operator algebras, classifying the type-III factors of von Neumann algebras.[^o9] Araki originated the concept of relative entropy of states of von Neumann algebras. In the 1970s he showed the equivalence in [quantum thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_thermodynamics) of, on the one hand, the [KMS condition](wikipedia://en/2834214) (named after [Ryogo Kubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryogo_Kubo), Paul C. Martin, and [Julian Schwinger](wikipedia://en/269980)) for the characterization of quantum mechanical states in [thermodynamic equilibrium](wikipedia://en/265823) with, on the other hand, the [variational principle](wikipedia://en/491097) for quantum mechanical spin systems on lattices.[^o10] With Yanase he worked on the foundations of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), i.e. the [Wigner-Araki-Yanase theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigner-Araki-Yanase_theorem), which describes restrictions that [conservation laws](wikipedia://en/6956) impose upon the physical measuring process.[^o11] Stated in more precise terms, they proved that an exact measurement of an operator, which additively replaces the operator with a conserved size, is impossible. However, Yanase did prove that the uncertainty of the measurement can be made arbitrarily small, provided that the measuring apparatus is sufficiently large.[^o12]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Huzihiro Araki was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1970 in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) and in 1978 in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki).[^o14] He was the second president of the [International Association of Mathematical Physics](wikipedia://en/23897340), during the period 1979–1981.[^o15] In 2003 he received, together with [Oded Schramm](wikipedia://en/9605580) and [Elliott Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Lieb), the [Henri Poincaré Prize](wikipedia://en/4890712).[^o16] In 1990 he was the chief organizer of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Kyoto. He was editor of the scientific journal *[Communications in Mathematical Physics](wikipedia://en/23886833)*[^o17] and founder of *[Reviews in Mathematical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reviews_in_Mathematical_Physics)*.[^o18] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o19]
