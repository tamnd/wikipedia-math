# Fundamental vector field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fundamental vector field"}

In the study of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and especially of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), **fundamental vector fields** are instruments that describe the infinitesimal behaviour of a smooth [Lie group action](wikipedia://en/39904936) on a [smooth manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219#Definition). Such [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641#Vector_fields_on_manifolds) find important applications in the study of [Lie theory](wikipedia://en/1595681), [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), and the study of [Hamiltonian group actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_map#Hamiltonian_group_actions).

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

Important to applications in mathematics and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939)[^o3] is the notion of a [flow](wikipedia://en/2619735) on a manifold. In particular, if $M$ is a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) and $X$ is a smooth [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641#Vector_fields_on_manifolds), one is interested in finding [integral curves](wikipedia://en/1322061) to $X$. More precisely, given $p \in M$ one is interested in curves $\gamma_p: \mathbb R \to M$ such that:

$$\gamma_p'(t) = X_{\gamma_p(t)}, \qquad \gamma_p(0) = p,$$

for which local solutions are guaranteed by the [Existence and Uniqueness Theorem of Ordinary Differential Equations](wikipedia://en/32742753#Existence_and_uniqueness_of_solutions). If $X$ is furthermore a [complete vector field](wikipedia://en/62641#Complete_vector_fields), then the flow of $X$, defined as the collection of all integral curves for $X$, is a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) of $M$. The flow $\phi_X: \mathbb R \times M \to M$ given by $\phi_X(t,p) = \gamma_p(t)$ is in fact an [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of the additive [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $(\mathbb R,+)$ on $M$.

Conversely, every smooth action $A:\mathbb R \times M \to M$ defines a complete vector field $X$ via the equation:

$$X_p = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} A(t,p).$$

It is then a simple result[^o4] that there is a bijective correspondence between $\mathbb R$-actions on $M$ and complete vector fields on $M$.

In the language of flow theory, the vector field $X$ is called the *infinitesimal generator*.[^o5] Intuitively, the behaviour of the flow at each point corresponds to the "direction" indicated by the vector field. It is a natural question to ask whether one may establish a similar correspondence between vector fields and more arbitrary Lie group actions on $M$.

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $G$ be a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) with corresponding [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944#Relation_to_Lie_groups) $\mathfrak g$. Furthermore, let $M$ be a smooth manifold endowed with a [smooth action](wikipedia://en/39904936) $A : G \times M \to M$. Denote the map $A_p: G \to M$ such that $A_p(g) = A(g,p)$, called the *orbit map of $A$ corresponding to $p$*.[^o8] For $X \in \mathfrak g$, the fundamental vector field $X^\#$ corresponding to $X$ is given by any of the following equivalent definitions:[^o4][^o8][^o9]

- $$X^\#_p = d_e A_p(X)$$
- $$X^\#_p = d_{(e,p)}A\left(X,0_{T_p M}\right)$$
- $$X^\#_p = \left. \frac{d}{dt} \right|_{t=0} A\left( \exp(tX), p \right)$$

where $d$ is the [differential of a smooth map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_of_a_smooth_map) and $0_{T_pM}$ is the [zero vector](wikipedia://en/1453977) in the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $T_p M$.

The map $\mathfrak g \to \Gamma(TM), X \mapsto -X^\#$ can then be shown to be a [Lie algebra homomorphism](wikipedia://en/17944#Homomorphisms,_subalgebras,_and_ideals).[^o9]

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

### Lie groups {#Lie_groups}

The Lie algebra of a Lie group $G$ may be identified with either the left- or right-invariant vector fields on $G$. It is a well-known result[^o5] that such vector fields are isomorphic to $T_e G$, the tangent space at identity. In fact, if we let $G$ act on itself via right-multiplication, the corresponding fundamental vector fields are precisely the left-invariant vector fields.

### Hamiltonian group actions {#Hamiltonian_group_actions}

In the [motivation](wikipedia://en/3946232#Motivation), it was shown that there is a bijective correspondence between smooth $\mathbb R$-actions and complete vector fields. Similarly, given a [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) $(M,\omega)$, there is a bijective correspondence between symplectic actions (the induced [diffeomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphisms) are all [symplectomorphisms](wikipedia://en/312304)) and complete [symplectic vector fields](wikipedia://en/1818385).

A closely related idea is that of [Hamiltonian vector fields](wikipedia://en/1818270). Given a symplectic manifold $(M,\omega)$, we say that $X_H$ is a Hamiltonian vector field if there exists a [smooth function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) $H: M \to \mathbb R$ satisfying

$$dH = \iota_{X_H}\omega$$

where the map $\iota$ is the [interior product](wikipedia://en/2154963). This motivates the definition of a *Hamiltonian group action* as follows: If $G$ is a Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ and $A: G\times M \to M$ is a group action of $G$ on a smooth manifold $M$, then we say that $A$ is a Hamiltonian group action if there exists a [moment map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_map) $\mu: M \to \mathfrak g^*$ such that for each: $X \in \mathfrak g$,

$$d\mu^X = \iota_{X^\#}\omega,$$

where $\mu^X:M \to \mathbb R, p \mapsto \langle \mu(p),X \rangle$ and $X^\#$ is the fundamental vector field of $X$.

:::
