# Frank W. Lewis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frank W. Lewis"}

**Frank Waring Lewis** (August 25, 1912 – November 18, 2010) was an American [cryptographer](wikipedia://en/7088) and [cryptic crossword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptic_crossword) compiler.[^o2] His puzzles were printed in *[The Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nation)* for over 60 years, for a total of 2,962 puzzles.[^o2] [Leonard Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Bernstein), [Kurt Vonnegut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Vonnegut), and [Katha Pollitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katha_Pollitt) were listed among the fans of his puzzles.[^o3]

:::

## Personal life and career {#Personal_life_and_career}

Lewis was born on August 25, 1912, in [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City), [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah).[^o2] His father was from [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England).[^o2]

Lewis attended secretarial school and the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah), from which he later earned a degree in absentia.[^o2] He passed the federal [civil service test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_civil_service), and earned a master's degree in music from [The Catholic University of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Catholic_University_of_America) in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.)[^o2]

In Washington, Lewis worked at government secretarial jobs.[^o2] Just before [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Lewis was approached by Col. [William F. Friedman](wikipedia://en/175115), head of the [U.S. Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army)'s [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) section, who was looking for "very smart" people.[^o2] Lewis was hired as a civilian and helped break the code used to coordinate Japanese ships.[^o2] After the war, he joined the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) (NSA).[^o2] He was awarded the [Decoration for Exceptional Civilian Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decoration_for_Exceptional_Civilian_Service) by both the [War Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_War) and the NSA.[^o3] He may be only person to have won two such medals.[^o3] Much of his work remains classified.[^o5]

In 1969, he retired with his wife to the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean), but they relocated to [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts) after the eruption of the [Montserrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montserrat) volcano.[^o6]

He died on November 18, 2010, at age 98. He was survived by his wife of 74 years, Sylvia (*[née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Née)* Shosteck) (October 7, 1915 - August 11, 2013).[^o2] They had six children.[^o7][^o8]

## Puzzles {#Puzzles}

Lewis discovered British-style cryptic crosswords while stationed during the war at the [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037) code-breaking station in England.[^o5] Lewis took over as *The Nation**s puzzle setter in 1947.[^o2][^o10] When*****The Nation*started running his puzzles every other week instead of weekly starting in 2008, the public outcry was so great it resumed printing the puzzles weekly.[^o2][^o10] Lewis published his last puzzle in*The Nation*in December 2009, after which the magazine began reprinting old ones.[^o2] After Lewis's death, the magazine continued to reprint old puzzles while it searched for a new puzzle setter.[^o2]***
