# Adhémar Barré de Saint-Venant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adhémar Barré de Saint-Venant"}

**Adhémar Jean Claude Barré de Saint-Venant** (; 23 August 1797 – 6 January 1886)[^o3] was a [mechanician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanician) and mathematician who contributed to early [stress analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_analysis) and also developed the unsteady open channel flow [shallow water equations](wikipedia://en/8631522), also known as the Saint-Venant equations that are a fundamental set of equations used in modern [hydraulic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_engineering). The [one-dimensional Saint-Venant equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-dimensional_Saint-Venant_equation) is a commonly used simplification of the shallow water equations. Although his full surname was Barré de Saint-Venant, in mathematical literature other than French he is known as Saint-Venant. His name is also associated with [Saint-Venant's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Venant's_principle) of statically equivalent systems of load, [Saint-Venant's theorem](wikipedia://en/14272194) and for [Saint-Venant's compatibility condition](wikipedia://en/11167824), the integrability conditions for a symmetric tensor field to be a strain.

:::

In 1843 he published the correct derivation of the [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395) for a [viscous flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscous_flow)[^o4] and was the first to "properly identify the [coefficient of viscosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficient_of_viscosity) and its role as a multiplying factor for the velocity gradients in the flow". Even though he published before [Stokes](wikipedia://en/212838), the equations do not bear his name.

Barré de Saint-Venant developed a version of [vector calculus](wikipedia://en/32640) similar to that of [Grassmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassmann) (now understood as exterior [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519)) which he published in 1845.[^o5] A dispute arose between Saint-Venant and Grassmann over priority for this invention. Grassmann had published his results in 1844, but Barré de Saint-Venant claimed he had developed the method in 1832.

Barré de Saint-Venant was born at the château de Fortoiseau, Villiers-en-Bière, Seine-et-Marne, France. His mother was Marie-Thérèse Josèphe Laborie (born Saint-Domingue, 1769). His father was Jean Barré de Saint-Venant, (1737–1810), a colonial officer of the Isle of [Saint-Domingue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Domingue) (later [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti)). Barré de Saint-Venant would follow in his father's footsteps in science, entering the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique), in 1813 at sixteen years old, and studying under [Gay-Lussac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay-Lussac). Graduating in 1816 he worked for the next 27 years as an engineer, initially his passion for chemistry led him to a post as a *élève-commissaire* (candidate commissioner) for the [Service des Poudres et Salpêtres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_des_Poudres_et_Salpêtres) (Powders and Saltpeter Service) and then as a civil engineer for the [Corps des Ponts et Chaussées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps_of_Bridges_and_Roads_%28France%29). He married in 1837, Rohaut Fleury from Paris. Following a disagreement on the issue of a certain road with the Municipal Administration of Paris, he was suddenly retired as "Chief Engineer, second class", on 1 April 1848. In 1850 Saint-Venant won a contest to be appointed the chair of Agricultural Engineering at the Agronomic Institute of Versailles, a post he occupied for two years.[^o7]

He went on to teach mathematics at the [École des Ponts et Chaussées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_des_Ponts_et_Chaussées) (National school of Civil Engineering) where he succeeded [Coriolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspard-Gustave_Coriolis).

In 1868, at 71 years old, he was elected to succeed [Poncelet](wikipedia://en/764818) in the mechanics section of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences), and continued research work for a further 18 years. He died in January 1886 at [Saint-Ouen, Loir-et-Cher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Ouen,_Loir-et-Cher). Sources differ on his date of death:[^o6] gives 6 January whereas[^o7] 22 January. In 1869 he was given the title 'Count' ([comte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count)) by [Pope Pius IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX).
