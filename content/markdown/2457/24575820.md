# Idealizer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Idealizer"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the **idealizer** of a subsemigroup *T* of a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) *S* is the largest subsemigroup of *S* in which *T* is an [ideal](wikipedia://en/27799#Subsemigroups_and_ideals).[^n1] Such an idealizer is given by

:::

$$\mathbb{I}_S(T)=\{s\in S \mid sT\subseteq T \text{ and } Ts\subseteq T\}.$$

In [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), if *A* is an additive subgroup of a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R*, then $\mathbb{I}_R(A)$ (defined in the multiplicative semigroup of *R*) is the largest subring of *R* in which *A* is a two-sided ideal.[^n2][^n3]

In [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944), if *L* is a [Lie ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_ring) (or [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944)) with Lie product \[*x*,*y*\], and *S* is an additive subgroup of *L*, then the set

$$\{r\in L\mid [r,S]\subseteq S\}$$

is classically called the **[normalizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalizer)** of *S*, however it is apparent that this set is actually the Lie ring equivalent of the idealizer. It is not necessary to specify that \[*S*,*r*\] ⊆ *S*, because [anticommutativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommutativity) of the Lie product causes \[*s*,*r*\] = −\[*r*,*s*\] ∈ *S*. The Lie "normalizer" of *S* is the largest subring of *L* in which *S* is a Lie ideal.

## Comments {#Comments}

Often, when right or left ideals are the additive subgroups of *R* of interest, the idealizer is defined more simply by taking advantage of the fact that multiplication by ring elements is already absorbed on one side. Explicitly,

$$\mathbb{I}_R(T)=\{r\in R \mid rT\subseteq T \}$$

if *T* is a right ideal, or

$$\mathbb{I}_R(L)=\{r\in R \mid Lr\subseteq L \}$$

if *L* is a left ideal.

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), the idealizer is related to a more general construction. Given a commutative ring *R*, and given two subsets *A* and *B* of a right [*R*-module](wikipedia://en/276410) *M*, the **conductor** or **transporter** is given by

$$(A:B):=\{r\in R \mid Br\subseteq A\}.$$

In terms of this conductor notation, an additive subgroup *B* of *R* has idealizer

$$\mathbb{I}_R(B)=(B:B).$$

When *A* and *B* are ideals of *R*, the conductor is part of the structure of the [residuated lattice](wikipedia://en/2483542) of ideals of *R*.

**Examples**

The [multiplier algebra](wikipedia://en/13542720) *M*(*A*) of a [C\*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_star_algebra) *A* is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the idealizer of *π*(*A*) where *π* is any faithful nondegenerate representation of *A* on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) *H*.
