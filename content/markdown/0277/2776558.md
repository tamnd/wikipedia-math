# Degree of an algebraic variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Degree of an algebraic variety"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **degree** of an [affine](wikipedia://en/320468) or [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) of [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) $n$ is the number of intersection points of the variety with $n$ [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736).[^o2] For an [algebraic set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_set), the intersection points must be counted with their [intersection multiplicity](wikipedia://en/19211790#Intersection_multiplicity), because of the possibility of multiple components. For (irreducible) varieties, if one takes into account the multiplicities and, in the affine case, the points at infinity, the hypothesis of *general position* may be replaced by the much weaker condition that the intersection of the variety has the dimension zero (that is, consists of a finite number of points). This is a generalization of [Bézout's theorem](wikipedia://en/149217). (For a proof, see Hilbert series and Hilbert polynomial.)

:::

The degree is not an intrinsic property of the variety, as it depends on a specific embedding of the variety in an affine or projective space.

The degree of a [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) is equal to the [total degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_degree) of its defining equation. A generalization of [Bézout's theorem](wikipedia://en/149217) asserts that, if an intersection of $n$ projective hypersurfaces has codimension $n$, then the degree of the intersection is the product of the degrees of the hypersurfaces.

The degree of a projective variety is the evaluation at $1$ of the numerator of the [Hilbert series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_series) of its [coordinate ring](wikipedia://en/23833909). It follows that, given the equations of the variety, the degree may be computed from a [Gröbner basis](wikipedia://en/357328) of the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of these equations.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For *V* embedded in a [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) *P*<sup>*n*</sup> and defined over some [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) *K*, the degree *d* of *V* is the number of points of intersection of *V*, defined over *K*, with a [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) *L* in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736), such that

$$\dim(V) + \dim(L) = n.$$

Here dim(*V*) is the [dimension](wikipedia://en/253251) of *V*, and the [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149) of *L* will be equal to that dimension. The degree *d* is an extrinsic quantity, and not intrinsic as a property of *V*. For example, the [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578) has an (essentially unique) [embedding of degree *n*](wikipedia://en/2380117) in *P*<sup>*n*</sup>.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The degree of a [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) *F* = 0 is the same as the [total degree](wikipedia://en/357416) of the [homogeneous polynomial](wikipedia://en/1698977) *F* defining it (granted, in case *F* has repeated factors, that intersection theory is used to count intersections with [multiplicity](wikipedia://en/19211790), as in [Bézout's theorem](wikipedia://en/149217)).

If two varieties *Y* and *Z* intersect transversally, then the degree of their intersection is the product of their degrees: *deg Y ∩ Z = (deg Y)(deg Z)*.

:::

## Other approaches {#Other_approaches}

For a more sophisticated approach, the [linear system of divisors](wikipedia://en/1186886) defining the embedding of *V* can be related to the [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) or [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859) defining the embedding by its space of sections. The [tautological line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_line_bundle) on *P*<sup>*n*</sup> pulls back to *V*. The degree determines the first [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349). The degree can also be computed in the [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) of *P*<sup>*n*</sup>, or [Chow ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_ring), with the class of a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) intersecting the class of *V* an appropriate number of times.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Extending Bézout's theorem"}

## Extending Bézout's theorem {#Extending_Bézout's_theorem}

The degree can be used to generalize Bézout's theorem in an expected way to intersections of *n* hypersurfaces in *P*<sup>*n*</sup>.

:::

## Notes {#Notes}

[^o2]: In the affine case, the general-position hypothesis implies that there is no intersection point at infinity.
