# Subnormal subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Subnormal subgroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) *H* of a given [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* is a **subnormal subgroup** of *G* if there is a finite chain of subgroups of the group, each one [normal](wikipedia://en/21918) in the next, beginning at *H* and ending at *G*.

:::

In notation, $H$ is $k$-subnormal in $G$ if there are subgroups

$$H=H_0,H_1,H_2,\ldots, H_k=G$$

of $G$ such that $H_i$ is normal in $H_{i+1}$ for each $i$.

A subnormal subgroup is a subgroup that is $k$-subnormal for some positive integer $k$. Some facts about subnormal subgroups:

- A 1-subnormal subgroup is a proper normal subgroup (and vice versa).
- A [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042) is [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/144569) if and only if each of its subgroups is subnormal.
- Every [quasinormal subgroup](wikipedia://en/2774267), and, more generally, every [conjugate-permutable subgroup](wikipedia://en/3247924), of a finite group is subnormal.
- Every [pronormal subgroup](wikipedia://en/3593621) that is also subnormal, is normal. In particular, a [Sylow subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_subgroup) is subnormal if and only if it is normal.
- Every 2-subnormal subgroup is a conjugate-permutable subgroup.

The property of subnormality is [transitive](wikipedia://en/200463), that is, a subnormal subgroup of a subnormal subgroup is subnormal. The relation of subnormality can be defined as the [transitive closure](wikipedia://en/292239) of the relation of normality.

If every subnormal subgroup of *G* is normal in *G*, then *G* is called a [T-group](wikipedia://en/10545261).
