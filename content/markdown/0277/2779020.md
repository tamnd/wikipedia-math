# Quarter-pixel motion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quarter-pixel motion"}

**Quarter-pixel motion** *(also known as **Q-pel motion** or **Qpel motion**)* refers to using a quarter of the distance between [pixels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixel) (or luma sample positions) as the [motion vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_vector) precision for [motion estimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_estimation) and [motion compensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_compensation) in [video compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_compression) schemes. It is used in many modern video coding formats such as [MPEG-4 ASP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPEG-4_ASP) and [H.264/AVC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.264/AVC).[^o2] Though higher precision motion vectors take more bits to encode, they can sometimes result in more efficient compression overall, by increasing the quality of the prediction signal.

:::

## Operation {#Operation}

Video encoding software products such as [Xvid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xvid), 3ivx, and [DivX Pro Codec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DivX_Pro_Codec), which are based upon the [MPEG-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPEG-4) specification, use [motion estimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_estimation) algorithms to significantly improve video compression. The default level of resolution for motion estimation for most MPEG-4 ASP implementations is half a pixel, although quarter pixel is specified under the standard. H.264 decoders always support quarter-pixel motion. Quarter-pixel resolution can improve the quality of the video prediction signal as compared to half-pixel resolution, although the improvement may not always be enough to offset the increased bit cost of the quarter-pixel-precision motion vector; additional techniques such as [rate–distortion optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate–distortion_optimization), which takes both quality and bit cost into account, are used to significantly improve the effectiveness of quarter-pel motion estimation.

## Interpolation methods {#Interpolation_methods}

Quarter-pixel [motion compensation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_compensation), much like half-pixel, is achieved through [interpolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpolation_%28computer_graphics%29). Different specific schemes are used in different designs:

- [VC-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VC-1) uses [bicubic interpolation](wikipedia://en/1396397).
- H.264/AVC uses a 6-tap filter for half-pixel interpolation and then simple [linear interpolation](wikipedia://en/160010) to achieve quarter-pixel precision from the half-pixel data.
- HEVC uses separable 7-tap or 8-tap filtering.

## Hardware compatibility in MPEG-4 ASP {#Hardware_compatibility_in_MPEG-4_ASP}

Videos encoded with quarter-pixel precision motion vectors require up to twice as much processing power to encode, and 30-60% more processing power to decode. As a result, to enable wider hardware compatibility, Qpel is disabled in the default DivX encoding profiles. However, with newer stand-alone players supporting more complex formats such as [VC-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VC-1) and [H.264](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.264), Qpel support in MPEG-4 ASP has become more common.

## Video formats that support quarter-pixel motion compensation {#Video_formats_that_support_quarter-pixel_motion_compensation}

- [HEVC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HEVC)
- [H.264/AVC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.264/AVC)
- [MPEG-4 ASP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPEG-4_ASP)
- [VC-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VC-1)
- [VP7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VP7)
