# Levi decomposition

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Levi decomposition"}

**Levi decomposition.**

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Levi decomposition"}

In [Lie theory](wikipedia://en/1595681) and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), the **Levi decomposition**, conjectured by [Wilhelm Killing](wikipedia://en/1531824)[^o3] and [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274)[^o4] and proved by (txt), states that any finite-dimensional [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) *g* over a field of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero is the [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of a [solvable](wikipedia://en/2966308) ideal and a [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1337587) subalgebra. One is its [**radical**](wikipedia://en/11484689), a maximal solvable ideal, and the other is a semisimple subalgebra, called a **Levi subalgebra**.

:::

When viewed as a factor-algebra of *g*, this semisimple Lie algebra is also called the **Levi factor** of *g*. To a certain extent, the decomposition can be used to reduce problems about finite-dimensional Lie algebras and Lie groups to separate problems about Lie algebras in these two special classes, solvable and semisimple.

Moreover, [Malcev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcev) (1942) showed that any two Levi subalgebras are [conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugacy) by an (inner) automorphism of the form

$$\exp(\mathrm{ad}(z))\$$

where *z* is in the [nilradical](wikipedia://en/22468313) (**Levi–Malcev theorem**).

An analogous result is valid for [associative algebras](wikipedia://en/2112) and is called the [Wedderburn principal theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedderburn_principal_theorem).

## Extensions of the results {#Extensions_of_the_results}

In representation theory, Levi decomposition of [parabolic subgroups](wikipedia://en/588356) of a reductive group is needed to construct a large family of the so-called [parabolically induced](wikipedia://en/11118768) representations. The [Langlands decomposition](wikipedia://en/11085278) is a slight refinement of the Levi decomposition for parabolic subgroups used in this context.

Analogous statements hold for simply connected [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), and, as shown by [George Mostow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mostow), for algebraic Lie algebras and simply connected [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) over a field of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero.

There is no analogue of the Levi decomposition for most infinite-dimensional Lie algebras; for example [affine Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1350865) have a radical consisting of their center, but cannot be written as a semidirect product of the center and another Lie algebra. The Levi decomposition also fails for finite-dimensional algebras over fields of positive characteristic.
