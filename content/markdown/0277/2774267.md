# Quasinormal subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasinormal subgroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **quasinormal subgroup**, or **permutable subgroup**, is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) that [commutes](wikipedia://en/294390) (permutes) with every other subgroup with respect to the [product of subgroups](wikipedia://en/24734#Product_of_subgroups). The term *quasinormal subgroup* was introduced by [Øystein Ore](wikipedia://en/1643733) in 1937.

:::

Two subgroups are said to permute (or commute) if any element from the first subgroup, times an element of the second subgroup, can be written as an element of the second subgroup, times an element of the first subgroup. That is, $H$ and $K$ as subgroups of $G$ are said to commute if *HK* = *KH*, that is, any element of the form $hk$ with $h \in H$ and $k \in K$ can be written in the form $k'h'$ where $k' \in K$ and $h' \in H$.

Every [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) is quasinormal, because a normal subgroup commutes with every element of the group. The converse is not true. For instance, any [extension of a cyclic $p$-group by another cyclic $p$-group](wikipedia://en/7515561) for the same (odd) prime has the property that all its subgroups are quasinormal. However, not all of its subgroups need be normal.

Every quasinormal subgroup is a [modular subgroup](wikipedia://en/3593883), that is, a modular element in the [lattice of subgroups](wikipedia://en/3983172). This follows from the [modular property of groups](wikipedia://en/3983172#Properties). If all subgroups are quasinormal, then the group is called an [Iwasawa group](wikipedia://en/16355158)—sometimes also called a *modular group*,[^o2] although this latter term has other meanings.

In any group, every quasinormal subgroup is [ascendant](wikipedia://en/4966647).

A [conjugate permutable subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_permutable_subgroup) is one that commutes with all its conjugate subgroups. Every quasinormal subgroup is conjugate permutable.

## In finite groups {#In_finite_groups}

Every quasinormal subgroup of a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) is a [subnormal subgroup](wikipedia://en/2774415). This follows from the somewhat stronger statement that every conjugate permutable subgroup is subnormal, which in turn follows from the statement that every maximal conjugate permutable subgroup is normal. (The finiteness is used crucially in the proofs.)

In summary, a subgroup *H* of a finite group *G* is permutable in *G* if and only if *H* is both modular and subnormal in *G*.[^o2][^o4]

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="PT-group"}

## PT-groups {#PT-groups}

Permutability is not a [transitive relation](wikipedia://en/200463) in general. The groups in which permutability is transitive are called PT-groups, by analogy with [T-groups](wikipedia://en/10545261) in which normality is transitive.[^o7]
