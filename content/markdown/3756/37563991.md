# Log semiring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Log semiring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [tropical analysis](wikipedia://en/59158120), the **log-semiring** is the [semiring](wikipedia://en/371468) structure on the [logarithmic scale](wikipedia://en/164055), obtained by considering the [extended real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_real_numbers) as [logarithms](wikipedia://en/17860). That is, the operations of addition and multiplication are defined by [conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugation_%28group_theory%29): [exponentiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponentiate) the real numbers, obtaining a positive (or zero) number, add or multiply these numbers with the ordinary [algebraic operations](wikipedia://en/5438924) on real numbers, and then take the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) to reverse the initial exponentiation. Such operations are also known as, e.g., **logarithmic addition**, etc. As usual in tropical analysis, the operations are denoted by ⊕ and ⊗ to distinguish them from the usual addition + and multiplication × (or ⋅). These operations depend on the choice of base $b$ for the exponent and logarithm ($b$ is a choice of [logarithmic unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_unit)), which corresponds to a scale factor, and are well-defined for any positive base other than 1; using a base $b < 1$ is equivalent to using a negative sign and using the inverse $1/b > 1$. If not qualified, the base is conventionally taken to be $e$ or $1/e$, which corresponds to $e$ with a negative.

:::

The log-semiring has the [tropical semiring](wikipedia://en/3892336) as limit ("[tropicalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropicalization)", "dequantization") as the base goes to infinity $b \to \infty$ ([max-plus semiring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-plus_semiring)) or to zero $b \to 0$ ([min-plus semiring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min-plus_semiring)), and thus can be viewed as a [deformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory) ("quantization") of the tropical semiring. Notably, the addition operation, *logadd* (for multiple terms, [LogSumExp](wikipedia://en/47422895)) can be viewed as a deformation of [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum) or [minimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum). The log-semiring has applications in [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033), since it replaces the non-smooth maximum and minimum by a smooth operation. The log-semiring also arises when working with numbers that are logarithms (measured on a [logarithmic scale](wikipedia://en/164055)), such as [decibels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decibel) (see Decibel), [log probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log_probability), or [log-likelihoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log-likelihood).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The operations on the log-semiring can be defined extrinsically by mapping them to the non-negative real numbers, doing the operations there, and mapping them back. The non-negative real numbers with the usual operations of addition and multiplication form a [semiring](wikipedia://en/371468) (there are no negatives), known as the [probability semiring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_semiring), so the log-semiring operations can be viewed as [pullbacks](wikipedia://en/9413508) of the operations on the probability semiring, and these are [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) as rings.

Formally, given the extended real numbers $\mathbf{R} \cup \{-\infty, +\infty$} and a positive base $b \ne 1$, one defines:

$$\begin{aligned} x \oplus_b y &= \log_b\left(b^x + b^y\right) \\ x \otimes_b y &= \log_b\left(b^x \times b^y\right) = \log_b\left(b^{x+y}\right) = x + y. \end{aligned}$$

Regardless of base, log-multiplication is the same as usual addition, $x \otimes_b y = x + y$, since logarithms take multiplication to addition; however, log-addition depends on base. The units for usual addition and multiplication are 0 and 1; accordingly, the unit for log-addition is $\log_b 0 = -\infty$ for $b > 1$ and $\log_b 0 = -\log_{1/b} 0 = +\infty$ for $b < 1$, and the unit for log-multiplication is $\log 1 = 0$, regardless of base.

More concisely, the unit log-semiring can be defined with the base $e$ as:

$$\begin{aligned} x \oplus y &= \log\left(e^x + e^y\right) \\ x \otimes y &= x + y. \end{aligned}$$

with additive unit $-\infty$ and multiplicative unit 0; this corresponds to the max convention.

The opposite convention is also common, and corresponds to the base $1/e$, the minimum convention:[^n1]

$$\begin{aligned} x \oplus y &= -\log\left(e^{-x} + e^{-y}\right) \\ x \otimes y &= x + y. \end{aligned}$$

with additive unit $+\infty$ and multiplicative unit 0.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

A log-semiring is in fact a [semifield](wikipedia://en/10417428), since all numbers other than the additive unit $-\infty$ (or $+\infty$) have a multiplicative inverse, given by $-x,$ since $x \otimes -x = \log_b(b^x \cdot b^{-x}) = \log_b (1) = 0.$ Thus, log-division ⊘ is well-defined, though log-subtraction ⊖ is not always defined.

A mean can be defined by log-addition and log-division (as the [quasi-arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/566869) corresponding to the exponent), as

$$M_\mathrm{lm}(x, y) := (x \oplus y) \oslash 2 = \log_b\bigl((b^x + b^y)/2\bigr) = \log_b (b^x + b^y) - \log_b 2 = (x \oplus y) - \log_b 2.$$

This is just addition shifted by $- \log_b 2,$ since log-division corresponds to linear subtraction.

A log-semiring has the usual Euclidean metric, which corresponds to the [logarithmic scale](wikipedia://en/164055) on the [positive real numbers](wikipedia://en/47172571).

Similarly, a log-semiring has the usual [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198), which is an [invariant measure](wikipedia://en/7635201) with respect to log-multiplication (usual addition, geometrically translation) which corresponds to the [logarithmic measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_measure) on the [probability semiring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_semiring).

:::
