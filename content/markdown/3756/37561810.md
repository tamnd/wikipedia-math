# Elliott Ward Cheney Jr.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elliott Ward Cheney Jr."}

**Elliott Ward Cheney Jr.** (June 28, 1929 – July 13, 2016) was an American mathematician and an emeritus professor at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin).[^o4] Known to his friends and colleagues as **Ward Cheney**, he was one of the pioneers in the fields of approximation theory and numerical analysis.[^o5] His 1966 book, *An Introduction to Approximation Theory*, remains in print and is "highly respected and well known", "a small book almost encyclopedic in character", and "is a classic with few competitors".[^o6][^o7]

:::

## Education and personal life {#Education_and_personal_life}

Cheney was born in [Gettysburg, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg,_Pennsylvania), to E. W. Cheney Sr., and Carleton (Pratt) Cheney. He was the second of two children. He grew up in [Washington, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_New_Jersey), and [Bethlehem, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_Pennsylvania).[^o9] Ward began clarinet studies at age ten and would play in chamber music groups throughout his life.[^o10]

Cheney was a 1947 graduate of Fountain Hill High School in Bethlehem, Pennsylvania. In 1951, he earned his bachelor's degree in mathematics from [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University), where his father was a physics professor. During undergraduate summers, Ward worked for the [United States Forest Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Forest_Service), where he met Elizabeth "Beth" Jean, whom he married in 1952. The young couple resided in [Lawrence, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas), while Ward studied and served as a mathematics instructor at [The University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Kansas), earning his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1957. Ward had three children with his first wife, Beth, all of whom earned doctorates: daughter Margaret is a professor of mathematics,[^o12] son David is a manager of international research projects,[^o13] and son Elliott is a professional cellist.[^o14] Their mother Beth remarried in 1975 and died in 1991. Ward and his wife Victoria had been together since 1983.

## Professional background {#Professional_background}

Following the launch of [Sputnik 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik_1) by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in 1957, the United States intensified its focus on its aerospace program. Cheney became a research scientist at [Convair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convair) Astronautics in [San Diego, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego,_California), where his mathematical team worked on calculations for the [Atlas rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_rocket)—which would take [John Glenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Glenn) into space.

Cheney also worked for Space Technology Laboratories (now [TRW Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRW_Inc.)), near Los Angeles, and taught at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA), with visiting positions at [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University) and [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University). In addition, Ward was a consultant and/or a guest worker at [Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing) Scientific Research Laboratories, [The Aerospace Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Aerospace_Corporation), and [IBM Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Research) Laboratory. In the summers of 1961–63, he was director of the NSF Summer Institute in Numerical Analysis at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles).[^o9]

In 1964, Ward joined the mathematics faculty of [The University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Texas_at_Austin), where he taught for the next 41 years, until his retirement at age 76.[^o4]

## Career and travel {#Career_and_travel}

Cheney served continuously on the editorial board of the [Journal of Approximation Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Approximation_Theory) from its inception in 1968 until sometime after the start of 2015, and published 14 papers there.[^o17][^o18]

Professor Cheney supervised 17 PhD students,[^o11] 35 Masters students, and worked with three post-doctoral students.[^o9] He was Associate Editor for ten mathematical journals as well as referee and reviewer for many other journals.[^o9] Cheney had over 100 published papers and was the author of two dozen mathematical textbooks, with several having multiple editions.[^o9] A reviewer wrote of his 1986 monograph: "Cheney's book scores highly on all ... points". Ward Cheney and Will Light wrote two graduate level books. Ward Cheney and David Kincaid co-authored two undergraduate textbooks, and a graduate textbook.

During his career, Ward frequently spent his summers and a sabbatical semester in England at [Lancaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster_University) and at [Leicester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_University). Also, he held a visiting professorship at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University), Sweden. Ward was a world-wide traveler and was frequently invited to give lectures on approximation theory at universities wherever he went.[^o9]

## Teaching and research {#Teaching_and_research}

Ward presented over 165 invited lectures and colloquium talks at universities and conferences around the world.[^o9] Special honors include an invited lectures at the *1963 National Meeting of the Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics (SIAM)*, in [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver), Colorado, and at the *1974 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842)*, in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver), Canada. Moreover, Professor Cheney was the honoree at the *1995 International Conference on Approximation Theory*, [College Station, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Station,_Texas).[^o9] For over 40 years, this has been the main general conference on approximation theory with presentations by international mathematician from academia, industry, and government.[^o20]

Cheney was awarded grants for his research on approximation theory from the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation), [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force) and [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army) as well as the [UK Research Councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Research_Councils) and the [Italian Scientific Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_%28Italy%29), among others.[^o9]

In 2012, Ward became a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), which was the first year the honor was awarded.[^o21]

Cheney died in July 2016, after having had [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease) for several years.
