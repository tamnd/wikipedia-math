# Stieltjes–Wigert polynomials

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stieltjes–Wigert polynomials"}

In mathematics, **Stieltjes–Wigert polynomials** (named after [Thomas Jan Stieltjes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jan_Stieltjes) and [Carl Severin Wigert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Severin_Wigert)) are a family of basic hypergeometric [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) in the basic [Askey scheme](wikipedia://en/32649081), for the weight function [^o2]

:::

$$w(x) = \frac{k}{\sqrt{\pi}} x^{-1/2} \exp(-k^2\log^2 x)$$

on the positive real line *x* > 0.

The [moment problem](wikipedia://en/1244992) for the Stieltjes–Wigert polynomials is indeterminate; in other words, there are many other measures giving the same family of orthogonal polynomials (see [Krein's condition](wikipedia://en/32876496)).

Koekoek et al. (2010) give in Section 14.27 a detailed list of the properties of these polynomials.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The polynomials are given in terms of [basic hypergeometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_hypergeometric_function) and the [Pochhammer symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pochhammer_symbol) by[^o5]

$$\displaystyle S_n(x;q) = \frac{1}{(q;q)_n}{}_1\phi_1(q^{-n},0;q,-q^{n+1}x),$$

where

$$q = \exp \left(-\frac{1}{2k^2} \right) .$$

:::

## Orthogonality {#Orthogonality}

Since the [moment problem](wikipedia://en/1244992) for these polynomials is indeterminate there are many different weight functions on \[0,∞\] for which they are orthogonal. Two examples of such weight functions are

$$\frac{1}{(-x,-qx^{-1};q)_\infty}$$

and

$$\frac{k}{\sqrt{\pi}} x^{-1/2} \exp \left(-k^2 \log^2 x \right) .$$
