# Teichmüller character

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Teichmüller character"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **Teichmüller character** $\omega$ (at a prime $p$) is a [character](wikipedia://en/691741) of $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$, where $q = p$ if $p$ is odd and $q = 4$ if $p = 2$, taking values in the roots of unity of the [*p*-adic integers](wikipedia://en/51423). It was introduced by [Oswald Teichmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Teichmüller). Identifying the roots of unity in the $p$-adic integers with the corresponding ones in the complex numbers, $\omega$ can be considered as a usual [Dirichlet character](wikipedia://en/174515) of conductor $q$. More generally, given a [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_%28topology%29) [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633) $O$ whose [residue field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residue_field_of_a_valuation) $k$ is [perfect](wikipedia://en/505088) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) $p$, there is a unique multiplicative [section](wikipedia://en/10630303) $\omega:k\to O$ of the natural surjection $O\to k$. The image of an element under this map is called its **Teichmüller representative**. The restriction of $\omega$ to $k^\times$ is called the **Teichmüller character**.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If $x$ is an integer mod $p$, then $\omega(x)$ is the unique solution of $\omega(x)^p = \omega(x)$ that is congruent to $x$ mod $p$. It can also be defined by

$$\omega(x)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} x^{p^n}$$

The multiplicative group of $p$-adic units is a product of the finite group of roots of unity and a group isomorphic to the $p$-adic integers. The finite group is cyclic of order $p-1$ or $2$, as $p$ is odd or even, respectively, and so it is isomorphic to $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times$. The Teichmüller character gives a canonical isomorphism between these two groups.

A detailed exposition of the construction of Teichmüller representatives for the $p$-adic integers, by means of [Hensel lifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hensel_lifting), is given in the article on [Witt vectors](wikipedia://en/5573710), where they provide an important role in providing a ring structure.
