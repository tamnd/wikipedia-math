# Wendel's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wendel's theorem"}

In [geometric probability](wikipedia://en/733613) theory, **Wendel's theorem**, named after James G. Wendel, gives the [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) that *N* points [distributed uniformly](wikipedia://en/1699223) at random on an [$n$-dimensional hypersphere](wikipedia://en/39782) all lie on the same "half" of the hypersphere. In other words, one seeks the probability that there is some [half-space](wikipedia://en/647297) with the origin on its boundary that contains all *N* points. Wendel's theorem says that the probability is[^o2]

:::

$$p_{n,N}=2^{-N+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{N-1}{k}.$$

The statement is equivalent to $p_{n,N}$ being the probability that the origin is not contained in the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of the *N* points and holds for any probability distribution on $\mathbf{R}^{n}$ that is symmetric around the origin. In particular this includes all distribution which are [rotationally invariant](wikipedia://en/424202) around the origin.

This is essentially a probabilistic restatement of [Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408)'s theorem that $N$ hyperplanes in general position in $\mathbb{R}^n$ divides it into $2\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{N-1}{k}$ regions.[^o3]
