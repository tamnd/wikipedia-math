# Robert Kass

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Kass"}

**Robert E. Kass** is the Maurice Falk University Professor of [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [Computational Neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_Neuroscience) in the Department of Statistics and Data Science, the [Machine Learning](wikipedia://en/233488) Department, and the Neuroscience Institute at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston), [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts) (1952), Kass earned a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in mathematics from [Antioch College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch_College), and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) degree in statistics from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1980, where his advisor was [Stephen Stigler](wikipedia://en/1276686). Kass is the son of the late Harvard medical researcher [Edward H. Kass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_H._Kass)[^o4] and stepson of the late [Amalie M. Kass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalie_Kass). His sister is the bioethicist [Nancy Kass](https://bioethics.jhu.edu/people/profile/nancy-kass/).

## Research and publications {#Research_and_publications}

Kass's early research was on [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) in statistics,[^o6] which formed the basis for his book *Geometrical Foundations of Asymptotic Inference*[^o7] (with Paul Vos), and on Bayesian methods. Since 2000 his research has focused on statistical methods in neuroscience.

Kass's best-known work includes a comprehensive re-evaluation of Bayesian hypothesis testing and model selection,[^o8][^o9] and the selection of prior distributions,[^o10] the relationship of Bayes and Empirical Bayes methods,[^o11] Bayesian asymptotics,[^o12][^o13] the application of point process statistical models to neural spiking data,[^o14][^o15] the challenges of multiple spike train analysis,[^o16][^o17] the state-space approach to brain-computer interface,[^o18] and the brain's apparent ability to solve the credit assignment problem during brain-controlled robotic movement.[^o19] Kass's book *Analysis of Neural Data*[^o20] (with [Emery Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emery_N._Brown) and Uri Eden) was published in 2014. Kass has also written on statistics education and the use of statistics, including the articles, "What is Statistics?",[^o21] "Statistical Inference: The Big Picture,"[^o22] and "Ten Simple Rules for Effective Statistical Practice".[^o23]

## Professional and administrative activities {#Professional_and_administrative_activities}

Kass has served Chair of the Section for Bayesian Statistical Science of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), Chair of the Statistics Section of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), founding Editor-in-Chief of [Bayesian Analysis (journal)](wikipedia://en/39096238), and Executive Editor (editor-in-chief) of the international review journal [Statistical Science](wikipedia://en/15936018). At Carnegie Mellon University he was Department Head of Statistics from 1995 to 2004 and Interim Co-director of the joint CMU–University of Pittsburgh Center for the Neural Basis of Cognition 2015–2018.[^o25][^o26]

## Honors {#Honors}

Kass is an elected Fellow of the American Statistical Association, the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics), and the American Association for the Advancement of Science, and an elected member of the National Academy of Sciences.[^o28] For his work on statistical modeling of neural synchrony,[^o29] in 2013 he received the Outstanding Statistical Application Award from the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), and in 2017 he received the R.A. Fisher Award and Lectureship, now known as the [COPSS Distinguished Achievement Award and Lectureship](wikipedia://en/15967717), from the Committee of Presidents of Statistical Societies.
