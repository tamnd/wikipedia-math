# Tamari lattice

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tamari lattice"}

*Image: Tamari lattice.svg.* Tamari lattice of order 4

In mathematics, the **Tamari lattice** is an algebraic structure that concisely represents some of the important logical and geometric properties of [associativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity). More formally, the Tamari lattice of order *n*, introduced by (txt) and sometimes notated T<sub>n</sub> or Y<sub>n</sub>, is a [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) in which the elements consist of all ways of bracketing a sequence of *n+1* letters using *n* pairs of parentheses, with the ordering induced by only rightward applications of the associative law ((*xy*)*z*) → (*x*(*yz*)). For instance, T<sub>3</sub> contains five elements (((*ab*)*c*)*d*), ((*ab*)(*cd*)), ((*a*(*bc*))*d*), (*a*((*bc*)*d*)), and (*a*(*b*(*cd*))), with (((*ab*)*c*)*d*) \< ((*ab*)(*cd*)) \< (*a*(*b*(*cd*))) and (((*ab*)*c*)*d*) \< ((*a*(*bc*))*d*) \< (*a*((*bc*)*d*)) \< (*a*(*b*(*cd*))). (The outermost pair of parentheses is redundant and often omitted when naming the elements of T<sub>n</sub>, as in the depiction of T<sub>4</sub> shown in the figure.)

:::

The number of elements in the Tamari lattice of order *n* is the *n*th [Catalan number](wikipedia://en/216488) C<sub>*n*</sub>. Its [Hasse diagram](wikipedia://en/338199) is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) to the [skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton_%28topology%29) of the [associahedron](wikipedia://en/19698446) of dimension *n-1*.

The [lattice property](wikipedia://en/469304) for the order (i.e., that any two bracketings have a [join and meet](wikipedia://en/3956618)) was stated without proof by (txt) in his thesis.[^o21] The property is non-trivial, and was established rigorously by Tamari together with his student [Haya Freedman](wikipedia://en/57460109) (Friedman Tamari 1967). A simpler proof appeared later with Tamari's student Samuel Huang (Huang Tamari 1972).

The Tamari lattice can be described in several other equivalent ways:

- It is the [poset](wikipedia://en/23572) of [binary trees](wikipedia://en/4321) with *n* nodes and *n*+1 leaves, ordered by [tree rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_rotation) operations.
- It is the poset of [triangulations](wikipedia://en/1440970) of a convex *n*-gon, ordered by flip operations that substitute one diagonal of the polygon for another.
- It is the poset of sequences of *n* integers *a*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *a*<sub>*n*</sub>, ordered coordinatewise, such that *i* ≤ *a*<sub>*i*</sub> ≤ *n* and if *i* ≤ *j* ≤ *a*<sub>*i*</sub> then *a*<sub>*j*</sub> ≤ *a*<sub>*i*</sub> (Huang Tamari 1972).
- It is the poset of [ordered forests](wikipedia://en/48560), in which one forest is earlier than another in the partial order if, for every *j*, the *j*th node in a [preorder](wikipedia://en/97034#Vertex_orderings) traversal of the first forest has at least as many descendants as the *j*th node in a preorder traversal of the second forest (Knuth 2005).

## Notation and indexing {#Notation_and_indexing}

The notation Y<sub>n</sub> is sometimes used for the Tamari lattice of order *n* (Chapoton 2005), which has the mnemonic value that the elements of the lattice may be considered as binary trees with *n* Y-shaped binary nodes. Note that the corresponding [associahedron](wikipedia://en/19698446) of dimension *n*-1 is notated K<sub>*n*+1</sub> since the indexing is by the number of leaves in the binary trees that form its vertices, rather than by the number of nodes.
