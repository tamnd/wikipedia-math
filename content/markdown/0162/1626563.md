# Jean Nicolas Pierre Hachette

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean Nicolas Pierre Hachette"}

**Jean Nicolas Pierre Hachette** (6 May 1769 – 16 January 1834), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), was born at [Mézières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleville-Mézières), where his father was a bookseller.[^n1]

:::

For his early education he proceeded first to the college of Charleville, and afterwards to that of [Reims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reims_University_%281548–1793%29). In 1788 he returned to Mézières, where he was attached to the [school of engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_royale_du_génie_de_Mézières) as draughtsman to the professors of physics and chemistry. In 1793 he became professor of [hydrography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrography) at [Collioure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collioure) and [Port-Vendre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port-Vendre). While there he sent several papers, in which some questions of navigation were treated geometrically, to [Gaspard Monge](wikipedia://en/77840), at that time minister of marine, through whose influence he obtained an appointment in Paris.[^n1]

Towards the close of 1794, when the [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique) was established, he was appointed along with Monge over the department of [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891). There he instructed some of the ablest Frenchmen of the day, among them [SD Poisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siméon_Poisson), [François Arago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Arago) and [A Fresnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_Jean_Fresnel). Accompanying [Guyton de Morveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyton_de_Morveau) in his expedition, earlier in the year, he was present at the [battle of Fleurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fleurus_%281794%29), and entered [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels) with the French army.[^n1]

In 1816, on the accession of [Louis XVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XVIII), he was expelled from his chair by government. He retained, however, until his death the office of professor in the faculty of sciences in the [École normale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29), to which he had been appointed in 1810. The necessary royal assent was in 1823 refused to the election of Hachette to the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences), and it was not until 1831, after the [July Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_Revolution), that he obtained that honour. He died at Paris on 16 January 1834.[^n1]

Hachette was held in high esteem for his private worth, as well as for his scientific attainments and great public services. His labours were chiefly in the field of descriptive geometry, with its application to the arts and mechanical engineering. It was left to him to develop the geometry of Monge, and to him also is due in great measure the rapid advancement which France made soon after the establishment of the École Polytechnique in the construction of machinery.[^n1]

Hachette's principal works are:

- *Deux Suppléments à la Géométrie descriptive de Monge* (1811 and 1818)
- *Éléments de géométrie à trois dimensions* (1817)
- *Collection des épures de géométrie*, etc. (1795 and 1817)
- *Applications de géométrie descriptive* (1817)
- *Traité de géométrie descriptive*, etc. (1822)
- *Traité élémentaire des machines* (1811)
- *Correspondance sur l'École Polytechnique* (1804–1815)

He also contributed many valuable papers to the leading scientific journals of his time. For a list of Hachette's writings see the *Catalogue of Scientific Papers* of the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London); also F Arago, *Œuvres* (1855); and [Silvestre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvestre_de_Sacy), *Notice sur J. N. P. Hachette* (Brussels, 1836).[^n1]
