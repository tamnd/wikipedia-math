# Closed immersion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Closed immersion"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **closed immersion** of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) is a [morphism of schemes](wikipedia://en/7482797) $f: Z \to X$ that identifies *Z* as a closed subset of *X* such that locally, [regular functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_function) on *Z* can be extended to *X*.[^o2] The latter condition can be formalized by saying that $f^\#:\mathcal{O}_X\rightarrow f_\ast\mathcal{O}_Z$ is surjective.[^o3]

:::

An example is the inclusion map $\operatorname{Spec}(R/I) \to \operatorname{Spec}(R)$ of [affine schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme) induced by the canonical [ring map](wikipedia://en/26411) $R \to R/I$.

## Other characterizations {#Other_characterizations}

The following are equivalent:

1. $f: Z \to X$ is a closed immersion.
1. For every open affine $U = \operatorname{Spec}(R) \subset X$, there exists an ideal $I \subset R$ such that $f^{-1}(U) = \operatorname{Spec}(R/I)$ as schemes over *U*.
1. There exists an open affine covering $X = \bigcup U_j, U_j = \operatorname{Spec} R_j$ and for each *j* there exists an ideal $I_j \subset R_j$ such that $f^{-1}(U_j) = \operatorname{Spec} (R_j / I_j)$ as schemes over $U_j$.
1. There is a quasi-coherent [sheaf of ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_of_ideals) $\mathcal{I}$ on *X* such that $f_\ast\mathcal{O}_Z\cong \mathcal{O}_X/\mathcal{I}$ and *f* is an isomorphism of *Z* onto the [global Spec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Spec) of $\mathcal{O}_X/\mathcal{I}$ over *X*.

### Definition for locally ringed spaces {#Definition_for_locally_ringed_spaces}

In the case of [locally ringed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_ringed_space)[^o6] a morphism $i:Z\to X$ is a closed immersion if a similar list of criteria is satisfied:

1. The map $i$ is a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) of $Z$ onto its image
1. The associated sheaf map $\mathcal{O}_X \to i_*\mathcal{O}_Z$ is surjective with kernel $\mathcal{I}$
1. The kernel $\mathcal{I}$ is locally generated by sections as an $\mathcal{O}_X$-module.[^o7]

The only varying condition is the third. It is instructive to look at a counter-example to get a feel for what the third condition yields by looking at a map which is not a closed immersion, $i:\mathbb{G}_m\hookrightarrow \mathbb{A}^1$ where

> $$\mathbb{G}_m = \text{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}])$$

If we look at the stalk of $i_*\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}_m}|_0$ at $0 \in \mathbb{A}^1$ then there are no sections. This implies for any open subscheme $U \subset \mathbb{A}^1$ containing $0$ the sheaf has no sections. This violates the third condition since at least one open subscheme $U$ covering $\mathbb{A}^1$ contains $0$.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A closed immersion is [finite](wikipedia://en/2139357) and [radicial](wikipedia://en/14483490) (universally injective). In particular, a closed immersion is universally closed. A closed immersion is stable under base change and composition. The notion of a closed immersion is local in the sense that *f* is a closed immersion if and only if for some (equivalently every) open covering $X=\bigcup U_j$ the induced map $f:f^{-1}(U_j)\rightarrow U_j$ is a closed immersion.[^o10][^o11]

If the composition $Z \to Y \to X$ is a closed immersion and $Y \to X$ is [separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separated_morphism), then $Z \to Y$ is a closed immersion. If *X* is a separated *S*-scheme, then every *S*-section of *X* is a closed immersion.[^o12]

If $i: Z \to X$ is a closed immersion and $\mathcal{I} \subset \mathcal{O}_X$ is the quasi-coherent sheaf of ideals cutting out *Z*, then the direct image $i_*$ from the category of quasi-coherent sheaves over *Z* to the category of quasi-coherent sheaves over *X* is exact, fully faithful with the essential image consisting of $\mathcal{G}$ such that $\mathcal{I} \mathcal{G} = 0$.[^o13]

A [flat](wikipedia://en/542520) closed immersion of finite presentation is the open immersion of an open closed subscheme.[^o14]
