# Deontic modality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Deontic modality"}

**Deontic modality** ([abbreviated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_glossing_abbreviations) **deo**) is a [linguistic modality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_modality) that indicates how the world ought to be[^o2] according to certain norms, expectations, speaker desires, etc. In other words, a deontic expression indicates that the state of the world (where 'world' is loosely defined here in terms of the surrounding circumstances) does not meet some standard or ideal, whether that standard be social (such as laws), personal (desires), etc. The sentence containing the deontic modal generally indicates some action that would change the world so that it becomes closer to the standard or ideal.

:::

This category includes the following subcategories:[^o3]

- Commissive modality (the speaker's commitment to do something, like a promise or threat; [alethic logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alethic_modality) or [temporal logic](wikipedia://en/321481) would apply):[^o4] "I shall help you."
- [Directive modality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directive_modality) (commands, requests, etc.; [deontic logic](wikipedia://en/1343980) would apply): "Come!", "Let's go!", "You've got to taste this curry!"
- [Volitive modality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volitive_modality) (wishes, desires, etc.; [boulomaic](wikipedia://en/3832714#Boulomaic) logic would apply): "If only I were rich!"

A related type of modality is [dynamic modality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_modality), which indicates a subject's internal capabilities or willingness as opposed to external factors such as permission or orders given.[^o5]

## Realisation in speech {#Realisation_in_speech}

**Deontic moods** are a category of [grammatical moods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_mood) that are used to express deontic modality. An example for a deontic mood is the [imperative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperative_mood) ("Come!").

However, many languages (like English) have additional ways to express deontic modality, like [modal verbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_verb) ("I *shall* help you.") and other verbs ("I *hope* to come soon."), as well as [adverbials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverbial) (*hopefully*) and other constructions.

### Constructed languages {#Constructed_languages}

#### Esperanto {#Esperanto}

[Esperanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanto) has a mood formally called "volitive" which is also used for various directive uses, so it can be seen as a broader deontic mood. However, it is not used to express commissive modality. It is formed by adding **\-u** to the verb stem, and it is used for orders and commands as well as for expressing will, desire, and purpose.[^o9]

- Est**u** feliĉa! "(May you) Be happy!"
- Don**u** al mi panon. "Give me bread."
- Ni ir**u**! "Let's go!"
- Mi leg**u** tion. "Let me read that."
- Mi volas, ke vi help**u** min. "I want you to help me."
- Ĉu mi far**u** tion? "Shall I do that?"
