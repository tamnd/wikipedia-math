# Harmonic index

*Image: Harmonic index.svg.* Vertices are labeled by their [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448). The edges in the graph are labeled 2 divided by the sum of the degrees of vertices [incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidence_%28graph%29) to it. The **Harmonic index** is the sum of these edge labels.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harmonic index"}

The **harmonic index** is a [topological index](wikipedia://en/14221614) based on the degrees of vertices in a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806). Introduced by Fajtlowicz in 1987,[^o2] it has become an important descriptor in [chemical graph theory](wikipedia://en/14222708) and has been extensively studied for its mathematical properties and applications.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For a simple connected graph $G = (V, E)$ with vertex set $V$ and edge set $E$, the harmonic index $H(G)$ is defined as:

$$\displaystyle H(G) = \sum_{uv \in E(G)} \frac{2}{d(u) + d(v)}$$

where $d(u)$ and $d(v)$ denote the [degrees](wikipedia://en/1374448) of vertices $u$ and $v$ respectively, and the sum is taken over all edges $uv$ in $G$.[^o5]

:::

## Relationship to other indices {#Relationship_to_other_indices}

The harmonic index is closely related to the [Randić index](wikipedia://en/24257203) and can be considered as one of its variants. While the Randić index uses $(d(u) \cdot d(v))^{-1/2}$ as the edge weight, the harmonic index uses the harmonic mean of the endpoint degrees.[^o5]

The harmonic index is also related to the [inverse degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_degree) of a graph:

$$\displaystyle ID(G) = \sum_{u \in V(G)} \frac{1}{d(u)}$$

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Bounds {#Bounds}

For a connected graph $G$ with $n$ vertices, $m$ edges, maximum degree $\Delta$, minimum degree $\delta$, and $p$ pendant edges:

**Lower bound:**[^o5]

$$\displaystyle H(G) \geq \frac{2p}{\Delta + 1} + \frac{m - p}{\Delta}$$

Equality holds if and only if $G$ is the star graph $K_{1,n-1}$, a [regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821), or a $(\Delta, 1)$-[semiregular graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiregular_graph).

**Upper bounds:** Various upper bounds have been established for specific graph classes. For example, for trees:[^o10]

- For any tree $T$ of order $n \geq 3$: $H(T) \leq H(P_n) = \frac{n-3}{4} + \frac{4}{3}$, with equality if and only if $T$ is the [path graph](wikipedia://en/4587190) $P_n$
- For any tree $T$ of order $n \geq 3$: $H(T) \geq H(S_n) = \frac{2(n-1)}{n}$, with equality if and only if $T$ is the [star graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_graph) $S_n$

### Extremal graphs {#Extremal_graphs}

Among all connected graphs with $n$ vertices:[^o10]

- The [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_n$ maximizes the harmonic index
- The [path graph](wikipedia://en/4587190) $P_n$ maximizes the harmonic index among trees
- The [star graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_graph) $S_n$ has the minimum harmonic index among trees

For [unicyclic graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicyclic_graph) of order $n$:[^o12]

- The [cycle](wikipedia://en/878962) $C_n$ achieves the maximum value: $H(C_n) = n/2$
- The graph $S(n-2, 1, 1)$ achieves the minimum value

:::

## Harmonic polynomial {#Harmonic_polynomial}

The **harmonic polynomial** of a graph $G$ is defined as:[^o14]

$$\displaystyle H(G, x) = \sum_{uv \in E(G)} x^{d(u) + d(v) - 1}$$

This polynomial is named for its relationship to the harmonic index:

$$\displaystyle H(G) = 2\int_0^1 H(G, x) \, dx$$

The harmonic polynomial is also related to the first [Zagreb polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_polynomial)

$$M_1(G, x) = \sum_{uv \in E} x^{d(u)+d(v)}$$

by the equality:

$M_1(G, x) = x \cdot H(G, x)$.[^o15]

## Values for specific graphs {#Values_for_specific_graphs}

For several common graph families, the harmonic index has closed-form expressions:[^o5][^o15]

- **[Complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816)** $K_n$: $H(K_n) = \frac{n}{2}$
- **[Cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962)** $C_n$: $H(C_n) = \frac{n}{2}$
- **[Path graph](wikipedia://en/4587190)** $P_n$: $H(P_n) = \frac{n-3}{4} + \frac{4}{3}$ for $n \geq 3$
- **[Star graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_graph)** $S_n$: $H(S_n) = \frac{2(n-1)}{n}$
- **[Complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320)** $K_{n_1, n_2}$: $H(K_{n_1, n_2}) = \frac{2n_1 n_2}{n_1 + n_2}$
- **[Hypercube graph](wikipedia://en/6706815)** $Q_n$: $H(Q_n) = 2^{n-1}$

## Hamiltonian properties {#Hamiltonian_properties}

Recent work has established connections between the harmonic index and Hamiltonian properties of graphs:[^o18]

::: {#o19 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem** (Li, 2024): Let $G$ be a $k$-connected graph with $n$ vertices and $e$ edges, where $k \geq 2$ and $n \geq 3$. If

$$\displaystyle H(G) \geq \frac{(\delta + \Delta)^2}{2M\delta\Delta} \cdot e^2$$

where $M = (k+1)\delta^2 + \frac{e^2}{n-(k+1)}$, then $G$ is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph).

A similar result holds for [traceable graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traceable_graph) with $k \geq 1$ and $n \geq 9$.[^o18]

:::

## Operations on graphs {#Operations_on_graphs}

The harmonic index has been studied extensively for various graph operations:[^o15]

### Cartesian product {#Cartesian_product}

For graphs $G_1$ and $G_2$, the harmonic index of their [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/2856852) satisfies:

$$\displaystyle H(G_1 \times G_2) \geq \frac{1}{2}H(G_1)ID(G_2) + \frac{1}{2}H(G_2)ID(G_1)$$

### Other products {#Other_products}

Similar results have been established for:

- [Corona product](wikipedia://en/79527671)
- [Join of graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_join)
- Cartesian sum
- [Lexicographic product](wikipedia://en/6026731)

## Graph perturbations {#Graph_perturbations}

The harmonic index exhibits predictable behavior under certain graph operations:[^o5]

- **Edge separation**: If $e = uv$ is a cut edge with both components having at least two vertices, contracting $e$ and adding a pendant edge decreases the harmonic index.
- **Edge grafting**: Moving pendant paths to concentrate at a single vertex generally increases the harmonic index.
- **Vertex deletion**: Removing a pendant vertex strictly decreases the harmonic index: $H(G) > H(G - v)$ for any pendant vertex $v$.

::: {#o25 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The harmonic index has found applications in [chemical graph theory](wikipedia://en/14222708), where it serves as a molecular descriptor for predicting physicochemical properties of chemical compounds. In [QSAR/QSPR studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QSAR) (quantitative structure-activity and structure-property relationships), the harmonic index has proven useful for correlating molecular structure with biological activity and physical properties.[^o5]

The harmonic index has also been applied to [network analysis](wikipedia://en/766409), where it helps measure connectivity and structural properties of various types of networks. Its mathematical properties, particularly its relationship to vertex degrees and its behavior under graph operations, make it a useful tool for understanding graph structure in both theoretical and applied contexts.[^o15]

:::

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

The harmonic index can be computed in $O(m)$ time for a graph with $m$ edges, since it requires only a single pass through all edges. An $O(n^2)$ algorithm for computing the harmonic polynomial has been developed.[^o15]
