# Axel Sophus Guldberg

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Axel Sophus Guldberg"}

**Axel Sophus Guldberg** (2 November 1838 – 28 February 1913) was a [Norwegian mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_mathematician).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in Christiania (now called Oslo), Guldberg was the second oldest out of 11 siblings.[^o7] He and his siblings were initially homeschooled, but he and his older brother, [Cato Maximilian Guldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_Maximilian_Guldberg), later began going to school in [Fredrikstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredrikstad), where they lived together with relatives.[^o7] He completed his [examen artium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examen_artium) in 1856,[^o7] [cand.real.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cand.real.) in 1863[^o7] and [dr.philos.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr.philos.) in 1867.[^o3][^o7]

In 1863, he was an [adjunct professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunct_professor) in [Drammen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drammen).[^o7] From 1864 to 1865, he studied mathematics in Germany and France, while simultaneously on his honeymoon.[^o7] In 1865, Guldberg became a [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) in [Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger).[^o3][^o7] The same year, he began teaching mathematics at the [Norwegian Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Military_Academy) until 1899.[^o3][^o7]

He was an important figure in the insurance industry.[^o3][^o7] He also served in the Norwegian [law commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_commission).[^o8]

In 1866, he had a son, [Alf Victor Guldberg](wikipedia://en/50527391), with his wife, Fredrikke Borchsenius.[^o9]
