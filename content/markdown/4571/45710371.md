**Victor Vâlcovici** (21 September – 21 June 1970) was a [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania) mechanician and mathematician.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born into a modest family in [Galați](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galați), he graduated first in his class in 1904 from [Nicolae Bălcescu High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Bălcescu_National_College) in [Brăila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brăila). Entering the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest) on a scholarship, he attended its faculty of sciences, where he had as teachers [Spiru Haret](wikipedia://en/1194762) and [Gheorghe Țițeica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Țițeica).[^o5] After graduating in 1907 with a degree in mathematics, he taught high school for two years before leaving for [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) on another scholarship to pursue a doctorate in mathematics. He wrote his thesis under the direction of [Ludwig Prandtl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Prandtl) and defended it in 1913; the thesis, titled *Ueber die diskontinuierliche Flussigkeitsbewegungen mit zwei freien Strahlen* (Discontinuous flow of liquids in two free dimensions),[^o7][^o8] amplified upon the work of [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980).[^o10]

He was subsequently named assistant professor of mechanics at the [University of Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza_University), rising to full professor in 1918.[^o9] In 1921, he became rector of the [Polytechnic School of Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politehnica_University_of_Timișoara). There, he was also professor of rational mechanics and founded a laboratory dedicated to the field.[^o10] During his nine years as rector, he worked to place the recently founded university on a solid foundation.[^o9] From 1930 until retiring in 1962, he taught experimental mechanics at the University of Bucharest.[^o10] In the government of [Nicolae Iorga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Iorga), he served as [Minister of Public Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transport_and_Infrastructure) from 1931 to 1932. During this time, he introduced a modern road network that featured paved highways.[^o10][^o9] In 1936 he gave an [invited talk](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo), with title *Sur le sillage derrière un obstacle circulaire* (In the wake of a circular obstacle).[^o11]

Elected a corresponding [member of the Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_Romanian_Academy) in 1936,[^o12] he was [stripped of his membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_purged_members_of_the_Romanian_Academy) by the new [communist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Romania) in 1948,[^o13] but made a titular member of the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy) in 1965.[^o14] His numerous articles on theoretical and applied mechanics covered topics such as the principles of variational mechanics, the mechanics of ideal fluid flow, the theory of elasticity and astronomy.[^o10]

He died in 1970 in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest), and was buried in the city's [Bellu Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellu_Cemetery). Streets have been named after Victor Vâlcovici in [Brăila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brăila), [Galați](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galați), and [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timișoara); a school in Galați also bears his name.
