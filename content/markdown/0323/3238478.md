# Bianchi group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bianchi group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Bianchi group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of the form

:::

$$\text{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_d)$$

where *d* is a positive [square-free integer](wikipedia://en/29525). Here, $\text{PSL}_2$ denotes the [projective special linear group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_linear_group) and $\mathcal{O}_d$ is the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of the [imaginary quadratic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_quadratic_field) $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$.

The groups were first studied by (txt) as a natural class of [discrete subgroups](wikipedia://en/468536) of $\text{PSL}_2(\mathbb{C})$, now termed [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652).

## Geometry {#Geometry}

As a subgroup of $\text{PSL}_2(\mathbb{C})$, a Bianchi group acts by [orientation-preserving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation-preserving) [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) on three-dimensional [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742) $\mathbb{H}^3$. The quotient

$$M_d=\text{PSL}_2(\mathcal{O}_d)\backslash\mathbb{H}^3$$

is a non-compact, hyperbolic 3-fold of finite volume, called a **Bianchi orbifold**. An exact formula for its volume in terms of the [Dedekind zeta function](wikipedia://en/1131243) of the underlying imaginary quadratic field was computed by [Humbert](wikipedia://en/33375250). If $D$ is the discriminant of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, then

$$\operatorname{vol}(M_d)=\frac{|D|^{3/2}}{4\pi^2}\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})}(2).$$

The cusps of $M_d$ are in bijection with the ideal classes of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$. Consequently, the number of cusps is equal to the [class number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_number) of the field.

## Arithmetic properties {#Arithmetic_properties}

Bianchi groups are [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/630741) Kleinian groups. Every non-cocompact arithmetic Kleinian group is commensurable, up to conjugacy in $\text{PSL}_2(\mathbb{C})$, with a Bianchi group.[^o5]
