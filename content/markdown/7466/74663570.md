# Kling–Gupta efficiency

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kling–Gupta efficiency"}

The **Kling–Gupta efficiency** (**KGE**) is a [goodness-of-fit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodness-of-fit) indicator widely used in the [hydrologic sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrologic_sciences) for comparing simulations to observations. It was created by hydrologic scientists Harald Kling and Hoshin Vijai Gupta.[^o2] Its creators intended for it to improve upon widely used metrics such as the [coefficient of determination](wikipedia://en/1500869) and the [Nash–Sutcliffe model efficiency coefficient](wikipedia://en/3337759).

:::

$$\text{KGE} =1-\sqrt{(r-1)^2+(\alpha-1)^2+(\beta-1)^2}$$

where:

- $r$ is the [Pearson correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/221708),
- $\alpha$ is a term representing the variability of prediction errors,
- $\beta$ is a bias term.

The terms $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are defined as follows:

$$\beta=\frac{\mu_s}{\mu_o}$$

where:

- $\mu_s$ is the mean of the simulated time series (e.g.: flows predicted by the model)
- $\mu_o$ is the mean of the observed time series

and

$$\alpha = \frac{\sigma_s}{\sigma_o}$$

where:

- $\sigma_s^2$ is the variance of the simulated time series, so $\sigma_s$ is estimated by the standard deviation of simulated data.
- $\sigma_o^2$ is the variance of the observed time series

A modified version, $\text{KGE’}$, which uses the ratio of [CVs](wikipedia://en/1012687) for $\alpha$ instead, was proposed by Kling et al. in 2012.[^o3]
