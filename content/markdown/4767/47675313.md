# Günter Lumer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Günter Lumer"}

**Günter Lumer** (May 29, 1929 – 2005) was a German-born mathematician known for his work in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). He is the namesake of the [Lumer–Phillips theorem](wikipedia://en/12281541) on [semigroups](wikipedia://en/27799) of operators on [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), and was the first to study [L-semi-inner products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-semi-inner_product). Born in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) and raised in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) and [Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay), he spent his professional career in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) and [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium).[^o3]

:::

Lumer was born in [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt), on May 29, 1929. His family fled the Nazis in 1933, moving to France and then again in 1941 to Uruguay, where he became a citizen. Lumer studied at the [Universidad de la República](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_de_la_República), where he came under the influence of [Paul Halmos](wikipedia://en/318598); his first [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) paper, published in 1953, was jointly authored by Halmos and Juan Jorge Schäffer. He completed a degree in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) at Montevideo in 1957, and traveled to Halmos' home institution, the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), on a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship).[^o3] At Chicago, he completed a doctorate in 1959 under the supervision of [Irving Kaplansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Kaplansky).[^o3][^o4]

Following short-term positions at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), he joined the faculty at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in 1961. He moved to the [University of Mons-Hainaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mons-Hainaut) in 1973, and then to the International Solvay Institutes for Physics and Chemistry in [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels) in 1999, where he remained until his death in 2005.[^o3]
