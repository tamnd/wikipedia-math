# Ed Posner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ed Posner"}

**Edward Charles Posner** (August 10, 1933 – June 15, 1993) was an American [information theorist](wikipedia://en/14773) and [neural network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_network) researcher. He became chief technologist at the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory), and founded the [Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_on_Neural_Information_Processing_Systems).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Posner was born on August 10, 1933, in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), and graduated from [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School) in 1950; at Stuyvesant, one of his close friends was mathematician [Paul Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Cohen_%28mathematician%29).[^o7] He took only two years to complete his undergraduate studies in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), graduating in 1952, and he then switched to [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) for a master's degree in 1953 and a PhD in 1957.[^o7][^o3] While a graduate student, he also visited [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs), and later claimed that he had been assigned to the desk there that had formerly been [Harry Nyquist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Nyquist)'s.[^o7] His doctoral thesis, supervised by [Irving Kaplansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Kaplansky), was on the subject of [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424) and entitled *Differentiably Simple Rings*; at only 26 pages long, it held the record for the shortest doctoral thesis at the university.[^o7]

After finishing his studies, he became a mathematics instructor at the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin) and then an assistant professor of mathematics at [Harvey Mudd College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Mudd_College).[^o7][^o3] In 1961, [Solomon W. Golomb](wikipedia://en/1860707) hired him to lead the Information Processing Group at JPL.[^o7] He led the group for 10 years and then, after a sequence of positions in higher management,[^o7] he became chief technologist in JPL's Office of Telecommunications and Data Acquisition in 1982.[^o3] He also held lecturer and visiting faculty positions in the [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) departments of the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) beginning in 1970.[^o7][^o3]

He died after being hit by a truck while bicycling to work on June 15, 1993.[^o3]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), Posner is the namesake of [Posner's theorem](wikipedia://en/43160575), stating that certain [tensor products of algebras](wikipedia://en/372395) with the [fields of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) of their [centers](wikipedia://en/342453) are [central simple algebras](wikipedia://en/404082).[^o6]

Posner's research in [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773) and [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869) was applied in the design of the [NASA Deep Space Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Deep_Space_Network), used for the communications between spacecraft and their base stations on Earth. He also studied [communications networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_network) and [cellular telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_telephone) [switching systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_exchange),[^o3] and was an advocate for [basic research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_research) in the US space program.[^o7]

Beginning in the early 1980s, Posner founded the study of [neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_network) at JPL and Caltech, and helped create the interdisciplinary graduate program in [Computation and Neural Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computation_and_Neural_Systems) at Caltech.[^o3] He also helped found the annual [Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_on_Neural_Information_Processing_Systems), served as general chair of the first conference in 1987, and chaired its oversight body, the NIPS Foundation.[^o7]
