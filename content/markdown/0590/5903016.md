# SMP (computer algebra system)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="SMP"}

**Symbolic Manipulation Program** (**SMP**) was a [computer algebra system](wikipedia://en/54427) designed by [Chris A. Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_A._Cole) and [Stephen Wolfram](wikipedia://en/52469) at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech) circa 1979. It was initially developed in the Caltech physics department with contributions from [Geoffrey C. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_C._Fox), Jeffrey M. Greif, Eric D. Mjolsness, Larry J. Romans, Timothy Shaw, and Anthony E. Terrano.

:::

## History {#History}

SMP was first sold commercially in 1981, by the Computer Mathematics Corporation of [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles), which later became part of [Inference Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inference_Corporation). Inference further developed the program and marketed it commercially from 1983 to 1988, but it was not a commercial success, and Inference became pessimistic about the market for symbolic math programs, and so abandoned SMP to concentrate on [expert systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expert_system).[^o3]

SMP was influenced by the earlier computer algebra systems [Macsyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macsyma) (of which Wolfram was a user) and [Schoonschip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schoonschip) (whose code Wolfram studied).[^o4][^o5]

SMP follows a rule-based approach, giving it a "consistent, pattern-directed language". Unlike Macsyma and Reduce, it was written in [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_programming_language).[^o6]

During the 1980s, it was one of the generally available general-purpose computer algebra systems, along with [Reduce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduce_%28computer_algebra_system%29), Macsyma, and [Scratchpad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_%28computer_algebra_system%29),[^o7] and later [muMATH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MuMATH) and [Maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_%28computer_algebra_system%29). It was often used for teaching college calculus.[^o8]

The design of SMP's interactive language and its "map" commands influenced the design of the 1984 version of [Scratchpad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratchpad_%28computer_algebra_system%29).[^o9]

## Reception {#Reception}

SMP has been criticized for various characteristics, notably its use of [floating-point numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating-point_numbers) instead of exact rational numbers, which can lead to incorrect results,[^o11] and makes [polynomial greatest common divisor calculations](wikipedia://en/9927028) problematic.[^o12] Many other problems in early versions of the system were purportedly fixed in later versions.[^o13]
