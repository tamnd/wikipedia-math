# Abel's test

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abel's test"}

Series

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Abel's test** (also known as **Abel's criterion**) is a method of testing for the [convergence](wikipedia://en/1743431) of an [infinite series](wikipedia://en/15287). The test is named after mathematician [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573), who proved it in 1826.[^o2] There are two slightly different versions of Abel's test – one is used with series of real numbers, and the other is used with [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759). **Abel's uniform convergence test** is a criterion for the [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652) of a [series](wikipedia://en/15287) of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) dependent on [parameters](wikipedia://en/25065#Mathematical_analysis).

:::

## Abel's test in real analysis {#Abel's_test_in_real_analysis}

Suppose the following statements are true:

1. $\sum a_n$ is a convergent series,
1. $b_n$ is a monotone sequence, and
1. $b_n$ is bounded.

Then $\sum a_nb_n$ is also convergent.

The test is mainly pertinent and useful in the context of non-absolutely convergent series $\sum a_n$. It can be proved directly using [summation by parts](wikipedia://en/246188).

## Abel's test in complex analysis {#Abel's_test_in_complex_analysis}

A closely related convergence test, also known as **Abel's test**, can often be used to establish the convergence of a [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) on the boundary of its [circle of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476). Specifically, Abel's test states that if a sequence $(a_n)$ of *positive real numbers* is decreasing monotonically with

$$\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} a_n = 0,$$

then the power series

$$f(z) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_nz^n$$

converges everywhere on the closed [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071), except possibly at *z* = 1. Abel's test cannot be applied when *z* = 1, so convergence at that single point must be investigated separately.

Abel's test implies in particular that the radius of convergence is at least 1. It can also be applied to a power series with radius of convergence *R* ≠ 1 by a simple change of variables $\zeta = z/R$.[^o5] Abel's test is a generalization of the [alternating series test](wikipedia://en/1598759), which is the special case *z* = −1.

::: {#o6 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof of Abel's test"}

**Proof of Abel's test:** Suppose that *z* is a point on the unit circle, *z* ≠ 1. For each $n\geq1$, we define

$$f_n(z):=\sum_{k=0}^n a_k z^k.$$

By multiplying this function by (1 − *z*), we obtain

$$\begin{aligned} (1-z)f_n(z) & = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k (1-z)z^k = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k z^k - \sum_{k=0}^n a_k z^{k+1} = a_0 + \sum_{k=1}^n a_k z^k - \sum_{k=1}^{n+1} a_{k-1} z^k \\ & = a_0 - a_n z^{n+1} + \sum_{k=1}^n (a_k - a_{k-1}) z^k . \end{aligned}$$

The first summand is constant, the second converges uniformly to zero (since by assumption the sequence $(a_n)$ converges to zero). It only remains to show that the series converges. We will show this by showing that it even converges absolutely: $\sum_{k=1}^\infty \left|(a_k - a_{k-1}) z^k \right| = \sum_{k=1}^\infty |a_k-a_{k-1}|\cdot |z|^k \leq \sum_{k=1}^\infty (a_{k-1}-a_{k})$ where the last sum is a converging telescoping sum. The absolute value vanished because the sequence $(a_n)$ is decreasing by assumption.

Hence, the sequence $(1-z)f_n(z)$ converges (even uniformly) on the closed unit disc. If $z\not = 1$, we may divide by (1 − *z*) and obtain the result.

Another way to obtain the result is to apply the [Dirichlet's test](wikipedia://en/3141677). Indeed, for $z\ne 1,\ |z|=1$ holds $\left|\sum_{k=0}^n z^k\right|=\left|\frac{z^{n+1}-1}{z-1}\right|\le \frac{2}{|z-1|}$, hence the assumptions of the Dirichlet's test are fulfilled.

The following strengthening of the test is also valid: one may replace the condition that $(a_n)$ is decreasing with the condition that it is decreasing for sufficiently large *n*, that is, that there is some natural number *m* for which $a_n \geq a_{n+1}$ whenever *n* > *m*.

:::

## Abel's uniform convergence test {#Abel's_uniform_convergence_test}

Abel's uniform convergence test is a criterion for the [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652) of a series of functions or an [improper integration](wikipedia://en/346030) of functions dependent on [parameters](wikipedia://en/25065#Mathematical_analysis). It is related to Abel's test for the convergence of an ordinary series of real numbers, and the proof relies on the same technique of [summation by parts](wikipedia://en/246188).

The test is as follows. Let $\{g_n\}$ be a [uniformly bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_bounded) sequence of real-valued [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) on a set $E$ such that $g_{n+1}(x) \leq g_n(x)$ for all $x \in E$ and positive integers $n$, and let $\{f_n\}$ be a sequence of real-valued functions such that the series $\sum f_n(x)$ converges uniformly on $E$. Then $\sum f_n(x)g_n(x)$ converges uniformly on $E$.

:::
