# Agata Smoktunowicz

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Agata Smoktunowicz"}

**Agata Smoktunowicz** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) (born 12 October 1973) is a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works as a professor at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh). She is known for her research in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), especially in [noncommutative ring theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_ring_theory), where she solved several long-standing mathematical problems and conducted important examples of algebraic structures such as [nil rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nil_ring).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Smoktunowicz's contributions to mathematics include constructing [noncommutative](wikipedia://en/24062516) [nil rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nil_ring), solving a "famous problem" formulated in 1970 by [Irving Kaplansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Kaplansky).[^o3][^o6] She proved the Artin–Stafford gap conjecture according to which the [Gelfand–Kirillov dimension](wikipedia://en/35987396) of a [graded](wikipedia://en/103094) [domain](wikipedia://en/487627) cannot fall within the [open interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_interval) (2,3).[^o3][^o7] She also found an example of a [nil ideal](wikipedia://en/3100140) of a ring *R* that does not lift to a nil ideal of the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) *R*\[*X*\], disproving a conjecture of Amitsur and hinting that the [Köthe conjecture](wikipedia://en/3100105) might be false.[^o8][^o9][^o10]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Smoktunowicz was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2006.[^o3] She won the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 2006, the [European Mathematical Society Prize](wikipedia://en/969470#Prizes) in 2008, and the [Sir Edmund Whittaker Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/4579429) of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940) in 2009.[^o3] In 2009, she was elected as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh),[^o12] and in 2012, she became one of the inaugural fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o13] She also won the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) annual research prize in 2018.[^o14] She was awarded the [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 2023.[^o15]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Smoktunowicz earned a master's degree from the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) in 1997, a PhD in 1999 from the Institute of Mathematics of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences), and a [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 2007, again from the Polish Academy of Sciences. After temporary positions at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) and the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego), she joined the University of Edinburgh in 2005, and was promoted to professor there in 2007.[^o4] At the university of Edinburgh she is part of a Algebra and Number Theory research group.

## Areas of research {#Areas_of_research}

Smoktunowicz main area of research is algebra and number theory. She has published research in the areas of noncommutative ring theory applications, Nil rings, and Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions.
