# Thornton Carle Fry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thornton Carle Fry"}

**Thornton Carle Fry** (7 January 1892, [Findlay, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Findlay,_Ohio) – 1 January 1991) was an applied mathematician, known for his two widely-used textbooks, *Probability and its engineering uses* (1928)[^o2][^o3] and *Elementary differential equations* (1929).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Thornton C. Fry received his bachelor's degree from [Findlay College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Findlay_College) in 1912 and then pursued graduate study in Wisconsin in mathematics, physics, and astronomy. He received his M.A. in 1913[^o7] and his Ph.D. in 1920 in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) from the [University of Wisconsin-Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin-Madison) with thesis under the supervision of [Charles S. Slichter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_S._Slichter).[^o9]

Fry was employed as an industrial mathematician by [Western Electric Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Electric_Company) from 1916 to 1924 and then by [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Telephone_Laboratories) (Bell Labs), which was half-owned by Western Electric. He headed a corporate division for industrial applications of mathematics and statistics and was involved in [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) for the U.S. federal government in both world wars.

After retiring (due to reaching the mandatory retirement age) from Bell Labs in 1956, he was hired by [William Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Norris_%28CEO%29) as a Senior Consultant for the [UNIVAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNIVAC) division of [Sperry Rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sperry_Corporation#Sperry_Rand). He would subsequently be appointed as Vice-President head of the UNIVAC Division over Norris in April 1957.[^o10] After retiring from Sperry-Rand in 1961 he worked as a consultant with Boeing Scientific Research Labs and also, during the 1960s, with [Walter Orr Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Orr_Roberts), director of the [National Center for Atmospheric Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Atmospheric_Research).[^o11]

In 1924 Fry was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Toronto.[^o12] In 1982 the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA) gave him the MAA's distinguished service award.[^o13]
