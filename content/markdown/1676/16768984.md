# Thomas Clausen (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Clausen"}

the Danish mathematician and astronomer **Thomas Clausen** (16 January 1801, [Snogbæk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snogbæk), [Sottrup Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sottrup), [Duchy of Schleswig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Schleswig) – 23 May 1885, [Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartu), [Imperial Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russia)) was a [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Clausen learned mathematics at home. In 1820, he became a trainee at the [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich) Optical Institute and in 1824, at the [Altona Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altona_Observatory) after he showed [Heinrich Christian Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Christian_Schumacher) his paper on calculating longitude by the [occultation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occultation) of stars by the moon.[^o4] In 1828, he discovered [Clausen's formula](wikipedia://en/19579133).[^o5] He eventually returned to Munich, where he conceived and published his best known works on mathematics. In 1832, he discovered the [Clausen function](wikipedia://en/406899).[^o6] In 1842, Clausen was hired by the staff of the [Tartu Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartu_Observatory), becoming its director in 1866–1872.

Works by Clausen include studies on the stability of [Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System), [comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comet) movement, ABC [telegraph code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraph_code) and calculation of 250 decimals of [pi](wikipedia://en/23601) (later, only 248 were confirmed to be correct). In 1840, he discovered the [Von Staudt–Clausen theorem](wikipedia://en/1084655). Also in 1840, he also found two compass and straightedge constructions of [lunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lune_%28mathematics%29) with equal area to a square, adding to three (including the [lune of Hippocrates](wikipedia://en/21854422)) known to the ancient Greek mathematician [Hippocrates of Chios](wikipedia://en/3632522); it was later shown that these five lunes are the only possible solutions to this problem.[^o7] In 1854, he factored the sixth [Fermat number](wikipedia://en/91127) as 2<sup>64</sup>+1 = 67280421310721 × 274177.
