# Hill differential equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hill differential equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hill equation** or **Hill differential equation** is the second-order linear [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753)

:::

$$\frac{d^2y}{dt^2} + f(t) y = 0,$$

where $f(t)$ is a [periodic function](wikipedia://en/76848) with minimal period $\pi$. By this we mean that for all $t$

$$f(t+\pi)=f(t),$$

and if $p$ is a number with $0 < p < \pi$, the equation $f(t+p) = f(t)$ must fail for some $t$.[^o2] It is named after [George William Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_William_Hill), who introduced it in 1886.[^o3]

Because $f(t)$ has period $\pi$, the Hill equation can be rewritten using the [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) of $f(t)$:

$$\frac{d^2y}{dt^2}+\left(\theta_0+2\sum_{n=1}^\infty \theta_n \cos(2nt)+\sum_{m=1}^\infty \phi_m \sin(2mt) \right ) y=0.$$

Important special cases of Hill's equation include the [Mathieu equation](wikipedia://en/1724802#Mathieu_equation) (in which only the terms corresponding to *n* = 0, 1 are included) and the [Meissner equation](wikipedia://en/37805543).

Hill's equation is an important example in the understanding of periodic differential equations. Depending on the exact shape of $f(t)$, solutions may stay bounded for all time, or the amplitude of the oscillations in solutions may grow exponentially.[^o4] The precise form of the solutions to Hill's equation is described by [Floquet theory](wikipedia://en/2987843). Solutions can also be written in terms of Hill determinants.[^o2]

Aside from its original application to lunar stability,[^o3] the Hill equation appears in many settings including in modeling of a [quadrupole mass spectrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrupole_mass_spectrometer),[^o5] as the one-dimensional [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874) of an electron in a crystal,[^o6] [quantum optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_optics) of two-level systems, [accelerator physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerator_physics#Beam_dynamics)[^o7] and electromagnetic structures that are periodic in space[^o8] and/or in time.[^o9]
