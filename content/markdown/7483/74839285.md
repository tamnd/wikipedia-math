# Sarah Peluse

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sarah Peluse"}

*Image: Sarah Peluse Oberwolfach 2019.jpg.* Peluse at [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematisches_Forschungsinstitut_Oberwolfach) in 2019

**Sarah Anne Peluse** is an American mathematician specializing in [arithmetic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243381) and [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), and known for her research on generalizations of [Szemerédi's theorem](wikipedia://en/591703) on the existence of polynomial progressions in dense sets of integers. She is an associate professor in the department of mathematics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Peluse's interest in mathematics was sparked by a sixth-grade teacher using the [Socratic method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socratic_method). After skipping seventh grade and running through all of the mathematics available at her local high school and community college, she enrolled at [Lake Forest College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Forest_College) in Illinois at age 15. The mathematics on offer there lasted her only for another two years, so she transferred to the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), with [Paul Sally](wikipedia://en/2789475) and later [Maryanthe Malliaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryanthe_Malliaris) as mentors. She also became a member of the University of Chicago track and field team, which competed at two national championship meets, and she was recognized as a Division III All-Academic Athlete by the [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA). She earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics in 2014.

Peluse completed her Ph.D. at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 2019. Her dissertation, *Bounds for sets with no nontrivial polynomial progressions*, was supervised by [Kannan Soundararajan](wikipedia://en/8053624). She became an NSF Postdoctoral Fellow at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), and then a Veblen Research Instructor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), before taking her position as a faculty member at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). In September 2024, she became an associate professor of mathematics at Stanford.[^o3]

### Research {#Research}

With Sean Prendiville, Peluse found effective bounds on polynomial progressions in dense sets of integers.[^o5]

Jointly with Rachel Greenfeld and [Marina Iliopoulou](wikipedia://en/78400292), Peluse proved a structure theorem for integer distance sets in the set $[-N,N]^{2}$.[^o6] It is well known that a circle or a line are the two structures within which integer distance sets can be found. (There are integer distance sets with seven points, no three points on a line, no four on a circle.[^o7] No such set with eight or more points is known.)

Peluse and Soundararajan extended earlier work of Peluse and others to prove Miller's conjecture[^o8] in the representation theory of symmetric groups: almost all entries in the character table of the symmetric group are multiples of any given prime.[^o9]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

As an undergraduate, Peluse won the 2014 [Alice T. Schafer Prize](wikipedia://en/57181252) of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) for her work in mathematics.

Peluse was the recipient of the 2022 [Dénes König Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dénes_König_Prize), given at the [SIAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM) Conference on Discrete Mathematics, for her work on polynomial generalizations of Szemerédi's theorem. She was also a 2022 recipient of the [Maryam Mirzakhani New Frontiers Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryam_Mirzakhani_New_Frontiers_Prize), associated with the [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933), "for contributions to arithmetic combinatorics and analytic number theory, particularly with regards to polynomial patterns in dense sets".

She won the 2023 [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize)[^o11] (joint with [Julian Sahasrabudhe](wikipedia://en/76040232)) for contributions to additive combinatorics and related fields, including her work on quantitative density theorems for polynomial configurations in arithmetic progressions, which have found application in discrete harmonic analysis and ergodic theory.

In September 2025 Peluse was named the 2026 recipient of the AWM Microsoft Research Prize in Algebra and Number Theory.[^o12]
