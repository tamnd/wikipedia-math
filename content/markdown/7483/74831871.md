# Angkana Rüland

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Angkana Rüland"}

**Angkana Rüland** (born 1987) is a German applied mathematician, a professor in mathematics and holder of a Hausdorff Chair in mathematics at the [Hausdorff Center for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/23690221) of the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn). Her research has included work on the mathematical modeling of [shape-memory alloys](wikipedia://en/665728) and on the [inverse problems](wikipedia://en/203956) arising in [animal echolocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_echolocation).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Rüland was born in 1987 in [Chiang Mai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Mai), but is a German citizen. She grew up in Bonn and was a mathematics student at the University of Bonn. She completed her doctorate in 2014 with the dissertation *On Some Rigidity Properties in PDEs* supervised by [Herbert Koch](wikipedia://en/27321694).

After postdoctoral research at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), working there with [John M. Ball](wikipedia://en/3578468), she became a researcher at the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics in the Sciences](wikipedia://en/2114506) in 2017. She took a professorship at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University) in 2020 before returning to the University of Bonn in 2023.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Rüland is one of the recipients of the 2024 [New Horizons in Mathematics Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Horizons_in_Mathematics_Prize), associated with the [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933), "for contributions to applied analysis, in particular the analysis of microstructure in solid-solid [phase transitions](wikipedia://en/54423) and the theory of inverse problems". In 2025, she received the [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize).[^o4]
