# Chiungtze C. Tsen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chiungtze C. Tsen"}

Tsen

*Image: ChiungstzeTsen1932 MFO.jpg.* 1932 near [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen)

**Chiungtze C. Tsen** (; [Chang-Du Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang-Du_Gan): \[tsɛn˦˨ tɕjuŋ˨˩˧ tsɹ̩˦˨\],[^n1] April 2, 1898[^o12] – October 1, 1940[^o17]), given name **Chiung** (),[^o13] was a Chinese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) born in [Nanchang, Jiangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang). He is known for his work in algebra. He was one of [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896)'s students at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), Germany.

:::

One of his research interests was [quasi-algebraic closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-algebraic_closure). In that area he proved a fundamental result which is now called [Tsen's theorem](wikipedia://en/10129446).

## Biography {#Biography}

Tsen was born in a poor fisherman's family in [Xinjian Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjian_District), [Nanchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang), [Jiangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangxi) Province. His father Tschu-Wun Tsen[^o11] (曾祖文 Zeng Zuwen) had two sons and several daughters, and Tsen was the eldest son.[^o16] His uncle Lei Heng (雷恒), who was a *[jinshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinshi)* and a member of the [Hanlin Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanlin_Academy), persuaded Tsen's father to send Tsen to school. Due to poverty, Tsen had to take leaves from school intermittently to work. After leaving primary school, he worked in a coal mine while self-studying.[^o19]

In 1917, he passed the entrance examination and was admitted to Jiangxi Provincial First Normal College in Nanchang. He was subsidised by Lei Heng's son Tsebu S. Lee[^o11] (雷子布 Lei Zibu, given name 宣 Xuan), who was studying in Japan on government scholarship. After graduation in 1920, Tsen taught in primary school for two years. In 1922, Tsen entered National Wuchang Senior Normal College, later National Wuchang University,[^o14] to study undergraduate mathematics, and he graduated in 1926. After graduation, he worked as teacher in high schools for two years to perform the mandatory teaching service of his degree.[^o19]

In 1927, when [Kuomintang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuomintang) split with the [Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party), Tsen and some teachers and students protested against the breakup and called for alliance. Several of them including Tsen were beaten up and were hospitalized. [Guo Moruo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guo_Moruo), then serving as director of the political department of the [National Revolutionary Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Revolutionary_Army), visited them in the hospital.[^o19]

In 1928, Tsen passed the Jiangxi provincial government scholarship examination for studying in Europe and America. He went to [Berlin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_University) for language training for a year, and then he started studying mathematics at [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in the summer semester of 1929. He studied algebra under Emmy Noether. Tsen received his doctoral degree in February 1934 under the supervision of Emmy Noether and [Friedrich Karl Schmidt](wikipedia://en/36084772), and he dedicated his dissertation to his elder cousin Tsebu S. Lee. Having fled to the US, Noether evaluated the dissertation in a letter as "sehr gut" (very good). As a research fellow sponsored by the China Foundation for the Promotion of Education and Culture, Tsen did a postdoctoral research with [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) at [Hamburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_University) for a year. There he became friend with [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905), who was a graduate student back then. Chern remembered him as a cordial and open-minded person well-liked by everyone.[^o16]

Tsen returned to China in July 1935 and was invited by [Chen Jiangong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Jiangong) to [National Chekiang University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Chekiang_University) in [Hangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou) as professor in the area of algebra. Chen was Tsen's teacher at Wuchang Senior Normal College and had encouraged Tsen to study in Germany. Tsen taught a course on algebra and a course on group theory based on the German textbooks of [van der Waerden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_der_Waerden) and [Andreas Speiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Speiser) respectively. As the books were in German, it was not easy for the students to understand, so he edited the notes taken by his student [Chuan-Chih Hsiung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuan-Chih_Hsiung) and printed out for the students.[^o19]

In 1936, Tsen published his third paper in the journal of the new [Chinese Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Mathematical_Society). The paper contained the work that he had done in Hamburg, and he dedicated it to the memory of his advisor Noether, who died in the previous year. The paper was hardly known outside China before 1970s, and the results therein were rediscovered by [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991) in his dissertation. [Ernst Witt](wikipedia://en/2619911), who was Tsen's friend and had also been a student of Noether, always talked about Tsen's results in his algebra lectures and would correct others if they attributed them to Lang but not Tsen, thus helped bring attention to this paper.[^o16]

Tsen and Chen fell out because of a failed matching of Tsen and Chen's younger sister for marriage.[^o20] In 1937, Tsen left Chekiang University and was invited by [National Beiyang Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peiyang_University) to become a professor. That year, the [full-scale Japanese invasion of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) started, and the school was evacuated from [Tianjin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianjin) to [Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an). Tsen went to Xi'an to take up his post. The school merged with some other evacuated universities to form National Xi'an Provisory University. The new university moved to [Hanzhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanzhong) and was renamed to [National Northwestern Associated University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Northwestern_Associated_University), and it moved again to [Chenggu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chenggu). The university soon split into several schools, one of which was [National Northwestern Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Institute_of_Technology), and Tsen became a professor of this school.

He married a high-school chemistry teacher Qin Hesui (秦禾穗) in Nanchang in 1937.[^o19] His wife suffered a miscarriage on the long and difficult journey over mountainous terrains to Xichang.[^o16] They had no children. He adopted a nephew as his son.[^o19]

He bought a lot of mathematics books while in Germany, and he brought the books and his manuscripts back to China in seven full metal trunks. After the start of war, he kept them in his relative's home at a village in Xinjian, Jiangxi. Unfortunately, when the village had fallen, all his seven trunks of books and manuscripts were burnt by the Japanese invaders.[^o19][^o21]

In 1939, [Shu-tien Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu-tien_Li), former president of Beiyang Institute of Technology and the president of the newly founded [National Xikang Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Xikang_Institute_of_Technology), invited Tsen to be a professor at the new school. The campus of the school was temples scattered on Mount Lu (Sichuan) in the suburb of [Xichang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xichang) in [Xikang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xikang) Province.[^o16] Tsen had a chronic stomach problem, and his condition was made worse by poor living condition and shortage of medical supplies in time of war. Tsen died of a [stomach ulcer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach_ulcer) in [Xichang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xichang), [Xikang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xikang)[^o19] on October 1, 1940,[^o17] and the school held a memorial service for him on November 18, 1940.[^o18][^n2]

*Image: Ningshu News October 1940 Zeng Jiongzhi died.jpg.* Tsen's death reported by the journal *Xin Ningyuan Yuekang*, Vol. 1, No. 3.

## Publications {#Publications}

- Tsen, Chiungtze C. *Divisionsalgebren über Funktionenkörpern.* Nachr. Ges. Wiss. Göttingen, Math.-Phys. Kl. I, No.44, II, No.48, 335–339 (1933).
- Tsen, Chiungtze C. *Algebren über Funktionenkörpern.* Göttingen: Diss. 19 S. (1934).
- Tsen, Chiungtze C. *Zur Stufentheorie der quasialgebraisch-Abgeschlossenheit kommutativer Körper.* J. Chin. Math. Soc. 1, 81–92 (1936).

A Chinese translation of these three papers was published in a book in memory of Tsen.[^o19]
