# Oscillation theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Oscillation theory"}

Oscillation theory (disambiguation) In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753), a nontrivial solution to an [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753)

:::

$$F(x,y,y',\ \dots,\ y^{(n-1)})=y^{(n)} \quad x \in [0,+\infty)$$

is called **oscillating** if it has an infinite number of [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function); otherwise it is called **non-oscillating**. The differential equation is called **oscillating** if it has an oscillating solution. The number of roots carries also information on the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137) of associated [boundary value problems](wikipedia://en/436779).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The differential equation

$$y'' + y = 0$$

is oscillating as sin(*x*) is a solution.

:::

## Connection with spectral theory {#Connection_with_spectral_theory}

Oscillation theory was initiated by [Jacques Charles François Sturm](wikipedia://en/1181554) in his investigations of [Sturm–Liouville problems](wikipedia://en/490990) from 1836. There he showed that the n'th eigenfunction of a Sturm–Liouville problem has precisely n-1 roots. For the one-dimensional [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874) the question about oscillation/non-oscillation answers the question whether the eigenvalues accumulate at the bottom of the continuous spectrum.

## Relative oscillation theory {#Relative_oscillation_theory}

In 1996 [Gesztesy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Gesztesy)–[Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Simon)–[Teschl](wikipedia://en/28661853) showed that the number of roots of the [Wronski determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wronski_determinant) of two eigenfunctions of a Sturm–Liouville problem gives the number of eigenvalues between the corresponding eigenvalues. It was later on generalized by Krüger–Teschl to the case of two eigenfunctions of two different Sturm–Liouville problems. The investigation of the number of roots of the Wronski determinant of two solutions is known as relative oscillation theory.
