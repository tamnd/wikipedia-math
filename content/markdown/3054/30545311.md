# Discrete transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Discrete transform"}

In [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), **discrete transforms** (or **discrete integral transform**) are [mathematical transforms](wikipedia://en/430912), often [linear transforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transform), of signals between discrete domains, such as between discrete time and discrete frequency.[^o2]

:::

Many common [integral transforms](wikipedia://en/430912) used in signal processing have their discrete counterparts. For example, for the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) the counterpart is the [discrete Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/8811).

In addition to [spectral analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_spectrum#Spectrum_analysis) of signals, discrete transforms play important role in [data compression](wikipedia://en/8013), [signal detection](wikipedia://en/1156527), [digital filtering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_filter) and [correlation](wikipedia://en/157057) analysis.[^o3] The [discrete cosine transform](wikipedia://en/59962) (DCT) is the most widely used [transform coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transform_coding) compression algorithm in [digital media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_media), followed by the [discrete wavelet transform](wikipedia://en/857564) (DWT).

Transforms between a discrete domain and a continuous domain are not discrete transforms. For example, the [discrete-time Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/1216914) and the [Z-transform](wikipedia://en/171589), from discrete time to continuous frequency, and the [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038), from continuous time to discrete frequency, are outside the class of discrete transforms.

Classical signal processing deals with one-dimensional discrete transforms. Other application areas, such as [image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_processing), [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision), [high-definition television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_television), visual telephony, etc. make use of two-dimensional and in general, multidimensional discrete transforms.
