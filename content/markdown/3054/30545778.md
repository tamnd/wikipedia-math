# Henry Mann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henry Mann"}

**Henry Berthold Mann** (27 October 1905, [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866) – 1 February 2000, [Tucson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson))[^o2] was a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University). Mann proved the [Schnirelmann-Landau conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mann's_theorem) in number theory, and as a result earned the 1946 [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177). He and his student [D. Ransom Whitney](wikipedia://en/80159700) developed the [("Mann-Whitney") *U*-statistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mann–Whitney_U) of [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369).[^o3] Mann published the first mathematical book on the [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541): (Mann 1949).[^o12]

:::

## Early life of a number theorist {#Early_life_of_a_number_theorist}

Born in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family, Mann earned his Ph.D. degree in mathematics in 1935 from the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) under the supervision of [Philipp Furtwängler](wikipedia://en/895349).[^o3] Mann immigrated to the United States in 1938, and lived in New York, supporting himself by tutoring students.[^o3]

In [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333), Mann proved the [Schnirelmann–Landau conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mann's_theorem) on the [asymptotic density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_density) of [sumsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumset) in 1942.[^o7] By doing so he established [Mann's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mann's_theorem) and earned the 1946 [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177).[^o3][^o8]

## Statistics {#Statistics}

In 1942 the [Carnegie Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Corporation_of_New_York) awarded Mann a fellowship to learn statistics while assisting the [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476) group of [Harold Hotelling](wikipedia://en/935621) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). His group also supported [Abraham Wald](wikipedia://en/1239430), and Wald and Mann collaborated on several papers. In statistics, Mann is known for the [("Mann–Whitney") *U*-statistic](wikipedia://en/433164) and its associated [hypothesis test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis_test) for [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369). Collaborating with Wald, Mann developed the [Mann–Wald theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mann–Wald_theorem) of [asymptotic statistics](wikipedia://en/26024794) and [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390).[^o3]

Mann wrote the first mathematical book on the [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541) (Mann 1949), whose principles allowed later statisticians to design and to analyze customized experiments. Like contemporary "self-help" and "how to" books, the earlier books gave easy-to-follow examples but little theory beyond exhortations to follow three principles of [Ronald A. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_A._Fisher)—to "[replicate](wikipedia://en/10306262)", to "establish control" (for example with [blocking](wikipedia://en/1188184)), and to "[randomize](wikipedia://en/65462)" ([assignment of treatments to units](wikipedia://en/2940730)). Earlier books provided useful examples of designed experiments along with the design's [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634), but no basis for constructing new designs for new problems, according to (Johnson 1951).[^o12]

According to Denis Conniffe:

Ronald A. Fisher was "interested in application and in the popularization of statistical methods and his early book *[Statistical Methods for Research Workers](wikipedia://en/1645218)*, published in 1925, went through many editions and motivated and influenced the practical use of statistics in many fields of study. His *Design of Experiments* (1935) \[promoted\] statistical technique and application. In that book he emphasized examples and how to design experiments systematically from a statistical point of view. The mathematical justification of the methods described was not stressed and, indeed, proofs were often barely sketched or omitted altogether ..., a fact which led H. B. Mann to fill the gaps with a rigorous mathematical treatment in his well known treatise, (Mann 1949)."[^o14]

## Later life {#Later_life}

In 1946 Mann returned to Ohio State University, where he served as a professor of mathematics until his retirement in 1964. Mann then became a professor at the [U.S. Army's Mathematics Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_Hall_bombing#Army_Mathematics_Research_Center) at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) 1964–1971. Mann was professor at the University of Arizona from 1971 to 1975.[^o3] His doctoral students include [George Marsaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Marsaglia) and [William Yslas Vélez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Yslas_Vélez).
