# Weighted catenary

*Image: St Louis night expblend cropped.jpg.* The [Gateway Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_Arch) is a weighted catenary: thick at the bottom, thin at the top.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weighted catenary"}

A **weighted catenary** (also **flattened catenary**, was defined by [William Rankine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Rankine) as **transformed catenary**[^o2] and thus sometimes called **Rankine curve**[^o3]) is a [catenary](wikipedia://en/7163) curve, but of a special form: while a catenary is the curve formed by a chain under its own weight, a weighted catenary is the curve formed if the chain's weight is not consistent along its length. Formally, a "regular" catenary has the equation

:::

$$y = a \, \cosh \left(\frac{x}{a}\right) = \frac{a\left(e^\frac{x}{a} + e^{-\frac{x}{a}}\right)}{2}$$

for a given value of *a*. A *weighted catenary* has the equation

$$y = b \, \cosh \left(\frac{x}{a}\right) = \frac{b\left(e^\frac{x}{a} + e^{-\frac{x}{a}}\right)}{2}$$

and now *two* constants enter: *a* and *b*.

## Significance {#Significance}

*Image: Kette Kettenkurve Catenary 2008 PD.JPG.* A hanging [chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain) is a regular [catenary](wikipedia://en/7163) — and is not weighted.

A freestanding [catenary arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catenary_arch) has a uniform thickness. However, if

1. the arch is not of uniform thickness,[^o5]
1. the arch supports more than its own weight,[^o6]
1. or if gravity varies,[^o7]

it becomes more complex. A weighted catenary is needed.

The [aspect ratio](wikipedia://en/1021) of a weighted catenary (or other curve) describes a rectangular frame containing the selected fragment of the curve theoretically continuing to the infinity. [^o8][^o9]

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [Gateway Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_Arch) in the American city of [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis), [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri), is the most famous example of a weighted catenary.

[Simple suspension bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_suspension_bridge) use weighted catenaries.[^o9]
