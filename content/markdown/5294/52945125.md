# First and second fundamental theorems of invariant theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="First and second fundamental theorems of invariant theory"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **first and second fundamental theorems of invariant theory** concern the generators and relations of the [ring of invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_invariants) in the [ring of polynomial functions](wikipedia://en/38608533) for [classical groups](wikipedia://en/10172878) (roughly, the first concerns the generators and the second the relations).[^o2] The theorems are among the most important results of [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762).

:::

Classically the theorems are [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). But [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621)-free invariant theory extends the theorems to a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of arbitrary characteristic.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="First fundamental theorem for \\operatorname{GL}(V)"}

## First fundamental theorem for $\operatorname{GL}(V)$ {#First_fundamental_theorem_for_\operatorname{GL}(V)}

The theorem states that the [ring of $\operatorname{GL}(V)$-invariant polynomial functions](wikipedia://en/38608533) on ${V^*}^p \oplus V^q$ is generated by the functions $\langle \alpha_i | v_j \rangle$, where $\alpha_i$ are in $V^*$ and $v_j \in V$.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Second fundamental theorem for general linear group"}

## Second fundamental theorem for general linear group {#Second_fundamental_theorem_for_general_linear_group}

Let *V*, *W* be [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) over the complex numbers. Then the only $\operatorname{GL}(V) \times \operatorname{GL}(W)$-invariant [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) in $\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{hom}(V, W)]$ are the determinant ideal $I_k = \mathbb{C}[\operatorname{hom}(V, W)]D_k$ generated by the [determinants](wikipedia://en/8468) of all the $k \times k$-[minors](wikipedia://en/262107).[^o9]

:::
