# Luce's choice axiom

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Luce's choice axiom"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Luce's choice axiom** states that the relative [odds](wikipedia://en/172069) of selecting one item over another from a pool of many items are not affected by the presence or absence of other items in the pool. Selection of this kind is said to have "[independence from irrelevant alternatives](wikipedia://en/259105)" (IIA).[^o2] It was formulated by [R. Duncan Luce](wikipedia://en/2082723) in 1959.[^o3]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

Consider a set $X$ of possible outcomes, and consider a selection rule such that for any $a\in A \subset X$ with $A$ a finite set, the selector selects $a$ from $A$ with probability $P(a \mid A)$.

Luce proposed two choice axioms. The first one is usually called "[independence from irrelevant alternatives](wikipedia://en/259105)" (IIA), and the second one is usually referred to as "Luce's choice axiom".[^o2]

1. **Independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA)**:

For any $a, b \in B \subset A$, if $P(a\mid A) = 0, P(b\mid A) > 0$, then we still have $P(a\mid B) = 0$.

1. **Path independence**:

For any $a \in B \subset A$,

$$P(a \mid A) = P(a\mid B)\sum_{b\in B}P(b\mid A)$$

Note that IIA implies path independence.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement cue=named-heading name="Matching law formulation"}

### Matching law formulation {#Matching_law_formulation}

Define the [matching law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matching_law) selection rule $P(a\mid A) = \frac{u(a)}{\sum_{a'\in A} u(a')}$, for some "value" function $u: A \to (0, \infty)$. This is sometimes called the [softmax](wikipedia://en/6152185) function, or the [Boltzmann distribution](wikipedia://en/4107).

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**: Any matching law selection rule satisfies Luce's choice axiom. Conversely, if $P(a\mid A)> 0$ for all $a\in A \subset X$, then Luce's choice axiom implies that it is a matching law selection rule.

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

In [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390) and [marketing science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing_science), this axiom provides the theoretical foundation for models that calculate a consumer's probability of choosing one brand over another based on its [utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_%28economics%29).[^n1]

In [behavioral psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behaviorism), it is used to model response behavior in the form of [matching law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matching_law).

In [cognitive science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_science), it is used to model approximately rational decision processes.

:::
