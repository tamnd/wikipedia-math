# Gottfried Achenwall

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gottfried Achenwall"}

**Gottfried Achenwall** (20 October 1719 – 1 May 1772) was a German philosopher, historian, economist, [jurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurist) and statistician. He is counted among the inventors of statistics.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Achenwall was born in [Elbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbing) ([Elbląg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbląg))[^o4][^o5] in the [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish–Lithuanian_Commonwealth) province of [Royal Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Prussia).[^o6] Beginning in 1738 he studied in the [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena), [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_University_of_Halle-Wittenberg), again Jena and [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University). In the years 1743 to 1746, he worked as controller in [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden). He was awarded his master's degree in 1746 by the philosophical faculty of Leipzig and went in the following to [Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Marburg) to work as assistant professor lecturing history, statistics, natural and international law. In 1748 he was called to the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) to become [extraordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraordinary_professor) of philosophy, and in 1753 he became an extraordinary professor of law and regular professor of philosophy. In 1761 he again shifted fields, becoming a professor of natural law and politics, and in 1762 he became a doctor of both laws.

*Image: Göttinger Gedenktafel - Achenwall, Gottfried.jpg.* Memorial plaque in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen)

In 1765, Achenwall became court counsellor of the Royal British and the Electoral court of [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Hanover). With financial support from King [George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III_of_the_United_Kingdom) he travelled to [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) and France in 1751 and to [Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland) and England in 1759. He died in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) on 1 May 1772, aged 52.[^o7]

In economics, he belonged to the school of "moderate [mercantilists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercantilism)"; but it is in statistics that he holds his greatest renown. The work by which he is best known is his *Staatsverfassung der Europäischen Reiche im Grundrisse* (Constitution of the Present Leading European States, 1752). In this work, he gave a comprehensive view of the constitutions of the various countries, described the condition of their agriculture, manufactures and commerce, and frequently supplied statistics in relation to these subjects. German economists claimed for him the title of "Father of Statistics"; but English writers disputed this, asserting that it ignored the prior claims of [William Petty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Petty) and other earlier writers on the subject. Achenwall gave currency to the term *Staatswissenschaft* (politics), which he proposed should mean all the knowledge necessary to statecraft or statesmanship.

## Publications {#Publications}

- *Abriß der neuen Staatswissenschaft der vornehmen Europäischen Reiche und Republiken*, 1749, in the following editions titled *Staatsverfassung der Europäischen Reiche im Grundrisse*, 1752 ff.
- (with [Johann Stephan Pütter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Stephan_Pütter):) *Naturrecht*, 1750, 1753
- *[Jus Naturae](https://books.google.com/books?id=KeIAAAAAcAAJ)*, 2 vol., 1755–56 ff, edition VII in 1781 with a preface from [Johann Henrick Christian de Selchow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Henrick_Christian_de_Selchow).
- *Grundsätze der Europäischen Geschichte, zur politischen Kenntnis der heutigen vornehmsten Staaten*, 1754, 2nd edition 1759 titled *Die Geschichte der heutigen vornehmsten Staaten im Grundrisse*, 5th edition 1779
- *Entwurf der Europäischen Staatshändel des 17. und 18. Jahrhunderts*, 1756, 4th edition 1759
- *Staatsklugheit nach ihren ersten Grundsätzen*, 1761, 4th edition 1759
- *Juris gentium Europaei practici primae lineae*, 1775, unfinished.
