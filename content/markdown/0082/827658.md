# Bundle (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **bundle** is a generalization of a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) dropping the condition of a [local product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_product) structure. The requirement of a local product structure rests on the bundle having a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450). Without this requirement, more general objects can be considered bundles. For example, one can consider a bundle π: *E* → *B* with *E* and *B* [sets](wikipedia://en/26691). It is no longer true that the [preimages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimage) $\pi^{-1}(x)$ must all look alike, unlike fiber bundles, where the fibers must all be [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) (in the case of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524)) and [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A bundle is a triple $(E, p, B)$ where $E, B$ are sets and $p : E \to B$ is a map.[^o4]

- $E$ is called the **total space**
- $B$ is the **base space** of the bundle
- $p$ is the **projection**

This definition of a bundle is quite unrestrictive. For instance, the [empty function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_function) defines a bundle. Nonetheless it serves well to introduce the basic terminology, and every type of bundle has the basic ingredients of above with restrictions on $E, p, B$ and usually there is additional structure.

For each $b \in B, p^{-1}(b)$ is the **fibre** or **fiber** of the bundle over $b$.

A bundle $(E*, p*, B*)$ is a **subbundle** of $(E, p, B)$ if $B* \subset B, E* \subset E$ and $p* = p|_{E*}$.

A [cross section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_%28fibre_bundle%29) is a map $s : B \to E$ such that $p(s(b)) = b$ for each $b \in B$, that is, $s(b) \in p^{-1}(b)$.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If $E$ and $B$ are [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) and $p$ is smooth, [surjective](wikipedia://en/27873) and in addition a [submersion](wikipedia://en/356413), then the bundle is a [fibered manifold](wikipedia://en/3247635). Here and in the following examples, the smoothness condition may be weakened to continuous or sharpened to analytic, or it could be anything reasonable, like continuously differentiable ($C^{1}$), in between.
- If for each two points $b_{1}$ and $b_{2}$ in the base, the corresponding fibers $p^{-1}(b_{1})$ and $p^{-1}(b_{2})$ are [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent), then the bundle is a [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826).
- If for each two points $b_{1}$ and $b_{2}$ in the base, the corresponding fibers $p^{-1}(b_{1})$ and $p^{-1}(b_{2})$ are [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic), and in addition the bundle satisfies certain conditions of *local triviality* outlined in the pertaining linked articles, then the bundle is a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526). Usually there is additional structure, e.g. a [group structure](wikipedia://en/19447) or a [vector space structure](wikipedia://en/32370#Definition), on the fibers besides a topology. Then is required that the homeomorphism is an isomorphism with respect to that structure, and the conditions of local triviality are sharpened accordingly.
- A [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) is a fiber bundle endowed with a right [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) with certain properties. One example of a principal bundle is the [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550).
- If for each two points $b_{1}$ and $b_{2}$ in the base, the corresponding fibers $p^{-1}(b_{1})$ and $p^{-1}(b_{2})$ are [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) of the same dimension, then the bundle is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) if the appropriate conditions of local triviality are satisfied. The [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) is an example of a vector bundle.

:::

## Bundle objects {#Bundle_objects}

More generally, bundles or **bundle objects** can be defined in any [category](wikipedia://en/53759): in a category **C**, a bundle is simply an [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539) π: *E* → *B*. If the category is not [concrete](wikipedia://en/167001), then the notion of a preimage of the map is not necessarily available. Therefore these bundles may have no fibers at all, although for sufficiently well behaved categories they do; for instance, for a category with [pullbacks](wikipedia://en/841689) and a [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_object) 1 the points of *B* can be identified with morphisms *p*:1→*B* and the fiber of *p* is obtained as the pullback of *p* and π. The category of bundles over *B* is a subcategory of the [slice category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slice_category) (**C**↓*B*) of objects over *B*, while the category of bundles without fixed base object is a subcategory of the [comma category](wikipedia://en/840950) (*C*↓*C*) which is also the [functor category](wikipedia://en/407039) **C**², the category of [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) in **C**.

The category of smooth vector bundles is a bundle object over the category of smooth manifolds in **Cat**, the [category of small categories](wikipedia://en/2072741). The [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) taking each manifold to its [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) is an example of a section of this bundle object.
