# Marion Ballantyne White

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marion Ballantyne White"}

**Marion Ballantyne White** (1871–1958) was an American mathematician and university professor. She was one of the few American women to earn her [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in mathematics before [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Marion White was born March 28, 1871, in [Peoria, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoria,_Illinois) to two teachers, Jennie E. McLaren and Samuel Holmes White.[^o4]

After her secondary school studies in Peoria, she attended [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College) in 1888 but left after one year to teach at a public school in Peoria. From 1890 to 1893 she studied at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), where she received a Bachelor of Philosophy degree in 1893. She then taught high school in [Pueblo, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pueblo,_Colorado) for two years, before returning to join the faculty at Peoria High School. From 1901 to 1908 she taught at the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign), and in 1906 she earned her master's degree from the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison). From 1908 to 1910 she earned her doctorate in mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) under the direction of [Gilbert Ames Bliss](wikipedia://en/3011234) as his third doctoral student. Her dissertation was titled: *The Dependence of the Focal Point on Curvature in Space Problems of the Calculus of Variations*.[^o5]

She began teaching at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas) in 1910. In 1914 she relocated to Michigan State Normal College (now [Eastern Michigan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Michigan_University)) in [Ypsilanti, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ypsilanti,_Michigan) and assumed the position of associate professor of mathematics and dean of women until 1918 when she went on to teach at [Carleton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_College) in [Northfield, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northfield,_Minnesota). She was dean of women there from 1922 to 1924 and also served as associate professor until 1937.[^o4]

After her retirement at 66 in 1937, she moved to [Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena,_California), California, where she spent the last two decades of her life. Marion White died there on January 30, 1958.[^o4] After she died the Carleton alumni magazine said "she was an especially successful classroom teacher known... for the personal stimulus and encouragement she gave the students.".[^o4]
