# Turán–Kubilius inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Turán–Kubilius inequality"}

The **Turán–Kubilius inequality** is a [mathematical theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_theorem) in [probabilistic number theory](wikipedia://en/3644492). It is useful for proving results about the [normal order of an arithmetic function](wikipedia://en/18747595).[^o2] The theorem was proved in a [special case](wikipedia://en/31308010) in 1934 by [Pál Turán](wikipedia://en/614998) and generalized in 1956 and 1964 by [Jonas Kubilius](wikipedia://en/22469208).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

This formulation is from [Tenenbaum](wikipedia://en/36258646).[^o2]p. 302 Other formulations are in Narkiewicz[^o5]p. 243 and in Cojocaru & Murty.[^o6]p. 45–46

Suppose *f* is an [additive](wikipedia://en/87372) complex-valued [arithmetic function](wikipedia://en/3170), and write *p* for an arbitrary prime and $\nu$ for an arbitrary positive integer. Write

$$A(x)=\sum_{p^\nu \le x} f(p^\nu) p^{-\nu}(1-p^{-1})$$

and

$$B(x)^2 = \sum_{p^\nu \le x} \left| f(p^\nu) \right| ^2 p^{-\nu}.$$

Then there is a function ε(*x*) that goes to zero when *x* goes to infinity, and such that for *x* ≥ 2 we have

$$\frac{1}{x} \sum_{n \le x} |f(n) - A(x)|^2 \le (2 + \varepsilon(x)) B(x)^2.$$

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Applications of the theorem"}

## Applications of the theorem {#Applications_of_the_theorem}

[Turán](wikipedia://en/614998) developed the inequality to create a simpler proof of the [Hardy–Ramanujan theorem](wikipedia://en/14743376) about the [normal order](wikipedia://en/18747595) of the number ω(*n*) of distinct prime divisors of an integer *n*.[^o2]p. 316 There is an exposition of Turán's proof in Hardy & Wright, §22.11.[^o9] Tenenbaum[^o2]p. 305–308 gives a proof of the Hardy–Ramanujan theorem using the Turán–Kubilius inequality and states without proof several other applications.

:::
