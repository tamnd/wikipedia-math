# Omnitruncated polyhedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Omnitruncated polyhedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **[omnitruncated](wikipedia://en/7083690) [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470)** is a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [quasiregular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8810651). When they are [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117), they produce the [snub polyhedra](wikipedia://en/3752558).

:::

All omnitruncated polyhedra are considered as [zonohedra](wikipedia://en/669402). They have [Wythoff symbol](wikipedia://en/3691548) *p q r |* and [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719) as *2p.2q.2r*.

More generally, an omnitruncated polyhedron is a **bevel** operator in [Conway polyhedron notation](wikipedia://en/5021705).

### Other even-sided nonconvex polyhedra {#Other_even-sided_nonconvex_polyhedra}

There are 8 nonconvex forms with mixed [Wythoff symbols](wikipedia://en/3691548) *p q (r s) |*, and bow-tie shaped [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719), 2p.2q.-2q.-2p. They are not true omnitruncated polyhedra. Instead, the true omnitruncates *p q r |* or *p q s |* have coinciding 2*r*-gonal or 2*s*-gonal faces that must be removed respectively to form a proper polyhedron. All these polyhedra are one-sided, i.e. [non-orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-orientable). The *p q r |* degenerate Wythoff symbols are listed first, followed by the actual mixed Wythoff symbols.

## General omnitruncations (bevel) {#General_omnitruncations_(bevel)}

Omnitruncations are also called cantitruncations or truncated rectifications (tr), and Conway's bevel (b) operator. When applied to nonregular polyhedra, new polyhedra can be generated, for example these 2-uniform polyhedra:
