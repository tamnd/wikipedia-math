# Lindley equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lindley equation"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **Lindley equation**, **Lindley recursion** or **Lindley process**[^o2] is a [discrete-time stochastic process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete-time_stochastic_process) *A*<sub>*n*</sub> where *n* takes [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) values and:

:::

*A*<sub>*n* + 1</sub> = max(0, *A*<sub>*n*</sub> + *B*<sub>*n*</sub>).

Processes of this form can be used to describe the waiting time of customers in a [queue](wikipedia://en/50578) or evolution of a queue length over time. The idea was first proposed in the discussion following [Kendall](wikipedia://en/4631370)'s 1951 paper.[^o3][^o4]

## Waiting times {#Waiting_times}

In [Dennis Lindley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Lindley)'s first paper on the subject[^o6] the equation is used to describe waiting times experienced by customers in a queue with the First-In First-Out (FIFO) discipline.

*W*<sub>*n* + 1</sub> = max(0,*W*<sub>*n*</sub> + *U*<sub>*n*</sub>)

where

- *T*<sub>*n*</sub> is the time between the *n*th and (*n*+1)th arrivals,
- *S*<sub>*n*</sub> is the service time of the *n*th customer, and
- *U*<sub>*n*</sub> = *S*<sub>*n*</sub> − *T*<sub>*n*</sub>
- *W*<sub>*n*</sub> is the waiting time of the *n*th customer.

The first customer does not need to wait so *W*<sub>1</sub> = 0. Subsequent customers will have to wait if they arrive at a time before the previous customer has been served.

## Queue lengths {#Queue_lengths}

The evolution of the queue length process can also be written in the form of a Lindley equation.

## Integral equation {#Integral_equation}

**Lindley's integral equation** is a relationship satisfied by the stationary waiting time distribution F(*x*) in a [G/G/1 queue](wikipedia://en/38677129).

$$F(x) = \int_{0^-}^\infty K(x-y)F(\text{d}y) \quad x \geq 0$$

Where K(*x*) is the distribution function of the random variable denoting the difference between the (*k* - 1)th customer's arrival and the inter-arrival time between (*k* - 1)th and *k*th customers. The [Wiener–Hopf method](wikipedia://en/4363034) can be used to solve this expression.[^o9]
