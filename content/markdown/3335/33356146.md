# Edward Lindsay Ince

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edward Lindsay Ince"}

Prof **Edward Lindsay Ince** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (30 November 1891 – 16 March 1941) was a British mathematician who worked on differential equations, especially those with periodic coefficients such as the [Mathieu equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathieu_equation) and the [Lamé equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamé_equation). He introduced the [Ince equation](wikipedia://en/33358296), a generalization of the [Mathieu equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathieu_equation).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Amblecote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amblecote) in [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire) on 30 November 1891, the only son of Caroline Clara Cutler and her husband Edward Ince, an [Inland Revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Revenue) officer. The family moved to [Criccieth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criccieth) near [Portmadoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmadoc) in Wales soon after he was born.[^o3] His family moved to [Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth,_Scotland) in Scotland around 1901, living at 6 Queens Avenue in the Craigie district, to the south-west of the city.[^o4] He attended [Perth Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Academy).[^o5]

He studied mathematics at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) from 1909, graduating in 1913. Failing the medical for [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) service, he won a scholarship and went to the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) where he graduated with an MA, and won the [Smith's Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize) in 1918. That same year he obtained a DSc from the University of Edinburgh.[^o6] He then began lecturing in mathematics at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds) In 1920 he moved to the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool).

He was elected to the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1923. His proposers were [Sir Edmund Taylor Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Taylor_Whittaker), [David Gibb](wikipedia://en/50969533), [Arthur Crichton Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Crichton_Mitchell) and [Ralph Allan Sampson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Allan_Sampson). The Society awarded him the [Makdougall Brisbane Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makdougall_Brisbane_Prize) for 1938–1940 for his work on periodic Lamé functions () but he died before he received the award.[^o5]

In 1926 he made a dramatic move, and accepted a professorship at the [Egyptian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_University) in [Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo). With a young family, and health problems arising from the [extreme heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_wave), he returned to Britain in 1931 to take a post at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) for one year then spent two years at [Imperial College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College,_London) before returning to Edinburgh permanently in 1935.

He died in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) on 16 March 1941.

## Family {#Family}

In 1923 he married Phyllis Fry. They had two daughters, Monica and Elizabeth. Monica Hughes(OC) went on to become a renowned author of children's and young adult books.[^o9]
