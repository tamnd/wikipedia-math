# Philonides of Laodicea

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Philonides of Laodicea"}

**Philonides** (Φιλωνίδης, c. 200 – c. 130 BCE) of [Laodicea in Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laodicea_in_Syria), was an [Epicurean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicurean) philosopher and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who lived in the [Seleucid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid) court during the reigns of [Antiochus IV Epiphanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiochus_IV_Epiphanes) and [Demetrius I Soter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrius_I_Soter).

:::

He is known principally from a *Life of Philonides*, which was discovered among the charred [papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyri_from_Herculaneum) scrolls at the [Villa of the Papyri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_of_the_Papyri) at [Herculaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum).[^o2] Philonides was born into a family with good connections with the Seleucid court.[^o3] He is said to have been taught by Eudemus and [Dionysodorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysodorus) the mathematician.[^o4] Philonides attempted to convert Antiochus IV Epiphanes to [Epicureanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicureanism), and later instructed his nephew, Demetrius I Soter, in philosophy.[^o3] Philonides was highly honoured in the court, and he is also known from various stone inscriptions.[^o5]

He was renowned as a mathematician, and is mentioned by [Apollonius of Perga](wikipedia://en/257242) in the preface to the second book of his *Conics*.[^o4][^o6]

Philonides was a zealous collector of the works of [Epicurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicurus) and his colleagues, and is said to have published over 100 treatises, probably compilations of the works he collected.[^o7]
