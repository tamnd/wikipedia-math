# Sheaf of modules

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sheaf of modules"}

In mathematics, a **sheaf of *O*-modules** or simply an ***O*-module** over a [ringed space](wikipedia://en/248014) (*X*, *O*) is a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of [abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_groups) *F* such that, for any open subset *U* of *X*, *F*(*U*) is an *O*(*U*)-module and the restriction maps *F*(*U*) → *F*(*V*) are compatible with the restriction maps *O*(*U*) → *O*(*V*): the restriction of *fs* is the restriction of *f* times the restriction of *s* for any *f* in *O*(*U*) and *s* in *F*(*U*).

:::

The standard case is when *X* is a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) and *O* its structure sheaf. If *O* is the [constant sheaf](wikipedia://en/2783581) $\underline{\mathbf{Z}}$, then a sheaf of *O*-modules is the same as a [sheaf of abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_of_abelian_groups) (i.e., an **abelian sheaf**).

If *X* is the [prime spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_spectrum) of a ring *R*, then any *R*-module defines an *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-module (called an **associated sheaf**) in a natural way. Similarly, if *R* is a [graded ring](wikipedia://en/103094) and *X* is the [Proj](wikipedia://en/2285000) of *R*, then any graded module defines an *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-module in a natural way. *O*-modules arising in such a fashion are examples of [quasi-coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-coherent_sheaves), and in fact, on affine or projective schemes, all quasi-coherent sheaves are obtained this way.

Sheaves of modules over a ringed space form an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063).[^o2] Moreover, this category has [enough injectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enough_injectives),[^o3] and consequently one can and does define the [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) $\operatorname{H}^i(X, -)$ as the *i*-th [right derived functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_derived_functor) of the [global section functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_section_functor) $\Gamma(X, -)$.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Given a ringed space (*X*, *O*), if *F* is an *O*-submodule of *O*, then it is called the sheaf of ideals or [ideal sheaf](wikipedia://en/9508138) of *O*, since for each open subset *U* of *X*, *F*(*U*) is an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of the ring *O*(*U*).
- Let *X* be a [smooth variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety) of dimension *n*. Then the [tangent sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_sheaf) of *X* is the dual of the [cotangent sheaf](wikipedia://en/44901757) $\Omega_X$ and the [canonical sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_sheaf) $\omega_X$ is the *n*-th exterior power ([determinant](wikipedia://en/984307)) of $\Omega_X$.
- A [sheaf of algebras](wikipedia://en/56371096) is a sheaf of modules that is also a sheaf of rings.

:::

## Operations {#Operations}

Let (*X*, *O*) be a ringed space. If *F* and *G* are *O*-modules, then their tensor product, denoted by

$F \otimes_O G$ or $F \otimes G$,

is the *O*-module that is the sheaf associated to the presheaf $U \mapsto F(U) \otimes_{O(U)} G(U).$ (To see that [sheafification](wikipedia://en/245466#Turning_a_presheaf_into_a_sheaf) cannot be avoided, compute the global sections of $O(1) \otimes O(-1) = O$ where *O*(1) is [Serre's twisting sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_twisting_sheaf) on a projective space.)

Similarly, if *F* and *G* are *O*-modules, then

$$\mathcal{H}om_O(F, G)$$

denotes the *O*-module that is the sheaf $U \mapsto \operatorname{Hom}_{O|_U}(F|_U, G|_U)$.[^o9] In particular, the *O*-module

$$\mathcal{H}om_O(F, O)$$

is called the **dual module** of *F* and is denoted by $\check F$. Note: for any *O*-modules *E*, *F*, there is a canonical homomorphism

$$\check{E} \otimes F \to \mathcal{H}om_O(E, F),$$

which is an isomorphism if *E* is a [locally free sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_free_sheaf) of finite rank. In particular, if *L* is locally free of rank one (such *L* is called an [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859) or a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366)),[^o10] then this reads:

$$\check{L} \otimes L \simeq O,$$

implying the isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves form a group. This group is called the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) of *X* and is canonically identified with the first cohomology group $\operatorname{H}^1(X, \mathcal{O}^*)$ (by the standard argument with [Čech cohomology](wikipedia://en/856614)).

If *E* is a locally free sheaf of finite rank, then there is an *O*-linear map $\check{E} \otimes E \simeq \operatorname{End}_O(E) \to O$ given by the pairing; it is called the [trace map](wikipedia://en/62338906) of *E*.

For any *O*-module *F*, the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628), [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) and [symmetric algebra](wikipedia://en/654098) of *F* are defined in the same way. For example, the *k*-th exterior power

$$\bigwedge^k F$$

is the sheaf associated to the presheaf $U \mapsto \bigwedge^k_{O(U)} F(U)$. If *F* is locally free of rank *n*, then $\bigwedge^n F$ is called the [determinant line bundle](wikipedia://en/984307) (though technically [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859)) of *F*, denoted by det(*F*). There is a natural perfect pairing:

$$\bigwedge^r F \otimes \bigwedge^{n-r} F \to \det(F).$$

Let *f*: (*X*, *O*) →(*X'*, *O'*) be a morphism of ringed spaces. If *F* is an *O*-module, then the [direct image sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_image_sheaf) $f_* F$ is an *O'*-module through the natural map *O'* →*f*<sub>\*</sub>*O* (such a natural map is part of the data of a morphism of ringed spaces.)

If *G* is an *O'*-module, then the module inverse image $f^* G$ of *G* is the *O*-module given as the tensor product of modules:

$$f^{-1} G \otimes_{f^{-1} O'} O$$

where $f^{-1} G$ is the [inverse image sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_image_sheaf) of *G* and $f^{-1} O' \to O$ is obtained from $O' \to f_* O$ by [adjuction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor).

There is an adjoint relation between $f_*$ and $f^*$: for any *O*-module *F* and *O'*-module *G*,

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{O}(f^* G, F) \simeq \operatorname{Hom}_{O'}(G, f_*F)$$

as abelian group. There is also the [projection formula](wikipedia://en/38275478): for an *O*-module *F* and a locally free *O'*-module *E* of finite rank,

$$f_*(F \otimes f^*E) \simeq f_* F \otimes E.$$

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Let (*X*, *O*) be a ringed space. An *O*-module *F* is said to be **generated by global sections** if there is a surjection of *O*-modules:

$$\bigoplus_{i \in I} O \to F \to 0.$$

Explicitly, this means that there are global sections *s*<sub>*i*</sub> of *F* such that the images of *s*<sub>*i*</sub> in each stalk *F*<sub>*x*</sub> generate *F*<sub>*x*</sub> as *O*<sub>*x*</sub>-module.

An example of such a sheaf is that associated in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) to an *R*-module *M*, *R* being any [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346), on the [spectrum of a ring](wikipedia://en/37291) *Spec*(*R*). Another example: according to [Cartan's theorem A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan's_theorem_A), any [coherent sheaf](wikipedia://en/603780) on a [Stein manifold](wikipedia://en/1053909) is spanned by global sections. (cf. Serre's theorem A below.) In the theory of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754), a related notion is [ample line bundle](wikipedia://en/1186896). (For example, if *L* is an ample line bundle, some power of it is generated by global sections.)

An injective *O*-module is [flasque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flasque_sheaf) (i.e., all restrictions maps *F*(*U*) → *F*(*V*) are surjective).[^o13] Since a flasque sheaf is acyclic in the category of abelian sheaves, this implies that the *i*-th right derived functor of the global section functor $\Gamma(X, -)$ in the category of *O*-modules coincides with the usual *i*-th sheaf cohomology in the category of abelian sheaves.[^o14]

:::

## Sheaf associated to a module {#Sheaf_associated_to_a_module}

Let $M$ be a module over a ring $A$. Put $X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ and write $D(f) = \{ f \ne 0 \} = \operatorname{Spec}(A[f^{-1}])$. For each pair $D(f) \subseteq D(g)$, by the [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) of localization, there is a natural map

$$\rho_{g, f}: M[g^{-1}] \to M[f^{-1}]$$

having the property that $\rho_{g, f} = \rho_{g, h} \circ \rho_{h, f}$. Then

$$D(f) \mapsto M[f^{-1}]$$

is a contravariant functor from the category whose objects are the sets *D*(*f*) and morphisms the inclusions of sets to the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748). One can show[^o17] it is in fact a [B-sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-sheaf) (i.e., it satisfies the gluing axiom) and thus defines the sheaf $\widetilde{M}$ on *X* called the sheaf associated to *M*.

The most basic example is the structure sheaf on *X*; i.e., $\mathcal{O}_X = \widetilde{A}$. Moreover, $\widetilde{M}$ has the structure of $\mathcal{O}_X = \widetilde{A}$-module and thus one gets the [exact functor](wikipedia://en/453945) $M \mapsto \widetilde{M}$ from Mod<sub>*A*</sub>, the [category of modules](wikipedia://en/11231812) over *A* to the category of modules over $\mathcal{O}_X$. It defines an [equivalence](wikipedia://en/379051) from Mod<sub>*A*</sub> to the category of [quasi-coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-coherent_sheaves) on *X*, with the inverse $\Gamma(X, -)$, the [global section functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_section_functor). When *X* is [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/2783891), the functor is an equivalence from the category of finitely generated *A*-modules to the category of coherent sheaves on *X*.

The construction has the following properties: for any *A*-modules *M*, *N*, and any morphism $\varphi:M\to N$,

- $M[f^{-1}]^{\sim} = \widetilde{M}|_{D(f)}$.[^o18]
- For any prime ideal *p* of *A*, $\widetilde{M}_p \simeq M_p$ as *O*<sub>*p*</sub> = *A*<sub>*p*</sub>-module.
- $(M \otimes_A N)^{\sim} \simeq \widetilde{M} \otimes_{\widetilde{A}} \widetilde{N}$.[^o19]
- If *M* is [finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_module), $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M, N)^{\sim} \simeq \mathcal{H}om_{\widetilde{A}}(\widetilde{M}, \widetilde{N})$.[^o19]
- $\operatorname{Hom}_A(M, N) \simeq \Gamma(X, \mathcal{H}om_{\widetilde{A}}(\widetilde{M}, \widetilde{N}))$, since the equivalence between Mod<sub>*A*</sub> and the category of quasi-coherent sheaves on *X*.
- $(\varinjlim M_i)^{\sim} \simeq \varinjlim \widetilde{M_i}$;[^o20] in particular, taking a direct sum and ~ commute.
- A sequence of *A*-modules is exact if and only if the induced sequence by $\sim$ is exact. In particular, $(\ker(\varphi))^{\sim}=\ker(\widetilde{\varphi}), (\operatorname{coker}(\varphi))^{\sim}=\operatorname{coker}(\widetilde{\varphi}), (\operatorname{im}(\varphi))^{\sim}=\operatorname{im}(\widetilde{\varphi})$.

:::

## Sheaf associated to a graded module {#Sheaf_associated_to_a_graded_module}

There is a graded analog of the construction and equivalence in the preceding section. Let *R* be a graded ring generated by degree-one elements as *R*<sub>0</sub>-algebra (*R*<sub>0</sub> means the degree-zero piece) and *M* a graded *R*-module. Let *X* be the [Proj](wikipedia://en/2285000) of *R* (so *X* is a [projective scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_scheme) if *R* is [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129)). Then there is an *O*-module $\widetilde{M}$ such that for any homogeneous element *f* of positive degree of *R*, there is a natural isomorphism

$$\widetilde{M}|_{\{f \ne 0\}} \simeq (M[f^{-1}]_0)^{\sim}$$

as sheaves of modules on the affine scheme $\{f \ne 0\} = \operatorname{Spec}(R[f^{-1}]_0)$;[^o22] in fact, this defines $\widetilde{M}$ by gluing.

::: {#o23 .example cue=inline-head name="Example"}

**Example**: Let *R*(1) be the graded *R*-module given by *R*(1)<sub>*n*</sub> = *R*<sub>*n*+1</sub>. Then $O(1) = \widetilde{R(1)}$ is called [Serre's twisting sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_twisting_sheaf), which is the dual of the [tautological line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tautological_line_bundle) if *R* is finitely generated in degree-one.

If *F* is an *O*-module on *X*, then, writing $F(n) = F \otimes O(n)$, there is a canonical homomorphism:

$$\left(\bigoplus_{n \ge 0} \Gamma(X, F(n))\right)^{\sim} \to F,$$

which is an isomorphism if and only if *F* is quasi-coherent.

:::

## Computing sheaf cohomology {#Computing_sheaf_cohomology}

Sheaf cohomology has a reputation for being difficult to calculate. Because of this, the next general fact is fundamental for any practical computation:

::: {#o25 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

Let *X* be a topological space, *F* an abelian sheaf on it and $\mathfrak{U}$ an open cover of *X* such that $\operatorname{H}^i(U_{i_0} \cap \cdots \cap U_{i_p}, F) = 0$ for any *i*, *p* and $U_{i_j}$'s in $\mathfrak{U}$. Then for any *i*,

$\operatorname{H}^i(X, F) = \operatorname{H}^{i}(C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{U}, F))$ where the right-hand side is the *i*-th [Čech cohomology](wikipedia://en/856614).

:::

[Serre's vanishing theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_vanishing_theorem)[^o26] states that if *X* is a projective variety and *F* a coherent sheaf on it, then, for sufficiently large *n*, the [Serre twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_twist) *F*(*n*) is generated by finitely many global sections. Moreover,

For each *i*, H<sup>*i*</sup>(*X*, *F*) is finitely generated over *R*<sub>0</sub>, and There is an integer *n*<sub>0</sub>, depending on *F*, such that

$$\operatorname{H}^i(X, F(n)) = 0, \, i \ge 1, n \ge n_0.$$

[^o27][^o28][^o29]

## Sheaf extension {#Sheaf_extension}

Let (*X*, *O*) be a ringed space, and let *F*, *H* be sheaves of *O*-modules on *X*. An **extension** of *H* by *F* is a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) of *O*-modules

$$0 \rightarrow F \rightarrow G \rightarrow H \rightarrow 0.$$

As with group extensions, if we fix *F* and *H*, then all equivalence classes of extensions of *H* by *F* form an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) (cf. [Baer sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baer_sum)), which is isomorphic to the [Ext group](wikipedia://en/840758) $\operatorname{Ext}_O^1(H,F)$, where the identity element in $\operatorname{Ext}_O^1(H,F)$ corresponds to the trivial extension.

In the case where *H* is *O*, we have: for any *i* ≥ 0,

$$\operatorname{H}^i(X, F) = \operatorname{Ext}_O^i(O,F),$$

since both the sides are the right derived functors of the same functor $\Gamma(X, -) = \operatorname{Hom}_O(O, -).$

**Note**: Some authors, notably Hartshorne, drop the subscript *O*.

Assume *X* is a projective scheme over a Noetherian ring. Let *F*, *G* be coherent sheaves on *X* and *i* an integer. Then there exists *n*<sub>0</sub> such that

$$\operatorname{Ext}_O^i(F, G(n)) = \Gamma(X, \mathcal{Ext}_O^i(F, G(n))), \, n \ge n_0,$$

where $\mathcal{Ext}_O$ denotes the derived functors of $\mathcal{Hom}_O$.[^o31]

### Locally free resolutions {#Locally_free_resolutions}

$\mathcal{Ext}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})$ can be readily computed for any coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ using a locally free [resolution](wikipedia://en/19745659):[^o33] given a complex

$$\cdots \to \mathcal{L}_2 \to \mathcal{L}_1 \to \mathcal{L}_0 \to \mathcal{F} \to 0$$

then

$$\mathcal{RHom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}) = \mathcal{Hom}(\mathcal{L}_\bullet,\mathcal{G})$$

hence

$$\mathcal{Ext}^k(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}) = h^k(\mathcal{Hom}(\mathcal{L}_\bullet,\mathcal{G}))$$

::: {#o35 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

#### Hypersurface {#Hypersurface}

Consider a smooth [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) $X$ of degree $d$. Then, we can compute a resolution

$$\mathcal{O}(-d) \to \mathcal{O}$$

and find that

$$\mathcal{Ext}^i(\mathcal{O}_X,\mathcal{F}) = h^i(\mathcal{Hom}(\mathcal{O}(-d) \to \mathcal{O}, \mathcal{F}))$$

#### Union of smooth complete intersections {#Union_of_smooth_complete_intersections}

Consider the scheme

$$X = \text{Proj}\left( \frac{\mathbb{C}[x_0,\ldots,x_n]}{(f)(g_1,g_2,g_3)} \right) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n$$

where $(f,g_1,g_2,g_3)$ is a smooth [complete intersection](wikipedia://en/3116835) and $\deg(f) = d$, $\deg(g_i) = e_i$. We have a complex

$$\mathcal{O}(-d-e_1-e_2-e_3) \xrightarrow{\begin{bmatrix} g_3 \\ -g_2 \\ -g_1 \end{bmatrix}} \begin{matrix} \mathcal{O}(-d-e_1-e_2) \\ \oplus \\ \mathcal{O}(-d-e_1-e_3) \\ \oplus \\ \mathcal{O}(-d-e_2-e_3) \end{matrix} \xrightarrow{\begin{bmatrix} g_2 & g_3 & 0 \\ -g_1 & 0 & -g_3 \\ 0 & -g_1 & g_2 \end{bmatrix}} \begin{matrix} \mathcal{O}(-d-e_1) \\ \oplus \\ \mathcal{O}(-d-e_2) \\ \oplus \\ \mathcal{O}(-d-e_3) \end{matrix} \xrightarrow{\begin{bmatrix} fg_1 & fg_2 & fg_3 \end{bmatrix}} \mathcal{O}$$

resolving $\mathcal{O}_X,$ which we can use to compute $\mathcal{Ext}^i(\mathcal{O}_X,\mathcal{F})$.

:::
