# Hall–Littlewood polynomials

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hall–Littlewood polynomials"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hall–Littlewood polynomials** are [symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/364460) depending on a parameter *t* and a [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_%28number_theory%29) λ. They are [Schur functions](wikipedia://en/3147062) when *t* is 0 and monomial symmetric functions when *t* is 1 and are special cases of [Macdonald polynomials](wikipedia://en/10354285). They were first defined indirectly by [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033) using the [Hall algebra](wikipedia://en/7611764), and later defined directly by [Dudley E. Littlewood](wikipedia://en/7603792) (1961).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Hall–Littlewood polynomial *P* is defined by

$$P_\lambda(x_1,\ldots,x_n;t) = \left( \prod_{i\geq 0} \prod_{j=1}^{m(i)} \frac{1-t}{1-t^{j}} \right) {\sum_{w\in S_n}w\left(x_1^{\lambda_1}\cdots x_n^{\lambda_n}\prod_{i<j}\frac{x_i-tx_j}{x_i-x_j}\right)},$$

where λ is a partition of at most *n* with elements λ<sub>*i*</sub>, and *m*(*i*) elements equal to *i*, and *S*<sub>*n*</sub> is the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) of order *n*!.

As an example,

$$P_{42}(x_1,x_2;t) = x_1^4 x_2^2 + x_1^2 x_2^4 + (1-t) x_1^3 x_2^3$$

### Specializations {#Specializations}

We have that $P_\lambda(x;1) = m_\lambda(x)$, $P_\lambda(x;0) = s_\lambda(x)$ and $P_\lambda(x;-1) = P_\lambda(x)$ where the latter is the Schur *P* polynomials.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Expanding the [Schur polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur_polynomials) in terms of the Hall–Littlewood polynomials, one has

$$s_\lambda(x) = \sum_\mu K_{\lambda\mu}(t) P_\mu(x,t)$$

where $K_{\lambda\mu}(t)$ are the [Kostka–Foulkes polynomials](wikipedia://en/22630866). Note that as $t=1$, these reduce to the ordinary Kostka coefficients.

A combinatorial description for the Kostka–Foulkes polynomials was given by Lascoux and Schützenberger,

$$K_{\lambda\mu}(t) = \sum_{T \in SSYT(\lambda,\mu)} t^{\mathrm{charge}(T)}$$

where "charge" is a certain combinatorial statistic on semistandard Young tableaux, and the sum is taken over the set $SSYT(\lambda,\mu)$ of all semi-standard Young tableaux *T* with shape *λ* and type *μ*.
