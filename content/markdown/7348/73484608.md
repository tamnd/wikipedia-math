# Generalized balanced ternary

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized balanced ternary"}

**Generalized balanced ternary** is a generalization of the [balanced ternary](wikipedia://en/376757) [numeral system](wikipedia://en/21170) to represent points in a higher-dimensional [space](wikipedia://en/9697). It was first described in 1982 by Laurie Gibson and Dean Lucas.[^o2] It has since been used for various applications, including geospatial[^o3] and high-performance scientific[^o4] computing.

:::

## General form {#General_form}

Like standard [positional numeral systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positional_numeral_system), generalized balanced ternary represents a point $p$ as powers of a **base** $B$ multiplied by **digits** $d_i$.

$$p = d_0 + B d_1 + B^2 d_2 + \ldots$$

Generalized balanced ternary uses a [transformation matrix](wikipedia://en/692458) as its base $B$. Digits are [vectors](wikipedia://en/32533) chosen from a finite subset $\{D_0 = 0, D_1, \ldots, D_n\}$ of the underlying space.

## One dimension {#One_dimension}

In one dimension, generalized balanced ternary is equivalent to standard balanced ternary, with three digits (0, 1, and −1). $B$ is a $1\times 1$ matrix, and the digits $D_i$ are length-1 vectors, so they appear here without the extra brackets.

$$\begin{aligned}B &= 3 \\ D_0 &= 0 \\ D_1 &= 1 \\ D_2 &= -1\end{aligned}$$

## Two dimensions {#Two_dimensions}

*Image: Visualization of three-digit 2D generalized balanced ternary numbers.png.* The 2D points addressable by three generalized balanced ternary digits. Each point is addressed by its path from the origin; the six colors correspond to the six non-zero digits.

In two dimensions, there are seven digits. The digits $D_1, \ldots, D_6$ are six points arranged in a regular hexagon centered at $D_0 = 0$.[^o9]

$$\begin{aligned} B &= \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 5 & \sqrt{3} \\ -\sqrt{3} & 5 \end{bmatrix} \\ D_0 &= 0 \\ D_1 &= \left( 0, \sqrt{3} \right) \\ D_2 &= \left( \frac{3}{2}, -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) \\ D_3 &= \left( \frac{3}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) \\ D_4 &= \left( -\frac{3}{2}, -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) \\ D_5 &= \left( -\frac{3}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) \\ D_6 &= \left( 0, -\sqrt{3} \right) \\ \end{aligned}$$

If there are two numerals in a cell, the left one is carried over to the next digit. Unlike standard addition, addition of two-dimensional generalized balanced ternary numbers may require multiple carries to be performed while computing a single digit.[^o9]
