# Interleaving distance

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Interleaving distance"}

*Image: An interleaving between two persistence modules.png.* A 1-interleaving between two $\mathbb Z$-indexed persistence modules M and N, represented as a diagram of vector spaces and linear maps between them.

In [topological data analysis](wikipedia://en/17740009), the **interleaving distance** is a measure of similarity between [persistence modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persistence_module), a common object of study in topological data analysis and [persistent homology](wikipedia://en/31069689). The interleaving distance was first introduced by Frédéric Chazal et al. in 2009.[^o2] since then, it and its generalizations have been a central consideration in the study of applied [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and topological data analysis.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A *persistence module* $\mathbb V$ is a collection $(V_t \mid t \in \mathbb R)$ of [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) indexed over the [real line](wikipedia://en/287188), along with a collection $(v^s_t : V_s \to V_t \mid s\leq t)$ of [linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102) such that $v^t_t$ is always an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828), and the relation $v^s_t \circ v^r_s = v^r_t$ is satisfied for every $r\leq s \leq t$. The case of $\mathbb R$ indexing is presented here for simplicity, though the interleaving distance can be readily adapted to more general settings, including multi-dimensional persistence modules.[^o10]

Let $\mathbb U$ and $\mathbb V$ be persistence modules. Then for any $\delta \in \mathbb R$, a *$\delta$-shift* is a collection $(\phi_t : U_t \to V_{t+\delta} \mid t \in \mathbb R)$ of linear maps between the persistence modules that commute with the internal maps of $\mathbb U$ and $\mathbb V$.

The persistence modules $\mathbb U$ and $\mathbb V$ are said to be $\delta$-interleaved if there are $\delta$-shifts $\phi_t: U_t \to V_{t+ \delta}$ and $\psi_t: V_t \to U_{t+ \delta}$ such that the following diagrams commute for all $s \leq t$.

*Image: Interleaving\_commutative\_diagram.png.*

It follows from the definition that if $\mathbb U$ and $\mathbb V$ are $\delta$-interleaved for some $\delta$, then they are also $(\delta + \varepsilon)$-interleaved for any positive $\varepsilon$. Therefore, in order to find the closest interleaving between the two modules, we must take the [infimum](wikipedia://en/39382) across all possible interleavings.

The *interleaving distance* between two persistence modules $\mathbb U$ and $\mathbb V$ is defined as $d_I (\mathbb U, \mathbb V) = \inf \{\delta \mid \mathbb U \text{ and } \mathbb V \text{ are } \delta\text{-interleaved}\}$.[^o11]

:::

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Metric properties {#Metric_properties}

It can be shown that the interleaving distance satisfies the [triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941). Namely, given three persistence modules $\mathbb U$, $\mathbb V$, and $\mathbb W$, the inequality $d_I (\mathbb U, \mathbb W) \leq d_I (\mathbb U, \mathbb V) + d_I (\mathbb V, \mathbb W)$ is satisfied.[^o11]

On the other hand, there are examples of persistence modules that are not isomorphic but that have interleaving distance zero. Furthermore, if no suitable $\delta$ exists then two persistence modules are said to have infinite interleaving distance. These two properties make the interleaving distance an *[extended](wikipedia://en/51698) [pseudometric](wikipedia://en/68503)*, which means non-identical objects are allowed to have distance zero, and objects are allowed to have infinite distance, but the other properties of a proper [metric](wikipedia://en/20018) are satisfied.

Further metric properties of the interleaving distance and its variants were investigated by Luis Scoccola in 2020.[^o15]

### Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

Computing the interleaving distance between two single-parameter persistence modules can be accomplished in [polynomial time](wikipedia://en/405944). On the other hand, it was shown in 2018 that computing the interleaving distance between two multi-dimensional persistence modules is [NP-hard.](wikipedia://en/54681)[^o17][^o18]
