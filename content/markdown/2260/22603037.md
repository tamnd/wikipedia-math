# Ákos Császár

Császár Ákos

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ákos Császár"}

**Ákos Császár** (Császár Ákos, ) (26 February 1924 – 14 December 2017) was a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) mathematician, specializing in [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649) and [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478). He discovered the [Császár polyhedron](wikipedia://en/11731171), a nonconvex [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) without diagonals.[^o2] He introduced the notion of [syntopogeneous spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntopogeneous_space), a generalization of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450).

:::

He was born in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest). Towards the end of 1944 his grandfather lost his life during the [Siege of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Budapest). Then his father, older brother and himself were arrested by the Germans and sent to a concentration camp approximatively 45 miles east of Budapest. An infectious illness spread in the camp, and his brother and father died, but Ákos survived.

He was a member of the group of five students of the late professor [Lipót Fejér](wikipedia://en/3934357), who called them "The Big Five". The other four are [John Horvath](wikipedia://en/65971885), [János Aczél](wikipedia://en/22717925), [Steven Gaal](wikipedia://en/24774553), and [László Fuchs](wikipedia://en/34809998), all of whom became mathematics professors in North America;[^o3] of that group, only Császár became a university professor in Budapest.

Between 1952 and 1992 he was head of the Department of Analysis at the [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) in Budapest. He was elected corresponding member (1970), and then full member (1979) of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o4] He was general secretary (1966–1980), president (1980–1990), and honorary president (after 1990) of the [János Bolyai Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/22777615). He received the [Kossuth Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kossuth_Prize) (1963) and the Gold Medal of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences (2009).[^o5][^o6] Császár died in Budapest on 14 December 2017, aged 93.[^o7]
