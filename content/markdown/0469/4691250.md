# Easton's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Easton's theorem"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), **Easton's theorem** is a result on the possible [cardinal numbers](wikipedia://en/6173) of [powersets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerset). (Easton 1970) (extending a result of [Robert M. Solovay](wikipedia://en/3160110)) showed via [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) that the only constraints on permissible values for 2<sup>*κ*</sup> when *κ* is a [regular cardinal](wikipedia://en/374128) are

:::

$$\kappa < \operatorname{cf}(2^\kappa)$$

(where cf(*α*) is the [cofinality](wikipedia://en/6693) of *α*) and

$$\text{if } \kappa < \lambda \text{ then } 2^\kappa\le 2^\lambda.$$

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

If $G$ is a [class function](wikipedia://en/20696#Classes_in_formal_set_theories) whose domain consists of [ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932) and whose range consists of ordinals such that

1. $G$ is non-decreasing,
1. the [cofinality](wikipedia://en/6693) of $\aleph_{G(\alpha)}$ is greater than $\aleph_\alpha$ for each $\alpha$ in the domain of $G$, and
1. $\aleph_\alpha$ is regular for each $\alpha$ in the domain of $G$,

then (assuming [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) is consistent) there is a model of ZFC such that

$$2^{\aleph_\alpha} = \aleph_{G(\alpha)}$$

for each $\alpha$ in the domain of $G$.

The proof of Easton's theorem uses [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) with a [proper class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_class) of forcing conditions over a model satisfying the [generalized continuum hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_continuum_hypothesis).

The first two conditions in the theorem are necessary. Condition 1 is a well known property of cardinality, while condition 2 follows from [Kőnig's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/König's_theorem_%28set_theory%29).

In Easton's model the powersets of [singular cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cardinal) have the smallest possible cardinality compatible with the conditions that $2^{\kappa}$ has cofinality greater than $\kappa$ and is a non-decreasing function of $\kappa$.

:::

## No extension to singular cardinals {#No_extension_to_singular_cardinals}

(Silver 1975) proved that a singular cardinal of uncountable cofinality cannot be the smallest cardinal for which the generalized continuum hypothesis fails. This shows that Easton's theorem cannot be extended to the class of all cardinals. The program of [PCF theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCF_theory) gives results on the possible values of $2^\lambda$ for singular cardinals $\lambda$. PCF theory shows that the values of the [continuum function](wikipedia://en/5488499) on singular cardinals are strongly influenced by the values on smaller cardinals, whereas Easton's theorem shows that the values of the continuum function on [regular cardinals](wikipedia://en/374128) are only weakly influenced by the values on smaller cardinals.
