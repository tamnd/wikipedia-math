# Zooko's triangle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zooko's triangle"}

*Image: Zooko's Triangle.svg.* Zooko's triangle defines three traits of a network protocol identifier as *Human-meaningful*, *Decentralized* and *Secure*.

**Zooko's triangle** is a [trilemma](wikipedia://en/488382) of three properties that some people consider desirable for names of participants in a [network protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_protocol):[^o2]

:::

- *Human-meaningful:* Meaningful and memorable (low-entropy) names are provided to the users.
- *Secure:* The amount of damage a malicious entity can inflict on the system should be as low as possible.
- *Decentralized:* Names correctly resolve to their respective entities without the use of a central authority or service.

## Overview {#Overview}

[Zooko Wilcox-O'Hearn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zooko_Wilcox-O'Hearn) conjectured that no single kind of name can achieve more than two. For example: [DNSSec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNSSec) offers a human-meaningful, secure naming scheme, but is not decentralized as it relies on trusted root-servers; [.onion](wikipedia://en/3648703) addresses and [bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265) addresses are secure and decentralized but not human-meaningful; and [I2P](wikipedia://en/538434) uses name translation services which are secure (as they run locally) and provide human-meaningful names – but fail to provide unique entities when used globally in a decentralised network without authorities.

## Solutions {#Solutions}

Several systems that exhibit all three properties of Zooko's triangle include:

- Computer scientist [Nick Szabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Szabo)'s paper "Secure Property Titles with Owner Authority" illustrated that all three properties can be achieved [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) the limits of [Byzantine fault tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_fault_tolerance).[^o5]
- Activist [Aaron Swartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Swartz) described a naming system based on [Bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265) employing Bitcoin's distributed blockchain as a [proof-of-work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_of_work) to establish consensus of [domain name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_name) ownership.[^o6] These systems remain vulnerable to [Sybil attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sybil_attack),[^o7] but are secure under Byzantine assumptions.
- Far-right political blogger [Curtis Yarvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtis_Yarvin) implemented a decentralized version of [IP addresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_address) in [Urbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbit) that hash to four-syllable, human-readable names.[^o8]

Several platforms implement refutations of Zooko's conjecture, including: [Twister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twister_%28software%29) (which use Swartz' system with a bitcoin-like system), [Blockstack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockstack) (separate blockchain), [Namecoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namecoin) (separate blockchain), [LBRY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LBRY) (separate blockchain – content discovery, ownership, and [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer) file-sharing), [Monero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monero_%28cryptocurrency%29), [OpenAlias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAlias),[^o9] [Ethereum Name Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereum_Name_Service), and the [Handshake Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handshake_Protocol).[^o10]
