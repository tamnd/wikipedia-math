# Prime manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prime manifold"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **prime manifold** is an *n*-[manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_%28topology%29) that cannot be expressed as a non-trivial [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) of two *n*-manifolds. Non-trivial means that neither of the two is an [*n*-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782). A similar notion is that of an **irreducible** *n*-manifold, which is one in which any embedded (*n* − 1)-sphere bounds an embedded *n*-[ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_%28topology%29). Implicit in this definition is the use of a suitable [category](wikipedia://en/53759), such as the category of [differentiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219) or the category of [piecewise-linear manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise-linear_manifold).

:::

A 3-manifold is irreducible if and only if it is prime, except for two cases: the product $S^2 \times S^1$ and the [non-orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-orientable) [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) of the 2-sphere over the circle $S^1$ are both prime but not irreducible. This is somewhat analogous to the notion in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) of [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/145132) generalizing [Irreducible elements](wikipedia://en/60693).

According to [a theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_decomposition_%283-manifold%29) of [Hellmuth Kneser](wikipedia://en/1368525) and [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632), every compact, [orientable](wikipedia://en/187446) [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) is the connected sum of a unique ([up to](wikipedia://en/44787) [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660)) collection of prime 3-manifolds.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Consider specifically [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257).

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Irreducible manifold"}

### Irreducible manifold {#Irreducible_manifold}

A 3-manifold is **irreducible** if every smooth sphere bounds a ball. More rigorously, a [differentiable](wikipedia://en/2119219) [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) 3-manifold $M$ is irreducible if every differentiable [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) $S$ [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to a [sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) bounds a subset $D$ (that is, $S=\partial D$) which is homeomorphic to the closed [ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_%28topology%29)

$$D^3 = \{x\in\mathbb{R}^3\ |\ |x|\leq 1\}.$$

The assumption of differentiability of $M$ is not important, because every topological 3-manifold has a unique differentiable structure. However it is necessary to assume that the sphere is *smooth* (a differentiable submanifold), even having a [tubular neighborhood](wikipedia://en/3192042). The differentiability assumption serves to exclude pathologies like the [Alexander's horned sphere](wikipedia://en/685665) (see below).

A 3-manifold that is not irreducible is called **reducible**.

:::

### Prime manifolds {#Prime_manifolds}

A connected 3-manifold $M$ is **prime** if it cannot be expressed as a [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) $N_1\# N_2$ of two manifolds neither of which is the 3-sphere $S^3$ (or, equivalently, neither of which is homeomorphic to $M$).

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Euclidean space {#Euclidean_space}

Three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^3$ is irreducible: all smooth 2-spheres in it bound balls.

On the other hand, [Alexander's horned sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander's_horned_sphere) is a non-smooth sphere in $\mathbb{R}^3$ that does not bound a ball. Thus the stipulation that the sphere be smooth is necessary.

### Sphere, lens spaces {#Sphere,_lens_spaces}

The [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) $S^3$ is irreducible. The [product space](wikipedia://en/24910) $S^2 \times S^1$ is not irreducible, since any 2-sphere $S^2 \times \{pt\}$ (where $pt$ is some point of $S^1$) has a connected complement which is not a ball (it is the product of the 2-sphere and a line).

A [lens space](wikipedia://en/885725) $L(p,q)$ with $p\neq 0$ (and thus not the same as $S^2 \times S^1$) is irreducible.

:::

## Prime manifolds and irreducible manifolds {#Prime_manifolds_and_irreducible_manifolds}

A 3-manifold is irreducible if and only if it is prime, except for two cases: the product $S^2 \times S^1$ and the [non-orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-orientable) [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) of the 2-sphere over the circle $S^1$ are both prime but not irreducible.

### From irreducible to prime {#From_irreducible_to_prime}

An irreducible manifold $M$ is prime. Indeed, if we express $M$ as a connected sum

$$M=N_1\#N_2,$$

then $M$ is obtained by removing a ball each from $N_1$ and from $N_2,$ and then gluing the two resulting 2-spheres together. These two (now united) 2-spheres form a 2-sphere in $M.$ The fact that $M$ is irreducible means that this 2-sphere must bound a ball. Undoing the gluing operation, either $N_1$ or $N_2$ is obtained by gluing that ball to the previously removed ball on their borders. This operation though simply gives a 3-sphere. This means that one of the two factors $N_1$ or $N_2$ was in fact a (trivial) 3-sphere, and $M$ is thus prime.

### From prime to irreducible {#From_prime_to_irreducible}

Let $M$ be a prime 3-manifold, and let $S$ be a 2-sphere embedded in it. Cutting on $S$ one may obtain just one manifold $N$ or perhaps one can only obtain two manifolds $M_1$ and $M_2.$ In the latter case, gluing balls onto the newly created spherical boundaries of these two manifolds gives two manifolds $N_1$ and $N_2$ such that

$$M = N_1\#N_2.$$

Since $M$ is prime, one of these two, say $N_1,$ is $S^3.$ This means $M_1$ is $S^3$ minus a ball, and is therefore a ball itself. The sphere $S$ is thus the border of a ball, and since we are looking at the case where only this possibility exists (two manifolds created) the manifold $M$ is irreducible.

It remains to consider the case where it is possible to cut $M$ along $S$ and obtain just one piece, $N.$ In that case there exists a closed simple [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) $\gamma$ in $M$ intersecting $S$ at a single point. Let $R$ be the union of the two [tubular neighborhoods](wikipedia://en/3192042) of $S$ and $\gamma.$ The [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) $\partial R$ turns out to be a 2-sphere that cuts $M$ into two pieces, $R$ and the complement of $R.$ Since $M$ is prime and $R$ is not a ball, the complement must be a ball. The manifold $M$ that results from this fact is almost determined, and a careful analysis shows that it is either $S^2 \times S^1$ or else the other, non-orientable, [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) of $S^2$ over $S^1.$
