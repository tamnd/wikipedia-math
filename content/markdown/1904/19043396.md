# Markov spectrum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Markov spectrum"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Markov spectrum**, devised by [Andrey Markov](wikipedia://en/915), is a complicated set of real numbers arising in [Markov Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/2965726) and also in the theory of [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558).

:::

## Quadratic form characterization {#Quadratic_form_characterization}

Consider a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) given by *f*(*x*,*y*) = *ax*<sup>2</sup> + *bxy* + *cy*<sup>2</sup> and suppose that its [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607#Quadratic_forms) is fixed, say equal to −1/4. In other words, *b*<sup>2</sup> − 4*ac* = 1.

One can ask for the minimal value achieved by $\left\vert f(x,y) \right\vert$ when it is evaluated at non-zero vectors of the grid $\mathbb{Z}^2$, and if this minimum does not exist, for the [infimum](wikipedia://en/39382).

The Markov spectrum *M* is the set obtained by repeating this search with different quadratic forms with discriminant fixed to −1/4:

$$M = \left\{ \left(\inf_{(x,y)\in \mathbb{Z}^2 \smallsetminus \{(0,0)\}} |f(x,y)| \right)^{-1} : f(x,y) = ax^2 + bxy + cy^2,\ b^2- 4ac = 1 \right\}$$

## Lagrange spectrum {#Lagrange_spectrum}

Starting from [Hurwitz's theorem](wikipedia://en/23190613) on Diophantine approximation, that any real number $\xi$ has a sequence of rational approximations *m*/*n* tending to it with

$$\left |\xi-\frac{m}{n}\right |<\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}\, n^2},$$

it is possible to ask for each value of 1/*c* with 1/*c* ≥ $\sqrt{5}$ about the existence of some $\xi$ for which

$$\left |\xi-\frac{m}{n}\right |<\frac{c} {n^2}$$

for such a sequence, for which *c* is the best possible (maximal) value. Such 1/*c* make up the **Lagrange spectrum** *L*, a set of real numbers at least $\sqrt{5}$ (which is the smallest value of the spectrum). The formulation with the reciprocal is awkward, but the traditional definition invites it; looking at the set of *c* instead allows a definition instead by means of an [inferior limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_superior_and_limit_inferior). For that, consider

$$\liminf_{n \to \infty}n^2\left |\xi-\frac{m}{n}\right |,$$

where *m* is chosen as an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) function of *n* to make the difference minimal. This is a function of $\xi$, and the reciprocal of the Lagrange spectrum is the range of values it takes on [irrational numbers](wikipedia://en/20647689).

### Relation with Markov spectrum {#Relation_with_Markov_spectrum}

The Lagrange spectrum is a proper subset of the Markov spectrum.[^o5] The initial part of the Lagrange spectrum, namely the part lying in the interval $\sqrt{5}$, 3, is also the initial part of Markov spectrum. The first few values are $\sqrt{5}$, $\sqrt{8}$, $\sqrt{221}$/5, $\sqrt{1517}$/13, ...[^o6] and the *n*th number of this sequence (that is, the *n*th [Lagrange number](wikipedia://en/16075933)) can be calculated from the *n*th [Markov number](wikipedia://en/2965726) by the formula

$$L_n = \sqrt{9 - {4 \over {m_n}^2}}.$$

**Freiman's constant** is the name given to the end of the last gap in the Lagrange spectrum, namely:

$F = \frac{2\,221\,564\,096 + 283\,748\sqrt{462}}{491\, 993\, 569} = 4.5278295661\dots$ .

All real numbers in ${{F}}, \infty$ - known as Hall’s ray - are members of the Lagrange spectrum.[^o7]

## Geometry of Markov and Lagrange spectrum {#Geometry_of_Markov_and_Lagrange_spectrum}

On one hand, the initial part of the Markov and Lagrange spectrum lying in the interval \[$\sqrt{5}$, 3) are both equal and they are a discrete set. On the other hand, the final part of these sets lying after Freiman's constant are also equal, but a continuous set. The geometry of the part between the initial part and final part has a fractal structure, and can be seen as a geometric transition between the discrete initial part and the continuous final part. This is stated precisely in the next theorem:[^o9]

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

Given $t \in \mathbb{R}$, the [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) of $L\cap(-\infty,t)$ is equal to the Hausdorff dimension of $M\cap(-\infty,t)$. Moreover, if *d* is the function defined as $d(t):=\dim_{H}(M\cap(-\infty,t))$, where dim<sub>*H*</sub> denotes the Hausdorff dimension, then *d* is continuous and maps **R** onto \[0,1\].

:::
