# Concentric objects

*Image: WA 80 cm archery target.svg.* An [archery target](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_target), featuring evenly spaced concentric circles that surround a "[bullseye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullseye_%28target%29)".

*Image: Kepler-solar-system-2.png.* Kepler's cosmological model formed by concentric spheres and regular polyhedra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Concentric objects"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), two or more [objects](wikipedia://en/19453961) are said to be ***concentric*** when they share the same [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_%28geometry%29). Any pair of (possibly unalike) objects with well-defined centers can be concentric, including [circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circles), [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859), [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306), [regular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/333299), parallelograms, cones, conic sections, and quadrics.[^o2]

:::

Geometric objects are ***[coaxial](wikipedia://en/542587)*** if they share the same [axis](wikipedia://en/6025673) (line of symmetry). Geometric objects with a well-defined axis include circles (any line through the center), spheres, [cylinders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29),[^o3] conic sections, and surfaces of revolution.

Concentric objects are often part of the broad category of *[whorled](wikipedia://en/1945043) patterns*, which also includes *[spirals](wikipedia://en/61563)* (a curve which emanates from a point, moving farther away as it revolves around the point).

## Geometric properties {#Geometric_properties}

In the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), two circles that are concentric necessarily have different radii from each other.[^o5] However, circles in three-dimensional space may be concentric, and have the same radius as each other, but nevertheless be different circles. For example, two different [meridians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_%28astronomy%29) of a terrestrial [globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe) are concentric with each other and with the [globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe) of the earth (approximated as a sphere). More generally, every two [great circles](wikipedia://en/48082) on a sphere are concentric with each other and with the sphere.[^o6]

By [Euler's theorem in geometry](wikipedia://en/3338987) on the distance between the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) and [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) of a triangle, two concentric circles (with that distance being zero) are the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) and [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle) of a triangle [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the radius of one is twice the radius of the other, in which case the triangle is [equilateral](wikipedia://en/173285).[^o7]

The circumcircle and the incircle of a [regular *n*-gon](wikipedia://en/333306), and the regular *n*-gon itself, are concentric. For the circumradius-to-inradius ratio for various *n*, see [Bicentric polygon#Regular polygons](wikipedia://en/13290844#Regular_polygons). The same can be said of a [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299)'s [insphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insphere), [midsphere](wikipedia://en/4709281) and [circumsphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumsphere).

The region of the plane between two concentric circles is an [annulus](wikipedia://en/356158), and analogously the region of space between two concentric spheres is a [spherical shell](wikipedia://en/28734956).[^o8]

For a given point *c* in the plane, the set of all circles having *c* as their center forms a [pencil of circles](wikipedia://en/5023947). Each two circles in the pencil are concentric, and have different radii. Every point in the plane, except for the shared center, belongs to exactly one of the circles in the pencil. Every two disjoint circles, and every hyperbolic pencil of circles, may be transformed into a set of concentric circles by a [Möbius transformation](wikipedia://en/314493).[^o9][^o10]

## Applications and examples {#Applications_and_examples}

The [ripples](wikipedia://en/669713) formed by dropping a small object into still water naturally form an expanding system of concentric circles.[^o12] Evenly spaced circles on the targets used in [target archery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_archery)[^o13] or similar sports provide another familiar example of concentric circles.

[Coaxial cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaxial_cable) is a type of electrical cable in which the combined neutral and earth core completely surrounds the live core(s) in system of concentric cylindrical shells.[^o14]

[Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736)'s *[Mysterium Cosmographicum](wikipedia://en/3194945)* envisioned a cosmological system formed by concentric regular polyhedra and spheres.[^o15]

Concentric circles have been used on firearms surfaces as means of holding lubrication or reducing friction on components, similar to [jewelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engine_turning).[^o16]

Concentric circles are also found in [diopter sights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diopter_sight), a type of mechanic sights commonly found on target rifles. They usually feature a large disk with a small-diameter hole near the shooter's eye, and a front globe sight (a circle contained inside another circle, called *tunnel*). When these sights are correctly aligned, the point of impact will be in the middle of the front sight circle.
