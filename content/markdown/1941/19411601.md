# Symplectic representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Symplectic representation"}

In [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), a **symplectic representation** is a [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) or a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/318712) on a [symplectic vector space](wikipedia://en/292852) (*V*, *ω*) which preserves the symplectic form *ω*. Here *ω* is a nondegenerate skew symmetric bilinear form

:::

$$\omega\colon V\times V \to \mathbb F$$

where **F** is the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of scalars. A representation of a group *G* preserves *ω* if

$$\omega(g\cdot v,g\cdot w)= \omega(v,w)$$

for all *g* in *G* and *v*, *w* in *V*, whereas a representation of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) **g** preserves *ω* if

$$\omega(\xi\cdot v,w)+\omega(v,\xi\cdot w)=0$$

for all *ξ* in **g** and *v*, *w* in *V*. Thus a representation of *G* or **g** is equivalently a group or Lie algebra homomorphism from *G* or **g** to the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) Sp(*V*,*ω*) or its Lie algebra **sp**(*V*,*ω*)

If *G* is a [compact group](wikipedia://en/750326) (for example, a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707)), and **F** is the field of complex numbers, then by introducing a compatible unitary structure (which exists by an averaging argument), one can show that any complex symplectic representation is a [quaternionic representation](wikipedia://en/695046). Quaternionic representations of finite or compact groups are often called symplectic representations, and may be identified using the [Frobenius–Schur indicator](wikipedia://en/882686).
