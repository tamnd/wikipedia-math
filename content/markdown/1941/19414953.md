# Megagon

Regular polygon stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Megagon"}

A **megagon** or **1,000,000-gon** (million-gon) is a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220)-like [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) with [one million](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_million) sides ([mega-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega-), from the Greek μέγας, meaning "great", being a unit prefix denoting a factor of one million).[^o4][^o2]

:::

## Regular megagon {#Regular_megagon}

A [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) megagon is represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {1,000,000} and can be constructed as a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) 500,000-gon, t{500,000}, a twice-truncated 250,000-gon, tt{250,000}, a thrice-truncated 125,000-gon, ttt{125,000}, or a four-fold-truncated 62,500-gon, tttt{62,500}, a five-fold-truncated 31,250-gon, ttttt{31,250}, or a six-fold-truncated 15,625-gon, tttttt{15,625}.

A [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) megagon has an interior angle of 179°59'58.704" or 3.14158637 radians.[^o4] The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) megagon with sides of length *a* is given by

$$A = 250,000 \ a^2 \cot \frac{\pi}{1,000,000}.$$

The [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of a regular megagon inscribed in the unit [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) is:

$$2,000,000 \ \sin\frac{\pi}{1,000,000},$$

which is exceedingly close to [2π](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2π). In fact, for a circle the size of the [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228)'s equator, with a [circumference](wikipedia://en/5916) of 40,075 kilometres, one edge of a megagon inscribed in such a circle would be slightly over 40 meters long. The difference between the perimeter of the inscribed megagon and the circumference of this circle comes to less than 1/16 millimeters.[^o5]

Because 1,000,000 = 2<sup>6</sup> × 5<sup>6</sup>, the number of sides is not a product of distinct [Fermat primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime) and a power of two. Thus the regular megagon is not a [constructible polygon](wikipedia://en/523430). Indeed, it is not even constructible with the use of an angle trisector, as the number of sides is neither a product of distinct [Pierpont primes](wikipedia://en/2595060), nor a product of powers of two and three.

## Philosophical application {#Philosophical_application}

Like [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525)'s example of the [chiliagon](wikipedia://en/523699), the million-sided polygon has been used as an illustration of a well-defined concept that cannot be visualised.[^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13]

The megagon is also used as an illustration of the convergence of regular polygons to a circle.[^o14]

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

The *regular megagon* has Dih<sub>1,000,000</sub> [dihedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry), order 2,000,000, represented by 1,000,000 lines of reflection. Dih<sub>1,000,000</sub> has 48 dihedral subgroups: (Dih<sub>500,000</sub>, Dih<sub>250,000</sub>, Dih<sub>125,000</sub>, Dih<sub>62,500</sub>, Dih<sub>31,250</sub>, Dih<sub>15,625</sub>), (Dih<sub>200,000</sub>, Dih<sub>100,000</sub>, Dih<sub>50,000</sub>, Dih<sub>25,000</sub>, Dih<sub>12,500</sub>, Dih<sub>6,250</sub>, Dih<sub>3,125</sub>), (Dih<sub>40,000</sub>, Dih<sub>20,000</sub>, Dih<sub>10,000</sub>, Dih<sub>5,000</sub>, Dih<sub>2,500</sub>, Dih<sub>1,250</sub>, Dih<sub>625</sub>), (Dih<sub>8,000</sub>, Dih<sub>4,000</sub>, Dih<sub>2,000</sub>, Dih<sub>1,000</sub>, Dih<sub>500</sub>, Dih<sub>250</sub>, Dih<sub>125</sub>, Dih<sub>1,600</sub>, Dih<sub>800</sub>, Dih<sub>400</sub>, Dih<sub>200</sub>, Dih<sub>100</sub>, Dih<sub>50</sub>, Dih<sub>25</sub>), (Dih<sub>320</sub>, Dih<sub>160</sub>, Dih<sub>80</sub>, Dih<sub>40</sub>, Dih<sub>20</sub>, Dih<sub>10</sub>, Dih<sub>5</sub>), and (Dih<sub>64</sub>, Dih<sub>32</sub>, Dih<sub>16</sub>, Dih<sub>8</sub>, Dih<sub>4</sub>, Dih<sub>2</sub>, Dih<sub>1</sub>). It also has 49 more [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) symmetries as subgroups: (Z<sub>1,000,000</sub>, Z<sub>500,000</sub>, Z<sub>250,000</sub>, Z<sub>125,000</sub>, Z<sub>62,500</sub>, Z<sub>31,250</sub>, Z<sub>15,625</sub>), (Z<sub>200,000</sub>, Z<sub>100,000</sub>, Z<sub>50,000</sub>, Z<sub>25,000</sub>, Z<sub>12,500</sub>, Z<sub>6,250</sub>, Z<sub>3,125</sub>), (Z<sub>40,000</sub>, Z<sub>20,000</sub>, Z<sub>10,000</sub>, Z<sub>5,000</sub>, Z<sub>2,500</sub>, Z<sub>1,250</sub>, Z<sub>625</sub>), (Z<sub>8,000</sub>, Z<sub>4,000</sub>, Z<sub>2,000</sub>, Z<sub>1,000</sub>, Z<sub>500</sub>, Z<sub>250</sub>, Z<sub>125</sub>), (Z<sub>1,600</sub>, Z<sub>800</sub>, Z<sub>400</sub>, Z<sub>200</sub>, Z<sub>100</sub>, Z<sub>50</sub>, Z<sub>25</sub>), (Z<sub>320</sub>, Z<sub>160</sub>, Z<sub>80</sub>, Z<sub>40</sub>, Z<sub>20</sub>, Z<sub>10</sub>, Z<sub>5</sub>), and (Z<sub>64</sub>, Z<sub>32</sub>, Z<sub>16</sub>, Z<sub>8</sub>, Z<sub>4</sub>, Z<sub>2</sub>, Z<sub>1</sub>), with Z<sub>n</sub> representing π/*n* radian rotational symmetry.

[John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) labeled these lower symmetries with a letter and order of the symmetry follows the letter.[^o17] **r2000000** represents full symmetry and **a1** labels no symmetry. He gives **d** (diagonal) with mirror lines through vertices, **p** with mirror lines through edges (perpendicular), **i** with mirror lines through both vertices and edges, and **g** for rotational symmetry.

These lower symmetries allows degrees of freedom in defining irregular megagons. Only the **g1000000** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

:::

## Megagram {#Megagram}

A megagram is a million-sided [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303). There are 199,999 regular forms given by [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394) of the form {1000000/*n*}, where *n* is an integer between 2 and 500,000 that is [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) to 1,000,000. There are also 300,000 regular [star figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_figure) in the remaining cases.

:::
