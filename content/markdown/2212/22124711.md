# Semi-reflexive space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Semi-reflexive space"}

In the area of mathematics known as [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a **semi-reflexive space** is a [locally convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex) [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS) *X* such that the canonical evaluation map from *X* into its bidual (which is the [strong dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_dual) of *X*) is bijective. If this map is also an isomorphism of TVSs then it is called **[reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091)**.

:::

Semi-reflexive spaces play an important role in the general theory of [locally convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex) TVSs. Since a [normable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normable) TVS is semi-reflexive if and only if it is reflexive, the concept of semi-reflexivity is primarily used with TVSs that are not normable.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition and notation"}

## Definition and notation {#Definition_and_notation}

### Brief definition {#Brief_definition}

Suppose that $X$ is a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS) over the field $\mathbb{F}$ (which is either the real or complex numbers) whose [continuous dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space), $X^{\prime}$, **separates points** on $X$ (i.e. for any $x \in X$ there exists some $x^{\prime} \in X^{\prime}$ such that $x^{\prime}(x) \neq 0$). Let $X^{\prime}_b$ and $X^{\prime}_{\beta}$ both denote the [strong dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_dual) of $X$, which is the vector space $X^{\prime}$ of continuous linear functionals on $X$ endowed with the [topology of uniform convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_of_uniform_convergence) on [bounded subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_of_uniform_convergence#Strong_dual_topology_b%28X*,_X%29) of $X$; this topology is also called the **strong dual topology** and it is the "default" topology placed on a continuous dual space (unless another topology is specified). If $X$ is a normed space, then the strong dual of $X$ is the continuous dual space $X^{\prime}$ with its usual norm topology. The **bidual** of $X$, denoted by $X^{\prime\prime}$, is the strong dual of $X^{\prime}_b$; that is, it is the space $\left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}_{b}$.[^n1]

For any $x \in X,$ let $J_x : X^{\prime} \to \mathbb{F}$ be defined by $J_x\left(x^{\prime}\right) = x^{\prime}(x)$, where $J_x$ is called the **evaluation map at $x$**; since $J_x : X^{\prime}_b \to \mathbb{F}$ is necessarily continuous, it follows that $J_x \in \left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}$. Since $X^{\prime}$ separates points on $X$, the map $J : X \to \left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}$ defined by $J(x) := J_x$ is injective where this map is called the **evaluation map** or the **canonical map**. This map was introduced by [Hans Hahn](wikipedia://en/1075212) in 1927.[^n2]

We call $X$ **semireflexive** if $J : X \to \left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}$ is bijective (or equivalently, [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective)) and we call $X$ **[reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091)** if in addition $J : X \to X^{\prime\prime} = \left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}_b$ is an isomorphism of TVSs.[^n1] If $X$ is a normed space then $J$ is a TVS-embedding as well as an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) onto its range; furthermore, by [Goldstine's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldstine's_theorem) (proved in 1938), the range of $J$ is a dense subset of the bidual $\left(X^{\prime\prime}, \sigma\left(X^{\prime\prime}, X^{\prime}\right)\right)$.[^n2] A [normable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normable) space is reflexive if and only if it is semi-reflexive. A [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) is reflexive if and only if its closed unit ball is $\sigma\left(X^{\prime}, X\right)$-compact.[^n2]

### Detailed definition {#Detailed_definition}

Let $X$ be a topological vector space over a number field $\mathbb{F}$ (of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\mathbb{R}$ or [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $\mathbb{C}$). Consider its [strong dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_topology_%28polar_topology%29) $X^{\prime}_b$, which consists of all [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) $f : X \to \mathbb{F}$ and is equipped with the [strong topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_topology_%28polar_topology%29) $b\left(X^{\prime}, X\right)$, that is, the topology of uniform convergence on bounded subsets in $X$. The space $X^{\prime}_b$ is a topological vector space (to be more precise, a locally convex space), so one can consider its strong dual space $\left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}_{b}$, which is called the **strong bidual space** for $X$. It consists of all continuous linear functionals $h : X^{\prime}_b \to {\mathbb F}$ and is equipped with the strong topology $b\left(\left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}, X^{\prime}_b \right)$. Each vector $x\in X$ generates a map $J(x) : X^{\prime}_b \to \mathbb{F}$ by the following formula:

$$J(x)(f) = f(x),\qquad f \in X'.$$

This is a continuous linear functional on $X^{\prime}_b$, that is, $J(x) \in \left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}_{b}$. One obtains a map called the **evaluation map** or the **canonical injection**:

$$J : X \to \left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}_{b}.$$

which is a linear map. If $X$ is locally convex, from the [Hahn–Banach theorem](wikipedia://en/13860) it follows that $J$ is injective and open (that is, for each neighbourhood of zero $U$ in $X$ there is a neighbourhood of zero $V$ in $\left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}_{b}$ such that $J(U) \supseteq V \cap J(X)$). But it can be non-surjective and/or discontinuous.

A locally convex space $X$ is called **semi-reflexive** if the evaluation map $J : X \to \left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}_{b}$ is surjective (hence bijective); it is called **reflexive** if the evaluation map $J : X \to \left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}_{b}$ is surjective and continuous, in which case $J$ will be an [isomorphism of TVSs](wikipedia://en/45752)).

:::

## Characterizations of semi-reflexive spaces {#Characterizations_of_semi-reflexive_spaces}

If $X$ is a Hausdorff locally convex space then the following are equivalent:

1. $X$ is semireflexive;
1. the weak topology on $X$ had the Heine-Borel property (that is, for the weak topology $\sigma\left(X, X^{\prime}\right)$, every closed and bounded subset of $X_{\sigma}$ is weakly compact).[^n1]
1. If linear form on $X^{\prime}$ that continuous when $X^{\prime}$ has the strong dual topology, then it is continuous when $X^{\prime}$ has the weak topology;[^n3]
1. $X^{\prime}_{\tau}$ is [barrelled](wikipedia://en/1733592), where the $\tau$ indicates the [Mackey topology](wikipedia://en/1790627) on $X^{\prime}$;[^n3]
1. $X$ weak the weak topology $\sigma\left(X, X^{\prime}\right)$ is [quasi-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-complete).[^n3]

::: {#o17 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem[^n4].**

A locally convex Hausdorff space $X$ is semi-reflexive if and only if $X$ with the $\sigma\left(X, X^{\prime}\right)$-topology has the Heine–Borel property (i.e. weakly closed and bounded subsets of $X$ are weakly compact).

:::

## Sufficient conditions {#Sufficient_conditions}

Every [semi-Montel space](wikipedia://en/1796319) is semi-reflexive and every [Montel space](wikipedia://en/1796319) is reflexive.

::: {#o20 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

If $X$ is a Hausdorff locally convex space then the canonical injection from $X$ into its bidual is a topological embedding if and only if $X$ is infrabarrelled.[^n5]

The strong dual of a semireflexive space is [barrelled](wikipedia://en/1733592). Every semi-reflexive space is [quasi-complete](wikipedia://en/63948433).[^n3] Every semi-reflexive normed space is a reflexive Banach space.[^n6] The strong dual of a semireflexive space is barrelled.[^n7]

:::

## Reflexive spaces {#Reflexive_spaces}

If $X$ is a Hausdorff locally convex space then the following are equivalent:

1. $X$ is [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091);
1. $X$ is semireflexive and [barrelled](wikipedia://en/1733592);
1. $X$ is barrelled and the weak topology on $X$ had the Heine-Borel property (which means that for the weak topology $\sigma\left(X, X^{\prime}\right)$, every closed and bounded subset of $X_{\sigma}$ is weakly compact).[^n8]
1. $X$ is semireflexive and [quasibarrelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasibarrelled_space).[^n9]

If $X$ is a [normed space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space) then the following are equivalent:

1. $X$ is reflexive;
1. the closed unit ball is compact when $X$ has the weak topology $\sigma\left(X, X^{\prime}\right)$.[^n10]
1. $X$ is a Banach space and $X^{\prime}_b$ is reflexive.[^n11]

::: {#o31 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Every non-[reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091) infinite-dimensional [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) is a [distinguished space](wikipedia://en/64266856) that is not semi-reflexive.[^n12] If $X$ is a dense proper vector subspace of a reflexive Banach space then $X$ is a normed space that not semi-reflexive but its strong dual space is a reflexive Banach space.[^n12] There exists a semi-reflexive [countably barrelled space](wikipedia://en/64267369) that is not [barrelled](wikipedia://en/1733592).[^n12]

:::
