# Architectonic and catoptric tessellation

*Image: Catoptric tessellation cells.png.* The 13 architectonic or catoptric tessellations, shown as uniform cell centers, and catoptric cells, arranged as multiples of the smallest cell on top.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Architectonic and catoptric tessellation"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) defines **architectonic and catoptric tessellations** as the [uniform tessellations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_convex_honeycomb) (or [honeycombs](wikipedia://en/6008654)) of Euclidean 3-space with prime space groups and their [duals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope), as three-dimensional analogue of the Platonic, Archimedean, and Catalan tiling of the plane. The singular [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of an **architectonic tessellation** is the dual of the [cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) of the corresponding **[catoptric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catoptric) tessellation**, and vice versa. The [cubille](wikipedia://en/3733318) is the only Platonic (regular) tessellation of 3-space, and is self-dual. There are other uniform honeycombs constructed as gyrations or [prismatic stacks](wikipedia://en/2961#Prismatic_stacks) (and their duals) which are excluded from these categories.

:::

## Enumeration {#Enumeration}

The pairs of **architectonic and catoptric tessellations** are listed below with their [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186). These tessellations only represent four symmetry [space groups](wikipedia://en/463721), and also all within the [cubic crystal system](wikipedia://en/666697). Many of these tessellations can be defined in multiple symmetry groups, so in each case the highest symmetry is expressed.
