# Therefore sign

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Therefore sign"}

In [logical argument](wikipedia://en/13018310) and [mathematical proof](wikipedia://en/82285), the **therefore sign**, **∴**, is generally used before a [logical consequence](wikipedia://en/37531624), such as the conclusion of a [syllogism](wikipedia://en/48046). The symbol consists of three dots placed in an upright [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) and is read *therefore*. While it is not generally used in formal writing, it is used in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [shorthand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorthand).

:::

## History {#History}

According to [Florian Cajori](wikipedia://en/542738) in *[A History of Mathematical Notations](wikipedia://en/68374755),* the Swiss mathematician [Johann Rahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Rahn) used both an upright and an inverted triangle of dots to mean *therefore*. In the German edition of *Teutsche Algebra* (1659), he used the upright triangle with its modern meaning, but in the 1668 English edition Rahn used the inverted triangle more often to mean 'therefore'.p. vol. 1, p. 211 Other authors in the 18th century also used three dots in a triangle shape to signify 'therefore', but as with Rahn, there was little consistency as to how the triangle was oriented. Use of an upright triangle exclusively to mean *therefore* (and an inverted triangle exclusively to mean *because*) appears to have originated in the 19th century. In the 20th century, the three-dot notation for 'therefore' became very rare in continental Europe, but it remains popular in [Anglophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglophone) countries.[^o4]

## Examples of use {#Examples_of_use}

Used in a [syllogism](wikipedia://en/48046):

All gods are immortal.

[Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeus) is a god.

∴ Zeus is immortal.

and in mathematics:

$$x + 1 = 10$$

$$\therefore x = 9$$

## Other uses {#Other_uses}

### Meteorology {#Meteorology}

In meteorology, a sign of three dots similar to the therefore sign is used to indicate "moderate rain" on a [station model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_model); the similar [typographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typography) symbol [asterism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asterism_%28typography%29) (⁂, three [asterisks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asterisk)) indicates moderate snow.[^o8][^o9]

### Freemasonry {#Freemasonry}

*Image: DiplomeMaçonnique1945.png.* A diploma from the Masonic *[Grande Loge de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Loge_de_France)* showing the symbol as a substitute for the dot of abbreviation

In [Freemasonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry) and certain related traditions, the symbol is used to indicate [a Masonic abbreviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Masonic_Abbreviations) (rather than the [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_stop) mark used conventionally with some abbreviations). For example, "R∴W∴ John Smith" is an abbreviation for "Right Worshipful John Smith" (the term [Right Worshipful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Worshipful) is an honorific and indicates that Smith is a [Grand Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Lodge) officer).[^o11]

### Anarchism {#Anarchism}

Originating from 19th ce French Anarchism, the three dot symbol is used as an anti-military and anti-authority signal, representing the French phrase *"Mort aux vaches!"*, which is equivalent to *"Death to pigs"* in English, although the original translates directly as *"Death to Cows"*.[^o13]

## Unicode {#Unicode}

The symbol has a Unicode [code point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_point) at 2234. See [Unicode input](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode_input) for keyboard-entering methods.

## LaTeX {#LaTeX}

One can write the symbol in [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX) by using the `amssymb` package with the `\therefore` command.

## Similar signs {#Similar_signs}

The inverted form, **∵**, known as the **because sign**, is sometimes used as a [shorthand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorthand) form of "because".

The character ஃ (visarga) in the [Tamil script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_script) represents the [āytam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Āytam), a special sound of the [Tamil language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language).

An [asterism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asterism_%28typography%29), ⁂, is a typographic symbol consisting of three [asterisks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asterisks) (\*) placed in a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654). Its purpose is to "indicate minor breaks in text", to call attention to a passage, or to separate sub-chapters in a book.

A similar sign serves as a [Japanese map symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_map_symbol) on the maps of the [Geographical Survey Institute of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_Survey_Institute_of_Japan), indicating a tea plantation. On some maps, a version of the sign with thicker dots, ⛬, is used to signal the presence of a [national monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_monument), [historic site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_site) or [ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruins); it has its own Unicode code point.[^o19]

In [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language) and [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language), a superficially similar symbol was formerly used as an explanatory symbol (*forklaringstegnet*). It can be typeset using the [open o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_o) followed by a colon, thus: **ɔ:**. It is used for the meaning "namely", id est ([i.e.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.e.)), videlicet ([viz.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viz.)) or similar.[^o20]

The punctuation mark ߷ (*gbakurunen*) of the [N'Ko script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Ko_script)—a modern [writing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing_system) for the [Manding languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manding_languages) of West Africa—is a paragraph mark which marks the end of a major section of text.[^o21] Its function is similar to that of the asterism.[^o22] It symbolizes three stones holding a cooking pot over a fire.[^o21] It is also used by internet users to pictorially represent a [fidget spinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidget_spinner).[^o23]
