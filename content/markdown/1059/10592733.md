# Positive form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Positive form"}

In [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), the term *positive form* refers to several classes of real [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) of [Hodge type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_decomposition#Hodge_decomposition) *(p, p)*.

:::

## (1,1)-forms {#(1,1)-forms}

Real (*p*,*p*)-forms on a complex manifold *M* are forms which are of type (*p*,*p*) and real, that is, lie in the intersection $\Lambda^{p,p}(M)\cap \Lambda^{2p}(M,{\mathbb R}).$ A real (1,1)-form $\omega$ is called **semi-positive**[^o3] (sometimes just *positive*[^o4]), respectively, **positive**[^o5] (or *positive definite*[^o6]) if any of the following equivalent conditions holds:

1. $-\omega$ is the imaginary part of a positive semidefinite (respectively, positive definite) [Hermitian form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_form).
1. For some basis $dz_1, ... dz_n$ in the space $\Lambda^{1,0}M$ of (1,0)-forms, $\omega$ can be written diagonally, as $\omega = \sqrt{-1} \sum_i \alpha_i dz_i\wedge d\bar z_i,$ with $\alpha_i$ real and non-negative (respectively, positive).
1. For any (1,0)-tangent vector $v\in T^{1,0}M$, $-\sqrt{-1}\omega(v, \bar v) \geq 0$ (respectively, $>0$).
1. For any real tangent vector $v\in TM$, $\omega(v, I(v)) \geq 0$ (respectively, $>0$), where $I:\; TM\mapsto TM$ is the [complex structure](wikipedia://en/509742) operator.

## Positive line bundles {#Positive_line_bundles}

In algebraic geometry, positive definite (1,1)-forms arise as curvature forms of [ample line bundles](wikipedia://en/1186896) (also known as *positive line bundles*). Let *L* be a holomorphic Hermitian line bundle on a complex manifold,

$$\bar\partial:\; L\mapsto L\otimes \Lambda^{0,1}(M)$$

its complex structure operator. Then *L* is equipped with a unique connection preserving the Hermitian structure and satisfying

$$\nabla^{0,1}=\bar\partial.$$

This connection is called *the [Chern connection](wikipedia://en/19454065)*.

The curvature $\Theta$ of the Chern connection is always a purely imaginary (1,1)-form. A line bundle *L* is called **positive** if $\sqrt{-1}\Theta$ is a positive (1,1)-form. (Note that the de Rham cohomology class of $\sqrt{-1}\Theta$ is $2\pi$ times the first [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349#Via_the_Chern–Weil_theory) of *L*.) The [Kodaira embedding theorem](wikipedia://en/2986151) claims that a positive line bundle is ample, and conversely, any [ample line bundle](wikipedia://en/1186896) admits a Hermitian metric with $\sqrt{-1}\Theta$ positive.

## Positivity for *(p, p)*-forms {#Positivity_for_(p,_p)-forms}

Semi-positive (1,1)-forms on *M* form a [convex cone](wikipedia://en/3558266). When *M* is a compact [complex surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_surface), $dim_{\mathbb C}M=2$, this cone is [self-dual](wikipedia://en/3558266#Dual_cone), with respect to the Poincaré pairing :$\eta, \zeta \mapsto \int_M \eta\wedge\zeta$

For *(p, p)*-forms, where $2\leq p \leq dim_{\mathbb C}M-2$, there are two different notions of positivity.[^o9] A form is called **strongly positive** if it is a linear combination of products of semi-positive forms, with positive real coefficients. A real *(p, p)*-form $\eta$ on an *n*-dimensional complex manifold *M* is called **weakly positive** if for all strongly positive *(n-p, n-p)*-forms ζ with compact support, we have $\int_M \eta\wedge\zeta\geq 0$.

Weakly positive and strongly positive forms form convex cones. On compact manifolds these cones are [dual](wikipedia://en/3558266#Dual_cone) with respect to the Poincaré pairing.
