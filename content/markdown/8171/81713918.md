# Kiros Berhane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kiros Berhane"}

**Kiros T. Berhane** is an [Ethiopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopians)–American [biostatistician](wikipedia://en/3878) noted for methodological work in [environmental epidemiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_epidemiology) and for team-science contributions linking ambient and traffic-related air pollution to impaired lung-function growth in adolescents—and later demonstrating lung growth improvements with cleaner air.[^o6][^o7] As of 2025 he is Chair of Biostatistics at [Columbia University's Mailman School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University_Mailman_School_of_Public_Health) and serves on the *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_%28journal%29)* magazine Board of Reviewing Editors; he is also an elected Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) and of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988).[^o8][^o9][^o10]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Berhane earned a [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in statistics and mathematics from [Addis Ababa University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addis_Ababa_University), an MS in statistics from the [University of Guelph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Guelph), and a PhD in biostatistics from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto). He completed a postdoctoral fellowship at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University).[^o12][^o13][^o14]

## Career {#Career}

Berhane joined the Graduate School of Public Health at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh), where he was an assistant professor of family medicine and [clinical epidemiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_epidemiology) in the School of Medicine and an assistant professor of biostatistics, with research interests in [nonparametric regression](wikipedia://en/4536068), [longitudinal data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitudinal_data) analysis, and time-series methods.[^o16]

He later moved to the [Keck School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_Keck_School_of_Medicine) of the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California), where he became a professor of [preventive medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preventive_healthcare) in the Division of Biostatistics and directed the school's epidemiology and biostatistics graduate degree programs.[^o17][^o18] From 2011 to 2019 he served as director of graduate programs in biostatistics and epidemiology at USC.[^o19] At USC he served as lead biostatistician for the Children's Health Study (CHS), a series of longitudinal cohort studies that provided evidence that long-term exposure to ambient air pollution slows lung function growth and worsens respiratory symptoms in children.[^o20][^o21][^o22]

In January 2020 Berhane was appointed Cynthia and Robert Citrone-Roslyn and Leslie Goldstein Professor of Biostatistics and Chair of the Department of Biostatistics at the Columbia University Mailman School of Public Health.[^o23][^o24] He directs the Center for Achieving Resilience in Climate and Health (C-ARCH) at Columbia, a climate and health "solutions lab" he co-leads with environmental health scientist [Darby Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darby_Jack) that develops and evaluates strategies to build resilience to climate-related health risks, with an emphasis on partnerships with governments and communities and on research capacity building in regions such as [Eastern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa).[^o25][^o26][^o13][^o14]

Berhane serves on various leadership capacities within the [NIH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health)-funded [Data Science Initiative for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harnessing_Data_Science_for_Health_Discovery_and_Innovation_in_Africa) (DS-I Africa), such as the Contact [Principal Investigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_investigator) of the *Advancing Public Health Research for Eastern Africa through Data Science Training* (APHREA-DST) training project, and as a member of the steering committee for the DS-I Africa Consortium.[^o27]

Outside academia, Berhane writes poetry in [Tigrinya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigrinya_language), [Amharic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amharic) and English and shares his work on the Ethiopian online literary site *Meskot* and on his [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube) "Literature Channel".[^o28][^o29]

## Research and contributions {#Research_and_contributions}

### Children's Health Study findings on air pollution and lung growth {#Children's_Health_Study_findings_on_air_pollution_and_lung_growth}

As a biostatistician in the CHS, he co-authored studies that provided longitudinal evidence that contemporary ambient air pollution slows adolescents' lung development (*[NEJM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_England_Journal_of_Medicine)*, 2004), and that living near heavy traffic is associated with impaired lung-function growth (*[The Lancet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lancet)*, 2007).[^o32][^o33] He also co-authored the *NEJM* report showing that cleaner regional air was associated with measurably better lung growth in adolescents (2015); contemporaneous coverage described it as "for the first time" linking improved air quality to better lung development in children.[^o34][^o35]

### Methodological contributions {#Methodological_contributions}

Berhane has developed and helped spread new statistical methods for studying how health measures change over time and how the timing and amount of exposure to workplace and environmental hazards are linked to later disease risk. With [Nuoo-Ting Molitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuoo-Ting_Molitor), he introduced an approach that looks at the overall shape of a person's [growth curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_curve_%28biology%29) over time (for example, when growth speeds up most) and connects those features to environmental conditions such as air pollution; this work grew out of analyses of lung growth in the CHS.[^o37]

In studies where cancers may appear many years after exposure, he co-authored one of the early methods that uses [smooth curves](wikipedia://en/89246) ("splines") to describe how cancer risk changes with time since exposure, using data from the Colorado Plateau Uranium Miners cohort.[^o38] He later helped develop a more flexible version of this idea that looks at age, time since exposure, and exposure level together, to better describe how long-term or repeated exposures affect disease risk (*[Statistics in Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_in_Medicine_%28journal%29)*, 2008).[^o39]

He has written about statistical challenges in long-term studies of air pollution and children's health, including how to capture adolescent growth spurts and other biologically meaningful features of children's lung function over time.[^o40]

Berhane has also contributed to genetic and gene–environment interaction studies in [asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma) and [childhood obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childhood_obesity), including work on [nitric oxide synthase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitric_oxide_synthase) and [arginase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arginase) [polymorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymorphism_%28biology%29) and exhaled nitric oxide in children, and on [TGF-β1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TGF_beta_1) promoter variants and susceptibility to early-onset childhood asthma.[^o41][^o42]

### Tigray {#Tigray}

During the 2020–2022 war in the [Tigray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigray_Region) region of Ethiopia, Berhane joined other members of the [Global Society of Tigray Scholars and Professionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Society_of_Tigray_Scholars_and_Professionals) in documenting the public health consequences of the conflict, serving as a co-author of a [BMJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_BMJ) *Global Health* assessment of war-related damage to Tigray's health system and a subsequent community-based BMJ *Global Health* study of war-related sexual and [gender-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender-based_price_discrimination_in_the_United_States) violence. He was a signatory to an [International Diabetes Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Diabetes_Federation) appeal calling for urgent restoration of access to [insulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin) and other essential medicines.[^o44][^o45][^o46]

## Publications {#Publications}

### Editorial boards {#Editorial_boards}

Berhane serves on the editorial or advisory boards of several scientific journals. He is an associate editor of the *[International Journal of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Journal_of_Public_Health)*, and has been a member of the editorial boards of *Environmental Epidemiology* and the *[Archives of Environmental & Occupational Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archives_of_Environmental_&_Occupational_Health)*, as well as an advisory board member for the *[East African Journal of Health Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Journal_of_Health_Sciences)*.[^o64][^o65][^o66][^o67] He also serves on the Board of Reviewing Editors for *Science*, published by the American Association for the Advancement of Science (AAAS).[^o68][^o69]
