# Cover time

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cover time"}

In mathematics, the [cover time](wikipedia://en/81712895) of a finite [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) is the number of steps taken by the chain, from a given starting state, until the first step at which all states have been reached. It is a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) that depends on the Markov chain and the choice of the starting state. The cover time of a connected [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) is the cover time of the Markov chain that takes a [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) on the graph, at each step moving from one vertex to a uniformly-random neighbor of that vertex.

:::

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Cover times of graphs have been extensively studied in [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392) for applications involving the complexity of [st-connectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St-connectivity), [algebraic graph theory](wikipedia://en/1620216) and the study of [expander graphs](wikipedia://en/9313), and modeling [Token Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Token_Ring) computer networking technology.

:::

## In different classes of graphs {#In_different_classes_of_graphs}

A classical problem in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the [coupon collector's problem](wikipedia://en/14721784), can be interpreted as the result that the expected cover time of a [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_n$ is $n\ln n(1+o(1))$. For every other $n$-vertex graph, the expected cover time is at least as large as this formula. Any $n$-vertex [regular](wikipedia://en/85821) expander graph also has expected cover time $\Theta(n\log n)$ from any starting vertex, and more generally the cover time of any regular graph is

$$O\left(\frac{n\log n}{1-\lambda_2}\right),$$

where $\lambda_2$ is the second-largest [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the graph, normalized so that the largest eigenvalue is one. For arbitrary $n$-vertex graphs, from any starting vertex, the cover time is at most

$$\left(\frac{4}{27}+o(1)\right)n^3,$$

and there exist graphs whose expected cover time is this large. In [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), the expected cover time is $\Omega(n\log^2 n)$ and $O(n^2)$.
