# Renaud Lambiotte

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Renaud Lambiotte"}

**Renaud Lambiotte** is a Belgian mathematician and physicist who is currently the Professor of Networks and Nonlinear Systems at the [Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Institute), [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), and a tutorial fellow at [Somerville College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerville_College), University of Oxford.[^o3] He has contributed to the field of [complex networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_networks). He became a fellow of the [Network Science Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellows_of_the_Network_Science_Society) in 2025.[^o4]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Born in [Namur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namur), [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium), Lambiotte went on to study physics at [Université libre de Bruxelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_libre_de_Bruxelles), graduating with a Master's degree.[^o6] He completed his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in 2004 with a thesis entitled *Inelastic Gases: A Paradigm for Far-from-equilibrium Systems*, supervised by [Léon Brenig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Léon_Brenig).[^o7] Following this, Lambiotte completed [post-docs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-docs) at [ENS Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENS_Lyon), the [University of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liège), [UC Louvain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Louvain), and [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) where he pivoted to the study of complex networks.[^o3][^o6][^o8] In 2011, he joined the faculty at the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Namur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Namur) and the [Namur Institute For Complex Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namur_Institute_For_Complex_Systems) as an [Assistant Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_Professor), becoming [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in 2014. In 2017, he moved to the Mathematical Institute at the University of Oxford as an [Associate Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_Professor), becoming Professor in 2020.[^o3]

In 2015, he shared the Verdickt-Rijdams Prize from the [Académie royale de langue et de littérature françaises de Belgique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_royale_de_langue_et_de_littérature_françaises_de_Belgique) for *La tortue de Zénon*, a book collection on the relations between science and literature.[^o9]

## Research {#Research}

Lambiotte has made contributions to both theoretical and applied topics in network science. His research has focused on [network dynamics](wikipedia://en/17590563) with applications to [brain networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large-scale_brain_network) and [social networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_networks).[^o3] He is a co-author of the books *A Guide to Temporal Networks* with [Naoki Masuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naoki_Masuda)[^o11] and *Modularity and Dynamics on Complex Networks* with [Michael Schaub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Schaub).[^o12]

### Community detection {#Community_detection}

Lambiotte has made contributions to the problem of [community detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_structure) in complex networks. Whilst at UCLouvain, he was a co-author of the [Louvain method](wikipedia://en/44454257),[^o14] an algorithm for maximising [modularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modularity), which has gone on to be to cited more than 20,000 times.[^o15]

Since then, he has made additional contributions such as the use of [random walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_walks) and [Markov chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_chains) to discover community structure, including approaches to directed, signed, higher-order and temporal networks.[^o16][^o17]

### Random walks {#Random_walks}

Lambiotte has made contributions to the study of random walks on networks.[^o19] In particular, how random walks can reveal community structure in networks, non-Markovian random walks,[^o20] and walks on temporal networks.[^o21]

### Brain networks {#Brain_networks}

Lambiotte has made contributions to the study of both the structure and dynamics of brain networks, in particular the modular organisation of the brain.[^o23][^o24]

### Higher-order networks {#Higher-order_networks}

Lambiotte has studied the dynamics of higher-order networks, such as [multiplex networks](wikipedia://en/44342518), [temporal networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporal_network) and [hypergraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergraphs).[^o26][^o27]

### Social networks {#Social_networks}

Lambiotte has made contributions to the field of social network analysis including analysis of [mobile phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone) data, online social networks, and signed networks.[^o29]
