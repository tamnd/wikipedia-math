# Minimum rank of a graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minimum rank of a graph"}

In mathematics, the **minimum rank** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) parameter $\operatorname{mr}(G)$ for a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) *G*. It was motivated by the [Colin de Verdière graph invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_de_Verdière_graph_invariant).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) is a [symmetric matrix](wikipedia://en/126474) whose rows and columns both correspond to the vertices of the graph. Its elements are all 0 or 1, and the element in row *i* and column *j* is nonzero whenever vertex *i* is adjacent to vertex *j* in the graph. More generally, a *generalized adjacency matrix* is any symmetric matrix of real numbers with the same pattern of nonzeros off the diagonal (the diagonal elements may be any real numbers). The minimum rank of $G$ is defined as the smallest [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) of any generalized adjacency matrix of the graph; it is denoted by $\operatorname{mr} (G)$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Here are some elementary properties.

- The minimum rank of a graph is always at most equal to *n* − 1, where *n* is the number of vertices in the graph.[^o6]
- For every [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) *H* of a given graph *G*, the minimum rank of *H* is at most equal to the minimum rank of *G*.[^o7]
- If a graph is [disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph), then its minimum rank is the sum of the minimum ranks of its [connected components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28graph_theory%29).[^o8]
- The minimum rank is a [graph invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_invariant): [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) graphs necessarily have the same minimum rank.

:::

## Characterization of known graph families {#Characterization_of_known_graph_families}

Several families of graphs may be characterized in terms of their minimum ranks.

- For $n\geq 2$, the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K*<sub>*n*</sub> on *n* vertices has minimum rank one. The only graphs that are connected and have minimum rank one are the complete graphs.[^o10]
- A [path graph](wikipedia://en/4587190) *P*<sub>*n*</sub> on *n* vertices has minimum rank *n* − 1. The only *n*-vertex graphs with minimum rank *n* − 1 are the path graphs.[^o11]
- A [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) *C*<sub>*n*</sub> on *n* vertices has minimum rank *n* − 2.[^o12]
- Let $G$ be a [2-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) graph. Then $\operatorname{mr}(G)=|G|-2$ if and only if $G$ is a linear 2-tree.[^o13]
- A graph $G$ has $\operatorname{mr}(G)\leq 2$ if and only if the complement of $G$ is of the form $(K_{s_1}\cup K_{s_2}\cup K_{p_1,q_1}\cup \cdots \cup K_{p_k,q_k} ) \vee K_r$ for appropriate nonnegative integers $k, s_1, s_2, p_1, q_1,\ldots , p_k, q_k, r$ with $p_i+q_i>0$ for all $i=1,\ldots, k$.[^o14]
