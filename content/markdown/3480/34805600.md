# Rod Downey

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rod Downey"}

**Rodney Graham Downey** (born 20 September 1957)[^o6] is a New Zealand and Australian mathematician and computer scientist,[^o3] an emeritus professor in the School of Mathematics and Statistics at [Victoria University of Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Wellington) in New Zealand.[^o4] He is known for his work in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), and in particular for founding the field of [parameterised complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parameterised_complexity) together with [Michael Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fellows).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Downey graduated from [Brisbane State High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane_State_High_School) in 1974, and earned a bachelor's degree with first class honours at the [University of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Queensland) in 1978, and then went on to graduate school at [Monash University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monash_University), earning a doctorate in 1982 under the supervision of [John Crossley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Crossley_%28mathematician%29).[^o6][^o4] After holding teaching and visiting positions at the [Chisholm Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monash_University,_Caulfield_campus), [Western Illinois University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Illinois_University), the [National University of Singapore](wikipedia://en/286880), and the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign), he came to New Zealand in 1986 as a lecturer at Victoria University. He was promoted to reader in 1991, was given a personal chair at Victoria in 1995, and retired in 2023.[^o6][^o3]

Downey was president of the [New Zealand Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Mathematical_Society) from 2001 to 2003.[^o6][^o8]

## Publications {#Publications}

Downey is the co-author of seven books:

- *Parameterized Complexity* (with [Michael Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fellows), Springer, 1999)
- *Algorithmic Randomness and Complexity* (with D. Hirschfeldt, Springer, 2010)
- *Fundamentals of Parameterized Complexity* (with [Michael Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fellows), Springer, 2013)
- *Minimal Weak Truth Table Degrees and Computably Enumerable Turing Degrees* (with Keng Meng Ng and David Reed Solomon, Memoirs American Mathematical Society, Vol. 2184, 2020)
- *A Hierarchy of Turing Degrees* (with Noam Greenberg, Annals of Mathematics Studies No. 206, Princeton University Press, 2020)
- *Computability and Complexity: Foundations and Tools for Pursuing Scientific Applications*, (Springer-Verlag Texts in Computer Science, 2024)
- *Computable Structure Theory: A Unified Approach*, (Springer-Verlag Theory and Applications of Computability, 2025)

Downey has edited or co-edited nine books including *Turing's Legacy* which was an ACM Computing Reviews notable book for 2014. [^o10] He is also the author or co-author of around 300 research papers,[^o6][^o11] including a highly cited sequence of four papers with Michael Fellows and Karl Abrahamson setting the foundation for the study of parameterised complexity.[^o12]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In 1990, Downey won the Hamilton Research Award from the [Royal Society of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_New_Zealand).[^o14] In 1992, Downey won the Research Award of the New Zealand Mathematical Society "for penetrating and prolific investigations that have made him a leading expert in many aspects of recursion theory, effective algebra and complexity".[^o15]

In 1994, he won the New Zealand Association of Scientists Research Award, and became a fellow of the Royal Society of New Zealand in 1996.[^o6][^o16] In 2006, he became the first New Zealand-based mathematician to give an Invited Lecture at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842).

He has also given invited lectures at the International Congress of Logic, Methodology and Philosophy of Science and the ACM Conference on Computational Complexity. He was elected as an [ACM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Fellow) in 2007 "for contributions to computability and complexity theory", becoming the second ACM Fellow in New Zealand,[^o17][^o18] and in the same year was elected as a fellow of the [New Zealand Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Mathematical_Society).[^o6] Also in 2007 he was awarded a [James Cook Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook_Research_Fellowship) for research on the nature of computation.[^o19]

In 2010 he won the Shoenfield Prize (for articles) of the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438) for his work with Denis Hirschfeldt, Andre Nies, and Sebastiaan Terwijn on [randomness](wikipedia://en/19196523).[^o20] In 2011, the Royal Society of New Zealand gave him their [Hector Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hector_Medal) "for his outstanding, internationally acclaimed work in recursion theory, computational complexity, and other aspects of mathematical logic and combinatorics."[^o21][^o22] In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o23] In 2013, he became a Fellow of the [Australian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/4579582).

In 2014, he was awarded the [Nerode Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerode_Prize) from the [European Association for Theoretical Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Association_for_Theoretical_Computer_Science), jointly with [Hans Bodlaender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bodlaender), Michael Fellows, Danny Hermelin, [Lance Fortnow](wikipedia://en/25303350) and Rahul Santhanam for their work on [kernelization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernelization) lower bounds. In October 2016, Downey received a distinguished [Humboldt Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Research_Award) for his academic contributions. With Denis Hirschfeldt, Downey won another Shoenfield Prize from the Association for Symbolic Logic, this time the 2016 book prize for *Algorithmic Randomness and Complexity*. In 2018, Downey delivered the [Gödel Lecture](wikipedia://en/69216431) of the Association for Symbolic Logic, titled *Algorithmic randomness*, at the European Summer Meeting at Udine, Italy. The same year, Downey was awarded the [Rutherford Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_Medal_%28Royal_Society_of_New_Zealand%29), the highest honour awarded by the Royal Society of New Zealand, "for his pre-eminent revolutionary research into computability, including development of the theory of parameterised complexity and the algorithmic study of randomness."[^o24]

In 2022, Downey was awarded the New Zealand Association of von Humboldt Fellows Research Award for research over the preceding five years.[^o25]

In 2023, Downey was awarded the S. Barry Cooper Prize from the Association for Computability in Europe.[^o26] This award is awarded every two to three years "to a researcher who has contributed to a broad understanding and foundational study of computability by outstanding results, by seminal and lasting theory building, by exceptional service to the research communities involved, or by a combination of these."[^o27]

In 2024, Downey was awarded the New Zealand Mathematics Society Kalman Prize "for a single publication of original research, which may be an article, monograph or book, having appeared within the last 5 calendar years: 2019-2024".[^o28] This publication was the monograph "A Hierarchy of Turing Degrees" published in the Annals of Mathematics Studies, jointly written with Noam Greenberg.

## Scottish Country Dancing {#Scottish_Country_Dancing}

Downey is a well-known deviser of Scottish Country Dances having devised over 150 dances and several novel formations such as The Rose Progression, and the Hello-Goodbye Poussette, both adopted by the Royal Scottish Country Dance Society. [^o30]
