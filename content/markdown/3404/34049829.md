::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Simplicial honeycomb"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **simplicial honeycomb** (or **$n$-simplex honeycomb**) is a dimensional infinite series of [honeycombs](wikipedia://en/6008654), based on the ${\tilde{A}}_n$ affine [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) symmetry. It is represented by a [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) as a cyclic graph of $n + 1$ nodes with one node ringed. It is composed of $n$-[simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) facets, along with all [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) $n$-simplices. It can be thought of as an $n$-dimensional [hypercubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/12237982) that has been subdivided along all [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) $x+y+\cdots\in\mathbb{Z}$, then stretched along its main diagonal until the simplices on the ends of the hypercubes become regular. The [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of an $n$-*simplex honeycomb* is an [expanded](wikipedia://en/6341070) $n$-[simplex](wikipedia://en/39781).

:::

In [2 dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_dimensions), the honeycomb represents the [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858), with Coxeter graph filling the plane with alternately colored triangles. In [3 dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_dimensions) it represents the [tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3735260), with Coxeter graph filling space with alternately tetrahedral and octahedral cells. In [4 dimensions](wikipedia://en/1364622) it is called the [5-cell honeycomb](wikipedia://en/31194124), with Coxeter graph , with [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821) and [rectified 5-cell](wikipedia://en/2720430) facets. In [5 dimensions](wikipedia://en/2851410) it is called the [5-simplex honeycomb](wikipedia://en/31202395), with Coxeter graph , filling space by [5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex), [rectified 5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectified_5-simplex), and [birectified 5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birectified_5-simplex) facets. In [6 dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-dimensional_space) it is called the [6-simplex honeycomb](wikipedia://en/31747782), with Coxeter graph , filling space by [6-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-simplex), [rectified 6-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectified_6-simplex), and [birectified 6-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birectified_6-simplex) facets.

## Projection by folding {#Projection_by_folding}

## Kissing number {#Kissing_number}

These honeycombs, seen as tangent n-spheres located at the center of each honeycomb vertex have a fixed number of contacting spheres and correspond to the number of vertices in the [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719). This represents the highest [kissing number](wikipedia://en/408555) for 2 and 3 dimensions, but falls short on higher dimensions. In 2-dimensions, the triangular tiling defines a circle packing of 6 tangent spheres arranged in a regular hexagon, and for 3 dimensions there are 12 tangent spheres arranged in a [cuboctahedral](wikipedia://en/6280) configuration. For 4 to 8 dimensions, the kissing numbers are [20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expanded_4-simplex), [30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expanded_5-simplex), [42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expanded_5-simplex), [56](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expanded_6-simplex), and [72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expanded_7-simplex) spheres, while the greatest solutions are 24, 40, 72, 126, and 240 spheres respectively.
