# Verena Huber-Dyson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Verena Huber-Dyson"}

**Verena Esther Huber-Dyson** (**Huber**; May 6, 1923 – March 12, 2016) was a Swiss-American mathematician, known for her work on [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [formal logic](wikipedia://en/19636).[^o62][^n1] She has been described as a "brilliant mathematician",[^n1] who did research on the interface between [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), focusing on [undecidability](wikipedia://en/15631055) in group theory. At the time of her death, she was emeritus faculty in the philosophy department of the [University of Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calgary), Alberta.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Huber-Dyson was born Verena Esther Huber in [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy), on May 6, 1923. Her parents, Karl (Charles) Huber (1893–1946) and Berthy Ryffel (1899–1945), were Swiss nationals[^n2] who raised Verena and her sister Adelheid ("Heidi", 1925–1987) in [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece), where the girls attended the German-speaking *Deutsche Schule*, or [German School of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_School_of_Athens), until forced to return to Switzerland in 1940 by the war.

Charles Huber, who had managed the Middle Eastern operations of Bühler AG, a Swiss food-process engineering firm, began working for the [International Committee of the Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_of_the_Red_Cross) (ICRC), monitoring the treatment of prisoners of war in internment camps. As the ICRC delegate to India and Ceylon, he was responsible for Italian prisoners held in British camps, but also visited German and Allied camps in Europe. In 1945-46 he served as an ICRC delegate to the United States, which he described to Verena as a place she "definitely ought to experience at length and in depth but just as definitely ought not to settle in."[^o62]

She studied mathematics, with minors in physics and philosophy, at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich), where she obtained her Ph.D. in mathematics in 1947 with a thesis in [finite group theory](wikipedia://en/323707)[^n3][^o63][^o64] under the supervision of [Andreas Speiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Speiser).

### Career {#Career}

Huber-Dyson accepted a postdoctoral fellow appointment at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1948,[^o66] where she worked on [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [formal logic](wikipedia://en/19636).[^n4][^n5] She also began teaching at [Goucher College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goucher_College) near Baltimore during this time.[^n5]

She moved to California with her daughter Katarina, began teaching at [San Jose State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_State_University) in 1959, and then joined [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920)'s Group in Logic and the Methodology of Science at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^n4][^n6]

Huber-Dyson taught at San Jose State University, the [University of Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zürich), [Monash University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monash_University), as well as at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), [Adelphi University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelphi_University), [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), and the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago), in mathematics and in philosophy departments. She accepted a position in the philosophy department of the [University of Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calgary) in 1973, becoming emerita in 1988.[^n7]

- Taught graduate courses on foundations of mathematics and the philosophy and methodology of the sciences
- Began work on the monograph, Gödel's theorems: a workbook on formalization[^n9]

### Non-academic employment {#Non-academic_employment}

- Consultant for [Remington Rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remington_Rand) (Univac) in Philadelphia
- Consultant for [Hughes Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Aircraft_Company) in Los Angeles

### Later life {#Later_life}

After retiring from Calgary, Verena Huber-Dyson moved back to [South Pender Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pender_Island) in [British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia), where she lived for 14 years.[^n10][^n11] She died on March 12, 2016, in [Bellingham, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellingham,_Washington), at the age of 92.[^o62][^n8]

### Personal life {#Personal_life}

Verena married Hans-Georg Haefeli, a fellow mathematician, in 1942, and was divorced in 1948. Her first daughter was born in 1945.[^n2][^n4]

She subsequently married [Freeman Dyson](wikipedia://en/11397) in Ann Arbor, Michigan, on August 11, 1950.[^o63] They had two children together, [Esther Dyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Dyson) and [George Dyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Dyson_%28science_historian%29),[^n1][^o63] and divorced in 1958.[^n4]
