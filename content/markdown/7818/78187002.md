# Alessandro Chiesa

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alessandro Chiesa"}

Academics **Alessandro Chiesa** is an Italian computer scientist. He is an associate professor at [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne) (EPFL), and a now-adjunct professor at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). He has contributed to the field of [zero-knowledge proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-knowledge_proofs), and was a co-founder of [Zcash](wikipedia://en/51849458) and [StarkWare Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarkWare_Industries).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Chiesa was born in [Varese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varese), Italy in 1987. He studied math and computer science at MIT, where he earned his SB, MEng and PhD. His PhD was in [theory of computation](wikipedia://en/30402), advised by [Silvio Micali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Micali) at the [MIT Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Computer_Science_and_Artificial_Intelligence_Laboratory).[^o3]

Chiesa became an assistant professor at UC Berkeley in 2015. In 2018 he was named in MIT's [Innovators Under 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovators_Under_35) list.[^o4] In 2021 he became an associate professor at EPFL.[^o5]

## Research {#Research}

In 2013, Chiesa coauthored a paper and implementation of TinyRAM, a virtual machine whose execution could be proven using zero-knowledge proofs.[^o7]

In 2014, Chiesa coauthored the design of Zerocash,[^o8] which laid the foundation for the [Zcash](wikipedia://en/51849458) protocol. The work was recognized by the Test of Time Award at the [IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Symposium_on_Security_and_Privacy).[^o9]

In 2020, Chiesa coauthored the design of Zexe, a related protocol which drew on ideas from Zerocash and extended it to arbitrary computations.[^o10] The Zexe design became the foundation of a blockchain called Aleo.[^o11]
