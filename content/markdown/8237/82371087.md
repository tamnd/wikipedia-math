# Dispersion function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dispersion function"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **dispersion function** is a functional that characterizes a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) by measuring the expected absolute deviation of a random variable from any given point. It was introduced by J. Muñoz-Pérez and A. Sánchez-Gómez in 1990 as a tool for studying [statistical dispersion](wikipedia://en/23773247) and inducing a partial ordering of distributions.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $X$ be a real-valued [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with a finite expectation ($X \in \mathcal{L}_1$). The **dispersion function** $D_X(u)$ is defined as the absolute moment of order $r=1$ of the random variable $X$ with respect to $u$:[^o2]

$$D_X(u) = E|X - u|, \quad \forall u \in \mathbb{R}$$

:::

## Characterization of the distribution {#Characterization_of_the_distribution}

The dispersion function uniquely determines the [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) (CDF) of $X$. If $C_F$ is the set of continuity points of $F_X$, the distribution function can be recovered via the derivative of the dispersion function:[^o2]

$$F_X(u) = \frac{1}{2}(D_X'(u) + 1)$$

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The dispersion function has the following properties:[^o2]

- **Convexity**: $D_X$ is a [convex function](wikipedia://en/245568) on $\mathbb{R}$.
- **Differentiability**: It is differentiable, and its derivative $D_X'$ has at most a countable number of discontinuity points.
- **Asymptotic behavior of the derivative**: The limits of the derivative are $\lim_{x \to \infty} D_X'(x) = 1$ and $\lim_{x \to -\infty} D_X'(x) = -1$.
- **Mean relationship**: The limits involving the mean $EX$ are given by $\lim_{x \to \infty} [D_X(x) - x] = -EX$ and $\lim_{x \to -\infty} [D_X(x) + x] = EX$.

:::

## Relation to Variance {#Relation_to_Variance}

For a random variable with finite variance $\sigma^2$, the $L_1$-distance between its dispersion function and the dispersion function of the degenerate random variable at its mean ($D_{EX}(u) = |EX - u|$) is exactly the variance:[^o2]

$$\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} |D_X(u) - D_{EX}(u)| du = \sigma^2$$

## Dispersive Ordering {#Dispersive_Ordering}

In the study of [stochastic orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_orders), the dispersion function provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the dispersive ordering. This concept builds upon earlier work by Bickel and Lehmann regarding descriptive statistics for non-parametric models.[^o10] According to Shaked and Shanthikumar,[^o11] this characterization allows for the comparison of distributions even when they have the same finite support, such as comparing a [continuous uniform distribution](wikipedia://en/1699223) to a [triangular distribution](wikipedia://en/663496) (Simpson's distribution).

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

A generalized **dispersion function of order p** is defined as the $L_p$-distance between the [quantile function](wikipedia://en/10167616) $Q_X$ and the quantile function of a degenerate variable at $u$:[^o2]

$$D_X(u,p) = \left( \int_{0}^{1} [Q_X(t) - u]^p d\Lambda(t) \right)^{1/p}$$

where $\Lambda$ is a probability distribution on $(0,1)$ and $p$ is any positive number.

:::
