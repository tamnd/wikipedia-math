# Thomas W. Scanlon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas W. Scanlon"}

**Thomas Warren Scanlon** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his work in [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858). He was selected for the [Gödel Lecture](wikipedia://en/69216431) in 2024.[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Scanlon studied mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), earning a bachelor’s degree in 1993, and obtained his Ph.D. at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1997 under [Ehud Hrushovski](wikipedia://en/3132981). His thesis was titled *Model Theory of Valued D-Fields with Applications to Diophantine Approximations in Algebraic Groups*. He is a professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).

His work lies in mathematical logic—particularly [Model theory](wikipedia://en/19858)—with applications to number theory and arithmetic geometry (including the [André–Oort conjecture](wikipedia://en/32262980))[^o5][^o6][^o7] and in algebra and [Differential algebra](wikipedia://en/1816587).

In 2006, Scanlon was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Madrid, speaking on *Analytic difference rings*.[^o8]

In 2024, Scanlon was selected for the [Gödel Lecture](wikipedia://en/69216431).[^o2]
