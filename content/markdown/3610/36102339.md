# Perpendicular bisector construction of a quadrilateral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perpendicular bisector construction of a quadrilateral"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **perpendicular bisector construction of a quadrilateral** is a construction which produces a new [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) from a given quadrilateral using the perpendicular bisectors to the sides of the former quadrilateral. This construction arises naturally in an attempt to find a replacement for the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) of a quadrilateral in the case that is non-cyclic.

:::

## Definition of the construction {#Definition_of_the_construction}

Suppose that the [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) of the [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) $Q$ are given by $Q_1,Q_2,Q_3,Q_4$. Let $b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4$ be the perpendicular bisectors of sides $Q_1Q_2,Q_2Q_3,Q_3Q_4,Q_4Q_1$ respectively. Then their intersections $Q_i^{(2)}=b_{i+2}b_{i+3}$, with subscripts considered modulo 4, form the consequent quadrilateral $Q^{(2)}$. The construction is then iterated on $Q^{(2)}$ to produce $Q^{(3)}$ and so on.

*Image: PerpendicularBisectorConstruction.svg.* First iteration of the perpendicular bisector construction

An equivalent construction can be obtained by letting the vertices of $Q^{(i+1)}$ be the [circumcenters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) of the 4 triangles formed by selecting combinations of 3 vertices of $Q^{(i)}$.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

\1. If $Q^{(1)}$ is not cyclic, then $Q^{(2)}$ is not degenerate.[^o5]

\2. Quadrilateral $Q^{(2)}$ is never cyclic.[^o5] Combining #1 and #2, $Q^{(3)}$ is always nondegenrate.

\3. Quadrilaterals $Q^{(1)}$ and $Q^{(3)}$ are [homothetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homothetic_transformation), and in particular, [similar](wikipedia://en/39377).[^o6] Quadrilaterals $Q^{(2)}$ and $Q^{(4)}$ are also homothetic.

\3. The perpendicular bisector construction can be reversed via [isogonal conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isogonal_conjugation).[^o7] That is, given $Q^{(i+1)}$, it is possible to construct $Q^{(i)}$.

\4. Let $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta$ be the angles of $Q^{(1)}$. For every $i$, the ratio of areas of $Q^{(i)}$ and $Q^{(i+1)}$ is given by[^o7]

$$(1/4)(\cot(\alpha)+\cot(\gamma))(\cot(\beta)+\cot(\delta)).$$

\5. If $Q^{(1)}$ is convex then the sequence of quadrilaterals $Q^{(1)}, Q^{(2)},\ldots$ converges to the [isoptic point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoptic_point) of $Q^{(1)}$, which is also the isoptic point for every $Q^{(i)}$. Similarly, if $Q^{(1)}$ is concave, then the sequence $Q^{(1)}, Q^{(0)}, Q^{(-1)},\ldots$ obtained by reversing the construction converges to the Isoptic Point of the $Q^{(i)}$'s.[^o7]

\6. If $Q^{(1)}$ is tangential then $Q^{(2)}$ is also tangential.[^o8]

:::
