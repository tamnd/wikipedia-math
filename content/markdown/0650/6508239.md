# Michael Lacey (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Lacey"}

**Michael Thoreau Lacey** (born September 26, 1959)[^o2] is an American mathematician. Lacey received his PhD from the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign) in 1987, under the direction of Walter Philipp.[^o3] His thesis was in the area of probability in [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), and solved a problem related to the [law of the iterated logarithm](wikipedia://en/496558) for [empirical characteristic functions](wikipedia://en/3229401). In the intervening years, his work has touched on the areas of [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), and [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147).

:::

His first postdoctoral positions were at the [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University), and the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill). While at UNC, Lacey and Walter Philipp gave their proof of the almost sure [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406).

He held a position at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University) from 1989 to 1996. While there, he received a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) Postdoctoral Fellowship, and during the tenure of this fellowship he began a study of the bilinear [Hilbert transform](wikipedia://en/574024). This transform was at the time the subject of a conjecture by [Alberto Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Calderón) that Lacey and [Christoph Thiele](wikipedia://en/56559503) solved in 1996, for which they were awarded the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize).[^o4]

Since 1996, he has been a professor of mathematics at the [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology). In 2004, he received a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) for joint work with Xiaochun Li.[^o5] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o6]
