# Conic bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conic bundle"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **conic bundle** is an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) that appears as a solution to a [Cartesian equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_equation) of the form:

:::

$$X^2 + aXY + b Y^2 = P (T).\,$$

Conic bundles can be considered as either a [Severi–Brauer](wikipedia://en/1669368) or [Châtelet surface](wikipedia://en/23342300). This can be a double covering of a [ruled surface](wikipedia://en/705158). It can be associated with the symbol $(a, P)$ in the second [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) of the field $k$ through an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828). In practice, it is more commonly observed as a surface with a well-understood [divisor class group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor_class_group), and the simplest cases share with [Del Pezzo surfaces](wikipedia://en/732323) the property of being a [rational surface](wikipedia://en/2380148). But many problems of contemporary mathematics remain open, notably, for those examples which are not rational, the question of [unirationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unirationality).

## Expression {#Expression}

In order to properly express a conic bundle, one must first simplify the [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) on the left side. This can be achieved through a transformation, such as:

$$X^2 - aY^2 = P (T). \,$$

This is followed by placement in [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) to complete the surface at infinity, which may be achieved by writing the equation in [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316) and expressing the first visible part of the fiber:

$$X^2 - aY^2 = P (T) Z^2. \,$$

That is not enough to complete the fiber as non-singular (smooth and proper), and then glue it to infinity by a change of classical maps.

Seen from infinity, (i.e. through the change $T\mapsto T'=1/ T$), the same fiber (excepted the fibers $T = 0$ and $T '= 0$), written as the set of solutions $X'^2 - aY'^2= P^* (T') Z'^2$ where $P^* (T ')$ appears naturally as the [reciprocal polynomial](wikipedia://en/1106042) of $P$. Details are below about the map-change $[x ':y': z ']$.

### The fiber *c* {#The_fiber_c}

For the sake of simplicity, suppose the field $k$ is of [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero) and denote by $m$ any nonzero integer. Denote by $P(T)$ a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) with coefficients in the field $k$, of degree $2m$ or $2m-1$, without multiple roots. Consider the scalar $a$.

$F_{a,P}$ is the surface obtained as "gluing" of the two surfaces $U$ and $U'$ of equations

$$X^2 - aY^ 2 = P (T) Z^2$$

$$X '^2 - aY'^2 = P^* (T ') Z'^ 2$$

$x '= x, y' = y,$ and $z '= z t^m$.

### Fundamental property {#Fundamental_property}

The surface *F*<sub>*a*,*P*</sub> is a *k* smooth and proper surface, the mapping defined by

$$p: U \to P_{1, k}$$

by

$$([x:y:z],t)\mapsto t$$

and the same definition applied to $U '$ gives to *F*<sub>*a*,*P*</sub> a structure of conic bundle over *P*<sub>1,*k*</sub>.
