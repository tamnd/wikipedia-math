# Vector (mathematics and physics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vector"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), a **vector** is a generalization of a single [number](wikipedia://en/21690). It may denote a [vector quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_quantity), i.e., [physical quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_quantity) that cannot be expressed by a single [scalar quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_%28physics%29). The term may also be used to refer to elements of [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), that can be added together and multiplied ("scaled") by [scalars](wikipedia://en/3588331). In some contexts, vectors are [tuples](wikipedia://en/132729), which are [finite sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_sequence) (of numbers or other objects) of a fixed length.

:::

Historically, vectors were introduced in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and physics (typically in [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics)) for quantities that have both a magnitude and a direction, such as [displacements](wikipedia://en/859275), [forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force) and [velocity](wikipedia://en/20491903). Such quantities are represented by [geometric vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_vector) in the same way as [distances](wikipedia://en/39378), [masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass) and [time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time) are represented by [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438).

Both geometric vectors and tuples can be added and scaled, and these vector operations led to the concept of a vector space, which is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) equipped with a [vector addition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_addition) and a [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373) that satisfy some [axioms](wikipedia://en/928) generalizing the main properties of operations on the above sorts of vectors. A vector space formed by geometric vectors is called a [Euclidean vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_vector_space), and a vector space formed by tuples is called a [coordinate vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_vector_space).

Many vector spaces are considered in mathematics, such as [extension fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extension_field), [polynomial rings](wikipedia://en/373065), [algebras](wikipedia://en/191788) and [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630). The term *vector* is generally not used for elements of these vector spaces, and is generally reserved for geometric vectors, tuples, and elements of unspecified vector spaces (for example, when discussing general properties of vector spaces).

## Vectors in algebra {#Vectors_in_algebra}

Every [algebra over a field](wikipedia://en/191788) is a vector space, but elements of an algebra are generally not called vectors. However, in some cases, they are called *vectors*, mainly due to historical reasons.

- [Vector quaternion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_quaternion), a [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) with a zero real part
- [Multivector](wikipedia://en/2727288) or [$p$-vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-vector), an element of the [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) of a vector space.
- [Spinors](wikipedia://en/29276), also called *spin vectors*, have been introduced for extending the notion of [rotation vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_vector). In fact, rotation vectors represent well rotations *locally*, but not globally, because a [closed loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_curve) in the space of rotation vectors may induce a curve in the space of rotations that is not a loop. Also, the [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) of rotation vectors is [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable_manifold), while the manifold of rotations is not. Spinors are elements of a vector subspace of some [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305).
- [Witt vector](wikipedia://en/5573710), an infinite sequence of elements of a commutative ring, which belongs to an [algebra over this ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_over_a_ring), and has been introduced for handling [carry](wikipedia://en/1629687) propagation in the operations on [p-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423).

## Data represented by vectors {#Data_represented_by_vectors}

The set $\mathbb R^n$ of [tuples](wikipedia://en/132729) of $n$ real numbers has a natural structure of vector space defined by component-wise addition and [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373). It is common to call these tuples *vectors*, even in contexts where vector-space operations do not apply. More generally, when some data can be represented naturally by vectors, they are often called *vectors* even when addition and scalar multiplication of vectors are not valid operations on these data.Tuples that aren’t really vectors? Here are some examples.

- [Rotation vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_vector), a [Euclidean vector](wikipedia://en/32533) whose direction is that of the axis of a [rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_%28geometry%29) and magnitude is the angle of the rotation.
- [Burgers vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgers_vector), a vector that represents the magnitude and direction of the lattice distortion of dislocation in a crystal lattice
- [Interval vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_vector), in musical set theory, an array that expresses the intervallic content of a pitch-class set
- [Probability vector](wikipedia://en/217133), in statistics, a vector with non-negative entries that sum to one.
- [Random vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_vector) or [multivariate random variable](wikipedia://en/49821), in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a set of [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) that may be [correlated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlated). However, a *random vector* may also refer to a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) that takes its values in a vector space.
- [Logical vector](wikipedia://en/4109196#Logical_vectors), a vector of 0s and 1s ([Booleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_data_type)).

## Vectors in calculus {#Vectors_in_calculus}

[Calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) serves as a foundational mathematical tool in the realm of vectors, offering a framework for the analysis and manipulation of vector quantities in diverse scientific disciplines, notably [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering). Vector-valued functions, where the output is a vector, are scrutinized using calculus to derive essential insights into motion within three-dimensional space. Vector calculus extends traditional calculus principles to vector fields, introducing operations like [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461), [divergence](wikipedia://en/8328), and [curl](wikipedia://en/6123), which find applications in physics and engineering contexts. [Line integrals](wikipedia://en/21245414), crucial for calculating work along a path within force fields, and [surface integrals](wikipedia://en/528867), employed to determine quantities like [flux](wikipedia://en/43590), illustrate the practical utility of calculus in vector analysis. [Volume integrals](wikipedia://en/1247265), essential for computations involving scalar or vector fields over three-dimensional regions, contribute to understanding [mass distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_distribution), [charge density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_density), and fluid flow rates.

### Vector fields {#Vector_fields}

A [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) is a [vector-valued function](wikipedia://en/3675281) that, generally, has a domain of the same dimension (as a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470)) as its codomain,

- [Conservative vector field](wikipedia://en/461454), a vector field that is the gradient of a scalar potential field
- [Hamiltonian vector field](wikipedia://en/1818270), a vector field defined for any energy function or Hamiltonian
- [Killing vector field](wikipedia://en/1025455), a vector field on a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) associated with a symmetry
- [Solenoidal vector field](wikipedia://en/419444), a vector field with zero divergence
- [Vector potential](wikipedia://en/422936), a vector field whose curl is a given vector field
- [Vector flow](wikipedia://en/2620168), a set of closely related concepts of the flow determined by a vector field
