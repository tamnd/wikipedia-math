# BF model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="BF model"}

The **BF model** or **BF theory** is a topological [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29), which when [quantized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantization_%28physics%29), becomes a [topological quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/443757). BF stands for background field **B** and **F**, as can be seen below, are also the variables appearing in the [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/40752010) of the theory, which is helpful as a mnemonic device.

:::

We have a 4-dimensional [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) M, a [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group) G, which has as "dynamical" fields a [2-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-form) **B** taking values in the [adjoint representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_group) of G, and a [connection form](wikipedia://en/706412) **A** for G.

The [action](wikipedia://en/312881) is given by

$$S=\int_M K[\mathbf{B}\wedge \mathbf{F}]$$

where K is an invariant [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate) [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) over $\mathfrak{g}$ (if G is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1337587), the [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386) will do) and **F** is the [curvature form](wikipedia://en/489440)

$$\mathbf{F}\equiv d\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A}\wedge \mathbf{A}$$

This action is [diffeomorphically](wikipedia://en/8564) invariant and [gauge invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_invariance). Its [Euler–Lagrange equations](wikipedia://en/294995) are

$\mathbf{F}=0$ (no curvature)

and

$d_\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}=0$ (the [covariant exterior derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariant_exterior_derivative) of **B** is zero).

In fact, it is always possible to gauge away any local degrees of freedom, which is why it is called a topological field theory.

However, if M is topologically nontrivial, **A** and **B** can have nontrivial solutions globally.

In fact, BF theory can be used to formulate discrete gauge theory. One can add additional twist terms allowed by group cohomology theory such as [Dijkgraaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbert_Dijkgraaf)–[Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) topological gauge theory.[^o2] There are many kinds of modified BF theories as [topological theories](wikipedia://en/443757), which give rise to [link invariants](wikipedia://en/451999) in 3 dimensions, 4 dimensions, and other general dimensions.[^o3]
