# Ergun equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ergun equation"}

The **Ergun equation**, derived by the [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey) [chemical engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineer) [Sabri Ergun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabri_Ergun) in 1952, expresses the friction factor in a [packed column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packed_bed) as a function of the modified [Reynolds number](wikipedia://en/23868856).

:::

## Equation {#Equation}

$$f_p = \frac {150}{Gr_p}+1.75$$

where:

- $$f_p = \frac{\Delta p}{L} \frac{D_p}{\rho v_s^2} \left(\frac{\epsilon^3}{1-\epsilon}\right),$$
- $$Gr_p = \frac{\rho v_s D_p}{(1-\epsilon)\mu} = \frac{Re}{(1-\epsilon)},$$
- $Gr_p$ is the modified Reynolds number,
- $f_p$ is the packed bed [friction factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darcy–Weisbach_equation),
- $\Delta p$ is the [pressure drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure_drop) across the bed,
- $L$ is the length of the bed (not the column),
- $D_p$ is the equivalent spherical diameter of the packing,
- $\rho$ is the [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) of [fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid),
- $\mu$ is the [dynamic viscosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_viscosity) of the fluid,
- $v_s$ is the [superficial velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superficial_velocity) (i.e. the velocity that the fluid would have through the empty tube at the same volumetric flow rate),
- $\epsilon$ is the void fraction ([porosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porosity)) of the bed, and
- $Re$ is the particle [Reynolds Number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reynolds_Number) (based on [superficial velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superficial_velocity)[^o3])..

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extension"}

## Extension {#Extension}

To calculate the pressure drop in a given reactor, the following equation may be deduced:

$$\Delta p = \frac{150\mu ~L}{D_p^2} ~\frac{(1-\epsilon)^2}{\epsilon^3}v_s + \frac{1.75~L~\rho}{D_p}~ \frac{(1-\epsilon)}{\epsilon^3}v_s|v_s|.$$

This arrangement of the Ergun equation makes clear its close relationship to the simpler [Kozeny-Carman equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozeny-Carman_equation), which describes [laminar flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laminar_flow) of fluids across packed beds via the first term on the right hand side. On the continuum level, the second-order velocity term demonstrates that the Ergun equation also includes the pressure drop due to inertia, as described by the [Darcy–Forchheimer equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darcy's_law#Darcy–Forchheimer_law). Specifically, the Ergun equation gives the following permeability $k$ and inertial permeability $k_1$ from the Darcy-Forchheimer law:

$$k = \frac{D_p^2}{150} ~\frac{\epsilon^3}{(1-\epsilon)^2},$$

and

$$k_1 = \frac{D_p}{1.75} ~\frac{\epsilon^3}{1-\epsilon}.$$

The extension of the Ergun equation to [fluidized beds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluidized_bed), where the solid particles flow with the fluid, is discussed by Akgiray and Saatçı (2001).

:::
