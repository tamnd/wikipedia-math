# NEAR (blockchain platform)

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="NEAR"}

**NEAR** is a public [blockchain](wikipedia://en/44065971) platform that uses a [proof-of-stake](wikipedia://en/41143378) consensus mechanism and provides [smart-contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_contract) functionality. Its native [cryptocurrency](wikipedia://en/36662188) is NEAR. It was founded in 2018 by Illia Polosukhin and Alexander Skidanov, with mainnet launching in 2020.[^o5]

:::

NEAR was designed with a [sharded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shard_%28database_architecture%29) architecture for decentralized applications and high-throughput transactions, intended to serve as an alternative to [Ethereum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethereum) and other [layer-one blockchain networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layer-1_blockchain).[^o6][^o7]

## History {#History}

### Origins and founding (2017–2018) {#Origins_and_founding_(2017–2018)}

NEAR Protocol was founded in early 2017 by Illia Polosukhin and Alexander Skidanov. Initially, the project was established as Near.ai, an [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) startup focused on [program synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Program_synthesis). Polosukhin was a former engineering manager at [Google Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Research), where he co-authored the 2017 paper "[Attention Is All You Need](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention_Is_All_You_Need)," which introduced the [transformer architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer_%28deep_learning_architecture%29). Skidanov was previously the director of engineering at [MemSQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SingleStore) and a software engineer at [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft).

### Mainnet launch and funding (2019–2021) {#Mainnet_launch_and_funding_(2019–2021)}

The project began development on its [sharded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shard_%28database_architecture%29) architecture in late 2018. In July 2019, NEAR raised \$12.1 million in a [funding round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funding_round) led by Metastable Capital and Accomplice.[^o11]

The NEAR [mainnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainnet) launched in April 2020, initially operating in a "restricted" [proof of authority](wikipedia://en/56036557) mode to ensure stability. In May 2020, the project closed a \$21.6 million funding round led by [Andreessen Horowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreessen_Horowitz).[^o12]

## Criticism and controversies {#Criticism_and_controversies}

In 2022, Skyward Finance, an IDO/launchpad built on NEAR blockchain, was exploited for roughly \$3 million after an attacker abused a flaw in the project’s treasury redemption contract. The bug, identified by security firm BlockSec, failed to check for duplicate token account IDs, enabling the attacker to redeem wrapped NEAR in a loop within a single transaction. Skyward acknowledged that its treasury had been drained and advised users to cease interacting with the contracts, noting that the incident rendered the SKYWARD token effectively worthless.[^o14][^o15]

In 2023, a blockchain analyst questioned validator decentralization on [Avalanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalanche_%28blockchain_platform%29), [Solana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solana_%28blockchain_platform%29), NEAR, and other blockchain platforms. A Messari study found that roughly 35% of NEAR’s staked tokens were hosted on [Amazon Web Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services), yielding a hosting “operational Nakamoto coefficient” of 1 (i.e., more than one-third of stake concentrated on a single provider). The report also observed geographic concentration of validators and stake in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), limited presence in underrepresented regions, and reliance on a single [validator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Validator) client at the time; taken together, NEAR’s aggregated operational [Nakamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoshi_Nakamoto) coefficient was estimated at ~1.3, indicating elevated susceptibility to correlated infrastructure or jurisdictional failures relative to a more distributed network.[^o16]

In 2024, NEAR blockchain drew criticism after its official [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter) (formerly Twitter) account abruptly changed its display name to “it’s all a lie” and posted anti-[crypto](wikipedia://en/36662188) messages that prompted widespread speculation the account had been hacked. NEAR later indicated the episode was a marketing tactic tied to an upcoming event, which industry commentators and developers criticized as tone-deaf given the prevalence of genuine security breaches in the sector; some argued the stunt risked harming the project’s credibility. During the period of the campaign, [*Bloomberg*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomberg_News) reported that NEAR’s token declined by roughly 15% over the preceding week. *Bloomberg* also said that the controversy followed a real security incident in May 2023, when a compromised moderator account on NEAR’s [Discord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discord) was used to promote a fraudulent airdrop.[^o17]
