# Measurable Riemann mapping theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Measurable Riemann mapping theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **measurable Riemann mapping theorem** is a theorem proved in 1960 by [Lars Ahlfors](wikipedia://en/61636) and [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972)[^o2] in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and [geometric function theory](wikipedia://en/5458932). Contrary to its name, it is not a direct generalization of the [Riemann mapping theorem](wikipedia://en/26215), but instead a result concerning [quasiconformal mappings](wikipedia://en/2749226) and solutions of the [Beltrami equation](wikipedia://en/31356616). The result was prefigured by earlier results of [Charles Morrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Morrey) from 1938 on quasi-linear [elliptic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/662787).[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

The theorem of Ahlfors and Bers states that if μ is a bounded measurable function on **C** with $\|\mu\|_\infty < 1$, then there is a unique solution *f* of the Beltrami equation

$$\partial_{\overline{z}} f(z) = \mu(z) \partial_z f(z)$$

for which *f* is a quasiconformal homeomorphism of **C** fixing the points 0, 1 and ∞. A similar result is true with **C** replaced by the [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) **D**. Their proof used the [Beurling transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beurling_transform), a [singular integral operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_integral_operator).

:::
