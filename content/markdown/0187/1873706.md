# Néron–Severi group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Néron–Severi group"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **Néron–Severi group** of a [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) is the group of divisors modulo [algebraic equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_equivalence); in other words it is the group of [components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28topology%29) of the [Picard scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_scheme) of a variety. Its rank is called the [Picard number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_number). It is named after [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716) and [André Néron](wikipedia://en/9175241).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In the cases of most importance to classical algebraic geometry, for a [complete variety](wikipedia://en/1047627) *V* that is [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities), the [connected component](wikipedia://en/6233) of the identity of the Picard scheme is an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) written

Pic<sup>0</sup>(*V*).

The quotient

Pic(*V*)/Pic<sup>0</sup>(*V*)

is an abelian group NS(*V*), called the **Néron–Severi group** of *V*. This is a [finitely-generated abelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely-generated_abelian_group) by the Néron–Severi theorem, which was proved by Severi over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) and by Néron over more general fields.

In other words, the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) fits into an [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215)

$$1\to \mathrm{Pic}^0(V)\to\mathrm{Pic}(V)\to \mathrm{NS}(V)\to 0$$

The fact that the rank is finite is [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716)'s **theorem of the base**; the rank is the **Picard number** of *V*, often denoted ρ(*V*). The elements of finite order are called Severi divisors, and form a finite group which is a birational invariant and whose order is called the **Severi number**. Geometrically NS(*V*) describes the [algebraic equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_equivalence) classes of [divisors](wikipedia://en/1693055) on *V*; that is, using a stronger, non-linear equivalence relation in place of [linear equivalence of divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_equivalence_of_divisors), the classification becomes amenable to discrete invariants. Algebraic equivalence is closely related to [numerical equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_equivalence), an essentially topological classification by [intersection numbers](wikipedia://en/384327).

:::

## First Chern class and integral valued 2-cocycles {#First_Chern_class_and_integral_valued_2-cocycles}

The [exponential sheaf sequence](wikipedia://en/1881339)

$$0\to 2\pi i\mathbb Z \to \mathcal O_V\to\mathcal O_V^*\to 0$$

gives rise to a long exact sequence featuring

$$\cdots \to H^1(V, \mathcal O_V^*)\to H^2(V, 2\pi i \mathbb Z)\to H^2(V,\mathcal O_V)\to \cdots.$$

The first arrow is the [first Chern class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Chern_class) on the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134)

$$c_1 \colon \mathrm {Pic}(V)\to H^2(V, \mathbb Z),$$

and the Neron-Severi group can be identified with its image. Equivalently, by exactness, the Neron-Severi group is the kernel of the second arrow

$$\exp^* \colon H^2(V, 2\pi i\mathbb Z)\to H^2(V,\mathcal O_V).$$

In the complex case, the Neron-Severi group is therefore the group of 2-cocycles whose [Poincaré dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_dual) is represented by a complex hypersurface, that is, a [Weil divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_divisor).

### For complex tori {#For_complex_tori}

Complex tori are special because they have multiple equivalent definitions of the Neron-Severi group. One definition uses its complex structure for the definition[^o6]<sup>pg 30</sup>. For a complex torus $X = V/\Lambda$, where $V$ is a complex vector space of dimension $n$ and $\Lambda$ is a lattice of rank $2n$ embedding in $V$, the first Chern class $c_1$ makes it possible to identify the Neron-Severi group with the group of Hermitian forms $H$ on $V$ such that

> $$\text{Im}H(\Lambda,\Lambda) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$$

Note that $\text{Im}H$ is an alternating integral form on the lattice $\Lambda$.
