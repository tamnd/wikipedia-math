# Zero ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zero ring"}

In [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **zero ring**[^n1] or **trivial ring** is the unique [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) (up to [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828)) consisting of one element. (Less commonly, the term "zero ring" is used to refer to any [rng of square zero](wikipedia://en/3405004#Rng_of_square_zero), i.e., a [rng](wikipedia://en/3405004) in which $xy = 0$ for all $x$ and $y$. This article refers to the one-element ring.)

:::

In the [category of rings](wikipedia://en/15300181), the zero ring is the [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object), whereas the ring of integers **Z** is the [initial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_object).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The zero ring, denoted {0} or simply **0**, consists of the [one-element set](wikipedia://en/381798) {0} with the [operations](wikipedia://en/3948) + and · defined such that 0 + 0 = 0 and 0 · 0 = 0.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The zero ring is the unique ring in which the [additive identity](wikipedia://en/4178225) 0 and [multiplicative identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicative_identity) 1 coincide.[^n1][^n2] (Proof: If 1 = 0 in a ring *R*, then for all *r* in *R*, we have *r* = 1*r* = 0*r* = 0. The proof of the last equality is found here.)
- The zero ring is commutative.
- The element 0 in the zero ring is a [unit](wikipedia://en/289666), serving as its own [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940).
- The [unit group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_group) of the zero ring is the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059) {0}.
- The element 0 in the zero ring is not a [zero divisor](wikipedia://en/51441#Zero_as_a_zero_divisor).
- The only [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) in the zero ring is the zero ideal {0}, which is also the unit ideal, equal to the whole ring. This ideal is neither [maximal](wikipedia://en/48164) nor [prime](wikipedia://en/24928).
- The zero ring is generally excluded from [fields](wikipedia://en/10603), while occasionally called as the *trivial field*. Excluding it agrees with the fact that its zero ideal is not maximal. (When mathematicians speak of the "[field with one element](wikipedia://en/13798040)", they are referring to a non-existent object, and their intention is to define the category that would be the category of schemes over this object if it existed.)
- The zero ring is generally excluded from [integral domains](wikipedia://en/15462).[^n3] Whether the zero ring is considered to be a [domain](wikipedia://en/487627) at all is a matter of convention, but there are two advantages to considering it not to be a domain. First, this agrees with the definition that a domain is a ring in which 0 is the only zero divisor (in particular, 0 is required to be a zero divisor, which fails in the zero ring). Second, this way, for a positive integer *n*, the ring **Z**/*n***Z** is a domain if and only if *n* is prime, but 1 is not prime.
- For each ring *A*, there is a unique [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) from *A* to the zero ring. Thus the zero ring is a [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object) in the [category of rings](wikipedia://en/15300181).[^n4]
- If *A* is a nonzero ring, then there is no ring homomorphism from the zero ring to *A*. In particular, the zero ring is not a [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of any nonzero ring.[^n4]
- The zero ring is the unique ring of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) 1.
- The only [module](wikipedia://en/276410) for the zero ring is the zero module. It is free of rank א for any [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173) א.
- The zero ring is not a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391). It is, however, a [semilocal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semilocal_ring).
- The zero ring is [Artinian](wikipedia://en/489273) and (therefore) [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129).
- The [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) of the zero ring is the empty [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754).[^n4]
- The [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) of the zero ring is −∞.
- The zero ring is [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring) but not [simple](wikipedia://en/443387).
- The zero ring is not a [central simple algebra](wikipedia://en/404082) over any field.
- The [total quotient ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_quotient_ring) of the zero ring is itself.

:::

::: {#o14 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Constructions"}

## Constructions {#Constructions}

- For any ring *A* and ideal *I* of *A*, the [quotient](wikipedia://en/184635) *A*/*I* is the zero ring if and only if *I* = *A*, i.e. if and only if *I* is the [unit ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ideal).
- For any commutative ring *A* and [multiplicative set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicative_set) *S* in *A*, the [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) *S*<sup>−1</sup>*A* is the zero ring if and only if *S* contains 0.
- If *A* is any ring, then the ring M<sub>0</sub>(*A*) of 0 × 0 [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) over *A* is the zero ring.
- The [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887) of an empty collection of rings is the zero ring.
- The [endomorphism ring](wikipedia://en/59623) of the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059) is the zero ring.
- The ring of [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) real-valued functions on the empty [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is the zero ring.
