# Integral cryptanalysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Integral cryptanalysis"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **integral cryptanalysis** is a [cryptanalytic attack](wikipedia://en/5715) that is particularly applicable to [block ciphers](wikipedia://en/4594) based on [substitution–permutation networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution–permutation_network). It was originally designed by [Lars Knudsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Ramkilde_Knudsen) as a dedicated attack against [Square](wikipedia://en/613945), so it is commonly known as the **Square attack**. It was also extended to a few other ciphers related to Square: [CRYPTON](wikipedia://en/7237011), [Rijndael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijndael), and [SHARK](wikipedia://en/619666). [Stefan Lucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Lucks) generalized the attack to what he called a *saturation attack* and used it to attack [Twofish](wikipedia://en/26508356), which is not at all similar to Square, having a radically different [Feistel network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feistel_network) structure. Forms of integral cryptanalysis have since been applied to a variety of ciphers, including [Hierocrypt](wikipedia://en/8439595), [IDEA](wikipedia://en/15076), [Camellia](wikipedia://en/532475), [Skipjack](wikipedia://en/474702), [MISTY1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MISTY1), [MISTY2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MISTY2), [SAFER++](wikipedia://en/630497), [KHAZAD](wikipedia://en/615967), and *FOX* (now called [IDEA NXT](wikipedia://en/2067069)).

:::

Unlike [differential cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/8536), which uses pairs of [chosen plaintexts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chosen_plaintext_attack) with a fixed [XOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XOR) difference, integral cryptanalysis uses [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) or even [multisets](wikipedia://en/305303) of chosen plaintexts of which part is held constant, and another part varies through all possibilities. For example, an attack might use 256 chosen plaintexts that have all but 8 of their bits the same, but all differ in those 8 bits. Such a set necessarily has an XOR sum of 0, and the XOR sums of the corresponding sets of ciphertexts provide information about the cipher's operation. This contrast between the differences of pairs of texts and the sums of larger sets of texts inspired the name "integral cryptanalysis", borrowing the terminology of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176).
