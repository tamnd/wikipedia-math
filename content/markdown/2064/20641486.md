# Mutual coherence (linear algebra)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mutual coherence"}

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), **mutual coherence** (or simply **coherence**) measures the maximum similarity between any two columns of a matrix, defined as the largest absolute value of their [cross-correlations](wikipedia://en/714163).[^o2][^o3] First explored by David Donoho and Xiaoming Huo in the late 1990s for pairs of orthogonal bases,[^o4] it was later expanded by Donoho and Michael Elad in the early 2000s to study [sparse representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparse_representation)[^o5]—where signals are built from a few key components in a larger set.

:::

In [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), mutual coherence is widely used to assess how well algorithms like [matching pursuit](wikipedia://en/5534001) and [basis pursuit](wikipedia://en/29276125) can recover a signal's sparse representation from a collection with extra building blocks, known as an overcomplete dictionary.[^o2][^o3][^o6]

Joel Tropp extended this idea with the [Babel function](wikipedia://en/33170733), which applies coherence from one column to a group, equaling mutual coherence for two columns while broadening its use for larger sets with any number of columns.[^o7]

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Formally, let $a_1, \ldots, a_m\in {\mathbb C}^d$ be the columns of the matrix *A*, which are assumed to be normalized such that $a_i^H a_i = 1.$ The mutual coherence of *A* is then defined as[^o2][^o3]

$$M = \max_{1 \le i \ne j \le m} \left| a_i^H a_j \right|.$$

A lower bound is[^o10]

$$M\ge \sqrt{\frac{m-d}{d(m-1)}}.$$

A deterministic matrix with the mutual coherence almost meeting the lower bound can be constructed by [Weil's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil's_theorem).[^o11]

:::
