# Farrell–Jones conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Farrell–Jones conjecture"}

In mathematics, the **Farrell–Jones conjecture**,[^o2] named after [F. Thomas Farrell](wikipedia://en/46890166) and [Lowell E. Jones](wikipedia://en/48870101), states that certain [assembly maps](wikipedia://en/21311179) are [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828). These maps are given as certain [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564).

:::

The motivation is the interest in the target of the assembly maps; this may be, for instance, the [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) of a [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223)

$$K_n(RG)$$

or the [L-theory](wikipedia://en/696568) of a group ring

$$L_n(RG),$$

where *G* is some [group](wikipedia://en/19447).

The sources of the assembly maps are [equivariant homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant_homology_theory) evaluated on the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) of *G* with respect to the family of [virtually cyclic subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtually_cyclic_group) of *G*. So assuming the Farrell–Jones conjecture is true, it is possible to restrict computations to virtually cyclic subgroups to get information on complicated objects such as $K_n(RG)$ or $L_n(RG)$.

The [Baum–Connes conjecture](wikipedia://en/11774498) formulates a similar statement, for the [topological K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468) of reduced group $C^*$-algebras $K^ {top}_n(C^r_*(G))$.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

One can find for any ring $R$ [equivariant homology theories](wikipedia://en/2976342) $KR^?_*,LR^?_*$ satisfying

$KR_n^G(\{\cdot\})\cong K_n(R[G])$ respectively $LR_n^G(\{\cdot\})\cong L_n(R[G]).$

Here $R[G]$ denotes the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223).

The K-theoretic Farrell–Jones conjecture for a group *G* states that the map $p:E_{VCYC}(G)\rightarrow \{\cdot\}$ induces an isomorphism on homology

$$KR_*^G(p):KR_*^G(E_{VCYC}(G))\rightarrow KR_*^G(\{\cdot\})\cong K_*(R[G]).$$

Here $E_{VCYC}(G)$ denotes the [classifying space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classifying_spaces_for_families) of the group *G* with respect to the family of virtually cyclic subgroups, i.e. a *G*-CW-complex whose [isotropy groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotropy_group) are virtually cyclic and for any virtually cyclic subgroup of *G* the [fixed point set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_point_set) is [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible).

The L-theoretic Farrell–Jones conjecture is analogous.

:::

## Computational aspects {#Computational_aspects}

The computation of the algebraic K-groups and the L-groups of a group ring $R[G]$ is motivated by obstructions living in those groups (see for example [Wall's finiteness obstruction](wikipedia://en/36722383), [surgery obstruction](wikipedia://en/21298802), [Whitehead torsion](wikipedia://en/681186)). So suppose a group $G$ satisfies the Farrell–Jones conjecture for algebraic K-theory. Suppose furthermore we have already found a model $X$ for the classifying space for virtually cyclic subgroups:

$$\emptyset=X^{-1}\subset X^0\subset X^1\subset \ldots \subset X$$

Choose $G$-pushouts and apply the [Mayer-Vietoris sequence](wikipedia://en/378677) to them:

$KR_n^G(\coprod_{j\in I_i} G/H_j\times S^{i-1})\rightarrow KR_n^G(\coprod_{j\in I_i} G/H_j\times D^i)\oplus KR_n^G(X^{i-1})\rightarrow KR_n^G(X^i)$$\rightarrow KR_{n-1}^G(\coprod_{j\in I_i} G/H_j\times S^{i-1})\rightarrow KR_{n-1}^G(\coprod_{j\in I_i} G/H_j\times D^i)\oplus KR_{n-1}^G(X^{i-1})$

This sequence simplifies to:

$\bigoplus_{j\in I_i}K_n(R[H_j])\oplus \bigoplus_{j\in I_i} K_{n-1}(RH_j)\rightarrow \bigoplus_{j\in I_i} K_n(RH_j)\oplus KR_n^G(X^{i-1})\rightarrow KR_n^G(X^i)$$\rightarrow \bigoplus_{j\in I_i}K_{n-1}(RH_j)\oplus\bigoplus_{j\in I_i}K_{n-2}(RH_j)\rightarrow \bigoplus_{j\in I_i} K_{n-1}(RH_j)\oplus KR^G_{n-1}(X^{i-1})$

This means that if any group satisfies a certain isomorphism conjecture one can compute its algebraic K-theory (L-theory) only by knowing the algebraic K-Theory (L-Theory) of virtually cyclic groups and by knowing a suitable model for $E_{VCYC}(G)$.

### Why the family of virtually cyclic subgroups? {#Why_the_family_of_virtually_cyclic_subgroups?}

One might also try to take for example the family of finite subgroups into account. This family is much easier to handle. Consider the infinite cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}$. A model for $E_{FIN}(\mathbb{Z})$ is given by the real line $\mathbb{R}$, on which $\mathbb{Z}$ acts freely by translations. Using the properties of equivariant K-theory we get

$$K_n^\mathbb{Z}(\mathbb{R})=K_n(S^1)=K_n(pt)\oplus K_{n-1}(pt)=K_n(R)\oplus K_{n-1}(R).$$

The [Bass-Heller-Swan decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass-Heller-Swan_decomposition) gives

$$K_n^\mathbb{Z}(pt)=K_n(R[\mathbb{Z}])\cong K_n(R)\oplus K_{n-1}(R)\oplus NK_n(R)\oplus NK_n(R).$$

Indeed one checks that the assembly map is given by the canonical inclusion.

$$K_n(R)\oplus K_{n-1}(R)\hookrightarrow K_n(R)\oplus K_{n-1}(R)\oplus NK_n(R)\oplus NK_n(R)$$

So it is an isomorphism if and only if $NK_n(R) =0$, which is the case if $R$ is a [regular ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_ring). So in this case one can really use the family of finite subgroups. On the other hand this shows that the isomorphism conjecture for algebraic K-Theory and the family of finite subgroups is not true. One has to extend the conjecture to a larger family of subgroups which contains all the counterexamples. Currently no counterexamples for the Farrell–Jones conjecture are known. If there is a counterexample, one has to enlarge the family of subgroups to a larger family which contains that counterexample.

## Inheritances of isomorphism conjectures {#Inheritances_of_isomorphism_conjectures}

The class of groups which satisfies the fibered Farrell–Jones conjecture contain the following groups

- virtually cyclic groups (definition)
- [hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817) (see [^o9])
- [CAT(0) groups](wikipedia://en/12942017) (see [^o10])
- [solvable groups](wikipedia://en/105012) (see [^o11])
- [mapping class groups](wikipedia://en/793295) (see [^o12])

Furthermore the class has the following inheritance properties:

- Closed under finite products of groups.
- Closed under taking subgroups.

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Meta-conjecture and fibered isomorphism conjectures"}

## Meta-conjecture and fibered isomorphism conjectures {#Meta-conjecture_and_fibered_isomorphism_conjectures}

Fix an equivariant homology theory $H^?_*$. One could say, that a group *G* satisfies the isomorphism conjecture for a family of subgroups$F$, if and only if the map induced by the projection $E_F(G)\rightarrow \{\cdot\}$ induces an isomorphism on homology:

$$H_*^G(E_F(G))\rightarrow H_*^G(\{\cdot\})$$

The group *G* satisfies the fibered isomorphism conjecture for the family of subgroups *F* if and only if for any [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) $\alpha :H\rightarrow G$ the group *H* satisfies the isomorphism conjecture for the family

$$\alpha^*F:=\{H'\le H|\alpha(H)\in F\}.$$

One gets immediately that in this situation $H$ also satisfies the fibered isomorphism conjecture for the family $\alpha^*F$.

::: {#o16 .statement cue=named-heading name="Transitivity principle"}

### Transitivity principle {#Transitivity_principle}

The transitivity principle is a tool to change the family of subgroups to consider. Given two families $F\subset F'$ of subgroups of $G$. Suppose every group $H\in F'$ satisfies the (fibered) isomorphism conjecture with respect to the family $F|_H:=\{H'\in F|H'\subset H\}$. Then the group $G$ satisfies the fibered isomorphism conjecture with respect to the family $F$ if and only if it satisfies the (fibered) isomorphism conjecture with respect to the family $F'$.

:::

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Isomorphism conjectures and group homomorphisms"}

### Isomorphism conjectures and group homomorphisms {#Isomorphism_conjectures_and_group_homomorphisms}

Given any group homomorphism $\alpha\colon H\rightarrow G$ and suppose that *G"' satisfies the fibered isomorphism conjecture for a family*F*of subgroups. Then also*H"' satisfies the fibered isomorphism conjecture for the family $\alpha^*F$. For example if $\alpha$ has finite kernel the family $\alpha^*VCYC$ agrees with the family of virtually cyclic subgroups of *H*.

For suitable $\alpha$ one can use the transitivity principle to reduce the family again.

:::

## Connections to other conjectures {#Connections_to_other_conjectures}

::: {#o22 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Novikov conjecture"}

### Novikov conjecture {#Novikov_conjecture}

There are also connections from the Farrell–Jones conjecture to the [Novikov conjecture](wikipedia://en/3035194). It is known that if one of the following maps

$$H^G_*(E_{VCYC}(G),L^{\langle-\infty\rangle}_R)\rightarrow H^G_*(\{\cdot\},L^{\langle-\infty\rangle}_R)= L^{\langle-\infty\rangle}_*(RG)$$

$$H^G_*(E_{FIN}(G),K^{top}) \rightarrow H^G_*(\{\cdot\},K^{top}) = K_n(C^*_r(G))$$

is rationally injective, then the Novikov-conjecture holds for $G$. See for example,.[^o23][^o24]

:::

::: {#o26 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Bost conjecture"}

### Bost conjecture {#Bost_conjecture}

The Bost conjecture (named for [Jean-Benoît Bost](wikipedia://en/58487989)) states that the assembly map

$$H^G_*(E_{FIN}(G),K^{top}_{l^1})\rightarrow H^G_*(\{\cdot\},K^{top}_{l^1})=K_*(l^1(G))$$

is an isomorphism. The ring homomorphism $l^1(G)\rightarrow C_r(G)$ induces maps in K-theory $K_*(l^1(G))\rightarrow K_*(C_r(G))$. Composing the upper assembly map with this homomorphism one gets exactly the assembly map occurring in the [Baum–Connes conjecture](wikipedia://en/11774498).

$$H^G_*(E_{FIN}(G),K^{top}_{l^1})=H^G_*(E_{FIN}(G),K^{top})\rightarrow H^G_*(\{\cdot\},K^{top})=K_*(C_r(G))$$

:::

::: {#o28 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Kaplansky conjecture"}

### Kaplansky conjecture {#Kaplansky_conjecture}

The [Kaplansky conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaplansky's_conjecture) predicts that for an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) $R$ and a torsion-free group $G$ the only idempotents in $R[G]$ are $0,1$. Each such idempotent $p$ gives a projective $R[G]$ module by taking the image of the right multiplication with $p$. Hence there seems to be a connection between the Kaplansky conjecture and the vanishing of $K_0(R[G])$. There are theorems relating the Kaplansky conjecture to the Farrell–Jones conjecture (compare [^o29]).

:::
