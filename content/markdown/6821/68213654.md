# Paula Diehr

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Paula Diehr"}

**Paula K. Hagedorn Diehr** is an American [biostatistician](wikipedia://en/3878) whose research topics generally concern [health systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_system) and [ageing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageing), and have included work on [spatial variability](wikipedia://en/2876444) and [longitudinal data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitudinal_data), health care utilization, [mental health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health), [insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance), [diagnosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagnosis), and prediction of healthy life expectancies. She is a [professor emerita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emerita) of biostatistics, with a joint appointment in health systems and population health, at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Diehr graduated from [Harvey Mudd College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Mudd_College) in 1963. She went to the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) for graduate study, earning a master's degree and Ph.D. in biostatistics there. Her 1970 doctoral dissertation, *The Mixture Problem in Tiny Samples*, was supervised by [Wilfrid Dixon](wikipedia://en/47219631). She joined the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) faculty in 1970.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Diehr was named a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 1994, a Fellow of the Association for Health Services Research in 1996, and a Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 1997.

In 2013 Harvey Mudd College gave her their HMC Outstanding Alumni Award.
