# Glossary of invariant theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glossary of invariant theory"}

This page is a glossary of terms in [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762). For descriptions of particular invariant rings, see [invariants of a binary form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariants_of_a_binary_form), [symmetric polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_polynomials). For geometric terms used in invariant theory see the [glossary of classical algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/35326250). Definitions of many terms used in invariant theory can be found in (Sylvester 1853), (Cayley 1860), (Burnside Panton 1881), (Salmon 1885), (Elliott 1895), (Grace Young 1903), (Glenn 1915), (Dolgachev 2012), and the index to the fourth volume of Sylvester's collected works includes many of the terms invented by him.

:::

::: {#o11 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Conventions"}

## Conventions {#Conventions}

::: {#o12 .entry cue=term name="-an"}

**\-an**

Nouns ending in -an are often invariants named after people, as in Cayleyan, Hessian, Jacobian, Steinerian.

:::

::: {#o13 .entry cue=term name="-ant"}

**\-ant**

Nouns ending in -ant are often invariants, as in determinant, covariant, and so on.

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=term name="-ary"}

**\-ary**

Adjectives ending in -ary often refer to the number of variables of a form, as in unary, binary, ternary, quaternary, quinary, senary, septenary, octonary, nonary, denary.

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=term name="-ic"}

**\-ic**

Adjectives or nouns ending in -ic often refer to the degree of a form, as in linear or monic, quadric or quadratic, cubic, quartic or biquadratic, quintic, sextic, septic or septimic, octic or octavic, nonic, decic or decimic, undecic or undecimic, duodecic or duodecimic, and so on.

:::

## !\$@ {#!$@}

::: {#o17 .entry cue=term}

Short for the form (${}^{n}_{0}$)*a*<sub>0</sub>*x*<sup>*n*</sup> + (${}^{n}_{1}$)*a*<sub>1</sub>*x*<sup>*n*–1</sup>*y*+ ... + (${}^{n}_{n}$)*a*<sub>*n*</sub>*y*<sup>*n*</sup>. When the first ) has a circumflex or arrow on top of it, this means that the binomial coefficients are omitted. The parentheses are sometimes overlapped: $(a_0,\ldots,a_n)\!\!(x,y)^n$

:::

::: {#o18 .entry cue=term name="[]"}

**\[\]**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548)

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=term name="(αβγ...)"}

**(αβγ...)**

The determinant of the matrix with entries α<sub>*i*</sub>, β<sub>*i*</sub>, γ<sub>*i*</sub>,... For example, (αβ) means α<sub>1</sub>β<sub>2</sub> – α<sub>2</sub>β<sub>1</sub>.

:::

## A {#A}

::: {#o22 .entry cue=term name="absolute"}

**absolute**

The absolute invariant is essentially the [j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988) of an elliptic curve.

An absolute invariant is something fixed by a group action, in other words a (relative) invariant (something that transforms according to a character) where the character is trivial.

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=term name="allotrious"}

**allotrious**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548), Archaic.

:::

::: {#o25 .entry cue=term name="alternant"}

**alternant**

An archaic term for the commutator *AB*–*BA* of two operators *A* and *B*. (Elliott 1895, p.144)

An [alternant matrix](wikipedia://en/4418897) is a matrix such that the entries of each column are given by some fixed function of a variable.

:::

::: {#o27 .entry cue=term name="annihilator"}

**annihilator**

An annihilator is a differential operator representing an element of a Lie algebra, so that invariants of a group are killed by the annihilators. (Elliott 1895, p.108)

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=term name="anti-invariant"}

**anti-invariant**

A relative invariant transforming according to a character of order 2 of a group such as the symmetric group.

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=term name="anti-seminvariant"}

**anti-seminvariant**

(Elliott 1895, p.126)

:::

::: {#o32 .entry cue=term name="apocopated"}

**apocopated**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548). Archaic.

:::

::: {#o34 .entry cue=term name="Arf invariant"}

**Arf invariant**

An invariant of quadratic forms over a field of order 2.

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=term name="Aronhold invariant"}

**Aronhold invariant**

One of the two generators of degrees 4 and 6 of the ring of invariants of ternary cubic forms. (Dolgachev 2012, 3.1.1)

:::

::: {#o37 .entry cue=term name="asyzygetic"}

**asyzygetic**

Linearly independent.

:::

## B {#B}

::: {#o39 .entry cue=term name="Bezoutiant"}

**Bezoutiant**

A symmetric square matrix associated to two binary forms.

:::

::: {#o40 .entry cue=term name="Bezoutic"}

**Bezoutic**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548). Archaic.

:::

::: {#o42 .entry cue=term name="Bezoutiod"}

**Bezoutiod**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548). Archaic.

:::

::: {#o44 .entry cue=term name="bidegree"}

**bidegree**

An ordered pair of integers, giving the degrees of a form relative to two sets of variables.

:::

::: {#o45 .entry cue=term name="biform"}

**biform**

A polynomial homogeneous in each of two sets of variables. In other words an element of S<sup>*m*</sup>*V*×S<sup>*n*</sup>*W*, usually considered as a representation of GL<sub>*V*</sub>×GL<sub>*W*</sub>.

:::

::: {#o46 .entry cue=term name="binary"}

**binary**

Depending on 2 variables. Same as bivariate.

:::

::: {#o47 .entry cue=term name="biquadratic"}

**biquadratic**

Same as quartic, meaning degree 4.

:::

::: {#o48 .entry cue=term name="biternary"}

**biternary**

A biternary form is one in 6 variables, 3 transforming according to the fundamental representation of SL<sub>3</sub> and 3 transforming according to its dual.

:::

::: {#o49 .entry cue=term name="bivariate"}

**bivariate**

Depending on 2 variables. Same as binary.

:::

::: {#o50 .entry cue=term name="Boolean invariant"}

**Boolean invariant**

An invariant for the orthogonal group. (Elliott 1895, p.344)

:::

::: {#o52 .entry cue=term name="bordered Hessian"}

**bordered Hessian**

An alternative name for the reciprocant

:::

::: {#o53 .entry cue=term name="bracket"}

**bracket**

An invariant given by either the pairing of a vector and a vector in the dual space, or the determinant of a matrix form by *n* vectors of an *n*-dimensional space (in other words their exterior product in the top exterior power).

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=term name="Brioschi covariant"}

**Brioschi covariant**

This is a degree 12 order 9 covariant of [ternary cubic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternary_cubic_form), introduced by (Brioschi 1863). (Dolgachev 2012, 3.4.3)

:::

## C {#C}

::: {#o58 .entry cue=term name="canonical form"}

**canonical form**

A particularly simple representation of a form, such as a sum of powers of linear forms, or with many zero coefficients. For example, the canonical form of a binary form of degree 2*m*+1 is a sum of *m*+1 powers of linear forms.

:::

::: {#o59 .entry cue=term name="canonisant"}

**canonisant**

:::

::: {#o60 .entry cue=term name="canonizant"}

**canonizant**

A covariant of a form, given by the catalecticant of the penultimate emanant. It is related to the canonical form of a form. For example, the canonizant of a binary form of degree 2*n*–1 has degree *n* and order *n*. (Elliott 1895, p.21)

:::

::: {#o62 .entry cue=term name="catalecticant"}

**catalecticant**

An invariant vanishing on forms that are the sum of an unusually small number of powers of linear forms.

:::

::: {#o63 .entry cue=term name="Cayley Ω process"}

**Cayley Ω process**

A certain differential operator used for constructing invariants.

:::

::: {#o64 .entry cue=term name="Cayleyan"}

**Cayleyan**

A contravariant.

:::

::: {#o65 .entry cue=term name="characteristic"}

**characteristic**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548)

:::

::: {#o67 .entry cue=term name="class"}

**class**

The class of a contravariant or concomitant is its degree in the covariant variables. See also degree and order.

:::

::: {#o68 .entry cue=term name="Clebsch invariant"}

**Clebsch invariant**

(Dolgachev 2012, p.283)

:::

::: {#o70 .entry cue=term name="co-Bezoutiant"}

**co-Bezoutiant**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548). Archaic.

:::

::: {#o72 .entry cue=term name="cogredient"}

**cogredient**

Transforming according to the natural representation of a linear group. (Elliott 1895, p.55)

:::

::: {#o74 .entry cue=term name="combinant"}

**combinant**

A joint relative invariant of several forms of the same degree, that is unchanged if a multiple of one of the forms is added to another. Essentially a relative invariant of a product of two general linear groups. (Elliott 1895, p.340) (txt, Glossary p. 543–548) (Salmon 1885, p.161)

:::

::: {#o78 .entry cue=term name="combinative"}

**combinative**

Related to invariants of a product of groups. For example a combinative covariant is a covariant of a product of two groups.

:::

::: {#o79 .entry cue=term name="commutant"}

**commutant**

A generalization of the determinant to arrays of dimension greater than 2. (Cayley 1860)

:::

::: {#o81 .entry cue=term name="complete"}

**complete**

A complete system of invariants is a set of generators for the ring of invariants.

:::

::: {#o82 .entry cue=term name="concomitant"}

**concomitant**

A relative invariant of GL(*V*) acting on the polynomials over *S*<sup>*n*</sup>(*V*)⊕*V*⊕V\*.

:::

::: {#o83 .entry cue=term name="conjunctive"}

**conjunctive**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548)

:::

::: {#o85 .entry cue=term name="connex"}

**connex**

A form in two sets of variables, one set corresponding to a vector space and the other to its dual, or in other words an element of the symmetric algebra of *V*⊕*V*\* for a vector space *V*. Introduced by Clebsch.

:::

::: {#o86 .entry cue=term name="continuant"}

**continuant**

A determinant of a [tridiagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/519218).(Salmon 1885, p.18)

:::

::: {#o88 .entry cue=term name="contragredient"}

**contragredient**

Transforming according to the dual of the natural representation of a linear group. (Elliott 1895, p.74)

:::

::: {#o90 .entry cue=term name="contravariant"}

**contravariant**

A relative invariant of GL(*V*) acting on the polynomials over *S*<sup>*n*</sup>(*V*)⊕*V*.

:::

::: {#o91 .entry cue=term name="convolution"}

**convolution**

A method of constructing invariants from two other invariants. (Glenn 1915, p.87)

:::

::: {#o93 .entry cue=term name="covariancy"}

**covariancy**

(Elliott 1895, p.83)

:::

::: {#o95 .entry cue=term name="covariant"}

**covariant**

(Noun) A relative invariant of GL(*V*) acting on the polynomials over *S*<sup>*n*</sup>(*V*)⊕*V*\*.

(Adjective) Invariant under the action of a group, especially for functions between two spaces acted on by the group.

:::

::: {#o96 .entry cue=term name="cross ratio"}

**cross ratio**

The [cross ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_ratio) is an invariant of 4 points of a projective line.

:::

::: {#o97 .entry cue=term name="cubic"}

**cubic**

(Adjective) Degree 3

(Noun) A form of degree 3

:::

::: {#o98 .entry cue=term name="cubicovariant"}

**cubicovariant**

A covariant of degree 3, in particular an order 3 degree 3 covariant of a binary cubic given by the Jacobian of the cubic and its Hessian. (Elliott 1895, p.50)

:::

::: {#o100 .entry cue=term name="cubinvariant"}

**cubinvariant**

An invariant of degree 3.

:::

::: {#o101 .entry cue=term name="cubo-"}

**cubo-**

Used to form compound adjectives such as cubo-linear, cubo-quadric, and so on, indicating the bidegree of something. For example, cubo-linear means having degree 3 in the first of two sets of variables and degree 1 in the second.

:::

::: {#o102 .entry cue=term name="cumulant"}

**cumulant**

The numerator or denominator of a continued fraction, often expressed as a determinant. (txt, Glossary p. 543–548).

:::

## D {#D}

::: {#o105 .entry cue=term name="decic"}

**decic**

:::

::: {#o106 .entry cue=term name="decimic"}

**decimic**

(Adjective) Degree 10

(Noun) A form of degree 10

:::

::: {#o107 .entry cue=term name="degree"}

**degree**

The degree of a form is the total power of the variables in it.

The degree of an invariant or covariant or contravariant means its degree in terms of the coefficients of the form. The degree of a form considered as a form is usually not its degree when considered as a covariant.

Some authors exchange the meanings of "degree" and "order" of a covariant or concomitant.

:::

::: {#o108 .entry cue=term name="denary"}

**denary**

Depending on 10 variables

:::

::: {#o109 .entry cue=term name="determinant"}

**determinant**

The [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) is a joint invariant of *n* vectors of an *n*-dimensional space.

:::

::: {#o110 .entry cue=term name="dialytic"}

**dialytic**

Sylvester's dialytic method is a method for calculating resultants, essentially by expressing them as the determinant of a [Sylvester matrix](wikipedia://en/2174514). See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548). Archaic.

:::

::: {#o112 .entry cue=term name="differentiant"}

**differentiant**

Another name for an invariant of a binary form. Archaic.

:::

::: {#o113 .entry cue=term name="discriminant"}

**discriminant**

The discriminant of a form in *n* variables is the multivariate resultant of the *n* differentials with respect to each of the variables. For binary forms the discriminant vanishes if the form has multiple roots and is essentially the same as the discriminant of a polynomial of 1 variable. The discriminant of a form vanishes when the corresponding hypersurface has singularities (as a scheme).

:::

::: {#o114 .entry cue=term name="disjunctive"}

**disjunctive**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548)

:::

::: {#o116 .entry cue=term name="divariant"}

**divariant**

An alternative name for a concomitant suggested by (Salmon 1885, p.121)

:::

::: {#o118 .entry cue=term name="duodecic"}

**duodecic**

:::

::: {#o119 .entry cue=term name="duodecimic"}

**duodecimic**

(Adjective) Degree 12

(Noun) A form of degree 12

## E {#E}

::: {#o121 .entry cue=term name="effective"}

**effective**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548)

:::

::: {#o123 .entry cue=term name="effluent"}

**effluent**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548). Archaic.

:::

::: {#o125 .entry cue=term name="eliminant"}

**eliminant**

De Morgan's name for the (multivariate) [resultant](wikipedia://en/2195020), an invariant of *n* forms in *n* variables that vanishes if they have a common nonzero solution. (Elliott 1895, p.16)

:::

::: {#o127 .entry cue=term name="emanant"}

**emanant**

The *r*th emanant of a binary form in variables *x*<sub>*i*</sub> is a covariant given by the action of the *r*th power of the differential operator Σ*y*<sub>*i*</sub>∂/∂*x*<sub>*i*</sub>. This is essentially the same as polarization. (Elliott 1895, p.56) (txt, Glossary p. 543–548)

:::

::: {#o130 .entry cue=term name="endoscopic"}

**endoscopic**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548). Archaic.

:::

::: {#o132 .entry cue=term name="equianharmonic contravariant"}

**equianharmonic contravariant**

A weight 4 contravariant of binary quartics (Dolgachev 2012, 6.4)

:::

::: {#o134 .entry cue=term name="evectant"}

**evectant**

A contravariant given by the action of an evector.

:::

::: {#o135 .entry cue=term name="evector"}

**evector**

A differential operator constructed from a binary form.

:::

::: {#o136 .entry cue=term name="excess"}

**excess**

The excess of a polynomial in the coefficients *a*<sub>0</sub>,...*a*<sub>*p*</sub> of a form of degree *p* is *ip*–2*w*, where *p* is the degree of the polynomial and *w* is its weight. (Elliott 1895, p.141)

:::

::: {#o138 .entry cue=term name="exoscopic"}

**exoscopic**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548). Archaic.

:::

::: {#o140 .entry cue=term name="extensor"}

**extensor**

An element of the *k*th exterior power of a vector space that can be written as the exterior product of *k* vectors.

:::

::: {#o141 .entry cue=term name="extent"}

**extent**

The extent of a polynomial in *a*<sub>0</sub>, *a*<sub>1</sub>,... is the largest value of *p* such that the polynomial involves *a*<sub>*p*</sub>. (Elliott 1895, p.138)

:::

## F {#F}

::: {#o144 .entry cue=term name="facient"}

**facient**

One of the variables of a form (Cayley 1860)

:::

::: {#o146 .entry cue=term name="facultative"}

**facultative**

A facultative point is one where a given function is positive. (Salmon 1885, p.243)

:::

::: {#o148 .entry cue=term name="form"}

**form**

A homogeneous polynomial in several variables, also called a quantic.

:::

::: {#o149 .entry cue=term name="functional determinant"}

**functional determinant**

An archaic name for Jacobians

:::

::: {#o150 .entry cue=term name="fundamental"}

**fundamental**

The **first fundamental theorem** describes generators (called brackets) for the ring of invariant polynomials on a sum of copies of a vector space *V* and its dual (for the special linear group of *V*). The **second fundamental theorem** describes the syzygies between the generators.

For **fundamental scale** see (txt, Glossary p. 543–548). Archaic.

A **fundamental invariant** is an element of a set of generators for a ring of invariants.

A **fundamental system** is a set of generators (for a ring of invariants, covariants, and so on).

:::

## G {#G}

::: {#o153 .entry cue=term name="Gordan"}

**Gordan**

Named for [Paul Gordan](wikipedia://en/1059850).

**Gordan's theorem** states that the ring of invariants of a binary form (or several binary forms) is finitely generated.

:::

::: {#o154 .entry cue=term name="grade"}

**grade**

The highest power of a bracket factor in the symbolic expression for an invariant. (Glenn 1915, 4.8)

:::

::: {#o156 .entry cue=term name="gradient"}

**gradient**

A homogeneous polynomial in *a*<sub>0</sub>, ..., *a*<sub>*p*</sub> all of whose terms have the same weight, where *a*<sub>*n*</sub> has weight *n*. (Elliott 1895, p.138) Archaic.

:::

::: {#o158 .entry cue=term name="Gröbner basis"}

**Gröbner basis**

A basis for an ideal of a ring of polynomials chosen according to some rule to make computations easier.

:::

::: {#o159 .entry cue=term name="ground form"}

**ground form**

An element of a minimal set of homogeneous generators for the invariants of a form. Archaic.

:::

## H {#H}

::: {#o161 .entry cue=term name="hectic"}

**hectic**

A joke term for a form of degree 100.

:::

::: {#o162 .entry cue=term name="harmonic contravariant"}

**harmonic contravariant**

A weight 6 contravariant of binary quartics (Dolgachev 2012, 6.4)

:::

::: {#o164 .entry cue=term name="harmonizant"}

**harmonizant**

A bilinear invariant of two forms whose vanishing means they are polar. (Dolgachev 2012, p.75)

:::

::: {#o166 .entry cue=term name="Hermite"}

**Hermite**

Named after [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422)

The **Hermite contravariant** is a degree 12 class 9 contravariant of [ternary cubics](wikipedia://en/35767402). (Dolgachev 2012, 3.4.3)

[Hermite's law of reciprocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermite's_law_of_reciprocity) states that the degree *m* covariants of a binary form of degree *n* correspond to the degree *n* covariants of a binary form of degree *m*.

The **Hermite invariant** is the degree 18 skew invariant of a binary quintic.

:::

::: {#o168 .entry cue=term name="Hessian"}

**Hessian**

A covariant of a form *u*, given by the determinant of the matrix with entries ∂<sup>2</sup>*u*/∂*x*<sub>*i*</sub>∂*x*<sub>*j*</sub>.

:::

::: {#o169 .entry cue=term name="Hilbert"}

**Hilbert**

Named after [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)

A **Hilbert series** is a formal power series whose coefficients are dimensions of spaces of invariants of various degrees.

**Hilbert's theorem** states that the ring of invariants of a finite-dimensional representation of a reductive group is finitely generated.

:::

::: {#o170 .entry cue=term name="homographic"}

**homographic**

A homographic transformation is a transformation taking *x* to (*ax*+*b*)/(*cx*+*d*).

A homographic relation between *x* and *y* is a relation of the form *axy* + *bx* + *cy* + *d*=0 .

:::

::: {#o171 .entry cue=term name="hyperdeterminant"}

**hyperdeterminant**

An invariant of a multidimensional array of coefficients, generalizing the determinant of a 2-dimensional array.

:::

## I {#I}

::: {#o173 .entry cue=term name="identity covariant"}

**identity covariant**

A form considered as a covariant of degree 1.

:::

::: {#o174 .entry cue=term name="immanant"}

**immanant**

A generalization of the determinant and permanent of a matrix

:::

::: {#o175 .entry cue=term name="inertia"}

**inertia**

The signature of a real quadratic form. See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548)

:::

::: {#o177 .entry cue=term name="integral rational function"}

**integral rational function**

A polynomial.

:::

::: {#o178 .entry cue=term name="intercalations"}

**intercalations**

See (txt, Glossary p. 543–548). Archaic.

:::

::: {#o180 .entry cue=term name="intermediate invariant"}

**intermediate invariant**

An invariant of two forms constructed from two invariants of each of the forms. (Elliott 1895, p.23)

:::

::: {#o182 .entry cue=term name="intermutant"}

**intermutant**

A special form of permutant. (Cayley 1860)

:::

::: {#o184 .entry cue=term name="invariant"}

**invariant**

(Adjective) Fixed by the action of a group

(Noun) An absolute invariant, meaning something fixed by a group action.

(Noun) A relative invariant, meaning something transforming according to a character of a group. In classical invariant theory it often refers to relatively invariant polynomials in the coefficients of a quantic, considered as a representation of a general linear group.

:::

::: {#o185 .entry cue=term name="involutant"}

**involutant**

See Sylvester's collected papers, volume IV, page 135

:::

::: {#o186 .entry cue=term name="irreducible"}

**irreducible**

Not expressible as a polynomial in things of smaller degree.

:::

::: {#o187 .entry cue=term name="isobaric"}

**isobaric**

All terms having the same weight. (Elliott 1895, p.32)

:::

## J {#J}

::: {#o190 .entry cue=term name="Jacobian"}

**Jacobian**

A covariant of *n* forms *f*<sub>*i*</sub> in *n* variables *x*<sub>*j*</sub>, given by the determinant of the matrix with entries ∂*f*<sub>*i*</sub>/∂*x*<sub>*j*</sub>.

:::

::: {#o191 .entry cue=term name="joint invariant"}

**joint invariant**

A relative invariant for polynomials over reducible representation of a group, in particular a relative invariant for several binary forms.

:::

## K {#K}

::: {#o193 .entry cue=term name="kenotheme"}

**kenotheme**

(txt, Glossary p. 543–548) defines this as "A finite system of discrete points defined by one or more homogeneous equations in number one less than the number of variables contained therein." This may mean an intersection of *n* hypersurfaces in *n*-dimensional projective space. Archaic.

:::

## L {#L}

::: {#o196 .entry cue=term name="linear"}

**linear**

Degree 1

:::

::: {#o197 .entry cue=term name="lineo-"}

**lineo-**

Used to form compound adjectives such as lineo-linear, lineo-quadric, and so on, indicating the bidegree of something. For example, lineo-linear means having degree 1 in each of two sets of variables. In particular the lineo-linear invariant of two binary forms has degree 1 in the coefficients of each form. (Elliott 1895, p.54)

:::

::: {#o199 .entry cue=term name="Lüroth invariant"}

**Lüroth invariant**

A degree 54 invariant vanishing on [Lüroth quartics](wikipedia://en/33272014) (nonsingular quartic plane curves containing the 10 vertices of a complete pentalateral). (Dolgachev 2012, p.295)

:::

## M {#M}

::: {#o202 .entry cue=term name="meicatalecticizant"}

**meicatalecticizant**

Sylvester's original term for what he later renamed the [catalecticant](wikipedia://en/30084691). Archaic.

::: {#o301 .entry cue=term name="skew"}

**skew**

A skew invariant is a relative invariant of a group *G* that changes sign under an element of order 2 in its abelianization. In particular for the general linear group it changes sign under elements of determinant –1, and for the symmetric group it changes sign under odd permutations. For binary forms skew invariants are the invariants of odd weight. They do not exist for binary quadrics, cubics, or quartics, but do for binary quintics. (Elliott 1895, p.112)
