# Ivar Ekeland

*Image: Animated cycle.gif.* Ivar Ekeland has written popular books about [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295) and about [fractals](wikipedia://en/10913),[^o1][^o2] such as the [Julia set](wikipedia://en/56434) *(animated)*. Ekeland's exposition provided mathematical inspiration to [Michael Crichton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Crichton)'s discussion of [chaos](wikipedia://en/6295) in *[Jurassic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic_Park_%28novel%29)*.[^o10]

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Ivar Ekeland"}

**Ivar I. Ekeland** (born 2 July 1944, Paris) is a French mathematician of Norwegian descent. Ekeland has written influential monographs and textbooks on nonlinear [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), and [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255), as well as popular books on mathematics, which have been published in French, English, and other languages. Ekeland is known as the author of [Ekeland's variational principle](wikipedia://en/21341034) and for his use of the [Shapley–Folkman lemma](wikipedia://en/29237460) in [optimization theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29). He has contributed to the [periodic solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_solution) of [Hamiltonian systems](wikipedia://en/1197531) and particularly to the theory of [Kreĭn indices](wikipedia://en/1190470) for linear systems ([Floquet theory](wikipedia://en/2987843)).[^o4] Ekeland is cited in the credits of [Steven Spielberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Spielberg)'s 1993 movie [Jurassic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic_Park_%28film%29) as an inspiration of the fictional [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295) specialist [Ian Malcolm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Malcolm_%28Jurassic_Park%29) appearing in [Michael Crichton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Crichton)'s 1990 novel *[Jurassic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic_Park_%28novel%29)*.[^o10]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Ekeland studied at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) (1963–1967). He is a senior research fellow at the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) (CNRS). He obtained his doctorate in 1970. He teaches mathematics and economics at the [Paris Dauphine University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University), the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique), the [École Spéciale Militaire de Saint-Cyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Spéciale_Militaire_de_Saint-Cyr), and the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver). He was the chairman of Paris-Dauphine University from 1989 to 1994.

Ekeland is a recipient of the D'Alembert Prize and the Jean Rostand prize. He is also a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

Ekeland has contributed to [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), particularly to [variational calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_calculus) and [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033).

::: {#o14 .statement cue=named-heading name="Variational principle"}

### Variational principle {#Variational_principle}

In [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), **Ekeland's variational principle**, discovered by Ivar Ekeland,[^o15][^o16][^n1] is a theorem that asserts that there exists a nearly optimal solution to a class of [optimization problems](wikipedia://en/1126536).[^o18]

Ekeland's variational principle can be used when the lower [level set](wikipedia://en/559622) of a minimization problem is not [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactness_%28topology%29), so that the [Bolzano&ndash;Weierstrass theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano&ndash;Weierstrass_theorem) can not be applied. Ekeland's principle relies on the [completeness of the metric space](wikipedia://en/7832).[^o19]

Ekeland's principle leads to a quick proof of the [Caristi fixed point theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caristi_fixed_point_theorem).[^o19][^o20]

Ekeland was associated with the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) when he proposed this theorem.[^o15]

:::

### Variational theory of Hamiltonian systems {#Variational_theory_of_Hamiltonian_systems}

Ivar Ekeland is an expert on [variational analysis](wikipedia://en/171882), which studies [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033) of [spaces of functions](wikipedia://en/11180). His research on [periodic solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_solution) of [Hamiltonian systems](wikipedia://en/1197531) and particularly to the theory of [Kreĭn indices](wikipedia://en/1190470) for linear systems ([Floquet theory](wikipedia://en/2987843)) was described in his monograph.[^o4]

### Additive optimization problems {#Additive_optimization_problems}

*Image: Shapley–Folkman lemma.svg.* Ivar Ekeland applied the [Shapley–Folkman lemma](wikipedia://en/29237460) to explain Claude Lemarechal's success with [Lagrangian relaxation](wikipedia://en/6348084) on non-convex minimization problems. This lemma concerns the [Minkowski addition](wikipedia://en/504105) of four sets. The point (+) in the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of the Minkowski sum of the four [non-convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292) (*right*) is the sum of four points (+) from the (left-hand) sets—two points in two non-convex sets plus two points in the convex hulls of two sets. The convex hulls are shaded pink. The original sets each have exactly two points (shown in red).

Ekeland explained the success of methods of convex minimization on large problems that appeared to be non-convex. In many optimization problems, the [objective function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29#objective_function) f are *separable*, that is, the sum of *many* summand-functions each with its own argument:

$$f(x) = f(x_1,\dots,x_N) = \sum_n f_n(x_n).$$

For example, problems of [linear optimization](wikipedia://en/43730) are separable. For a separable problem, we consider an optimal solution

$$x_\min = (x_1,\dots,x_N)_\min$$

with the minimum value *f*(*x*<sub>min</sub>). For a separable problem, we consider an optimal solution (*x*<sub>min</sub>, *f*(*x*<sub>min</sub>)) to the "*convexified problem*", where convex hulls are taken of the graphs of the summand functions. Such an optimal solution is the [limit of a sequence](wikipedia://en/285773) of points in the convexified problem

$(x_j, f(x_j)) \in \mathrm{Conv}(\mathrm{Graph}(f_n)). \,$[^o25][^o23] An application of the [Shapley–Folkman lemma](wikipedia://en/29237460) represents the given optimal-point as a sum of points in the graphs of the original summands and of a small number of convexified summands.

This analysis was published by Ivar Ekeland in 1974 to explain the apparent convexity of separable problems with many summands, despite the non-convexity of the summand problems. In 1973, the young mathematician [Claude Lemaréchal](wikipedia://en/26313585) was surprised by his success with [convex minimization](wikipedia://en/1674411) [methods](wikipedia://en/15237) on problems that were known to be non-convex.[^o24][^o25][^o26] Ekeland's analysis explained the success of methods of convex minimization on *large* and *separable* problems, despite the non-convexities of the summand functions.[^o25][^o26][^o27] The Shapley–Folkman lemma has encouraged the use of methods of convex minimization on other applications with sums of many functions.[^o25][^o28][^o29][^o30]
