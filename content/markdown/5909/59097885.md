# Vera Myller

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vera Myller"}

*Image: Vera Lebedeva-Myller.jpg.* Vera Myller

**Vera Myller-Lebedev** (1 December 1880 – 12 December 1970) was a Russian-born [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who earned her doctorate in Germany with [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) and became the first female university professor in Romania.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Vera Lebedev was born in [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg) and educated in [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veliky_Novgorod). From 1897 through 1902 she participated in the [Bestuzhev Courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bestuzhev_Courses) in Saint Petersburg. She then traveled to the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where she completed a doctorate in 1906 under the supervision of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). Her dissertation was *Die Theorie der Integralgleichungen in Anwendungen auf einige Reihenentwickelungen*, and concerned [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234).

## Marriage and career {#Marriage_and_career}

In Göttingen, she met Romanian mathematician [Alexander Myller](wikipedia://en/70713199). She married him in 1907, moved with him to Romania, enrolled in the [University of Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza_University), and in 1910 joined the mathematics faculty there. In 1918 she was promoted to full professor, becoming Romania's first female professor.

She died in [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iași) in 1970, and is buried at the city's [Eternitatea Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternitatea_cemetery).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

She wrote Romanian-language textbooks on algebra (1942) and algebraic applications of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) (1945), and won the Romanian State Prize in 1953 for her algebra text.
