# Thomas Schick

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Schick"}

**Thomas Schick** (born 22 May 1969 in [Alzey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzey)) is a German mathematician, specializing in algebraic topology and differential geometry.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Schick studied mathematics and physics at the [Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Gutenberg_University_Mainz), where he received in 1994 his [Diplom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplom) in mathematics and in 1996 his PhD ([Promotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_%28Germany%29)) under the supervision of [Wolfgang Lück](wikipedia://en/26197113) with thesis *Analysis on Manifolds of Bounded Geometry, Hodge-deRham Isomorphism and $L^2$-Index Theorem*. As a [postdoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoc) he was from 1996 to 1998 at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster) and from 1998 to 2000 an assistant professor at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University), where he worked with [Nigel Higson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Higson) and [John Roe](wikipedia://en/40285963). Schick received his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 2000 from the University of Münster and is since 2001 a professor for pure mathematics at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen).

His research deals with topological invariants, *e.g.* $L^2$-invariants and those invariants which result from the [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748) of [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987). Such invariants arise in generalizations of the [Atiyah-Singer index theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiyah-Singer_index_theorem).

Schick, with Wolfgang Lück, introduced the strong Atiyah conjecture. Given a discrete group G, the Atiyah conjecture states that the $L^2$-[Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) of a finite [CW-complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex) that has fundamental group G are integers, provided that G is torsion-free; furthermore, in the general case, the $L^2$-Betti numbers are rational numbers with denominators determined by the finite subgroups of G. In 2007 Schick, with Peter Linnell, proved a theorem which established conditions under which the Atiyah conjecture for a torsion-free group G implies the Atiyah conjecture for every finite extension of G; furthermore, they proved that the conditions are satisfied for a certain class of groups.[^o5] In 2000 Schick proved the Atiyah conjecture for a large class of special cases.[^o6] In 2007 he presented a method which proved the Baum-Connes conjecture for the full braid groups, and for other classes of groups which arise as (finite) extensions for which the Baum-Connes conjecture is known to be true.[^o7][^o8]

In the 1990s there were proofs of many special cases of the Gromov-Lawson-Rosenberg conjecture concerning criteria for the existence of a metric with positive [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622); in 1997 Schick published the first counterexample.[^o9]

He was the coordinator of the Courant Research Center's *Strukturen höherer Ordnung in der Mathematik* (Structures of Higher Order in Mathematics) at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen).[^o10] A major goal of the research center was the investigation of mathematical structures that could play a role in modern theoretical physics, especially [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) and [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312).

He was the managing editor for [Mathematische Annalen](wikipedia://en/1423381). In 2014 he was an invited speaker with talk *The topology of scalar curvature* at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul). In 2016 he became a full member of the [Göttingen Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities).
