# William Mollison (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William Mollison"}

**William Loudon Mollison** (19 September 1851 – 10 March 1929)[^o3] was a Scottish mathematician and academic. From 1915 to 1929, he was [Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_%28college%29) of [Clare College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge).[^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Mollison was born on 19 September 1851 in [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen), Scotland.[^o7][^o8] He was educated at [Aberdeen Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Grammar_School), then an all-boys grammar school.[^o9] He studied mathematics and [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy) at the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen), graduating in 1872 with a [first class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_honours) degree.[^o10] That year, he was awarded the Ferguson Scholarship by Aberdeen and matriculated into [Clare College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge) to continue his mathematical studies.[^o7][^o9] He became a Foundation Scholar in 1873.[^o7] His private tutor while at Cambridge was [Edward Routh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Routh).[^o11] He graduated from the University of Cambridge in 1876 as the [Second Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Wrangler).[^o7][^o11][^o12]

## Career {#Career}

On 29 April 1876, Mollison was elected a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_%28Oxbridge%29) of [Clare College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge).[^o14] He was an examiner for the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews) between 1876 and 1880.[^o8] He was a mathematics lecturer at [Jesus College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Cambridge) from 1877 to 1882, and at Clare College from 1882.[^o9] In addition to his college teaching, he was a private tutor or "coach" in mathematics.[^o7]

Due to ill health, he moved from teaching a large number of students, privately and through his college, into administration.[^o7] He was appointed junior tutor of Clare College in 1880,[^o9] and was made its senior tutor in May 1894.[^o15] He was elected a member of the [Council of the Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Senate) of the University Of Cambridge in 1892,[^o7] and appointed Secretary of the General Board of Studies of the University in 1904: he stepped down from both these posts in 1920.[^o9] He served as *[locum tenens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locum_tenens)* for the then Master (Edward Atkinson) from 1913 to 1915.[^o9] Mollison was unanimously elected as Atkinson successor as the 38th [Master of Clare College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Clare_College,_Cambridge) in March 1915.[^o16]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Mollison was married to Ellen Mayhew.[^o9] They had one son and two daughters,[^o18][^o3] one of whom pre-deceased him.[^o3][^o9] His wife died in 1917, and he provided the endowment for the [Mayhew Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayhew_Prize), a mathematics prize awarded by the University of Cambridge, in her honour.[^o9] His son, William Mayhew Mollison, was a distinguished ear, nose and throat surgeon,[^o19] and his son [Patrick Mollison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Mollison), a noted haematologist.[^o20]

Mollison died on 10 March 1929 in London, England; he was aged 77.[^o10][^o9] His funeral was held at the chapel of Clare College, Cambridge,[^o7] and he was buried in the [Ascension Parish Burial Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Parish_Burial_Ground) alongside his wife.[^o7]
