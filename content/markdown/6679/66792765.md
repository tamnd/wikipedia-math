# Emily Coddington Williams

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Emily Coddington Williams"}

**Emily Coddington Williams** (born **Emily Matilda Coddington**, October 21, 1873 – August 8, 1952) was an American [historian of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), translator, novelist, playwright, and biographer.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Coddington was born on October 21, 1873, in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City); her parents were of well-off colonial stock. Her father, a lawyer, died in 1876, and she came to live in [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan) with her mother and grandmother. She passed the entrance examination for [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1891, allowing her to study at the Harvard Annex, a precursor to [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College). Instead, she went to the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) from 1894 to 1896, and earned a bachelor's degree there. In 1898, [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) awarded her a master's degree in mathematics, minoring in mechanics and Greek, based on a thesis she wrote at the end of her studies in London concerning the history of [determinants](wikipedia://en/8468).

She completed a Ph.D. at Columbia in 1905. Her dissertation, *A Brief Account of the Historical Development of Pseudospherical Surfaces from 1827 to 1887*, concerned the history of [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610) and the [pseudosphere](wikipedia://en/60766),b also including material on a paper of [Albert Victor Bäcklund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Victor_Bäcklund) on [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), which she translated from Swedish into English.a

Beginning in 1909 Coddington studied law at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), gaining admission to the bar in 1912 and earning a law degree in 1913. Although Coddington did not become a professional mathematician or lawyer, she remained a lifelong member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), and attended the 1908 and 1912 [International Congresses of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in England and Italy, respectively.

## Later life {#Later_life}

Coddington married businessman William Henry Williams in 1917, and her subsequent publications were under the name Emily Coddington Williams. She participated in several clubs concerning gardening and [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage), and traveled frequently to Europe. With her husband, she continued to live in New York, summering in [Newport, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Rhode_Island). They were childless, although her husband had adopted Irma Williams, his niece, after Irma's mother died in childbirth in 1901.

Williams was the author of two one-act plays, *Then* (1914)c and *Pals* (1925),d two novels, *Homing Pigeon* (1927)e and *Quest for Love* (1929)f and a 1941 biography of [William Coddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Coddington) (1601–1678), governor of Rhode Island.g

Her husband died in 1943. In 1952, Williams set sail for Europe on the [RMS *Queen Mary*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Queen_Mary), but she broke her hip while on board, and died in a hospital in Paris on August 8, 1952, leaving a large legacy to her godchildren and the societies she belonged to. Her Newport estate, Villa Rosa, was sold to a real estate developer who demolished it and replaced it with a condominium complex.
