# Nadirashvili surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nadirashvili surface"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **Nadirashvili surface** is an [immersed](wikipedia://en/6993953) complete bounded [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734) in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with negative [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623). The first example of such a surface was constructed by Nikolai Nadirashvili in 1996. This simultaneously answered a question of [Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675) about whether there was an immersed complete bounded surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with negative Gaussian curvature, and a question of [Eugenio Calabi](wikipedia://en/621000) and [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137) about whether there was an immersed complete bounded minimal surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$.[^o8]

:::

(Hilbert 1901) showed that a complete immersed surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ cannot have constant negative Gaussian curvature,[^o7] and (Efimov 1963) show that the curvature cannot be bounded above by a negative constant.[^o6] Therefore, Nadirashvili's surface necessarily has points where the Gaussian curvature is arbitrarily close to 0. As a minimal surface, its [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210) is 0 everywhere. Topologically, it is a disk. As an immersed surface, it intersects itself; it is not embedded. These self-intersections are necessary, as Colding and Minicozzi proved in 2008 that embedded complete bounded minimal disks do not exist.[^o5]
