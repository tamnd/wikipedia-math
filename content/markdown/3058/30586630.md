# Edwin Battistella

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Edwin Battistella"}

**Edwin Battistella** (born 1955) is an American linguist known for work on [markedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markedness), [syntax](wikipedia://en/26860), and [language attitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_attitudes). He is an emeritus professor of Humanities and Culture at [Southern Oregon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Oregon_University) in [Ashland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashland,_Oregon).

:::

## Background {#Background}

Battistella studied Slavic languages and literatures at Rutgers College, completing a BA in 1976, and [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) at the [City University of New York Graduate School and University Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUNY_Graduate_Center), receiving a Ph.D. in 1981. His dissertation, *Topics in the Theory of Inflection*, written under the direction of Robert Fiengo, developed the early theory of abstract case introduced in [Noam Chomsky’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Chomsky) 1980 article "On Case Theory".[^o4] The dissertation proposed that many instances of grammatical agreement could be treated as the assignment of abstract case features from clauses to the categories they contained, making a distinction between inherent and assigned case features.

## Scholarly contributions {#Scholarly_contributions}

Battistella is the author of two books on the theory of [markedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markedness). *Markedness: The Evaluative Superstructure of Language*, was a study of the Prague School principle of structural asymmetry as developed by Nikolai Trubetzkoy and [Roman Jakobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Jakobson).[^o6] In *Markedness*, Battistella identified the diagnostics of marked and unmarked opposites in Jakobson’s work from the 1920s through the 1980s, proposed a comprehensive set of markedness values for English grammar and connected markedness to the idea of intrinsic hierarchy. In *The Logic of Markedness*, Battistella more explicitly developed the connections between Jakobson’s ideas and those of Noam Chomsky and Morris Halle, attempting to reconcile structuralist and generative views of markedness.[^o7]

Two later books have focused on 19th and 20th century language attitudes. *Bad Language: Are Some Words Better Than Others?* published in 2005, develops the case for a linguistically informed view of usage rather one based on oversimplifications and folklore.[^o8] *Do You Make These Mistakes in English? The Story of Sherwin Cody’s Famous Language School*, published in 2009, combined a biography of correspondence school entrepreneur [Sherwin Cody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwin_Cody) with an exploration of language and cultural attitudes that made self-improvement and correspondence learning educational forces in the first half of the twentieth century.[^o9]

A fifth book, his 2014 *Sorry About That: The Language of Public Apology*, analyzes the public apologies of politicians, entertainers, businessmen, and others, with the goal of showing how certain language creates sincere or insincere apologies.[^o10][^o11] The work connects actual apologies with the broader social, ethical, and linguistic principles behind them. His 2020 book, *Dangerous Crooked Scoundrels: Insulting the President, from Washington to Trump*, analyzes and categorizes over 500 political insults aimed at the forty-five U.S. presidents and how they responded.[^o12][^o13] The work puts political insults in historical context, emphasizes the importance of the First Amendment, and shows contemporary American politics to be part of a broader narrative.

In addition, Professor Battistella is the author of a number of articles and book chapters on pronoun reference, the syntax of the English verb system, [traditional grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_grammar) and style.

## Professional activities {#Professional_activities}

Battistella has held teaching, administrative and research positions at the [University of Alabama in Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alabama_in_Birmingham), the 1986 Linguistic Society of America Summer Institute, the IBM [Thomas J. Watson Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_J._Watson_Research_Center), and [Wayne State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_State_College) in Nebraska. He has been at Southern Oregon University since 2000, serving as Dean of Arts and Letters from 2000 to 2006 and as Interim Provost from 2007 to 2008.[^o15]

From 1995 to 2001, Battistella served as the first book review editor for [*Language*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_%28journal%29), the journal of the [Linguistic Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_Society_of_America).[^o16] In 2014, he began a three-year term on the Society's Executive Committee.

In 2006 he became the editor of Wiley-Blackwell’s *Linguistic Abstracts*, succeeding D. Terence Langendoen, and he and [Rochelle Lieber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochelle_Lieber) became the founding editors-in-chief of Wiley-Blackwell's [peer-reviewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review) online journal *[Language and Linguistics Compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_and_Linguistics_Compass)*.[^o17]

In 1986, he served as an expert witness for the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union), in a Federal Court case in which a trucker challenged an Alabama state statute prohibiting the public display of "any bumper sticker, sign or writing which depicts obscene language descriptive of sexual or excretory activities".[^o18] From 2009 to 2015 Battistella served on the board of directors of Oregon Humanities, a state affiliate of the [National Endowment for the Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Endowment_for_the_Humanities).[^o19] He is also a member of the editorial board of [The Oregon Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oregon_Encyclopedia), a peer-reviewed online state-encyclopedia established during Oregon’s sesquicentennial.[^o20] He has contributed entries on [Damon Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Knight), [Kim Novak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Novak), [Les Schwab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Schwab), [Lenn Hannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenn_Hannon), and Albert Kitzhaber, among others.
