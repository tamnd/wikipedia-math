# Essential dimension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Essential dimension"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **essential dimension** is an [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) defined for certain [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364) such as [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) and [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478). It was introduced by [Joe Buhler](wikipedia://en/58419310) and [Zinovy Reichstein](wikipedia://en/31869776)[^o2] and in its full generality defined by [Alexander Merkurjev](wikipedia://en/31902178).[^o3]

:::

Basically, essential dimension measures the complexity of algebraic structures via their [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) of definition. For example, if $K$ is a field and $V$ a $K$-vector space, a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) $q:V\to K$ is said to be defined over a [subfield](wikipedia://en/11634) $L$ of $K$ if there exists a $K$-[basis](wikipedia://en/18420) $e_1,\dots,e_n$ of $V$ such that $q$ can be expressed in the form

$$q\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i e_i\bigg) = \sum a_{ij} x_ix_j$$

with all coefficients $a_{ij}$ belonging to $L$. If $K$ has [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) different from 2, every quadratic form is [diagonalizable](wikipedia://en/251478#Equivalence_of_Quadratic_Forms). Therefore, $q$ has a field of definition generated by $n$ elements. Technically, one always works over a (fixed) base field $k$ and the fields $K$ and $L$ in consideration are supposed to contain $k$. The essential dimension of $q$ is then defined as the least [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree) over $k$ of a subfield $L$ of $K$ over which $q$ is defined.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Fix an arbitrary field $k$ and let $\operatorname{Fields}$$/k$ denote the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of finitely generated [field extensions](wikipedia://en/11634) of $k$ with inclusions as [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891). Consider a (covariant) [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $F:$ $\operatorname{Fields}$$/k\to$ $\operatorname{Set}$. For a field extension $K/k$ and an element $a$ of $F(K/k)$ a *field of definition of* $a$ is an [intermediate field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate_field) $K/L/k$ such that $a$ is contained in the image of the map $F(L/k)\to F(K/k)$ induced by the inclusion of $L$ in $K$.

The *essential dimension of* $a$, denoted by $\operatorname{ed}(a)$, is the least transcendence degree (over $k$) of a field of definition for $a$. The **essential dimension of the functor *F***, denoted by $\operatorname{ed}(F)$, is the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) of $\operatorname{ed}(a)$ taken over all elements $a$ of $F(K/k)$ and objects $K/k$ of $\operatorname{Fields}$$/k$.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

- Essential dimension of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478): For a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $n$ consider the functor $Q_n:$ $\operatorname{Fields}$$/k\to$ $\operatorname{Set}$ taking a field extension $K/k$ to the set of [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of non-degenerate $n$-dimensional quadratic forms over $K$ and taking a morphism $L/k\to K/k$ (given by the inclusion of $L$ in $K$) to the map sending the isomorphism class of a quadratic form $q:V\to L$ to the isomorphism class of the quadratic form $q_K : V \otimes_L K \to K$.
- Essential dimension of [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924): for an algebraic group $G$ over $k$ denote by $H^1(-,G):$ $\operatorname{Fields}$$/k\to$ $\operatorname{Set}$ the functor taking a field extension $K/k$ to the set of isomorphism classes of $G$-[torsors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsor) over $K$ (in the [fppf](wikipedia://en/10538912)-topology). The essential dimension of this functor is called the *essential dimension of the algebraic group* $G$, denoted by $\operatorname{ed}(G)$.
- Essential dimension of a [fibered category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibered_category): let $\mathcal{F}$ be a category fibered over the category $\operatorname{Aff}/k$ of affine $k$-schemes, given by a functor $p : \mathcal{F} \to \operatorname{Aff}/k$. For example, $\mathcal{F}$ may be the [moduli stack](wikipedia://en/3010057) $\mathcal{M}_g$ of genus $g$ curves or the classifying stack $\mathcal{BG}$ of an algebraic group. Assume that for each $A\in\operatorname{Aff}/k$ the isomorphism classes of objects in the fiber $p^{-1}(A)$ form a set. Then we get a functor $F_p:$ $\operatorname{Fields}$$/k\to$ $\operatorname{Set}$ taking a field extension $K/k$ to the set of isomorphism classes in the fiber $p^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}(K))$. The essential dimension of the fibered category $\mathcal{F}$ is defined as the essential dimension of the corresponding functor $F_p$. In the case of the classifying stack $\mathcal{F} = \mathcal{BG}$ of an algebraic group $G$, the value coincides with the previously defined essential dimension of $G$.

:::

## Known results {#Known_results}

- The essential dimension of a [linear algebraic group](wikipedia://en/395885) $G$ is always finite and bounded by the minimal dimension of a generically free [representation](wikipedia://en/6355948) minus the dimension of $G$.
- For $G$ a [Spin group](wikipedia://en/411231) over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) $k$, the essential dimension is listed in .
- The essential dimension of a finite algebraic [$p$-group](wikipedia://en/24664) over $k$ equals the minimal dimension of a [faithful representation](wikipedia://en/2617872), provided that the base field $k$ contains a primitive $p$-th [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950).
- The essential dimension of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_n$ (viewed as an algebraic group over $k$) is known for $n\leq 5$ (for every base field $k$), for $n=6$ (for $k$ of characteristic not 2) and for $n=7$ (in characteristic 0).
- Let $T$ be an [algebraic torus](wikipedia://en/504697) admitting a [Galois splitting field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_field) $L/k$ of degree a power of a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$. Then the essential dimension of $T$ equals the least rank of the kernel of a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) of $\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)$-[lattices](wikipedia://en/469246) $P\to X(T)$ with [cokernel](wikipedia://en/279715) finite and of order [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) to $p$, where $P$ is a permutation lattice.
