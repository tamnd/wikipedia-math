# Krieger–Nelson Prize

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Krieger–Nelson Prize"}

The **Krieger–Nelson Prize** is presented by the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) in recognition of an outstanding woman in mathematics. It was first awarded in 1995. The award is named after [Cecilia Krieger](wikipedia://en/11581652) and [Evelyn Nelson](wikipedia://en/11664159), both known for their contributions to [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Recipients {#Recipients}

While the award has largely been awarded to a female mathematician working at a [Canadian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Canada), it has also been awarded to [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada)-born or -educated women working outside of the country. For example, [Cathleen Morawetz](wikipedia://en/1254108), past president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), and a faculty member at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) (a division of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University)) was awarded the Krieger–Nelson Prize in 1997. (Morawetz was educated at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada)). According to the call for applications, the award winner should be a "member of the Canadian mathematical community".[^o5]

The recipient of the Krieger–Nelson Prize delivers a lecture to the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055), typically during its summer meeting.[^o5]
