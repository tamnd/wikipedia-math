# Federico Amodeo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Federico Amodeo"}

**Federico Amodeo** (8 October 1859, [Avellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avellino) – 3 November 1946, [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples)) was an Italian mathematician, specializing in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), and a historian of mathematics.

:::

He received in 1883 his Ph.D. (*laurea*) in mathematics from the [University of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II), where he became an instructor (*libero docente*) and from 1885 to 1923 taught projective geometry.[^o2] He also taught as a professor in Naples at the Istituto Tecnico "Gianbattista Della Porta" from 1890 to 1923, when he retired.[^o2] In 1890–1891 he visited the geometers at the [University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin).[^o3]

As a historian, he specialised in the history of mathematics in Naples before 1860, which he explicated in a two-volume work entitled *Vita matematica napoletana*; volume I (1905), volume II (1924). At the University of Naples from 1905 to 1922, he taught a course on the history of mathematics.[^o2]

Amodeo was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in 1900 at Paris[^o4] and again in 1908 in Rome. He was elected a member of the [Accademia Pontaniana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Pontaniana).
