# Anna C. Gilbert

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Anna C. Gilbert"}

**Anna Catherine Gilbert** (born 1972)[^o3] is an American mathematician who works as the John C. Malone Professor of Statistics & Data Science, Applied Mathematics, and Electrical Engineering at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University).[^o4] She was previously a professor at the University of Michigan. Her research expertise is in [randomized algorithms](wikipedia://en/495383) for [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_processing), [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), and large data sets.[^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Gilbert earned a bachelor's degree from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1993, and completed her Ph.D. in 1997 from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) under the supervision of [Ingrid Daubechies](wikipedia://en/1430989).[^o5] After postdoctoral research at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), she joined [AT&T Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T_Labs), and continued there as a staff member until 2004, when she moved to Michigan.[^o5]

## Research {#Research}

Gilbert's research discoveries have included the existence of [multifractal](wikipedia://en/6498864) behavior in [TCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_Control_Protocol)-based internet traffic,[^o9] the development of [streaming algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_algorithm) based on random projections for aggregating information from large data streams using very small amounts of working memory,[^o10] and a foundational analysis of the ability of orthogonal [matching pursuit](wikipedia://en/5534001) to recover sparse signals with her student [Joel Tropp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Tropp).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

She became a [Sloan Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) in 2006.[^o5] In addition to that, she won the NSF (National Science Foundation) Career award in 2006 (12). In 2008, she was awarded the [NAS Award for Initiatives in Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Award_for_Initiatives_in_Research) and the [Association for Computing Machinery Douglas Engelbart Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery_Douglas_Engelbart_Award).[^o12] She also won the Signal Processing Best Paper award alongside Martin Strauss and Joel Tropp in 2010 from the European Association for Signal Processing.[^o13] In 2013 she won the Ralph E. Kleinman Prize of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) "for her creative and deep contributions to the mathematics of signal processing, data analysis and communications".[^o14] She was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2014, speaking on "Mathematics in Science and Technology".[^o15] She gave the von Neumann Lecture at the 2022 [Joint Mathematics Meeting](wikipedia://en/14985143), titled "Metric representations: Algorithms and Geometry."[^o16]
