# Parafactorial local ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Parafactorial local ring"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) *R* is called **parafactorial** if it has [depth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_of_a_local_ring) at least 2 and the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) Pic(Spec(*R*) − *m*) of its [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_%28ring_theory%29) with the [closed point](wikipedia://en/33086408) *m* removed is trivial.

:::

More generally, a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X* is called **parafactorial** along a closed subset *Z* if the subset *Z* is "too small" for [invertible sheaves](wikipedia://en/581859) to detect; more precisely if for every open set *V* the map from *P*(*V*) to *P*(*V* ∩ *U*) is an [equivalence](wikipedia://en/379051) of [categories](wikipedia://en/53759), where *U* = *X* – *Z* and *P*(*V*) is the category of invertible sheaves on *V*. A Noetherian local ring is parafactorial if and only if its spectrum is parafactorial along its closed point.

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every Noetherian local ring of dimension at least 2 that is factorial is parafactorial. However local rings of dimension at most 1 are not parafactorial, even if they are factorial.
- Every Noetherian [complete intersection local ring](wikipedia://en/3116851) of dimension at least 4 is parafactorial.
- For a [locally Noetherian scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_Noetherian_scheme), a closed subset is parafactorial if the local ring at every point of the subset is parafactorial. For a locally Noetherian [regular scheme](wikipedia://en/43016199), the closed parafactorial subsets are those of codimension at least 2.
