::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasiregular polyhedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **quasiregular polyhedron** is a [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223) that has exactly two kinds of [regular faces](wikipedia://en/333306), which alternate around each [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417). They are [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) and [edge-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-transitive), hence a step closer to [regular polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedra) than the [semiregular](wikipedia://en/1548894), which are merely vertex-transitive.

:::

Their dual figures are [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive) and edge-transitive; they have exactly two kinds of regular [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719), which alternate around each [face](wikipedia://en/149926). They are sometimes also considered quasiregular.

There are only two [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885) quasiregular polyhedra: the [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) and the [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023). Their names, given by [Kepler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler), come from recognizing that their faces are all the faces (turned differently) of the [dual](wikipedia://en/8815)-pair [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) and [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), in the first case, and of the dual-pair [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) and [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407), in the second case.

These forms representing a pair of a regular figure and its dual can be given a vertical [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\begin{Bmatrix} p \\ q \end{Bmatrix}$ or ***r{p,q}***, to represent that their faces are all the faces (turned differently) of both the regular ***{p,q}*** and the dual regular ***{q,p}***. A quasiregular polyhedron with this symbol will have a [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) ***p.q.p.q*** (or ***(p.q)<sup>2</sup>***).

More generally, a quasiregular figure can have a [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) ***(p.q)<sup>r</sup>***, representing ***r*** (2 or more) sequences of the faces around the vertex.

[Tilings](wikipedia://en/321671) of the plane can also be quasiregular, specifically the [trihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864397), with vertex configuration (3.6)<sup>2</sup>. [Other quasiregular tilings](wikipedia://en/18640974) exist on the hyperbolic plane, like the [triheptagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/8834492), (3.7)<sup>2</sup>. Or more generally: ***(p.q)<sup>2</sup>***, with ***1/p + 1/q \< 1/2***.

Regular polyhedra and tilings with an even number of faces at each vertex can also be considered quasiregular by differentiating between faces of the same order, by representing them differently, like coloring them alternately (without defining any surface orientation). A regular figure with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) ***{p,q}*** can be considered quasiregular, with vertex configuration ***(p.p)<sup>q/2</sup>***, if ***q*** is even.

Examples:

The regular [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), with Schläfli symbol {3,4} and 4 being even, can be considered quasiregular as a *tetratetrahedron* (2 sets of 4 triangles of the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606)), with vertex configuration (3.3)<sup>4/2</sup> = (3<sub>a</sub>.3<sub>b</sub>)<sup>2</sup>, alternating two colors of triangular faces.

The [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809), with vertex configuration 4<sup>4</sup> and 4 being even, can be considered quasiregular, with vertex configuration (4.4)<sup>4/2</sup> = (4<sub>a</sub>.4<sub>b</sub>)<sup>2</sup>, colored as a *checkerboard*.

The [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858), with vertex configuration 3<sup>6</sup> and 6 being even, can be considered quasiregular, with vertex configuration (3.3)<sup>6/2</sup> = (3<sub>a</sub>.3<sub>b</sub>)<sup>3</sup>, alternating two colors of triangular faces.

## The convex quasiregular polyhedra {#The_convex_quasiregular_polyhedra}

There are two uniform [convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) quasiregular polyhedra:

1. The [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) $\begin{Bmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{Bmatrix}$, vertex configuration **(3.4)<sup>2</sup>**, [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram)
1. The [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023) $\begin{Bmatrix} 3 \\ 5 \end{Bmatrix}$, vertex configuration **(3.5)<sup>2</sup>**, *Coxeter-Dynkin diagram*

In addition, the [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), which is also [regular](wikipedia://en/333299), $\begin{Bmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \end{Bmatrix}$, vertex configuration **(3.3)<sup>2</sup>**, can be considered quasiregular if alternate faces are given different colors. In this form it is sometimes known as the *tetratetrahedron*. The remaining convex regular polyhedra have an odd number of faces at each vertex so cannot be colored in a way that preserves edge transitivity. It has *Coxeter-Dynkin diagram*

Each of these forms the common core of a [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) pair of [regular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/333299). The names of two of these give clues to the associated dual pair: respectively [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) $\cap$ [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), and [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) $\cap$ [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407). The [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) is the common core of a dual pair of [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) (a compound known as the [stella octangula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula)); when derived in this way, the [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) is sometimes called the *tetratetrahedron*, as [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) $\cap$ [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606).

## Quasiregular duals {#Quasiregular_duals}

Some authorities argue that, since the duals of the quasiregular solids share the same symmetries, these duals should be called quasiregular too. But not everybody uses this terminology. These duals are transitive on their edges and faces (but not on their vertices); they are the edge-transitive [Catalan solids](wikipedia://en/564393). The convex ones are, in corresponding order as above:

1. The [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), with two *types* of alternating vertices, 8 with three rhombic faces, and 6 with four rhombic faces.
1. The [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025), with two *types* of alternating vertices, 20 with three rhombic faces, and 12 with five rhombic faces.

In addition, by duality with the octahedron, the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), which is usually [regular](wikipedia://en/333299), can be made quasiregular if alternate vertices are given different colors.

Their [face configurations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration) are of the form V3.n.3.n, and [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram)

## Quasiregular polytopes and honeycombs {#Quasiregular_polytopes_and_honeycombs}

In higher dimensions, Coxeter defined a quasiregular polytope or honeycomb to have regular facets and quasiregular vertex figures. It follows that all vertex figures are congruent and that there are two kinds of facets, which alternate.[^o31]

In Euclidean 4-space, the regular [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) can also be seen as quasiregular as an alternated [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112), h{4,3,3}, [Coxeter diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram): = , composed of alternating [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) and [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29). Its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is the quasiregular [tetratetrahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetratetrahedron) (an octahedron with tetrahedral symmetry), .

The only quasiregular honeycomb in Euclidean 3-space is the [alternated cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternated_cubic_honeycomb), h{4,3,4}, Coxeter diagrams: = , composed of alternating tetrahedral and [octahedral](wikipedia://en/80177635) [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29). Its vertex figure is the quasiregular [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280), .[^o31]

In hyperbolic 3-space, one quasiregular honeycomb is the [alternated order-5 cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternated_order-5_cubic_honeycomb), h{4,3,5}, Coxeter diagrams: = , composed of alternating tetrahedral and [icosahedral](wikipedia://en/43856084) [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29). Its vertex figure is the quasiregular [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023), .[^o32] A related paracompact [alternated order-6 cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternated_order-6_cubic_honeycomb), h{4,3,6} has alternating tetrahedral and hexagonal tiling cells with vertex figure is a quasiregular [trihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864397), .

Regular polychora or honeycombs of the form {p,3,4} or can have their symmetry cut in half as into quasiregular form , creating alternately colored {p,3} cells. These cases include the Euclidean [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318) {4,3,4} with [cubic](wikipedia://en/6285) cells, and compact hyperbolic {5,3,4} with [dodecahedral](wikipedia://en/8407) cells, and paracompact {6,3,4} with infinite [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719) cells. They have four cells around each edge, alternating in 2 colors. Their [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719) are quasiregular tetratetrahedra, = .

*Image: Uniform polyhedron-33-t1.svg.* Common vertex figure is the quasiregular tetratetrahedron, , same as regular [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635)

Similarly regular hyperbolic honeycombs of the form {p,3,6} or can have their symmetry cut in half as into quasiregular form , creating alternately colored {p,3} cells. They have six cells around each edge, alternating in 2 colors. Their [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719) are quasiregular [triangular tilings](wikipedia://en/2863858), .
