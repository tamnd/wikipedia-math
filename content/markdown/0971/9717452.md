# Viacheslav Belavkin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Viacheslav Belavkin"}

**Viacheslav Pavlovich Belavkin** (Вячеслав Павлович Белавкин; 20 May 1946 – 27 November 2012) was a Russian-British [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham). An active researcher, he was one of the pioneers of [quantum probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_probability). His research spanned areas such as [quantum filtering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_filtering_theory), [quantum information](wikipedia://en/25274) and [quantum chaos](wikipedia://en/313779).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv), and graduated from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) in 1970 where his teachers include [Evgeny Lifshitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Lifshitz), [Victor Pavlovich Maslov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Pavlovich_Maslov), [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161) and [Ruslan L. Stratonovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruslan_L._Stratonovich). In the 1980s Belavkin held visiting professorship in the [Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies), and the [Volterra Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volterra_Centre) in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) before taking up an appointment at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham) in 1992. He was promoted to a Chair in Mathematical Physics in 1996.[^o4] He and [Ruslan L. Stratonovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruslan_L._Stratonovich) were awarded the [State Prize of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Prize_of_the_Russian_Federation) (formerly the [Lenin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin_Prize)) for outstanding achievements in science and technology, in part due to his work on the [measurement problem](wikipedia://en/773292).[^o5][^o6] He is survived by his wife Nadezda Belavkin and son [Roman Belavkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_X) (also known as Solar X).[^o7]
