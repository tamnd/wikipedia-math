# Bing metrization theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bing metrization theorem"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), the **Bing metrization theorem**, named after [R. H. Bing](wikipedia://en/967353), characterizes when a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrizable).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Formal statement"}

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

The theorem states that a topological space $X$ is metrizable [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is [regular](wikipedia://en/53058) and **[T<sub>0</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov_axiom)** and has a σ-discrete [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29). A family of sets is called σ-discrete when it is a [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of [countably many](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_many) discrete collections, where a family $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of a space $X$ is called discrete, when every point of $X$ has a [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28mathematics%29) that intersects at most one member of $\mathcal{F}.$

:::

## History {#History}

The theorem was [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) by [Bing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RH_Bing) in 1951 and was an independent discovery with the [Nagata–Smirnov metrization theorem](wikipedia://en/1236569) that was proved independently by both [Nagata](wikipedia://en/36481031) (1950) and Smirnov (1951). Both theorems are often merged in the Bing-Nagata-Smirnov metrization theorem. It is a common tool to prove other [metrization theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrization_theorem), e.g. the Moore metrization theorem – a [collectionwise normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectionwise_normal), [Moore space](wikipedia://en/8016525) is metrizable – is a direct consequence.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Comparison with other metrization theorems"}

## Comparison with other metrization theorems {#Comparison_with_other_metrization_theorems}

Unlike the Urysohn's [metrization theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrization_theorem) which provides a sufficient condition for metrization, this theorem provides both a necessary and sufficient condition for a space to be metrizable. Another theorem that provides both a necessary and sufficient condition for a space to be metrizable is [Nagata–Smirnov metrization theorem](wikipedia://en/1236569).

:::
