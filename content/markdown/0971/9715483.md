# Collectionwise normal space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Collectionwise normal space"}

In mathematics, a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is called **collectionwise normal** if for every discrete family *F*<sub>*i*</sub> (*i* ∈ *I*) of [closed subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_subset) of $X$ there exists a [pairwise disjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pairwise_disjoint) family of open sets *U*<sub>*i*</sub> (*i* ∈ *I*), such that *F*<sub>*i*</sub> ⊆ *U*<sub>*i*</sub>. Here a family $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $X$ is called *discrete* when every point of $X$ has a [neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) that intersects at most one of the sets from $\mathcal{F}$. An equivalent definition[^o2] of collectionwise normal demands that the above *U*<sub>*i*</sub> (*i* ∈ *I*) themselves form a discrete family, which is *a priori* stronger than pairwise disjoint.

:::

Some authors assume that $X$ is also a [T<sub>1</sub> space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T₁_space) as part of the definition, but no such assumption is made here.

The property is intermediate in strength between [paracompactness](wikipedia://en/48631) and [normality](wikipedia://en/48629), and occurs in [metrization theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrization_theorem).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A collectionwise normal space is [collectionwise Hausdorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectionwise_Hausdorff).
- A collectionwise normal space is [normal](wikipedia://en/48629).
- A [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [paracompact space](wikipedia://en/48631) is collectionwise normal.[^n1] In particular, every [metrizable space](wikipedia://en/19738) is collectionwise normal. Note: The Hausdorff condition is necessary here, since for example an infinite set with the [cofinite topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofinite_topology) is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), hence paracompact, and T<sub>1</sub>, but is not even normal.
- Every normal [countably compact space](wikipedia://en/8293816) (hence every normal compact space) is collectionwise normal. *Proof*: Use the fact that in a countably compact space any discrete family of nonempty subsets is finite.
- An [F<sub>σ</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-sigma)-set in a collectionwise normal space is also collectionwise normal in the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926). In particular, this holds for closed subsets.
- The *Moore metrization theorem* states that a collectionwise normal [Moore space](wikipedia://en/8016525) is [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrizable).

:::

## Hereditarily collectionwise normal space {#Hereditarily_collectionwise_normal_space}

A topological space *X* is called **hereditarily collectionwise normal** if every subspace of *X* with the subspace topology is collectionwise normal.

In the same way that [hereditarily normal spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditarily_normal_space) can be characterized in terms of [separated sets](wikipedia://en/148911), there is an equivalent characterization for hereditarily collectionwise normal spaces. A family $F_i (i \in I)$ of subsets of *X* is called a **separated family** if for every *i*, we have $F_i \cap \operatorname{cl}(\bigcup_{j \ne i}F_j) = \emptyset$, with cl denoting the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) operator in *X*, in other words if the family of $F_i$ is discrete in its union. The following conditions are equivalent:[^n2]

1. *X* is hereditarily collectionwise normal.
1. Every open subspace of *X* is collectionwise normal.
1. For every separated family $F_i$ of subsets of *X*, there exists a pairwise disjoint family of open sets $U_i (i \in I)$, such that $F_i \subseteq U_i$.

### Examples of hereditarily collectionwise normal spaces {#Examples_of_hereditarily_collectionwise_normal_spaces}

- Every [linearly ordered topological space](wikipedia://en/213650) (LOTS)[^o10][^o11]
- Every [generalized ordered space](wikipedia://en/213650) (GO-space)
- Every [metrizable space](wikipedia://en/19738). This follows from the fact that metrizable spaces are collectionwise normal and being metrizable is a hereditary property.
- Every [monotonically normal space](wikipedia://en/11291250)[^o12]
