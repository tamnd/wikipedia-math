# John Speidell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Speidell"}

**John Speidell** ([fl.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floruit) 1600–1634) was an English mathematician. He is known for his early work on the calculation of logarithms.

:::

Speidell was a mathematics teacher in London[^o2][^o3] and one of the early followers of the work [John Napier](wikipedia://en/15993) had previously done on [natural logarithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_logarithms).[^o4] In 1619 Speidell published a table entitled "New Logarithmes" in which he calculated the natural logarithms of sines, tangents, and secants.[^o5][^o6]

He then diverged from Napier's methods in order to ensure all of the logarithms were positive.[^o7] A new edition of "New Logarithmes" was published in 1622 and contained an appendix with the natural logarithms of all numbers 1-1000.[^o8]

Along with [William Oughtred](wikipedia://en/475249) and [Richard Norwood](wikipedia://en/13872613), Speidell helped push toward the abbreviations of [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367).[^o8]

Speidel published a number of work about mathematics, including *An Arithmeticall Extraction* in 1628.[^o9] His son, [Euclid Speidell](wikipedia://en/63364427), also published mathematics texts.[^o10]
