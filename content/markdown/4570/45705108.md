# Goodwin–Staton integral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Goodwin–Staton integral"}

In mathematics, the **Goodwin–Staton integral** is the [special function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function) [^o2]

:::

$$G(z)=\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-t^2}}{t+z}\,dt,\qquad |\arg z|<\pi.$$

The restriction on the argument excludes the non-positive real axis, where the denominator can vanish along the path of integration. The integral is named after E. T. Goodwin and J. Staton, who published a numerical table of the corresponding real integral in 1948.[^o3]

## Relation to other special functions {#Relation_to_other_special_functions}

For positive real $x$, the Goodwin–Staton integral is related to [Dawson's integral](wikipedia://en/406903) $F$ and the [exponential integral](wikipedia://en/648614) by[^o5]

$$G(x)=\sqrt{\pi}\,F(x)-\frac12 e^{-x^2}\mathrm{Ei}(x^2),\qquad x>0.$$

## Differential equations {#Differential_equations}

Differentiating the defining integral and integrating by parts gives the first-order inhomogeneous differential equation

$$G'(z)+2zG(z)=\sqrt{\pi}-\frac1z.$$

Differentiating this relation twice and eliminating the inhomogeneous term gives the third-order linear homogeneous equation

$$zG'''(z)+(2+2z^2)G''(z)+8zG'(z)+4G(z)=0.$$

## Properties on the positive real axis {#Properties_on_the_positive_real_axis}

For $x>0$, differentiation under the integral sign gives

$$G^{(n)}(x)=(-1)^n n!\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-t^2}}{(t+x)^{n+1}}\,dt.$$

Hence

$$(-1)^nG^{(n)}(x)>0,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots,$$

so $G$ is a [completely monotonic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_monotonic_function) on $(0,\infty)$. In particular, $G$ is positive, strictly decreasing, and convex there.

## Series near the origin {#Series_near_the_origin}

D. S. Jones studied the generalized Goodwin–Staton integral and obtained convergent series representations.[^o9] Specializing Jones's equation (2.11) gives, for $|\arg z|<\pi$,

$$G(z)=e^{-z^2}\left(-\log z-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac12\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2m}}{m!\,m}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{2m+1}}{m!\left(m+\frac12\right)}\right),$$

where $\log z$ is the principal logarithm and $\gamma$ is the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant).

Consequently, as $z\to0$ within the cut plane,

$$G(z)=-\log z-\frac{\gamma}{2}+\sqrt{\pi}\,z+\left(\log z+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac12\right)z^2-\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}}{3}z^3+\left(\frac38-\frac{\gamma}{4}-\frac12\log z\right)z^4+O(z^5).$$

In particular,

$$G(x)\sim-\log x-\frac{\gamma}{2}\qquad (x\to0^+).$$

## Asymptotic expansion {#Asymptotic_expansion}

For large positive $x$, the integral has the asymptotic expansion[^o9]

$$G(x)\sim\frac12\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^n\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)x^{-n-1}\qquad (x\to+\infty).$$

Thus its first terms are

$$G(x)\sim\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2x}-\frac{1}{2x^2}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4x^3}-\frac{1}{2x^4}+\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}}{8x^5}-\frac{1}{x^6}+\cdots.$$

The [NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIST_Digital_Library_of_Mathematical_Functions) refers to Olver for corresponding expansions with bounds on the remainder for both real and complex values of the argument.[^o11]

## Generalized Goodwin–Staton integral {#Generalized_Goodwin–Staton_integral}

Jones considered the generalized integral[^o9]

$$I(\mu,z)=\int_0^\infty \frac{t^\mu e^{-t^2}}{t-z}\,dt,$$

where $\mu$ is real with $\mu>-1$ and $z$ lies off the positive real axis. The ordinary Goodwin–Staton integral is the special case

$$G(z)=I(0,-z).$$

The generalized integral is a [Stieltjes transform](wikipedia://en/11503699) and satisfies the recurrence

$$I(\mu+1,z)=\frac12\Gamma\left(\frac{\mu+1}{2}\right)+zI(\mu,z),$$

as well as

$$\frac{\partial I(\mu,z)}{\partial z}=\mu I(\mu-1,z)-2I(\mu+1,z),\qquad \mu>0.$$

Jones derived convergent series, asymptotic expansions, and explicit error bounds, and also treated oscillatory and logarithmic variants.[^o9] Mamedov later gave another evaluation of generalized Goodwin–Staton integrals using the binomial expansion theorem.[^o13]

## Numerical evaluation {#Numerical_evaluation}

For positive real arguments, numerical evaluation can be reduced to Dawson's integral and the exponential integral through the relation above.[^o5] For small and moderate arguments, Jones's convergent series can be used, while for large arguments the asymptotic expansion is more suitable.[^o9] The DLMF section on computation of the Goodwin–Staton integral cites the original table of Goodwin and Staton.[^o15]
