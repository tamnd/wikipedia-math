# Hadamard manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hadamard manifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Hadamard manifold**, named after [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675) — more often called a **Cartan–Hadamard manifold**, after [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) — is a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $(M, g)$ that is [complete](wikipedia://en/7832) and [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) and has everywhere non-positive [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618).[^o2][^o3] By [Cartan–Hadamard theorem](wikipedia://en/11939373) all Cartan–Hadamard manifolds are diffeomorphic to the Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n.$ Furthermore it follows from the [Hopf–Rinow theorem](wikipedia://en/681265) that every pairs of points in a Cartan–Hadamard manifold may be connected by a unique geodesic segment. Thus Cartan–Hadamard manifolds are some of the closest relatives of $\mathbb{R}^n.$

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$ with its usual metric is a Cartan–Hadamard manifold with constant sectional curvature equal to $0.$

Standard $n$-dimensional [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742) $\mathbb{H}^n$ is a Cartan–Hadamard manifold with constant sectional curvature equal to $-1.$

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

In Cartan-Hadamard manifolds, the map $\exp_p : \operatorname{T}M_p \to M$ is a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) for all $p \in M.$
