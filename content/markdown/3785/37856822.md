# Ramanujan's ternary quadratic form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ramanujan's ternary quadratic form"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Ramanujan's ternary quadratic form** is the algebraic expression *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup> with [integral](wikipedia://en/14563) values for *x*, *y* and *z*.[^o2][^o3] [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717) considered this expression in a footnote in a paper[^o4] published in 1916 and briefly discussed the representability of integers in this form. After giving [necessary and sufficient conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_and_sufficient_conditions) that an integer cannot be represented in the form *ax*<sup>2</sup> + *by*<sup>2</sup> + *cz*<sup>2</sup> for certain specific values of *a*, *b* and *c*, Ramanujan observed in a footnote: "(These) results may tempt us to suppose that there are similar simple results for the [form](wikipedia://en/251478) *ax*<sup>2</sup> + *by*<sup>2</sup> + *cz*<sup>2</sup> whatever are the values of *a*, *b* and *c*. It appears, however, that in most cases there are no such simple results."[^o4] To substantiate this observation, Ramanujan discussed the form which is now referred to as Ramanujan's ternary quadratic form.

:::

## Properties discovered by Ramanujan {#Properties_discovered_by_Ramanujan}

In his 1916 paper[^o4] Ramanujan made the following observations about the form *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup>.

- The [even](wikipedia://en/143135) numbers that are not of the form *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup> are 4<sup>λ</sup>(16*μ* + 6).
- The [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) numbers that are not of the form *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup>, viz. 3, 7, 21, 31, 33, 43, 67, 79, 87, 133, 217, 219, 223, 253, 307, 391, ... do not seem to obey any simple law.

## Odd numbers beyond 391 {#Odd_numbers_beyond_391}

By putting an ellipsis at the end of the list of odd numbers not representable as *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup>, Ramanujan indicated that his list was incomplete. It was not clear whether Ramanujan intended it to be a finite list or infinite list. This prompted others to look for such odd numbers. In 1927, Burton W. Jones and Gordon Pall[^o3] discovered that the number 679 could not be expressed in the form *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup> and they also verified that there were no other such numbers below 2000. This led to an early [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) that the seventeen numbers – the sixteen numbers in Ramanujan's list and the number discovered by them – were the only odd numbers not representable as *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup>. However, in 1941, H Gupta[^o7] showed that the number 2719 could not be represented as *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup>. He also verified that there were no other such numbers below 20000. Further progress in this direction took place only after the development of modern computers. W. Galway wrote a computer program to determine odd integers not expressible as *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup>. Galway verified that there are only eighteen numbers less than 2 × 10<sup>10</sup> not representable in the form *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup>.[^o2] Based on Galway's computations, [Ken Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Ono) and [K. Soundararajan](wikipedia://en/8053624) formulated the following conjecture:[^o2]

The odd positive integers which are not of the form *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup> are: 3, 7, 21, 31, 33, 43, 67, 79, 87, 133, 217, 219, 223, 253, 307, 391, 679, 2719.

## Some known results {#Some_known_results}

The conjecture of Ken Ono and Soundararajan has not been fully resolved. However, besides the results enunciated by Ramanujan, a few more general results about the form have been established. The [proofs](wikipedia://en/82285) of some of them are quite simple while those of the others involve quite complicated concepts and arguments.[^o2]

- Every integer of the form 10*n* + 5 is represented by Ramanujan's ternary quadratic form.
- If *n* is an odd integer which is not [square-free](wikipedia://en/29525) then it can be represented in the form *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup>.
- There are only a finite number of odd integers which cannot be represented in the form *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + 10*z*<sup>2</sup>.
- If the [generalized Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/152567) is true, then the conjecture of Ono and Soundararajan is also true.
- Ramanujan's ternary quadratic form is not regular in the sense of [L.E. Dickson](wikipedia://en/1790247).[^o9]
