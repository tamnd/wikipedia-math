# William Kneale

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Kneale"}

**William Calvert Kneale** (22 June 1906, [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool) – 24 June 1990, [Grassington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassington)) was an English [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) best known for his 1962 book *The Development of Logic*, a history of logic from its beginnings in [Ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) written with his wife [Martha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Kneale). Kneale was also known as a [philosopher of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science) and the author of a book on [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [induction](wikipedia://en/393736).

:::

Educated at the Liverpool Institute High School for boys, he later became a fellow of [Exeter College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter_College,_Oxford), and in 1960 succeeded to the [White's Professor of Moral Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White's_Professor_of_Moral_Philosophy) previously occupied by the linguistic philosopher [J. L. Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._L._Austin). He retired in 1966.

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Kneale's interest in the [history of logic](wikipedia://en/59945) began in the 1940s. The focus of much of Kneale's early work was the legacy of the work of the 19th century logician [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298). His first major publication in the history of logic was his paper "Boole and the Revival of Logic," published in the philosophy journal [Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_%28journal%29) in 1948. He was also the author of a number of papers in [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939), particularly on the nature of [truth](wikipedia://en/29920) for [natural languages](wikipedia://en/21173), and the role that linguistic concepts play in the treatment of logical [paradoxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradoxes).

Kneale worked on his great history of logic from 1947 to 1957 together with his wife Martha (who was responsible for the chapters on the ancient Greeks). The result was the 800-page *The Development of Logic*, first published in 1962, which went through five impressions before going into a second, paperback, edition in 1984.

The 'History' is commonly referred to in the academic world simply as "Kneale and Kneale". It was the only major history of logic available in English in the mid-twentieth century, and the first major history of logic in English since *The Development of Symbolic Logic* published in 1906 by [A. T. Shearman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._T._Shearman). The treatise has been a standard work in the history of logic for decades.

In 1938, he married [Martha Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Kneale); they had two children, George (born 1942) and [Jane (married name Heal)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Heal); born 1946).
