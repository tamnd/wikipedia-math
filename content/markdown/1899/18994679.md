# Googol

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Googol"}

A **googol** is the [large number](wikipedia://en/187750) 10<sup>100</sup> or ten to the power of one hundred. In decimal notation, it is written as the [digit](wikipedia://en/280582) 1 followed by one hundred zeros: **10,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000**. Its [systematic name](wikipedia://en/682403) is **ten duotrigintillion** ([short scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_scale)) or **ten sexdecilliard** ([long scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_scale)). Its [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorization) is 2<sup>100</sup> × 5<sup>100</sup>.

:::

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The term was coined in 1920 by nine-year-old Milton Sirotta (1911–1981), nephew of American mathematician [Edward Kasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Kasner).[^o3] He may have been inspired by the contemporary comic strip character [Barney Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barney_Google_and_Snuffy_Smith).[^o4] Kasner popularized the concept in his 1940 book *[Mathematics and the Imagination](wikipedia://en/31052235)*.[^o5] Other names for this quantity include *ten duotrigintillion* on the short scale (commonly used in English–speaking countries),[^o6] *ten thousand sexdecillion* on the long scale, or *ten sexdecilliard* on the [Peletier long scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peletier_long_scale).

## Size {#Size}

A googol has no special significance in mathematics. However, it is useful when comparing with other very large quantities, such as the number of [subatomic particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_particles) in the visible universe or the number of hypothetical possibilities in a [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess) game. Kasner used it to illustrate the difference between an unimaginably large number and [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824), and in this role it is sometimes used in teaching mathematics. To put in perspective the size of a googol, the mass of an electron, just under 10<sup>−30</sup> kg, can be compared to the mass of the visible universe, estimated at between 10<sup>50</sup> and 10<sup>60</sup> kg.[^o8] It is a ratio in the order of about 10<sup>80</sup> to 10<sup>90</sup>, or at most one ten-billionth of a googol (0.00000001% of a googol).

[Carl Sagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sagan) pointed out that the total number of elementary particles in the observable universe is around 10<sup>80</sup> (the [Eddington number](wikipedia://en/461770)) and that if the whole universe were packed with [neutrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron) so that there would be no empty space anywhere, there would be around 10<sup>128</sup>. He also noted the similarity of the second calculation to that of [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) in *[The Sand Reckoner](wikipedia://en/276396)*. By Archimedes's calculation, the universe of [Aristarchus](wikipedia://en/74857) (roughly 2 light years in diameter), if fully packed with sand, would contain 10<sup>63</sup> grains. If the much larger observable universe of today were filled with sand, it would still only equal × 10<sup>95</sup> grains. The number would have to be 100,000 times bigger to make a googol.[^o9]

The decay time for a supermassive [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650) of roughly 1 galaxy-mass (10<sup>11</sup> [solar masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_mass)) due to [Hawking radiation](wikipedia://en/173724) is on the order of 10<sup>100</sup> years.[^o10] Therefore, the [heat death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_death_of_the_universe) of an [expanding universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_an_expanding_universe) is lower-bounded to occur at least one googol years in the future.

A googol is considerably smaller than a [centillion](wikipedia://en/682403).[^o11]

## Properties {#Properties}

A googol is approximately equal to $70!\approx1.1979\times10^{100}$ ([factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) of 70). Using an [integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_%28computer_science%29), [binary numeral system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system), one would need 333 bits to represent a googol, i.e., $10^{100}=2^{(100/\mathrm{log}_{10}2)}\approx2^{332.19280949}$. However, a googol is well within the maximum bounds of an IEEE 754 [double-precision floating point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-precision_floating-point_format) type without full precision in the mantissa.

Using [modular arithmetic](wikipedia://en/20087), the series of [residues](wikipedia://en/20087#Residue_systems) (mod *n*) of one googol, starting with mod 1, is as follows:

0, 0, 1, 0, 0, 4, 4, 0, 1, 0, 1, 4, 3, 4, 10, 0, 4, 10, 9, 0, 4, 12, 13, 16, 0, 16, 10, 4, 16, 10, 5, 0, 1, 4, 25, 28, 10, 28, 16, 0, 1, 4, 31, 12, 10, 36, 27, 16, 11, 0, ...

This sequence is the same as that of the [residues (mod n) of](wikipedia://en/12365#Mod_n) a [googolplex](wikipedia://en/12365) up until the 17th position.

:::

## Cultural impact {#Cultural_impact}

Googol is a homophone of the company name *[Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google)*, an intentional misspelling of "googol" by the company's founders; it suggests that the search engine provides large quantities of information.[^o15] In 2004, Kasner's heirs considered suing Google over their use of "googol";[^o16] however, no suit was ever filed.[^o17]

Since October 2009, Google has used the domain "1e100.net", "1e100" being [E notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E_notation) for 1 googol, to identify servers across its network.[^o18][^o19]

"Googol" was the £1 million answer in a 2001 episode of the British *[Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Wants_to_Be_a_Millionaire?_%28British_game_show%29)*, which the [contestant allegedly won by cheating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Wants_to_Be_a_Millionaire?_%28British_game_show%29#Charles_Ingram_cheating_scandal).[^o20]

A 1976 [Richie Rich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richie_Rich_%28character%29) comic strip featured "The Googol", a masked villain so named because he had once been a US pilot pursued by 100 [Zeros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_A6M_Zero) in the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War)[^o21].
