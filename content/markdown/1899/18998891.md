# C-chart

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="C-chart"}

In [statistical quality control](wikipedia://en/593680), the **c-chart** is a type of [control chart](wikipedia://en/435754) used to monitor "count"-type data, typically total number of nonconformities per unit.[^o3] It is also occasionally used to monitor the total number of events occurring in a given unit of time.

:::

The c-chart differs from the [p-chart](wikipedia://en/3135066) in that it accounts for the possibility of more than one nonconformity per inspection unit, and that (unlike the [p-chart](wikipedia://en/3135066) and [u-chart](wikipedia://en/18998895)) it requires a fixed sample size. The p-chart models "pass"/"fail"-type inspection only, while the c-chart (and [u-chart](wikipedia://en/18998895)) give the ability to distinguish between (for example) 2 items which fail inspection because of one fault each and the same two items failing inspection with 5 faults each; in the former case, the p-chart will show two non-conformant items, while the c-chart will show 10 faults.

Nonconformities may also be tracked by type or location which can prove helpful in tracking down [assignable causes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assignable_cause).

Examples of processes suitable for monitoring with a c-chart include:

- Monitoring the number of voids per inspection unit in [injection molding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injection_molding) or [casting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casting) processes
- Monitoring the number of discrete components that must be re-[soldered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldered) per [printed circuit board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printed_circuit_board)
- Monitoring the number of product returns per day

The [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) is the basis for the chart and requires the following assumptions:[^o4]

- The number of opportunities or potential locations for nonconformities is very large
- The probability of nonconformity at any location is small and constant
- The inspection procedure is same for each sample and is carried out consistently from sample to sample

The control limits for this chart type are $\bar c \pm 3\sqrt{\bar c}$ where $\bar c$ is the estimate of the long-term process mean established during control-chart setup.
