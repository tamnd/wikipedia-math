# Martin Schechter (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Martin Schechter"}

**Martin Schechter** (1930, [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia) – June 7, 2021) was an American mathematician whose work concerned [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) (specially [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and their applications to [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416)). He was a professor at the [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine).[^o3][^o2]

:::

Schechter did his undergraduate studies at the [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York).[^o3]

He obtained his Ph.D. in 1957 from [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) (NYU) with [Louis Nirenberg](wikipedia://en/1814725) and [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972) as thesis advisors; his dissertation was entitled *On estimating partial differential operator in the L<sub>2</sub>-norm*. He taught at NYU from 1957 to 1966, and at [Yeshiva University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_University) from 1966 to 1983, before moving to UC Irvine.[^o3]

He is the author of several books, including the textbook *Principles of Functional Analysis* (Academic Press, 1971; 2nd ed., [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822), 2002).[^o5]

Schechter was a member of the [Association of Orthodox Jewish Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Orthodox_Jewish_Scientists).

Schechter is buried in the Har Menuchot cemetery in the Givat Shaul neighborhood of Jerusalem, Israel.
