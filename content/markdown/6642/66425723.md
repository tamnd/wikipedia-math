# Jenny Baglivo

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jenny Baglivo"}

**Jenny Antoinette Baglivo** (born 1948) is an American mathematician, statistician, and book author. She is retired as a professor of mathematics at [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College), where she retains an affiliation as research professor.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Baglivo is originally from [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), the daughter of a watch repairer and granddaughter of Italian immigrants. The only one of four siblings to attend college, she majored in mathematics at [Fordham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University), graduating in 1970. She went to [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University) for graduate study, earning a master's degree in mathematics in 1972, a second master's degree in computer and information science in 1976, and a Ph.D. in mathematics in 1976, specializing in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). Her dissertation, *Equivariant Wall Obstruction Theory*, concerned [Wall's finiteness obstruction](wikipedia://en/36722383), and was supervised by Douglas R. Anderson.

After completing her doctorate, she became an assistant professor of mathematics at [Fairfield University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield_University), and was promoted to associate professor in 1981. While continuing to hold an affiliation at Fairfield, she also became a postdoctoral researcher at the [Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Sloan_Kettering_Cancer_Center) from 1980 to 1982. There, she worked as a [biostatistician](wikipedia://en/3878) on [CT scans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan) of brains. She moved from Fairfield to [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College) in 1986, and was promoted to full professor in 1992.

## Books {#Books}

Baglivo's book *[Incidence and Symmetry in Design and Architecture](wikipedia://en/66425726)* (Cambridge University Press, 1983, with Jack E. Graver) won an [Alpha Sigma Nu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Sigma_Nu) Book Award in 1983, and has been recommended for undergraduate mathematics libraries by the Basic Library List Committee of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).

She is also the author of *Mathematica Laboratories for Mathematical Statistics: Emphasizing Simulation and Computer Intensive Methods* (SIAM, 2005).
