# Alison Motsinger-Reif

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alison Motsinger-Reif"}

**Alison Anne Motsinger-Reif** is an American biostatistician and human geneticist specialized in association analyses, [big data](wikipedia://en/27051151), and genomic analyses. In December 2018, she became the chief of the [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) and [computational biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_biology) branch at the [National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Environmental_Health_Sciences). Montsinger-Reif was previously a professor of statistics at the [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Motsinger-Reif was a lab technician in the department of physiology and pharmacology at [Wake Forest University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_University) from 1997 to 2000. Her advisor was James E. Smith. She worked as a research assistant with Derya Unutmaz at Vanderbilt University from 2000 to 2003. She earned a B.S. in Biological Sciences (2002), M.S. in Applied Statistics (2006) and Ph.D. in Human Genetics (2007) at [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University) studying with Marylyn Ritchie.[^o4]

She held varying faculty roles at [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University) from 2007 to 2018 including professor of statistics and biostatistics core director.[^o4] On December 10, 2018, she joined [National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Environmental_Health_Sciences) (NIEHS) as chief of the Biostatistics & Computational Biology Branch.[^o4][^o5] Her group focuses on the development and application of modern statistical approaches for understanding the etiology of common, complex diseases.[^o6] Her lab also applies statistical approaches to evolution where she develops tree models and algorithms to detect discovery rates.[^o7] She conducts association analyses looking at large-scale genetics and [genomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genomics) data to find genetic factors that predict complex disease, and responses to drugs and environmental chemicals.[^o5]
