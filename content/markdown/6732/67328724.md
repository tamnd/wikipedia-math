# Denis Auroux

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Denis Auroux"}

**Denis Auroux** (born April 1977)[^o3] is a French mathematician working in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Auroux was admitted in 1993 to the [École normale supérieure (Paris)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29). In 1994, he received a [licentiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licentiate_%28degree%29#France) and *maîtrise* in mathematics from [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) (Paris 7). In 1995, he received a licentiate in physics from [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University) (Paris 6) and passed the *[agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation)*. In 1995, he received a master's degree in mathematics from [Paris-Sud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_University) with a thesis on [Seiberg-Witten invariants](wikipedia://en/14407762) of [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356). In 1999, he received his doctorate from the [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique) with supervisors [Jean-Pierre Bourguignon](wikipedia://en/1520814) and [Mikhael Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michail_Leonidowitsch_Gromow) for a thesis on structure theorems for compact symplectic manifolds via almost-complex techniques.[^o5] In 2003, he completed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at Paris-Sud University with a thesis on approximately holomorphic techniques and monodromy invariants in symplectic topology.

As a postdoc, he was a [C. L. E. Moore Instructor](wikipedia://en/21957255) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) from 1999 to 2002, where he became an assistant professor in 2002, an associate professor in 2004 (tenured in 2006), and a professor in 2009 (on leave from 2009 to 2011). From 2009 to 2018, he was a professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). Since Fall 2018, he has been at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University),[^o6] where he taught [Math 55](wikipedia://en/22008131), two-semester honors undergraduate course on algebra and analysis.[^o7]

His research deals with [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774), and [mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/644671).[^o8][^o9]

In 2002, he received the [Prix Peccot](wikipedia://en/60897406) from the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France). In 2005, he received a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship).[^o6] He was an invited speaker in 2010 with talk *Fukaya Categories and bordered Heegaard-Floer Homology*[^o10] at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Hyderabad and in 2004 at the European Congress of Mathematicians in Stockholm.[^o11]

He is also the author of the [free and open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_open-source_software) handwritten note-taking software Xournal, which has been developed into Xournal++.[^o12]
