# Overlap (term rewriting)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Overlap"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) and [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), **overlap**, as a property of the reduction rules in [term rewriting system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_rewriting_system), describes a situation where a number of different reduction rules specify potentially contradictory ways of reducing a reducible expression, also known as a redex, within a [term](wikipedia://en/24885593).[^o2]

:::

More precisely, if a number of different reduction rules share function symbols on the left-hand side, overlap can occur. Often we do not consider trivial overlap with a redex and itself.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Consider the term rewriting system defined by the following reduction rules:

$$\rho_1\ :\ f(g(x), y) \rightarrow y$$

$$\rho_2\ :\ g(x) \rightarrow f(x, x)$$

The term $f(g(x), y)$ can be reduced via ρ<sub>1</sub> to yield $y$, but it can also be reduced via ρ<sub>2</sub> to yield $f(f(x, x), y).$. Note how the redex $g(x)$ is contained in the redex $f(g(x), y)$. The result of reducing different redexes is described in a what is known as a [critical pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_pair_%28logic%29); the critical pair arising out of this term rewriting system is $(f(f(x, x), y), y)$.

Overlap may occur with fewer than two reduction rules.

Consider the term rewriting system defined by the following reduction rule:

$$\rho_1\ :\ g(g(x)) \rightarrow x$$

The term $g(g(g(x)))$ has overlapping redexes, which can be either applied to the innermost occurrence or to the outermost occurrence of the $g(g(x))$ term.

:::
