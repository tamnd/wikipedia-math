# James Stewart (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="James Stewart"}

**James Drewry Stewart**, (March 29, 1941 – December 3, 2014) was a Canadian mathematician, violinist, and [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) of mathematics at [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University). Stewart is best known for his series of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) textbooks used for high school, college, and university-level courses.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Stewart received his Master of Science at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and his doctor of philosophy from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 1967. He worked for two years as a postdoctoral fellow at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London), where his research focused on [harmonic](wikipedia://en/14147) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). His books are standard textbooks in universities in many countries. One of his best-known textbooks is *Calculus: Early Transcendentals* (1995),[^o4] a set of textbooks which is accompanied by a [website](http://stewartcalculus.com) for students.

Stewart was also a violinist and a former member of the [Hamilton Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Philharmonic_Orchestra).[^o10]

## Integral House {#Integral_House}

From 2003 to 2009[^o6] a house designed by [Brigitte Shim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigitte_Shim) and Howard Sutcliffe was constructed for Stewart in the Rosedale neighbourhood of Toronto at a cost of \$32 million. He paid an additional \$5.4 million for the existing house and lot which was torn down to make room for his new home.[^o7] Called [Integral House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_House) (a reference to its curved walls, and their similarity to the mathematical [integral symbol](wikipedia://en/3171907)), the house includes a concert hall that seats 150. Stewart has said, "My books and my house are my twin legacies. If I hadn't commissioned the house I'm not sure what I would have spent the money on." [Glenn Lowry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Lowry), director of the [Museum of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Modern_Art), called the house "one of the most important private houses built in North America in a long time."[^o8]

## Personal life and political activism {#Personal_life_and_political_activism}

Stewart was gay and involved in [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) activism. According to [Joseph Clement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Clement_%28filmmaker%29), a documentary filmmaker who is working on a film about Stewart and Integral House, Stewart brought gay rights activist [George Hislop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Hislop) to speak at [McMaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University) in the early 1970s, when the LGBT liberation movement was in its infancy, and was involved in protests and demonstrations.[^o10]

## Death {#Death}

In the summer of 2013, Stewart was diagnosed with [multiple myeloma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_myeloma), a blood cancer.[^o12] He died on December 3, 2014, aged 73.[^o13]

## Honors {#Honors}

In 2015, he was posthumously awarded the [Meritorious Service Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritorious_Service_Cross).[^o15]
