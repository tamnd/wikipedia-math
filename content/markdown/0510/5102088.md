# Manjul Bhargava

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Manjul Bhargava"}

**Manjul Bhargava** (born 8 August 1974)[^o4] is an Indian [Canadian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Americans) mathematician. He is the Robert Gunning-Brandon Fradd, Class of 1983, Professor of Mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), the Stieltjes Professor of Number Theory[^o5] at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University), and also holds Adjunct Professorships at the [Tata Institute of Fundamental Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Institute_of_Fundamental_Research), the [Indian Institute of Technology Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Technology_Bombay), and the [University of Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hyderabad). He is known primarily for his contributions to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

Bhargava was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 2014. According to the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) citation, he was awarded the prize "for developing powerful new methods in the [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153), which he applied to count rings of small rank and to bound the average rank of elliptic curves".[^o6][^o7][^o8] He was also a member of the [Padma Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Award) committee in 2023.[^o9]

In February 2026, he became the first president of the [National Museum of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Mathematics) (MoMath) in New York city.[^o10]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Bhargava was born to an [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) family in [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), Canada, but grew up and attended school primarily on [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29). His mother Mira Bhargava, a mathematician at [Hofstra University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofstra_University), was his first mathematics teacher.[^o12][^o13] He completed all of his high school math and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) courses by age 14.[^o14] He attended [Plainedge High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plainedge_High_School) in [North Massapequa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Massapequa,_New_York), and graduated in 1992 as the class [valedictorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian). He obtained his AB from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1996, where he was elected to [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa). For his research as an undergraduate, he was awarded the 1996 [Morgan Prize](wikipedia://en/4180157). The research was eventually published in *[The American Mathematical Monthly](wikipedia://en/1179483)* in 2000, and established the [Bhargava factorial](wikipedia://en/48496573).[^o15] Bhargava went on to pursue graduate studies at Princeton University, where he completed a doctoral dissertation titled "Higher composition laws" under the supervision of [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027) and received his PhD in 2001, with the support of a [Hertz Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz_Foundation).[^o16] He was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 2001–02,[^o17] and at Harvard University in 2002–03. Princeton appointed him as a tenured Full Professor in 2003. He was appointed to the Stieltjes Chair in [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) in 2010.

Bhargava has also studied the [tabla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabla) under gurus such as [Zakir Hussain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakir_Hussain_%28musician%29).[^o18] He also studied [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) from his grandfather Purushottam Lal Bhargava, a scholar of Sanskrit and ancient Indian history.[^o19][^o20] He is an admirer of Sanskrit poetry.[^o21]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Bhargava’s PhD thesis generalized [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss)'s classical law for composition of [binary quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/1867129) to many other situations. One major use of his results is the parametrization of quartic and quintic orders in [number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822), thus allowing the study of the asymptotic behavior of the arithmetic properties of these orders and fields.

His research also includes fundamental contributions to the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478), to [interpolation problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpolation_theory) and [p-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327), to the study of [ideal class groups](wikipedia://en/150907) of [algebraic number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822), and to the arithmetic theory of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225).[^o23] A short list of his specific mathematical contributions are:

- Fourteen new Gauss-style composition laws.
- Determination of the asymptotic density of [discriminants](wikipedia://en/55607) of quartic and [quintic number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822).
- Proofs of the first-known cases of the [Cohen-Lenstra-Martinet heuristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen-Lenstra-Martinet_heuristics) for [class groups](wikipedia://en/150907).
- Proof of the [15 theorem](wikipedia://en/5405018), including an extension of the theorem to other number sets such as the odd numbers and the [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666).
- Proof (with Jonathan Hanke) of the [290 theorem](wikipedia://en/5405018).
- A novel [generalization](wikipedia://en/12746) of the [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) function, [Bhargava factorial](wikipedia://en/48496573),[^o24] providing an answer to a decades-old question of [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya).[^o25]
- Proof (with Arul Shankar) that the average [rank](wikipedia://en/51244926) of all [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) over **Q** (when ordered by height) is bounded.
- Proof that most hyperelliptic curves over **Q** have no rational points.

In 2015, Manjul Bhargava and Arul Shankar proved the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161) for a positive proportion of elliptic curves.[^o26]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

*Image: Four\_Fields\_medallists\_plus\_epsilon.jpg.* Four Fields medallists left to right (Artur Avila, Martin Hairer (at back), Maryam Mirzakhani, with Maryam's daughter Anahita) and Bhargava at the ICM 2014 in Seoul

Bhargava has won several awards for his research, the most prestigious being the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859), the highest award in the field of mathematics, which he won in 2014.

He received the [Morgan Prize](wikipedia://en/4180157) in 1996.[^o28] and [Hertz Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz_Fellowship)[^o29] He was named one of *[Popular Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Science)* magazine's "Brilliant 10" in November 2002. He then received a [Clay](wikipedia://en/7655) 5-year Research Fellowship and the Merten M. Hasse Prize from the [MAA](wikipedia://en/198772) in 2003,[^o30] the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167), the [SASTRA Ramanujan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SASTRA_Ramanujan_Prize), and the Leonard M. and Eleanor B. Blumenthal Award for the Advancement of Research in Pure Mathematics in 2005.

[Peter Sarnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sarnak) of [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) has said of Bhargava:[^o31]

At mathematics he's at the very top end. For a guy so young I can't remember anybody so decorated at his age. He certainly started out with a bang and has not let it get to his head, which is unusual. Of course he couldn't do what he does if he wasn't brilliant. It's his exceptional talent that's so striking.

In 2008, Bhargava was awarded the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)'s [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177).[^o32] The citation reads:

Bhargava's original and surprising contribution is the discovery of laws of composition on forms of higher degree. His techniques and insights into this question are dazzling; even in the case considered by Gauss, they lead to a new and clearer presentation of that theory.

In 2009, he was awarded the Face of the Future award at the India Abroad Person of the Year ceremony in New York City.[^o33] In 2014, the same publication gave the India Abroad Publisher's Prize for Special Excellence.[^o34]

In 2011, he was awarded the [Fermat Prize](wikipedia://en/10919195) for "various generalizations of the Davenport-Heilbronn estimates and for his startling recent results (with Arul Shankar) on the average rank of elliptic curves".[^o35] In 2012, Bhargava was named an inaugural recipient of the Simons Investigator Award,[^o36] and became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in its inaugural class of fellows.[^o37] He was awarded the 2012 [Infosys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys_Prize) in mathematics for his "extraordinarily original work in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), which has revolutionized the way in which [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_fields) and [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves) are counted".[^o38]

In 2013, he was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o39]

In 2014, Bhargava was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul)[^o20] for "developing powerful new methods in the geometry of numbers, which he applied to count rings of small rank and to bound the average rank of elliptic curves".[^o40]

In 2015, he was awarded the [Padma Bhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bhushan), the third-highest civilian award of India.[^o41]

In 2017, Bhargava was elected as a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o42] In 2018, Bhargava was named as the inaugural occupant of The Distinguished Chair for the Public Dissemination of Mathematics at The [National Museum of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Mathematics) (MoMath). This is the first visiting professorship in the United States dedicated exclusively to raising public awareness of mathematics.[^o43] Bhargava was conferred a Fellowship at the Royal Society in 2019.[^o44]
