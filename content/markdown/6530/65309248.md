# List of topologies

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of topologies"}

The following is a list of named [topologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_%28structure%29) or [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450), many of which are counterexamples in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and related branches of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). This is not a list of [properties](wikipedia://en/1337282) that a topology or topological space might possess; for that, see [List of general topology topics](wikipedia://en/352950) and [Topological property](wikipedia://en/1337282).

:::

## Discrete and indiscrete {#Discrete_and_indiscrete}

::: {#o3 .entry cue=list-entry name="Discrete topology"}

- [Discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology) − All subsets are open.

:::

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Indiscrete topology"}

- [Indiscrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiscrete_topology), chaotic topology, or [Trivial topology](wikipedia://en/382447) − Only the empty set and its complement are open.

:::

::: {#o7 .definition cue=dfni name="cocountable extension topology"}

  - Given a topological space $(X, \tau),$ the [**cocountable extension topology**](wikipedia://en/801246#Cocountable_extension_topology) on $X$ is the topology having as a subbasis the union of $\tau$ and the family of all subsets of $X$ whose complements in $X$ are countable.

### Finite spaces {#Finite_spaces}

::: {#o15 .entry cue=list-entry name="Discrete two-point space"}

- [Discrete two-point space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_two-point_space) − The simplest example of a [totally disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_disconnected) [discrete space](wikipedia://en/56061).

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=list-entry name="Finite topological space"}

- [Finite topological space](wikipedia://en/14682596)

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pseudocircle"}

- [Pseudocircle](wikipedia://en/3441237) − A [finite topological space](wikipedia://en/14682596) on 4 elements that fails to satisfy any [separation axiom](wikipedia://en/40117302) besides [T<sub>0</sub>](wikipedia://en/68121). However, from the viewpoint of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), it has the remarkable property that it is indistinguishable from the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) $S^1.$

:::

::: {#o18 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sierpiński space"}

- [Sierpiński space](wikipedia://en/387651), also called the [connected two-point set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_two-point_set) − A 2-point set $\{0, 1\}$ with the [particular point topology](wikipedia://en/5336200) $\{\varnothing, \{1\}, \{0,1\}\}.$

:::

## Integers {#Integers}

::: {#o20 .entry cue=list-entry name="Arens–Fort space"}

- [Arens–Fort space](wikipedia://en/8588347) − A Hausdorff, regular, normal space that is not first-countable or compact. It has an element (i.e. $p := (0, 0)$) for which there is no sequence in $X \setminus \{p\}$ that converges to $p$ but there is a sequence $x_\bullet = \left(x_i\right)_{i=1}^\infty$ in $X \setminus \{(0, 0)\}$ such that $(0, 0)$ is a cluster point of $x_\bullet.$

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=list-entry name="The Baire space"}

- [The Baire space](wikipedia://en/2078245) − $\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}$ with the product topology, where $\mathbb{N}$ denotes the [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers) endowed with the discrete topology. It is the space of all sequences of natural numbers.

::: {#o147 .entry cue=list-entry name="Duplication of a point"}

- [Duplication of a point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duplication_of_a_point): Let $x \in X$ be a non-[isolated point](wikipedia://en/390557) of $X,$ let $d \not\in X$ be arbitrary, and let $Y = X \cup \{d\}.$ Then $\tau = \{V \subseteq Y : \text{ either } V \text{ or } ( V \setminus \{d\}) \cup \{x\} \text{ is an open subset of } X\}$ is a topology on $Y$ and $x$ and $d$ have the same [neighborhood filters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_filter) in $Y.$ In this way, $x$ has been duplicated.[^n1]
