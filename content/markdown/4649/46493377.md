# The Algorithm Auction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="The Algorithm Auction"}

**The Algorithm Auction** is the world's first auction of [computer algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_algorithms).[^o2] Created by Ruse Laboratories, the initial auction featured seven lots and was held at the [Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Hewitt,_Smithsonian_Design_Museum) on March 27, 2015.[^o3]

:::

Five lots were physical representations of famous code or algorithms, including a signed, handwritten copy of the original [Hello, World!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello,_World!) [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29) program by its creator [Brian Kernighan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Kernighan) on dot-matrix printer paper, a printed copy of 5,000 lines of [Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language) code comprising the earliest known version of [Turtle Graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtle_Graphics), signed by its creator [Hal Abelson](wikipedia://en/382535), a necktie containing the six-line [qrpff](wikipedia://en/2117827) algorithm capable of decrypting content on a commercially produced [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD) video disc, and a pair of drawings representing [OkCupid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OkCupid)'s original Compatibility Calculation algorithm, signed by the company founders.[^o4] The [qrpff](wikipedia://en/2117827) lot sold for \$2,500.[^o5]

Two other lots were “living algorithms,” including a set of [JavaScript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JavaScript) tools for building applications that are accessible to the [visually impaired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visually_impaired) and the other was for a program that converts lines of software code into music.[^o6] Winning bidders received, along with artifacts related to the algorithms, a full [intellectual property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_property) license to use, modify, or open-source the code.[^o7] All lots were sold, with Hello World receiving the most bids.[^o8]

Exhibited alongside the auction lots were a facsimile of the [Plimpton 322](wikipedia://en/2983547) tablet on loan from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), and Nigella, an art-world facing computer virus named after [Nigella Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigella_Lawson) and created by cypherpunk and hacktivist Richard Jones.[^o9]

Sebastian Chan, Director of Digital & Emerging Media at the [Cooper–Hewitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Hewitt,_Smithsonian_Design_Museum),[^o10] attended the event remotely from [Milan, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan,_Italy) via a Beam Pro telepresence [robot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot).[^o11]

## Effects {#Effects}

Following the auction, the [Museum of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Modern_Art) held a salon titled *The Way of the Algorithm* highlighting algorithms as "a ubiquitous and indispensable component of our lives."[^o13]
