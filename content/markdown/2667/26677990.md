# 62 knot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="62 knot"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **6<sub>2</sub> knot** is one of three [prime knots](wikipedia://en/315030) with [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298598) six, the others being the [stevedore knot](wikipedia://en/26652059) and the [6<sub>3</sub> knot](wikipedia://en/26684180). This knot is sometimes referred to as the **Miller Institute knot**, because it appears in the logo[^o4] of the [Miller Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Institute) for Basic Research in Science at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).

:::

The 6<sub>2</sub> knot can be represented with the following [parametric equation](wikipedia://en/576108):[^o5]

$$\begin{aligned} x &= \cos(2\phi) \\ y &= \cos(7\phi + \pi/4) \\ z &= \cos(\phi + 1.9065) + \cos(7\phi + 5.01637) \end{aligned}$$

with $0\leq\phi\leq 2\pi$.

The 6<sub>2</sub> knot is [invertible](wikipedia://en/17351839) but not [amphichiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphichiral_knot). Its [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) is

$$\Delta(t) = -t^2 + 3t -3 + 3t^{-1} - t^{-2}, \,$$

its [Conway polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647#Alexander.E2.80.93Conway_polynomial) is

$$\nabla(z) = -z^4 - z^2 + 1, \,$$

and its [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642) is

$V(q) = q - 1 + 2q^{-1} - 2q^{-2} + 2q^{-3} - 2q^{-4} + q^{-5}. \,$[^o6]

The 6<sub>2</sub> knot is a [hyperbolic knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_knot), with its [complement](wikipedia://en/1350120) having a [volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_volume_%28knot%29) of approximately 4.40083.
