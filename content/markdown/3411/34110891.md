# Stella octangula number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stella octangula number"}

*Image: StellaOctangulaNumber.jpg.* 124 [magnetic balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neodymium_magnet_toys) arranged into the shape of a [stella octangula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula)

In mathematics, a **stella octangula number** is a [figurate number](wikipedia://en/325260) based on the [stella octangula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula), of the form $n(2n^{2} - 1)$.[^o2][^o3]

:::

The sequence of stella octangula numbers is

0, 1, 14, 51, 124, 245, 426, 679, 1016, 1449, 1990, ... [^o2]

Only two of these numbers are [square](wikipedia://en/213919).

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Ljunggren equation"}

## Ljunggren's equation {#Ljunggren's_equation}

There are only two positive [square](wikipedia://en/213919) stella octangula numbers, $1$ and $9653449 = 3107^{2} = (13 \times 239)^{2}$, corresponding to $n = 1$ and $n = 169$ respectively.[^o2][^o6] This is related to that there are only two [Pell numbers](wikipedia://en/1355482) that are [square numbers](wikipedia://en/213919): $1$ and $169$, and $1$ and $239$ are their corresponding [NSW numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSW_number) (which are one half the [companion Pell numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_Pell_number)). The [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) describing the square stella octangula numbers,

$$m^2 = n (2n^2 - 1)$$

may be placed in the equivalent Weierstrass form

$$x^2 = y^3 - 2y$$

by the change of variables $x = 2m$, $y = 2n$. Because the two factors $n$ and $2n^{2} - 1$ of the square number $m^{2}$ are [relatively prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_prime), they must each be squares themselves, and the second change of variables $X=m/\sqrt{n}$ and $Y=\sqrt{n}$ leads to **Ljunggren's equation**

$X^2 = 2Y^4 - 1$[^o6]

A theorem of [Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602) states that every elliptic curve has only finitely many integer solutions, and (txt) found a difficult proof that the only integer solutions to his equation were $(1,1)$ and $(239,13)$, corresponding to the two square stella octangula numbers.[^o8] [Louis J. Mordell](wikipedia://en/991484) conjectured that the proof could be simplified, and several later authors published simplifications.[^o6][^o9][^o10]

:::

## Additional applications {#Additional_applications}

The stella octangula numbers arise in a parametric family of instances to the [crossed ladders problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossed_ladders_problem) in which the lengths and heights of the ladders and the height of their crossing point are all integers. In these instances, the ratio between the heights of the two ladders is a stella octangula number.[^o12]
