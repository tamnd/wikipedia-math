# Goran Švob

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Goran Švob"}

**Goran Švob** (; 29 May 1947 – 18 April 2013) was a Croatian [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), [logician](wikipedia://en/46426065), and author. He was an associate professor at the Department of Philosophy of [Faculty of Humanities and Social Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zagreb_Faculty_of_Humanities_and_Social_Sciences), [University of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zagreb) where he taught logic and the [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language), being employed there since 1975.

:::

Švob has written two books and published numerous scientific papers in the academic field of [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065). He was greatly influenced by the [analytic philosophy](wikipedia://en/159211) and particularly by the philosophers [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416), [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163), and [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741).

## Education and academic career {#Education_and_academic_career}

Švob was born on 29 May 1947 in [Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb), then part of [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia). There he finished his primary school education and graduated from [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29). He then enrolled at the [Faculty of Humanities and Social Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zagreb_Faculty_of_Humanities_and_Social_Sciences) of the [University of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zagreb) where he got his [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [English language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) in 1971. The same year he managed to get employed at the Department of Philosophy of the same faculty.

During the academic year 1973–1974 Švob spent six months as a research fellow at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford). He also had shorter visits at the universities in Austria, [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany), Great Britain, Japan, and China. In 1988, he got his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in philosophy at the Faculty of Humanities and Social Sciences, and the following year he was selected to the position of an assistant professor, and was further promoted to the position of associate professor in 2001. He has been teaching logic and philosophy of language there since 1975. He was also a guest lecturer at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna), [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich), [Peking University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_University), [Hokkaido University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaido_University), [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo), and [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University).

Švob was the leader of scientific research project *Logic, Universal Language and the Philosophy of Language* ([Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language): *Logika, univerzalni jezik i filozofija jezika*) of the [Ministry of Science, Education and Sports of the Republic of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Science,_Education_and_Sports_%28Croatia%29).[^o8]

## Published work {#Published_work}

Švob published scientific papers in leading philosophical journals, in particular the journal of [Praxis School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praxis_School), *Filozofska istraživanja*, *SOL*, and, most notably, *Acta Analytica*. His first book, titled *Frege: Pojmovno pismo* (English: *Frege: Conceptual Notation*), was published in 1992.[^o8]

*Od slike do igre* (English: *From Image to Game*) was a second book written by Švob, published in 2009. The book is consolidated of articles which differ by subject and time when they were written, though they all mainly focus on the work of three philosophers: [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416), [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163), and [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741). The focal point of the book, however, is the shift in Wittgenstein's philosophy of language, or the transition from the early to late Wittgenstein.[^o6]
