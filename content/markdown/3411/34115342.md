# Wilhelm Ljunggren

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wilhelm Ljunggren"}

*Image: WilhelmLjunggren.png.* Wilhelm Ljunggren

**Wilhelm Ljunggren** (7 October 1905 – 25 January 1973) was a [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), specializing in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o2]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Ljunggren was born in [Kristiania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo) and [finished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examen_artium) his secondary education in 1925. He studied at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo), earning a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cand.real.) in 1931 under the supervision of [Thoralf Skolem](wikipedia://en/453765), and found employment as a secondary school mathematics teacher in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen), following Skolem who had moved in 1930 to the Chr. Michelsen Institute there. While in Bergen, Ljunggren continued his studies, earning a [dr.philos.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr.philos.) from the University of Oslo in 1937.[^o2][^o4]

In 1938 he moved to work as a teacher at Hegdehaugen in Oslo. In 1943 he became a fellow of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters), and he also joined the [Selskapet til Vitenskapenes Fremme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selskapet_til_Vitenskapenes_Fremme). He was appointed as a [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent) at the University of Oslo in 1948, but in 1949 he returned to Bergen as a professor at the recently founded [University of Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bergen). He moved back to the University of Oslo again in 1956, where he served until his death in 1973 in Oslo.[^o2][^o4][^o5]

### Research {#Research}

Ljunggren's research concerned number theory, and in particular [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109).[^o2] He showed that [Ljunggren's equation](wikipedia://en/34110891),

*X*<sup>2</sup> = 2*Y*<sup>4</sup> − 1.

has only the two integer solutions (1,1) and (239,13);[^o7] however, his proof was complicated, and after [Louis J. Mordell](wikipedia://en/991484) conjectured that it could be simplified, simpler proofs were published by several other authors.[^o8][^o9][^o10]

Ljunggren also posed the question of finding the integer solutions to the [Ramanujan–Nagell equation](wikipedia://en/2412819)

2<sup>*n*</sup> − 7 = *x*<sup>2</sup>

(or equivalently, of finding [triangular](wikipedia://en/213915) [Mersenne numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_number)) in 1943,[^o11] independently of [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717) who had asked the same question in 1913.

Ljunggren's publications are collected in a book edited by [Paulo Ribenboim](wikipedia://en/9129242).[^o12]
