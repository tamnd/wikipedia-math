# Uniformly distributed measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uniformly distributed measure"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) — specifically, in [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694) — a **uniformly distributed measure** on a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) is one for which the measure of an [open ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_ball) depends only on its radius and not on its centre. By convention, the measure is also required to be [Borel regular](wikipedia://en/542326), and to take positive and finite values on open balls of finite radius. Thus, if (*X*, *d*) is a metric space, a Borel regular measure *μ* on *X* is said to be **uniformly distributed** if

:::

$$0 < \mu(\mathbf{B}_{r}(x)) = \mu(\mathbf{B}_{r}(y)) < + \infty$$

for all points *x* and *y* of *X* and all 0 \< *r* \< +∞, where

$$\mathbf{B}_{r}(x) := \{ z \in X | d(x, z) < r \}.$$

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Christensen's lemma"}

## Christensen's lemma {#Christensen's_lemma}

As it turns out, uniformly distributed measures are very rigid objects. On any "decent" metric space, the uniformly distributed measures form a one-parameter linearly dependent family:

Let *μ* and *ν* be uniformly distributed Borel regular measures on a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) metric space (*X*, *d*). Then there is a constant *c* such that *μ* = *cν*.

:::
