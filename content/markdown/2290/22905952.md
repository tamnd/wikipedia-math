# Spherical measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spherical measure"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) — specifically, in [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694) — **spherical measure** *σ*<sup>*n*</sup> is the "natural" [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) on the [*n*-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) **S**<sup>*n*</sup>. Spherical measure is often normalized so that it is a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) on the sphere, i.e. so that *σ*<sup>*n*</sup>(**S**<sup>*n*</sup>) = 1.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition of spherical measure"}

## Definition of spherical measure {#Definition_of_spherical_measure}

There are several ways to define spherical measure. One way is to use the usual "round" or "[arclength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arclength)" [metric](wikipedia://en/20018) *ρ*<sub>*n*</sub> on **S**<sup>*n*</sup>; that is, for points *x* and *y* in **S**<sup>*n*</sup>, *ρ*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*, *y*) is defined to be the (Euclidean) angle that they subtend at the centre of the sphere (the origin of **R**<sup>*n*+1</sup>). Now construct *n*-dimensional [Hausdorff measure](wikipedia://en/504109) *H*<sup>*n*</sup> on the metric space (**S**<sup>*n*</sup>, *ρ*<sub>*n*</sub>) and define

$$\sigma^{n} = \frac{1}{H^{n}(\mathbf{S}^{n})} H^{n}.$$

One could also have given **S**<sup>*n*</sup> the metric that it inherits as a subspace of the Euclidean space **R**<sup>*n*+1</sup>; the same spherical measure results from this choice of metric.

Another method uses [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) *λ*<sup>*n*+1</sup> on the ambient Euclidean space **R**<sup>*n*+1</sup>: for any measurable subset *A* of **S**<sup>*n*</sup>, define *σ*<sup>*n*</sup>(*A*) to be the (*n* + 1)-dimensional volume of the "wedge" in the ball **B**<sup>*n*+1</sup> that it subtends at the origin. That is,

$$\sigma^{n}(A) := \frac{1}{\alpha(n + 1)} \lambda^{n + 1} ( \{ t x \mid x \in A, t \in [0, 1] \} ),$$

where

$\alpha(m) := \lambda^{m} (\mathbf{B}_{1}^{m} (0)) \text{ } (\mathbf{B}_1^{m} (0) \text{ is the ball of radius 1 centered at the origin in }$**R**<sup>*n*+1</sup>).

The fact that all these methods define the same measure on **S**<sup>*n*</sup> follows from an elegant result of Christensen: all these measures are obviously [uniformly distributed](wikipedia://en/22906120) on **S**<sup>*n*</sup>, and any two uniformly distributed Borel regular measures on a separable metric space must be constant (positive) multiples of one another. Since all our candidate *σ*<sup>*n*</sup>'s have been normalized to be probability measures, they are all the same measure.

:::

## Relationship with other measures {#Relationship_with_other_measures}

The relationship of spherical measure to Hausdorff measure on the sphere and Lebesgue measure on the ambient space has already been discussed.

Spherical measure has a nice relationship to [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) on the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954). Let O(*n*) denote the orthogonal group [acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on **R**<sup>*n*</sup> and let *θ*<sup>*n*</sup> denote its normalized Haar measure (so that *θ*<sup>*n*</sup>(O(*n*)) = 1). The orthogonal group also acts on the sphere **S**<sup>*n*−1</sup>. Then, for any *x* ∈ **S**<sup>*n*−1</sup> and any *A* ⊆ **S**<sup>*n*−1</sup>,

$$\theta^{n}(\{ g \in \mathrm{O}(n) \mid g(x) \in A \}) = \sigma^{n - 1}(A).$$

In the case that **S**<sup>*n*</sup> is a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) (that is, when *n* is 0, 1 or 3), spherical measure *σ*<sup>*n*</sup> coincides with (normalized) Haar measure on **S**<sup>*n*</sup>.

::: {#o6 .statement cue=named-heading name="Isoperimetric inequality"}

## Isoperimetric inequality {#Isoperimetric_inequality}

There is an [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182) for the sphere with its usual metric and spherical measure (see Ledoux & Talagrand, chapter 1):

If *A* ⊆ **S**<sup>*n*−1</sup> is any Borel set and *B*⊆ **S**<sup>*n*−1</sup> is a *ρ*<sub>*n*</sub>-ball with the same *σ*<sup>*n*</sup>-measure as *A*, then, for any *r* > 0,

$$\sigma^{n}(A_{r}) \geq \sigma^{n}(B_{r}),$$

where *A*<sub>*r*</sub> denotes the "inflation" of *A* by *r*, i.e.

$$A_{r} := \{ x \in \mathbf{S}^{n} \mid \rho_{n}(x, A) \leq r \}.$$

In particular, if *σ*<sup>*n*</sup>(*A*) ≥ $\tfrac{1}{2}$ and *n* ≥ 2, then

$$\sigma^{n}(A_{r}) \geq 1 - \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}} \, \exp \left( - \frac{(n - 1) r^{2}}{2} \right).$$

:::
