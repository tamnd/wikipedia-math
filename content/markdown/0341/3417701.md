# Frank Nelson Cole

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frank Nelson Cole"}

**Frank Nelson Cole** (September 20, 1861 – May 26, 1926) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is most famous for discovering the factors of the [Mersenne number](wikipedia://en/18908) 2<sup>67</sup> − 1.

:::

## Life and works {#Life_and_works}

Cole was born in [Ashland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashland,_Massachusetts), [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts). When he was very young, the family moved to Marlborough, Massachusetts where he attended school and graduated from [Marlborough High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlborough_High_School_%28Massachusetts%29). He attended [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) from 1878, graduating in 1882. From 1883 to 1885 he studied at [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University) under [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) on a fellowship. He returned to Harvard, presented his thesis, and lectured on [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) there from until 1887.

Later, he was employed at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) (from 1888 to 1895) and [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University)[^o4] (from 1895 until his death in 1926). Professor Cole became the Secretary of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 1895 and an editor of the [Bulletin of the AMS](wikipedia://en/2668304) in 1897.

Cole published a number of important papers, including *The Diurnal Variation of Barometric Pressure* (1892).[^o4] In 1893 in Chicago, his paper *On a Certain Simple Group* (the group is [PSL(2,8)](wikipedia://en/382667)) was read (but not by him) at the International Mathematical Congress held in connection with the [World's Columbian Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Columbian_Exposition).[^o5][^o6][^o7]

On October 31, 1903, Cole famously made a presentation to a meeting of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) where he identified the factors of the [Mersenne number](wikipedia://en/18908) 2<sup>67</sup> − 1, or M<sub>67</sub>.[^o8] [Édouard Lucas](wikipedia://en/150102) had demonstrated in 1876 that M<sub>67</sub> must have factors (i.e., is not prime), but he was unable to determine what those factors were. During Cole's so-called "lecture", he approached the chalkboard and in complete silence proceeded to calculate the value of M<sub>67</sub>, with the result being 147,573,952,589,676,412,927. Cole then moved to the other side of the board and wrote 193,707,721 × 761,838,257,287, and worked through the calculations by hand. Upon completing the multiplication and demonstrating that the result equaled M<sub>67</sub>, Cole returned to his seat, not having uttered a word during the hour-long presentation. His audience greeted the presentation with a standing ovation. Cole later admitted that finding the factors had taken "three years of Sundays".[^o9]

Cole died in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City), aged 64. The American Mathematical Society's [Cole Prizes](wikipedia://en/1372177) in Algebra and Number Theory are named in his honor.
