# Michael Viscardi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Viscardi"}

**Michael Anthony Viscardi** (born February 22, 1989, in [Plano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plano,_Texas), [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas)) of [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego,_California), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California), is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who, as a highschooler, won the 2005 [Siemens Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_Competition) and [Davidson Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidson_Fellow) with a mathematical project on the [Dirichlet problem](wikipedia://en/571109), whose applications include describing the flow of heat across a metal surface, winning \$100,000 and \$50,000 in scholarships, respectively.[^o3][^o4] Viscardi's theorem is an expansion of the 19th-century work of [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002).[^o5] He was also named a finalist with the same project in the [Intel Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Science_Talent_Search). Viscardi placed Best of Category in Mathematics at the International Science and Engineering Fair ([ISEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISEF)) in May 2006. Viscardi also qualified for the [United States of America Mathematical Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America_Mathematical_Olympiad) and the Junior Science and Humanities Symposium.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Viscardi was [homeschooled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeschooling) for high school, supplemented with mathematics classes at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego).[^o7][^o8] He is also a pianist and violinist, and onetime concertmaster of the San Diego Youth Symphony.[^o8]

Viscardi is a member of the [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College) class of 2010.[^o9] He graduated summa cum laude from Harvard, receiving a 2010 [Thomas T. Hoopes, Class of 1919, Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_T._Hoopes,_Class_of_1919,_Prize), and earning the 2011 [Morgan Prize](wikipedia://en/4180157) honorable mention for his senior thesis "Alternate Compactifications of the Moduli Space of Genus One Maps".[^o10] He worked as a postdoc at [UC Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Berkeley) from 2016 to 2018.[^o11]
