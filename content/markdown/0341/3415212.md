# Callan–Symanzik equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Callan–Symanzik equation"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the **Callan–Symanzik equation** is a [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309) describing the evolution of the [*n*-point correlation functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_function_%28quantum_field_theory%29) under variation of the energy scale at which the theory is defined and involves the [beta function](wikipedia://en/1037781) of the theory and the anomalous dimensions.

:::

The Callan–Symanzik equation was discovered independently by [Curtis Callan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtis_Callan)[^o2] and [Kurt Symanzik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Symanzik)[^o3][^o4] in 1970. Later it was used to understand [asymptotic freedom](wikipedia://en/648008).

This equation arises in the framework of [renormalization group](wikipedia://en/291462). It is possible to treat the equation using [perturbation theory](wikipedia://en/297069).

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

As an example, for a [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) with one massless scalar field and one self-coupling term, denote the bare field strength by $\phi_0$ and the bare coupling constant by $g_0$. In the process of [renormalization](wikipedia://en/291453), a mass scale *M* must be chosen. Depending on *M*, the field strength is rescaled by a constant: $\phi = Z\phi_0$, and as a result the bare coupling constant $g_0$ is correspondingly shifted to the renormalised coupling constant *g*.

Of physical importance are the renormalised *n*-point functions, computed from connected [Feynman diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_diagrams), schematically of the form

$$G^{(n)}(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n;M,g) = \langle \phi(x_1)\phi(x_2)\cdots\phi(x_n)\rangle$$

For a given choice of renormalisation scheme, the computation of this quantity depends on the choice of *M*, which affects the shift in *g* and the rescaling of $\phi$. If the choice of $M$ is slightly altered by $\delta M$, then the following shifts will occur:

$$M\to M + \delta M$$

$$g\to g + \delta g$$

$$\phi = Z\phi_0 \to Z'\phi_0 = (1+\delta\eta)\phi$$

$$G^{(n)} \to (1+n\,\delta\eta)G^{(n)}$$

The Callan–Symanzik equation relates these shifts:

$$n\,\delta\eta G^{(n)} = \frac{\partial G^{(n)}}{\partial M}\delta M + \frac{\partial G^{(n)}}{\partial g}\delta g$$

After the following definitions

$$\beta = \frac{M}{\delta M}\delta g$$

$$\gamma = -\frac{M}{\delta M}\delta\eta$$

the Callan–Symanzik equation can be put in the conventional form:

$$\left[M\frac{\partial }{\partial M}+\beta(g)\frac{\partial }{\partial g}+n\gamma\right] G^{(n)}(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n;M,g)=0$$

$\beta(g)$ being the [beta function](wikipedia://en/1037781).

In [quantum electrodynamics](wikipedia://en/25268) this equation takes the form

$$\left[M\frac{\partial }{\partial M}+\beta(e)\frac{\partial }{\partial e}+n\gamma_2 +m\gamma_3\right]G^{(n,m)}(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n;y_1,y_2,\ldots,y_m;M,e)=0$$

where *n* and *m* are the numbers of [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron) and [photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon) fields, respectively, for which the correlation function $G^{(n,m)}$ is defined. The renormalised coupling constant is now the renormalised [elementary charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_charge) *e*. The electron field and the photon field rescale differently under renormalisation, and thus lead to two separate functions, $\gamma_2$ and $\gamma_3$, respectively.
