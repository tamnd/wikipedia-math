# Inserter category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inserter category"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **inserter category** is a variation of the [comma category](wikipedia://en/840950) where the two functors are required to have the same domain category.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If *C* and *D* are two categories and *F* and *G* are two functors from *C* to *D*, the inserter category Ins(*F*, *G*) is the category whose objects are pairs (*X*, *f*) where *X* is an object of *C* and *f* is a morphism in *D* from *F*(*X*) to *G*(*X*) and whose morphisms from (*X*, *f*) to (*Y*, *g*) are morphisms *h* in *C* from *X* to *Y* such that $G(h) \circ f = g \circ F(h)$.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

If *C* and *D* are [locally presentable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_presentable_category), *F* and *G* are functors from *C* to *D*, and either *F* is [cocontinuous](wikipedia://en/39248#Preservation_of_limits) or *G* is [continuous](wikipedia://en/39248#Preservation_of_limits); then the inserter category Ins(*F*, *G*) is also locally presentable.[^o7]

:::
