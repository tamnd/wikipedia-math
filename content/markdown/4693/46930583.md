# José F. Escobar

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="José F. Escobar"}

**José Fernando "Chepe" Escobar** (born 20 December 1954, in [Manizales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manizales), [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia)) was a Colombian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his work on [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations). He was a professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).[^o3][^o4] He contributed to the solution of the [Yamabe problem](wikipedia://en/11757994) on [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) with [boundary](wikipedia://en/2073470#Manifold_with_boundary).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

He completed his mathematical undergraduate program at [Universidad del Valle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Valle), Colombia. He received a scholarship that permitted him to do a master in science studies at the [Instituto Nacional de Matemática Pura e Aplicada](wikipedia://en/2334708) (IMPA) in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.[^o6]

Escobar obtained his Ph.D. from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1986, under the supervision of [Richard Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen). In his thesis he solved the problem known as the "boundary [Yamabe problem](wikipedia://en/11757994)", that had been previously settled only for the case of [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) without boundary.[^o4]

He died from cancer on 3 January 2004, at the age 49.[^o4]

Mathematician [Fernando Codá Marques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Codá_Marques) was a Ph.D. student of his.[^o7]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Among the awards he received for his work are the Alfred P. Sloan Doctoral Dissertation Fellowship (1985-86) and the [Presidential Faculty Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Young_Investigator_Award#NSF_Presidential_Faculty_Fellowship) (1992).[^o9]

In 2016, the [Colombian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/76532939) established the *Premio José Fernando Escobar* for investigation in mathematics.[^o10]
