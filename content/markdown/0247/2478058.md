# Kummer–Vandiver conjecture

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Kummer–Vandiver conjecture"}

**Kummer–Vandiver conjecture.**

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kummer–Vandiver conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Kummer–Vandiver conjecture**, or **Vandiver conjecture**, states that a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$ does not divide the [class number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_number_%28number_theory%29) $h_K$ of the maximal real [subfield](wikipedia://en/11634) $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)^+$ of the $p$-th [cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462). The [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) was first made by [Ernst Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117), on 28 December 1849 and 24 April 1853 in letters to [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450),[^n1] and independently rediscovered around 1920 by [Philipp Furtwängler](wikipedia://en/895349) and [Harry Vandiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Vandiver).[^n2]

:::

As of 2011, there is no particularly strong evidence either for or against the conjecture and it is unclear whether it is true or false, though it is likely that [counterexamples](wikipedia://en/143151) are very rare.

## Background {#Background}

The class number $h$ of the cyclotomic field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ is a product of two [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $h_1$ and $h_2$, called the first and second factors of the class number, where $h_2$ is the class number of the maximal real subfield $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)^+$ of the $p$-th cyclotomic field. The first factor $h_1$ is well understood and can be computed easily in terms of [Bernoulli numbers](wikipedia://en/4964), and is usually rather large. The second factor $h_2$ is not well understood and is hard to compute explicitly, and in the cases when it has been computed it is usually small.

Kummer showed that if a prime $p$ does not divide the class number $h$, then [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) holds for exponent $p$.

The Kummer–Vandiver conjecture states that $p$ does not divide the second factor $h_2$. Kummer showed that if $p$ divides the second factor, then it also divides the first factor. In particular the Kummer–Vandiver conjecture holds for [regular primes](wikipedia://en/323689) (those for which $p$ does not divide the first factor).

## Evidence for and against the Kummer–Vandiver conjecture {#Evidence_for_and_against_the_Kummer–Vandiver_conjecture}

Kummer verified the Kummer–Vandiver conjecture for $p$ less than 200, and Vandiver extended this to $p$ less than 600. [Buhler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Buhler), [Crandall](wikipedia://en/9210048) et al. verified it for *p* \< 12000000.[^n3] Buhler and Harvey extended this to primes less than 163000000,[^n4] and Hart, Harvey, and Ong extended this to primes less than 2<sup>31</sup>.[^n5]

[Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_C._Washington) describes an informal probability argument,[^n6] based on rather dubious assumptions about the equidistribution of class numbers modulo $p$, suggesting that the number of primes less than $x$ that are exceptions to the Kummer–Vandiver conjecture might grow like $(\log\log x)/2$. This grows extremely slowly, and suggests that the computer calculations do not provide much evidence for Vandiver's conjecture: for example, the probability argument (combined with the calculations for small primes) suggests that one should only expect about 1 counterexample in the first 10<sup>100</sup> primes, suggesting that it is unlikely any counterexample will be found by further brute force searches even if there are an infinite number of exceptions.

[Schoof](wikipedia://en/35745797) gave conjectural calculations of the class numbers of real cyclotomic fields for primes up to 10000, which strongly suggest that the class numbers are not randomly distributed mod $p$.[^n7] They tend to be quite small and are often just $1$. For example, assuming the [generalized Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/152567), the class number of the real cyclotomic field for the prime $p$ is $1$ for $p<163$, and divisible by $4$ for $p=163$. This suggests that Washington's informal probability argument against the conjecture may be misleading.

Mihăilescu gave a refined version of Washington's heuristic argument, suggesting that the Kummer–Vandiver conjecture is probably true.[^n8]

:::

## Consequences of the Kummer–Vandiver conjecture {#Consequences_of_the_Kummer–Vandiver_conjecture}

Kurihara showed that the conjecture is equivalent to a statement in the [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) of the integers, namely that $K_n(\mathbb{Z})=0$ whenever $n$ is a multiple of $4$.[^n9][^n10] In fact, from the Kummer–Vandiver conjecture and the [norm residue isomorphism theorem](wikipedia://en/27126857) follows a full conjectural calculation of the $K$-groups for all values of $n$; see [Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture](wikipedia://en/31619812) for details.

:::
