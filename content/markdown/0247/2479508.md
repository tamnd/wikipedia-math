# Helmholtz theorem (classical mechanics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helmholtz theorem"}

The **Helmholtz theorem of classical mechanics** reads as follows:

:::

Let

$$H(x,p;V) = K(p) + \varphi(x;V)$$

be the [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/14381) of a one-dimensional system, where

$$K = \frac{p^2}{2m}$$

is the [kinetic energy](wikipedia://en/17327) and

$$\varphi(x;V)$$

is a "U-shaped" [potential energy](wikipedia://en/23703) profile which depends on a parameter $V$. Let $\left\langle \cdot \right\rangle _{t}$ denote the time average. Let

- $$E = K + \varphi,$$
- $$T = 2\left\langle K\right\rangle _{t},$$
- $$P = \left\langle -\frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial V}\right\rangle _{t},$$
- $$S(E,V)=\log \oint \sqrt{2m\left( E-\varphi \left( x,V\right) \right) }\,dx.$$

Then

$$dS = \frac{dE+PdV}{T}.$$

## Remarks {#Remarks}

The thesis of this theorem of [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586) reads exactly as the [heat theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_theorem) of [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/29952). This fact shows that thermodynamic-like relations exist between certain mechanical quantities. This in turn allows to define the "[thermodynamic state](wikipedia://en/2747182)" of a one-dimensional mechanical system. In particular the [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050) $T$ is given by time average of the kinetic energy, and the [entropy](wikipedia://en/9891) $S$ by the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) of the [action](wikipedia://en/312881) (i.e., $\oint dx \sqrt{2m\left( E - \varphi \left( x, V\right) \right) }$). The importance of this theorem has been recognized by [Ludwig Boltzmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Boltzmann) who saw how to apply it to macroscopic systems (i.e. multidimensional systems), in order to provide a mechanical foundation of [equilibrium thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_thermodynamics). This research activity was strictly related to his formulation of the [ergodic hypothesis](wikipedia://en/258980). A multidimensional version of the Helmholtz theorem, based on the [ergodic theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic_theorem) of [George David Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/30871906) is known as the [generalized Helmholtz theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Helmholtz_theorem).

## Generalized version {#Generalized_version}

The **generalized Helmholtz theorem** is the multi-dimensional generalization of the Helmholtz theorem, and reads as follows.

Let

$$\mathbf{p}=(p_1,p_2,...,p_s),$$

$$\mathbf{q}=(q_1,q_2,...,q_s),$$

be the [canonical coordinates](wikipedia://en/706311) of a *s*-dimensional [Hamiltonian system](wikipedia://en/1197531), and let

$$H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q};V)=K(\mathbf{p})+\varphi(\mathbf{q};V)$$

be the [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/198319#Mathematical_formalism) function, where

$$K=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{p_i^2}{2m},$$

is the [kinetic energy](wikipedia://en/17327) and

$$\varphi(\mathbf{q};V)$$

is the [potential energy](wikipedia://en/23703) which depends on a parameter $V$. Let the hyper-surfaces of constant energy in the 2*s*-dimensional phase space of the system be **metrically indecomposable** and let $\left\langle \cdot \right\rangle_t$ denote time average. Define the quantities $E$, $P$, $T$, $S$, as follows:

$$E = K + \varphi,$$

$$T = \frac{2}{s}\left\langle K\right\rangle _{t},$$

$$P = \left\langle -\frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial V}\right\rangle _{t},$$

$$S(E,V) = \log \int_{H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q};V) \leq E} d^s\mathbf{p}d^s \mathbf{q}.$$

Then:

$$dS = \frac{dE+PdV}{T}.$$
