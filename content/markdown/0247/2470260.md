# Maris–McGwire–Sosa pair

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maris–McGwire–Sosa pair"}

In [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446), **Maris–McGwire–Sosa pairs** (**MMS pairs**, also **MMS numbers**)[^o2] are two consecutive [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) such that adding each number's digits (in [base 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_10)) to the digits of its [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorization) gives the same sum.

:::

Thus 61 → 6 + 1 (the sum of its digits) + 6 + 1 (since 61 is its prime factorization)

and 62 → 6 + 2 (the sum of its digits) + 3 + 1 + 2 (since 31 × 2 is its prime factorization).

The above two sums are equal (= 14), so 61 and 62 form an MMS pair.

MMS pairs are so named because in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Major_League_Baseball_home_run_record_chase) the [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball) players [Mark McGwire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_McGwire) and [Sammy Sosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sammy_Sosa) both hit their 62nd [home runs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_run) for the season, passing the old record of 61, held by [Roger Maris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Maris). American engineer [Mike Keith](wikipedia://en/5286832) noticed this property of these numbers and named pairs of numbers like these MMS pairs.[^o3]
