# Total sum of squares

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Total sum of squares"}

In [statistical data analysis](wikipedia://en/26685) the **total sum of squares** (**TSS** or **SST**) is a quantity that appears as part of a standard way of presenting results of such analyses. For a set of observations, $y_i, i\leq n$, it is defined as the sum over all squared differences between the observations and their overall [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) $\bar{y}$.:[^o2]

:::

$$\mathrm{TSS}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_{i}-\bar{y}\right)^2$$

For wide classes of [linear models](wikipedia://en/48758386), the total sum of squares equals the [explained sum of squares](wikipedia://en/2473431) plus the [residual sum of squares](wikipedia://en/2473303). For proof of this in the multivariate OLS case, see [partitioning in the general OLS model](wikipedia://en/2473431#Partitioning_in_the_general_OLS_model).

In [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634) (ANOVA) the total sum of squares is the sum of the so-called "within-samples" sum of squares and "between-samples" sum of squares, i.e., partitioning of the sum of squares. In [multivariate analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/682847) (MANOVA) the following equation applies[^o3]

$$\mathbf{T} = \mathbf{W} + \mathbf{B},$$

where **T** is the total sum of squares and products (SSP) [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859), **W** is the within-samples SSP matrix and **B** is the between-samples SSP matrix. Similar terminology may also be used in [linear discriminant analysis](wikipedia://en/1470657), where **W** and **B** are respectively referred to as the within-groups and between-groups SSP matrices.[^o3]
