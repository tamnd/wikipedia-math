# Abu Kamil

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abu Kamil"}

**Abū Kāmil Shujāʿ ibn Aslam ibn Muḥammad Ibn Shujāʿ** ([Latinized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinization_%28literature%29) as **Auoquamel**,[^o3] أبو كامل شجاع بن أسلم بن محمد بن شجاع, also known as ***Al-ḥāsib al-miṣrī***—lit. "The Egyptian Calculator") (c. 850 – c. 930) was a prominent [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptians) mathematician during the [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age). He is considered the first mathematician to systematically use and accept [irrational numbers](wikipedia://en/20647689) as solutions and [coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficients) to equations.[^o12] His mathematical techniques were later adopted by [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949), thus allowing Abu Kamil an important part in introducing algebra to [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe).[^o9]

:::

Abu Kamil made important contributions to [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).[^o15] He was the first [Islamic mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematician) to work easily with algebraic equations with powers higher than $x^2$ (up to $x^8$),[^o9][^o26] and solved sets of non-linear [simultaneous equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_equations) with three unknown [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109).[^o4] He illustrated the rules of signs for expanding the multiplication $(a \pm b)(c \pm d)$.[^o5] He wrote all problems rhetorically, and some of his books lacked any [mathematical notation](wikipedia://en/277184) beside those of integers. For example, he uses the Arabic expression "māl māl shayʾ" ("square-square-thing") for $x^5$ (as $x^5 = x^2\cdot x^2\cdot x$).[^o9][^o6] One notable feature of his works was enumerating all the possible solutions to a given equation.[^o21]

The Muslim [encyclopedist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopédistes) [Ibn Khaldūn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Khaldūn) classified Abū Kāmil as the second greatest algebraist chronologically after [al-Khwarizmi](wikipedia://en/649861).[^o7]

## Life {#Life}

Almost nothing is known about the life and career of Abu Kamil except that he was a successor of [al-Khwarizmi](wikipedia://en/649861), whom he never personally met.

## Works {#Works}

### *Book of Algebra (Kitāb fī al-jabr wa al-muqābala)* {#Book_of_Algebra_(Kitāb_fī_al-jabr_wa_al-muqābala)}

The *Algebra* is perhaps Abu Kamil's most influential work, which he intended to supersede and expand upon that of [Al-Khwarizmi](wikipedia://en/649861).[^o12][^o20] Whereas the [*Algebra* of al-Khwarizmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Compendious_Book_on_Calculation_by_Completion_and_Balancing) was geared towards the general public, Abu Kamil was addressing other mathematicians, or readers familiar with [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s *Elements*.[^o20] In this book Abu Kamil solves systems of [equations](wikipedia://en/9284) whose solutions are [whole numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) and [fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractions), and accepted [irrational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_numbers) (in the form of a [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) or [fourth root](wikipedia://en/235029)) as solutions and [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) to [quadratic equations](wikipedia://en/25175).[^o12]

The first chapter teaches algebra by solving problems of application to geometry, often involving an unknown variable and square roots. The second chapter deals with the [six types of problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Compendious_Book_on_Calculation_by_Completion_and_Balancing#The_book) found in Al-Khwarizmi's book,[^o21] but some of which, especially those of $x^2$, were now worked out directly instead of first solving for $x$ and accompanied with geometrical illustrations and proofs.[^o26][^o21] The third chapter contains examples of [quadratic irrationalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_irrationalities) as solutions and coefficients.[^o21] The fourth chapter shows how these irrationalities are used to solve problems involving [polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygons). The rest of the book contains solutions for sets of [indeterminate equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminate_equation), problems of application in realistic situations, and problems involving unrealistic situations intended for [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446).[^o21]

A number of Islamic mathematicians wrote commentaries on this work, including al-Iṣṭakhrī al-Ḥāsib and ʿAli ibn Aḥmad al-ʿImrānī (d. 955 or 956),[^o13] but both commentaries are now lost.[^o15]

In Europe, similar material to this book is found in the writings of [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949), and some sections were incorporated and improved upon in the Latin work of [John of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_Seville), *Liber mahameleth*.[^o21] A partial translation to Latin was done in the 14th century by William of Luna, and in the 15th century the whole work also appeared in a Hebrew translation by Mordekhai Finzi.[^o21]

### *Book of Rare Things in the Art of Calculation (Kitāb al-ṭarā’if fi’l-ḥisāb)* {#Book_of_Rare_Things_in_the_Art_of_Calculation_(Kitāb_al-ṭarā’if_fi’l-ḥisāb)}

Abu Kamil describes a number of systematic procedures for finding [integral solutions](wikipedia://en/15532) for [indeterminate equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminate_equation).[^o15] It is also the earliest known Arabic work where solutions are sought to the type of indeterminate equations found in [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110)'s *[Arithmetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetica)*. However, Abu Kamil explains certain methods not found in any extant copy of the *Arithmetica*.[^o9] He also describes one problem for which he found 2,678 solutions.[^o18]

### *On the Pentagon and Decagon (Kitāb al-mukhammas wa’al-mu‘ashshar)* {#On_the_Pentagon_and_Decagon_(Kitāb_al-mukhammas_wa’al-mu‘ashshar)}

In this treatise algebraic methods are used to solve geometrical problems.[^o15] Abu Kamil uses the equation $x^4 + 3125 = 125x^2$ to calculate a numerical approximation for the side of a regular [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014) in a circle of diameter 10.[^o17] He also uses the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) in some of his calculations.[^o18] [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949) knew about this treatise and made extensive use of it in his *Practica geometriae*.[^o15]

### *Book of Birds (Kitāb al-ṭair)* {#Book_of_Birds_(Kitāb_al-ṭair)}

A small treatise teaching how to solve indeterminate [linear systems](wikipedia://en/722503) with positive [integral solutions](wikipedia://en/15532).[^o20] The title is derived from a type of problems known in the east which involve the purchase of different species of birds. Abu Kamil wrote in the introduction:

> I found myself before a problem that I solved and for which I discovered a great many solutions; looking deeper for its solutions, I obtained two thousand six hundred and seventy-six correct ones. My astonishment about that was great, but I found out that, when I recounted this discovery, those who did not know me were arrogant, shocked, and suspicious of me. I thus decided to write a book on this kind of calculations, with the purpose of facilitating its treatment and making it more accessible.[^o20]

According to Jacques Sesiano, Abu Kamil remained seemingly unparalleled throughout the Middle Ages in trying to find all the possible solutions to some of his problems.[^o21]

### *On Measurement and Geometry (Kitāb al-misāḥa wa al-handasa)* {#On_Measurement_and_Geometry_(Kitāb_al-misāḥa_wa_al-handasa)}

A manual of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) for non-mathematicians, like land surveyors and other government officials, which presents a set of rules for calculating the volume and surface area of solids (mainly rectangular [parallelepipeds](wikipedia://en/23975), right circular [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455), and circular [cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_%28geometry%29)). The first few chapters contain rules for determining the [area](wikipedia://en/1209), [diagonal](wikipedia://en/349251), [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636), and other parameters for different types of triangles, rectangles and squares.[^o9]

### Lost works {#Lost_works}

Some of Abu Kamil's lost works include:

- A treatise on the use of double [false position](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_position), known as the *Book of the Two Errors* (*Kitāb al-khaṭaʾayn*).[^o24]
- *Book on Augmentation and Diminution* (*Kitāb al-jamʿ wa al-tafrīq*), which gained more attention after historian [Franz Woepcke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Woepcke) linked it with an anonymous Latin work, *Liber augmenti et diminutionis*.[^o15]
- *Book of Estate Sharing using Algebra* (*Kitāb al-waṣāyā bi al-jabr wa al-muqābala*), which contains algebraic solutions for problems of [Islamic inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_inheritance_jurisprudence) and discusses the opinions of known [jurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_jurisprudence).[^o21]

[Ibn al-Nadim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_al-Nadim) in his *[Fihrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fihrist)* listed the following additional titles: *Book of Fortune* (*Kitāb al-falāḥ*), *Book of the Key to Fortune* (*Kitāb miftāḥ al-falāḥ*), *Book of the Adequate* (*Kitāb al-kifāya*), and *Book of the Kernel* (*Kitāb al-ʿasīr*).[^o26]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The works of Abu Kamil influenced other mathematicians, like [al-Karaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Karaji) and [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949), and as such had a lasting impact on the development of algebra.[^o26][^o27] Many of his examples and algebraic techniques were later copied by Fibonacci in his *Practica geometriae* and other works.[^o26][^o18] Unmistakable borrowings, but without Abu Kamil being explicitly mentioned and perhaps mediated by lost treatises, are also found in Fibonacci's *[Liber Abaci](wikipedia://en/167699)*.[^o28]

## On al-Khwarizmi {#On_al-Khwarizmi}

Abu Kamil was one of the earliest mathematicians to recognize [al-Khwarizmi](wikipedia://en/649861)'s contributions to [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), defending him against Ibn Barza who attributed the authority and precedent in algebra to his grandfather, ['Abd al-Hamīd ibn Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Abd_al-Hamīd_ibn_Turk).[^o9] Abu Kamil wrote in the introduction of his *Algebra*:

> I have studied with great attention the writings of the mathematicians, examined their assertions, and scrutinized what they explain in their works; I thus observed that the book by Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī known as *Algebra* is superior in the accuracy of its principle and the exactness of its argumentation. It thus behooves us, the community of mathematicians, to recognize his priority and to admit his knowledge and his superiority, as in writing his book on algebra he was an initiator and the discoverer of its principles...[^o20]
