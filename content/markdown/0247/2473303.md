# Residual sum of squares

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Residual sum of squares"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **residual sum of squares** (**RSS**), also known as the **sum of squared residuals** (**SSR**) or the **sum of squared estimate of errors** (**SSE**), is the [sum](wikipedia://en/246160) of the [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28arithmetic%29) of [residuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors_and_residuals_in_statistics) (deviations predicted from actual empirical values of data). It is a measure of the discrepancy between the data and an estimation model, such as a [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386). A small RSS indicates a tight fit of the model to the data. It is used as an [optimality criterion](wikipedia://en/15015787) in parameter selection and [model selection](wikipedia://en/3664073).

:::

In general, [total sum of squares](wikipedia://en/2473449) = [explained sum of squares](wikipedia://en/2473431) + residual sum of squares. For a proof of this in the multivariate [ordinary least squares](wikipedia://en/1651906) (OLS) case, see [partitioning in the general OLS model](wikipedia://en/2473431#Partitioning_in_the_general_ordinary_least_squares_model).

## One explanatory variable {#One_explanatory_variable}

In a model with a single explanatory variable, RSS is given by:[^o3]

$$\operatorname{RSS} = \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - f(x_i)\right)^2$$

where *y*<sub>*i*</sub> is the *i*<sup>th</sup> value of the variable to be predicted, *x*<sub>*i*</sub> is the *i*<sup>th</sup> value of the explanatory variable, and $f(x_i)$ is the predicted value of *y*<sub>*i*</sub> (also termed $\hat{y_i}$). In a standard linear simple [regression model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regression_model), $y_i = \alpha + \beta x_i+\varepsilon_i\,$, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203), *y* and *x* are the [regressand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regressand) and the [regressor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regressor), respectively, and ε is the [error term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors_and_residuals_in_statistics). The sum of squares of residuals is the sum of squares of $\widehat{\varepsilon\,}_i$; that is

$$\operatorname{RSS} = \sum_{i=1}^n \left(\widehat{\varepsilon}_i\right)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - (\widehat{\alpha\,} + \widehat{\beta}\, x_i)\right)^2$$

where $\widehat{\alpha\,}$ is the estimated value of the constant term $\alpha$ and $\widehat{\beta\,}$ is the estimated value of the slope coefficient $\beta$.

## Matrix expression for the OLS residual sum of squares {#Matrix_expression_for_the_OLS_residual_sum_of_squares}

The general regression model with $n$ observations and $k$ explanators, the first of which is a constant unit vector whose coefficient is the regression intercept, is

$$y = X \beta + e$$

where $y$ is an *n* × 1 vector of dependent variable observations, each column of the *n* × *k* matrix $X$ is a vector of observations on one of the *k* explanators, $\beta$ is a *k* × 1 vector of true coefficients, and $e$ is an *n*× 1 vector of the true underlying errors. The [ordinary least squares](wikipedia://en/1651906) estimator for $\beta$ is

$$\begin{aligned} &X \hat \beta = y \\[1ex] \iff & X^\operatorname{T} X \hat \beta = X^\operatorname{T} y \\[1ex] \iff & \hat \beta = \left(X^\operatorname{T} X\right)^{-1}X^\operatorname{T} y. \end{aligned}$$

The residual vector $\hat e = y - X \hat \beta = y - X (X^\operatorname{T} X)^{-1}X^\operatorname{T} y$; so the residual sum of squares is:

$$\operatorname{RSS} = \hat e ^\operatorname{T} \hat e = \left\| \hat e \right\|^2 ,$$

(equivalent to the square of the [norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_norm) of residuals). In full:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{RSS} &= y^\operatorname{T} y - y^\operatorname{T} X \left(X^\operatorname{T} X\right)^{-1} X^\operatorname{T} y \\[1ex] &= y^\operatorname{T} \left[I - X \left(X^\operatorname{T} X\right)^{-1} X^\operatorname{T}\right] y \\[1ex] &= y^\operatorname{T} \left[I - H\right] y, \end{aligned}$$

where $H$ is the [hat matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat_matrix), or the projection matrix in linear regression.

## Relation with Pearson's product-moment correlation {#Relation_with_Pearson's_product-moment_correlation}

The [least-squares regression line](wikipedia://en/82359) is given by

$$y = ax + b,$$

where $b=\bar{y}-a\bar{x}$ and $a=\frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}}$, where $S_{xy}=\sum_{i=1}^n(\bar{x}-x_i)(\bar{y}-y_i)$ and $S_{xx}=\sum_{i=1}^n(\bar{x}-x_i)^2.$

Therefore,

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{RSS} & = \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - f(x_i)\right)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - (ax_i+b)\right)^2 \\[1ex] &= \sum_{i=1}^n \left(y_i - ax_i-\bar{y} + a\bar{x}\right)^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \left[a\left(\bar{x} - x_i\right) - \left(\bar{y} - y_i\right)\right]^2 \\[1ex] &= a^2 S_{xx} - 2aS_{xy} + S_{yy} = S_{yy} - aS_{xy} \\[1ex] &=S_{yy} \left(1 - \frac{S_{xy}^2}{S_{xx} S_{yy}} \right) \end{aligned}$$

where $S_{yy}=\sum_{i=1}^n (\bar{y}-y_i)^2 .$

The [Pearson product-moment correlation](wikipedia://en/221708) is given by $r=\frac{S_{xy}}{\sqrt{S_{xx}S_{yy}}};$ therefore, $\operatorname{RSS}=S_{yy}(1-r^2).$
