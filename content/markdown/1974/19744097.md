# Sérsic profile

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sérsic profile"}

The **Sérsic profile** (or **Sérsic model** or **Sérsic's law**) is a mathematical function that describes how the [intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiance#Intensity) $I$ of a [galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy) varies with distance $R$ from its center. It is a generalization of [de Vaucouleurs' law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Vaucouleurs'_law). [José Luis Sérsic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Luis_Sérsic) first published his law in 1963.[^o2]

:::

*Image: Sérsic models with different (n) indices.jpg.* Sérsic models with different indices $n$. The order of $n$ is reversed for large radii.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Sérsic profile has the form

$$\ln I(R) = \ln I_{0} - k R^{1/n} ,$$

or

$$I(R) = I_0 \exp{\!\left(-k R^{1/n}\right)},$$

where $I_{0}$ is the [intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_brightness) at $R = 0$. The parameter $n$, called the "Sérsic index," controls the degree of curvature of the profile (see figure). The smaller the value of $n$, the less centrally concentrated the profile is and the shallower (steeper) the logarithmic slope at small (large) radii is. The equation for describing this is:

$$\frac{\mathrm{d} \ln I}{\mathrm{d} \ln R} = -(k/n)\ R^{1/n} .$$

Today, it is more common to write this function in terms of the [half-light radius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-light_radius), R<sub>e</sub>, and the intensity at that radius, I<sub>e</sub>, such that

$$I(R)=I_e \exp\left\{ -b_n\left[ \left( \frac{R}{R_e}\right) ^{1/n} -1\right] \right\},$$

where $b_n$ is approximately $2n-1/3$ for $n>8$. $b_n$ can also be approximated to be $2n - 1/3 + \frac{4}{405 n} + \frac{46}{25515 n^2} + \frac{131}{1148175 n^3} - \frac{2194697}{30690717750 n^4}$, for $n > 0.36$.[^o5] It can be shown that $b_n$ satisfies $\gamma(2n; b_n) = \frac{1}{2} \Gamma(2n)$, where $\Gamma$ and $\gamma$ are respectively the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) and lower [incomplete Gamma function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incomplete_Gamma_function). Many related expressions, in terms of the surface brightness, also exist.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

*Image: Messier 87 Hubble WikiSky.jpg.* Massive elliptical galaxies have high Sérsic indices and a high degree of central concentration. This galaxy, M87, has a Sérsic index n~ 4.[^o9]

*Image: M33HunterWilson09.jpg.* Discs of spiral galaxies, such as the [Triangulum Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangulum_Galaxy), have low Sérsic indices and a low degree of central concentration.

Most galaxies are fit by Sérsic profiles with indices in the range 1/2 \< *n* \< 10. The best-fit value of *n* correlates with galaxy size and luminosity, such that bigger and brighter galaxies tend to be fit with larger *n*.[^o10][^o11] Setting $n = 4$ gives the [de Vaucouleurs profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Vaucouleurs_profile):

$$I(R) \propto e^{-bR^{1/4}}$$

which is a rough approximation of ordinary [elliptical galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptical_galaxies). Setting $n = 1$ gives the exponential profile:

$$I(R) \propto e^{-bR}$$

which is a good approximation of [spiral galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral_galaxy) disks and a rough approximation of [dwarf elliptical galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_elliptical_galaxies). The correlation of Sérsic index (i.e. galaxy concentration[^o12]) with galaxy morphology is sometimes used in automated schemes to determine the [Hubble type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_type) of distant galaxies.[^o13] Sérsic indices have also been shown to correlate with the mass of the [supermassive black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermassive_black_hole) at the centers of the galaxies.[^o14]

Sérsic profiles can also be used to describe [dark matter halos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter_halo), where the Sérsic index correlates with halo mass.[^o15][^o16]

:::

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations of the Sérsic profile"}

## Generalizations of the Sérsic profile {#Generalizations_of_the_Sérsic_profile}

The brightest elliptical galaxies often have low-density cores that are not well described by Sérsic's law. The [core-Sérsic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core-Sersic_model) family of models was introduced[^o19][^o20][^o21] to describe such galaxies. Core-Sérsic models have an additional set of parameters that describe the core.

[Dwarf elliptical galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_elliptical_galaxies) and [bulges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulge_%28astronomy%29) often have point-like [nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy_nucleus) that are also not well described by Sérsic's law. These galaxies are often fit by a Sérsic model with an added central component representing the nucleus.[^o22][^o23] [^o24]

The [Einasto profile](wikipedia://en/19767753) is mathematically identical to the Sérsic profile, except that $I$ is replaced by $\rho$, the volume density, and $R$ is replaced by $r$, the internal (not projected on the sky) distance from the center.
