# Alan M. Frieze

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alan M. Frieze"}

**Alan M. Frieze** (born 25 October 1945 in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_England), England) is a professor in the Department of Mathematical Sciences at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh), United States. He graduated from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) in 1966, and obtained his PhD from the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) in 1975. His research interests lie in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [discrete optimisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_optimisation) and [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392). Currently, he focuses on the probabilistic aspects of these areas; in particular, the study of the asymptotic properties of [random graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_graphs), the average case [analysis of algorithms](wikipedia://en/2230), and [randomised algorithms](wikipedia://en/495383). His recent work has included [approximate counting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximate_counting) and volume computation via [random walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_walks); finding edge disjoint paths in [expander graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expander_graphs), and exploring [anti-Ramsey theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Ramsey_theory) and the stability of [routing algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Routing_algorithms).

:::

## Key contributions {#Key_contributions}

Two key contributions made by Alan Frieze are:

(1) polynomial time algorithm for approximating the volume of [convex bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_bodies)

(2) algorithmic version for [Szemerédi regularity lemma](wikipedia://en/1962417)

Both these algorithms will be described briefly here.

### Polynomial time algorithm for approximating the volume of convex bodies {#Polynomial_time_algorithm_for_approximating_the_volume_of_convex_bodies}

The paper [^o4] is a joint work by [Martin Dyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Dyer), Alan Frieze and [Ravindran Kannan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravindran_Kannan).

The main result of the paper is a randomised algorithm for finding an $\epsilon$ approximation to the volume of a convex body $K$ in $n$-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) by assume the existence of a membership oracle. The algorithm takes time bounded by a polynomial in $n$, the dimension of $K$ and $1/\epsilon$.

The algorithm is a sophisticated usage of the so-called [Markov chain Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/236801) (MCMC) method. The basic scheme of the algorithm is a nearly uniform sampling from within $K$ by placing a grid consisting *n*-dimensional cubes and doing a random walk over these cubes. By using the theory of [rapidly mixing Markov chains](wikipedia://en/6101938), they show that it takes a polynomial time for the random walk to settle down to being a nearly uniform distribution.

### Algorithmic version for Szemerédi regularity partition {#Algorithmic_version_for_Szemerédi_regularity_partition}

This paper [^o6] is a combined work by Alan Frieze and [Ravindran Kannan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravindran_Kannan). They use two lemmas to derive the algorithmic version of the [Szemerédi regularity lemma](wikipedia://en/1962417) to find an $\epsilon$-regular partition.

**Lemma 1:** Fix k and $\gamma$ and let $G=(V,E)$ be a graph with $n$ vertices. Let $P$ be an equitable partition of $V$ in classes $V_0, V_1, \ldots ,V_k$. Assume $|V_1| > 4^{2k}$ and $4^k >600 \gamma ^2$. Given proofs that more than $\gamma k^2$ pairs $(V_r,V_s)$ are not $\gamma$-regular, it is possible to find in O(n) time an equitable partition $P'$ (which is a refinement of $P$) into $1+k4^k$ classes, with an exceptional class of cardinality at most $|V_0|+n/4^k$ and such that $\operatorname{ind}(P')\geq \operatorname{ind}(P) + \gamma^5/20$

**Lemma 2:** Let $W$ be a $R \times C$ matrix with $|R|=p$, $|C|=q$ and $\|W\|_\inf\leq1$ and $\gamma$ be a positive real. (a) If there exist $S \subseteq R$, $T \subseteq C$ such that $|S|\geq\gamma p$, $|T|\geq\gamma q$ and $|W(S,T)|\geq\gamma |S||T|$ then $\sigma_1(W)\geq\gamma^3\sqrt{pq}$ (b) If $\sigma_1(W)\geq\gamma\sqrt{pq}$, then there exist $S\subseteq R$, $T\subseteq C$ such that $|S|\geq\gamma'p$, $|T|\geq\gamma'q$ and $W(S,T)\geq\gamma'|S||T|$ where $\gamma'=\gamma^3/108$. Furthermore, $S$, $T$ can be constructed in polynomial time.

These two lemmas are combined in the following algorithmic construction of the [Szemerédi regularity lemma](wikipedia://en/1962417).

**\[Step 1\]** Arbitrarily divide the vertices of $G$ into an equitable partition $P_1$ with classes $V_0,V_1,\ldots,V_b$ where $|V_i|\lfloor n/b \rfloor$ and hence $|V_0|<b$. denote $k_1=b$. **\[Step 2\]** For every pair $(V_r,V_s)$ of $P_i$, compute $\sigma_1(W_{r,s})$. If the pair $(V_r,V_s)$ are not $\epsilon-$regular then by Lemma 2 we obtain a proof that they are not $\gamma=\epsilon^9/108-$regular. **\[Step 3\]** If there are at most $\epsilon \left( \begin{array}{c} k_1\\ 2 \\ \end{array} \right)$ pairs that produce proofs of non $\gamma-$regularity that halt. $P_i$ is $\epsilon-$regular. **\[Step 4\]** Apply Lemma 1 where $P=P_i$, $k=k_i$, $\gamma=\epsilon^9/108$ and obtain $P'$ with $1+k_i4^{k_i}$ classes **\[Step 5\]** Let $k_i+1 = k_i4^{k_i}$, $P_i+1=P'$, $i=i+1$ and go to Step 2.

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

- In 1991, Frieze received (jointly with [Martin Dyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Dyer) and [Ravi Kannan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_Kannan)) the [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916) in Discrete Mathematics awarded by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and the [Mathematical Programming Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Programming_Society). The award was for the paper "*A random polynomial time algorithm for approximating the volume of convex bodies*" in the *[Journal of the ACM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_ACM)*).
- In 1997 he was a Guggenheim Fellow.
- In 2000, he received the IBM Faculty Partnership Award.
- In 2006 he jointly received (with [Michael Krivelevich](wikipedia://en/39536536)) the Professor Pazy Memorial Research Award from the United States-Israel Binational Science Foundation.
- In 2011 he was selected as a SIAM Fellow.[^o8]
- In 2012 he was selected as an AMS Fellow.[^o9]
- In 2014 he gave a [plenary talk at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in Seoul, South Korea.
- In 2015 he was selected as a Simons Fellow.
- In 2017 he was promoted to University professor.
- In 2022 he became the Orion Hoch, S 1952 Professor.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Frieze is married to [Carol Frieze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Frieze), who directs two outreach efforts for the computer science department at Carnegie Mellon University.[^o11]
