# Hirsch–Plotkin radical

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hirsch–Plotkin radical"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the study of infinite [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), the **Hirsch–Plotkin radical** is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) describing the [normal](wikipedia://en/21918) [locally nilpotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_nilpotent_group) subgroups of the group. It was named by (Gruenberg 1961) after [Kurt Hirsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Hirsch) and Boris I. Plotkin, who [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that the join of normal locally nilpotent subgroups is locally nilpotent; this fact is the key ingredient in its construction.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

The Hirsch–Plotkin radical is defined as the subgroup generated by the [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of the normal locally nilpotent subgroups (that is, those normal subgroups such that every [finitely generated subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_generated_subgroup) is nilpotent). The Hirsch–Plotkin radical is itself a locally nilpotent normal subgroup, so is the unique largest such.[^o6] In a finite group, the Hirsch–Plotkin radical coincides with the [Fitting subgroup](wikipedia://en/1416042) but for infinite groups the two subgroups can differ.[^o7] The subgroup generated by the union of infinitely many normal nilpotent subgroups need not itself be nilpotent,[^o8] so the Fitting subgroup must be modified in this case.[^o9]
