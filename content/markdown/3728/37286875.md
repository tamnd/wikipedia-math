# Norman H. Anning

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Norman H. Anning"}

**Norman Herbert Anning** (1883-8-28 – 1963) was an American mathematician and academic. He is known for a proof of the characterization of [infinite sets](wikipedia://en/142679) of points in the plane with mutually [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) distances, known as the [Erdős–Anning theorem](wikipedia://en/12600251).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Anning was originally from Holland Township (currently [Chatsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatsworth,_Ontario)), [Grey County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_County), [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario), Canada. In 1902, he won a scholarship to [Queen's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston),[^o5] and received the Arts bachelor's degree in 1905, and the Arts master's degree in 1906 from the same institution.[^o5]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Anning served in the faculty of the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) since 1920, until he retired on 1953.[^o5][^o6]

From 1909 to 1910, he held a teaching position in the department of Mathematics and Science at [Chilliwack High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilliwack_Secondary_School), British Columbia.[^o5] Anning was appointed as chairperson at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) from 1951 to 1952,[^o7] and treasurer secretary from 1925 to 1926 at the same institution.[^o7]

The name of Norman Anning must certainly be familiar to every contributor to this department. He has been solving problems for this department for more years than its present editor has known of *School Science and Mathematics*.

With [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), he published a paper in 1945 containing what is now known as the [Erdős–Anning theorem](wikipedia://en/12600251). The theorem states that an infinite number of points in the plane can have mutual integer distances only if all the points lie on a straight line.[^o8]

Anning retired on August 28, 1953. He died in [Sunnydale, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnydale,_California) on May 1, 1963.[^o5]
