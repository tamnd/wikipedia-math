# Hans Jörg Stetter

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hans Jörg Stetter"}

**Hans Jörg Stetter** (8 April 1930 – 27 October 2025) was a German mathematician, specialising in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506). He taught at the [Technical University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Vienna) starting from 1965. He established the Institute of Analysis and Scientific Computing and the university's first computing centre. He published a pioneering book, *Numerical Polynomial Algebra*, in 2004.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Stetter was born in Munich on 8 April 1930.[^o9][^o4] He studied at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) and then at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich).[^o9] For one academic year, he was an undergraduate exchange student in [Fort Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Collins) at the [Colorado State College of Agriculture and Mechanic Arts, *i.e.* Colorado A&M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_State_University) (renamed, in 1957, Colorado State University), where he participated in the [Putnam competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_competition) and was awarded an honorable mention. After receiving a master's degree as a qualification for teaching in secondary school, he studied the numerical analysis of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) (PDEs) with applications to fluid dynamics and received his [promotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_%28Germany%29) from the Technical University of Munich under [Robert Max Friedrich Sauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Max_Friedrich_Sauer) with the 1956 doctoral dissertation *Beiträge zum Wechselwirkungsproblem in linearisierter Überschallströmung* (Contributions to the interaction problem in linearized supersonic flow),[^o4] devoted to hyperbolic partial differential equations in gas dynamics.[^o9] Stetter became in 1965 a professor ordinarius at the [Technical University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Vienna),[^o9][^o8] where he established the university’s first computing centre, and the field of computer science as an independent discipline at the university, including advocating for it on a federal level, leading to a change in national law regarding technical studies in 1969.[^o9][^o4][^o6]

In the 1960s, he turned to the numerical analysis of [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753) (ODEs)[^o9][^o8] and specialized in error analysis and asymptotic developments, among other ODE topics. Based upon ideas published by the physicist [Lewis Fry Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Fry_Richardson) and by the astronomer [Pedro E. Zadunaisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_E._Zadunaisky), Stetter developed in the 1970s an iterative method, now called the defect correction method, for error estimation in ODEs.[^o9] He also dealt with polynomial algebra at the interface between numerical analysis and computer algebra. His book *Numerical Polynomial Algebra* (2004) was the first in the field.[^o8]

In 1974, Stetter was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in Vancouver.[^o7] In 1984 he was elected a member of the [Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina).[^o8]

Stetter taught generations of students, in a style described as clear in methodology, uniting "ideas, formal rigor, and intuition".[^o9][^o4] After retirement, he remained connected to the Institute of Analysis and Scientific Computing as a regular guest at events.[^o9]

Stetter died on 27 October 2025, at the age of 95.[^o9]
