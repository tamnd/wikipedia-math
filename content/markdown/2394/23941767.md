# Prosolvable group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prosolvable group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more precisely in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), a **prosolvable group** (less common: **prosoluble group**) is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) that is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of an [inverse system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_system) of [solvable groups](wikipedia://en/105012). Equivalently, a group is called **prosolvable**, if, viewed as a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315), every [open neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_neighborhood) of the identity contains a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) whose corresponding [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) is a solvable group.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Let *p* be a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666), and denote the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [p-adic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_numbers), as usual, by $\mathbf{Q}_p$. Then the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) $\text{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_p/\mathbf{Q}_p)$, where $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_p$ denotes the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of $\mathbf{Q}_p$, is prosolvable. This follows from the fact that, for any finite [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) $L$ of $\mathbf{Q}_p$, the Galois group $\text{Gal}(L/\mathbf{Q}_p)$ can be written as [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) $\text{Gal}(L/\mathbf{Q}_p)=(R \rtimes Q) \rtimes P$, with $P$ cyclic of order $f$ for some $f\in\mathbf{N}$, $Q$ [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) of order dividing $p^f-1$, and $R$ of $p$-power order. Therefore, $\text{Gal}(L/\mathbf{Q}_p)$ is solvable.[^o4]

:::
