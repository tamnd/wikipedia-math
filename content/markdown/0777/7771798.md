# Quasinormal operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasinormal operator"}

In [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), **quasinormal operators** is a class of [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) defined by weakening the requirements of a [normal operator](wikipedia://en/190167).

:::

Every quasinormal operator is a [subnormal operator](wikipedia://en/7930037). Every quasinormal operator on a finite-dimensional [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) is normal.

## Definition and some properties {#Definition_and_some_properties}

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

Let *A* be a bounded operator on a Hilbert space *H*, then *A* is said to be **quasinormal** if *A* commutes with *A\*A*, i.e.

$$A(A^*A) = (A^*A) A.\,$$

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

A normal operator is necessarily quasinormal.

Let *A* = *UP* be the [polar decomposition](wikipedia://en/1369241) of *A*. If *A* is quasinormal, then *UP = PU*. To see this, notice that the positive factor *P* in the polar decomposition is of the form (*A\*A*)<sup>$\tfrac{1}{2}$</sup>, the unique positive square root of *A\*A*. Quasinormality means *A* commutes with *A\*A*. As a consequence of the [continuous functional calculus](wikipedia://en/784808) for [self-adjoint operators](wikipedia://en/187408), *A* commutes with *P* = (*A\*A*)<sup>$\tfrac{1}{2}$</sup> also, i.e.

$$U P P = P U P.\,$$

So *UP = PU* on the range of *P*. On the other hand, if *h* ∈ *H* lies in kernel of *P*, clearly *UP h* = 0. But *PU h* = 0 as well. because *U* is a [partial isometry](wikipedia://en/1348079) whose initial space is closure of range *P*. Finally, the self-adjointness of *P* implies that *H* is the direct sum of its range and kernel. Thus the argument given proves *UP* = *PU* on all of *H*.

On the other hand, one can readily verify that if *UP* = *PU*, then *A* must be quasinormal. Thus the operator *A* is quasinormal if and only if *UP* = *PU*.

When *H* is finite dimensional, every quasinormal operator *A* is normal. This is because that in the finite dimensional case, the partial isometry *U* in the polar decomposition *A* = *UP* can be taken to be unitary. This then gives

$$A^*A = (UP)^* UP = PU (PU)^* = AA^*.\,$$

In general, a partial isometry may not be extendable to a unitary operator and therefore a quasinormal operator need not be normal. For example, consider the [unilateral shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unilateral_shift) *T*. *T* is quasinormal because *T\*T* is the identity operator. But *T* is clearly not normal.

:::

## Quasinormal invariant subspaces {#Quasinormal_invariant_subspaces}

It is not known that, in general, whether a bounded operator *A* on a Hilbert space *H* has a nontrivial [invariant subspace](wikipedia://en/585826). However, when *A* is normal, an affirmative answer is given by the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495). Every normal operator *A* is obtained by integrating the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069) with respect to a spectral measure *E* = {*E<sub>B</sub>*} on the spectrum of *A*, *σ*(*A*):

$$A = \int_{\sigma(A)} \lambda \, dE (\lambda).\,$$

For any Borel set *B* ⊂ *σ*(*A*), the projection *E<sub>B</sub>* commutes with *A* and therefore the range of *E<sub>B</sub>* is an invariant subspace of *A*.

The above can be extended directly to quasinormal operators. To say *A* commutes with *A\*A* is to say that *A* commutes with (*A\*A*)<sup>$\tfrac{1}{2}$</sup>. But this implies that *A* commutes with any projection *E<sub>B</sub>* in the spectral measure of (*A\*A*)<sup>$\tfrac{1}{2}$</sup>, which proves the invariant subspace claim. In fact, one can conclude something stronger. The range of *E<sub>B</sub>* is actually a *[reducing subspace](wikipedia://en/69116423)* of *A*, i.e. its orthogonal complement is also invariant under *A*.
