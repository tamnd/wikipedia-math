# Seidel adjacency matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Seidel adjacency matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Seidel adjacency matrix** of a [simple undirected graph](wikipedia://en/325806) *G* is a [symmetric matrix](wikipedia://en/126474) with a row and column for each vertex, having 0 on the diagonal, −1 for positions whose rows and columns correspond to adjacent vertices, and +1 for positions corresponding to non-adjacent vertices. It is also called the **Seidel matrix** or – its original name – the (−1,1,0)-**adjacency matrix**. It can be interpreted as the result of subtracting the [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of *G* from the adjacency matrix of the [complement](wikipedia://en/1175649) of *G*.

:::

The [multiset](wikipedia://en/305303) of [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of this matrix is called the **Seidel spectrum**.

The Seidel matrix was introduced by [J. H. van Lint](wikipedia://en/12019625) and Johan Jacob Seidel in 1966 and extensively exploited by Seidel and coauthors.

The Seidel matrix of *G* is also the adjacency matrix of a [signed complete graph](wikipedia://en/2472618) *K<sub>G</sub>* in which the edges of *G* are negative and the edges not in *G* are positive. It is also the adjacency matrix of the [two-graph](wikipedia://en/2705947) associated with *G* and *K<sub>G</sub>*.

The eigenvalue properties of the Seidel matrix are valuable in the study of [strongly regular graphs](wikipedia://en/1399856).
