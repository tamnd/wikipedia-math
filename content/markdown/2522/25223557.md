# Patrick Dehornoy

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Patrick Dehornoy"}

**Patrick Dehornoy** (11 September 1952 – 4 September 2019) was a mathematician at the [University of Caen Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Caen_Normandy) who worked on [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Dehornoy was born on 11 September 1952 in [Rouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouen), France.[^o6] He graduated from the [Lycée Pierre-Corneille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Pierre-Corneille) in 1971.[^o6] He studied at the [École normale supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29) from 1971 to 1975 and completed his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1978 at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), with a thesis written under the direction of Kenneth Walter McAloon.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Dehornoy was a researcher at the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) (CNRS) from 1975 to 1982.[^o6] He was at the [University of Caen Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Caen_Normandy) as a Professor from 1983 to 2017 and as an Emeritus Professor from 2017 until his death.[^o6] From 2009 to 2013, he was an adjunct scientific director of the *Institut national des sciences mathématiques et de leurs interactions* (INSMI) at the CNRS.[^o6] Dehornoy died on 4 September 2019 in [Villejuif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villejuif), France at the age of 66.[^o7]

## Research {#Research}

Dehornoy found one of the first applications of [large cardinals](wikipedia://en/383644) to algebra by constructing a certain left-invariant [total order](wikipedia://en/30330), called the [Dehornoy order](wikipedia://en/31905690), on the [braid group](wikipedia://en/550138).[^o9] In his later career, he was a major contributor to the theory of braid groups, including creating a fast algorithm for comparing braids,[^o10] and was one of the main contributors to the development of [Garside methods](wikipedia://en/20247295).[^o7]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 1999, Dehornoy received the [Ferran Sunyer i Balaguer Prize](wikipedia://en/57181134).[^o6] In 2002, he was elected a senior member of the [Institut Universitaire de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Universitaire_de_France) (renewed in 2007).[^o6] In 2005, he received the Prix Langevin of the French Academy of Sciences.[^o6] In 2014, he received the [EMS](wikipedia://en/969470) Monograph Award for his book *Foundations of Garside Theory*.[^o6]
