# Arturo Reghini

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Arturo Reghini"}

**Arturo Reghini** (12 November 1878 – 1 July 1946) was an Italian mathematician, philosopher and esotericist.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Arturo Reghini was born in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) on 12 November 1878.[^o4] In 1898, he became a member of the [Theosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theosophical_Society) for which he founded a section in Rome. In 1903, he published in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) the first books of the editorial series named *Biblioteca Teosofica* (Theosophical Library) and later *Biblioteca filosofica*). In the same year, he was initiated in the Memphis' rite, a Masonic spiritual path that is derived by the ancient Egyptians and in Italy is uniquely practised in Palermo. In 1907, he was admitted to the regular [Scottish Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Rite) [Masonic Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonic_Lodge) "[Lucifero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucifer)" in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), affiliated to the [Grand Orient of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Orient_of_Italy).[^o5] Subsequently, Reghini adhered for a short period to the [Martinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinism) of [Gérard Encausse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gérard_Encausse) and started to report the errors of the lawyer and Grand Master Sacchi about his administration of the Italian Freemasonry, also confuting his publications.

In 1907, [Amedeo Rocco Armentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amedeo_Rocco_Armentano) introduced Reghini to the knowledge of the [Pythagoreanism](wikipedia://en/291170). In 1912, Reghini was in directorate of the Italian Freemasonry (in Italian: *Supremo Consiglio Universale* of the *[Rito filosofico italiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rito_filosofico_italiano)*) from which he resigned in 1940 with a strongly negative judgement about the national brotherhood.[^o7]

In 1921, he was initiated to the 33rd and highest degree of the Scottish Rite. Then he was elected as effective member of the Supremo Consiglio d'Italia of which he became the Great Commendor and the General Secretary. In 1925, Reghini signed the internal decree No 245 related to its termination. [^o8] On May 19, the Italian Parliament had approved the law of reform for the freedom of association, banning the masonic lodges out of the country.

Reghini edited the journals *Atanór* (1924) and *Ignis* (1925) devoted to initiate studies, covering topics such as [Pythagoreanism](wikipedia://en/291170), [yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga), Hebrew [Cabalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabbalah) and the [Freemasonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry) of [Alessandro Cagliostro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Cagliostro). A circle of esotericists formed around these journals and adopted the name *[Gruppo di Ur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruppo_di_Ur)*. The group's members included [Julius Evola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Evola) and the [anthroposophists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthroposophy) [Giovanni Colazza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Colazza) and [Giovanni Antonio Colonna di Cesarò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Antonio_Colonna_di_Cesarò). From 1927 to 1928 the group published the monthly journal *UR*.[^o9] Reghini fell out with Evola and the *Ur* group in 1928; a major reason was Reghini's support for Freemasonry, which was not in line with the direction the journal had taken. Reghini left the editorial board and *UR* was discontinued. It was briefly replaced in 1929 by a journal named *Krur*, without Reghini's involvement.[^o10]

Reghini was opposed to Christianity, which he associated with [modernity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernity) and [egalitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egalitarianism), and sought to establish a form of [modern Paganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Paganism) he called "*magia colta*", "cultured magic", which he drew from [Hermeticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermeticism) and [Platonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonism).[^o10] A critic of democracy and an advocate for the [ancient Roman aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_class_in_ancient_Rome), Reghini welcomed the rise of [Italian Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Fascism), which he associated with the ancient world. He wrote in *Atanór* in 1924 that he had anticipated the emergence of such a regime in Italy 15 years prior.[^o9] From the second half of the 1920s, he wrote critically about [clerical fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clerical_fascism) and the increasing fascist hostility towards non-Catholic religious views. He adopted an ironic writing style associated with the [anti-clericalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-clericalism) of the era before [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) and the [Risorgimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification).[^o4]

Reghini died in [Budrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budrio) on 1 July 1946.[^o4]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Reghini was an important influence on Evola during the years 1924 to 1930. He introduced Evola to the major texts on [alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy), which became the basis for Evola's book *The Hermetic Tradition* (1931).[^o9] It was also through Reghini that Evola came in contact with [René Guénon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Guénon), whose [Traditionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalist_School) would have a profound impact on his thinking.[^o10] Reghini's journals and the works of the [*Ur* group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UR_Group) have influenced the development of Italic-Roman neopaganism and [Roman polytheistic reconstructionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival_of_Roman_paganism).[^o12]
