# Umbilic torus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Umbilic torus"}

*Image: Umbilic Torus SBU.jpg.* Ferguson's *Umbilic Torus* sculpture at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University)

*Image: Umbilic torus.png.* Umbilic torus surface

The **umbilic torus** or **umbilic bracelet** is a single-edged 3-dimensional shape. The lone edge goes three times around the ring before returning to the starting point. The shape also has a single external face. A [cross section](wikipedia://en/1337370) of the surface forms a [deltoid](wikipedia://en/769176).

:::

The umbilic torus occurs in the mathematical subject of [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763), in particular in the classification of [umbilical points](wikipedia://en/9757480) which are determined by real [cubic forms](wikipedia://en/6680683) $a x^3 + 3 b x^2 y + 3 c x y^2 + d y^3$. The equivalence classes of such cubics form a three-dimensional real projective space and the subset of parabolic forms define a surface – the umbilic torus. [Christopher Zeeman](wikipedia://en/871415) named this set the umbilic bracelet in 1976.[^o2]

The torus is defined by the following set of [parametric equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_equations).[^o3]

$$x = \sin u \left(7+\cos\left({u \over 3} - 2v\right) + 2\cos\left({u \over3} + v\right)\right)$$

$$y = \cos u \left(7 + \cos\left({u \over 3} - 2v\right) + 2\cos\left({u \over 3} + v\right)\right)$$

$$z = \sin\left({u \over 3} - 2v\right) + 2\sin \left({u \over 3} + v\right)$$

$$\text{for }-\pi \le u \le \pi,\quad -\pi \le v \le \pi$$

## In sculpture {#In_sculpture}

[John Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Robinson_%28sculptor%29) created a sculpture *Eternity* based on the shape in 1989, this had a triangular cross-section rather than a deltoid of a true Umbilic bracelet. This appeared on the cover of Geometric Differentiation by [Ian R. Porteous](wikipedia://en/28112074).[^o2]

[Helaman Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helaman_Ferguson) has created a 27-inch (69 centimeters) bronze sculpture, *Umbilic Torus*, and it is his most widely known piece of art. In 2010, it was announced that [Jim Simons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Harris_Simons) had commissioned an Umbilic Torus sculpture to be constructed outside the Math and Physics buildings at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University), in proximity to the [Simons Center for Geometry and Physics](wikipedia://en/25568388). The torus is made out of cast bronze, and is mounted on a stainless steel column. The total weight of the sculpture is 65 tonnes, and has a height of 28 ft. The torus has a diameter of 24 ft, the same diameter as the granite base. Various mathematical formulas defining the torus are inscribed on the base. Installation was completed in September, 2012.[^o5]

## In literature {#In_literature}

In the short story *What Dead Men Tell*[^o7] by [Theodore Sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Sturgeon), the main action takes place in a seemingly endless corridor with the cross section of an equilateral triangle. At the end the protagonist speculates that the corridor is actually a triangular shape twisted back on itself like a [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817) but with the ends rotated 120 degrees before connecting them. This gave an endless corridor in which after three passes one came back to the point where one started.
