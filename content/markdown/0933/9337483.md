# Charles Loewner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Loewner"}

**Charles Loewner** (29 May 1893 – 8 January 1968) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). His name was **Karel Löwner** in Czech and **Karl Löwner** in German.

:::

## Early life and career {#Early_life_and_career}

Karl Loewner was born into a Jewish family in [Lány](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lány_%28Kladno_District%29), about 30 km from Prague, where his father Sigmund Löwner was a store owner.[^o4]

Loewner received his Ph.D. from the [University of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Ferdinands-Universität) in 1917 under supervision of [Georg Pick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Alexander_Pick). One of his central mathematical contributions is the proof of the [Bieberbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Branges'_theorem) in the first highly nontrivial case of the third coefficient. The technique he introduced, the [Loewner differential equation](wikipedia://en/34134544), has had far-reaching implications in [geometric function theory](wikipedia://en/5458932); it was used in the final solution of the Bieberbach conjecture by [Louis de Branges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Branges) in 1985.

Loewner taught mathematics at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) and [University of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Ferdinands-Universität) before leaving for the United States. "[Richard von Mises](wikipedia://en/821517) wrote from Istanbul, in a 1939 letter of recommendation for Löwner: 'During his activity at the University of Berlin, \[Lõwner\] was, among all the instructors in Mathematics, the one who had the strongest influence upon the students, stimulated them to independent research, and helped them in his unselfish way. Much of the work involved in the published theses of his pupils is not only due to his influence, but can in a true sense be considered as his work'."[^o4]

In the United States he taught at [University of Louisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Louisville), [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University) and eventually at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). His students include [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972), [Roger Horn](wikipedia://en/53179191), [Adriano Garsia](wikipedia://en/18857510), and [P. M. Pu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._M._Pu).

## Loewner's torus inequality {#Loewner's_torus_inequality}

In 1949 Loewner proved his [torus inequality](wikipedia://en/12003118), which states that for every metric on the 2-torus, its [systol](wikipedia://en/2545455) **sys** satisfies the optimal inequality[^o7]

$$\operatorname{sys}^2 \leq \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \operatorname{area} (\mathbb T^2).$$

The boundary case of equality is attained if and only if the metric is flat and homothetic to the so-called *equilateral torus*, the torus whose group of [deck transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deck_transformation) is precisely the [hexagonal lattice](wikipedia://en/2868762) spanned by the [cube roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_roots_of_unity) in $\mathbb C$.

:::

## Loewner matrix theorem {#Loewner_matrix_theorem}

The **Loewner matrix** (in [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422)) is a [square matrix](wikipedia://en/166022) or, more specifically, a [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) (of real $C^1$ functions) associated with two input parameters consisting of (1) a real [continuously differentiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuously_differentiable) function on a subinterval of the real numbers and (2) an $n$-dimensional [vector](wikipedia://en/23218956) with elements chosen from the subinterval; the two input parameters are assigned an output parameter consisting of an $n \times n$ matrix.[^o10]

Let $f$ be a real-valued function that is continuously differentiable on the [open interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_interval) $(a,b)$.

For any $s, t \in (a, b)$ define the **divided difference** of $f$ at $s, t$ as

$$f^{[1]}(s,t) = \begin{cases} \displaystyle \frac{f(s)-f(t)}{s-t}, & \text{if } s \neq t \\ f'(s), & \text{if } s = t \end{cases}.$$

Given $t_1, \ldots, t_n \in (a,b)$, the **Loewner matrix** $L_f (t_1, \ldots, t_n)$ associated with $f$ for $(t_1,\ldots,t_n)$ is defined as the $n \times n$ [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) whose $(i,j)$-entry is $f^{[1]}(t_i,t_j)$.

In his fundamental 1934 paper, Loewner proved that for each positive integer $n$, $f$ is [$n$-monotone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotone_function) on $(a,b)$ if and only if $L_f (t_1, \ldots, t_n)$ is [positive semidefinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_semi-definite_matrix) for any choice of $t_1,\ldots,t_n \in (a,b)$.[^o10] Most significantly, using this equivalence, he proved that $f$ is [$n$-monotone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotone_function) on $(a,b)$ for all $n$ if and only if $f$ is real analytic with an analytic continuation to the upper half plane that has a positive imaginary part on the upper plane. See *[Operator monotone function](wikipedia://en/57226096)*.

:::

## Continuous groups {#Continuous_groups}

"During \[Loewner's\] 1955 visit to Berkeley he gave a course on [continuous groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_group), and his lectures were reproduced in the form of duplicated notes. Loewner planned to write a detailed book on continuous groups based on these lecture notes, but the project was still in the formative stage at the time of his death." [Harley Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley_Flanders) and [Murray H. Protter](wikipedia://en/18876634) "decided to revise and correct the original lecture notes and make them available in permanent form."[^o12] *Charles Loewner: Theory of Continuous Groups* (1971) was published by [The MIT Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_MIT_Press), and re-issued in 2008.[^o13]

In Loewner's terminology, if $x\in S$ and a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) is performed on $S$, then $x$ is called a *quantity* (page 10). The distinction is made between an abstract group $\mathfrak{g},$ and a realization of $\mathfrak{g},$ in terms of [linear transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) that yield a [group representation](wikipedia://en/12695). These linear transformations are [Jacobians](wikipedia://en/195351) denoted $J(\overset{u}{v})$ (page 41). The term *invariant density* is used for the [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645), which Loewner attributes to [Adolph Hurwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolph_Hurwitz) (page 46). Loewner proves that [compact groups](wikipedia://en/750326) have equal left and right invariant densities (page 48).
