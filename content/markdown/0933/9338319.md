# Henryk Iwaniec

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henryk Iwaniec"}

**Henryk Iwaniec** (born October 9, 1947) is a [Polish-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-American) mathematician and New Jersey Professor Emeritus of Mathematics at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University). He is a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences). He has made important contributions to [analytic](wikipedia://en/251513) and [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) as well as [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147). He is the recipient of [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) (2002), [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize) (2011), and [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) (2015).

:::

## Background and education {#Background_and_education}

Iwaniec studied at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw), where he got his PhD in 1972 under [Andrzej Schinzel](wikipedia://en/11523982). He then held positions at the Institute of Mathematics of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) until 1983 when he left Poland. He held visiting positions at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), and [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder) before being appointed New Jersey Professor of Mathematics at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) in 1987. He is a citizen of both Poland and the United States.[^o4]

He and mathematician [Tadeusz Iwaniec](wikipedia://en/22241690) are twin brothers.

## Work {#Work}

Iwaniec studies both [sieve methods](wikipedia://en/1035915) and deep [complex-analytic techniques](wikipedia://en/5759), with an emphasis on the theory of [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146) and [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147).

In 1997, Iwaniec and [John Friedlander](wikipedia://en/9704785) proved that there are infinitely many [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) of the form $a^{2} + b^{4}$.[^o6][^o7] Results of this strength had previously been seen as [completely out of reach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_problem_%28sieve_theory%29): sieve theory—used by Iwaniec and Friedlander in combination with other techniques—cannot usually distinguish between primes and [products of two primes](wikipedia://en/339542), say. He also showed that there are infinitely many numbers of the form $n^2+1$ with at most two prime factors.[^o8]

In 2001, Iwaniec was awarded the seventh [Ostrowski Prize](wikipedia://en/3159411).[^o9] The prize citation read, in part, "Iwaniec's work is characterized by depth, profound understanding of the difficulties of a problem, and unsurpassed technique. He has made deep contributions to the field of [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), mainly in [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) on $GL(2)$ and [sieve methods](wikipedia://en/1035915)."[^o9]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

He became a fellow of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences in 1995. He was awarded the fourteenth [Frank Nelson Cole Prize in Number Theory](wikipedia://en/1372177) in 2002. In 2006, he became a member of the National Academy of Science. He received the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Mathematical Exposition in 2011. In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o11] In 2015 he was awarded the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) in Mathematics.[^o12] In 2017, he was awarded the [AMS Doob Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMS_Doob_Prize) (jointly with John Friedlander) for their book *Opera de Cribro*, which is about [sieve theory](wikipedia://en/1035915).
