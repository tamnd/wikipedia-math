# Fransén–Robinson constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fransén–Robinson constant"}

*Image: Fransen-Robinson-Constant-integral.gif.* The curve is $1/\Gamma(x)$, the shaded region is $F = \int_0^a \frac{1}{\Gamma(x)}\,dx$. As the upper bound of integration (a) goes to infinity, the approximation above approaches the actual value.

The **Fransén–Robinson constant**, sometimes denoted *F*, is the [mathematical constant](wikipedia://en/48779551) that represents the area between the graph of the [reciprocal Gamma function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_Gamma_function), $1/\Gamma(x)$, and the positive *x* axis. That is,

:::

$$F = \int_0^\infty \frac{1}{\Gamma(x)}\,dx = 2.8077702420285...$$

## Other expressions {#Other_expressions}

The Fransén–Robinson constant has numerical value *F* = 2.8077702420285... [^o3], and [continued fraction representation](wikipedia://en/46802) \[2; 1, 4, 4, 1, 18, 5, 1, 3, 4, 1, 5, 3, 6, ...\] . The constant is somewhat close to [Euler's number](wikipedia://en/9633) *e* = 2.71828... . This fact can be explained by approximating the integral by a sum:

$$F = \int_0^\infty \frac{1}{\Gamma(x)}\,dx \approx \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{\Gamma(n)} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{n!},$$

and this sum is the standard series for *e*.[^o4] The difference is

$$F - e = \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-x}}{\pi^2 + (\ln x)^2}\,dx$$

or equivalently

$$F = e + \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} e^{\pi \tan \theta} e^{-e^{\pi \tan \theta}}\,d\theta.$$

The Fransén–Robinson constant can also be expressed using the [Mittag-Leffler function](wikipedia://en/1603459) as the limit

$$F = \lim_{\alpha \to 0} \alpha E_{\alpha, 0}(1).$$

It is however unknown whether *F* can be expressed in [closed form](wikipedia://en/585143) in terms of other known constants.[^o5]

## Calculation history {#Calculation_history}

A fair amount of effort has been made to calculate the numerical value of the Fransén–Robinson constant with high accuracy.

The value was computed to 36 decimal places by Herman P. Robinson using 11 point [Newton–Cotes quadrature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton–Cotes_quadrature), to 65 digits by A. Fransén using [Euler–Maclaurin summation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Maclaurin_summation), and to 80 digits by Fransén and S. Wrigge[^o7] using [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) and other methods. William A. Johnson computed 300 digits, and Pascal Sebah was able to compute 1025 digits using [Clenshaw–Curtis integration](wikipedia://en/7020660).[^o8]
