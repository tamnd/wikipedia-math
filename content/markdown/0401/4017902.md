# Reciprocal gamma function

*Image: Rgamma plot real.svg.* Plot of $\tfrac{1}{\Gamma(x)}$ along the real axis

*Image: Reciprocal-gamma-domain-coloring.png.* Reciprocal gamma function $\tfrac{1}{\Gamma(z)}$ in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628), plotted using [domain coloring](wikipedia://en/5280990).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reciprocal gamma function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **reciprocal gamma function** is the [function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function)

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{\Gamma(z)},$$

where $\Gamma(z)$ denotes the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316). Since the gamma function is [meromorphic](wikipedia://en/80857) and nonzero everywhere in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628), its reciprocal is an [entire function](wikipedia://en/10372). As an entire function, it is of order 1 (meaning that $\log \log \lvert \tfrac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\rvert$ grows no faster than $\log \lvert z\rvert$), but of infinite type (meaning that $\log \lvert \tfrac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\rvert$ grows faster than any multiple of $\lvert z\rvert$, since its growth is approximately proportional to $\lvert z\rvert \log \lvert z\rvert$ in the left-half plane).

:::

The reciprocal is sometimes used as a starting point for [numerical computation](wikipedia://en/21506) of the gamma function, and a few software libraries provide it separately from the regular gamma function.

[Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459) called the reciprocal gamma function the "factorielle" and used it in his development of the [Weierstrass factorization theorem](wikipedia://en/1585155).

## Infinite product expansion {#Infinite_product_expansion}

Following from the [infinite product](wikipedia://en/26382006) definitions for the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316), due to [Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) and [Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459) respectively, we get the following infinite product expansion for the reciprocal gamma function:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{\Gamma(z)} &= z \prod_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1+\frac{z}{n}}{\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^z} \\ \frac{1}{\Gamma(z)} &= z e^{\gamma z} \prod_{n=1}^\infty \left(1 + \frac{z}{n}\right) e^{-\frac{z}{n}} \end{aligned}$$

where $\gamma = 0.577216...$ is the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant). These expansions are valid for all complex numbers $z$.

## Taylor series {#Taylor_series}

[Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) expansion around $0$ gives:[^o4]

$$\frac{1}{\ \Gamma(z)\ } = z + \gamma\ z^2 + \left(\frac{\gamma^2}{2} - \frac{\pi^2}{12}\right)\ z^3 + \left(\frac{\gamma^3}{6} - \frac{\gamma\pi^2}{12} + \frac{\zeta(3)}{3}\ \right)z^4 + \cdots\$$

where $\gamma$ is the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant). For $n > 2$, the coefficient $a_{n}$ for the $z^{n}$ term can be computed recursively as[^o5][^o6]

$$a_n = \frac{\ {a_2\ a_{n-1} + \sum_{j=2}^{n-1} (-1)^{j+1}\ \zeta(j)\ a_{n-j}}\ }{n-1} = \frac{\ \gamma\ a_{n-1} - \zeta(2)\ a_{n-2} + \zeta(3)\ a_{n-3}-\cdots\ }{n-1}$$

where $\zeta$ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809). An integral representation for these coefficients was recently found by Fekih-Ahmed (2014):[^o6]

$$a_n = \frac{(-1)^n}{\pi n!}\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\ \operatorname{Im}\Bigl[\ \bigl( \log(t)-i\pi \bigr)^n\ \Bigr]\ \mathrm{d} t ~.$$

Fekih-Ahmed (2014)[^o6] also gives an approximation for $a_{n}$:

$$a_n \approx \frac{(-1)^n}{\ (n-1)!\ } \ \sqrt{ \frac{2}{\ \pi n \ }\ }\ \operatorname{Im} \left( \frac{\ z_0^{\left( 1/2 - n \right)}\ e^{-n z_0}\ }{\sqrt{ 1 + z_0\ }} \right)\ ,$$

where $z_{0} = -1/n \exp(W_{-1}(-n))$, and $W_{-1}$ is the negative-first branch of the [Lambert $W$ function](wikipedia://en/92465).

The Taylor expansion around $1$ has the same (but shifted) coefficients, i.e.:

$$\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+z)} = \frac{1}{z\Gamma(z)} = 1 + \gamma\ z + \left(\frac{\gamma^2}{2} - \frac{\pi^2}{12}\right)\ z^2 + \left(\frac{\gamma^3}{6} - \frac{\gamma\pi^2}{12} + \frac{\zeta(3)}{3}\ \right)z^3 + \cdots\$$

(the reciprocal of [Gauss's pi function](wikipedia://en/12316#Pi_function)).

## Asymptotic expansion {#Asymptotic_expansion}

As $\lvert z\rvert$ goes to infinity at a constant $\arg(z)$ we have:

$$\ln (1/\Gamma(z)) \sim -z \ln (z) + z + \tfrac{1}{2} \ln \left (\frac{z}{2\pi} \right ) - \frac{1}{12z} + \frac{1}{360z^3} -\frac{1}{1260 z^5}\qquad \text{for}~ \left|\arg(z)\right| < \pi$$

## Contour integral representation {#Contour_integral_representation}

An integral representation due to [Hermann Hankel](wikipedia://en/932515) is

$$\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)} = \frac{i}{2\pi} \oint_H (-t)^{-z} e^{-t} \, dt,$$

where $H$ is the [Hankel contour](wikipedia://en/1268560), that is, the path encircling 0 in the positive direction, beginning at and returning to positive infinity with respect for the [branch cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branch_cut) along the positive real axis. According to Schmelzer & Trefethen,[^o10] numerical evaluation of Hankel's integral is the basis of some of the best methods for computing the gamma function.

## Integral representations at the positive integers {#Integral_representations_at_the_positive_integers}

For positive integers $n \ge 1$, there is an integral for the reciprocal [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) function given by[^o12]

$$\frac{1}{n!} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{-nit} e^{e^{it}}\ dt.$$

Similarly, for any real $c > 0$ and $z \in \mathbf{C}$ such that $\operatorname{Re}(z) > 0$ we have the next integral for the reciprocal gamma function along the real axis in the form of:[^o13]

$$\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} (c+ it)^{-z} e^{c+it} dt,$$

where the particular case when $z = n + 1/2$ provides a corresponding relation for the reciprocal [double factorial](wikipedia://en/507208) function,

$$\frac{1}{(2n-1)!!} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^n \cdot \Gamma\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)}.$$

## Integral along the real axis {#Integral_along_the_real_axis}

Integration of the reciprocal gamma function along the positive real axis gives the value

$$\int_{0}^\infty \frac{1}{\Gamma(x)}\, dx \approx 2.80777024,$$

which is known as the [Fransén&ndash;Robinson constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fransén&ndash;Robinson_constant).

We have the following formula[^o16]

$$\int_0^\infty \dfrac{a^x}{\Gamma(x)}\,dx=ae^a+a\int_0^\infty\dfrac{e^{-ax}}{\log^2(x)+\pi^2}\,dx$$
