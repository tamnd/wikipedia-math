# Ceva

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ceva"}

**Ceva**, the ancient **Ceba**, is a small [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) town in the [province of Cuneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Cuneo), region of [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont), 49 km east of [Cuneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneo). It lies on the right bank of the [Tanaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanaro_River) on a wedge of land between that river and the Cevetta stream.

:::

## History {#History}

In the pre-Roman period the territory around Ceva was inhabited by the branch of the mountain [Ligures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligures) known as Epanterii.

*Image: View of Ceva 1835.png.* Ceva in 1835

The upper Val Tanaro was [Romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) in the second century BC and it is known that the area was organized around a *[municipium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipium)*. However, it is not certain that this was Ceba: [Mombasiglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasiglio) is also regarded as a candidate. In the first century AD [Columella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Columella) referred to a particular breed of cattle raised here, and [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) praised its sheep's milk cheese in his *[Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_History_%28Pliny%29)*. The town is on the site of the old Roman road from [Augusta Taurinorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_Taurinorum) via [Pollentia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollentia) to the coast[^n1] and it is probable that there was a market here from which the cheese produced in the region was exported to Rome via the Ligurian ports of [Vada Sabatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vada_Sabatia) (the modern [Vado Ligure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vado_Ligure)) and/or [Albingaunum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albingaunum) (Albenga).

In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) it was the seat of [a small marquisate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquisate_of_Ceva), which lasted until the late 15th century when it was acquired by [Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Savoy). Ceva was home to a fortress defending the confines of Piedmont towards [Liguria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liguria), but the fortifications on the rock above the town were demolished in 1800 by the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), to whom it had been ceded in 1796.

Ceva was heavily damaged by a flood of the [Tanaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanaro), Cevetta and Bovina rivers.

## Napoleonic Era {#Napoleonic_Era}

During the beginning of [Napoleon Bonaparte's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte's) military career in the Italian Campaign, he captured and used Ceva as a point to outflank the [Piedmontese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmontese) garrison at Cuni in April of 1796. Napoleon's presence in Ceva also threatened [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin) greatly, allowing Napoleon to use Ceva as another launch point for an invasion of [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin). [^o8]

## Main sights {#Main_sights}

The 16th-century castle of the [Pallavicino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallavicini_family) stands in an area of green parkland between the Tanaro and the Cevetta and comprises two small palaces: the original, red *palazzina rossa* and the later, white *palazzina bianca*. Remains of the 16th-century fort are also present.

The [Duomo of Ceva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duomo_of_Ceva), also known as the Collegiata dell'Assunzione della Beata Vergine Maria, was completed in 1605.
