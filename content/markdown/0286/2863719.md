# Hexagonal tiling

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hexagonal tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **hexagonal tiling** or **hexagonal tessellation** is a [regular tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_tiling) of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), in which exactly three [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733) meet at each vertex. It has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) of $\{6,3\}$ or $t\{3,6\}$ (as a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) triangular tiling).

:::

English mathematician [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) called it a **hextille**.

The [internal angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) of the hexagon is 120 degrees, so three hexagons at a point make a full 360 degrees. It is one of [three regular tilings of the plane](wikipedia://en/505253#Euclidean_tilings). The other two are the [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858) and the [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809).

## Structure and properties {#Structure_and_properties}

The hexagonal tiling has a structure consisting of a [regular hexagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_hexagon) only as its [prototile](wikipedia://en/39372), sharing two vertices with other identical ones, an example of monohedral tiling.[^o4] Each vertex at the tiling is surrounded by three regular hexagons, denoted as $6.6.6$ by [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745).[^o5] The dual of a hexagonal tiling is [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858), because the center of each hexagonal tiling connects to another center of one, forming [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285).[^o6]

Every mutually incident vertex, edge, and tile of a hexagonal tiling can be [act transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29#Types_of_actions) to another of those three by mapping the first ones to the second through the symmetry operation. In other words, they are [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) (mapping the vertex of a tile to another), [edge-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-transitive) (mapping the edge to another), and [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive) (mapping regular hexagonal tile to another). From these, the hexagonal tiling is categorized as one of three [regular tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_tiling); the remaining being its dual and [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809).[^n1] The [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) of a hexagonal tiling is p6m.[^n2]

## Applications {#Applications}

*Image: Kissing-2d.svg.* The densest [circle packing](wikipedia://en/16879665) is arranged like the hexagons in this tiling

If a circle is inscribed in each hexagon, the resulting figure is the densest way to [arrange circles](wikipedia://en/368621) in two dimensions; its [packing density](wikipedia://en/43518103) is $\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{3}} \approx 0.907$.[^o11] The [honeycomb theorem](wikipedia://en/6545682) states that hexagonal tiling is the best way to divide a surface into regions of equal area with the least total perimeter.[^o12] The optimal three-dimensional structure for making honeycomb (or rather, soap bubbles) was investigated by [Lord Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kelvin), who believed that the Kelvin structure (or [body-centered cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body-centered_cubic) lattice) is optimal. However, the less regular [Weaire–Phelan structure](wikipedia://en/2795211) is slightly better.

The hexagonal tiling is commonly found in nature, such as the sheet of [graphene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphene) with strong covalent carbon bonds. Tubular graphene sheets have been synthesised, known as [carbon nanotubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_nanotube).[^o13] They have many potential applications, due to their high [tensile strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensile_strength) and electrical properties. [Silicene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicene) has a similar structure as graphene.

[Chicken wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_wire) consists of a hexagonal lattice of wires, although the shape is not regular.[^o14]

The hexagonal tiling appears in many crystals. In three dimensions, the [face-centered cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-centered_cubic) and [hexagonal close packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagonal_close_packing) are common crystal structures. They are the densest sphere packings in three dimensions. Structurally, they comprise parallel layers of hexagonal tilings, similar to the structure of graphite. They differ in the way that the layers are staggered from each other, with the face-centered cubic being the more regular of the two. Pure [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), amongst other materials, forms a face-centered cubic lattice.

## Uniform colorings {#Uniform_colorings}

The (*h*,*k*) represent the periodic repeat of one colored tile, counting hexagonal distances as *h* first, and *k* second. ### Chamfered hexagonal tiling {#Chamfered_hexagonal_tiling}

A [chamfered](wikipedia://en/43468103) hexagonal tiling replaces edges with new hexagons and transforms into another hexagonal tiling. In the limit, the original faces disappear, and the new hexagons degenerate into rhombi, and it becomes a [rhombic tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombic_tiling).

### Symmetry mutations {#Symmetry_mutations}

This tiling is topologically related as a part of a sequence of regular tilings with [hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) faces, starting with the hexagonal tiling, with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {6,n}, and [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) , progressing to infinity.

This tiling is topologically related to regular polyhedra with [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) *n*<sup>3</sup>, as a part of a sequence that continues into the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/318742).

It is similarly related to the uniform [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) polyhedra with vertex figure *n*.6.6.

This tiling is also part of a sequence of truncated rhombic polyhedra and tilings with \[n,3\] [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) symmetry. The cube can be seen as a rhombic hexahedron where the rhombi are squares. The truncated forms have regular n-gons at the truncated vertices, and nonregular hexagonal faces.

## Circle packing {#Circle_packing}

*Image: comparison\_hex\_tiling.svg.* Hexagonal (mid­dle) vs equivalent circle (top) vs staggered rectan­gular tilings (bottom)

The hexagonal tiling can be used as a [circle packing](wikipedia://en/16879665), placing equal-diameter circles at the center of every point. Every circle is in contact with 3 other circles in the packing ([kissing number](wikipedia://en/408555)).[^o41] The gap inside each hexagon allows for one circle, creating the densest packing from the [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858#A2_lattice_and_circle_packings), with each circle in contact with a maximum of 6 circles.

## Related regular complex apeirogons {#Related_regular_complex_apeirogons}

There are 2 [regular complex apeirogons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_complex_apeirogon), sharing the vertices of the hexagonal tiling. Regular complex apeirogons have vertices and edges, where edges can contain 2 or more vertices. Regular apeirogons *p*{*q*}*r* are constrained by: 1/*p* + 2/*q* + 1/*r* = 1. Edges have *p* vertices, and vertex figures are *r*-gonal.[^o43]

The first is made of 2-edges, three around every vertex, the second has hexagonal edges, three around every vertex. A third complex apeirogon, sharing the same vertices, is quasiregular, which alternates 2-edges and 6-edges.
