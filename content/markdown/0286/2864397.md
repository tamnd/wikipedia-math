# Trihexagonal tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trihexagonal tiling"}

Uniform tiling stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **trihexagonal tiling** is one of 11 [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635) of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) [by regular polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiling_by_regular_polygons).[^o2] It consists of [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) and [regular hexagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_hexagon), arranged so that each hexagon is surrounded by triangles and vice versa. The name derives from the fact that it combines a regular [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719) and a regular [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858). Two hexagons and two triangles alternate around each [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417), and its edges form an infinite [arrangement of lines](wikipedia://en/864149). Its [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_tiling) is the [rhombille tiling](wikipedia://en/2867012).

:::

This pattern, and its place in the classification of uniform tilings, was already known to [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) in his 1619 book *[Harmonices Mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonices_Mundi)*.[^o4] The pattern has long been used in Japanese [basketry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketry), where it is called **kagome**. The Japanese term for this pattern has been taken up in physics, where it is called a **kagome lattice**. It occurs also in the crystal structures of certain minerals. [Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls it a **hexadeltille**, combining alternate elements from a hexagonal tiling (hextille) and triangular tiling (deltille).[^o5]

## Kagome {#Kagome}

*Image: p12fig1.jpg.* Japanese basket showing the kagome pattern

**Kagome** (籠目) is a traditional Japanese woven bamboo pattern; its name is composed from the words *kago*, meaning "basket", and *me*, meaning "eye(s)", referring to the pattern of holes in a woven basket.

The kagome pattern is common in bamboo weaving in East Asia. In 2022, archaeologists found bamboo weaving remains at the Dongsunba ruins in Chongqing, China, 200 BC. After 2200 years, the kagome pattern is still clear.[^o8][^o9]

It is a [woven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving) [arrangement](wikipedia://en/864149) of [laths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lath) composed of interlaced triangles such that each point where two laths cross has four neighboring points, forming the pattern of a trihexagonal tiling. The [woven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving) process gives the Kagome a chiral [wallpaper group](wikipedia://en/386214) symmetry, [p6](wikipedia://en/386214#Group_p6) (632).

## Kagome lattice {#Kagome_lattice}

The term **kagome lattice** was coined by the Japanese physicist [Kôdi Husimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kôdi_Husimi), and first appeared in a 1951 paper by his assistant Ichirō Shōji.[^o12] The kagome lattice in this sense consists of the vertices and edges of the trihexagonal tiling. Despite the name, these crossing points do not form a [mathematical lattice](wikipedia://en/469246).

A related three-dimensional structure formed by the vertices and edges of the [quarter cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3757507), filling space by regular [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) and [truncated tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_tetrahedra), has been called a *hyper-kagome lattice*.[^o13] It is represented by the vertices and edges of the [quarter cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3757507), filling space by regular [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) and [truncated tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_tetrahedra). It contains four sets of parallel planes of points and lines, each plane being a two dimensional kagome lattice. A second expression in three dimensions has parallel layers of two dimensional lattices and is called an *orthorhombic-kagome lattice*.[^o13] The [trihexagonal prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trihexagonal_prismatic_honeycomb) represents its edges and vertices.

Some [minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral), namely [jarosites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarosite) and [herbertsmithite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbertsmithite), contain two-dimensional layers or three-dimensional kagome lattice arrangements of [atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom) in their [crystal structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_structure). These minerals display novel physical properties connected with [geometrically frustrated magnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrically_frustrated_magnet). For instance, the spin arrangement of the magnetic ions in Co<sub>3</sub>V<sub>2</sub>O<sub>8</sub> rests in a kagome lattice which exhibits fascinating magnetic behavior at low temperatures.[^o14] Quantum magnets realized on [Kagome metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagome_metal) have been discovered to exhibit many unexpected electronic and magnetic phenomena.[^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18] It is also proposed that [SYK behavior](wikipedia://en/62223541) can be observed in two dimensional kagome lattice with impurities.[^o19]

The term is much in use nowadays in the scientific literature, especially by theorists studying the magnetic properties of a theoretical kagome lattice.

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Tiling Dual Semiregular V4-6-12 Bisected Hexagonal.svg.* 30-60-90 triangle [fundamental domains](wikipedia://en/469365) of p6m (\*632) symmetry

The trihexagonal tiling has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) of r{6,3}, or [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram), , symbolizing the fact that it is a [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719), {6,3}. Its [symmetries](wikipedia://en/53741) can be described by the [wallpaper group](wikipedia://en/386214) p6mm, (\*632),[^o22] and the tiling can be derived as a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) within the reflectional [fundamental domains](wikipedia://en/469365) of this group. The trihexagonal tiling is a [quasiregular tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiregular_tiling), alternating two types of polygons, with [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) (3.6)<sup>2</sup>. It is also a [uniform tiling](wikipedia://en/12126635), one of eight derived from the regular hexagonal tiling.

### Uniform colorings {#Uniform_colorings}

There are two distinct [uniform colorings](wikipedia://en/18624814) of a trihexagonal tiling. Naming the colors by indices on the 4 faces around a vertex (3.6.3.6): 1212, 1232.[^o2] The second is called a **cantic [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719)**, h<sub>2</sub>{6,3}, with two colors of triangles, existing in [p3m1](wikipedia://en/386214#Group_p3m1) (\*333) symmetry.

### Circle packing {#Circle_packing}

The trihexagonal tiling can be used as a [circle packing](wikipedia://en/16879665), placing equal diameter circles at the center of every point.[^o30] Every circle is in contact with 4 other circles in the packing ([kissing number](wikipedia://en/408555)).

## Related quasiregular tilings {#Related_quasiregular_tilings}

The *trihexagonal tiling* exists in a sequence of symmetries of quasiregular tilings with [vertex configurations](wikipedia://en/3603745) (3.*n*)<sup>2</sup>, progressing from tilings of the sphere to the Euclidean plane and into the hyperbolic plane. With [orbifold notation](wikipedia://en/2915678) symmetry of \**n*32 all of these tilings are [wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) within a [fundamental domain](wikipedia://en/469365) of symmetry, with generator points at the right angle corner of the domain.[^o34][^o35]

## Related regular complex apeirogons {#Related_regular_complex_apeirogons}

There are 2 [regular complex apeirogons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_complex_apeirogon), sharing the vertices of the trihexagonal tiling. Regular complex apeirogons have vertices and edges, where edges can contain 2 or more vertices. Regular apeirogons *p*{*q*}*r* are constrained by: 1/*p* + 2/*q* + 1/*r* = 1. Edges have *p* vertices arranged like a [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306), and [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719) are *r*-gonal.[^o37]

The first is made of triangular edges, two around every vertex, second has hexagonal edges, two around every vertex.
