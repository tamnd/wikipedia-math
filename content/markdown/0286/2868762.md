::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hexagonal lattice"}

The **hexagonal lattice** (sometimes called **triangular lattice**) is one of the five two-dimensional [Bravais lattice](wikipedia://en/661808) types.[^o3] The [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) category of the lattice is [wallpaper group](wikipedia://en/386214) p6m.[^o4] The primitive translation vectors of the hexagonal lattice form an angle of 120° and are of equal lengths,

:::

$$|\mathbf a_1| = |\mathbf a_2| = a.$$

The [reciprocal lattice](wikipedia://en/664488) of the hexagonal lattice is a hexagonal lattice in reciprocal space with orientation changed by 90° and primitive lattice vectors of length

$$g=\frac{4\pi}{a\sqrt 3}.$$

## Honeycomb point set {#Honeycomb_point_set}

*Image: Honeycomb lattice - hexagonal lattice with a two-atom basis.svg.* Honeycomb point set as a hexagonal lattice with a two-atom basis. The gray rhombus is a primitive cell. Vectors $\mathbf a_1$ and $\mathbf a_2$ are primitive translation vectors.

The honeycomb point set is a special case of the hexagonal lattice with a two-atom basis.[^o3] The centers of the hexagons of a honeycomb form a hexagonal lattice, and the honeycomb point set can be seen as the union of two offset hexagonal lattices.

In nature, [carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon) atoms of the two-dimensional material [graphene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphene) are arranged in a honeycomb point set.
