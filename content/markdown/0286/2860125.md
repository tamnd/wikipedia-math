# Hexagonal antiprism

Prism polyhedron stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hexagonal antiprism"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **hexagonal antiprism** is the 4th in an infinite set of [antiprisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiprisms) formed by an even-numbered sequence of triangle sides closed by two [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) caps.

:::

Antiprisms are similar to [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) except the bases are twisted relative to each other, and that the side faces are triangles, rather than [quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/25278).

In the case of a regular *n*-sided base, one usually considers the case where its copy is twisted by an angle $\tfrac{180^{\circ}}{n}$. Extra regularity is obtained by the line connecting the base centers being perpendicular to the base planes, making it a **right antiprism**. As faces, it has the two [$n$-gonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-gon) bases and, connecting those bases, $2n$ [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428).

If faces are all regular, it is a [semiregular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/1548894).

## Crossed antiprism {#Crossed_antiprism}

A **crossed hexagonal antiprism** is a [star polyhedron](wikipedia://en/7087334), topologically identical to the convex *hexagonal antiprism* with the same [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644), but it can't be made uniform; the sides are [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428). Its [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) is 3.3/2.3.6, with one triangle retrograde. It has D<sub>6d</sub> symmetry, order 24.

:::

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

The hexagonal faces can be replaced by coplanar triangles, leading to a nonconvex polyhedron with 24 equilateral triangles.
