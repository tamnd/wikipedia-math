# Triangular tiling

Reg tiling stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Triangular tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **triangular tiling** or **triangular tessellation** is one of the three [regular tilings](wikipedia://en/1548669#Regular_tilings) of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), and is the only such tiling where the constituent shapes are not [parallelogons](wikipedia://en/15302229). Because the internal angle of the [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) is 60 degrees, six triangles at a point occupy a full 360 degrees. The triangular tiling has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) of $\{3,6\}.$

:::

English mathematician [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) called it a **deltille**, named from the triangular shape of the Greek letter [delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_%28letter%29) (Δ). The triangular tiling can also be called a **kishextille** by a [kis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway_kis_operator) operation that adds a center point and triangles to replace the faces of a [hextille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hextille).

It is one of [three regular tilings of the plane](wikipedia://en/505253#Euclidean_tilings). The other two are the [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809) and the [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719).

## Uniform colorings {#Uniform_colorings}

*Image: Triangular\_tiling\_4-color.svg.* A 4-uniform triangular tiling, 4 colored triangles, related to the [geodesic polyhedron](wikipedia://en/53024913) as {3,6+}<sub>2,0</sub>.

There are 9 distinct [uniform colorings](wikipedia://en/18624814) of a triangular tiling. (Naming the colors by indices on the 6 triangles around a vertex: 111111, 111112, 111212, 111213, 111222, 112122, 121212, 121213, 121314) Three of them can be derived from others by repeating colors: 111212 and 111112 from 121213 by combining 1 and 3, while 111213 is reduced from 121314.[^o3]

There is one class of [Archimedean colorings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedean_coloring), 111112, (marked with a \*) which is not 1-uniform, containing alternate rows of triangles where every third is colored. The example shown is 2-uniform, but there are infinitely many such Archimedean colorings that can be created by arbitrary horizontal shifts of the rows.

## A2 lattice and circle packings {#A2_lattice_and_circle_packings}

*Image: Compound 3 triangular tilings.svg.* The A\* lattice as three triangular tilings: + +

The [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644) of the triangular tiling is called an [A<sub>2</sub> lattice](wikipedia://en/277087#Explicit_construction_of_the_irreducible_root_systems).[^o8] It is the 2-dimensional case of a [simplicial honeycomb](wikipedia://en/34049829).

The A\* lattice (also called A3) can be constructed by the union of all three A<sub>2</sub> lattices, and equivalent to the A<sub>2</sub> lattice.

\+ + = dual of =

The vertices of the triangular tiling are the centers of the densest possible [circle packing](wikipedia://en/16879665).[^o9] Every circle is in contact with 6 other circles in the packing ([kissing number](wikipedia://en/408555)). The packing density is $\tfrac{\pi}{\sqrt{12}}$ or 90.69%. The [voronoi cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronoi_cell) of a triangular tiling is a [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733), and so the [voronoi tessellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronoi_tessellation), the hexagonal tiling, has a direct correspondence to the circle packings.

## Geometric variations {#Geometric_variations}

Triangular tilings can be made with the equivalent {3,6} topology as the regular tiling (6 triangles around every vertex). With identical faces ([face-transitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive)) and [vertex-transitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive), there are 5 variations. Symmetry given assumes all faces are the same color.[^o11]

## Related polyhedra and tilings {#Related_polyhedra_and_tilings}

The planar tilings are related to [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra). Putting fewer triangles on a vertex leaves a gap and allows it to be folded into a [pyramid](wikipedia://en/3307045). These can be expanded to [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905): five, four and three triangles on a vertex define an [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084), [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), and [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) respectively.

This tiling is topologically related as a part of sequence of regular polyhedra with [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394) {3,n}, continuing into the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/318742).

It is also topologically related as a part of sequence of [Catalan solids](wikipedia://en/564393) with [face configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration) Vn.6.6, and also continuing into the hyperbolic plane.

### Wythoff constructions from hexagonal and triangular tilings {#Wythoff_constructions_from_hexagonal_and_triangular_tilings}

Like the [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223) there are eight [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635) that can be based from the regular hexagonal tiling (or the dual triangular tiling).

Drawing the tiles colored as red on the original faces, yellow at the original vertices, and blue along the original edges, there are 8 forms, 7 which are topologically distinct. (The *truncated triangular tiling* is topologically identical to the hexagonal tiling.)

## Related regular complex apeirogons {#Related_regular_complex_apeirogons}

There are 4 [regular complex apeirogons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_complex_apeirogon), sharing the vertices of the triangular tiling. Regular complex apeirogons have vertices and edges, where edges can contain 2 or more vertices. Regular apeirogons *p*{*q*}*r* are constrained by: 1/*p* + 2/*q* + 1/*r* = 1. Edges have *p* vertices, and vertex figures are *r*-gonal.[^o17]

The first is made of 2-edges, and next two are triangular edges, and the last has overlapping hexagonal edges.
