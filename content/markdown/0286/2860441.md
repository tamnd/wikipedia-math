# Great dodecahedron

*Image: Great dodecahedron(full).stl.* 3D model of a great dodecahedron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Great dodecahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great dodecahedron** is one of four [Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler–Poinsot_polyhedra). It is composed of 12 [pentagonal](wikipedia://en/20741014) faces (six pairs of parallel pentagons), intersecting each other making a [pentagrammic](wikipedia://en/197544) path, with five pentagons meeting at each vertex.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

One way to construct a great dodecahedron is by [faceting](wikipedia://en/8613537) the [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968). In other words, it is constructed from the regular icosahedron by removing its polygonal faces without changing or creating new vertices. For each vertex of the icosahedron, the five neighboring vertices become those of a regular pentagon face of the great dodecahedron. The resulting shape has a [pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544) as its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719), so its [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) is $\{5, \tfrac{5}{2}\}$.

The great dodecahedron may also be interpreted as the *second stellation of dodecahedron*. The construction started from a [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361) by attaching 12 pentagonal pyramids onto each of its faces, known as the *first stellation*. The second stellation appears when 30 [wedges](wikipedia://en/2951640) are attached to it.

:::

## Formulas {#Formulas}

Given a great dodecahedron with edge length $E$,

| [Inradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inradius): | $\quad E \times \frac{\sqrt{50+10\sqrt{5}}}{20}$ |
|---|---|
| [Midradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midradius): | $\quad E \times \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{4}$ |
| [Circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius): | $\quad E \times \frac{\sqrt{10+2\sqrt{5}}}{4}$ |
| [Surface area](wikipedia://en/27725): | $\quad E^2 \times 15\sqrt{5-2\sqrt{5}}$ |
| [Volume](wikipedia://en/32498): | $\quad E^3 \times \frac{5\sqrt{5}-5}{4}$ |

:::

## Appearance {#Appearance}

Historically, the great dodecahedron is one of two solids discovered by [Louis Poinsot](wikipedia://en/764639) in 1810, with some people named it after him, *Poinsot solid*. As for the background, Poinsot rediscovered two other solids that were already discovered by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736)—the [small stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029843) and the [great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029878). However, the great dodecahedron appeared in the 1568 *[Perspectiva Corporum Regularium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspectiva_Corporum_Regularium)* by [Wenzel Jamnitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenzel_Jamnitzer).

The great dodecahedron appeared in popular culture and toys. An example is [Alexander's Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander's_Star) puzzle, a [twisty puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twisty_puzzle) that is based on a great dodecahedron.[^o8]

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

The *compound of small stellated dodecahedron and great dodecahedron* is a [polyhedron compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedron_compound) where the great dodecahedron is internal to its [dual](wikipedia://en/8815), the [small stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029843). This can be seen as one of the two three-dimensional equivalents of the compound of two pentagrams ($\{\tfrac{10}{4}\}$ "[decagram](wikipedia://en/19546742)"); this series continues into the fourth dimension as [compounds of star 4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/23901#Compounds_with_regular_star_4-polytopes).

A [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429) process applied to the great dodecahedron produces a series of [nonconvex uniform polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconvex_uniform_polyhedron). Truncating edges down to points produces the [dodecadodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157379) as a rectified great dodecahedron. The process completes as a birectification, reducing the original faces down to points, and producing the [small stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029843).

It shares the same [edge arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_arrangement) as the convex regular [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084); the compound with both is the [small complex icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/25923673).
