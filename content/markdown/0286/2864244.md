# Truncated square tiling

Uniform tiling stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Truncated square tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **truncated square tiling** is a [semiregular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiregular_tiling) [tiling by regular polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiling_by_regular_polygons) of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) with one [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) and two [octagons](wikipedia://en/314575) on each [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417). This is the only edge-to-edge tiling by [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) [convex polygons](wikipedia://en/660019) which contains an octagon. It has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) of *t{4,4}*.

:::

[Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls it a **truncated quadrille**, constructed as a [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429) operation applied to a [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809) (quadrille).

Other names used for this pattern include **Mediterranean tiling** and **octagonal tiling**, which is often represented by smaller squares, and nonregular octagons which alternate long and short edges.

There are 3 [regular](wikipedia://en/505253#Euclidean_tilings) and 8 [semiregular tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_uniform_tilings) in the plane.

## Circle packing {#Circle_packing}

The truncated square tiling can be used as a [circle packing](wikipedia://en/16879665), placing equal diameter circles at the center of every point. Every circle is in contact with 3 other circles in the packing ([kissing number](wikipedia://en/408555)).[^o5]

## Variations {#Variations}

*Image: Alternate-sized truncated square tiling.svg.* The squares from the truncation can be alternately sized. In the limit, half of the vertices can remain untruncated, leading to a [chamfered square tiling](wikipedia://en/43468546).

*Image: Dodecatile-alt-skew-truncsquare.svg.* A skew equilateral form with squares into rhombi, and flattened octagons.

One variations on this pattern, often called a *Mediterranean pattern*, is shown in stone tiles with smaller squares and diagonally aligned with the borders. Other variations stretch the squares or octagons.

The [Pythagorean tiling](wikipedia://en/33371056) alternates large and small squares, and may be seen as topologically identical to the truncated square tiling. The squares are rotated 45 degrees and octagons are distorted into squares with mid-edge vertices.

A [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving) pattern also has the same topology, with [octagons](wikipedia://en/314575) flattened [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048).

### Wythoff constructions from square tiling {#Wythoff_constructions_from_square_tiling}

Drawing the tiles colored as red on the original faces, yellow at the original vertices, and blue along the original edges, all 8 forms are distinct. However treating faces identically, there are only three unique topologically forms: [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809), truncated square tiling, [snub square tiling](wikipedia://en/2864884).

### Tetrakis square tiling {#Tetrakis_square_tiling}

*Image: 1-uniform 2 dual.svg.* The [tetrakis square tiling](wikipedia://en/2886280)

The **tetrakis square tiling** is the tiling of the Euclidean plane dual to the truncated square tiling. It can be constructed [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809) with each square divided into four [isosceles](wikipedia://en/315428) [right triangles](wikipedia://en/39420) from the center point, forming an infinite [arrangement of lines](wikipedia://en/864149). It can also be formed by subdividing each square of a grid into two triangles by a diagonal, with the diagonals alternating in direction, or by overlaying two square grids, one rotated by 45 degrees from the other and scaled by a factor of [$\sqrt{2}$](wikipedia://en/341682).

[Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls it a **kisquadrille**,[^o15] represented by a [kis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway_kis_operator) operation that adds a center point and triangles to replace the faces of a [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809) (quadrille). It is also called the **Union Jack lattice** because of the resemblance to the [UK flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Jack) of the triangles surrounding its degree-8 vertices.[^o16]
