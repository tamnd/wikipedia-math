# Elongated triangular tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elongated triangular tiling"}

Uniform tiling stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **elongated triangular tiling** is a [semiregular tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiling_by_regular_polygons) of the Euclidean plane. There are three triangles and two squares on each [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417). It is named as a [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858) [elongated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongation_%28geometry%29) by rows of squares, and given [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {3,6}:e.

:::

[Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls it a **isosnub quadrille**.[^o2]

There are 3 [regular](wikipedia://en/505253#Euclidean_tilings) and 8 [semiregular tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_uniform_tilings) in the plane. This tiling is similar to the [snub square tiling](wikipedia://en/2864884) which also has 3 triangles and two squares on a vertex, but in a different order.

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

It is also the only convex [uniform tiling](wikipedia://en/12126635) that can not be created as a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777). It can be constructed as alternate layers of [apeirogonal prisms](wikipedia://en/18119040) and [apeirogonal antiprisms](wikipedia://en/18119626).

:::

## Uniform colorings {#Uniform_colorings}

There is one [uniform colorings](wikipedia://en/18624814) of an elongated triangular tiling. Two 2-uniform colorings have a single vertex figure, 11123, with two colors of squares, but are not 1-uniform, repeated either by reflection or glide reflection, or in general each row of squares can be shifted around independently. The 2-uniform tilings are also called [Archimedean colorings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedean_coloring). There are infinite variations of these Archimedean colorings by arbitrary shifts in the square row colorings.

## Circle packing {#Circle_packing}

The elongated triangular tiling can be used as a [circle packing](wikipedia://en/16879665), placing equal diameter circles at the center of every point. Every circle is in contact with 5 other circles in the packing ([kissing number](wikipedia://en/408555)).[^o8]

## Related tilings {#Related_tilings}

Sections of stacked triangles and squares can be combined into radial forms. This mixes two vertex configurations, 3.3.3.4.4 and 3.3.4.3.4 on the transitions. Twelve copies are needed to fill the plane with different center arrangements. The duals will mix in [cairo pentagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2957180) pentagons.[^o10]

### Symmetry mutations {#Symmetry_mutations}

It is first in a series of symmetry mutations[^o13] with [hyperbolic uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/18640974#Quadrilateral_domains) with 2\**n*2 [orbifold notation](wikipedia://en/2915678) symmetry, [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) 4.*n*.4.3.3.3, and [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) . Their duals have hexagonal faces in the hyperbolic plane, with [face configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration) V4.*n*.4.3.3.3.

### Prismatic pentagonal tiling {#Prismatic_pentagonal_tiling}

The prismatic pentagonal tiling is a [dual uniform tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_uniform_planar_tilings) in the Euclidean plane. It is one of 15 known [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) [pentagon tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagon_tiling). It can be seen as a stretched [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719) with a set of parallel bisecting lines through the hexagons.

[Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls it an iso(4-)pentille.[^o2] Each of its pentagonal [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) has three 120° and two 90° angles.

It is related to the [Cairo pentagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2957180) with [face configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration) V3.3.4.3.4.
