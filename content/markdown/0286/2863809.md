# Square tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Square tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **square tiling**, **square tessellation** or **square grid** is a [regular tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_tiling) of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) consisting of four [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) around every [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417). [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) called it a **quadrille**.[^o3]

:::

## Structure and properties {#Structure_and_properties}

The square tiling has a structure consisting of one type of congruent [prototile](wikipedia://en/39372), the [square](wikipedia://en/659939), sharing two vertices with other identical ones. This is an example of monohedral tiling.[^o5] Each vertex at the tiling is surrounded by four squares, which denotes in a [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) as $4.4.4.4$ or $4^4$.[^n1] The vertices of a square can be considered as the lattice, so the square tiling can be formed through the [square lattice](wikipedia://en/2874853).[^o7] This tiling is commonly familiar with the flooring and game boards.[^o8] It is [self-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual), meaning the center of each square connects to another of the adjacent tile, forming square tiling itself.[^o9]

The square tiling [acts transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29#Types_of_actions) on the *flags* of the tiling. In this case, the [flag](wikipedia://en/9352497) consists of a mutually incident vertex, edge, and tile of the tiling. Simply put, every pair of flags has a symmetry operation mapping the first flag to the second: they are [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) (mapping the vertex of a tile to another), [edge-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-transitive) (mapping the edge to another), and [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive) (mapping square tile to another). By meeting these three properties, the square tiling is categorized as one of three [regular tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_tiling); the remaining being [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858) and [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719) with its prototiles are [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) and [regular hexagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_hexagon), respectively.[^n2] The [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) of a square tiling is p4m: there is an [order-4 dihedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_group_of_order_4) of a tile and an order-2 [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) around the vertex surrounded by four squares lying on the line of reflection.[^n3]

The square tiling is alternatively formed by the [assemblage of infinitely many circles](wikipedia://en/16879665) arranged vertically and horizontally, wherein their equal diameter at the center of every point contact with four other circles.[^o12] Its densest packing is $\frac{\pi}{4} \approx 0.785$.[^o13]

## Topologically equivalent tilings {#Topologically_equivalent_tilings}

Isohedral tilings have identical faces ([face-transitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive)) and [vertex-transitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive). There are eighteen variations, with six identified as triangles that do not connect edge-to-edge, or as quadrilateral with two collinear edges. Symmetry given assumes all faces are the same color.[^n4]

## Related regular complex apeirogons {#Related_regular_complex_apeirogons}

There are 3 [regular complex apeirogons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_complex_apeirogon), sharing the vertices of the square tiling. Regular complex apeirogons have vertices and edges, where edges can contain 2 or more vertices. Regular apeirogons p{q}r are constrained by: 1/*p* + 2/*q* + 1/*r* = 1. Edges have *p* vertices, and vertex figures are *r*-gonal.[^o17]
