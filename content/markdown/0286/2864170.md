# Truncated trihexagonal tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Truncated trihexagonal tiling"}

Uniform tiling stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **truncated trihexagonal tiling** is one of eight [semiregular tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_uniform_tilings) of the Euclidean plane. There are one [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29), one [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733), and one [dodecagon](wikipedia://en/508486) on each [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417). It has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) of *tr*{3,6}.

*Image: Rhombic\_truncated\_trihexagonal\_tiling.svg.* An equilateral variation with rhombi instead of squares, and [isotoxal](wikipedia://en/6289189) hexagons instead of regular

:::

## Names {#Names}

| The name *truncated trihexagonal tiling* is analogous to *[truncated cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/285879)* and *[truncated icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/286534)*, and misleading in the same way. An actual [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429) of the [trihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864397) has rectangles instead of squares, and its hexagonal and dodecagonal faces can not both be regular. Alternate interchangeable names are: Great rhombitrihexagonal tiling Rhombitruncated trihexagonal tiling Omnitruncated hexagonal tiling, omnitruncated triangular tiling [Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls it a *truncated hexadeltille*.[^o3] |  |
|---|---|

## Uniform colorings {#Uniform_colorings}

There is only one [uniform coloring](wikipedia://en/18624814) of a truncated trihexagonal tiling, with faces colored by polygon sides. A 2-uniform coloring has two colors of hexagons. 3-uniform colorings can have 3 colors of dodecagons or 3 colors of squares.

## Related 2-uniform tilings {#Related_2-uniform_tilings}

The *truncated trihexagonal tiling* has three related [2-uniform tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-uniform_tiling), one being a 2-uniform coloring of the semiregular [rhombitrihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864311). The first dissects the hexagons into 6 triangles. The other two dissect the [dodecagons](wikipedia://en/508486) into a central hexagon and surrounding triangles and square, in two different orientations.[^o7][^o8]

## Circle packing {#Circle_packing}

The Truncated trihexagonal tiling can be used as a [circle packing](wikipedia://en/16879665), placing equal diameter circles at the center of every point. Every circle is in contact with 3 other circles in the packing ([kissing number](wikipedia://en/408555)).[^o11]

## Kisrhombille tiling {#Kisrhombille_tiling}

The **kisrhombille tiling** or **3-6 kisrhombille tiling** is a tiling of the Euclidean plane. It is constructed by congruent [30-60-90 triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_60_90) with 4, 6, and 12 triangles meeting at each vertex.

Subdividing the faces of these tilings creates the kisrhombille tiling. (Compare the disdyakis [hexa-](wikipedia://en/722554#Symmetry), [dodeca-](wikipedia://en/724752#Symmetry) and [triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/725111#Symmetry), three [Catalan solids](wikipedia://en/564393) similar to this tiling.)

### Construction from rhombille tiling {#Construction_from_rhombille_tiling}

[Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls it a **kisrhombille**[^o3] for his [kis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway_kis_operator) vertex bisector operation applied to the [rhombille tiling](wikipedia://en/2867012). More specifically it can be called a **3-6 kisrhombille**, to distinguish it from other similar hyperbolic tilings, like [3-7 kisrhombille](wikipedia://en/27603113).

It can be seen as an equilateral [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719) with each hexagon divided into 12 triangles from the center point. (Alternately it can be seen as a bisected [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858) divided into 6 triangles, or as an infinite [arrangement of lines](wikipedia://en/864149) in six parallel families.)

It is labeled V4.6.12 because each right triangle face has three types of vertices: one with 4 triangles, one with 6 triangles, and one with 12 triangles.

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetry"}

### Symmetry {#Symmetry}

The *kisrhombille tiling* triangles represent the fundamental domains of p6m, \[6,3\] (\*632 [orbifold notation](wikipedia://en/2915678)) [wallpaper group](wikipedia://en/386214) symmetry. There are a number of small index subgroups constructed from \[6,3\] by mirror removal and alternation. \[1<sup>\+</sup>,6,3\] creates \*333 symmetry, shown as red mirror lines. \[6,3<sup>\+</sup>\] creates 3\*3 symmetry. \[6,3\]<sup>\+</sup> is the rotational subgroup. The commutator subgroup is \[1<sup>\+</sup>,6,3<sup>\+</sup>\], which is 333 symmetry. A larger index 6 subgroup constructed as \[6,3\*\], also becomes (\*333), shown in blue mirror lines, and which has its own 333 rotational symmetry, index 12.

:::

## Related polyhedra and tilings {#Related_polyhedra_and_tilings}

There are eight [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635) that can be based from the regular hexagonal tiling (or the dual [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858)). Drawing the tiles colored as red on the original faces, yellow at the original vertices, and blue along the original edges, there are 8 forms, 7 which are topologically distinct. (The *truncated triangular tiling* is topologically identical to the hexagonal tiling.)

### Symmetry mutations {#Symmetry_mutations}

This tiling can be considered a member of a sequence of uniform patterns with vertex figure (4.6.2p) and [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) . For *p* \< 6, the members of the sequence are [omnitruncated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnitruncation_%28geometry%29) polyhedra ([zonohedra](wikipedia://en/669402)), shown below as spherical tilings. For *p* > 6, they are tilings of the hyperbolic plane, starting with the [truncated triheptagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/8834676).
