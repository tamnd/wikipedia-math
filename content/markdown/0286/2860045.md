# Square antiprism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Square antiprism"}

*Image: Square antiprism.stl.* 3D model of a uniform square antiprism

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **square antiprism** is the second in an infinite family of [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851) formed by an [even-numbered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_number) sequence of triangle sides closed by two [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) caps. It is also known as an **anticube**.

:::

If all its faces are [regular](wikipedia://en/333306), it is a [semiregular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/1548894) or [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223).

A nonuniform *D*<sub>4</sub>-symmetric variant is the cell of the [noble](wikipedia://en/9352244) square antiprismatic 72-cell.

## Description {#Description}

A square antiprism is a polyhedron with two square bases, one of which is rotated 45 degrees relative to the other, connected with edges forming an alternating band of eight triangles.If these edges are equal in length, so that [the triangles become regular polygons](wikipedia://en/173285), the square antiprism is a [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223). In total, there are ten faces, formed by sixteen edges and eight vertices. This square antiprism has a [three-dimensional point group](wikipedia://en/2694525), the [antiprismatic symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiprismatic_symmetry) $\mathrm{D}_{4\mathrm{d}}$ of order 16.

Given that $h$ and $\ell$ are the height and an edge length of an $n$-sided polygonal antiprism, respectively, the volume is formulated as:

$$V = \frac{hn\ell^2}{12} \left(\cot \frac{\pi}{n} + 4 \cot \frac{\pi}{2n} \right).$$

In the case of a square antiprism $n = 4$, the volume becomes:

$$V_\text{square} = \frac{h\ell^2}{3} \left(5 + 4 \sqrt{2}\right).$$

## Applications {#Applications}

### Architecture {#Architecture}

*Image: OneWorldTradeCenter.jpg.* [One World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_World_Trade_Center) building

The main building block of the [One World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_World_Trade_Center) (at the site of the old [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_%281973–2001%29) destroyed on [September 11, 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11,_2001)) has the shape of an extremely tall tapering square antiprism. It is not a true antiprism because of its taper: the top square has half the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of the bottom one.

### Points on a sphere {#Points_on_a_sphere}

When eight points are distributed on the surface of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) with the aim of maximising the distance between them in some sense, the resulting shape corresponds to a square antiprism rather than a [cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_%28geometry%29). Specific methods of distributing the points include, for example, the [Thomson problem](wikipedia://en/3104018) (minimizing the sum of all the [reciprocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_%28mathematics%29) of distances between points), maximising the distance of each point to the nearest point, or minimising the sum of all reciprocals of squares of distances between points.

### Molecules with square antiprismatic geometry {#Molecules_with_square_antiprismatic_geometry}

*Image: Square-antiprismatic-3D-balls.png.* [Square antiprismatic molecular geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_antiprismatic_molecular_geometry)

According to the [VSEPR theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VSEPR_theory) of [molecular geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_geometry) in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), which is based on the general principle of maximizing the distances between points, a square antiprism is the favoured geometry when eight pairs of electrons surround a central [atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom). One [molecule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule) with this geometry is the octafluoroxenate(VI) [ion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion) (XeF) in the salt [nitrosonium octafluoroxenate(VI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrosonium_octafluoroxenate%28VI%29) (NO); however, the molecule is distorted away from the idealized square antiprism.[^o9] Very few ions are cubical because such a shape would cause large repulsion between [ligands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligand); Pa is one of the few examples.

In addition, the element [sulfur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur) forms octatomic S<sub>8</sub> molecules as its most stable [allotrope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allotropy). The S<sub>8</sub> molecule has a structure based on the square antiprism, in which the eight atoms occupy the eight vertices of the antiprism, and the eight triangle-triangle edges of the antiprism correspond to single [covalent bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covalent_bonds) between sulfur atoms.

## Topologically identical polyhedra {#Topologically_identical_polyhedra}

### Twisted prism {#Twisted_prism}

A [twisted prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twisted_prism) can be made (clockwise or counterclockwise) with the same [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644). It can be seen as the convex form with 4 [tetrahedrons](wikipedia://en/30606) excavated around the sides. However, after this it can no longer be triangulated into tetrahedra without adding new vertices. It has half of the [symmetry](wikipedia://en/2871265#Dihedral_symmetry) of the uniform solution: D<sub>4</sub> group of order 8.[^o13][^o14]

*Image: Twisted square antiprism.png.*

::: {#o16 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Crossed square antiprism"}

### Crossed antiprism {#Crossed_antiprism}

A **crossed square antiprism** is a [star polyhedron](wikipedia://en/7087334), [topologically](wikipedia://en/29954) identical to the *square antiprism* with the same [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644), but it cannot be made uniform; the sides are [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428). Its [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) is 3.3/2.3.4, with one triangle retrograde. It has D<sub>4d</sub> symmetry group of order 16.

:::

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

### Derived polyhedra {#Derived_polyhedra}

The [gyroelongated square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1123470) is a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034) (*J*<sub>10</sub>) constructed by augmenting one square face with a [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455). Similarly, the [gyroelongated square bipyramid](wikipedia://en/715707) (*J*<sub>17</sub>) is a [deltahedron](wikipedia://en/493995) (a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) whose [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) are all [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285)) constructed by replacing both squares of a square antiprism with a square pyramid.

The [snub disphenoid](wikipedia://en/1133565) (*J*<sub>84</sub>) is another deltahedron, constructed by replacing the two squares of a square antiprism by pairs of equilateral triangles.

The [snub square antiprism](wikipedia://en/1133918) (*J*<sub>85</sub>) can be seen as a square antiprism with a chain of equilateral triangles inserted around the middle, or as an alternated truncated square antiprism:

The [sphenocorona](wikipedia://en/1142750) (*J*<sub>86</sub>) and the [sphenomegacorona](wikipedia://en/1142743) (*J*<sub>88</sub>) are other Johnson solids that, like the square antiprism, consist of two squares and an even number of equilateral triangles.

### Symmetry mutation {#Symmetry_mutation}

As an [antiprism](wikipedia://en/1851), the square antiprism belongs to a family of polyhedra that includes the [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) (which can be seen as a triangle-capped antiprism), the [pentagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860091), the [hexagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860125), and the [octagonal antiprism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octagonal_antiprism).

The *square antiprism* is first in a series of [snub polyhedra](wikipedia://en/3752558) and tilings with [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) 3.3.4.3.*n*.

## Euclidean geometry {#Euclidean_geometry}

If $a$ denotes the side length of each of the two squares, and $h$ the (vertical) distance between the two squares, one can embed the 8 corners into Euclidean geometry by placing the 4 corners of the top square at Cartesian coordinates $(\pm a/2, \pm a/2, h/2)$ and the 4 corners of the bottom square—rotated by 45 degrees around the vertical axis relative to the top square—at $(\pm a/\surd 2 ,0,-h/2)$ and $(0,\pm a/\surd 2,-h/2)$.

The distance of each of these 8 corners to the center of coordinates is $\sqrt{2a^2+h^2}/2$.

If the length of the edges of the 8 triangular sides are isosceles, also equal to $a$, $h$ must be set to $h=a/\sqrt[4]{2}$, and the antiprism is equilateral. The distance of each of the 8 corners to the center of coordinates then is the radius of the circumsphere, $r= \sqrt{8+2^{3/2}}a/4\approx 0.82266 a$. By building dot products between vectors from the center to pairs of corners one finds that the smallest angle $\phi$ at which edges appear from the center is $\cos\phi = 1/(1+2^{3/2})$, which gives $\phi\approx 1.30652 \approx 74.858^\circ$. (This is the analog to the tetrahedral bond angle of the regular tetrahedron.)
