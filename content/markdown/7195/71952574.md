# Debra T. Silverman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Debra T. Silverman"}

**Debra Toby Silverman** (born December 31, 1948) is an American biostatistician and epidemiologist specialized in [bladder cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bladder_cancer) epidemiology and the [carcinogenicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogenicity) of [diesel exhaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_exhaust). Silverman is the chief of the occupational and environmental epidemiology branch at the [National Cancer Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cancer_Institute).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Silverman was born December 31, 1948.[^o6] She completed a B.A. in Mathematics at [Brooklyn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_College) in June 1970.[^o6][^o4] Silverman earned a Sc.M. in [health statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_statistics) from the [Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_Bloomberg_School_of_Public_Health).[^o5] Her master's degree was funded by a [U.S. Public Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Public_Health_Service) traineeship. Her 1972 thesis was titled *Maternal smoking and birth weight*.[^o6] Her advisor was [James Tonascia](wikipedia://en/60085623).[^o6] Professor [George W. Comstock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Comstock) had suggested the idea for her thesis and allowed Silverman to use data collected during the 1963 [Washington County, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Maryland) census.[^o6] When Silverman completed her training in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), she interviewed for a number of positions at the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health) (NIH) and was not sure which institute to join.[^o4] Silverman asked [Abraham Lilienfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lilienfeld), who was chair of epidemiology at Johns Hopkins University, what he thought. And he said, "Oh, go to cancer. That’s where all the money was."[^o4] This was in 1972; the [National Cancer Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cancer_Act) was passed in 1971 and the field was growing tremendously.[^o4] Silverman joined the [National Cancer Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cancer_Institute) (NCI) as a [biostatistician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biostatistician) in 1972.[^o5]

After three years at NCI, Silverman decided to go back to school to get her doctorate. NCI paid her tuition and full salary.[^o4] In 1981, she completed a Sc.D. in epidemiology from the [Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_T.H._Chan_School_of_Public_Health).[^o5] Her doctoral studies built on previous bladder cancer epidemiologic research conducted by her professor [Philip Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Cole_%28epidemiologist%29) and her mentor [Robert N. Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_N._Hoover).[^o4] Her dissertation was titled, *A case-control study of lower-urinary-tract cancer in Detroit*.[^o7] Silverman returned to NCI and has worked as a [cancer epidemiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_epidemiology) since 1983.[^o5] During the mid-1980s into the 1990s, her supervisor, [Joseph F. Fraumeni Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_F._Fraumeni_Jr.) allowed Silverman, and her colleagues [Shelia Hoar Zahm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelia_Hoar_Zahm) and [Patricia Hartge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_Hartge) to all work part-time so they could raise families.[^o4] She was part-time for 16 years, having her first daughter in 1986.[^o4] [Alan S. Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_S._Morrison) served as an additional mentor of Silverman.[^o4]

Silverman is chief of the NCI Occupational and Environmental Epidemiology Branch (OEEB) within the Division of Cancer Epidemiology and Genetics (DCEG).[^o5] In November 2024, Silverman will retire from the NCI after 16 years as Director of the OEEB. [^o8]

She specializes in [bladder cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bladder_cancer) epidemiology and the [carcinogenicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogenicity) of [diesel exhaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_exhaust).[^o4]

## Awards {#Awards}

Silverman has received awards, including the Harvard School of Public Health Alumni Award of Merit for the scientific importance and public health impact of her research; the PHS Special Recognition Award for research on environmental determinants of bladder and other cancers; the [American Occupational Medical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Occupational_Medical_Association) Merit in Authorship Award for her contributions to a paper on a job/exposure linkage system; the NIH Director’s Award, the NCI Special Act Award, the [NIOSH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Occupational_Safety_and_Health) Alice Hamilton Science Award for Occupational Safety and Health, and the [British Occupational Hygiene Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Occupational_Hygiene_Society) Award in recognition of her work on the Diesel Exhaust in Miners Study; the NIH Merit Award for her contributions to pancreatic cancer research; and the DCEG Exemplary Service and Investigator Award.[^o5] Silverman is an elected member of the [American Epidemiological Society](wikipedia://en/49585065) and a Fellow of the [American College of Epidemiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_College_of_Epidemiology).[^o5][^o10]
