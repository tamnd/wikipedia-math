# Perko pair

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perko pair"}

In the [mathematical theory of knots](wikipedia://en/153008), the **Perko pair**, named after Kenneth Perko, is a pair of entries in classical [knot tables](wikipedia://en/17647177) that actually represent the same knot. In [Dale Rolfsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Rolfsen)'s knot table, this supposed pair of distinct knots is labeled 10<sub>161</sub> and 10<sub>162</sub>. In 1973, while working to complete the classification by knot type of the Tait–Little knot tables of knots up to 10 crossings (dating from the late 19th century),[^o3] Perko found the duplication in [Charles Newton Little](wikipedia://en/48704630)'s table.[^o4] This duplication had been missed by [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) several years before in his knot table and subsequently found its way into Rolfsen's table.[^o5] The Perko pair gives a counterexample to a "theorem" claimed by Little in 1900 that the [writhe](wikipedia://en/968591) of a reduced diagram of a knot is an invariant (see [Tait conjectures](wikipedia://en/2749975)), as the two diagrams for the pair have different writhes.

:::

In some later knot tables, the knots have been renumbered slightly (knots 10<sub>163</sub> to 10<sub>166</sub> are renumbered as 10<sub>162</sub> to 10<sub>165</sub>) so that knots 10<sub>161</sub> and 10<sub>162</sub> are different. Some authors have mistaken these two renumbered knots for the Perko pair and claimed incorrectly that they are the same.[^o6]

The Perko pair was correctly illustrated and explained on the first page of the Science section of the July 8, 1986 *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)*.

The Perko pair is one of five knots with 10 crossings where the topological and smooth [4-genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-genus) are different; the former is equal to 2, while the latter is 3.[^o7]
