# Ishimori equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ishimori equation"}

The **Ishimori equation** is a [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) proposed by the Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) (Ishimori 1984). Its interest is as the first example of a nonlinear spin-one field model in the plane that is [integrable](wikipedia://en/2573213) (Sattinger Tracy Venakides 1991, 78).

:::

## Equation {#Equation}

The Ishimori equation has the form

::: {#o5 .equation cue=numblk name="1a"}

$$\frac{\partial \mathbf{S}}{\partial t} = \mathbf{S}\wedge \left(\frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{S}}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{S}}{\partial y^2}\right)+ \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\frac{\partial \mathbf{S}}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\frac{\partial \mathbf{S}}{\partial x}, \tag{1a}$$

:::

::: {#o6 .equation cue=numblk name="1b"}

$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}-\alpha^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}=-2\alpha^2 \mathbf{S} \cdot \left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{S}}{\partial x}\wedge \frac{\partial \mathbf{S}}{\partial y}\right). \tag{1b}$$

:::

## Lax representation {#Lax_representation}

The [Lax representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lax_representation)

::: {#o8 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$L_t = AL - LA \tag{2}$$

:::

of the equation is given by

::: {#o9 .equation cue=numblk name="3a"}

$$L =\Sigma \partial_x + \alpha I\partial_y, \tag{3a}$$

:::

::: {#o10 .equation cue=numblk name="3b"}

$$A = -2i\Sigma\partial_x^2+(-i\Sigma_x-i\alpha\Sigma_y\Sigma+u_yI-\alpha^3u_x\Sigma)\partial_x. \tag{3b}$$

:::

Here

::: {#o11 .equation cue=numblk name="4"}

$$\Sigma=\sum_{j=1}^3S_j\sigma_j, \tag{4}$$

:::

the $\sigma_i$ are the [Pauli matrices](wikipedia://en/24868) and $I$ is the identity matrix.

## Reductions {#Reductions}

The Ishimori equation admits an important reduction: in 1+1 dimensions it reduces to the [continuous classical Heisenberg ferromagnet equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_model_%28classical%29) (CCHFE). The CCHFE is integrable.

## Equivalent counterpart {#Equivalent_counterpart}

The equivalent counterpart of the Ishimori equation is the [Davey-Stewartson equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davey-Stewartson_equation).
