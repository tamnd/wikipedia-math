# Spinh structure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spinh structure"}

In [spin geometry](wikipedia://en/32013990), a **spin<sup>h</sup> structure** (or **quaternionic spin structure**) is a generalization of a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303). In mathematics, these are used to describe [spinor bundles](wikipedia://en/348085) and [spinors](wikipedia://en/29276), which in physics are used to describe [spin](wikipedia://en/19593829), an intrinsic [angular momentum](wikipedia://en/2839) of [particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle) after which they have been named. Since spin<sup>h</sup> structures also exist under weakened conditions, which might not allow spin structures, they provide a suitable alternative for such situations. [Orientable](wikipedia://en/187446) manifolds with spin<sup>h</sup> structures are called **spin<sup>h</sup> manifolds**.[^o2] H stands for the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440), which are denoted $\mathbb{H}$ and appear in the definition of the underlying [spin<sup>h</sup> group](wikipedia://en/79367898).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $M$ be a $n$-dimensional [orientable manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable_manifold). Its [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) $TM$ is described by a classifying map $M\rightarrow\operatorname{BSO}(n)$ into the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/76359810) $\operatorname{BSO}(n)$ of the [special orthogonal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) $\operatorname{SO}(n)$. It can factor over the map $\operatorname{BSpin}^\mathrm{h}(n)\rightarrow\operatorname{BSO}(n)$ induced by the canonical projection $\operatorname{Spin}^\mathrm{h}(n)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{SO}(n)$ on [classifying spaces](wikipedia://en/923556). In this case, the classifying map lifts to a continuous map $M\rightarrow\operatorname{BSpin}^\mathrm{h}(n)$ into the classifying space $\operatorname{BSpin}^\mathrm{h}(n)$ of the [spin<sup>h</sup> group](wikipedia://en/79367898) $\operatorname{Spin}^\mathrm{h}(n)$. Its [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) class is called *spin<sup>h</sup> structure*.[^o5]

Assume $M$ has a spin<sup>h</sup> structure. Let then $\operatorname{Spin}^\mathrm{h}(M)$ denote the set of spin<sup>h</sup> structures on $M$. The first symplectic group [$\operatorname{Sp}(1)$](wikipedia://en/80364893) is the second factor of the spin<sup>h</sup> group and using its [classifying space](wikipedia://en/76359866) [$\operatorname{BSp}(1) \cong\operatorname{BSU}(2)$](wikipedia://en/80364893), which is the infinite [quaternionic projective space](wikipedia://en/2808931) [$\mathbb{H}P^\infty$](wikipedia://en/80364893) and through its [Postnikov tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postnikov_tower) projects onto the [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) $K(\mathbb{Z},4)$, there is a map:

$$\operatorname{Spin}^\mathrm{h}(M) \cong[M,\operatorname{BSp}(1)] \cong[M,\mathbb{H}P^\infty] \rightarrow[M,K(\mathbb{Z},4)] \cong H^4(M,\mathbb{Z}).$$

The former isomorphism follows from the [Puppe sequence](wikipedia://en/4734210) for the fibration $\mathbb{H}P^\infty\hookrightarrow\operatorname{BSpin}^\mathrm{h}(n)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{BSO}(n)$ (when applying [$[M,-]$](wikipedia://en/80364893)).[^o6] Although this map is not a bijection in general, it is in special cases, for example for a [4-manifold](wikipedia://en/1362795) $M$.

Due to the canonical projection $\operatorname{BSpin}^\mathrm{h}(n)\rightarrow\operatorname{SU}(2)/\mathbb{Z}_2 \cong\operatorname{SO}(3)$, every spin<sup>h</sup> structure induces a principal $\operatorname{SO}(3)$-bundle or equivalently a orientable real vector bundle of third rank.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- Every spin and even every [spin<sup>c</sup> structure](wikipedia://en/79367906) induces a spin<sup>h</sup> structure. Reverse implications don't hold as the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155) $\mathbb{C}P^2$ and the [Wu manifold](wikipedia://en/77123741) $\operatorname{SU}(3)/\operatorname{SO}(3)$ show.[^o9]
- If an orientable manifold $M$ has a spin<sup>h</sup> structure, then its fifth integral Stiefel–Whitney class $W_5(M) \in H^5(M,\mathbb{Z})$ vanishes, hence is the image of the fourth ordinary Stiefel–Whitney class $w_4(M) \in H^4(M,\mathbb{Z})$ under the canonical map $H^4(M,\mathbb{Z}_2)\rightarrow H^4(M,\mathbb{Z})$.
- Every compact orientable smooth manifold with seven or less dimensions has a spin<sup>h</sup> structure.[^o10]
- In eight dimensions, there are infinitely many [homotopy types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_type) of [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) manifolds without spin<sup>h</sup> structure.[^o11]
- For a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) spin<sup>h</sup> manifold $M$ of even dimension with either vanishing fourth [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) $b_4(M)=\dim H^4(M,\mathbb{R})$ or the first [Pontrjagin class](wikipedia://en/385944) $p_1(E)\in H^4(M,\mathbb{Z})$ of its canonical principal $\operatorname{SO}(3)$-bundle $E\twoheadrightarrow M$ being torsion, twice its [Â genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Â_genus) $2\widehat{A}(M)$ is integer.[^o12]

The following properties hold more generally for the lift on the Lie group $\operatorname{Spin}^k(n) :=\left( \operatorname{Spin}(n)\times\operatorname{Spin}(k) \right)/\mathbb{Z}_2$, with the particular case $k=3$ giving:

- If $M\times N$ is a spin<sup>h</sup> manifold, then $M$ and $N$ are spin<sup>h</sup> manifolds.[^o13]
- If $M$ is a spin manifold, then $M\times N$ is a spin<sup>h</sup> manifold iff $N$ is a spin<sup>h</sup> manifold.[^o13]
- If $M$ and $N$ are spin<sup>h</sup> manifolds of same dimension, then their [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) $M\# N$ is a spin<sup>h</sup> manifold.[^o14]
- The following conditions are equivalent:[^o15]
  - $M$ is a spin<sup>h</sup> manifold.
  - There is a real vector bundle $E\twoheadrightarrow M$ of third rank, so that $TM\oplus E$ has a spin structure or equivalently $w_2(TM\oplus E) =0$.
  - $M$ can be immersed in a spin manifold with three dimensions more.
  - $M$ can be embedded in a spin manifold with three dimensions more.

:::

## Cohomology of infinite classifying space {#Cohomology_of_infinite_classifying_space}

The [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) of the infinite classifying space $\operatorname{BSpin}^\mathrm{h} :=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{BSpin}^\mathrm{h}(n)$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}_2$ can be expressed using [Steenrod squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steenrod_square) and [Wu classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_class):[^o17][^o18]

$$H^*(\operatorname{BSpin}^\mathrm{h},\mathbb{Z}_2) \cong H^*(\operatorname{BSO},\mathbb{Z}_2)/(\operatorname{Sq}^1\nu_{2^r},r\geq 2).$$
