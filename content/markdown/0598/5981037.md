# Ravi Vakil

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ravi Vakil"}

**Ravi D. Vakil** (born February 22, 1970) is a Canadian-American mathematician working in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). He is the current president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Vakil was born on February 22, 1970. His father was a professor of preventative medicine at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) and his mother was a high school math teacher.[^o5] He later attended high school at [Martingrove Collegiate Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martingrove_Collegiate_Institute) in [Etobicoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etobicoke), Ontario, where he won several mathematical contests and olympiads.[^o6] After earning a BSc and MSc from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 1992, he completed a PhD in mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1997 under [Joe Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Daniel_Harris).[^o7] He has since been an instructor at both [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). Since the fall of 2001, he has taught at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University),[^o8] becoming a full professor in 2007. *[The Rising Sea: Foundations of Algebraic Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rising_Sea:_Foundations_of_Algebraic_Geometry)*, a mathematical textbook about algebraic geometry by Ravi Vakil, was published in 2025, although drafts were available online ever since he began to write it in 2010.[^o9]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Vakil is an [algebraic geometer](wikipedia://en/1997) and his research work spans over [enumerative geometry](wikipedia://en/3069092), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [Gromov&ndash;Witten theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gromov&ndash;Witten_theory), and classical algebraic geometry. He has solved several old problems in [Schubert calculus](wikipedia://en/3061740). Among other results, he proved that all Schubert problems are enumerative[^o11] over the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), a result that resolves an issue mathematicians have worked on for at least two decades.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Vakil has received many awards, including an [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) CAREER Fellowship, a [Sloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan_Foundation) Research Fellowship, an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) Centennial Fellowship, a [G. de B. Robinson prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._de_B._Robinson_Award) for the best paper published (2000) in the [Canadian Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Journal_of_Mathematics) and the [Canadian Mathematical Bulletin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Mathematical_Bulletin),[^o13] and the André-Aisenstadt Prize from the [Centre de Recherches Mathématiques](wikipedia://en/3505436) at the [Université de Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Montréal) (2005), and the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize)[^o3] (2014).

In 2013 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o14] Vakil was elected as its president in 2024 and began his two-year term on 1 February 2025.[^o15]

## Mathematics contests {#Mathematics_contests}

He was a member of the Canadian team in three [International Mathematical Olympiads](wikipedia://en/82208), winning silver, gold (perfect score), and gold in 1986, 1987, and 1988 respectively. He was also the fourth person to be a four-time [Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) Fellow in the history of the contest. Also, he has been the coordinator of weekly Putnam preparation seminars at Stanford.[^o17]
