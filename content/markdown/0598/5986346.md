# William Guy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Guy"}

*Image: William Augustus Guy.jpg.* William Augustus Guy

**William Augustus Guy** (13 June 1810 – 10 September 1885) was a British physician and medical statistician.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Chichester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chichester) and educated at [Christ's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_Hospital) and [Guy's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy's_Hospital); he then studied at the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg) and the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) before getting a Bachelor of Medicine degree from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), 1837.

In 1842, he was appointed professor of forensic medicine at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) and assistant physician at King's College Hospital, 1842; he was dean of the faculty of medicine, 1846–58. He also served as Medical Superintendent at Millbank Prison from 1859 to 1869, acting as a semi-official government advisor on prison health, diet and hygiene.

He edited the *[Journal of the Statistical Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_Statistical_Society_of_London)* (now the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664)), 1852–6 and was its president, 1873–5. The Society still presents the [Guy Medals](wikipedia://en/6752269) (in gold, silver and bronze) in his memory.

He was vice-president of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society), 1876–7, and [Croonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croonian) (1860) and [Lumleian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumleian) (1868) lecturer, and [Harveian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harveian_Oration) (1875) orator at the [Royal College of Physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians).

He was a founder of the [Health of Towns Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_of_Towns_Association) and a member of the [Commission on Penal Servitude and Criminal Lunacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Penal_Servitude_and_Criminal_Lunacy).

## Works {#Works}

Guy's *Principles of Forensic Medicine*, first published in 1844, became a standard work; the fourth and later editions were edited by Dr. [David Ferrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ferrier). Other major works were:

- *R. Hooper's Physician's Vade-Mecum; enlarged and improved by W.A.G.*, (1842 and many subsequent editions). Based on the work by [Robert Hooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hooper_%28physician%29).
- T. Walker's *Original*, edited with additions by W.A.G. 1875; another edition 1885. Based on the publication *The Original* by [Thomas Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Walker_%28author%29).
- *Public Health; a Popular Introduction to Sanitary Science*, pt. i. 1870; pt. ii. 1874.
- *The Factors of the Unsound Mind, with special reference to the Plea of Insanity in Criminal Cases*, 1881.
- *John Howard's Winter's Journey*, 1882.

Guy published lectures, and contributed papers to the [Statistical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_Society), including the *Influence of Employments on Health*, *The Duration of Life among different Classes*, *Temperance and its relation to Mortality*, *The Mortality of London Hospitals*, *Prison Dietaries*, and *John Howard's True Place in History*.
