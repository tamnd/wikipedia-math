# Cohn's irreducibility criterion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cohn's irreducibility criterion"}

**Cohn's irreducibility criterion** is a sufficient condition for a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) to be [irreducible](wikipedia://en/188725) in [$\mathbb{Z}[x]$](wikipedia://en/373065)—that is, for it to be unfactorable into the product of lower-[degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) polynomials with [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The criterion is often stated as follows:

If a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$ is expressed in [base 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_10) as $p = a_m 10^m + a_{m-1} 10^{m-1} +\cdots+ a_1 10 + a_0$ (where $0\leq a_i\leq 9$) then the polynomial

$$f(x)=a_mx^m+a_{m-1}x^{m-1}+\cdots+a_1x+a_0$$

is irreducible in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$.[^o14]

The theorem can be generalized to other bases as follows:

Assume that $b \ge 2$ is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) and $p(x) = a_k x^k + a_{k-1} x^{k-1} +\cdots+ a_1 x + a_0$ is a polynomial such that $0\leq a_i \leq b-1$. If $p(b)$ is a prime number then $p(x)$ is irreducible in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$.[^o14]

:::

## History and extensions {#History_and_extensions}

The base 10 version of the theorem is attributed to Cohn by [Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) and [Szegő](wikipedia://en/46065) in *[Problems and Theorems in Analysis](wikipedia://en/77356586)*[^o5] while the generalization to any base *b* is due to Brillhart, [Filaseta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Filaseta), and [Odlyzko](wikipedia://en/1986042).[^o6] It is clear from context that the "A. Cohn" mentioned by Polya and Szegő is Arthur Cohn (1894–1940), a student of [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522) who was awarded his doctorate from [Frederick William University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) in 1921.[^o7][^o8]

A further generalization of the theorem allowing coefficients larger than digits was given by Filaseta and Gross.[^o9] In particular, let $f(x)$ be a polynomial with non-negative integer coefficients such that $f(10)$ is prime. If all coefficients are $\leq$ 49598666989151226098104244512918, then $f(x)$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Z}[x]$. Moreover, they proved that this bound is also sharp. In other words, coefficients larger than 49598666989151226098104244512918 do not guarantee irreducibility. The method of Filaseta and Gross was also generalized to provide similar sharp bounds for some other bases by Cole, Dunn, and Filaseta.[^o10] Analogue results exist in bases other than 10.

An analogue of the theorem also holds for [algebraic function fields](wikipedia://en/447645) over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615).[^o14]

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Converse"}

## Converse {#Converse}

The [converse](wikipedia://en/155941) of this criterion is that, if *p* is an irreducible polynomial with integer coefficients that have [greatest common divisor](wikipedia://en/12354) 1, then there exists a base such that the coefficients of *p* form the representation of a prime number in that base. This is the [Bunyakovsky conjecture](wikipedia://en/12511846) and its truth or falsity remains an open question.[^o14]

:::
