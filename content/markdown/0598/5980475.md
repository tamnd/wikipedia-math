# Claire Voisin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Claire Voisin"}

**Claire Voisin** (born 4 March 1962) is a French mathematician known for her work in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). She is a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) and held the chair of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) from 2015 to 2020.

:::

## Work {#Work}

She is noted for her work in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) particularly as it pertains to variations of [Hodge structures](wikipedia://en/501590#Hodge_structures) and [mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/644671), and has written several books on [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590). In 2002, Voisin proved that the generalization of the [Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518) for compact [Kähler varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_manifolds) is false.[^o4] The [Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518) is one of the seven [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655) [Millennium Prize Problems](wikipedia://en/27599234) which were selected in 2000, each having a prize of one million US dollars.

Voisin won the [European Mathematical Society Prize](wikipedia://en/969470) in 1992 and the Servant Prize awarded by the Academy of Sciences in 1996.[^o5] She received the [Sophie Germain Prize](wikipedia://en/27791#Sophie_Germain_Prize) in 2003[^o6] and the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) in 2008 for her disproof of the [Kodaira](wikipedia://en/646326) conjecture on deformations of compact [Kähler](wikipedia://en/390538) [manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifolds).[^o7] In 2007, she was awarded the [Ruth Lyttle Satter Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/37554140) for, in addition to her work on the Kodaira conjecture, solving the generic case of [Green's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2980541#Green's_Conjecture) on the [syzygies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syzygy_%28mathematics%29) of the canonical embedding of an algebraic curve.[^o8] This case of Green's conjecture had received considerable attention from algebraic geometers for over two decades prior to its resolution by Voisin (the full conjecture for *arbitrary* curves is still partially open).

*Image: ClaireVoisinBMC2014.JPG.* Voisin at [Queen Mary University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_University_of_London) in 2014

She was an invited speaker at the 1994 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) in the section 'Algebraic Geometry', and she was also invited as a plenary speaker at the 2010 International Congress of Mathematicians in [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad).[^o9]

In 2014, she was elected to the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea).[^o10] She served on the Mathematical Sciences jury of the [Infosys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys_Prize) from 2017 to 2019.

In 2009 she became a member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina).[^o11] In May 2016, she was elected as a foreign associate of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o12] Also in 2016, she became the first female mathematician member of the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) and is the first holder of the Chair of Algebraic Geometry.[^o13] She received the [Gold medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS_Gold_medal) of the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) (CNRS) in September 2016. The latter is the highest scientific research award in France.[^o14] In 2017, she received the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) in Mathematical Sciences together with [János Kollár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/János_Kollár).[^o15] She was named [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) Clay Senior Scholar for 2008-2009 and Spring 2019.[^o16] She was elected [Foreign Member of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 2021.[^o17] She was elected International Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2022.[^o18] For 2023 she was awarded the [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Award) in Basic Sciences (jointly with [Yakov Eliashberg](wikipedia://en/20652422)).[^o19] In 2024 she became the first woman awarded the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize) in Mathematics.[^o20][^o21]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

She is married to the applied mathematician [Jean-Michel Coron](wikipedia://en/25110499). They have five children.[^o23]
