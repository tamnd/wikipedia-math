# Paul Schupp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Schupp"}

**Paul Eugene Schupp** (March 12, 1937 – January 24, 2022) was an American-born British [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Illinois at Urbana Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana_Champaign). He is known for his contributions to [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/6511) and the [theory of computability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_computability).[^o3]

:::

He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1966 under the direction of [Roger Lyndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Lyndon).

Together with [Roger Lyndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Lyndon) he is the coauthor of the book "Combinatorial Group Theory" which provided a comprehensive account of the subject of Combinatorial Group Theory, starting with the work of Dehn in the 1910s and to late 1970s and remains a modern standard for the subject of [small cancellation theory](wikipedia://en/19882883).[^o3] Starting 1980's he worked on problems that explored the connections between [Group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Science) and [Complexity Theory](wikipedia://en/7543). Together with [David Muller](wikipedia://en/35667779) he proved that a [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042) *G* has [context-free](wikipedia://en/6867) [word problem](wikipedia://en/33563) if and only if *G* is [virtually free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtually_free_group), which is now known as [Muller–Schupp theorem](wikipedia://en/54414184).[^o4]

In 1977, Schupp received a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship). In 2012, he was named an inaugural fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). In 2017, the conference "Groups and Computation" was organized at Stevens Institute of Technology celebrating the mathematical contributions of Paul Schupp.[^o5] Schupp died on January 24, 2022, at the age of 84.[^o6]
