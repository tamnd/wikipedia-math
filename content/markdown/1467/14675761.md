# Birkhoff–Grothendieck theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Birkhoff–Grothendieck theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Birkhoff–Grothendieck theorem** classifies [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/4794668) [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) over the complex [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578). In particular every holomorphic vector bundle over $\mathbb{CP}^1$ is a direct sum of holomorphic [line bundles](wikipedia://en/361366). The theorem was proved by (txt, Theorem 2.1),[^o3] and is more or less equivalent to [Birkhoff factorization](wikipedia://en/32023896) introduced by (txt).[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

More precisely, the statement of the theorem is as the following.

Every [holomorphic vector bundle](wikipedia://en/4794668) $\mathcal{E}$ on $\mathbb{CP}^1$ is holomorphically isomorphic to a direct sum of line bundles:[^o8]

$$\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{O}(a_1)\oplus \cdots \oplus \mathcal{O}(a_n).$$

The notation implies each summand is a [Serre twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_twist) some number of times of the [trivial bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_bundle). The representation is unique up to permuting factors.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

The same result holds in algebraic geometry for [algebraic vector bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_vector_bundle) over $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ for any field $k$.[^o11] It also holds for $\mathbb{P}^1$ with one or two orbifold points, and for chains of projective lines meeting along nodes. [^o12]

:::

::: {#o14 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

One application of this theorem is it gives a classification of all coherent sheaves on $\mathbb{CP}^1$. We have two cases, vector bundles and coherent sheaves supported along a subvariety, so $\mathcal{O}(k), \mathcal{O}_{nx}$ where n is the degree of the fat point at $x \in \mathbb{CP}^1$. Since the only subvarieties are points, we have a complete classification of [coherent sheaves](wikipedia://en/603780).

:::
