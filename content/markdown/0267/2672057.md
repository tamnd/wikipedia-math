# Artin–Schreier theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Artin–Schreier theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Artin–Schreier theory** is a branch of [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316), specifically a positive [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) analogue of [Kummer theory](wikipedia://en/581610), for Galois [extensions](wikipedia://en/11634) of degree equal to the characteristic *p*. (txt) introduced Artin–Schreier theory for extensions of prime degree *p*, and (txt) generalized it to extensions of prime power degree *p*<sup>*n*</sup>.

:::

If *K* is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of characteristic *p*, a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666), any [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) of the form

$$X^p - X - \alpha,\,$$

for $\alpha$ in *K*, is called an *Artin–Schreier polynomial*. When $\alpha\neq \beta^p-\beta$ for all $\beta \in K$, this polynomial is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/1001293) in *K*\[*X*\], and its [splitting field](wikipedia://en/174440) over *K* is a [cyclic extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_extension) of *K* of degree *p*. This follows since for any root *β*, the numbers *β* + *i*, for $1\le i\le p$, form all the roots—by [Fermat's little theorem](wikipedia://en/53453)—so the splitting field is $K(\beta)$.

Conversely, any Galois extension of *K* of degree *p* equal to the characteristic of *K* is the splitting field of an Artin–Schreier polynomial. This can be proved using additive counterparts of the methods involved in [Kummer theory](wikipedia://en/581610), replacing [Hilbert's theorem 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert's_theorem_90) by the [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) of the additive group. These extensions are called *Artin–Schreier extensions*.

Artin–Schreier extensions play a role in the theory of [solvability by radicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvability_by_radicals), in characteristic *p*, representing one of the possible classes of extensions in a solvable chain.

They also play a part in the theory of [abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties) and their [isogenies](wikipedia://en/1085176). In characteristic *p*, an isogeny of degree *p* of abelian varieties must, for their function fields, give either an Artin–Schreier extension or a [purely inseparable extension](wikipedia://en/4770099).

## Artin–Schreier–Witt extensions {#Artin–Schreier–Witt_extensions}

There is an analogue of Artin–Schreier theory which describes cyclic extensions in characteristic *p* of *p*-power degree (not just degree *p* itself), using [Witt vectors](wikipedia://en/5573710), developed by (txt).
