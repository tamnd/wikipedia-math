# Faddeev equations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Faddeev equations"}

The **Faddeev equations**, named after their discoverer [Ludvig Faddeev](wikipedia://en/4854639), describe, at once, all the possible exchanges/[interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_interaction) in a system of three [particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle) in a fully [quantum mechanical](wikipedia://en/25202) formulation. They can be [solved iteratively](wikipedia://en/15237).

:::

In general, Faddeev equations need as input a [potential](wikipedia://en/461654) that describes the interaction between two individual particles. It is also possible to introduce a term in the equation in order to take also [three-body forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-body_force) into account.

The Faddeev equations are the most often used non-[perturbative](wikipedia://en/102140) formulations of the quantum-mechanical three-body problem. Unlike the three body problem in [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586), the quantum three body problem is [uniformly](wikipedia://en/50652) soluble.

In [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics), the off the [energy shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_shell_and_off_shell) [nucleon-nucleon interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_force) has been studied by analyzing (n,2n) and (p,2p) reactions on [deuterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium) targets, using the Faddeev Equations. The nucleon-nucleon interaction is **expanded** (approximated) as a [series](wikipedia://en/15287) of separable potentials. The [Coulomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulomb's_law) interaction between two protons is a special problem, in that its expansion in separable potentials does not converge, but this is handled by matching the Faddeev solutions to long range Coulomb solutions, instead of to [plane waves](wikipedia://en/41559).

**Separable** potentials are interactions that do not preserve a particle's location. Ordinary **local** [potentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential) can be expressed as sums of separable potentials. The physical nucleon-nucleon interaction, which involves exchange of [mesons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meson), is not expected to be either local or separable.
