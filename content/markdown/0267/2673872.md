# Logics for computability

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Logics for computability"}

**Logics for computability** are formulations of [logic](wikipedia://en/19636) that capture some aspect of [computability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computability) as a basic notion. This usually involves a mix of special [logical connectives](wikipedia://en/18153) as well as a [semantics](wikipedia://en/29107) that explains how the logic is to be interpreted in a computational way.

:::

Probably the first formal treatment of logic for computability is the *[realizability interpretation](wikipedia://en/11009758)* by [Stephen Kleene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Kleene) in 1945, who gave an interpretation of [intuitionistic number theory](wikipedia://en/2545815) in terms of [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403) computations. His motivation was to make precise the *[Heyting–Brouwer–Kolmogorov (BHK) interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BHK_interpretation)* of intuitionism, according to which proofs of mathematical statements are to be viewed as constructive procedures.

With the rise of many other kinds of logic, such as [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) and [linear logic](wikipedia://en/579675), and novel semantic models, such as [game semantics](wikipedia://en/617121), logics for computability have been formulated in several contexts. Here we mention two.

## Modal logic for computability {#Modal_logic_for_computability}

Kleene's original realizability interpretation has received much attention among those who study connections between computability and logic. It was extended to full [higher-order](wikipedia://en/562883) [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262) by [Martin Hyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Hyland) in 1982, who constructed the [effective topos](wikipedia://en/30939514). In 2002, [Steve Awodey](wikipedia://en/26946267), [Lars Birkedal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Birkedal), and [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875) formulated a **modal logic for computability**, which extended the usual realizability interpretation with two modal operators expressing the notion of being "computably true".

## Japaridze's computability logic {#Japaridze's_computability_logic}

[Computability logic](wikipedia://en/616985) refers to a research programme initiated by [Giorgi Japaridze](wikipedia://en/1866981) in 2003. Its ambition is to redevelop logic from a game-theoretic semantics. Such a semantics sees games as formal equivalents of interactive computational problems, and their "truth" as existence of algorithmic winning strategies.
