# Bel decomposition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bel decomposition"}

In [semi-Riemannian geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-Riemannian_geometry), the **Bel decomposition**, taken with respect to a specific [timelike congruence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timelike_congruence), is a way of breaking up the [Riemann tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_tensor) of a [pseudo-Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/287555) into lower order tensors with properties similar to the [electric field](wikipedia://en/41092) and [magnetic field](wikipedia://en/36563). Such a decomposition was partially described by Alphonse Matte in 1953[^o2] and by [Lluis Bel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lluis_Bel) in 1958.[^o3]

:::

This decomposition is particularly important in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). This is the case of four-dimensional [Lorentzian manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold), for which there are only three pieces with simple properties and individual physical interpretations.

## Decomposition of the Riemann tensor {#Decomposition_of_the_Riemann_tensor}

In four dimensions the Bel decomposition of the Riemann tensor, with respect to a timelike unit vector field $\vec{X}$, not necessarily geodesic or hypersurface orthogonal, consists of three pieces:

1. the *electrogravitic tensor* $E[\vec{X}]_{ab} = R_{ambn} \, X^m \, X^n$
   - Also known as the **[tidal tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_tensor)**. It can be physically interpreted as giving the tidal stresses on small bits of a material object (which may also be acted upon by other physical forces), or the tidal accelerations of a small cloud of [test particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_particle) in a [vacuum solution](wikipedia://en/1441464) or [electrovacuum solution](wikipedia://en/2424431).
1. the *magnetogravitic tensor* $B[\vec{X}]_{ab} = {{}^\star R}_{ambn} \, X^m \, X^n$
   - Can be interpreted physically as a specifying possible [spin-spin forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin-spin_force) on spinning bits of matter, such as spinning [test particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_particle).
1. the *topogravitic tensor* $L[\vec{X}]_{ab} = {{}^\star R^\star}_{ambn} \, X^m \, X^n$
   - Can be interpreted as representing the sectional curvatures for the spatial part of a frame field.

Because these are all *transverse* (i.e. projected to the spatial hyperplane elements orthogonal to our timelike unit vector field), they can be represented as linear operators on three-dimensional vectors, or as three-by-three real matrices. They are respectively symmetric, [traceless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traceless), and symmetric (6,8,6 linearly independent components, for a total of 20). If we write these operators as **E**, **B**, **L** respectively, the principal invariants of the Riemann tensor are obtained as follows:

- $K_1/4$ is the trace of **E**<sup>2</sup> + **L**<sup>2</sup> - 2 **B** **B**<sup>T</sup>,
- $-K_2/8$ is the trace of **B** ( **E** - **L** ),
- $K_3/8$ is the trace of **E** **L** - **B**<sup>2</sup>.
