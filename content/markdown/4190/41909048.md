# Toponome

Toponymy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toponome"}

The **toponome** is the [spatial network](wikipedia://en/1708671) code of [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein) and other [biomolecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomolecule) in morphologically intact [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28biology%29) and [tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_%28biology%29).[^o2] It is mapped and decoded by [imaging cycler microscopy](wikipedia://en/42191418) (ICM) in situ able to co-map many thousand [supermolecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supramolecular_chemistry#History) in one sample (tissue section or cell sample at high subcellular resolution). The term "toponome" is derived from the [ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) nouns "topos" (τόπος: "place, position") and "nomos" (νόμος: "law"), and the term "[toponomics](wikipedia://en/41909058)" refers to the study of the toponome. It was introduced by Walter Schubert in 2003.[^o3] It addresses the fact that the network of biomolecules in cells and tissues follows [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) rules enabling coordinated actions. For example, the [cell surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_membrane) toponome provides the spatial protein interaction code for the execution of a cell movement, a "code of conduct".[^o3][^o4][^o5] This is intrinsically dependent on the specific spatial arrangement of similar and dissimilar compositions of supermolecules (compositional periodicity) with a specific spatial order along a cell surface membrane. This spatial order is periodically repeated when the cell tries to enter the exploratory state from the spherical state (spatial periodicity).[^o6] This spatial toponome code is hierarchically organized with lead biomolecule(s), anti-colocated (absent) biomolecule(s)[^o3][^o4] and wildcard molecules which are variably associated with the lead biomolecule(s). It has been shown that inhibition of lead molecule(s) in a surface membrane leads to disassembly of the corresponding biomolecular network and loss of function.[^o4][^o5]

:::
