# Toponomics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toponomics"}

**Toponomics** is a discipline in [systems biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_biology), [molecular cell biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_biology), and [histology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histology) concerning the study of the [toponome](wikipedia://en/41909048) of organisms.[^o2][^o3] It is the field of study that proposes to decode the complete toponome in health and disease (the human toponome project)[^o4]—which is the next big challenge in human [biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology) after having decoded the human genome.[^o4][^o5]

:::

A **toponome** is the [spatial network](wikipedia://en/1708671) code of proteins and other [biomolecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomolecule) in [morphologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphology_%28biology%29) intact cells and tissues.[^o3][^o6]

The spatial organization of biomolecules in cells is directly revealed by [imaging cycler microscopy](wikipedia://en/42191418) with parameter- and dimension-unlimited functional resolution. The resulting toponome structures are hierarchically organized and can be described by a three symbol code.[^o2][^o6][^o7][^o8]

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The terms *toponome* and *toponomics* were introduced in 2003 by Walter Schubert based on observations with [imaging cycler microscopes (ICM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaging_Cycler_Microscopy).[^o2]

*Toponome* derived from the ancient Greek nouns *topos* (τόπος, 'place, position') and **nomos*' (νόμος, 'law'). Hence*toponomics*is a descriptive term addressing the fact that the spatial network of biomolecules in cells follows [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) rules enabling coordinated actions.[^o2]***
