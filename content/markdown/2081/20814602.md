# Collapse (topology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Collapse"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of mathematics, a **collapse** reduces a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686) (or more generally, a [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022)) to a [homotopy-equivalent](wikipedia://en/212250#Homotopy_equivalence) subcomplex. Collapses, like CW complexes themselves, were invented by [J. H. C. Whitehead](wikipedia://en/385179).[^o2] Collapses find applications in [computational homology](wikipedia://en/1248833#Computational_homology).[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $K$ be an [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105).

Suppose that $\tau, \sigma$ are two simplices of $K$ such that the following two conditions are satisfied:

1. $\tau \subsetneq \sigma,$ in particular $\dim \tau < \dim \sigma;$
1. $\sigma$ is a maximal face of $K$ and no other maximal face of $K$ contains $\tau,$

then $\tau$ is called a **free face**.

A simplicial **collapse** of $K$ is the removal of all simplices $\gamma$ such that $\tau \subseteq \gamma \subseteq \sigma,$ where $\tau$ is a free face. If additionally we have $\dim \tau = \dim \sigma - 1,$ then this is called an **elementary collapse**.

A simplicial complex that has a sequence of collapses leading to a point is called **collapsible**. Every collapsible complex is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414), but the converse is not true.

This definition can be extended to [CW-complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex) and is the basis for the concept of [simple-homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/20897472).[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Complexes that do not have a free face cannot be collapsible. Two such interesting examples are [R. H. Bing](wikipedia://en/967353)'s [house with two rooms](wikipedia://en/4446017) and [Christopher Zeeman](wikipedia://en/871415)'s [dunce hat](wikipedia://en/5148246); they are [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible) (homotopy equivalent to a point), but not collapsible.
- Any *n*-dimensional [PL manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PL_manifold) that is collapsible is in fact piecewise-linearly isomorphic to an *n*-ball.[^o2]
