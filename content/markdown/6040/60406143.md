# Song Sun

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Song Sun"}

Song Sun

**Song Sun** (, born in 1987) is a Chinese mathematician whose research concerns geometry and topology. A [Sloan Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellow), he was a professor in the Department of Mathematics of the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) from 2018 until 2023. In 2019, he was awarded the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075). As of 2024, Sun is a professor at the Institute for Advanced Study in Mathematics (IASM), [Zhejiang University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang_University).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Sun attended Huaining High School in [Huaining County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaining_County), [Anhui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhui), China, before being admitted to the [Special Class for the Gifted Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Class_for_the_Gifted_Young) at the [University of Science and Technology of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Science_and_Technology_of_China) in 2002.[^o5] After graduating from the program with a B.S. in 2006, he moved to the United States to pursue graduate studies at the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin), obtaining his Ph.D. in mathematics ([differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625)) in 2010.[^o5][^o6] His doctoral advisor was [Xiuxiong Chen](wikipedia://en/60414667), and his dissertation was titled "[Kempf–Ness theorem](wikipedia://en/37702763) and uniqueness of extremal metrics".[^o7]

Sun worked as a research associate at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) before becoming an assistant professor at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University) in 2013.[^o6] He was awarded the [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) in 2014.[^o6] In 2018, he was appointed an associate professor at the Department of Mathematics of the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley),[^o8] where he was promoted to full professor in 2021.

He was an invited speaker at the 2018 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Rio de Janeiro.[^o9] In 2021, he was awarded the New Horizons Prize in Mathematics by the [Breakthrough Prize Foundation](wikipedia://en/43125933), for many groundbreaking contributions to complex differential geometry, including existence results for Kähler-Einstein metrics and connections with moduli questions and singularities.[^o10]

In January 2024, he returned to China and joined the Institute for Advanced Study in Mathematics (IASM) at [Zhejiang University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang_University).[^o11]

## Conjecture on Fano manifolds and Veblen Prize {#Conjecture_on_Fano_manifolds_and_Veblen_Prize}

In 2019, Sun was awarded the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075), together with his former advisor [Xiuxiong Chen](wikipedia://en/60414667) and English mathematician [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305), for proving a long-standing conjecture on [Fano manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fano_manifolds), which states that "a Fano manifold admits a Kähler–Einstein metric if and only if it is K-stable". It had been one of the most actively investigated topics in geometry since a rough version of it was conjectured in the 1980s by [Fields Medalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medalist) [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137), who had previously proved the [Calabi conjecture](wikipedia://en/5263012).[^o14] The conjecture was later given a precise formulation by Donaldson, based in part on earlier work by [Gang Tian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_Tian). The solution found by Chen, Donaldson and Sun was published in the *[Journal of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society)* in 2015 as a holistic three-article series, "Kähler–Einstein metrics on Fano manifolds, I, II and III".[^o14][^o15]

:::
