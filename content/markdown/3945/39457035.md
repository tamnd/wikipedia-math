# Fontaine's period rings

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fontaine's period rings"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Fontaine's period rings** are a collection of [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346) first defined by [Jean-Marc Fontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marc_Fontaine)[^o2] that are used to classify $p$-adic [Galois representations](wikipedia://en/486929).

:::

## The ring B<sub>dR</sub> {#The_ring_BdR}

The ring $\mathbf{B}_{dR}$ is defined as follows. Let $\mathbb{C}_p$ denote the completion of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}_p}$. Let

$$\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^+ = \varprojlim_{x\mapsto x^p} \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}/(p).$$

An element of $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^+$ is a sequence $(x_1,x_2,\ldots)$ of elements $x_i\in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}/(p)$ such that $x_{i+1}^p \equiv x_i \!\!\!\pmod p$. There is a natural projection map $f:\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^+ \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}/(p)$ given by $f(x_1,x_2,\dotsc) = x_1$. There is also a multiplicative (but not additive) map $t:\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^+\to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}$ defined by

$$t(x_1,x_2,\dotsc) = \lim_{i\to \infty} \tilde x_i^{p^i},$$

where the $\tilde x_i$ are arbitrary lifts of the $x_i$ to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}$. The composite of $t$ with the projection $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}/(p)$ is just $f$.

The general theory of [Witt vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witt_vectors) yields a unique [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) $\theta:W(\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^+) \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}$ such that $\theta([x]) = t(x)$ for all $x\in \tilde{\mathbf{E}}^+$, where $[x]$ denotes the [Teichmüller representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teichmüller_representative) of $x$. The ring $\mathbf{B}_{dR}^+$ is defined to be completion of $\tilde{\mathbf{B}}^+ = W(\tilde{\mathbf{E}}^+)[1/p]$ with respect to the ideal $\ker( \theta : \tilde{\mathbf{B}}^+ \to \mathbb{C}_p)$. Finally, the field $\mathbf{B}_{dR}$ is just the [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) of $\mathbf{B}_{dR}^+$.
