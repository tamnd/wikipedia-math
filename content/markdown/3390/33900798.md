# List of formulas in elementary geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of formulas in elementary geometry"}

This is a short list of some common mathematical shapes and figures and the formulas that describe them.

:::

## Two-dimensional shapes {#Two-dimensional_shapes}

::: {#o3 .table cue=table}

| Shape | [Area](wikipedia://en/1209) | Perimeter/Circumference | Meanings of symbols |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Square](wikipedia://en/659939) | $l^2$ | $4l$ | $l$ is the length of a side |
| [Rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) | $lb$ | $2(l+b)$ | $l$ is length, $b$ is breadth |
| [Circle](wikipedia://en/6220) | $\pi r^2$ | $2\pi r$ or $\pi d$ | where $r$ is the radius and $d$ is the diameter |
| [Ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) | $\pi ab$ |  | where $a$ is the [semimajor axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semimajor_axis) and $b$ is the [semiminor axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiminor_axis) |
| [Triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) | $\frac{bh}{2}$ | $a+b+c$ | $b$ is base; $h$ is height; $a,b,c$ are sides |
| [Parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) | $bh$ | $2(a+b)$ | $b$ is base, $h$ is height, $a$ is side |
| [Trapezoid](wikipedia://en/201826) | $\frac{a+b}{2}h$ |  | $a$ and $b$ are the bases |

:::

## Three-dimensional shapes {#Three-dimensional_shapes}

This is a list of volume formulas of basic shapes:[^o15]p. 405–406

::: {#o16 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cone"}

- [Cone](wikipedia://en/782427) – $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$, where $r$ is the [base](wikipedia://en/9579379)'s radius and $h$ is the cone's height;

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cube"}

- [Cube](wikipedia://en/6285) – $a^3$, where $a$ is the side's length;

:::

::: {#o18 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cuboid"}

- [Cuboid](wikipedia://en/156802) – $abc$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are the sides' length;

:::

::: {#o19 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cylinder"}

- [Cylinder](wikipedia://en/859283) – $\pi r^2 h$, where $r$ is the base's radius and $h$ is the cylinder's height;

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=list-entry name="Ellipsoid"}

- [Ellipsoid](wikipedia://en/145381) – $\frac{4}{3}\pi abc$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are the [semi-major and semi-minor axes](wikipedia://en/23409699)' length;

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sphere"}

- [Sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) – $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$, where $r$ is the radius;

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=list-entry name="Parallelepiped"}

- [Parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975) – $abc\sqrt{K}$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are the sides' length,$K = 1 + 2\cos(\alpha)\cos(\beta)\cos(\gamma) - \cos^2(\alpha) - \cos^2(\beta) - \cos^2(\gamma)$, and $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$ are angles between the two sides;

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=list-entry name="Prism"}

- [Prism](wikipedia://en/283007) – $Bh$, where $B$ is the base's area and $h$ is the prism's height;

:::

::: {#o24 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pyramid"}

- [Pyramid](wikipedia://en/3307045) – $\frac{1}{3}Bh$, where $B$ is the base's area and $h$ is the pyramid's height;

:::

::: {#o25 .entry cue=list-entry name="Tetrahedron"}

- [Tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) – ${\sqrt{2}\over12}a^3$, where $a$ is the side's length.

:::

### Sphere {#Sphere}

The basic quantities describing a sphere (meaning a [2-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782), a 2-dimensional surface inside 3-dimensional space) will be denoted by the following variables

- $r$ is the radius,

::: {#o27 .entry cue=list-entry name="circumference"}

- $C = 2 \pi r$ is the [circumference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumference_of_a_sphere) (the length of any one of its [great circles](wikipedia://en/48082)),

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="Surface area"}

- $S$ is the [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725),

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=list-entry name="Volume"}

- $V$ is the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498).

:::

**Surface area**:

$$\begin{alignedat}{4} S &= 4 \pi r^2 \\[0.3ex] &= \frac{1}{\pi} C^2 \\[0.3ex] &= \sqrt[3]{\pi (6 V)^2} \\[0.3ex] \end{alignedat}$$

**Volume**:

$$\begin{alignedat}{4} V &= \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \\[0.3ex] &= \frac{1}{6 \pi^2} C^3 \\[0.3ex] &= \frac{1}{6 \sqrt{\pi}} S^{3/2} \\[0.3ex] \end{alignedat}$$

**Radius**:

$$\begin{alignedat}{4} r &= \frac{1}{2 \pi} C \\[0.3ex] &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{4 \pi} S} \\[0.3ex] &= \sqrt[3]{\frac{3}{4 \pi} V} \\[0.3ex] \end{alignedat}$$

**[Circumference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumference_of_a_sphere)**:

$$\begin{alignedat}{4} C &= 2 \pi r \\[0.3ex] &= \sqrt{\pi S} \\[0.3ex] &= \sqrt[3]{\pi^2 6 V} \\[0.3ex] \end{alignedat}$$
