# Existential generalization

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Existential generalization"}

**Existential generalization.**

There exists a member $x$ in a universal set with a property of $Q$

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Existential generalization"}

In [predicate logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_logic), **existential generalization**[^o3][^o4] (also known as **existential introduction**, **∃I**) is a [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742) [rule of inference](wikipedia://en/252311) that allows one to move from a specific statement, or one instance, to a quantified generalized statement, or [existential proposition](wikipedia://en/91420). In [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983), it is often used as a rule for the [existential quantifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_quantifier) ($\exists$) in formal proofs.

:::

Example: "Rover loves to wag his tail. Therefore, something loves to wag its tail."

Example: "Alice made herself a cup of tea. Therefore, Alice made someone a cup of tea."

Example: "Alice made herself a cup of tea. Therefore, someone made someone a cup of tea."

In the [Fitch-style calculus](wikipedia://en/7316682):

$$Q(a) \to\ \exists{x}\, Q(x) ,$$

where $Q(a)$ is obtained from $Q(x)$ by replacing all its free occurrences of $x$ (or some of them) by $a$.[^o5]

## Quine {#Quine}

According to [Willard Van Orman Quine](wikipedia://en/33833), [universal instantiation](wikipedia://en/1564194) and existential generalization are two aspects of a single principle, for instead of saying that $\forall x \, x=x$ implies $\text{Socrates}=\text{Socrates}$, we could as well say that the denial $\text{Socrates} \ne \text{Socrates}$ implies $\exists x \, x \ne x$. The principle embodied in these two operations is the link between [quantifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantification_%28logic%29) and the singular statements that are related to them as instances. Yet it is a principle only by courtesy. It holds only in the case where a term names and, furthermore, occurs [referentially](wikipedia://en/20110874#Semantics).[^o7]
