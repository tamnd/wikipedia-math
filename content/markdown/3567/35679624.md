# Eilenberg–Ganea theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eilenberg–Ganea theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), the **Eilenberg–Ganea theorem** states for every finitely generated group *G* with certain conditions on its [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) (namely $3\le \operatorname{cd}(G)\le n$), one can construct an [aspherical](wikipedia://en/7147287) [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) *X* of dimension *n* whose [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) is *G*. The theorem is named after Polish mathematician [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501) and Romanian mathematician [Tudor Ganea](wikipedia://en/8185502). The theorem was first published in a short paper in 1957 in the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)*.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

**Group cohomology:** Let $G$ be a group and let $X=K(G,1)$ be the corresponding [Eilenberg−MacLane space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg−MacLane_space). Then we have the following singular [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) which is a [free resolution](wikipedia://en/19745659) of $\mathbb{Z}$ over the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) $\mathbb{Z}[G]$ (where $\mathbb{Z}$ is a trivial $\mathbb{Z}[G]$-module):

$$\cdots \xrightarrow{\delta_n+1} C_n(E)\xrightarrow{\delta_n} C_{n-1}(E)\rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow C_1(E)\xrightarrow{\delta_1} C_0(E)\xrightarrow{\varepsilon} \mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0,$$

where $E$ is the universal cover of $X$ and $C_k(E)$ is the [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) generated by the singular $k$-chains on $E$. The [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) of the group $G$ with coefficient in a $\mathbb{Z}[G]$-module $M$ is the cohomology of this [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) with coefficients in $M$, and is denoted by $H^*(G,M)$.

**Cohomological dimension:** A group $G$ has cohomological dimension $n$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}$ (denoted by $\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G)$) if

$$n=\sup \{k : \text{There exists a }\mathbb{Z}[G]\text{ module }M\text{ with }H^{k}(G,M)\neq 0\}.$$

**Fact:** If $G$ has a [projective resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_resolution) of length at most $n$, i.e., $\mathbb{Z}$ as trivial $\mathbb{Z}[G]$ module has a projective resolution of length at most $n$ if and only if $H^i_{\mathbb{Z}}(G,M)=0$ for all $\mathbb{Z}$-modules $M$ and for all $i>n$.

Therefore, we have an alternative definition of cohomological dimension as follows,

*The cohomological dimension of G with coefficient in* $\mathbb{Z}$ *is the smallest n (possibly infinity) such that G has a projective resolution of length* *n*, i.e., $\mathbb{Z}$ *has a projective resolution of length* *n* *as a trivial* $\mathbb{Z}[G]$ *module.*

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Eilenberg−Ganea theorem"}

## Eilenberg−Ganea theorem {#Eilenberg−Ganea_theorem}

Let $G$ be a finitely presented group and $n\ge 3$ be an integer. Suppose the [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) of $G$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}$ is at most $n$, i.e., $\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G)\le n$. Then there exists an $n$-dimensional [aspherical](wikipedia://en/7147287) [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) $X$ such that the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of $X$ is $G$, i.e., $\pi_1(X)=G$.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Converse"}

## Converse {#Converse}

Converse of this theorem is a consequence of [cellular homology](wikipedia://en/2113969), and the fact that every free module is projective.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** Let *X* be an aspherical *n*-dimensional CW complex with *π*<sub>1</sub>(*X*) = *G*, then cd<sub>*Z*</sub>(*G*) ≤ *n*.

:::

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Related results and conjectures"}

## Related results and conjectures {#Related_results_and_conjectures}

For *n* = 1 the result is one of the consequences of [Stallings theorem about ends of groups](wikipedia://en/19196631).[^o12]

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** Every finitely generated group of cohomological dimension one is free.

For $n=2$ the statement is known as the [Eilenberg–Ganea conjecture](wikipedia://en/8211999).

**Eilenberg−Ganea Conjecture:** If a group *G* has cohomological dimension 2 then there is a 2-dimensional aspherical CW complex *X* with $\pi_1(X)=G$.

It is known that given a group *G* with $\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G)=2$, there exists a 3-dimensional aspherical CW complex *X* with $\pi_1(X)=G$.
