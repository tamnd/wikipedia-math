# MPDATA

*Image: MPDATA numerical scheme example.gif.* Comparison of UPWIND (top panel), basic MPDATA (middle panel) and 3-iteration non-oscillatory MPDATA (bottom panel) applied to transport of a cone-shaped signal in a two-dimensional stationary rotational flow ("Molenkamp test")

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="MPDATA"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), **MPDATA** is a family of [iterative](wikipedia://en/15237) [finite-difference](wikipedia://en/6054681)/[finite-volume](wikipedia://en/345017) methods for [numerical integration](wikipedia://en/170089) of [hyperbolic differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_differential_equation) modelling [conservation laws](wikipedia://en/6956) of the form:

:::

::: {#o2 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\partial_t (G\psi) + \nabla \cdot (\mathbf{v}\psi) = GR, \tag{1}$$

:::

where $\psi(\mathbf{x}, t)$ is an [advected](wikipedia://en/166377) [scalar field](wikipedia://en/161879) (or *advectee*); $\mathbf{v}=G\dot{\mathbf{x}}$ is the [flow velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_velocity) [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) (or *advector*), $G(\mathbf{x})$ may either play the role of the [fluid density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_density), the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/22705596) of [coordinate transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_transformation) from [Cartesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) to [curvilinear](wikipedia://en/755300) framework $\mathbf{x}$, or their product; $R(\mathbf{x})$ combines all source terms.[^o3]

MPDATA stands for Multidimensional Positive Definite Advection Transport Algorithm. The algorithm was formulated by Piotr K. Smolarkiewicz[^o4][^o5] (and is also referred to as Smolarkiewicz's method[^o6]) at the [US National Center for Atmospheric Research - NCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Atmospheric_Research) (at the time, Smolarkiewicz was a fellow of the NCAR Advanced Study Program and a recent graduate from [Krzysztof Haman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzysztof_Edward_Haman)'s group at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw); the seminal 1983 and 1984 MPDATA publications mention both institutions).

The crux of the method lies in iterative application of the [upwind scheme](wikipedia://en/14645977). The first iteration employs the advective velocity $\mathbf{v}$, while each subsequent iteration employs a so-called antidiffusive velocity which corrects solution from prior iteration reducing the [numerical diffusion](wikipedia://en/2785898). The antidiffusive velocities can be derived through modified equation analysis[^o7] of the [upwind scheme](wikipedia://en/14645977) and feature cross-dimensional dependencies (i.e., applying MPDATA in multiple dimensions is not equivalent to application of one-dimensional MPDATA in all dimensions), the scheme is thus not [dimensionally split](wikipedia://en/44583545), hence "M" in the algorithm name. Since each iteration of the scheme constitutes a [forward-in-time](wikipedia://en/3652485) upwind pass, the scheme inherits characteristics of the upwind scheme: [CFL stability criterion](wikipedia://en/2029932), conservativeness, [embarrassingly parallel](wikipedia://en/1738712) domain decomposition, and sign-preservation. For non-negative fields $\psi$, sign-preservation translates to [positive definiteness](wikipedia://en/649616), hence the "PD" in the algorithm name. Application of the corrective iterations improves scheme [convergence rate](wikipedia://en/999701) compared with first-order upwind. Depending on the MPDATA variant, [convergence](wikipedia://en/999701) of up to third-order in time and space can be achieved.[^o8]

While the original formulation of MPDATA employed [structured grids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structured_grid), the algorithm has been subsequently also formulated for [unstructured grids](wikipedia://en/3772072).[^o9] Despite being formulated for and named in reference to [advection](wikipedia://en/166377) problems, as any other advection numerical scheme, MPDATA also applies to solutions of [advection-diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convection-diffusion_equation) as well as [diffusion-only](wikipedia://en/645335) problems if [Fickian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fickian) diffusive terms are expressed as advective fluxes[^o10] (approach referred to as the pseudo-velocity technique[^o11][^o12]).

## Description of the basic scheme in 1D {#Description_of_the_basic_scheme_in_1D}

MPDATA is inherently multi-dimensional, and primarily used in [computational fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/305924) where the advective volocities and problem geometries are variable in time. Still, the key idea underlying the MPDATA approach can be conveyed with a basic example of [solenoidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solenoidal) [stationary flow](wikipedia://en/11034#Steady_versus_unsteady_flow) in one dimension[^o14] (i.e., $\mathbf{v}=[u]$ constant in time and space), without coordinate transformation ($G=1$), for the case of [homogeneous](wikipedia://en/6954092) advection ($R=0$) of a nonnegative scalar field ($\psi \ge 0$), with the following flux form of the [advection equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advection_equation):

::: {#o15 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$\partial_t \psi + \partial_x (u\psi) = 0. \tag{2}$$

:::

[Upwind](wikipedia://en/14645977) [discretisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discretisation) of the problem on a [regular](wikipedia://en/3771917) [staggered grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staggered_grid) with a time step $\Delta t$ and a grid step $\Delta x$, with $n=t/\Delta t$, $i=x/\Delta x$, and the half-integer spatial indices corresponding to grid-cell walls:

|  | $u_{i-3/2}$ |  | $u_{i-1/2}$ |  | $u_{i+1/2}$ |  | $u_{i+3/2}$ |  |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ... | \\| | **·** | \\| | **·** | \\| | **·** | \\| | ... |
|  |  | $\psi_{i-1}$ |  | $\psi_{i}$ |  | $\psi_{i+1}$ |  |  |

can be formulated with:

::: {#o16 .equation cue=numblk name="3"}

$$\frac{\psi^{n+1}_i - \psi^{n}_i}{\Delta t} +\frac{\overbrace{f(\psi_i^n, \psi^n_{i+1}, u^n_{i+1/2})}^\text{right-hand wall flux} - \overbrace{f(\psi_{i-1}^n, \psi^n_{i}, u^n_{i-1/2})}^\text{left-hand wall flux}}{\Delta x}=0 \tag{3}$$

:::

with the [flux](wikipedia://en/43590) function defined using [positive and negative parts](wikipedia://en/3344921) of $u_{i\pm 1/2}$ as:

::: {#o17 .equation cue=numblk name="4"}

$$f(\psi_l, \psi_r, u) = \!\!\overbrace{\frac{u+|u|}{2}}^\text{positive part}\! \psi_l + \!\!\overbrace{\frac{u-|u|}{2}}^\text{negative part} \!\psi_r. \tag{4}$$

:::

Introducing the [non-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-dimensional_number) [Courant number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_number) $C=u {\Delta t} / {\Delta x}$, the resultant [explicit-in-time](wikipedia://en/3652485) scheme (referred to as "upwind", "upstream" or "donor-cell"), for a constant $C$ reads:

::: {#o18 .equation cue=numblk name="5"}

$$\psi_i^{n+1} = \psi_i^{n} - \left[ \frac{C+|C|}{2}(\psi^n_i-\psi^n_{i-1}) + \frac{C-|C|}{2}(\psi^n_{i+1}-\psi^n_i) \right] \tag{5}$$

:::

which is conservative, sing-preserving and stable for $|C| \le 1$. However, the scheme is only [first-order accurate](wikipedia://en/999701) in space ($\Delta x$) and time ($\Delta t$), and incurs [numerical diffusion](wikipedia://en/2785898) which can be quantified with the modified-equation analysis by substituting the discretised values other than $\psi_i^n$ in ((5)) with their [Taylor expansions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_expansion) (using the [Big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578)):

::: {#o19 .equation cue=numblk name="6"}

$$\begin{aligned} \psi_i^{n+1} &= \psi_i^{n} + \frac{\left.\partial_t\psi\right|_i^n}{1!} (\,+\Delta t\,) + \frac{\left.\partial^2_t\psi\right|_i^n}{2!} (\,+\Delta t\,)^2 + O(\Delta t^3) \\ \psi_{i+1}^n &= \psi_i^n + \frac{\left.\partial_x\psi\right|_i^n}{1!} (+\Delta x) + \frac{\left.\partial^2_x\psi\right|_i^n}{2!} (+\Delta x)^2 + O(\Delta x^3)\\ \psi_{i-1}^n &= \psi_i^n + \frac{\left.\partial_x\psi\right|_i^n}{1!} (-\Delta x) + \frac{\left.\partial^2_x\psi\right|_i^n}{2!} (-\Delta x)^2 + O(\Delta x^3) \end{aligned} \tag{6}$$

:::

which yields (up to second-order terms):

::: {#o20 .equation cue=numblk name="7"}

$$\begin{aligned} \left.\partial_t\psi\right|_i^n \Delta t + \underbrace{\partial^2_t\psi}_{u^2\partial_x^2\psi}|_i^n \frac{\Delta t^2}{2} &= \frac{C}{2} \left( \psi^n_{i-1} - \psi^n_{i+1}\right) + \frac{|C|}{2} \left( \psi^n_{i-1} - 2 \psi_i^n + \psi^n_{i+1}\right)\\ &=- \,C\, \Delta x \, \partial_x \left.\psi\right|^n_i + \frac{|C|}{2} \,\Delta x^2 \, \partial_x^2\left.\psi\right|^n_i \end{aligned} \tag{7}$$

:::

in which the highlighted second-order time derivative can be replaced with second-order spatial derivative using the [Cauchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy)-[Kovalevskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Kovalevskaya) procedure (i.e., by substituting eq. ((2)) into a time-derivative of eq. ((2)) giving $\partial^2_t \psi = -u \partial_x \partial_t \psi = u^2 \partial^2_x \psi$) yielding the following, so-called, modified equation:

::: {#o21 .equation cue=numblk name="8"}

$$\partial_t\psi|_i^n + u \partial_x \psi|_i^n = \underbrace{\left(|u| \frac{\Delta x}{2} - u^2 \frac{\Delta t}{2}\right)}_{k}\, \partial_x^2 \psi|_i^n \tag{8}$$

:::

which confirms that, as $\Delta t \rightarrow 0$ and $\Delta x \rightarrow 0$, the upwind scheme approximates eq. ((2)), but the [truncation error](wikipedia://en/9366097) has a leading-order [Fickian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fickian) source term with a coefficient of [numerical diffusion](wikipedia://en/2785898) given by $k$.[^o22]

The concept of MPDATA lies in reversing the effect of [numerical diffusion](wikipedia://en/2785898) by using the pseudo-velocity technique[^o11][^o12] that allows to express diffusion terms in transport problems using an equation of the same form as ((2)). To this end, Smolarkiewicz introduced the anti-diffusive pseudo velocity (note the opposite sign stemming from, de facto, integrating backwards in time to reverse the effect of diffusion):

::: {#o23 .equation cue=numblk name="9"}

$$\partial_t\psi + \partial_x \big(\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\underbrace{k \frac{\partial_x \psi}{\psi}}_\text{anti-diffusive velocity} \!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\psi\big) = 0 \tag{9}$$

:::

and proposed discretisation allowing to perform a corrective step using upwind integration with an anti-diffusive [Courant number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_number) field:

::: {#o24 .equation cue=numblk name="10"}

$$C^{m+1}_{i+ 1/2 } = \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x} k^m_{i+ 1/2} \left.\frac{\partial_x \psi}{\psi}\right|^m_{i+ 1/2} \approx \begin{cases} 0 &\text{ if } \psi^m_{i+1}+\psi^m_{i} = 0\\ \left[|C^m_{i+1/2}|-(C^m_{i+1/2})^2\right]\frac{\psi^m_{i+1}-\psi^m_i}{\psi^m_{i+1}+\psi^m_i} &\text{ otherwise } \end{cases} \tag{10}$$

:::

where $m > 0$ numbers algorithm iterations. The first pass of the scheme is an ordinary upwind integration using $C^{m=1}=C$ and yielding $\psi^{m=1}$. In the second pass, the values of the anti-diffusive Courant number $C^{m=2}$ are computed based on $C^{m=1}$ and $\psi^{m=1}$, and used to perform an upwind anti-diffusive pass yielding $\psi^{m=2}$. Note that even for constant-in-$x$ physical $C^{m=1}$, the anti-diffusive $C^{m=1}$ varies in $x$ (and thus corresponds to a divergent flow). Subsequent iterations are correcting the integration of the anti-diffusive corrections from previous passes. If $M$ iterations are used, $\psi^{m=M}$ is used as the result of integration of the advective term over one $\Delta t$.

Note that despite a constant flow field, the anti-diffusive velocity varies in space and time and is not solenoidal.

The values of $\psi^n_{i-1}$ at the left edge of the domain, and $\psi^n_{i+1}$ at the right edge of the domain are obtained by specifying [boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_conditions_in_fluid_dynamics).

## Algorithm variants and techniques used in concert with MPDATA {#Algorithm_variants_and_techniques_used_in_concert_with_MPDATA}

The basic formulation of the scheme has been extended yielding a multitude of MPDATA variants, including (in chronological order of literature references):

- Divergent-flow correction applicable for non-solenoidal flows (including the antidiffusive velocities).[^o5]
- Double-Pass Donor Cell (DPDC) in which an analytical approximation of the sum of all corrective iterations is used to define a single corrective pass[^o26]
- Non-oscillatory variant featuring a [flux limiter](wikipedia://en/5678338) to ensure monotone solutions[^o27]
- Infinite-gauge variant for transport of variable-sign fields (e.g., momentum)[^o28]
- third-order accurate anti-diffusive velocities for a wide range of flow types.[^o8]

Note that some of the non-basic variants of the algorithm have larger [stencils](wikipedia://en/18558187)) extent than the original formulation.

Some of the pertinent numerical techniques used together with MPDATA include:

- application to problems featuring pressure-gradient source terms (e.g., [Boussinesq approximation (buoyancy)](wikipedia://en/393813) of the [Navier-Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier-Stokes) equations)
- [immersed boundary method](wikipedia://en/17943941)[^o29]
- [adaptive mesh refinement](wikipedia://en/1822182) controlled by antidiffusive velocities.[^o30]

## Applications {#Applications}

Equation ((1)), which is numerically approximated with MPDATA, is used in modelling a wide range of [transport phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_phenomena) across different [scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_scales) and . MPDATA has documented applications in the following domains:

- [numerical weather prediction](wikipedia://en/1505381) (NWP) systems:
  - compressible dynamical core for the COSMO system[^o33]
  - nonhydrostatic finite-volume dynamical core for the [ECMWF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECMWF) [Integrated Forecast System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Forecast_System) (IFS)[^o34]
- ocean modelling:
  - passive tracer advection in terrain-following coordinates in the [Regional Ocean Modeling System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Ocean_Modeling_System) (ROMS)[^o35]
- [large eddy simulations](wikipedia://en/1178438):
  - moist atmospheric flows (including representation of cloud and precipitation processes)[^o36]
  - [multi-phase](wikipedia://en/5587769) [particle-laden flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle-laden_flows) (e.g., [contrail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrail) formation,[^o37] aerosol-cloud-precipitation interactions[^o38])
  - turbulent [magnetohydrodynamics](wikipedia://en/40310)[^o39] (including [Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_current) magnetohydrodynamics[^o40])
  - simulations of wind-driven [sand dune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_dune) dynamics[^o41]
  - simulations of flows past complex structures (e.g. the [Pentagon building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagon_building)[^o29] and the [Palace of Culture and Science in Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Culture_and_Science_in_Warsaw)[^o42])
- [direct numerical simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_numerical_simulation):
  - multi-phase flows (fog formation)[^o43]
- solvers for [shallow water equations](wikipedia://en/8631522) resolving:
  - double-gyre circulation[^o44]
  - wetting–drying areas[^o45]
- [aerosol size-spectrum dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosol#Dynamics):
  - advection problem in particle size dimension[^o46]
  - two-dimensional advection in size-spectral and spatial dimensions[^o47]
- [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680):
  - solutions to the [Black-Scholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-Scholes)-type equations (a [stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/1361454) transformed into a [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) using [Itô's lemma](wikipedia://en/203962)) cast as an advective transport problem for pricing of [European and American options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_style)[^o48]

:::

## Open-source implementations {#Open-source_implementations}

- [reusable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_reuse) MPDATA libraries and packages:
  - **libmpdata++**: structured-grid MPDATA implemented in [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++) using [Blitz++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitz++) for array handling and array-valued expressions, and [OpenMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenMP) for [multi-threading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thread_%28computing%29). [API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/API) and usage examples documented in a journal article.[^o50] Featuring distributed-memory parallelism using [Message Passing Interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Message_Passing_Interface).[^o51] Supports integration in 1-, 2- and 3-dimensional domains. Implements arbitrary number of corrective iterations, the infinite-gauge, non-oscillatory and fully-third-order variants. Features implementations of a [shallow-water equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shallow-water_equation) system solver, and a conjugate-residual [Poisson equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_equation) solver for handling pressure terms in momentum conservation equations. The library has been developed as a dynamical core for the UWLCM[^o52] [large eddy simulation](wikipedia://en/1178438) system. Released under the [GNU GPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_GPL) v3 license.
  - **PyMPDATA** structured-grid MPDATA implemented in [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) using [NumPy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NumPy) and [Numba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numba) for array handling, [just-in-time compilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just-in-time_compilation) and [multi-threading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thread_%28computing%29),[^o53] supporting distributed-memory parallelism via Numba-MPI.[^o54] Supports integration in 1-, 2- and 3-dimensional domains, arbitrary number of corrective iterations, implements infinite-gauge, non-oscillatory and double-pass-donor-cell algorithm variants. Pure-Python wheels available on [PyPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PyPI), release tarballs archived at Zenodo.[^o55] Released under the [GNU GPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_GPL) v3 license together with a set of examples in a form of [Jupyter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupyter) notebooks. Web-based [code-coupled documentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docstring) in English.[^o56]
- MPDATA implementations integrated in other software:
  - **PISM** (Parallel Ice Sheet Model, [GPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPL) v3 license) includes implementation of the non-oscillatory variant of two-dimensional MPDATA in [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++)
  - **AtmosFOAM** (a derivative of [OpenFOAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenFOAM); OpenFOAM is distributed under the [GPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPL) v3 license) features implementations of MPDATA in both [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++) and [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29)/[Numba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numba)[^o57]
  - **mpdat\_2d**: a [FORTRAN 77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FORTRAN_77) subroutine implementing 2-dimensional structured-grid MPDATA including the non-oscillatory option (likely a derivative of the original MPDATA implementation created at [NCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAR)) has been publicly released[^o58] as a part of babyEULAG code.[^o59] No documentation or [README](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/README), [unspecified license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/License-free_software) and authorship.
  - **smolar**: structured-grid MPDATA implemented as a C library supporting multi-threading and multi-process parallelism, as well as offloading of anti-diffusive velocity computation to [GPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPU) using [CUDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUDA). Code publicly available on GitHub[^o60] without release versioning, [unspecified license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/License-free_software), documentation in [Russian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) credited to [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences).
  - **advectionHPCtester**: structured-grid 3-dimensional implementation of the basic scheme in FORTRAN. Code archived on Zenodo.[^o61] Released under the [GNU LGPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_LGPL).
