# Mathematical linguistics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematical linguistics"}

**Mathematical linguistics** is the application of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) to model phenomena and solve problems in general [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) and [theoretical linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_linguistics). Mathematical linguistics has a significant amount of overlap with [computational linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_linguistics).

:::

## Discrete mathematics {#Discrete_mathematics}

[Discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) is used in language modeling, including formal grammars, language representation, and historical linguistic trends.

### Set theory {#Set_theory}

[Semantic classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_class), [word classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_of_speech), [natural classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_class), and the [allophonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allophonic) variations of each [phoneme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoneme) in a language are all examples of applied [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553). Set theory and [concatenation theory](wikipedia://en/40965300) are used extensively in phonetics and phonology.

### Combinatorics {#Combinatorics}

In [phonotactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonotactics), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) is useful for determining which sequences of phonemes are permissible in a given language, and for calculating the total number of possible syllables or words, based on a given set of phonological constraints. [Combinatorics on words](wikipedia://en/20913490) can reveal patterns within words, morphemes, and sentences.

### Finite-state transducers {#Finite-state_transducers}

Context-sensitive rewriting rules of the form *a* → *b* / *c* \_ *d*, used in linguistics to model [phonological rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonological_rule) and [sound change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_change), are computationally equivalent to [finite-state transducers](wikipedia://en/1071289), provided that application is nonrecursive, i.e. the rule is not allowed to rewrite the same substring twice.[^o6]

Weighted FSTs found applications in [natural language processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing), including [machine translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_translation), and in [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488).[^o7][^o8] An implementation for [part-of-speech tagging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part-of-speech_tagging) can be found as one component of the OpenGrm[^o9] library.

::: {#o11 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Algorithms"}

### Algorithms {#Algorithms}

[Optimality theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimality_theory) (OT) and maximum entropy (Maxent) phonotactics use [algorithmic](wikipedia://en/775) approaches when evaluating candidate forms (phoneme strings) for determining the phonotactic constraints of a language.[^o12]

:::

### Graph theory {#Graph_theory}

[Trees](wikipedia://en/48560) have several applications in linguistics, including:

- [Parsing trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parse_tree)
- [Sentence diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentence_diagram)
- [Language family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_family) trees
- [Etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology) trees

Other graphs that are used in linguistics include:

- [Weighted graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_graph_theory_terms#weighted_graph), which are used to model the lexical similarity between different languages (after computing lexicostatistics).
- [Semantic networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_network)
- [Lattice graphs](wikipedia://en/19635706), which can model [optimality theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimality_theory).

### Topology {#Topology}

The concept of topology has recently been introduced to linguistics. Semantic topology is a framework for discourse analysis that applies [circuit topology](wikipedia://en/43717327) to measure the semantic structure of sentence arrangements within a text.[^o15] By representing recurring themes through series, parallel, or cross configurations, one can uncover statistical differences in communication styles.

## Formal linguistics {#Formal_linguistics}

Formal linguistics is the branch of [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) which uses [formal languages](wikipedia://en/10939), [formal grammars](wikipedia://en/18020716) and [first-order logical](wikipedia://en/10983) expressions for the analysis of [natural languages](wikipedia://en/21173). Since the 1980s, the term is often used to refer to [Chomskyan linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chomskyan_linguistics).[^o17] Generative models of formal linguistics, such as [head-driven phrase structure grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head-driven_phrase_structure_grammar), have also been used in natural language processing.

### Logic {#Logic}

Logic is used to model [syntax](wikipedia://en/26860), [formal semantics](wikipedia://en/31395652), and [pragmatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatics). [Modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) can model syntax that employs different [grammatical moods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_mood).[^o19] Most [linguistic universals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_universals) (e.g. [Greenberg's linguistic universals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenberg's_linguistic_universals)) employ [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154). [Lexical relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexical_relations) between words can be determined based on whether a pair of words satisfies [conditional](wikipedia://en/658808) propositions.[^o20]

### Semiotics {#Semiotics}

Methods of formal linguistics were introduced by [semioticians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiotician) such as [Charles Sanders Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117) and [Louis Hjelmslev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Hjelmslev). Building on the work of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) and [Rudolf Carnap](wikipedia://en/57987), Hjelmslev proposed the use of formal grammars to analyse, generate and explain language in his 1943 book *Prolegomena to a Theory of Language*.[^o33][^o34] In this view, language is regarded as arising from a mathematical relationship between meaning and form.

The formal description of language was further developed by linguists including [J. R. Firth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._R._Firth) and [Simon Dik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Dik), giving rise to modern grammatical frameworks such as [systemic functional linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systemic_functional_linguistics) and [functional discourse grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_discourse_grammar). Computational methods have been developed by the framework [functional generative description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_generative_description) among others.

[Dependency grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependency_grammar), created by French [structuralist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_linguistics) [Lucien Tesnière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucien_Tesnière),[^o35] has been used widely in [natural language processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing).

## Differential equations and multivariate calculus {#Differential_equations_and_multivariate_calculus}

The [fast Fourier transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_recognition#Hidden_Markov_models), [Kalman filters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalman_filters), and [autoencoding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_machine_translation) are all used in signal processing (advanced phonetics, speech recognition).

## Statistics {#Statistics}

In linguistics, statistical methods are necessary to describe and validate research results, as well as to understand observations and trends within an area of study.

### Corpus statistics {#Corpus_statistics}

[Student's *t*-test](wikipedia://en/536080) can be used to determine whether the occurrence of a [collocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collocation) in a corpus is statistically significant.[^o39] For a [bigram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bigram) $w_1w_2$, let $P(w_1) = \frac{\#w_1}{N}$ be the unconditional probability of occurrence of $w_1$ in a corpus with size $N$, and let $P(w_2) = \frac{\#w_2}{N}$ be the unconditional probability of occurrence of $w_2$ in the corpus. The t-score for the bigram $w_1w_2$ is calculated as:

$$t = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu}{\sqrt{\frac{s^2}{N}}},$$

where $\bar{x} = \frac{\# w_iw_j}{N}$ is the sample mean of the occurrence of $w_1w_2$, $\#w_1w_2$ is the number of occurrences of $w_1w_2$, $\mu = P(w_i)P(w_j)$ is the probability of $w_1w_2$ under the null-hypothesis that $w_1$ and $w_2$ appear independently in the text, and $s^2 = \bar{x}(1-\bar{x}) \approx \bar{x}$ is the sample variance. With a large $N$, the *t*-test is equivalent to a [*Z*-test](wikipedia://en/332213).

### Lexicostatistics {#Lexicostatistics}

Lexicostatistics can model the lexical similarities between languages that share a language family, [sprachbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprachbund), [language contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_contact), or other historical connections.

### Quantitative linguistics {#Quantitative_linguistics}

Quantitative linguistics (QL) deals with language learning, language change, and application as well as structure of natural languages. QL investigates languages using statistical methods; its most demanding objective is the formulation of language laws and, ultimately, of a general [theory of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_language) in the sense of a set of interrelated languages laws.[^o42] [Synergetic linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synergetic_linguistics) was from its very beginning specifically designed for this purpose.[^o43] QL is empirically based on the results of language statistics, a field which can be interpreted as statistics of languages or as statistics of any linguistic object. This field is not necessarily connected to substantial theoretical ambitions. [Corpus linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_linguistics) and [computational linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_linguistics) are other fields which contribute important [empirical evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_evidence).

### Quantitative comparative linguistics {#Quantitative_comparative_linguistics}

Quantitative comparative linguistics is a subfield of quantitative linguistics which applies [quantitative analysis](wikipedia://en/389564) to [comparative linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_linguistics). It makes use of [lexicostatistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexicostatistics) and [glottochronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glottochronology), and the borrowing of [phylogenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenetics) from biology.
