# Windmill graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Windmill graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **windmill graph** $\operatorname{Wd}(k,n)$ is an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) constructed for $k \ge 2$ and $n \ge 2$ by joining $n$ copies of the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_{k}$ at a shared [universal vertex](wikipedia://en/51352780). That is, it is a [1-clique-sum](wikipedia://en/17598460) of these complete graphs.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

It has $n(k - 1) + 1$ vertices and $nk(k - 1)/2$ edges, girth 3 (if $k > 2$), radius 1 and diameter 2. It has [vertex connectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) 1 because its central vertex is an articulation point; however, like the complete graphs from which it is formed, it is $(k - 1)$-edge-connected. It is [trivially perfect](wikipedia://en/18033364) and a [block graph](wikipedia://en/29106883).

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

By construction, the windmill graph $\operatorname{Wd}(3,n)$ is the [friendship graph](wikipedia://en/24363442) $F_{n}$, the windmill graph $\operatorname{Wd}(2,n)$ is the [star graph](wikipedia://en/19310129) $S_{n}$ and the windmill graph $\operatorname{Wd}(3,2)$ is the [butterfly graph](wikipedia://en/4372111).

:::

## Labeling and colouring {#Labeling_and_colouring}

The windmill graph has [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) $k$ and [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) $n(k - 1)$. Its [chromatic polynomial](wikipedia://en/1460126) can be deduced from the chromatic polynomial of the complete graph and is equal to

$$x\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(x-i)^n.$$

The windmill graph $\operatorname{Wd}(k,n)$ is proved not [graceful](wikipedia://en/8978968) if $k > 5$.[^o10] In 1979, Bermond has conjectured that $\operatorname{Wd}(4,n)$ is graceful for all $n \ge 4$.[^o11] Through an equivalence with perfect difference families, this has been proved for $n \le 1000$. [^o12] Bermond, [Kotzig](wikipedia://en/25648858), and Turgeon proved that $\operatorname{Wd}(k,n)$ is not graceful when $k = 4$ and $n = 2$ or $n = 3$, and when $k = 5$ and $n = 2$.[^o13] The windmill $\operatorname{Wd}(3,n)$ is graceful if and only if $n \equiv 0\pmod{4}$ or $n \equiv 1\pmod{4}$.[^o14]
