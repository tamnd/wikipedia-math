# Jan Willem Nienhuys

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jan Willem Nienhuys"}

**Jan Willem Nienhuys** (born 16 April 1942) is a Dutch mathematician, book translator and [skeptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_skepticism). He taught mathematics at the [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology). He is also a board member and secretary of [Stichting Skepsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stichting_Skepsis) and an editor of its magazine *[Skepter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skepter)*.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Nienhuys studied mathematics in the Netherlands, and in 1966/67 at [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University) in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans), where he met his future wife. On 14 September 1970, he earned his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in mathematics at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University) under guidance of his promotor [Hans Freudenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Freudenthal).[^o5] His dissertation was published the same year in the journal *[Indagationes Mathematicae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indagationes_Mathematicae) (Proceedings)*.[^o6] He then taught mathematics for two years at the [National Taiwan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Taiwan_University) in [Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei), [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan). Since 1973, Nienhuys taught mathematics at the [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology). He also assisted several writers in completing their books, and translated books to [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language).

Nienhuys married and had two sons with Cheng Shan-Hwei. Born in [Sichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan) during the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War), she was raised in Taiwan where she also studied mathematics, finishing her studies in the United States. She went on to teach informatics at the [Erasmus University Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_University_Rotterdam) and become the director of the Chinese School Eindhoven. The couple published an article together in 1979, *Onwaar versus onzinnig* in the Dutch journal *Euclides*, and co-authored a book about China, *China: Geschiedenis, Cultuur, Wetenschap, Kunst En Politiek* (2007).[^o7]

## Skepticism {#Skepticism}

Nienhuys is a prolific skeptic. Amongst other things he has written several articles on [pseudoscience](wikipedia://en/23047), mainly about [quackery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quackery) such as [homeopathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeopathy) and the [anti-vaccination movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccine_controversies). Since the late 1980s he has served as a board member, and since 2003 secretary of, [Stichting Skepsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stichting_Skepsis). Nienhuys also writes articles as an editorial staff member of the magazine *[Skepter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skepter)*. From July 2008 until 6 November 2010, he was editor-in-chief of the [Vereniging tegen de Kwakzalverij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vereniging_tegen_de_Kwakzalverij) website.[^o9]

In 1983, Nienhuys criticised the confluence model of [Robert B. Zajonc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Zajonc) and Gregory B. Markus. This mathematical model would serve as proof that a connection existed between the order of birth and intelligence to the advantage of the firstborn, as Lillian Belmont and Francis A. Marolla concluded in 1973 from a registration of the [Dutch armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_forces_of_the_Netherlands). This record consisted of the data of nearly 400,000 19-year-old men born in the period 1944–1947, originally collected to investigate the effects of the [Hunger Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_famine_of_1944) (1944–1945) on mental and physical development.[^o10][^o11][^o12] According to Nienhuys, Zajonc and Markus's model contained errors in the logic, calculations and methodology used.[^o13]

Since 2010, Nienhuys has been a fellow of the [Committee for Skeptical Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_Skeptical_Inquiry).[^o14] He often lectures at [skeptical conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_skeptical_conferences) such as Skepsis congresses and [SKEPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SKEPP) conventions.[^o15][^o16] On 4 October 2014, Nienhuys received the Gebroeders Bruinsma Erepenning, an award of the Vereniging tegen de Kwakzalverij.[^o17] The society praised Nienhuys for his "enormous engagement, an amazing ability to quickly master certain issues, an enormous productivity and an unparalleled accuracy."[^o18]
