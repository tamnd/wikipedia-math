# Prism graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prism graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **prism graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) that has one of the [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) as its skeleton.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The individual graphs may be named after the associated solid:

- [Triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584) graph – 6 vertices, 9 edges
- [Cubical graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubical_graph) – 8 vertices, 12 edges
- [Pentagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859659) graph – 10 vertices, 15 edges
- [Hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859668) graph – 12 vertices, 18 edges
- [Heptagonal prism](wikipedia://en/24278888) graph – 14 vertices, 21 edges
- [Octagonal prism](wikipedia://en/3026569) graph – 16 vertices, 24 edges
- ...

Although geometrically the [star polygons](wikipedia://en/313303) also form the faces of a different sequence of (self-intersecting and non-convex) prismatic polyhedra, the graphs of these star prisms are isomorphic to the prism graphs, and do not form a separate sequence of graphs.

## Construction {#Construction}

Prism graphs are examples of [generalized Petersen graphs](wikipedia://en/23716451), with parameters GP(*n*,1). They may also be constructed as the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/2856852) of a [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) with a single edge.[^o7]

As with many vertex-transitive graphs, the prism graphs may also be constructed as [Cayley graphs](wikipedia://en/358277). The order-*n* [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) is the group of symmetries of a regular *n*-gon in the plane; it acts on the *n*-gon by rotations and reflections. It can be generated by two elements, a rotation by an angle of 2$\pi$/*n* and a single reflection, and its Cayley graph with this generating set is the prism graph. Abstractly, the group has the [presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) $\langle r,f\mid r^n, f^2, (rf)^2\rangle$ (where *r* is a rotation and *f* is a reflection or flip) and the Cayley graph has *r* and *f* (or *r*, *r*<sup>−1</sup>, and *f*) as its generators.

The *n*-gonal prism graphs with odd values of *n* may be constructed as [circulant graphs](wikipedia://en/3498787) $C_{2n}^{2,n}$. However, this construction does not work for even values of *n*.[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The graph of an *n*-gonal prism has 2*n* vertices and 3*n* edges. They are [regular](wikipedia://en/85821), [cubic graphs](wikipedia://en/980508). Since the prism has symmetries taking each vertex to each other vertex, the prism graphs are [vertex-transitive graphs](wikipedia://en/581175). As [polyhedral graphs](wikipedia://en/24656270), they are also [3-vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314). Every prism graph has a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle).[^o10] even sided prism graphs are [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431).

Among all [biconnected](wikipedia://en/4989397) [cubic graphs](wikipedia://en/980508), the prism graphs have within a constant factor of the largest possible number of [1-factorizations](wikipedia://en/3298854). A 1-factorization is a partition of the edge set of the graph into three perfect matchings, or equivalently an [edge coloring](wikipedia://en/690647) of the graph with three colors. Every biconnected *n*-vertex cubic graph has *O*(2<sup>*n*/2</sup>) 1-factorizations, and the prism graphs have *Ω*(2<sup>*n*/2</sup>) 1-factorizations.[^o11]

The number of [spanning trees](wikipedia://en/455770) of an *n*-gonal prism graph is given by the formula[^o12]

$$\frac{n}{2}\bigl( (2+\sqrt{3})^n+(2-\sqrt{3})^n-2)\bigr.$$

For *n* = 3, 4, 5, ... these numbers are

75, 384, 1805, 8100, 35287, 150528, ... .

The *n*-gonal prism graphs for even values of *n* are [partial cubes](wikipedia://en/30697444). They form one of the few known infinite families of [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) partial cubes, and (except for four sporadic examples) the only vertex-transitive cubic partial cubes.[^o13]

The pentagonal prism is one of the [forbidden minors](wikipedia://en/13547826) for the graphs of [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) three.[^o14] The triangular prism and cube graph have treewidth exactly three, but all larger prism graphs have treewidth four.

:::

## Related graphs {#Related_graphs}

Other infinite sequences of polyhedral graph formed in a similar way from polyhedra with regular-polygon bases include the [antiprism graphs](wikipedia://en/45012210) (graphs of [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851)) and [wheel graphs](wikipedia://en/1655876) (graphs of [pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045)). Other vertex-transitive polyhedral graphs include the [Archimedean graphs](wikipedia://en/44969442).

If the two cycles of a prism graph are broken by the removal of a single edge in the same position in both cycles, the result is a [ladder graph](wikipedia://en/19657952). If these two removed edges are replaced by two crossed edges, the result is a non-planar graph called a [Möbius ladder](wikipedia://en/7355278).[^o16]

A **crossed prism graph** is similar but pairs up lateral crossed edges, alternating forward and backwards, for even-sided prisms. The set are also [regular](wikipedia://en/85821), vertex transitive [cubic graphs](wikipedia://en/980508), and [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431) (also called bicubic graphs). A 4-crossed prism graph is the same as the [cubical graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubical_graph) with 8 vertices, 12 edges. A 6-crossed prism graph is also the [Franklin graph](wikipedia://en/23717056) with 12 vertices, 18 edges. In *An Atlas of Graphs* the first few are listed in the set of *Connected cubic transitive graphs* indexed as Ct5, Ct12, Ct19, Ct29, Ct42, Ct54, and Ct74 for 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, and 16 sides respectively.[^o18]
