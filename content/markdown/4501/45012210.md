# Antiprism graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Antiprism graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **antiprism graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) that has one of the [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851) as its skeleton. An $n$-sided antiprism has $2n$ vertices and $4n$ edges. They are [regular](wikipedia://en/85821), [polyhedral](wikipedia://en/24656270) (and therefore by necessity also [3-vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph), [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581175), and [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314)), and also [Hamiltonian graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph).[^o2]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

The first graph in the sequence, the [tetrahedral graph](wikipedia://en/30606#Orthogonal_projections_of_the_regular_tetrahedron), has 4 verticies and 6 edges. The second, the [octahedral graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedral_graph), has 6 vertices and 12 edges. Later graphs in the sequence are named after the type of antiprism they correspond to:

- [Square antiprismatic](wikipedia://en/2860045) graph – 8 vertices, 16 edges
- [Pentagonal antiprismatic](wikipedia://en/2860091) graph – 10 vertices, 20 edges
- [Hexagonal antiprismatic](wikipedia://en/2860125) graph – 12 vertices, 24 edges
- [Heptagonal antiprismatic](wikipedia://en/8670186) graph – 14 vertices, 28 edges
- [Octagonal antiprismatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octagonal_antiprism) graph – 16 vertices, 32 edges
- ...

## Related graphs {#Related_graphs}

An antiprism graph is a special case of a [circulant graph](wikipedia://en/3498787), Ci<sub>2*n*</sub>(2,1).

Other infinite sequences of polyhedral graph formed in a similar way from polyhedra with regular-polygon bases include the [prism graphs](wikipedia://en/45012199) (graphs of [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007)) and [wheel graphs](wikipedia://en/1655876) (graphs of [pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045)). Other vertex-transitive polyhedral graphs include the [Archimedean graphs](wikipedia://en/44969442).
