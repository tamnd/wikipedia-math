# Markov chain approximation method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Markov chain approximation method"}

In [numerical methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_methods) for [stochastic differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_differential_equations), the **Markov chain approximation method (MCAM)** belongs to the several numerical (schemes) approaches used in [stochastic control theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_control_theory). Regrettably the simple adaptation of the deterministic schemes for matching up to stochastic models such as the Runge–Kutta method does not work at all.

:::

It is a powerful and widely usable set of ideas, due to the current infancy of stochastic control it might be even said 'insights.' for numerical and other approximations problems in [stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_processes).[^o2][^o3] They represent counterparts from deterministic control theory such as [optimal control theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_control_theory).[^o4]

The basic idea of the MCAM is to approximate the original [controlled process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_process) by a chosen [controlled markov process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_markov_process) on a [finite state space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_state_space). In case of need, one must as well approximate the [cost function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_function_%28control_theory%29) for one that matches up the [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) chosen to approximate the original stochastic process.
