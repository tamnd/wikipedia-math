# David Broomhead

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Broomhead"}

**David S. Broomhead** (13 November 1950 – 24 July 2014) was a British mathematician specialising in [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) and was professor of applied mathematics at the [School of Mathematics, University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Manchester).[^o11][^o12]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Broomhead was born on 13 November 1950 in Leeds. He attended [Aireborough Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aireborough_Grammar_School) and, after spending a year teaching in [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda), Broomhead moved to [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford), where he read [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) for his first degree. He remained in Oxford for his [D.Phil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.Phil.), researching [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) under the supervision of [Peter Atkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Atkins). He completed his thesis *Molecules in Electromagnetic Fields* in 1976.[^o11][^o13]

## Career {#Career}

After a year as a postdoc at the [Atomic Energy Research Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_Energy_Research_Establishment), Broomhead moved to Japan. He held at two-year NATO Postdoctoral Fellowship in the Department of Physics at the [University of Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kyoto), in K. Tomita's group. On returning to the U.K., he worked as a postdoc with George Rowlands at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick), again in the Physics Department. In 1983, Broomhead began working in the Signal Processing group at the [Royal Signals and Radar Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Signals_and_Radar_Establishment) (RSRE, now QinetiQ) in Malvern, becoming Senior Principal Scientific Officer. In 1995 he moved to Manchester, taking a Chair in Applied Mathematics, initially at [UMIST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester_Institute_of_Science_and_Technology) and then after 2004, at the [School of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Manchester) at the new University of Manchester.[^o11][^o13][^o14]

From 1989 to 1992 he was Coordinator of the [EPSRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPSRC) Nonlinear Mathematics Initiative. He held visiting positions at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) and [Hiroshima University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima_University). Broomhead was a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) (IMA), a member of the IMA Council from 1998 and was Chair of the Editorial Board of Mathematics Today from 2002.[^o13][^o5] In 2013 he was made an Honorary Fellow of the IMA.[^o11]

## Research {#Research}

*Image: David Broomhead 2010.jpg.* Broomhead in 2010

Broomhead's main interest was the development of methods for [time series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series_analysis) and nonlinear [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) using techniques from the theory of nonlinear [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087).[^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18] He also championed applying these ideas in interdisciplinary research.[^o19][^o7][^o8]

In Japan, Broomhead began to work seriously on applied nonlinear dynamics and [chaos](wikipedia://en/6295). With Greg King he developed techniques to determine whether an experimental time series had been generated by a deterministic chaotic system by combining the pure mathematical results on topological embedding due to Takens with the engineering method of singular value decompositions.[^o11][^o13]

While in Malvern, Broomhead wrote his influential papers on delay embedding and on [neural networks](wikipedia://en/21523). In 1989 he was awarded the John Benjamin Memorial Prize for work with David Lowe and Andrew Webb that exploited an analogy between neural networks and interpolation using the newly developed [radial basis functions](wikipedia://en/2310753) from [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).[^o11][^o13]

At Manchester he became increasingly interested in applications to [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology). He worked initially on eye movement control with Richard Abadi. Later, as a member of the [Manchester Centre for Integrative Systems Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Centre_for_Integrative_Systems_Biology), he worked with [Douglas Kell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Kell) on large-scale models of [metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism), and with Mike White on the dynamics of intracellular signalling cascades. He also developed a deep interest in hybrid systems and asynchronous processes, founding the Centre for Interdisciplinary Computational and Dynamical Analysis (CICADA).[^o20][^o21][^o22][^o23]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Broomhead met his wife Eleanor at Merton College, and they had one son, Nathan.[^o11]

He died suddenly on 24 July 2014 at [the National Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hospital_for_Neurology_and_Neurosurgery) in London.[^o24]
