# Vincent average

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vincent average"}

In [applied statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_statistics), **Vincentization**[^o2] was described by Ratcliff (1979),[^o3] and is named after biologist S. B. Vincent (1912),[^o4] who used something very similar to it for constructing [learning curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_curve) at the beginning of the 1900s. It basically consists of averaging $n\geq 2$ subjects' estimated or elicited [quantile functions](wikipedia://en/10167616) in order to define group quantiles from which $F$ can be constructed.

:::

To cast it in its greatest generality, let $F_1,\dots, F_n$ represent arbitrary (empirical or theoretical) [distribution functions](wikipedia://en/5793) and define their corresponding quantile functions by

$$F_i^{-1}(\alpha) = \inf\{t\in \mathbb{R} : F_i(t)\ge\alpha) \},\quad 0<\alpha\leq 1.$$

The Vincent average of the $F_i$'s is then computed as

$$F^{-1}(\alpha) = \sum w_i F_i^{-1}(\alpha),\quad 0<\alpha\leq 1,\quad i = 1,\ldots,n$$

where the non-negative numbers $w_1,\dots,w_n$ have a sum of $1$.
