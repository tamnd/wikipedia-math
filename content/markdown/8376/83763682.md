# Levent Alpöge

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Levent Alpöge"}

**Levent Hasan Ali Alpöge** (born April 1, 1992) is an American and Turkish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is known for his work in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [arithmetic statistics](wikipedia://en/26685). He was a Junior Fellow in the [Harvard Society of Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Society_of_Fellows) and later joined the artificial-intelligence company [Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic) as a member of the technical staff.[^o3][^o29] In 2025, he and collaborators gave a second proof that [Hilbert's tenth problem](wikipedia://en/101851) has a negative answer over the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of every [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822), a result originally established by Koymans and Pagano in 2024.[^o23][^o4]

:::

On July 19, 2026, Alpöge presented an explicit [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) to the [Jacobian conjecture](wikipedia://en/1193823) in three-dimensional space, stating that it was discovered using the [Claude Fable 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Mythos) AI model.[^o5][^o6] He announced a counterexample to the [Carathéodory conjecture](wikipedia://en/17228280) on August 19, 2026 using the model. Four days later, he announced a positive answer to [Hopf's problem](wikipedia://en/514258) concerning the existence of a complex structure on a 6-dimensional sphere using AI tools.[^o7]

His work on [Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness](wikipedia://en/6013654) with Tristan Buckmaster[^o8] was cited by [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI) in their solution to the [Millennium Prize Problem](wikipedia://en/27599234) on September 8, 2026.[^o9][^o10]

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

*Image: Levent Alpöge 2018 MFO.jpg.* Levent Alpöge in 2018

Alpöge was born on April 1, 1992, and grew up on [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island), New York, where he attended [Half Hollow Hills High School West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Hollow_Hills_Central_School_District) in [Dix Hills, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dix_Hills,_New_York).[^o3][^o12] He holds dual United States and Turkish citizenship.[^o3]

In 2010, he was a finalist in the [Intel Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regeneron_Science_Talent_Search) for a computer-science project on the algorithmic analysis of [MRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging) scans of blood vessels. His earliest publication, on a [CUDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUDA)-optimized vessel-detection method, dates from the same period.[^o12][^o3] He earned a [Barry Goldwater Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Goldwater_Scholarship_and_Excellence_in_Education_Foundation).[^o3][^o14] The [main-belt asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid_belt) 25898 Alpöge is named after him.[^o13]

Alpöge studied at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he received a Bachelor of Arts in mathematics and a Master of Arts in physics in 2014, graduating [summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude).[^o3] His undergraduate thesis, *The average elliptic curve has few integral points*, was supervised by [Jacob Tsimerman](wikipedia://en/48497888).[^o3][^o14][^o15]

After earning a graduate fellowship from the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation), Alpöge studied Part III of the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) as a [Churchill Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Scholarship),[^o16] earning a [Master of Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Advanced_Studies) degree in 2015.[^o14][^o50] He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 2020 with a dissertation titled *Points on Curves*, supervised by [Manjul Bhargava](wikipedia://en/5102088).[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

After completing his doctorate, Alpöge held an [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) Postdoctoral Fellowship affiliated with [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and Harvard from 2020, and was a Junior Fellow in the [Harvard Society of Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Society_of_Fellows) from 2021 to 2025.[^o3][^o20] He subsequently joined the artificial-intelligence company [Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic) as a member of the technical staff.[^o29]

## Research {#Research}

Alpöge works in number theory and arithmetic statistics, including the study of [rational](wikipedia://en/2960200) and integral points on [elliptic](wikipedia://en/10225) and higher-genus curves, [Selmer groups](wikipedia://en/1970862), and the distribution of arithmetic invariants of number fields.[^o3]

### Sums of two rational cubes {#Sums_of_two_rational_cubes}

With [Manjul Bhargava](wikipedia://en/5102088) and Ari Shnidman, Alpöge established the first proven bounds on the proportion of integers that can be written as a sum of two [cubes](wikipedia://en/451445) of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024). Their 2022 work showed that at most about five-sixths of integers are so expressible, combining [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153) techniques with the [circle method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Littlewood_circle_method).[^o20] The upper bound for the density of integers that are so expressible has been subsequently improved to 68.05% by Peter Koymans and Alexander Smith. [^o21]

### Hilbert's tenth problem over number fields {#Hilbert's_tenth_problem_over_number_fields}

In a paper published in *[Inventiones Mathematicae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventiones_Mathematicae)* in 2025, Alpöge, Bhargava, [Wei Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei_Ho_%28mathematician%29), and Ari Shnidman proved that for any [quadratic extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_extension) of number fields $K/F$ there exists an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) over $F$ of positive [rank](wikipedia://en/244541) whose rank does not grow after [base change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_change_map) to $K$. This implies that [Hilbert's tenth problem](wikipedia://en/101851) has a negative solution over the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of every algebraic number field—that is, there is no [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) to decide whether a polynomial equation has solutions in that ring.[^o23] The result gave an alternative completion of a program relating Hilbert's tenth problem to the rank stability of elliptic curves and abelian varieties initiated by [Bjorn Poonen](wikipedia://en/16283918), [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679), [Karl Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Rubin), and Alexandra Shlapentokh; the Mazur--Rubin-Poonen-Shlapentokh program had been completed in 2024 by Peter Koymans and Carlo Pagano, using instead elliptic curves. [^o4][^o24]

### Jacobian conjecture counterexample {#Jacobian_conjecture_counterexample}

In July 2026, Alpöge announced on the social-media platform [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_%28social_network%29) that, using Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 [large language model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model), he had found a counterexample to the [Jacobian conjecture](wikipedia://en/1193823), a problem in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) begun by [Ludwig Kraus](wikipedia://en/83766264) in 1884 and formally posed by [Ott-Heinrich Keller](wikipedia://en/1193984) in 1939.[^o27][^o28] The example is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) map from three-dimensional [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) space to itself whose [Jacobian determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_determinant) is the nonzero constant −2 but which is not injective, disproving the conjecture in dimensions three and above. The two-variable case of the conjecture remains open. The announcement was made outside of formal [peer review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review), and the arithmetic of the example was independently checked by other mathematicians in the days that followed.[^o29]

:::

### Hadamard matrices {#Hadamard_matrices}

On August 12, 2026, Alpöge announced on X that, together with Philippe Voinov and Saul Reynolds-Haertle and using Anthropic's Claude, he had constructed [Hadamard matrices](wikipedia://en/507553) for all twelve orders below 2000 for which no such matrix had previously been known: 668, 716, 892, 1132, 1244, 1388, 1436, 1676, 1772, 1916, 1948 and 1964.[^o31][^o32] Order 668 had been the smallest open case of the [Hadamard conjecture](wikipedia://en/507553#Hadamard_conjecture) since 2005.[^o31]

### Carathéodory conjecture counterexample {#Carathéodory_conjecture_counterexample}

On August 19, 2026, he announced an explicit $C^\infty$ counterexample for the [Carathéodory conjecture](wikipedia://en/17228280) checked with John-Paul Smith and Anthropic's artificial-intelligence system Claude.[^o35] Once again the result was announced in a tweet[^o36], but on this occasion Alpoge offered a more comprehensive backstory, asserting that "i literally wanted to just read about it but i did see the complicated literature situation and so in hopes i could help asked him if it was ok to look at with a model" before confirming that "i did wanna have it recorded somewhere public though" and then giving the example.

:::

### Hopf's problem {#Hopf's_problem}

Alpöge announced an answer to [Hopf's problem](wikipedia://en/514258) on X on August 23, 2026, stating that he constructed a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) structure on a [6-dimensional sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) using one of Anthropic's models.[^o38][^o39] The same day Alpöge published a 100-page document containing a proof of the positive solution on his personal website.[^o40].

Proposed formalizations by Dean Cureton[^o41] and by Boris Alexeev[^o42] were subsequently posted on [GitHub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GitHub), and Philip Engel published some notes about the proof.[^o43] Manon Bischoff reported on the finding in *Spektrum der Wissenschaft*, the German language edition of *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)*.[^o44]

### Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness {#Navier–Stokes_existence_and_smoothness}

Alpöge worked with [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) mathematician Tristan Buckmaster on the [Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness](wikipedia://en/6013654) problem, one of the seven [Millennium Prize Problems](wikipedia://en/27599234). On August 15, 2026, the pair proved that the [Euler equations](wikipedia://en/396022), the frictionless counterpart of Navier–Stokes, can "blow up" in finite time, extending a forcing technique developed by Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa.[^o8] After verifying the proof in [Lean](wikipedia://en/62889984), the pair published a preprint establishing finite-time blow-up with smooth forcing for the incompressible porous medium equation, the 2D Boussinesq system, and the 3D incompressible Euler equations, using Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's [Codex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI_Codex_%28AI_agent%29) in the work.[^o8]

After word of the pair's progress reached OpenAI, a team led by OpenAI scientist [Sébastien Bubeck](wikipedia://en/72294530) used an internal model to find a solution for Navier–Stokes equations.[^o46] On September 8, 2026, OpenAI announced a full solution to the problem that resembled the approach used by the pair.[^o9] Buckmaster suggested OpenAI researchers may have accessed private work he had stored in a Codex session before publication.[^o8][^o47][^o9][^o48]
