# Conformal dimension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conformal dimension"}

In mathematics, the **conformal dimension** of a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) *X* is the infimum of the [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) over the [conformal gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_gauge) of *X*, that is, the class of all metric spaces [quasisymmetric](wikipedia://en/30220726) to *X*.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let *X* be a metric space and $\mathcal{G}$ be the collection of all metric spaces that are quasisymmetric to *X*. The conformal dimension of *X* is defined as such

$$\mathrm{Cdim} X = \inf_{Y \in \mathcal{G}} \dim_H Y$$

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

We have the following [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489), for a metric space *X*:

$$\dim_T X \leq \mathrm{Cdim} X \leq \dim_H X$$

The second inequality is true by definition. The first one is deduced from the fact that the [topological dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_dimension) is invariant by [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) and it is always smaller than or equal to the [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294).

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The conformal dimension of $\mathbf{R}^N$ is *N*, since the topological and Hausdorff dimensions of [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697) agree.
- The [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172) *K* is of null conformal dimension. However, there is no metric space quasisymmetric to *K* with a 0 Hausdorff dimension.

:::
