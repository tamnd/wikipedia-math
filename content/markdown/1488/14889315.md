# Weyl connection

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weyl connection"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **Weyl connection** (also called a **Weyl structure**) is a generalization of the [Levi-Civita connection](wikipedia://en/249176) that makes sense on a [conformal manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_manifold). They were introduced by [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) (Weyl 1918) in an attempt to unify general relativity and electromagnetism. His approach, although it did not lead to a successful theory,[^o3] lead to further developments of the theory in [conformal geometry](wikipedia://en/295907), including a detailed study by [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) (Cartan 1943). They were also discussed in (Eisenhart 1927).

:::

Specifically, let $M$ be a smooth manifold, and $[g]$ a conformal class of (non-degenerate) [metric tensors](wikipedia://en/195795) on $M$, where $h,g\in[g]$ iff $h=e^{2\gamma}g$ for some smooth function $\gamma$ (see [Weyl transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_transformation)). A Weyl connection is a torsion free [affine connection](wikipedia://en/607690) on $M$ such that, for any $g\in [g]$,

$$\nabla g = \alpha_g \otimes g$$

where $\alpha_g$ is a one-form depending on $g$.

If $\nabla$ is a Weyl connection and $h=e^{2\gamma}g$, then $\nabla h = (2\,d\gamma+\alpha_g)\otimes h$ so the one-form transforms by $\alpha_{e^{2\gamma}g} = 2\,d\gamma+\alpha_g.$ Thus the notion of a Weyl connection is conformally invariant, and the change in one-form is mediated by a [de Rham cocycle](wikipedia://en/221530).

An example of a Weyl connection is the Levi-Civita connection for any metric in the conformal class $[g]$, with $\alpha_g=0$. This is not the most general case, however, as any such Weyl connection has the property that the one-form $\alpha_h$ is closed for all $h$ belonging to the conformal class. In general, the [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582) of a Weyl connection is not symmetric. Its skew part is the dimension times the two-form $d\alpha_g$, which is independent of $g$ in the conformal class, because the difference between two $\alpha_g$ is a de Rham cocycle. Thus, by the [Poincaré lemma](wikipedia://en/600030), the Ricci curvature is symmetric if and only if the Weyl connection is locally the Levi-Civita connection of some element of the conformal class.[^o6]

Weyl's original hope was that the form $\alpha_g$ could represent the [vector potential](wikipedia://en/422936) of electromagnetism (a gauge dependent quantity), and $d\alpha_g$ the field strength (a gauge invariant quantity). This synthesis is unsuccessful in part because the [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group) is wrong: electromagnetism is associated with a $U(1)$ gauge field, not an $\mathbb R$ gauge field.

(Hall 1992) showed that an affine connection is a Weyl connection if and only if its [holonomy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holonomy_group) is a subgroup of the [conformal group](wikipedia://en/364775). The possible holonomy algebras in Lorentzian signature were analyzed in (Dikarev 2021).

A **Weyl manifold** is a manifold admitting a global Weyl connection. The global analysis of Weyl manifolds is actively being studied. For example, (LeBrun Mason 2009) considered complete Weyl manifolds such that the [Einstein vacuum equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_equations) hold, an [Einstein–Weyl geometry](wikipedia://en/75072610), obtaining a complete characterization in three dimensions.

Weyl connections also have current applications in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) and [holography](wikipedia://en/14286).[^o10][^o11]

Weyl connections have been generalized to the setting of [parabolic geometries](wikipedia://en/30556018), of which conformal geometry is a special case, in (Čap Slovák 2003).
