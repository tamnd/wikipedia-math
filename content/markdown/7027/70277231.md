# Mixed Poisson distribution

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mixed Poisson distribution"}

A **mixed Poisson distribution** is a [univariate](wikipedia://en/9253219) discrete [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) in stochastics. It results from assuming that the conditional distribution of a random variable, given the value of the rate parameter, is a [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144), and that the [rate parameter](wikipedia://en/769503#Rate_parameter) itself is considered as a random variable. Hence it is a special case of a [compound probability distribution](wikipedia://en/28897820). Mixed Poisson distributions can be found in [actuarial mathematics](wikipedia://en/189373) as a general approach for the distribution of the number of claims and is also examined as an [epidemiological model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_modelling_of_infectious_disease).[^o3] It should not be confused with [compound Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/949562) or [compound Poisson process](wikipedia://en/1082294).[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) *X* satisfies the mixed Poisson distribution with density $\pi$(*λ*) if it has the probability distribution[^o7]

$$\operatorname{P}(X=k) = \int_0^\infty \frac{\lambda^k}{k!}e^{-\lambda} \,\,\pi(\lambda)\, d\lambda.$$

If we denote the probabilities of the Poisson distribution by $q_{\lambda}(k)$, then

$$\operatorname{P}(X=k) = \int_0^\infty q_\lambda(k) \,\,\pi(\lambda)\, d\lambda.$$

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) is always bigger than the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653). This property is called [overdispersion](wikipedia://en/4190350). This is in contrast to the Poisson distribution where mean and variance are the same.
- In practice, almost only densities of [gamma distributions](wikipedia://en/207079), [logarithmic normal distributions](wikipedia://en/102476) and [inverse Gaussian distributions](wikipedia://en/5246161) are used as densities $\pi$(*λ*). If we choose the density of the [gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079), we get the [negative binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/45177), which explains why this is also called the Poisson gamma distribution.

In the following let $\mu_\pi=\int_0^\infty \lambda \,\,\pi(\lambda) \, d\lambda\,$ be the expected value of the density $\pi(\lambda)\,$ and $\sigma_\pi^2 = \int_0^\infty (\lambda-\mu_\pi)^2 \,\,\pi(\lambda) \, d\lambda\,$ be the variance of the density.

### Expected value {#Expected_value}

The [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of the mixed Poisson distribution is

$$\operatorname{E}(X) = \mu_\pi.$$

### Variance {#Variance}

For the [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) one gets[^o7]

$$\operatorname{Var}(X) = \mu_\pi+\sigma_\pi^2.$$

### Skewness {#Skewness}

The [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) can be represented as

$$\operatorname{v}(X) = \Bigl(\mu_\pi+\sigma_\pi^2\Bigr)^{-3/2} \,\Biggl[\int_0^\infty(\lambda-\mu_\pi)^3\,\pi(\lambda)\,d{\lambda}+\mu_\pi\Biggr].$$

### Characteristic function {#Characteristic_function}

The [characteristic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_function) has the form

$$\varphi_X(s) = M_\pi(e^{is}-1).\,$$

Where $M_\pi$ is the [moment generating function](wikipedia://en/194634) of the density.

### Probability generating function {#Probability_generating_function}

For the [probability generating function](wikipedia://en/236841), one obtains[^o7]

$$m_X(s) = M_\pi(s-1).\,$$

### Moment-generating function {#Moment-generating_function}

The [moment-generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment-generating_function) of the mixed Poisson distribution is

$$M_X(s) = M_\pi(e^s-1).\,$$

## Examples {#Examples}

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

Compounding a [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) with rate parameter distributed according to a [gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079) yields a [negative binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/45177).[^o7]

:::

::: {#o19 .proof cue=math-proof}

Let $\pi(\lambda)=\frac{(\frac{p}{1-p})^r}{\Gamma(r)} \lambda^{r-1} e^{-\frac{p}{1-p}\lambda}$ be a density of a $\operatorname{\Gamma}\left(r,\frac{p}{1-p}\right)$ distributed random variable.

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{P}(X=k)&= \frac{1}{k!} \int_0^\infty \lambda^k e^{-\lambda} \frac{(\frac{p}{1-p})^r}{\Gamma(r)} \lambda^{r-1} e^{-\frac{p}{1-p}\lambda} \, d \lambda \\ & = \frac{p^r(1-p)^{-r}}{\Gamma(r) k!} \int_0^\infty \lambda^{k+r-1} e^{-\lambda \frac{1}{1-p}} \, d \lambda \\ & = \frac{p^r(1-p)^{-r}}{\Gamma(r) k!} (1-p)^{k+r} \underbrace{\int_0^\infty \lambda^{k+r-1} e^{-\lambda} \, d \lambda}_{= \Gamma(r+k)} \\ & = \frac{\Gamma(r+k)}{\Gamma(r) k!} (1-p)^k p^r \end{aligned}$$

Therefore we get $X\sim\operatorname{NegB}(r,p).$

:::

::: {#o20 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

Compounding a [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) with rate parameter distributed according to an [exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906) yields a [geometric distribution](wikipedia://en/45922).

:::

::: {#o21 .proof cue=math-proof}

Let $\pi(\lambda)=\frac1\beta e^{-\frac \lambda\beta}$ be a density of a $\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac1\beta\right)$ distributed random variable. Using [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252) $k$ times yields:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{P}(X=k)&=\frac{1}{k!}\int_0^\infty \lambda^k e^{-\lambda} \frac1\beta e^{-\frac \lambda\beta} \, d\lambda\\ &=\frac{1}{k!\beta}\int_0^\infty \lambda^k e^{-\lambda\left(\frac{1+\beta}{\beta}\right)}\, d \lambda\\ &=\frac{1}{k!\beta}\cdot k!\left(\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\right)^k\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda\left(\frac{1+\beta}{\beta}\right)}\, d \lambda\\ &=\left(\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\right)^k\left(\frac{1}{1+\beta}\right) \end{aligned}$$

Therefore we get $X\sim\operatorname{Geo\left(\frac{1}{1+\beta}\right)}.$

:::
