# François Cosserat

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="François Cosserat"}

**François Cosserat** (26 October 1852 – 22 March 1914) was a French [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known by his theories about deformable bodies written with his brother [Eugène](wikipedia://en/24064567).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

François Cosserat was the eldest of the three sons of François-Constant Cosserat, a textile manufacturer in [Amiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiens). The three sons achieved to study in one of the *grandes écoles* of [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989). François studied at the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) from 1870[^n1] to 1872 and then in the [École Nationale des Ponts et Chaussées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_des_ponts_ParisTech) until 1875. François Cosserat followed a typical career as civil engineer in the French East Railroad Company constructing and designing bridges, tunnels, etc. In 1895 he became chief engineer.[^n2] Besides his engineer career he worked with his junior brother, [Eugène](wikipedia://en/24064567), in [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics) and [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29), publishing papers and a book about it.

In 1913 he was elected president of the [Société mathématique de France](wikipedia://en/1691205).

His most important work is his book, a joint work with his brother, *[Théorie des corps déformables (Theory of deformable bodies)](https://books.google.com/books?id=U3e4AAAAIAAJ)* (1909) in which laid the foundations of the mechanics of the generalized continua.[^n3] His approach can be particularly useful in modeling nonlinear elastic inhomogeneous deformations and inelastic deformations.[^n4] Other remarkable joint works are: *Théorie de l'élasticité* (1896), *Note sur la cinématique d'un milieu continu* (1897), *Note sur la dynamique du point et du corps invariable* (1906) and *Note sur la théorie de l'action euclidienne* (1909).
