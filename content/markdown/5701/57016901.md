# Victor Lomonosov

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Victor Lomonosov"}

**Victor Lomonosov** (7 February 1946 – 29 March 2018) was a Russian-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his work in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). In [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), he is best known for his work in 1973 on the [invariant subspace problem](wikipedia://en/585826), which was described by [Walter Rudin](wikipedia://en/5491766) in his classical book on Functional Analysis as "[Lomonosov's spectacular invariant subspace theorem](wikipedia://en/72876243)".[^o2] Lomonosov gives a very short proof, using the [Schauder fixed point theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schauder_fixed_point_theorem), that if a bounded linear operator *T* on a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) commutes with a non-zero [compact operator](wikipedia://en/577441) then *T* has a non-trivial invariant subspace.[^o3] Lomonosov has also published on the [Bishop–Phelps theorem](wikipedia://en/40644951)[^o4] and Burnside's Theorem.[^o5]

:::

Lomonosov received his master's degree from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) in 1969 and his Ph.D. from the [National University of Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Kharkiv) in 1974 (adviser [Vladimir Matsaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Matsaev)). He was appointed as an Associate Professor at [Kent State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_State_University) in the fall of 1991 and was promoted to Professor at the same university in 1999.
