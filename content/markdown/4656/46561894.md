# Continuous or discrete variable

*Image: Continuous and discrete variables.png.* Variables can be divided into two main categories: [qualitative (categorical)](wikipedia://en/821165) and quantitative (numerical). Continuous and discrete variables are subcategories of quantitative variables. Note that this schematic is not exhaustive in terms of the types of variables.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Continuous or discrete variable"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a quantitative [variable](wikipedia://en/3728109) may be **continuous** or **discrete**. If it can take on two [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) values and all the values between them, the variable is *continuous* in that [interval](wikipedia://en/49172).[^o2] If it can take on a value such that there is a non-[infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) gap on each side of it containing no values that the variable can take on, then it is *discrete* around that value.[^o3] In some contexts, a variable can be discrete in some ranges of the [number line](wikipedia://en/287188) and continuous in others. In statistics, continuous and discrete variables are distinct [statistical data types](wikipedia://en/35269346) which are described with different [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543).

:::

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Continuous data"}

## Continuous variable {#Continuous_variable}

A **continuous variable** is a variable such that there are possible values between any two values.

For example, a variable over a non-empty range of the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) is continuous if it can take on any value in that range.[^o6]

Methods of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) are often used in problems in which the variables are continuous, for example in continuous [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization) problems.[^o7]

In [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/26685), the [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) of continuous variables can be expressed in terms of [probability density functions](wikipedia://en/43487).[^o8]

In [continuous-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_time) [dynamics](wikipedia://en/9087), the variable *time* is treated as continuous, and the equation describing the evolution of some variable over time is a [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309).[^o9] The [instantaneous rate of change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instantaneous_rate_of_change) is a well-defined concept that takes the ratio of the change in the dependent variable to the independent variable at a specific instant.

*Image: Continuous or discrete variables example.jpg.* This is an image of vials with different amounts of liquid. A continuous variable could be the volume of liquid in the vials. A discrete variable could be the number of vials.

:::

## Discrete variable {#Discrete_variable}

In contrast, a variable is a **discrete variable** if and only if there exists a one-to-one correspondence between this variable and a subset of $\mathbb{N}$, the set of [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers).[^o12] In other words, a discrete variable over a particular interval of real values is one for which, for any value in the range that the variable is permitted to take on, there is a positive minimum distance to the nearest other permissible value. The number of permitted values is either finite or [countably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite). Common examples are variables that must be [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), non-negative integers, positive integers, or only the integers 0 and 1.[^o13]

Methods of calculus do not readily lend themselves to problems involving discrete variables. Especially in multivariable calculus, many models rely on the assumption of continuity.[^o14] Examples of problems involving discrete variables include [integer programming](wikipedia://en/411215).

In statistics, the probability distributions of discrete variables can be expressed in terms of [probability mass functions](wikipedia://en/154725).[^o8]

In [discrete time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_time) dynamics, the variable *time* is treated as discrete, and the equation of evolution of some variable over time is called a [difference equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_equation).[^o15] For certain discrete-time dynamical systems, the system response can be modelled by solving the difference equation for an analytical solution.

In [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390) and more generally in [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997), sometimes some of the variables being [empirically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical) related to each other are 0-1 variables, being permitted to take on only those two values.[^o16] The purpose of the discrete values of 0 and 1 is to use the dummy variable as a ‘switch’ that can ‘turn on’ and ‘turn off’ by assigning the two values to different parameters in an equation. A variable of this type is called a [dummy variable](wikipedia://en/285156). If the [dependent variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_variable) is a dummy variable, then [logistic regression](wikipedia://en/226631) or [probit regression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probit_regression) is commonly employed. In the case of regression analysis, a dummy variable can be used to represent subgroups of the sample in a study (e.g. the value 0 corresponding to a constituent of the control group).[^o17]

:::

## Mixture of continuous and discrete variables {#Mixture_of_continuous_and_discrete_variables}

A mixed multivariate model can contain both discrete and continuous variables. For instance, a simple mixed multivariate model could have a discrete variable $x$, which only takes on values 0 or 1, and a continuous variable $y$.[^o19] An example of a mixed model could be a research study on the risk of psychological disorders based on one binary measure of psychiatric symptoms and one continuous measure of cognitive performance.[^o20] Mixed models may also involve a single variable that is discrete over some range of the number line and continuous at another range.

In probability theory and statistics, the probability distribution of a mixed random variable consists of both discrete and continuous components. A mixed random variable does not have a [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) that is discrete or everywhere-continuous. An example of a mixed type random variable is the probability of wait time in a queue. The likelihood of a customer experiencing a zero wait time is discrete, while non-zero wait times are evaluated on a continuous time scale.[^o21] In physics (particularly quantum mechanics, where this sort of distribution often arises), [Dirac delta functions](wikipedia://en/37021) are often used to treat continuous and discrete components in a unified manner. For example, the previous example might be described by a probability density $p(t)=\alpha \delta (t) + g(t)$, such that $P(t>0)=\int_0^\infty g(t)=1-\alpha$, and $P(t=0)=\alpha$.
