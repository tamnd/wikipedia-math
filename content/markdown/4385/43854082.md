# Direct sum of topological groups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Direct sum of topological groups"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) $G$ is called the **topological direct sum**[^o2] of two [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) $H_1$ and $H_2$ if the map

$$\begin{aligned} H_1\times H_2 &\longrightarrow G \\ (h_1,h_2) &\longmapsto h_1 h_2 \end{aligned}$$

is a topological isomorphism, meaning that it is a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) and a [group isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

More generally, $G$ is called the direct sum of a finite set of [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) $H_1, \ldots, H_n$ of the map

$$\begin{aligned} \prod^n_{i=1} H_i &\longrightarrow G \\ (h_i)_{i\in I} &\longmapsto h_1 h_2 \cdots h_n \end{aligned}$$

is a topological isomorphism.

If a topological group $G$ is the topological direct sum of the family of subgroups $H_1, \ldots, H_n$ then in particular, as an abstract group (without topology) it is also the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/106341) (in the usual way) of the family $H_i.$

:::

## Topological direct summands {#Topological_direct_summands}

Given a topological group $G,$ we say that a subgroup $H$ is a **topological direct summand** of $G$ (or that **splits topologically** from $G$) if and only if there exist another subgroup $K \leq G$ such that $G$ is the direct sum of the subgroups $H$ and $K.$

A the subgroup $H$ is a topological direct summand if and only if the [extension of topological groups](wikipedia://en/43834296)

$$0 \to H\stackrel{i}{{} \to {}} G\stackrel{\pi}{{} \to {}} G/H\to 0$$

splits, where $i$ is the natural inclusion and $\pi$ is the natural projection.

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Suppose that $G$ is a [locally compact abelian group](wikipedia://en/1651204) that contains the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) $\mathbb{T}$ as a subgroup. Then $\mathbb{T}$ is a topological direct summand of $G.$ The same assertion is true for the [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) $\mathbb{R}$.[^o8]

:::
