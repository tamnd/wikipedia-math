# Shu Jiang

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shu Jiang"}

**Shu Jiang**, also known as **Joy Jiang**, is a Canadian [biostatistician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biostatistician) and tenured associate professor in the Division of Public Health Sciences at the [Washington University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_School_of_Medicine) in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis).[^o2][^o3] She is also the co-founder of Prognosia Inc with [Graham Colditz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Colditz), a technology company that develops [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI) models that assess digital [mammograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammograms) to accurately predict the risk of [breast cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_cancer).[^o4] Prognosia's first tool, Prognosia Breast, earned Breakthrough Device Designation from the [U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Drug_Administration) in June 2025,[^o5] and the company was acquired by Lunit in September 2025.[^o6][^o7]

:::

Jiang is known for her methodological contributions to breast cancer risk prediction using high-dimensional mammogram images.[^o8] In April 2023, *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* and *[CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN)* interviewed Jiang for a feature about breast density and cancer risk, in which she discussed recent findings on how changes in density over time may indicate elevated breast cancer risk.[^o9][^o10] In December 2024, *[U.S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report)* cited Jiang in an article about artificial intelligence and breast cancer screening. Discussing her team's work on longitudinal mammogram analysis, Jiang said: "Our new method is able to detect subtle changes over time in repeated mammogram images that are not visible to the eye."[^o11] More recently, media coverage has also included BreastCancer.org, which described a newer AI-based approach in which Jiang used multiple years of mammograms to enhance risk prediction.[^o12]

She is an associate editor for the journal *[Biometrics](wikipedia://en/11885692)*[^o13] and a statistical reviewer for *[JAMA Network Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JAMA_Network_Open)*.[^o14] Among other awards and honors, Jiang has been named a *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* 30 Under 30 in Healthcare in 2023[^o15][^o16] and received the [MERIT award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health_MERIT_Award) from the [National Cancer Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cancer_Institute) (2021-2027).[^o17]

## Education {#Education}

Jiang received a B.Sc. in statistics from [Simon Fraser University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fraser_University) (2012-2015), an M.Sc. in statistics from [Western University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_University_%28Canada%29) (2015-2016), and a Ph.D. in statistics from [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) (2016-2018), where her supervisor was Richard Cook.[^o24][^o19] From 2018-2019, she trained as a postdoctoral fellow in biostatistics at the [Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_T.H._Chan_School_of_Public_Health), where her supervisor was [Rebecca Betensky](wikipedia://en/55578037).[^o2]
