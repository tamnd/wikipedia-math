# Half-integer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Half-integer"}

*Image: Ciudad de Quebec 93.jpg.* Half-integer house number in [Quebec City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City)

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **half-integer** is a [number](wikipedia://en/21690) of the form

$$n + \tfrac{1}{2},$$

where $n$ is an integer. For example,

$$4\tfrac12,\quad 7/2,\quad -\tfrac{13}{2},\quad 8.5$$

are all *half-integers*. The name "half-integer" is perhaps misleading, as each integer $n$ is itself half of the integer $2n$. A name such as "integer-plus-half" may be more accurate, but while not literally true, "half integer" is the conventional term.

:::

Note that halving an integer does not always produce a half-integer; this is only true for [odd integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_integer). For this reason, half-integers are also sometimes called **half-odd-integers**. Half-integers are a subset of the [dyadic rationals](wikipedia://en/56263) (numbers produced by dividing an integer by a [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948)).[^o2]

## Notation and algebraic structure {#Notation_and_algebraic_structure}

The [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of all half-integers is often denoted

$$\mathbb Z + \tfrac{1}{2} \quad = \quad \left( \tfrac{1}{2} \mathbb Z \right) \smallsetminus \mathbb Z ~.$$

The integers and half-integers together form a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) under the addition operation, which may be denoted[^o4]

$$\tfrac{1}{2} \mathbb Z ~.$$

However, these numbers do not form a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) because the product of two half-integers is not a half-integer; e.g. $~\tfrac{1}{2} \times \tfrac{1}{2} ~=~ \tfrac{1}{4} ~ \notin ~ \tfrac{1}{2} \mathbb Z ~.$[^o5] The [smallest ring containing](wikipedia://en/24873145) them is $\mathbb{Z}\left[\tfrac12\right]$, the ring of [dyadic rationals](wikipedia://en/56263).

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The sum of $n$ half-integers is a half-integer if and only if $n$ is odd. This includes $n=0$ since the empty sum 0 is not half-integer.
- The negative of a half-integer is a half-integer.
- The [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of the set of half-integers is equal to that of the integers. This is due to the existence of a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) from the integers to the half-integers: $f:x\to x+0.5$, where $x$ is an integer.

:::

## Uses {#Uses}

### Sphere packing {#Sphere_packing}

The densest [lattice packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_packing) of [unit spheres](wikipedia://en/27033090) in four dimensions (called the [*D*<sub>4</sub> lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D4_lattice)) places a sphere at every point whose coordinates are either all integers or all half-integers. This packing is closely related to the [Hurwitz integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurwitz_integer): [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) whose real coefficients are either all integers or all half-integers.[^o11]

### Physics {#Physics}

In physics, the [Pauli exclusion principle](wikipedia://en/24669) results from definition of [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion) as particles which have [spins](wikipedia://en/19593829) that are half-integers.[^o13]

The [energy levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_level) of the [quantum harmonic oscillator](wikipedia://en/50719) occur at half-integers and thus its lowest energy is not zero.[^o14]

### Sphere volume {#Sphere_volume}

Although the [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) function is defined only for integer arguments, it can be extended to fractional arguments using the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316). The gamma function for half-integers is an important part of the formula for the [volume of an $n$-dimensional ball](wikipedia://en/21067650) of radius $R$,[^o16]

$$V_n(R) = \frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2} + 1)}R^n~.$$

The values of the gamma function on half-integers are rational multiples of the square root of [pi](wikipedia://en/23601):

$$\Gamma\left(\tfrac{1}{2} + n\right) ~=~ \frac{\,(2n-1)!!\,}{2^n}\, \sqrt{\pi\,} ~=~ \frac{(2n)!}{\,4^n \, n!\,} \sqrt{\pi\,} ~$$

where $n!!$ denotes the [double factorial](wikipedia://en/507208).
