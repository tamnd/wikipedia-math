# Sigma-ideal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sigma-ideal"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), a **\-ideal**, or **sigma ideal**, of a [σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/29586) (, read "sigma") is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) with certain desirable [closure](wikipedia://en/293802) properties. It is a special type of [ideal](wikipedia://en/12633368). Its most frequent application is in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).

:::

Let $(X, \Sigma)$ be a [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333) (meaning $\Sigma$ is a -algebra of subsets of $X$). A subset $N$ of $\Sigma$ is a -ideal if the following properties are satisfied:

1. $$\varnothing \in N;$$
1. When $A \in N$ and $B \in \Sigma$ then $B \subseteq A$ implies $B \in N$;
1. If $\left\{A_n\right\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \subseteq N$ then $\bigcup_{n \in \mathbb{N}} A_n \in N.$

Briefly, a sigma-ideal must contain the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) and contain measurable subsets and countable unions of its elements. The concept of -ideal is [dual](wikipedia://en/600618) to that of a [countably](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably) [complete](wikipedia://en/549445) (-) [filter](wikipedia://en/19719).

If a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) $\mu$ is given on $(X, \Sigma),$ the set of $\mu$-[negligible sets](wikipedia://en/195132) ($S \in \Sigma$ such that [$\mu(S) = 0$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_zero)) is a -ideal.

The notion can be generalized to [preorders](wikipedia://en/23582) $(P, \leq, 0)$ with a bottom element $0$ as follows: $I$ is a -ideal of $P$ just when

(i') $0 \in I,$

(ii') $x \leq y \text{ and } y \in I$ implies $x \in I,$ and

(iii') given a sequence $x_1, x_2, \ldots \in I,$ there exists some $y \in I$ such that $x_n \leq y$ for each $n.$

Thus $I$ contains the bottom element, is downward closed, and satisfies a countable analogue of the property of being [upwards directed](wikipedia://en/9165).

A **\-ideal** of a set $X$ is a -ideal of the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of $X.$ That is, when no -algebra is specified, then one simply takes the full power set of the underlying set. For example, the [meager subsets](wikipedia://en/226975) of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) are those in the -ideal generated by the collection of closed subsets with empty interior.
