# Daniel Gorenstein

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Gorenstein"}

**Daniel E. Gorenstein** (January 1, 1923 – August 26, 1992) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best remembered for his contribution to the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445).[^o3]

:::

Gorenstein mastered calculus at age 12 and subsequently matriculated at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University),[^o4] where he earned his bachelor's and master's degrees.[^o3] During the Second World War, he taught mathematics to military personnel.[^o4] After the war, he stayed at Harvard and earned his PhD 1950 under the supervision of [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352). In his dissertation, Gorenstein introduced a duality principle for plane curves that motivated [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042)'s introduction of [Gorenstein rings](wikipedia://en/515613). Gorenstein held posts at [Clark University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_University) and [Northeastern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_University_%28Boston,_Massachusetts%29), before moving to [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) in 1969, where he remained for the rest of his life. He became the founding director of the Center for Discrete Mathematics and Theoretical Computer Science ([DIMACS](wikipedia://en/3684858)) at Rutgers in 1989, and remained at this post until his death.[^o5]

He was recognized, in addition to his own research contributions such as work on [signalizer functors](wikipedia://en/28206366), as a leader in directing the classification of finite simple groups, one of the largest collaborative pieces of [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) ever attempted.[^o3] In 1972, he was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow) and a [Fulbright Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Scholar). In 1978, he gained membership in the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences). In 1989, Gorenstein won the [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize) in mathematical exposition from the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for his book *Finite Simple Groups: An Introduction to Their Classification* (1982).[^o3] In 1985, he wrote an article explaining his mathematical work to the layperson for *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)*.[^o6]

He died in 1992 after a brief illness. He was 69 years old.[^o3]
