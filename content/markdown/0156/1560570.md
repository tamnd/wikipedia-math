# David Crighton

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Crighton"}

**David George Crighton**, [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o3] (15 November 1942 – 12 April 2000) was a British mathematician and physicist.[^o4]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Crighton was born in [Llandudno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llandudno), North Wales, where his mother, Violet Grace Garrison, had been sent because of the bombing of [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). He did not become interested in mathematics until his last two years at [Watford Grammar School for Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watford_Grammar_School_for_Boys). He entered [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), in 1961 and started lecturing at Woolwich Polytechnic (today [University of Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greenwich)) in 1964, having completed only his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree).

A few years later he met [John Ffowcs Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ffowcs_Williams) and started to work for him at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London), while simultaneously studying for his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) (awarded in 1969) at the same place. In 1974, he was appointed as a [research fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_fellow) in the [Department of Engineering at the University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Engineering,_University_of_Cambridge). However, he never took up this post, but instead accepted the chair in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds), which he held until 1986.

He then returned to Cambridge as Professor of [Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Mathematics) in succession to [George Batchelor](wikipedia://en/1763397).

He became Master of [Jesus College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Cambridge) in 1997 and held that post until his death.[^o6][^o7] He was also head of the Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics Department ([DAMTP](wikipedia://en/3254271)) in Cambridge between 1991 and 2000.[^o8]

Away from his mathematical work, Crighton was a devotee of the music of [Richard Wagner](wikipedia://en/25452), as well as music for the [piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano).[^o9]

## Work {#Work}

Crighton's scientific interests were primarily in the theory of [waves](wikipedia://en/33516) and [aeroacoustics](wikipedia://en/2518272), as well as in some areas of [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988). He published over 120 papers. He also published one book in 1977 entitled *Introduction to Wiener-Hopf methods in acoustics and vibration*.[^o11] Crighton has also developed council initiatives to fund the research of nonlinear mathematics.[^o12]

In his first paper, Crighton studied the [sound wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_wave) associated with [turbulent flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbulent_flow) over a discontinuous [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_volume) formed by two semi-infinite flexible planes. Over the years, he worked broadly in the fields of [acoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustics), [equation theory](wikipedia://en/45200) and [quasi-diabatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adiabatic_circuit) systems including [solitons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitons). This included works on the generalised [Burgers' equation](wikipedia://en/1023353) and [inverse scattering theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_scattering_theory).

Of his research, Crighton, with FG Leppington, coupled the [method of matched asymptotic expansions](wikipedia://en/4265892) and the [Wiener-Hopf method](wikipedia://en/4363034) to study the scattering of sound waves by solids. Other highlights include his study of how vortices in the flow behind jet engines interact with solid boundaries to produce sound, and his analysis on the states of vibrations of a fluid-loaded rib plate.[^o13]

The distinction of his work was recognised by the award of the [Rayleigh Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh_Medal) of the [Institute of Acoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Acoustics_%28United_Kingdom%29), the Per Bruel Gold Medal of the [American Society of Mechanical Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Mechanical_Engineers) and the [Otto Laporte Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Laporte_Award) of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society).

## David Crighton Medal {#David_Crighton_Medal}

The [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) and the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) instituted the David Crighton Medal in 2002 in honour of Crighton. The award is made biennially, and was first presented in 2003.[^o15] Holders of the medal include [Frank Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Kelly_%28mathematician%29), [Peter Neumann](wikipedia://en/7151472), [Keith Moffatt](wikipedia://en/9071425), [Christopher Zeeman](wikipedia://en/871415), [John Ball](wikipedia://en/3578468), [David Abrahams](wikipedia://en/10322293) and [Alain Goriely](wikipedia://en/64431963).[^o16]
