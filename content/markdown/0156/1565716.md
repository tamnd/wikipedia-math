# Francesco Severi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Francesco Severi"}

*Image: Severi.jpeg.* Francesco Severi (photo by Konrad Jacobs)

**Francesco Severi** (13 April 1879 – 8 December 1961) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was the chair of the committee on [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1936, at the first delivery.

:::

Severi was born in [Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arezzo), Italy. He is famous for his contributions to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and the [theory of functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables). He became the effective leader of the [Italian school of algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/383424). Together with [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661), he won the [Bordin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordin_Prize) from the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).

He contributed in a major way to [birational geometry](wikipedia://en/382733), the theory of [algebraic surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311), in particular of the curves lying on them, the theory of [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) and the [theory of functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables). He wrote prolifically, and some of his work (following the intuition-led approach of [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661)) has subsequently been shown to be not rigorous according to the then new standards set in particular by [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352) and [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019). Although many of his arguments have since been made rigorous, a significant fraction were not only lacking in rigor but also wrong (in contrast to the work of Enriques, which though not rigorous was almost entirely correct). At the personal level, according to (Roth 1963) he was easily offended, and he was involved in a number of controversies. Most notably, he was a staunch supporter of the Italian fascist regime of [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini) and was included on a committee of academics that was to conduct an antisemitic purge of all scholarly societies and academic institutions.[^o5]

## Biography {#Biography}

His childhood was marked by the death of his father, which occurred when he was 9 years old. This had serious economic repercussions on their family. Although he had to earn a living while conducting private lessons, Francesco Severi managed to continue his studies and enroll in the engineering course at the [University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin). Due to the influence of courses by [Corrado Segre](wikipedia://en/2422061), Severi quickly found a passion for pure mathematics.

In 1900, he completed his training with a thesis in the [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153), which would later become his favorite subject.

After his thesis, he became assistant to [Enrico D'Ovidio](wikipedia://en/55588432) at the University of Turin and from 1902 to 1905, he was a lecturer in [projective](wikipedia://en/243849) and [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891). But soon, he obtained his transfer to the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) as assistant to [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661). Then at the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa) as assistant to [Eugenio Bertini](wikipedia://en/33285389).

In 1904, in consideration of the results he obtained in the geometry of numbers (founding the theory of [birational invariants](wikipedia://en/2605247) of [algebraic surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311)), he obtained the chair of projective and descriptive geometry at the [University of Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Parma). However, he spent a year at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua). where, he teaches different subjects, and takes the direction of the engineering unit.

In 1906, he obtained a theorem of existence of algebraic curves drawn on certain types of surfaces, thus beginning the search for the classification of [rational surfaces](wikipedia://en/2380148).[^o7]

Mobilized during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), Severi enlisted in the artillery.

In 1921, he obtained the chair of algebraic geometry at [La Sapienza University in Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome).

In 1923, he was elected rector of this university. But in 1925, following [the assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Matteotti#Murder) of the socialist politician [Matteotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Matteotti), he gave up his duties as rector. Nevertheless, Severi would remain without reaction against [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) and would accept the application of the [racial laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Jewish_laws).

In 1938, Severi was one of the founders of the *[Istituto Nazionale di Alta Matematica](wikipedia://en/34249626)*. [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352) is one of his most famous students.

In 1959, he converted to Catholicism and published his autobiography *Dalla scienza alla fede* (1959), he repents of his lack of political discernment

> *Mathematics is the art of giving the same name to various things, and mathematicians often make mistakes in politics, because it is, conversely, the art of giving different names to identical things.*

During his career, Severi received numerous awards, including the Gold Medal of the National Academy of Sciences and, together with Federigo Enriques, the Bordin Prize of the Paris Academy of Sciences (this award, created in 1835 by Charles-Laurent Bordin is a biennial prize awarded to authors of works on subjects of public interest).

He was a member of numerous Italian and foreign academies, including the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) in 1910 and the [Accademia delle Scienze di Torino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino) in 1918.
