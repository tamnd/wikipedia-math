# Disjoint union (topology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Disjoint union"}

In [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649) and related areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **disjoint union**[^o2] (also called the **direct sum**, **free union**, **free sum**, **topological sum**, or **coproduct**) of a [family](wikipedia://en/23624339) of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) is a space formed by equipping the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of the underlying sets with a [natural topology](wikipedia://en/11934923) called the **disjoint union topology**. Roughly speaking, in the disjoint union the given spaces are considered as part of a single new space where each looks as it would alone and they are isolated from each other.

:::

The name *coproduct* originates from the fact that the disjoint union is the [categorical dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_dual) of the [product space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_space) construction.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let {*X*<sub>*i*</sub> : *i* ∈ *I*} be a family of topological spaces indexed by *I*. Let

$$X = \coprod_i X_i$$

be the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of the underlying sets. For each *i* in *I*, let

$$\varphi_i : X_i \to X\,$$

be the **canonical injection** (defined by $\varphi_i(x)=(x,i)$). The **disjoint union topology** on *X* is defined as the [finest topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finest_topology) on *X* for which all the canonical injections $\varphi_i$ are [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) (i.e.: it is the [final topology](wikipedia://en/1819736) on *X* induced by the canonical injections).

Explicitly, the disjoint union topology can be described as follows. A subset *U* of *X* is [open](wikipedia://en/39358) in *X* [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its [preimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimage) $\varphi_i^{-1}(U)$ is open in *X*<sub>*i*</sub> for each *i* ∈ *I*. Yet another formulation is that a subset *V* of *X* is open relative to *X* [iff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iff) its intersection with *X<sub>i</sub>* is open relative to *X<sub>i</sub>* for each *i*.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The disjoint union space *X*, together with the canonical injections, can be characterized by the following [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245): If *Y* is a topological space, and *f<sub>i</sub>* : *X<sub>i</sub>* → *Y* is a continuous map for each *i* ∈ *I*, then there exists *precisely one* continuous map *f* : *X* → *Y* such that the following set of diagrams [commute](wikipedia://en/138484):

This shows that the disjoint union is the [coproduct](wikipedia://en/310889) in the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051). It follows from the above universal property that a map *f* : *X* → *Y* is continuous [iff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iff) *f<sub>i</sub>* = *f* o φ<sub>*i*</sub> is continuous for all *i* in *I*.

In addition to being continuous, the canonical injections φ<sub>*i*</sub> : *X*<sub>*i*</sub> → *X* are [open and closed maps](wikipedia://en/372090). It follows that the injections are [topological embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_embedding) so that each *X*<sub>*i*</sub> may be canonically thought of as a [subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_%28topology%29) of *X*.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

If each *X*<sub>*i*</sub> is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to a fixed space *A*, then the disjoint union *X* is homeomorphic to the [product space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_space) *A* × *I* where *I* has the [discrete topology](wikipedia://en/56061).

:::

## Preservation of topological properties {#Preservation_of_topological_properties}

- Every disjoint union of [discrete spaces](wikipedia://en/56061) is discrete
- *Separation*
  - Every disjoint union of [T<sub>0</sub> spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T0_space) is T<sub>0</sub>
  - Every disjoint union of [T<sub>1</sub> spaces](wikipedia://en/148721) is T<sub>1</sub>
  - Every disjoint union of [Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/13637) is Hausdorff
- *Connectedness*
  - The disjoint union of two or more nonempty topological spaces is [disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disconnected_%28topology%29)
