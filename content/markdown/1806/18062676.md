# Monogenic field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monogenic field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **monogenic field** is an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) *K* for which there exists an element *a* such that the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) *O*<sub>*K*</sub> is the subring **Z**\[*a*\] of *K* generated by *a*. Then *O*<sub>*K*</sub> is a quotient of the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) **Z**\[*X*\] and the powers of *a* constitute a **power integral basis**.

:::

In a monogenic field *K*, the [field discriminant](wikipedia://en/3022963) of *K* is equal to the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) of the [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) of α.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of monogenic fields include:

- [Quadratic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_fields):

if $K = \mathbf{Q}(\sqrt d)$ with $d$ a [square-free integer](wikipedia://en/29525), then $O_K = \mathbf{Z}[a]$ where $a = (1+\sqrt d)/2$ if *d* ≡ 1 (mod 4) and $a = \sqrt d$ if *d* ≡ 2 or 3 (mod 4).

- [Cyclotomic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotomic_fields):

if $K = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta)$ with $\zeta$ a [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950), then $O_K = \mathbf{Z}[\zeta].$ Also the maximal real subfield $\mathbf{Q}(\zeta)^{+} = \mathbf{Q}(\zeta + \zeta^{-1})$ is monogenic, with ring of integers $\mathbf{Z}[\zeta+\zeta^{-1}]$.

While all quadratic fields are monogenic, already among cubic fields there are many that are not monogenic. The first example of a non-monogenic number field that was found is the [cubic field](wikipedia://en/22076179) generated by a root of the polynomial $X^3 - X^2 - 2X - 8$, due to [Richard Dedekind](wikipedia://en/23475106).

:::
