# Multiplicative group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multiplicative group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the term **multiplicative group** refers to one of the following concepts:

:::

- the **[group](wikipedia://en/19447) under multiplication** of the [invertible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertible) elements of a [field](wikipedia://en/10603),[^n1] [ring](wikipedia://en/48404), or other structure for which one of its operations is referred to as multiplication. In the case of a field *F*, the group is (*F* ∖ $\{0\}$, •), where 0 refers to the [zero element](wikipedia://en/3476379) of *F* and the [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) • is the field [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845),
- the [algebraic torus](wikipedia://en/504697) GL(1).

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [multiplicative group of integers modulo *n*](wikipedia://en/1195234) is the group under multiplication of the invertible elements of $\mathbf{Z}/n\mathbf{Z}$. When *n* is not prime, there are elements other than zero that are not invertible.
- The multiplicative group of [positive real numbers](wikipedia://en/47172571) $\mathbf{R}^+$ is an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) with 1 as its [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962). The [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) is a [group isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397) of this group to the [additive group](wikipedia://en/332229) of real numbers, $\mathbf{R}$.
- The multiplicative group of a field $F$ is the set of all nonzero elements: $F^\times = F \smallsetminus \{0\}$, under the multiplication operation. If $F$ is [finite](wikipedia://en/11615) of order *q* (for example *q* = *p* a prime, and $F = \mathbb F_p=\mathbf{Z}/p\mathbf{Z}$), then the [multiplicative group](wikipedia://en/11615#Multiplicative_structure) is cyclic: $F^\times \cong \mathrm{C}_{q-1}$.

:::

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Group scheme of roots of unity"}

## Group scheme of roots of unity {#Group_scheme_of_roots_of_unity}

The **group scheme of *n*th [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity)** is by definition the kernel of the *n*-power map on the multiplicative group GL(1), considered as a [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523). That is, for any integer *n* > 1 we can consider the morphism on the multiplicative group that takes *n*th powers, and take an appropriate [fiber product of schemes](wikipedia://en/56444110), with the morphism *e* that serves as the identity.

The resulting group scheme is written *μ*<sub>*n*</sub> (or $\mu\!\!\mu_n$[^n2]). It gives rise to a [reduced scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_scheme), when we take it over a field *K*, [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_%28field%29) of *K* does not divide *n*. This makes it a source of some key examples of non-reduced schemes (schemes with [nilpotent elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilpotent_element) in their [structure sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_sheaf)); for example *μ*<sub>*p*</sub> over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) with *p* elements for any [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*.

This phenomenon is not easily expressed in the classical language of algebraic geometry. For example, it turns out to be of major importance in expressing the [duality theory of abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duality_theory_of_abelian_varieties) in characteristic *p* (theory of [Pierre Cartier](wikipedia://en/1470549)). The [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) of this group scheme is a way of expressing [Kummer theory](wikipedia://en/581610).
