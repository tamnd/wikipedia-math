# Hall–Janko graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hall–Janko graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Hall–Janko graph**, also known as the **Hall-Janko-Wales graph**, is a 36-[regular](wikipedia://en/85821) [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) with 100 vertices and 1800 edges.

:::

It is a [rank 3](wikipedia://en/29763000) [strongly regular graph](wikipedia://en/1399856) with parameters (100,36,14,12) and a maximum [coclique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coclique) of size 10. This parameter set is not unique, it is however uniquely determined by its parameters as a rank 3 graph. The Hall–Janko graph was originally constructed by D. Wales to establish the existence of the [Hall-Janko group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall-Janko_group) as an [index](wikipedia://en/230581) 2 subgroup of its [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/15094186).

The Hall–Janko graph can be constructed out of objects in U<sub>3</sub>(3), the simple group of order 6048:[^o4][^o5]

- In U<sub>3</sub>(3) there are 36 simple maximal subgroups of order 168. These are the vertices of a subgraph, the U<sub>3</sub>(3) graph. A 168-subgroup has 14 maximal subgroups of order 24, isomorphic to S<sub>4</sub>. Two 168-subgroups are called adjacent when they intersect in a 24-subgroup. The U<sub>3</sub>(3) graph is strongly regular, with parameters (36,14,4,6)
- There are 63 involutions (elements of order 2). A 168-subgroup contains 21 involutions, which are defined to be neighbors.
- Outside U<sub>3</sub>(3) let there be a 100th vertex **C**, whose neighbors are the 36 168-subgroups. A 168-subgroup then has 14 common neighbors with C and in all 1+14+21 neighbors.
- An involution is found in 12 of the 168-subgroups. C and an involution are non-adjacent, with 12 common neighbors.
- Two involutions are defined as adjacent when they generate a dihedral subgroup of order 8.[^o6] An involution has 24 involutions as neighbors.

The characteristic polynomial of the Hall–Janko graph is $(x-36)(x-6)^{36}(x+4)^{63}$. Therefore the Hall–Janko graph is an [integral graph](wikipedia://en/24845923): its [spectrum](wikipedia://en/534914) consists entirely of integers.
