# Lyapunov time

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lyapunov time"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Lyapunov time** is the characteristic timescale on which a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) is [chaotic](wikipedia://en/6295). It is named after the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Aleksandr Lyapunov](wikipedia://en/65425). It is defined as the inverse of a system's largest [Lyapunov exponent](wikipedia://en/166441).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Use"}

## Use {#Use}

The Lyapunov time mirrors the limits of the [predictability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predictability) of the system. By convention, it is defined as the time for the distance between nearby trajectories of the system to increase by a factor of *[e](wikipedia://en/9633)*. However, measures in terms of 2-foldings and 10-foldings are sometimes found, since they correspond to the loss of one bit of information or one digit of precision respectively.[^o10][^o5]

While it is used in many applications of dynamical systems theory, it has been particularly used in [celestial mechanics](wikipedia://en/102182) where it is important for the problem of the [stability of the Solar System](wikipedia://en/18412841). However, empirical estimation of the Lyapunov time is often associated with computational or inherent uncertainties.[^o6][^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Typical values are:[^o10]

::: {#o11 .table cue=table}

| System | Lyapunov time |
|---|---|
| [Pluto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluto)'s orbit | 20 million years |
| [Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System) | 5 million years |
| [Axial tilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt) of [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars) | 1–5 million years |
| Orbit of [36 Atalante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36_Atalante) | 4,000 years |
| Rotation of [Hyperion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperion_%28moon%29) | 36 days |
| Chemical chaotic oscillations | \5.4 minutes |
| [Hydrodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamic) chaotic oscillations | 2 seconds |
| 1 cm<sup>3</sup> of [argon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argon) at room temperature | \3.7×10<sup>−11</sup> seconds |
| 1 cm<sup>3</sup> of argon at triple point (84 K, 69 kPa) | \3.7×10<sup>−16</sup> seconds |

:::
