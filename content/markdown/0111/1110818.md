# Held group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Held group"}

In the area of modern algebra known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Held group** *He* is a [sporadic simple group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_simple_group) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527)

:::

4,030,387,200 = 2<sup>10</sup>3<sup>3</sup>5<sup>2</sup>7<sup>3</sup>17

≈ 4.

## History {#History}

*He* is one of the 26 sporadic groups and was found by (txt) during an investigation of simple groups containing an involution whose centralizer is an extension of the [extra special group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_special_group) 2<sup>1+6</sup> by the [linear group](wikipedia://en/382667) L<sub>3</sub>(2), which is the same involution centralizer as the [Mathieu group M<sub>24</sub>](wikipedia://en/15200706). A second such group is the linear group L<sub>5</sub>(2). The Held group is the third possibility, and its construction was completed by [John McKay](wikipedia://en/4583624) and [Graham Higman](wikipedia://en/2600152). In all of these groups, the extension splits.

The [outer automorphism group](wikipedia://en/628466) has order 2 and the [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587) is trivial.

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Representations"}

## Representations {#Representations}

The smallest faithful complex representation has dimension 51; there are two such representations that are duals of each other.

It [centralizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) an element of order 7 in the [Monster group](wikipedia://en/47422). As a result the prime 7 plays a special role in the theory of the group; for example, the smallest representation of the Held group over any field is the 50-dimensional representation over the field with 7 elements, and it acts naturally on a [vertex operator algebra](wikipedia://en/1287577) over the field with 7 elements.

The smallest permutation representation is a rank 5 action on 2058 points with point stabilizer Sp<sub>4</sub>(4):2. The [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) associated with this representation has rank 5 and is [directed](wikipedia://en/19721986); the outer automorphism reverses the direction of the edges, decreasing the rank to 4.

Since He is the normalizer of a [Frobenius group](wikipedia://en/1716123) 7:3 in the [Monster group](wikipedia://en/47422), it does not just commute with a 7-cycle, but also some 3-cycles. Each of these 3-cycles is normalized by the [Fischer group Fi<sub>24</sub>](wikipedia://en/36207356), so He:2 is a subgroup of the derived subgroup Fi<sub>24</sub>' (the non-simple group Fi<sub>24</sub> has 2 conjugacy classes of He:2, which are fused by an outer automorphism). As mentioned above, the smallest permutation representation of He has 2058 points, and when realized inside Fi<sub>24</sub>', there is an [orbit](wikipedia://en/12781#Orbits_and_stabilizers) of 2058 [transpositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischer_groups).

:::

## Generalized monstrous moonshine {#Generalized_monstrous_moonshine}

Conway and Norton suggested in their 1979 paper that [monstrous moonshine](wikipedia://en/728168) is not limited to the monster, but that similar phenomena may be found for other groups. Larissa Queen and others subsequently found that one can construct the expansions of many Hauptmoduln from simple combinations of dimensions of sporadic groups. For *He*, the relevant McKay-Thompson series is $T_{7A}(\tau)$ where one can set the constant term a(0) = 10 (),

$$\begin{aligned} j_{7A}(\tau) &= T_{7A}(\tau)+10\\ &= \left(\left(\tfrac{\eta(\tau)}{\eta(7\tau)}\right)^{2} + 7\left(\tfrac{\eta(7\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\right)^2\right)^2\\ &= \frac{1}{q} + 10 + 51q + 204q^2 + 681q^3 + 1956q^4 + 5135q^5 + \dots \end{aligned}$$

and *η*(*τ*) is the [Dedekind eta function](wikipedia://en/447020).

## Presentation {#Presentation}

It can be defined in terms of the generators *a* and *b* and relations

$$a^2 = b^7 = (ab)^{17} = [a, b]^6 = \left [a, b^3 \right ]^5 = \left [a, babab^{-1}abab \right ] = (ab)^4 ab^2 ab^{-3} ababab^{-1}ab^3 ab^{-2}ab^2 = 1.$$
