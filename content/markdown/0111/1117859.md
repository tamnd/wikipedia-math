# Landau distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Landau distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **Landau distribution** is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) named after [Lev Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Landau) who used it in 1944.[^o2]

:::

Because of the distribution's "fat" tail, the [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of the distribution, such as mean or variance, are undefined. The distribution is a particular case of [stable distribution](wikipedia://en/1117869).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487), as written originally by Landau, is defined by the [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [integral](wikipedia://en/15532):

$$p(x) = \frac{1}{2 \pi i} \int_{a-i\infty}^{a+i\infty} e^{s \ln s + x s}\, ds ,$$

where $a$ is an arbitrary positive [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438), meaning that the integration path can be any parallel to the imaginary axis, intersecting the real positive semi-axis, and $\ln$ refers to the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476). In other words, it is the [inverse Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/245688) of the function $s^s$.[^o5]

The following real integral is equivalent to the above:

$$p(x) = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^\infty e^{-t \ln t - x t} \sin(\pi t)\, dt.$$

The full family of Landau distributions is obtained by extending the original distribution to a [location-scale family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location-scale_family) of [stable distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_distributions) with parameters $\alpha=1$ and $\beta=1$,[^o6] with [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401):[^o7]

$$\varphi(t;\mu,c)=\exp\left(it\mu -\tfrac{2ict}{\pi}\ln|t|-c|t|\right)$$

where $c\in(0,\infty)$ and $\mu\in(-\infty,\infty)$, which yields a density function:

$$p(x;\mu,c) = \frac{1}{\pi c}\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-t} \cos\left(\left(x-\mu\right) \frac{t}{c} + \frac{2t}{\pi}\ln \frac{t}{c}\right)\, dt ,$$

Taking $\mu = 0$ and $c = \frac{\pi}{2}$ we get the original form of $p(x)$ above.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Landau\_pdf.svg.* The approximation function for $\mu=0,\,c=1$

- Translation: If $X \sim \textrm{Landau}(\mu,c)\,$ then $X + m \sim \textrm{Landau}(\mu + m , \, c) \,$.
- Scaling: If $X \sim \textrm{Landau}(\mu,c)\,$ then $aX \sim \textrm{Landau}(a\mu - \tfrac{2}{\pi}ac\ln a, \, ac)$.
- Sum: If $X \sim \textrm{Landau}(\mu_1, c_1)$ and $Y \sim \textrm{Landau}(\mu_2, c_2) \,$ then $X+Y \sim \textrm{Landau}(\mu_1+\mu_2, \, c_1+c_2)$.

These properties can all be derived from the characteristic function. Together they imply that the Landau distributions are closed under [affine transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_transformations).

### Approximations {#Approximations}

In the "standard" case $\mu = 0$ and $c = \pi/2$, the pdf can be approximated[^o11] using [Lindhard theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindhard_theory) which says:

$$p(x+\ln x - 1 + \gamma) \approx \frac{\exp(-1/x)}{x(1+x)},$$

where $\gamma$ is [Euler's constant](wikipedia://en/148594).

A similar approximation [^o12][^o13] of $p(x;\mu,c)$ for $\mu = 0$ and $c = 1$ is:

$$p(x) \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{x + e^{-x}}{2}\right).$$

:::

::: {#o15 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

In nuclear and particle physics, the Landau distribution appears as a probability that a fast particle with a given initial energy will lose a given energy after passing the layer of matter with given thickness.[^o16]

:::

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

- The Landau distribution is a [stable distribution](wikipedia://en/1117869) with stability parameter $\alpha$ and skewness parameter $\beta$ both equal to 1.
