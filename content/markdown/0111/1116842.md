# Discrepancy theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Discrepancy theory"}

In mathematics, **discrepancy theory** describes the deviation of a situation from the state one would like it to be in. It is also called the **theory of irregularities of distribution**. This refers to the theme of *classical* discrepancy theory, namely distributing points in some space such that they are evenly distributed with respect to some (mostly geometrically defined) subsets. The discrepancy (irregularity) measures how far a given distribution deviates from an ideal one.

:::

Discrepancy theory can be described as the study of inevitable irregularities of distributions, in [measure-theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) and [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) settings. Just as [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175) elucidates the impossibility of total disorder, discrepancy theory studies the deviations from total uniformity.

A significant event in the history of discrepancy theory was the 1916 paper of [Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) on the uniform distribution of sequences in the unit interval.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Theorems"}

## Theorems {#Theorems}

Discrepancy theory is based on the following classic theorems:

- [Geometric discrepancy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_discrepancy_theory)
- The theorem of [van Aardenne-Ehrenfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatyana_Pavlovna_Ehrenfest)
- Arithmetic progressions (Roth, Sarkozy, [Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jozsef_Beck), Matousek & [Spencer](wikipedia://en/4244250))
- [Beck–Fiala theorem](wikipedia://en/39400139)[^o5]
- Six Standard Deviations Suffice (Spencer)[^o6]

:::

## Major open problems {#Major_open_problems}

The unsolved problems relating to discrepancy theory include:

- Axis-parallel rectangles in dimensions three and higher (folklore)
- [Komlós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/János_Komlós_%28mathematician%29) conjecture
- [Heilbronn triangle problem](wikipedia://en/1013950) on the minimum area of a triangle determined by three points from an *n*-point set

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Applications for discrepancy theory include:

- Numerical integration: [Monte Carlo methods](wikipedia://en/56098) in high dimensions
- Computational geometry: [Divide-and-conquer algorithm](wikipedia://en/201154)
- Image processing: [Halftoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halftone)
- Random trial formulation: [Randomized controlled trial](wikipedia://en/163180)[^o10][^o11][^o12]

:::
