# Augustus Edward Hough Love

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Augustus Edward Hough Love"}

**Augustus Edward Hough Love** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o3] (17 April 1863, [Weston-super-Mare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weston-super-Mare) – 5 June 1940, [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford)), often known as **A. E. H. Love**, was an English mathematical physicist famous for research on [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29). He also studied wave propagation. He won the Adams Prize in 1911 for his work on the structure of the Earth in *Some Problems of [Geodynamics](wikipedia://en/9134092)*, in which he developed a mathematical model of surface waves known as [Love waves](wikipedia://en/2063011).[^o6] Love also contributed to the theory of [tidal locking](wikipedia://en/60162) and introduced the parameters known as [Love numbers](wikipedia://en/25141419), used in problems related to [Earth tides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_tides), the tidal deformation of the solid Earth due to the gravitational attraction of the Moon and Sun.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

He was educated at [Wolverhampton Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton_Grammar_School) and in 1881 won a scholarship to [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), where he was at first undecided whether to study classics or mathematics. In the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) of 1885 he placed as [Second Wrangler](wikipedia://en/19355609#Senior_Wranglers_and_runners_up,_1748–1909), vindicating his choice of mathematics. The next year he was elected Fellow of the college. In 1899 he was appointed [Sedleian Professor of Natural Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedleian_Professor_of_Natural_Philosophy) in the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), a position which he retained until his death in 1940. He was also a Fellow of [Queen's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_College,_Oxford).

He authored the two volume classic, *A Treatise on the Mathematical Theory of Elasticity*.

He was the author of several articles in the 1911 *[Encyclopædia Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopædia_Britannica)*, including Elasticity[^o8] and Infinitesimal Calculus.[^o9]

His other awards include the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) Royal Medal in 1909 and [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal) in 1937, and the [De Morgan Medal](wikipedia://en/1096532) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 1926. He was secretary to the London Mathematical Society between 1895 and 1910, and president for 1912–1913.
