# Standard Borel space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Standard Borel space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **standard Borel space** is the [Borel space](wikipedia://en/43327#Standard_Borel_spaces_and_Kuratowski_theorems) associated with a [Polish space](wikipedia://en/396622). Except in the case of [discrete](wikipedia://en/56061) Polish spaces, the standard Borel space is unique, up to [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) of [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333) $(X, \Sigma)$ is said to be "standard Borel" if there exists a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) on $X$ that makes it a [complete](wikipedia://en/7832) [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) metric space in such a way that $\Sigma$ is then the [Borel σ-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_σ-algebra).[^o4] Standard Borel spaces have several useful properties that do not hold for general measurable spaces.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- If $(X, \Sigma)$ and $(Y, T)$ are standard Borel then any bijective [measurable mapping](wikipedia://en/44055) $f : (X, \Sigma) \to (Y, \mathrm{T})$ is an isomorphism (that is, the inverse mapping is also measurable). This follows from [Souslin's theorem](wikipedia://en/634261), as a set that is both [analytic](wikipedia://en/634261) and [coanalytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coanalytic) is necessarily Borel.
- If $(X, \Sigma)$ and $(Y, T)$ are standard Borel spaces and $f : X \to Y$ then $f$ is measurable if and only if the [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of $f$ is Borel.
- The product and direct union of a [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) family of standard Borel spaces are standard.
- Every [complete](wikipedia://en/44775) [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) on a standard Borel space turns it into a [standard probability space](wikipedia://en/10406190).

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Kuratowski's theorem"}

## Kuratowski's theorem {#Kuratowski's_theorem}

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**. Let $X$ be a [Polish space](wikipedia://en/396622), that is, a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) such that there is a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) $d$ on $X$ that defines the topology of $X$ and that makes $X$ a complete separable metric space. Then $X$ as a Borel space is [Borel isomorphic](wikipedia://en/33605690) to one of (1) $\mathbb{R},$ (2) $\mathbb{Z}$ or (3) a finite discrete space. (This result is reminiscent of [Maharam's theorem](wikipedia://en/18759825).)

It follows that a standard Borel space is characterized up to isomorphism by its [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174),[^o10] and that any uncountable standard Borel space has the [cardinality of the continuum](wikipedia://en/1574901).

Borel isomorphisms on standard Borel spaces are analogous to [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) on [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450): both are bijective and closed under composition, and a homeomorphism and its inverse are both [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_%28topology%29), instead of both being only Borel measurable.

:::
