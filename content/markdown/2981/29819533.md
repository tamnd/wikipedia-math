# Mamikon Mnatsakanian

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mamikon Mnatsakanian"}

**Mamikon A. Mnatsakanian** (17 April 1942 – 12 April 2021[^o3]) (Մամիկոն Մնացականյան) was an Armenian physicist. In 1959, he discovered a new proof of the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034).[^o4]

:::

He received a Ph.D. in physics in 1969 from [Yerevan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerevan_State_University), where he became professor of astrophysics. As an undergraduate he specialized in the development of geometric methods for solving calculus problems by a visual approach that makes no use of formulas, which he later developed into his system of [visual calculus](wikipedia://en/10458716).

He was a Project Associate at [Project Mathematics!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Mathematics!) at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology).

With co-author [Tom Apostol](wikipedia://en/2196529), he won the [Paul R. Halmos – Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_R._Halmos_–_Lester_R._Ford_Award) given by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for author excellence, in 2005, 2008, and 2010.

When Apostol met Mamikon he wrote, "As a teacher of calculus for more than 50 years and as an author of a couple of textbooks on the subject, I was stunned to learn that many standard problems in calculus can be easily solved by an innovative visual approach that makes no use of formulas."[^o5]

In 2010, he was nominated by Caltech for the [Ambartsumians International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Hambardzumyan#Ambartsumians_International_Prize), awarded annually by the [President of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Armenia), for his contributions in the field of [theoretical astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_astrophysics).[^o6]

The book, *New Horizons in Geometry*, was the result of 15 years of collaboration between Tom Apostol and Mamikon.
