# George Grätzer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Grätzer"}

**George A. Grätzer** (Grätzer György; born 2 August 1936, in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest)) is a Hungarian-Canadian mathematician, specializing in [lattice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_theory) and [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200). He is known for his books on [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX)[^o2] and his proof with E. Tamás Schmidt of the Grätzer–Schmidt theorem.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

His father József Grätzer was famous in Hungary as the "Puzzle King" ("rejtvénykirály"). George Grätzer received his PhD from [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) in 1960 under the supervision of [László Fuchs](wikipedia://en/34809998). In 1963 Grätzer and Schmidt published their theorem on the characterization of [congruence lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congruence_lattice) of algebras.[^o7] In 1963 Grätzer left Hungary and became a professor at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University). In 1966 he became a professor at the [University of Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manitoba) and later a Canadian citizen. In 1970 Grätzer became the founder and editor-in-chief of the journal *[Algebra Universalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_Universalis)*. His mathematical articles—over 260, all listed on [Research Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ResearchGate)—are widely cited, and he has written several influential books.

Grätzer has received several awards and honours. He is married and has two children (Tom Gratzer and [David Gratzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gratzer)) and five grandchildren.

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

- Grünwald Memorial Prize (1967)
- [Steacie Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steacie_Prize) (1971)[^o9]
- Fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) (1973)
- [Jeffery–Williams Prize](wikipedia://en/18739069) (1978)
- Zubek Prize (1987)
- Elected Foreign Member of [Magyar Tudományos Akadémia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyar_Tudományos_Akadémia) (1997)

## Publications {#Publications}

More than 270 research articles in mathematics, and 36 books including

- *Elmesport egy esztendőre* 1959 (2008-as kiadása: ); trans. into English as [*Train your brain: A year's worth of puzzles*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Train_Your_Brain.html?id=6kJ-Es86orUC) 2011
- *Universal Algebra* 1967
- *Lattice Theory* 1971
- *General Lattice Theory* 1978
- [VP-Info](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VP-Info)*VP-Info Database Language* 1986
- [*First Steps in LaTeX*](https://books.google.com/books/about/First_Steps_in_LaTeX.html?id=mLdg5ZdDKToC) 1999
- [*More Math into LaTeX*](https://books.google.com/books/about/More_Math_Into_LaTeX.html?id=6WTZ0Rpiwp8C), sixth edition 2007
- *Lattice Theory: Foundation* 2011
- *Practical LaTeX* 2014
- *The Congruences of a Finite Lattice: A Proof-by-Picture Approach, third edition* 2023
- *Math into LaTeX, sixth edition* 2024
- *Math into English,* 2024
- *Write Better,* 2024
