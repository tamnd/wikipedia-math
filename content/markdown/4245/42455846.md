# Ran space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ran space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Ran space** (or **Ran's space**) of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* is a topological space $\operatorname{Ran}(X)$ whose underlying set is the set of all [nonempty](wikipedia://en/9566) finite [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of *X*: for a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) *X* the [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) is induced by the [Hausdorff distance](wikipedia://en/429296). The notion is named after [Ziv Ran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziv_Ran).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In general, the topology of the Ran space is generated by sets

$$\{ S \in \operatorname{Ran}(U_1 \cup \dots \cup U_m) \mid S \cap U_1 \ne \emptyset, \dots, S \cap U_m \ne \emptyset \}$$

for any [disjoint](wikipedia://en/39359) [open subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_subset) $U_i \subset X, i = 1, ..., m$.

There is an analog of a Ran space for a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754):[^o4] the **Ran prestack** of a [quasi-projective scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-projective_scheme) *X* over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *k*, denoted by $\operatorname{Ran}(X)$, is the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) whose [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) are triples $(R, S, \mu)$ consisting of a finitely generated [*k*-algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) *R*, a nonempty set *S* and a map of sets $\mu: S \to X(R)$, and whose [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) $(R, S, \mu) \to (R', S', \mu')$ consist of a [*k*-algebra homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_homomorphism) $R \to R'$ and a [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) map $S \to S'$ that commutes with $\mu$ and $\mu'$. Roughly, an *R*-point of $\operatorname{Ran}(X)$ is a nonempty finite set of *R*-rational points of *X* "with labels" given by $\mu$. A theorem of Beilinson and Drinfeld continues to hold: $\operatorname{Ran}(X)$ is [acyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acyclic_complex) if *X* is connected.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A theorem of Beilinson and Drinfeld states that the Ran space of a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) is [weakly contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_contractible).[^o7]

:::

## Topological chiral homology {#Topological_chiral_homology}

If *F* is a [cosheaf](wikipedia://en/42012361) on the Ran space $\operatorname{Ran}(M)$, then its space of global sections is called the **topological chiral homology** of *M* with coefficients in *F*. If *A* is, roughly, a family of commutative algebras parametrized by points in *M*, then there is a [factorizable sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factorizable_sheaf) associated to *A*. Via this construction, one also obtains the topological chiral homology with coefficients in *A*. The construction is a generalization of [Hochschild homology](wikipedia://en/10499629).[^o9]
