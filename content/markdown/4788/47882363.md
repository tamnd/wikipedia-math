# Universal space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Universal space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **universal space** is a certain [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) that contains all metric spaces whose [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) is bounded by some fixed constant. A similar definition exists in [topological dynamics](wikipedia://en/16379713).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a class $\textstyle \mathcal{C}$ of topological spaces, $\textstyle \mathbb{U}\in\mathcal{C}$ is **universal** for $\textstyle \mathcal{C}$ if each member of $\textstyle \mathcal{C}$ embeds in $\textstyle \mathbb{U}$. [Menger](wikipedia://en/149431) stated and proved the case $\textstyle d=1$ of the following theorem. The theorem in full generality was proved by Nöbeling.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:**[^o5] The $\textstyle (2d+1)$-dimensional cube $\textstyle [0,1]^{2d+1}$ is universal for the class of compact metric spaces whose [Lebesgue covering dimension](wikipedia://en/398886) is less than $\textstyle d$.

[Nöbeling](wikipedia://en/14175633) went further and proved:

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** The subspace of $\textstyle [0,1]^{2d+1}$ consisting of the set of points, at most $\textstyle d$ of whose coordinates are rational, is universal for the class of [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) metric spaces whose Lebesgue covering dimension is less than $\textstyle d$.

The last theorem was generalized by Lipscomb to the class of metric spaces of [weight](https://www.encyclopediaofmath.org/index.php/Weight_of_a_topological_space) $\textstyle \alpha$, $\textstyle \alpha>\aleph_{0}$: There exist a one-dimensional metric space $\textstyle J_{\alpha}$ such that the subspace of $\textstyle J_{\alpha}^{2d+1}$ consisting of set of points, at most $\textstyle d$ of whose coordinates are "rational"*(suitably defined),*is universal for the class of metric spaces whose Lebesgue covering dimension is less than $\textstyle d$ and whose weight is less than $\textstyle \alpha$.[^o7]

:::

## Universal spaces in topological dynamics {#Universal_spaces_in_topological_dynamics}

Consider the category of [topological dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_dynamical_system) $\textstyle (X,T)$ consisting of a compact metric space $\textstyle X$ and a homeomorphism $\textstyle T:X\rightarrow X$. The topological dynamical system $\textstyle (X,T)$ is called **minimal** if it has no proper non-empty closed $\textstyle T$-invariant subsets. It is called **infinite** if $\textstyle |X|=\infty$. A topological dynamical system $\textstyle (Y,S)$ is called a **factor** of $\textstyle (X,T)$ if there exists a continuous surjective mapping $\textstyle \varphi:X\rightarrow Y$ which is **equivariant**, i.e. $\textstyle \varphi(Tx)=S\varphi(x)$ for all $\textstyle x\in X$.

Similarly to the definition above, given a class $\textstyle \mathcal{C}$ of topological dynamical systems, $\textstyle \mathbb{U}\in\mathcal{C}$ is **universal** for $\textstyle \mathcal{C}$ if each member of $\textstyle \mathcal{C}$ embeds in $\textstyle \mathbb{U}$ through an equivariant continuous mapping. [Lindenstrauss](wikipedia://en/28371257) proved the following theorem:

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**[^o10]**:** Let $\textstyle d\in\mathbb{{N}}$. The compact metric topological dynamical system $\textstyle (X,T)$ where $\textstyle X=([0,1]^{d})^{\mathbb{{Z}}}$ and $\textstyle T:X\rightarrow X$ is the shift homeomorphism $\textstyle (\ldots,x_{-2},x_{-1},\mathbf{x_{0}},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\rightarrow(\ldots,x_{-1},x_{0},\mathbf{x_{1}},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots)$

is universal for the class of compact metric topological dynamical systems whose [mean dimension](wikipedia://en/47803999) is strictly less than $\textstyle \frac{d}{36}$ and which possess an infinite minimal factor.

In the same article Lindenstrauss asked what is the largest constant $\textstyle c$ such that a compact metric topological dynamical system whose mean dimension is strictly less than $\textstyle cd$ and which possesses an infinite minimal factor embeds into $\textstyle ([0,1]^{d})^{\mathbb{{Z}}}$. The results above implies $\textstyle c \geq \frac{1}{36}$. The question was answered by Lindenstrauss and Tsukamoto[^o11] who showed that $\textstyle c \leq \frac{1}{2}$ and Gutman and Tsukamoto[^o12] who showed that $\textstyle c \geq \frac{1}{2}$. Thus the answer is $\textstyle c=\frac{1}{2}$.

:::
