# Teruhisa Matsusaka

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Teruhisa Matsusaka"}

**Teruhisa Matsusaka** (1926–2006) was a Japanese-born American mathematician, who specialized in algebraic geometry.

:::

Matsusaka received his Ph.D. in 1954 at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University);[^o3] he was a member of the Brandeis Mathematics Department from 1961 until his retirement in 1994, and was that department's chair from 1984–1986. He was invited to address the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) held in Edinburgh in 1958 and was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1966.[^o5]

During the difficult years after the Second World War, Matsusaka worked on several problems connected with [Weil](wikipedia://en/2019)'s *[Foundations of Algebraic Geometry](wikipedia://en/26402563)*. This led to a correspondence and eventually Weil invited Matsusaka to the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) (1954–57) where they became life-long friends. After three years at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) and a year at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179),[^o6] [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), he went to Brandeis University in 1961 where he stayed until 1994, helping to build the department to its current prominence.[^o4]

Matsusaka was awarded a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) for the academic year 1959–1960.[^o7]

In 1972, Matsusaka introduced [Matsusaka's big theorem](wikipedia://en/42982817), a key technical result on [ample line bundles](wikipedia://en/1186896).
