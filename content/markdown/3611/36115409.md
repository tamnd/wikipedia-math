# Weyl–von Neumann theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weyl–von Neumann theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Weyl–von Neumann theorem** is a result in [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) due to [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) and [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942). It states that, after the addition of a [compact operator](wikipedia://en/577441) ((Weyl 1909)) or [Hilbert–Schmidt operator](wikipedia://en/976673) ((von Neumann 1935)) of arbitrarily small norm, a bounded [self-adjoint operator](wikipedia://en/187408) or [unitary operator](wikipedia://en/225999) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) is conjugate by a unitary operator to a diagonal operator. The results are subsumed in later generalizations for bounded [normal operators](wikipedia://en/190167) due to David Berg (1971, compact perturbation) and [Dan-Virgil Voiculescu](wikipedia://en/7656821) (1979, Hilbert–Schmidt perturbation). The theorem and its generalizations were one of the starting points of operator [K-homology](wikipedia://en/3021282), developed first by [Lawrence G. Brown](wikipedia://en/48948022), [Ronald Douglas](wikipedia://en/14044625) and [Peter Fillmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Fillmore) and, in greater generality, by [Gennadi Kasparov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennadi_Kasparov).

:::

In 1958 Kuroda showed that the Weyl–von Neumann theorem is also true if the Hilbert–Schmidt class is replaced by any [Schatten class](wikipedia://en/10698304) *S*<sub>*p*</sub> with *p* ≠ 1. For *S*<sub>1</sub>, the [trace-class operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace-class_operator), the situation is quite different. The **Kato–Rosenblum theorem**, proved in 1957 using [scattering theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_theory), states that if two bounded self-adjoint operators differ by a trace-class operator, then their [absolutely continuous parts](wikipedia://en/976834#Decomposing_the_spectrum) are unitarily equivalent. In particular if a self-adjoint operator has absolutely continuous spectrum, no perturbation of it by a trace-class operator can be unitarily equivalent to a diagonal operator.
