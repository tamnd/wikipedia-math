# Joseph Madachy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph Madachy"}

**Joseph Steven Madachy** (March 16, 1927 – March 27, 2014) was a research chemist, technical editor and recreational mathematician. He was the lead editor of *[Journal of Recreational Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1118492)*[^o3] for nearly 30 years and then served as editor emeritus. He was owner, publisher and editor of its predecessor, *Recreational Mathematics Magazine,* which appeared from 1961 to 1964.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Madachy was born in Star Junction, Pennsylvania, to Steven and Anne Madachy.[^o5] He was raised in Cleveland, Ohio.[^o5] He became interested in recreational mathematics after reading [Eugene Northrop](wikipedia://en/56167324)'s 1944 book, *Riddles in Mathematics*.[^o9] After service in World War II, he attended [Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Reserve_University) on the [G.I. Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.I._Bill) and earned a bachelor's and a master's in Chemistry.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Madachy moved to [Dayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Ohio), Ohio, and worked for [Mound Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mound_Laboratories).[^o5] He made original contributions to the field of recreational mathematics.[^o7] In 1960 he wrote to recreational mathematician [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435), asking whether Gardner knew of any publications devoted solely to recreational mathematics, as he was considering starting such a project.[^o8] Gardner responded in the negative, including a box containing his correspondence and suggesting Madachy could use the addresses to promote the magazine.[^o8] From February 1961 to 1964 Madachy published the bimonthly *Recreational Mathematics Magazine*.[^o5][^o9]

*Image: Joseph Madachy - Mathematics on Vacation.jpg.* Mathematics on Vacation

In 1967, [Greenwood Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwood_Press) asked him to start the journal again under the title *Journal of Recreational Mathematics,* which was published by [Baywood Publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baywood_Publishing) starting in 1973. He authored several books on recreational mathematics, including *Mathematics on Vacation* (1966),[^o10][^o11][^o12] *Madachy's Mathematical Recreations*[^o13] and *Mathematical Diversions.*[^o14] He served as the literary agent for [Dmitri Borgmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Borgmann)'s *[Language on Vacation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_on_Vacation)*.[^o15] Longtime colleagues and co-authors include [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435), [Harry L. Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_L._Nelson), and [Isaac Asimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Asimov), and [Solomon Golomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Golomb) (with [pentominos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentominos)).

He worked with [polyominoes](wikipedia://en/72634),[^o8] pentominos,[^o16] [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666),[^o17][^o18] and [amicable numbers](wikipedia://en/3259). He worked developing mathematical concepts such as [cryptarithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptarithmetic), used in cyber security applications.[^o19] He made contributions to Fibonacci series and [narcissistic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcissistic_number)[^o20][^o21][^o22] and devised puzzles using [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number).[^o23] His recreational mathematics work included areas in chess, [magic squares](wikipedia://en/52202)[^o24] and calculator art.[^o25]

Madachy retired from editing *Journal of Recreational Mathematics* in 2000.[^o8]p. 272

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

Madachy is mentioned in the [Jack Reacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Reacher) novel series in the book *[Never Go Back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Never_Go_Back_%28Lee_Child_novel%29),* which uses [perfect digit-to-digit invariant](wikipedia://en/24356530) numbers in the plot: "Such numbers had been much discussed by a guy called Joseph Madachy, who once upon a time had been the owner, publisher, and editor of a magazine called Journal of Recreational Mathematics."

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Madachy and his wife, Juliana, lived in Dayton, Ohio and had six children.[^o5]
