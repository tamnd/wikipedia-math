# HOMFLY polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="HOMFLY polynomial"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **HOMFLY polynomial** or **HOMFLYPT polynomial**, sometimes called the generalized [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642), is a 2-variable [knot polynomial](wikipedia://en/313735), i.e. a [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) in the form of a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) of variables *m* and *l*.

:::

A central question in the [mathematical theory of knots](wikipedia://en/153008) is whether two [knot diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_diagram) represent the same knot. One tool used to answer such questions is a knot polynomial, which is computed from a diagram of the knot and can be shown to be an [invariant of the knot](wikipedia://en/166898), i.e. diagrams representing the same knot have the same [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000). The converse may not be true. The HOMFLY polynomial is one such invariant and it generalizes two polynomials previously discovered, the [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) and the [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642), both of which can be obtained by appropriate substitutions from HOMFLY. The HOMFLY polynomial is also a [quantum invariant](wikipedia://en/22624170).

The name *HOMFLY* combines the initials of its co-discoverers: [Jim Hoste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Hoste), [Adrian Ocneanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Ocneanu), [Kenneth Millett](wikipedia://en/45353475), [Peter J. Freyd](wikipedia://en/4593361), [W. B. R. Lickorish](wikipedia://en/11177096), and David N. Yetter.[^o2] The addition of *PT* recognizes independent work carried out by [Józef H. Przytycki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_H._Przytycki) and Paweł Traczyk.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The polynomial is defined using [skein relations](wikipedia://en/314902):

$$P( \mathrm{unknot} ) = 1,\,$$

$$\ell P(L_+) + \ell^{-1}P(L_-) + mP(L_0)=0,\,$$

where $L_+, L_-, L_0$ are links formed by crossing and smoothing changes on a local region of a link diagram, as indicated in the figure.

*Image: Skein (HOMFLY).svg.*

The HOMFLY polynomial of a link *L* that is a split union of two links $L_1$ and $L_2$ is given by

$$P(L) = \frac{-(\ell+\ell^{-1})}{m} P(L_1)P(L_2).$$

See the page on [skein relation](wikipedia://en/314902) for an example of a computation using such relations.

:::

## Other HOMFLY skein relations {#Other_HOMFLY_skein_relations}

This polynomial can be obtained also using other skein relations:

$$\alpha P(L_+) - \alpha^{-1}P(L_-) = zP(L_0),\,$$

$$xP(L_+) + yP(L_-) + zP(L_0)=0,\,$$

## Main properties {#Main_properties}

$P(L_1 \# L_2)=P(L_1)P(L_2),\,$, where # denotes the [knot sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_sum). In other words, the HOMFLY polynomial of a [composite knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_knot) is the product of the HOMFLY polynomials of its components.

$P_K(\ell,m)=P_{\text{Mirror Image}(K)}(\ell^{-1},m)$, so the HOMFLY polynomial can often be used to distinguish between two knots of different [chirality](wikipedia://en/32703814). However there exist chiral pairs of knots that have the same HOMFLY polynomial, e.g. knots 9<sub>42</sub> and 10<sub>71</sub> together with their respective mirror images.[^o8]

The Jones polynomial, *V*(*t*), and the Alexander polynomial, $\Delta(t)\,$ can be computed in terms of the HOMFLY polynomial (the version in $\alpha$ and $z$ variables) as follows:

$$V(t)=P(\alpha=t^{-1},z=t^{1/2}-t^{-1/2}),\,$$

$$\Delta(t)=P(\alpha=1,z=t^{1/2}-t^{-1/2}),\,$$
