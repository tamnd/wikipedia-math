# Baden Powell (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Baden Powell"}

**Baden Powell**, [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Oxbridge%29) [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) [FRGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Geographical_Society) (22 August 1796 – 11 June 1860)[^o3] was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England) priest. He held the [Savilian Chair of Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savilian_Professor_of_Geometry) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) from 1827 to 1860. Powell was a prominent [liberal theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Christianity) who put forward advanced ideas about [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution).

:::

## Origins {#Origins}

Baden Powell II was born at [Stamford Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Hill), [Hackney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hackney,_London) in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London).[^o5] His father, Baden Powell I (1767–1841), of Langton and Speldhurst in Kent, was a wine merchant,[^o5] who served as [High Sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Sheriff) of [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent) in 1831, and as Master of the [Worshipful Company of Mercers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worshipful_Company_of_Mercers) in 1822.[^o6] The mother of Baden Powell II was Hester Powell (1776–1848), his father's paternal first cousin,[^o5][^o9] a daughter of James Powell (1737–1824) of Clapton, Hackney, Middlesex, Master of the [Worshipful Company of Salters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worshipful_Company_of_Salters) in 1818.[^o7]

The Powell family can be traced back to the early 16th century, where they were yeomen farmers at [Mildenhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mildenhall,_Suffolk) in Suffolk.[^o7] Baden Powell's eleventh great-grandfather, David Powell (1725–1810) of Homerton, Middlesex,[^o7] a second son, migrated to the [City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London) aged 17 in 1712, subsequently going into business as a merchant at [Old Broad Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Street_%28ward%29) and buying the manor of [Wattisfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wattisfield) in Suffolk.[^o8][^o9] In 1740 a branch of the family bought the [Whitefriars Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitefriars_Glass) works.[^o10]

The name Baden originated in Susanna Baden (1663–1737), the maternal grandmother of David Powell (1725–1810) of Homerton, Middlesex,[^o7] and one of the ten children of Andrew Baden (1637–1716), a Mercer who served as Mayor of Salisbury in 1682.[^o11]

## Education {#Education}

Powell was admitted as an undergraduate at [Oriel College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriel_College,_Oxford) in 1814, and graduated with a first-class honours degree in mathematics in 1817.[^o5]

## Ordination {#Ordination}

Powell was ordained as a priest of the Church of England in 1821, having served as curate of [Midhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midhurst), [Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex).[^o5] His first living was as Vicar of [Plumstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plumstead), Kent, of which the [advowson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advowson) was owned by his family.[^o5] He immediately began his scientific work there, starting with experiments on radiant heat.[^o5]

Shortly after Powell's death in 1860, his wife renamed the remaining children of his third marriage 'Baden-Powell'; the name was eventually legally changed by royal licence on 30 April 1902.[^o18] Baden Henry Powell is often also referred to as Baden Henry Baden-Powell,[^o19] and was using this name by the 1891 census.

## Evolution {#Evolution}

Powell was an outspoken advocate of the constant uniformity of the laws of the material world. His views were liberal, and he was sympathetic to [evolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary) theory long before [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin) had revealed his ideas. He argued that science should not be placed next to scripture or the two approaches would conflict, and in his own version of [Francis Bacon](wikipedia://en/11319)'s dictum, contended that the book of God's works was separate from the book of God's word, claiming that moral and physical phenomena were completely independent.[^o21]

His faith in the [uniformity of nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformitarianism) (except man's mind) was set out in a [theological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology) argument; if God is a lawgiver, then a "miracle" would break the lawful edicts that had been issued at Creation. Therefore, a belief in miracles would be entirely atheistic.[^o22] Powell's most significant works defended, in succession, the [uniformitarian geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformitarianism_%28science%29) set out by [Charles Lyell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Lyell) and the evolutionary ideas in *[Vestiges of the Natural History of Creation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestiges_of_the_Natural_History_of_Creation)* published anonymously by [Robert Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Chambers_%28journalist%29) which applied uniform laws to the history of life in contrast to more respectable ideas such as [catastrophism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catastrophism) involving a series of divine creations.[^o21] "He insisted that no tortured interpretation of [Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis) would ever suffice; we had to let go of the [Days of Creation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genesis_creation_myth) and base [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) on the moral laws of the [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament)."[^o23]

The boldness of Powell and other theologians in dealing with science led [Joseph Dalton Hooker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Dalton_Hooker) to comment in a letter to [Asa Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asa_Gray) dated 29 March 1857: "These parsons are so in the habit of dealing with the abstractions of doctrines as if there was no difficulty about them whatever, so confident, from the practice of having the talk all to themselves for an hour at least every week with no one to gainsay a syllable they utter, be it ever so loose or bad, that they gallop over the course when their field is Botany or Geology as if we were in the pews and they in the pulpit. Witness the self-confident style of Whewell and Baden Powell, Sedgwick and Buckland." [William Whewell](wikipedia://en/205488), [Adam Sedgwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Sedgwick) and [William Buckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Buckland) opposed evolutionary ideas.[^o24]

When the idea of [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection) was opened to debate by Charles Darwin and [Alfred Russel Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Russel_Wallace) in their 1858 papers to the [Linnaean Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linnaean_Society), both Powell and his brother-in-law[^o25] [William Henry Flower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Flower) thought that natural selection made creation rational.[^o26]

The 'Philosophy of Creation' has been treated in a masterly manner by the Rev. Baden Powell, in his 'Essays on the Unity of Worlds,' 1855. Nothing can be more striking than the manner in which he shows that the introduction of new species is "a regular, not a casual phenomenon," or, as Sir John Herschel expresses it, "a natural in contra-distinction to a miraculous process."[^o27]

## *Essays and Reviews* {#Essays_and_Reviews}

He was one of seven liberal theologians who produced a manifesto titled *[Essays and Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essays_and_Reviews)* around February 1860, which amongst other things joined in the debate over *[On the Origin of Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Origin_of_Species)*. These [Anglicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) included Oxford professors, country clergymen, the headmaster of [Rugby school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_school) and a layman. Their declaration that miracles were irrational stirred up unprecedented anger, drawing much of the fire away from Charles Darwin. *Essays* sold 22,000 copies in two years, more than the *Origin* sold in twenty years, and sparked five years of increasingly polarised debate with books and pamphlets furiously contesting the issues.[^o22]

Referring to "Mr Darwin's masterly volume" and restating his argument that belief in miracles is atheistic, Baden Powell wrote that the book "must soon bring about an entire revolution in opinion in favour of the grand principle of the self-evolving powers of nature.": Just a similar scepticism has been evinced by nearly all the first physiologists of the day, who have joined in rejecting the development theories of Lamarck and the Vestiges; and while they have strenuously maintained successive creations, have denied strenuously maintained successive creations, have denied and denounced the alleged production of organic life by Messrs. Crosse and Weekes, and stoutly maintained the impossibility of spontaneious generation, on the alleged ground of contradiction to experience. Yet it is now acknowledged under the high sanction of the name of Owen (British Association Address 1858), that 'creation' is only another name for our ignorance of the mode of production; and it has been the unanswered and unanswerable argument of another reasoner that new species must have originated either out of their inorganic elements, or out of previously organized forms; either development or spontaneous generation must be true: while a work has now appeared by a naturalist of the most acknowledged authority, Mr. Darwin's masterly volume on The Origin of Species by the law of 'natural selection' – which now substantiates on undeniable grounds the very principle so long denounced by the first naturalist – the origination of new species by natural causes: a work which must soon bring about an entire revolution of opinion in favour of the grand principle of the self-evolving powers of nature.[^o29]

He would have been on the platform at the [British Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) [1860 Oxford evolution debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1860_Oxford_evolution_debate) that was a highlight of the [reaction to Darwin's theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_to_Darwin's_theory). [Huxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Henry_Huxley)'s antagonist [Wilberforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Wilberforce) was also the foremost critic of *Essays and Reviews*. Powell died of a heart attack a fortnight before the meeting.[^o22] He is buried in [Kensal Green Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensal_Green_Cemetery), London.
