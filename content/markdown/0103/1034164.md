# Nigel Hitchin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nigel Hitchin"}

**Nigel James Hitchin** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (born 2 August 1946) is a British mathematician working in the fields of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/64324611), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). He is a Professor Emeritus of Mathematics at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Hitchin attended [Ecclesbourne School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesbourne_School), [Duffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duffield,_Derbyshire), and earned his [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in mathematics from [Jesus College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Oxford), in 1968.[^o5] After moving to [Wolfson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfson_College,_Oxford), he received his [D.Phil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1972. From 1971 to 1973 he visited the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) and 1973/74 the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University). He then was a research fellow in Oxford and starting in 1979 tutor, lecturer and fellow of [St Catherine's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Catherine's_College,_Oxford). In 1990 he became a professor at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) and in 1994 the [Rouse Ball Professor of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/649081) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). In 1997 he was appointed to the [Savilian Chair of Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savilian_Professor_of_Geometry) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), a position he held until his retirement in 2016.

Amongst his notable discoveries are the [Hitchin&ndash;Thorpe inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchin&ndash;Thorpe_inequality); Hitchin's [projectively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_differential_geometry) [flat connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_connection) over [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661); the Atiyah–Hitchin monopole metric; the [Atiyah–Hitchin–Singer theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiyah–Hitchin–Singer_theorem); the [ADHM construction](wikipedia://en/5165589) of [instantons](wikipedia://en/296077) (of [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698), [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747), Hitchin, and [Yuri Manin](wikipedia://en/1692086)); the [hyperkähler quotient](wikipedia://en/35171991) (of Hitchin, Anders Karlhede, [Ulf Lindström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulf_Lindström) and [Martin Roček](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Roček)); [Higgs bundles](wikipedia://en/34143255), which arise as solutions to the Hitchin equations, a 2-dimensional reduction of the self-dual [Yang–Mills equations](wikipedia://en/672202); and the [Hitchin system](wikipedia://en/23652892), an algebraically [completely integrable Hamiltonian system](wikipedia://en/2573213) associated to the data of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) and a complex [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386). He and [Shoshichi Kobayashi](wikipedia://en/20695112) independently conjectured the [Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence](wikipedia://en/37856668). Higgs bundles, which are also developed in the work of [Carlos Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Simpson), are closely related to the Hitchin system, which has an interpretation as a [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) of semistable Higgs bundles over a compact [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) or algebraic curve.[^o6] This moduli space has emerged as a focal point for deep connections between algebraic geometry, differential geometry, [hyperkähler geometry](wikipedia://en/646120), mathematical physics, and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).

In his article[^o7] on generalized [Calabi–Yau manifolds](wikipedia://en/188932), he introduced the notion of [generalized complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/6871218), providing a single structure that incorporates, as examples, [Poisson manifolds](wikipedia://en/882902), [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356) and [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742). These have found wide applications as the geometries of [flux compactifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactification_%28physics%29#Flux_compactification) in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) and also in [topological string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_string_theory).

In the span of his career, Hitchin has supervised 37 research students, including [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305) (part-supervised with Atiyah).

Until 2013 Nigel Hitchin served as the managing editor of the journal *[Mathematische Annalen](wikipedia://en/1423381)*.

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

In 1991 he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society).[^o9]

In 2003 he was awarded an Honorary Degree (Doctor of Science) from the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath).

Hitchin was elected as an [Honorary Fellow of Jesus College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Honorary_Fellows_of_Jesus_College,_Oxford) in 1998,[^o5] and the [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prizes) (1990), the [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal) (2000) and the [Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya_Prize_%28LMS%29) (2002) have been awarded to him in honour of his far-reaching work. A conference was held in honour of his 60th birthday, in conjunction with the 2006 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Spain.

In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o10] In 2014 he was awarded another Honorary Degree (Doctor of Science) from the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick). In 2016 he received the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) in Mathematical Sciences.[^o11]
