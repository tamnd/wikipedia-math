# Kappa curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kappa curve"}

*Image: Kappa curve with asymptotes - by Pt.png.* The kappa curve has two vertical [asymptotes](wikipedia://en/3107)

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **kappa curve** or **Gutschoven's curve** is a [quartic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_curve) plane [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) resembling the [Greek letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet) [$\varkappa$ (kappa)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kappa_%28letter%29). The kappa curve was first studied by [Gérard van Gutschoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gérard_van_Gutschoven) around 1662. In the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), it is remembered as one of the first examples of [Isaac Barrow](wikipedia://en/97805)'s application of rudimentary calculus methods to determine the [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) of a curve. [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) and [Johann Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/268886) continued the studies of this curve subsequently.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Using the [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706) it can be expressed as

$$x^2\left(x^2 + y^2\right) = a^2y^2$$

or, using [parametric equations](wikipedia://en/576108),

$$\begin{aligned} x &= a\sin t,\\ y &= a\sin t\tan t. \end{aligned}$$

In [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates) its equation is even simpler:

$$r = a\tan\theta.$$

It has two vertical [asymptotes](wikipedia://en/3107) at $x = \pm a$, shown as dashed blue lines in the figure at right.

The kappa curve's [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770):

$$\kappa(\theta) = \frac{8\left(3 - \sin^2\theta\right)\sin^4\theta}{a \left(\sin^2(2\theta) + 4\right)^\frac32}.$$

[Tangential](wikipedia://en/31482) angle:

$$\phi(\theta) = -\arctan\left(\tfrac12 \sin(2\theta)\right).$$

:::

## Tangents via infinitesimals {#Tangents_via_infinitesimals}

The tangent lines of the kappa curve can also be determined geometrically using [differentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_%28infinitesimal%29) and the elementary rules of [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) arithmetic. Suppose $x$ and $y$ are variables, while a is taken to be a constant. From the definition of the kappa curve,

$$x^2\left(x^2 + y^2\right)-a^2y^2 = 0$$

Now, an infinitesimal change in our location must also change the value of the left hand side, so

$$d \left(x^2\left(x^2 + y^2\right)-a^2y^2\right) = 0$$

Distributing the differential and applying [appropriate rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_%28calculus%29),

$$\begin{aligned} d \left(x^2\left(x^2 + y^2\right)\right)-d \left(a^2y^2\right) &= 0 \\[6px] (2 x\,dx ) \left(x^2+y^2\right) + x^2 (2x\,dx + 2y\,dy) - a^2 2y\,dy &= 0 \\[6px] \left( 4 x^3 + 2 x y^2\right) dx + \left( 2 y x^2 - 2 a^2 y \right) dy &= 0 \\[6px] x \left( 2 x^2 + y^2 \right) dx + y \left(x^2 - a^2\right) dy &= 0 \\[6px] \frac{ x \left( 2 x^2 + y^2 \right) }{ y \left(a^2 - x^2\right)} &= \frac{dy}{dx} \end{aligned}$$

## Derivative {#Derivative}

If we use the modern concept of a functional relationship $y(x)$ and apply [implicit differentiation](wikipedia://en/470397), the slope of a tangent line to the kappa curve at a point $(x,y)$ is:

$$\begin{aligned} 2 x \left( x^2 + y^2 \right) + x^2 \left( 2x + 2 y \frac{dy}{dx} \right) &= 2 a^2 y \frac{dy}{dx} \\[6px] 2 x^3 + 2 x y^2 + 2 x^3 &= 2 a^2 y \frac{dy}{dx} - 2 x^2 y \frac{dy}{dx} \\[6px] 4 x^3 + 2 x y^2 &= \left(2 a^2 y - 2 x^2 y \right) \frac{dy}{dx} \\[6px] \frac{2 x^3 + x y^2}{a^2 y - x^2 y} &= \frac{dy}{dx} \end{aligned}$$
