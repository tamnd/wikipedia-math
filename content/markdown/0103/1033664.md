# Morava K-theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Morava K-theory"}

In [stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/2946655), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Morava K-theory** is one of a collection of [cohomology theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theories) introduced in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) by [Jack Morava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Morava) in unpublished preprints in the early 1970s. For every [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p* (which is suppressed in the notation), it consists of theories *K*(*n*) for each nonnegative integer *n*, each a [ring spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_spectrum_%28homotopy_theory%29) in the sense of [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831). (Johnson Wilson 1975) published the first account of the theories.

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The theory *K*(0) agrees with [singular homology](wikipedia://en/431041) with rational coefficients, whereas *K*(1) is a summand of mod-*p* [complex K-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_K-theory). The theory *K*(*n*) has coefficient ring[^o4]

**F**<sub>*p*</sub>\[*v*<sub>*n*</sub>,*v*<sub>*n*</sub><sup>−1</sup>\]

where *v*<sub>*n*</sub> has degree 2(*p*<sup>*n*</sup> − 1). In particular, Morava K-theory is periodic with this period, in much the same way that complex K-theory has period 2.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- They have [Künneth isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/918047) for arbitrary pairs of spaces: that is, for *X* and *Y* CW complexes, we have

$$K(n)_*(X \times Y) \cong K(n)_*(X) \otimes_{K(n)_*} K(n)_*(Y).$$

- For every prime number $p$ and every natural number $n\in\mathbb{N}$, Morava K-theory $K(n)$ is a "field" in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of [ring spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_spectrum_%28homotopy_theory%29), meaning the [graded ring](wikipedia://en/103094) $\pi_0K(n)$ is even a graded field, meaning every nonzero homogeneous element in it is invertible.[^o7] Therefore every [module spectrum](wikipedia://en/41276205) over $K(n)$ is free, meaning it's a [wedge](wikipedia://en/684143) of [suspensions](wikipedia://en/1251702) of $K(n)$.
- They are [complex oriented](wikipedia://en/1033666) (at least after being periodified by taking the wedge sum of (*p*<sup>*n*</sup> − 1) shifted copies), and the [formal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_group) they define has [height](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_group#The_height_of_a_formal_group_law) *n*.
- Every finite *p*-local [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_%28homotopy_theory%29) *X* has the property that *K*(*n*)<sub>∗</sub>(*X*) = 0 if and only if *n* is less than a certain number *N*, called the [type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_%28homotopy_theory%29) of the spectrum *X*. By a theorem of Devinatz–[Hopkins](wikipedia://en/20652569)–Smith, every [thick subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thick_subcategory) of the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of finite *p*-local spectra is the subcategory of type-*n* spectra for some *n*.
- A connected [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) [spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_manifold) 10-manifold is generalized oriented in second integral Morava K-theory $\widetilde{K}(2)$ (for $p=2$) if and only if its seventh integral [Stiefel–Whitney class](wikipedia://en/877761) $W_7$ vanishes.

:::

## K(n)-local spheres {#K(n)-local_spheres}

For the [sphere spectrum](wikipedia://en/21118593) $\mathbb{S}$ (with $\mathbb{S}_n=S^n$), its Bousfield localizations $L_{K(n)}\mathbb{S}$ are also called $K(n)$-local spheres.
