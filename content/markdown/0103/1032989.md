# Kitarō Nishida

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Kitarō Nishida"}

**Kitarō Nishida** was a Japanese moral [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), philosopher of mathematics and science, and religious scholar. He was the founder of what has been called the [Kyoto School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_School) of philosophy. He graduated from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) during the [Meiji period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era) in 1894 with a degree in [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy). He was named [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of the Fourth Higher School in [Ishikawa Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishikawa_Prefecture) in 1899 and later became professor of philosophy at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University). Nishida retired in 1927. In 1940, he was awarded the [Order of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Culture) (文化勲章, *bunka kunshō*). He participated in establishing the [Chiba Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiba_Institute_of_Technology) (千葉工業大学) from 1940.

:::

Nishida Kitarō died at the age of 75 of a [renal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney) infection. His cremated remains were divided up and interred at different locations. Part of his remains are interred in the Nishida family grave in his birthplace of [Unoke, Ishikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unoke,_Ishikawa). A second grave can be found at [Tōkei-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tōkei-ji) Temple in [Kamakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura), where his friend [D. T. Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._T._Suzuki) organized Nishida's funeral and was later interred in the adjacent plot. Nishida's third grave is at Reiun'in (霊雲院, *Reiun'in*), a [temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple) in the [Myōshin-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myōshin-ji) compound in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto).[^o5]

## Philosophy {#Philosophy}

Being born in the third year of the [Meiji period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era), Nishida was presented with a new, unique opportunity to contemplate [Eastern philosophical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_philosophy) issues in the fresh light that [Western philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_philosophy) shone on them. Nishida's original and creative philosophy, incorporating ideas of [Zen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen) and Western philosophy, was aimed at bringing the East and West closer. Throughout his lifetime, Nishida published a number of books and essays including *[An Inquiry into the Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Inquiry_into_the_Good)* and *The Logic of the Place of Nothingness and the Religious Worldview*. Taken as a whole, Nishida's life work was the foundation for the [Kyoto School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_School) of philosophy and the inspiration for the original thinking of his disciples.

One of the most famous concepts in Nishida's philosophy is the [logic of *basho*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_of_basho) (Japanese: 場所; usually translated as "place" or "topos"), a non-dualistic concrete logic, meant to overcome the inadequacy of the subject-object distinction essential to the subject logic of [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308) and the predicate logic of [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant), through the affirmation of what he calls the "absolutely contradictory self-identity", a dynamic tension of opposites that, unlike the [dialectical logic](wikipedia://en/52813) of [G.W.F. Hegel](wikipedia://en/12598), does not resolve in a synthesis. Rather, it defines its proper subject by maintaining the tension between affirmation and negation as opposite poles or perspectives.

In David A. Dilworth's survey of Nishida's works, he did not mention the debut book, *[An Inquiry into the Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Inquiry_into_the_Good)*. There, Nishida writes about experience, reality, good and religion. He argues that the most profound form of experience is the pure experience. Nishida analyzes the thought, the will, the intellectual intuition, and the pure experience among them.[^o7] According to Nishida's vision as well as to the essence of Asian wisdom, one craves harmony in experience, for unity.[^o8]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

*Image: Nishida Kitarōs Grab in Kamakura.jpg.* Tomb of Nishida Kitarō in [Kamakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura,_Kanagawa)

According to Masao Abe, "During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) right-wing thinkers attacked him as antinationalistic for his appreciation of [Western philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_philosophy) and logic. But after the war left-wing thinkers criticized his philosophy as nationalistic because of his emphasis on the traditional notion of *[nothingness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothingness#Philosophy)*. He recognized a kind of universality in Western philosophy and logic but did not accept that it was the only universality."[^o10]

Nishida considered God "indispensable and decisive".[^o11]

Nishida breaks this history into four periods: \[1\] The Beginning ([Hobbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hobbes)); \[2\] The First Phase ([Cudworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Cudworth), [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Cumberland_%28philosopher%29), [Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke)); \[3\] The Second Phase ([Shaftesbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ashley-Cooper,_3rd_Earl_of_Shaftesbury), [Hutcheson](wikipedia://en/188405), [Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Butler), [Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hume), [Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Smith), [Hartley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hartley_%28philosopher%29)); and \[4\] The Third Phase (Intuition School: [Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Price), [Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Reid); Utilitarianism: [Paley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Paley), [Bentham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Bentham), [Mill](wikipedia://en/15626); Evolutional Ethics: [Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Spencer)).

Nishida's sources include [Sidgwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Sidgwick), *Outlines of the History of Ethics for English Readers* (1886); [Martineau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Martineau), *Types of Ethical Theory* (1885); and [James Mackintosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mackintosh), *Dissertation on the Progress of Ethical Philosophy: Chiefly During the Seventeenth and Eighteenth Centuries* (1836)
