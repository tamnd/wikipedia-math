# Abraham Robertson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Abraham Robertson"}

**Abraham** or **Abram Robertson** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (4 November 1751 – 4 December 1826), was a [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer). He held the [Savilian Chair of Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savilian_chair_of_geometry) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) from 1797 to 1809.

:::

Robertson was born at [Duns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duns,_Scottish_Borders), [Berwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwickshire), the son of Abraham Robertson, “a man of humble station”. He attended school at Great Ryle in [Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland), and later at Duns. At age 24, he moved to [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London), he had hopes of travelling to the [East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indies), but his patron died. During the 1770s, Robertson completed a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) and Master in Arts.

In 1795, the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) elected him a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in recognition of his work on conic sections.

In 1792, Robertson published *Sectionum conicarum libri septem*.
