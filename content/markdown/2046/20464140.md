# Vladimir Boltyansky

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vladimir Boltyansky"}

**Vladimir Grigorevich Boltyansky** (Влади́мир Григо́рьевич Болтя́нский; 26 April 1925 – 16 April 2019),[^o3] also transliterated as **Boltyanski**, **Boltyanskii**, or **Boltjansky**, was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [educator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educator) and author of popular mathematical books and articles. He was best known for his books on [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [combinatorial geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_geometry) and [Hilbert's third problem](wikipedia://en/152703).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Boltyansky was born in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow).[^o5] He served in the [Soviet army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_army) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), when he was a [signaller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signaller) on the [2nd Belorussian Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Belorussian_Front).[^o6] He graduated from [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_University) in 1948, where his advisor was [Lev Pontryagin](wikipedia://en/582075). He defended his "[Doktor nauk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doktor_nauk) in physics and mathematics" ([higher doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_doctorate)) degree in 1955, became a professor in 1959.

Boltyansky was awarded the [Lenin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin_Prize) (for the work led by Pontryagin, [Revaz Gamkrelidze](wikipedia://en/32712098), and Evgenii Mishchenko) for applications of [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations) to [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565), where he was one of the discoverers of the [Pontryagin's maximum principle](wikipedia://en/831689).[^o7] In 1967 he received [Uzbek SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) prize for the work on [ordered rings](wikipedia://en/525697). He taught at [CIMAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIMAT).[^o8]

He was the corresponding member of the [Russian Academy of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Education). He was the author of over 200 books and mathematical articles.
