# Quadratic Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quadratic Lie algebra"}

A **quadratic Lie algebra** is a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) together with a compatible [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375). Compatibility means that it is invariant under the [adjoint representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_algebra). Examples of such are [semisimple Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1337587), such as [su(*n*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%28N%29#Lie_algebra) and [sl(*n*,**R**)](wikipedia://en/144823#Lie_subgroup).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A quadratic Lie algebra is a Lie algebra (**g**,\[.,.\]) together with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form $(.,.)\colon \mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to \mathbb{R}$ that is invariant under the adjoint action, i.e.

(\[*X*,*Y*\],*Z*)+(*Y*,\[*X*,*Z*\])=0

where *X,Y,Z* are elements of the Lie algebra **g**. A localization/ generalization is the concept of [Courant algebroid](wikipedia://en/10109649) where the vector space **g** is replaced by (sections of) a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524).

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

As a first example, consider *R*<sup>n</sup> with zero-bracket and standard inner product

$$((x_1,\dots,x_n),(y_1,\dots,y_n)):= \sum_j x_jy_j.$$

Since the bracket is trivial the invariance is trivially fulfilled.

As a more elaborate example consider [so(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SO%283%29#Lie_algebra), i.e. *R*<sup>3</sup> with base *X,Y,Z*, standard inner product, and Lie bracket

$$[X,Y]=Z,\quad [Y,Z]=X,\quad [Z,X]=Y.$$

Straightforward computation shows that the inner product is indeed preserved. A generalization is the following.

### Semisimple Lie algebras {#Semisimple_Lie_algebras}

A big group of examples fits into the category of semisimple Lie algebras, i.e. Lie algebras whose adjoint representation is faithful. Examples are [sl(n,R)](wikipedia://en/144823#Lie_subgroup) and [su(n)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%28N%29#Lie_algebra), as well as [direct sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_Lie_algebras) of them. Let thus **g** be a semi-simple Lie algebra with adjoint representation *ad*, i.e.

$$\mathrm{ad}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}):X\mapsto (\mathrm{ad}_X\colon Y\mapsto [X,Y]).$$

Define now the [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386)

$$k\colon\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}: X\otimes Y \mapsto -\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}_X\circ\mathrm{ad}_Y).$$

Due to the [Cartan criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan_criterion), the Killing form is non-degenerate if and only if the Lie algebra is semisimple.

If **g** is in addition a [simple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/59500649), then the Killing form is up to rescaling the only invariant symmetric bilinear form.
