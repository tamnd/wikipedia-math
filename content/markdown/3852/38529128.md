# Philip Candelas

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Philip Candelas"}

**Philip Candelas**, [^o14] (born 24 October 1951 in London, UK)[^o3][^o5] is a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people) [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o12][^o6] After 20 years at the University of Texas at Austin, he served as [Rouse Ball Professor of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/649081) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) until 2020 and is a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of [Wadham College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadham_College,_Oxford).[^o3]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Candelas was educated at [Christ's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_College,_Cambridge) and [Wadham College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadham_College,_Oxford), where he was a student of [Dennis Sciama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Sciama), from 1972, receiving his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in 1973.[^o3] From 1975 he was a research fellow at [Balliol College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford), and in 1976-77 was at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) with [John Archibald Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Archibald_Wheeler). In 1977 he received his [DPhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) from Oxford for research on [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312) supervised by [Dennis Sciama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Sciama), Derek J. Raine and M. R. Brown.[^o9]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

After his DPhil, Candelas continued at the University of Texas, where he became an assistant professor in 1977, associate professor in 1983, and full professor in 1989.

He was at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 1993 to 1994, a visiting scientist at [CERN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERN) from 1991 to 1993 and a visiting professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1995. He was the [Rouse Ball Professor of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/649081) at Oxford from 1999 to 2020 and also the Head of the Mathematical Physics Group at Oxford.[^o5]

Candelas is most known for his 1985 work with [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894), [Andrew Strominger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Strominger), and [Gary Horowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Horowitz) in which they introduced [compactification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactification_%28physics%29) to string theory using [Calabi–Yau manifolds](wikipedia://en/188932).[^o11]

Candelas is also notable for his contributions in the field of [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) (QFT) especially the renormalisation of QFT near [black holes](wikipedia://en/4650). He also contributed to the understanding of the behaviour of quantum fields near boundaries, with applications to the [Casimir effect](wikipedia://en/7555) and quark confinement.[^o12][^o14]

### Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Candelas was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) in 2010.[^o14]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Candelas has both British and United States citizenship. He is married to mathematics professor [Xenia de la Ossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenia_de_la_Ossa) and has two daughters.[^o3][^o5]
