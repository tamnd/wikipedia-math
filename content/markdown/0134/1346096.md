# Transfer operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Transfer operator"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **transfer operator** encodes information about an [iterated map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iterated_map) and is frequently used to study the behavior of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), [quantum chaos](wikipedia://en/313779) and [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractals). In all usual cases, the largest eigenvalue is 1, and the corresponding eigenvector is the [invariant measure](wikipedia://en/7635201) of the system.

:::

The transfer operator is sometimes called the **Ruelle operator**, after [David Ruelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ruelle), or the **Perron–Frobenius operator** or **Ruelle–Perron–Frobenius operator**, in reference to the applicability of the [Perron–Frobenius theorem](wikipedia://en/1540333) to the determination of the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the operator.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The iterated function to be studied is a map $f\colon X\rightarrow X$ for an arbitrary set $X$.

The transfer operator is defined as an operator $\mathcal{L}$ acting on the space of functions $\{\Phi\colon X\rightarrow \mathbb{C}\}$ as

$$(\mathcal{L}\Phi)(x) = \sum_{y\,\in\, f^{-1}(x)} g(y) \Phi(y)$$

where $g\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}$ is an auxiliary valuation function. When $f$ has a [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) determinant $|J|$, then $g$ is usually taken to be $g=1/|J|$.

The above definition of the transfer operator can be shown to be the point-set limit of the measure-theoretic [pushforward](wikipedia://en/9412979) of *g*: in essence, the transfer operator is the [direct image functor](wikipedia://en/3022744) in the category of [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333). The left-adjoint of the Perron–Frobenius operator is the [Koopman operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koopman_operator) or [composition operator](wikipedia://en/2702039). The general setting is provided by the [Borel functional calculus](wikipedia://en/876294).

As a general rule, the transfer operator can usually be interpreted as a (left-)[shift operator](wikipedia://en/467384) acting on a [shift space](wikipedia://en/15470598). The most commonly studied shifts are the [subshifts of finite type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subshifts_of_finite_type). The adjoint to the transfer operator can likewise usually be interpreted as a right-shift. Particularly well studied right-shifts include the [Jacobi operator](wikipedia://en/28868152) and the [Hessenberg matrix](wikipedia://en/519204), both of which generate systems of [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) via a right-shift.

:::

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Whereas the iteration of a function $f$ naturally leads to a study of the orbits of points of X under iteration (the study of [point dynamics](wikipedia://en/6295)), the transfer operator defines how (smooth) maps evolve under iteration. Thus, transfer operators typically appear in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) problems, such as [quantum chaos](wikipedia://en/313779) and [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), where attention is focused on the time evolution of smooth functions. In turn, this has medical applications to [rational drug design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_drug_design), through the field of [molecular dynamics](wikipedia://en/198608).

It is often the case that the transfer operator is positive, has discrete positive real-valued [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue), with the largest eigenvalue being equal to one. For this reason, the transfer operator is sometimes called the Frobenius–Perron operator.

The [eigenfunctions](wikipedia://en/228601) of the transfer operator are usually fractals. When the logarithm of the transfer operator corresponds to a quantum [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_%28quantum_theory%29), the eigenvalues will typically be very closely spaced, and thus even a very narrow and carefully selected [ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_ensemble) of quantum states will encompass a large number of very different fractal eigenstates with non-zero [support](wikipedia://en/381013) over the entire volume. This can be used to explain many results from classical statistical mechanics, including the irreversibility of time and the increase of [entropy](wikipedia://en/9891).

The transfer operator of the [Bernoulli map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_map) $b(x)=2x-\lfloor 2x\rfloor$ is exactly solvable and is a classic example of [deterministic chaos](wikipedia://en/6295); the discrete eigenvalues correspond to the [Bernoulli polynomials](wikipedia://en/228161). This operator also has a continuous spectrum consisting of the [Hurwitz zeta function](wikipedia://en/406952).

The transfer operator of the Gauss map $h(x)=1/x-\lfloor 1/x \rfloor$ is called the [Gauss–Kuzmin–Wirsing (GKW) operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss&ndash;Kuzmin&ndash;Wirsing_operator). The theory of the GKW dates back to a hypothesis by Gauss on [continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199) and is closely related to the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).
