# Ihara zeta function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ihara zeta function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Ihara zeta function** is a [zeta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeta_function) associated with a finite [graph](wikipedia://en/325806). It closely resembles the [Selberg zeta function](wikipedia://en/3680650), and is used to relate closed walks to the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206136) of the [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463). The Ihara zeta function was first defined by [Yasutaka Ihara](wikipedia://en/37592148) in the 1960s in the context of [discrete subgroups](wikipedia://en/468536) of the two-by-two [p-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823). [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) suggested in his book *Trees* that Ihara's original definition can be reinterpreted graph-theoretically. It was [Toshikazu Sunada](wikipedia://en/22019166) who put this suggestion into practice in 1985. As observed by Sunada, a [regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) is a [Ramanujan graph](wikipedia://en/849412) if and only if its Ihara zeta function satisfies an analogue of the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Ihara zeta function is defined as the analytic continuation of the infinite product

$$\zeta_{G}(u)=\prod_{p}\frac{1}{1-u^{{L}(p)}},$$

where *L*(*p*) is the *length* $L(p)$ of $p$. The product in the definition is taken over all prime [closed geodesics](wikipedia://en/19903176) $p$ of the graph $G = (V, E)$, where geodesics which differ by a [cyclic rotation](wikipedia://en/1917276) are considered equal. A *closed geodesic* $p$ on $G$ (known in graph theory as a "[reduced closed walk](wikipedia://en/168609)"; it is not a graph geodesic) is a finite sequence of vertices $p = (v_0, \ldots, v_{k-1})$ such that

$$(v_i, v_{(i+1)\bmod k}) \in E,$$

$$v_i \neq v_{(i+2) \bmod k}.$$

The integer $k$ is the length $L(p)$. The closed geodesic $p$ is *prime* if it cannot be obtained by repeating a closed geodesic $m$ times, for an integer $m > 1$.

This graph-theoretic formulation is due to Sunada.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=named-heading name="Ihara's formula"}

## Ihara's formula {#Ihara's_formula}

Ihara (and Sunada in the graph-theoretic setting) showed that for regular graphs the zeta function is a rational function. If $G$ is a $q+1$-regular graph with [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) $A$ then[^o7]

$$\zeta_G(u) = \frac{1}{(1-u^2)^{r(G)-1}\det(I - Au + qu^2I)},$$

where $r(G)$ is the [circuit rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_rank) of $G$. If $G$ is connected and has $n$ vertices, $r(G)-1=(q-1)n/2$.

The Ihara zeta-function is in fact always the reciprocal of a [graph polynomial](wikipedia://en/56012962):

$$\zeta_G(u) = \frac{1}{\det (I-Tu)}~,$$

where $T$ is Ki-ichiro Hashimoto's edge adjacency operator. [Hyman Bass](wikipedia://en/7085910) gave a determinant formula involving the adjacency operator.

:::

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Ihara zeta function plays an important role in the study of [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735), [spectral graph theory](wikipedia://en/534914), and [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), especially [symbolic dynamics](wikipedia://en/1986263), where the Ihara zeta function is an example of a [Ruelle zeta function](wikipedia://en/37041839).[^o10]

:::
