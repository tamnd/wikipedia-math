# Bell series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bell series"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Bell series** is a [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) used to study properties of arithmetical functions. Bell series were introduced and developed by [Eric Temple Bell](wikipedia://en/255236).

:::

Given an [arithmetic function](wikipedia://en/3170) $f$ and a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$, define the formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) $f_p(x)$, called the Bell series of $f$ modulo $p$ as:

$$f_p(x)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty f(p^n)x^n.$$

Two [multiplicative functions](wikipedia://en/20059) can be shown to be identical if all of their Bell series are equal; this is sometimes called the *uniqueness theorem*: given multiplicative functions $f$ and $g$, one has $f=g$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922):

$f_p(x)=g_p(x)$ for all primes $p$.

Two series may be multiplied (sometimes called the *multiplication theorem*): For any two [arithmetic functions](wikipedia://en/3170) $f$ and $g$, let $h=f*g$ be their [Dirichlet convolution](wikipedia://en/179017). Then for every prime $p$, one has:

$$h_p(x)=f_p(x) g_p(x).\,$$

In particular, this makes it trivial to find the Bell series of a [Dirichlet inverse](wikipedia://en/179017).

If $f$ is [completely multiplicative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_multiplicative), then formally:

$$f_p(x)=\frac{1}{1-f(p)x}.$$

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The following is a table of the Bell series of well-known arithmetic functions.

- The [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961) $\mu$ has $\mu_p(x)=1-x.$
- The [Mobius function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobius_function) squared has $\mu_p^2(x) = 1+x.$
- [Euler's totient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler's_phi_function) $\varphi$ has $\varphi_p(x)=\frac{1-x}{1-px}.$
- The multiplicative identity of the [Dirichlet convolution](wikipedia://en/179017) $\delta$ has $\delta_p(x)=1.$
- The [Liouville function](wikipedia://en/104463) $\lambda$ has $\lambda_p(x)=\frac{1}{1+x}.$
- The power function Id<sub>*k*</sub> has $(\textrm{Id}_k)_p(x)=\frac{1}{1-p^kx}.$ Here, Id<sub>*k*</sub> is the completely multiplicative function $\operatorname{Id}_k(n)=n^k$.
- The [divisor function](wikipedia://en/407249) $\sigma_k$ has $(\sigma_k)_p(x)=\frac{1}{(1-p^kx)(1-x)}.$
- The [constant function](wikipedia://en/320861), with value 1, satisfies $1_p(x) = (1-x)^{-1}$, i.e., is the [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630).
- If $f(n) = 2^{\omega(n)} = \sum_{d|n} \mu^2(d)$ is the power of the [prime omega function](wikipedia://en/57197043), then $f_p(x) = \frac{1+x}{1-x}.$
- Suppose that *f* is multiplicative and *g* is any [arithmetic function](wikipedia://en/3170) satisfying $f(p^{n+1}) = f(p) f(p^n) - g(p) f(p^{n-1})$ for all primes *p* and $n \geq 1$. Then $f_p(x) = \left(1-f(p)x + g(p)x^2\right)^{-1}.$
- If $\mu_k(n) = \sum_{d^k|n} \mu_{k-1}\left(\frac{n}{d^k}\right) \mu_{k-1}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)$ denotes the [Möbius function of order *k*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Möbius_function_of_order_k), then $(\mu_k)_p(x) = \frac{1-2x^k+x^{k+1}}{1-x}.$
