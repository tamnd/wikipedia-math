# P wave

*Image: Onde compression impulsion 1d 30 petit.gif.* Plane P wave

*Image: Ondes compression 2d 20 petit.gif.* Representation of the propagation of a P wave on a 2D grid (empirical shape)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="P wave"}

In [continuum mechanics](wikipedia://en/5918), a **P wave** (**primary wave** or **pressure wave**) is one of the two main types of elastic [body waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_wave_%28seismology%29) or [seismic waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismic_waves). P waves travel faster than other seismic waves and hence are the first signal from an earthquake to arrive at any affected location or at a [seismograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismograph). P waves may be transmitted through gases, liquids, or solids.

:::

## Nomenclature {#Nomenclature}

The name *P wave* can stand for either **pressure wave** (as it is formed from alternating [compressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_%28physical%29) and [rarefactions](wikipedia://en/601086)) or **primary wave** (as it has high velocity and is therefore the first wave to be recorded by a seismograph).[^o3] The name *[S wave](wikipedia://en/1937145)* represents another seismic wave propagation mode, standing for secondary or shear wave, a usually more destructive wave than the primary wave.

## Seismic waves in the Earth {#Seismic_waves_in_the_Earth}

*Image: Speeds of seismic waves.svg.* Velocity of seismic waves in the Earth versus depth.[^o5] The negligible S wave velocity in the outer core occurs because it is liquid, while in the solid inner core the S wave velocity is non-zero.

Primary and secondary waves are body waves that travel within the Earth. The motion and behavior of both P and S waves in the Earth are monitored to probe the interior [structure of the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_of_the_Earth). Discontinuities in velocity as a function of depth are indicative of changes in phase or composition. Differences in arrival times of waves originating in a seismic event like an earthquake as a result of waves taking different paths allow mapping of the Earth's inner structure.[^o6][^o7]

### P wave shadow zone {#P_wave_shadow_zone}

*Image: Earthquake wave shadow zone.svg.* P wave shadow zone (from [USGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USGS))

Almost all the information available on the structure of the Earth's deep interior is derived from observations of the travel times, [reflections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_%28physics%29), [refractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refraction) and phase transitions of seismic body waves, or [normal modes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_modes). P waves travel through the fluid layers of the [Earth's interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_structure_of_Earth), and yet they are refracted slightly when they pass through the transition between the semisolid [mantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantle_%28geology%29) and the liquid [outer core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_structure_of_Earth#Core). As a result, there is a P wave "[shadow zone](wikipedia://en/5035905)" between 103° and 142°[^o9] from the earthquake's focus, where the initial P waves are not registered on seismometers. In contrast, S waves do not travel through liquids.

### As an earthquake warning {#As_an_earthquake_warning}

Advance earthquake warning is possible by detecting the nondestructive primary waves that travel more quickly through the Earth's crust than do the destructive [secondary](wikipedia://en/1937145) and [Rayleigh waves](wikipedia://en/20756012).

The amount of warning depends on the delay between the arrival of the P wave and other destructive waves, generally on the order of seconds up to about 60 to 90 seconds for deep, distant, large quakes such as the [2011 Tohoku earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Tohoku_earthquake_and_tsunami). The effectiveness of a warning depends on accurate detection of the P waves and rejection of [ground vibrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_vibrations) caused by local activity (such as trucks or construction). [Earthquake early warning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_early_warning) systems can be automated to allow for immediate safety actions, such as issuing alerts, stopping elevators at the nearest floors, and switching off utilities.

## Propagation {#Propagation}

### Velocity {#Velocity}

In [isotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotropic) and homogeneous solids, a P wave travels in a straight line [longitudinally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitudinal_wave); thus, the particles in the solid vibrate along the axis of propagation (the direction of motion) of the wave energy. The velocity of P waves in that kind of medium is given by

$$v_\mathrm{p} \; = \; \sqrt{ \frac{\, K + \tfrac{4}{3} \mu \;}{\rho} } \; = \; \sqrt{ \frac{\, \lambda + 2 \mu \;}{\rho} }$$

where $K$ is the [bulk modulus](wikipedia://en/1092110) (the modulus of incompressibility), $\mu$ is the [shear modulus](wikipedia://en/1575447) (modulus of rigidity, sometimes denoted as $G$ and also called the second [Lamé parameter](wikipedia://en/8983708)), $\rho$ is the [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) of the material through which the wave propagates, and $\lambda$ is the first [Lamé parameter](wikipedia://en/8983708).

In typical situations in the interior of the Earth, the density $\rho$ usually varies much less than $K$ or $\mu$, so the velocity is mostly "controlled" by these two parameters.

The [elastic moduli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastic_moduli) [P-wave modulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-wave_modulus), $M$, is defined so that $\, M = K + \tfrac{4}{3} \mu \,$ and thereby

$$v_\mathrm{p} = \sqrt{ \frac{\, M \;}{\rho} }$$

Typical values for P wave velocity in earthquakes are in the range 5 to 8 km/s. The precise speed varies according to the region of the Earth's interior, from less than 6 km/s in the Earth's crust to 13.5 km/s in the lower mantle, and 11 km/s through the inner core.[^o13]

Geologist [Francis Birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Birch_%28geophysicist%29) discovered a relationship between the velocity of P waves and the density of the material the waves are traveling in:

$$v_\mathrm{p} = a ( \bar{M} ) + b \, \rho$$

which later became known as [Birch's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch's_law). (The symbol $a()$ is an empirically tabulated function, and $b$ is a constant.)
