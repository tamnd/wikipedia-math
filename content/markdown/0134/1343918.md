# Ralph Fox

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ralph Fox"}

**Ralph Hartzler Fox** (March 24, 1913 – December 23, 1973) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). As a professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), he taught and advised many of the contributors to the *Golden Age of [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562)*, and he played an important role in the modernization of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008) and of bringing it into the mainstream.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Ralph Fox attended [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College) for two years, while studying piano at the Leefson Conservatory of Music in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia). He earned a master's degree from [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) degree from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1939. His doctoral dissertation, *On the Lusternick–Schnirelmann Category*, was directed by [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384). (In later years he disclaimed all knowledge of the [Lusternik–Schnirelmann category](wikipedia://en/764433), and certainly never published on the subject again.) He directed 21 doctoral dissertations, including those of [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632), [John Stallings](wikipedia://en/18729795), [Francisco González-Acuña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Javier_González-Acuña), [Guillermo Torres-Diaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Torres-Diaz) and [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679), and supervised [Ken Perko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Perko)'s undergraduate thesis.

He was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) held in 1950 in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts).[^o5] His mathematical contributions include [Fox *n*-coloring](wikipedia://en/4925201) of knots, the [Fox–Artin arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox–Artin_arc), and the [free differential calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_differential_calculus). He also identified the [compact-open topology](wikipedia://en/726335) on [function spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_spaces) as being particularly appropriate for [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831).

Aside from his strictly mathematical contributions, he was responsible for introducing several basic phrases to [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008): the phrases *[slice knot](wikipedia://en/4158126)*, *[ribbon knot](wikipedia://en/9809292)*, and *[Seifert circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifert_circle)* all appear in print for the first time under his name, and he also popularized (if he did not introduce) the phrase *[Seifert surface](wikipedia://en/1343951)*.

He popularized the playing of the game of [Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_%28game%29) at both Princeton and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).
