# Uniform isomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform isomorphism"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) a **uniform isomorphism** or **uniform homeomorphism** is a special [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) between [uniform spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_spaces) that respects [uniform properties](wikipedia://en/1529885). Uniform spaces with uniform maps form a [category](wikipedia://en/53759). An [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) between uniform spaces is called a uniform isomorphism.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f$ between two uniform spaces $X$ and $Y$ is called a **uniform isomorphism** if it satisfies the following properties

- $f$ is a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942)
- $f$ is [uniformly continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_continuous)
- the [inverse function](wikipedia://en/14907) $f^{-1}$ is uniformly continuous

In other words, a **uniform isomorphism** is a [uniformly continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_continuous) [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between [uniform spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_spaces) whose [inverse](wikipedia://en/14907) is also uniformly continuous.

If a uniform isomorphism exists between two uniform spaces they are called **uniformly isomorphic** or **uniformly equivalent**.

**Uniform embeddings**

A **uniform embedding** is an injective uniformly continuous map $i : X \to Y$ between uniform spaces whose inverse $i^{-1} : i(X) \to X$ is also uniformly continuous, where the image $i(X)$ has the subspace uniformity inherited from $Y.$

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The uniform structures induced by [equivalent norms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalent_norms#Properties) on a vector space are uniformly isomorphic.
