# Reflected Brownian motion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reflected Brownian motion"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **reflected Brownian motion** (or **regulated Brownian motion**,[^o2][^o15] both with the acronym **RBM**) is a [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984) in a space with reflecting boundaries.[^o3] In the [physical](wikipedia://en/22939) literature, this process describes [diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion) in a confined space and it is often called confined Brownian motion. For example it can describe the motion of hard spheres in water confined between two walls.[^o4]

:::

RBMs have been shown to describe [queueing models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queueing_model) experiencing [heavy traffic](wikipedia://en/36781768)[^o15] as first proposed by [Kingman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kingman)[^o5] and proven by Iglehart and [Whitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_Whitt).[^o6][^o7]

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A *d*–dimensional reflected Brownian motion *Z* is a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) on $\mathbb R^d_+$ uniquely defined by

- a *d*–dimensional drift vector *μ*
- a *d*×*d* non-singular covariance matrix *Σ* and
- a *d*×*d* reflection matrix *R*.[^o10]

where *X*(*t*) is an unconstrained [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) with drift *μ* and variance *Σ*, and[^o12]

$$Z(t) = X(t) + R Y(t)$$

with *Y*(*t*) a *d*–dimensional vector where

- *Y* is continuous and non–decreasing with *Y*(0) = 0
- *Y*<sub>*j*</sub> only increases at times for which *Z*<sub>*j*</sub> = 0 for *j* = 1,2,...,*d*
- *Z*(*t*) ∈ $\mathbb R^d_+$, t ≥ 0.

The reflection matrix describes boundary behaviour. In the interior of $\scriptstyle \mathbb R^d_+$ the process behaves like a [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984); on the boundary "roughly speaking, *Z* is pushed in direction *R*<sup>*j*</sup> whenever the boundary surface $\scriptstyle \{ z \in \mathbb R^d_+ : z_j=0\}$ is hit, where *R*<sup>*j*</sup> is the *j*th column of the matrix *R*."[^o12] The process *Y*<sub>*j*</sub> is the [local time](wikipedia://en/5678101) of the process on the corresponding section of the boundary.

:::

## Stability conditions {#Stability_conditions}

Stability conditions are known for RBMs in 1, 2, and 3 dimensions. "The problem of recurrence classification for SRBMs in four and higher dimensions remains open."[^o12] In the special case where *R* is an [M-matrix](wikipedia://en/7939066) then necessary and sufficient conditions for stability are[^o12]

1. *R* is a [non-singular matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-singular_matrix) and
1. *R*<sup>−1</sup>*μ* \< 0.

## Marginal and stationary distribution {#Marginal_and_stationary_distribution}

### One dimension {#One_dimension}

The [marginal distribution](wikipedia://en/506077) (transient distribution) of a one-dimensional Brownian motion starting at 0 restricted to positive values (a single reflecting barrier at 0) with drift *μ* and variance *σ*<sup>2</sup> is

$$\mathbb P(Z(t) \leq z) = \Phi \left(\frac{z-\mu t}{\sigma t^{1/2}} \right) - e^{-2 \mu z /\sigma^2} \Phi \left( \frac{-z-\mu t}{\sigma t^{1/2}} \right)$$

for all *t* ≥ 0, (with Φ the [cumulative distribution function of the normal distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulative_distribution_function_of_the_normal_distribution)) which yields (for *μ* \< 0) when taking t → ∞ an [exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906)[^o15]

$$\mathbb P(Z<z) = 1-e^{-2\mu z/\sigma^2}.$$

For fixed *t*, the distribution of *Z(t)* coincides with the distribution of the running maximum *M(t)* of the Brownian motion,

$$Z(t) \sim M(t)=\sup_{s\in [0,t]} X(s).$$

But be aware that the distributions of the processes as a whole are very different. In particular, *M(t)* is increasing in *t*, which is not the case for *Z(t)*.

The heat kernel for reflected Brownian motion at $p_b$:

$$f(x,p_b)=\frac{e^{-((x-u)/a)^2/2}+e^{-((x+u-2p_b)/a)^2/2}}{a(2\pi)^{1/2}}$$

### Multiple dimensions {#Multiple_dimensions}

The stationary distribution of a reflected Brownian motion in multiple dimensions is tractable analytically when there is a [product form stationary distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_form_stationary_distribution),[^o17] which occurs when the process is stable and[^o18]

$$2 \Sigma = RD + DR'$$

where *D* = [diag](wikipedia://en/174080)(*Σ*). In this case the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) is[^o10]

$$p(z_1,z_2,\ldots,z_d) = \prod_{k=1}^d \eta_k e^{-\eta_k z_k}$$

where *η*<sub>*k*</sub> = 2*μ*<sub>*k*</sub>*γ*<sub>*k*</sub>/*Σ*<sub>*kk*</sub> and *γ* = *R*<sup>−1</sup>*μ*. [Closed-form expressions](wikipedia://en/585143) for situations where the product form condition does not hold can be computed numerically as described below in the simulation section.

## Simulation {#Simulation}

### One dimension {#One_dimension}

In one dimension the simulated process is the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) of a [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984). The following [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412) program creates a sample path.[^o21]

```matlab
% rbm.m
n = 10^4; h=10^(-3); t=h.*(0:n); mu=-1;
X = zeros(1, n+1); M=X; B=X;
B(1)=3; X(1)=3;
for k=2:n+1
    Y = sqrt(h) * randn; U = rand(1);
    B(k) = B(k-1) + mu * h - Y;
    M = (Y + sqrt(Y ^ 2 - 2 * h * log(U))) / 2;
    X(k) = max(M-Y, X(k-1) + h * mu - Y);
end
subplot(2, 1, 1)
plot(t, X, 'k-');
subplot(2, 1, 2)
plot(t, X-B, 'k-');
```

The error involved in discrete simulations has been quantified.[^o22]

[QNET](http://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~dai/QNET/) allows simulation of steady state RBMs.[^o24][^o25][^o26]

## Other boundary conditions {#Other_boundary_conditions}

Feller described possible boundary condition for the process[^o28][^o29][^o30]

- absorption[^o28] or killed Brownian motion,[^o31] a [Dirichlet boundary condition](wikipedia://en/423504)
- instantaneous reflection,[^o28] as described above a [Neumann boundary condition](wikipedia://en/735443)
- elastic reflection, a [Robin boundary condition](wikipedia://en/9945920)
- delayed reflection[^o28] (the time spent on the boundary is positive with probability one)
- partial reflection[^o28] where the process is either immediately reflected or is absorbed
- sticky Brownian motion.[^o32]
