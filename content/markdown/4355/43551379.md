# Lauren Williams (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lauren Williams"}

**Lauren Kiyomi Williams** (born c. 1978) is an American mathematician known for her work on [cluster algebras](wikipedia://en/27038423), [tropical geometry](wikipedia://en/867041), [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582), [amplituhedra](wikipedia://en/40564571), and the positive [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810).[^o6] She is Dwight Parker Robinson Professor of Mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o3] She was awarded a [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program) in 2025.[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Williams's father is an engineer; her mother is third-generation [Japanese American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Americans). She grew up in Los Angeles, where she was born. Her interest in mathematics was sparked by winning a fourth-grade mathematics contest.[^o6] She graduated as [valedictorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian) of [Palos Verdes Peninsula High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palos_Verdes_Peninsula_High_School) in 1996.[^o3] As a high school student, Williams attended the 1994 [Research Science Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Science_Institute) at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT),[^o7] where she conducted research with Satomi Okazaki, a student of her eventual Ph.D. advisor [Richard P. Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397).[^o6] She graduated [magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_cum_laude) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 2000 with an A.B. in mathematics[^o3] and in 2001 completed Part III of the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) with distinction at Cambridge University in England. In 2005 she received her Ph.D. from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) under the supervision of Stanley. Her dissertation was titled *Combinatorial aspects of total positivity*.[^o9]

## Work {#Work}

After postdoctoral positions at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and Harvard, Williams rejoined the Berkeley mathematics department as an assistant professor in 2009, and was promoted to associate professor in 2013 and then full professor in 2016.[^o3]

Starting in the fall of 2018, she rejoined the Harvard mathematics department as a full professor, making her the second ever tenured female math professor at Harvard. The first, [Sophie Morel](wikipedia://en/36360630), left Harvard in 2012.[^o11]

Along with colleagues Olya Mandelshtam (her former student, now an assistant professor at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo)) and [Sylvie Corteel](wikipedia://en/57433897), in 2018 Williams developed a new characterization of both symmetric and nonsymmetric [Macdonald polynomials](wikipedia://en/10354285) using the combinatorial exclusion process.[^o12]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 2012, she became one of the inaugural [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o14] She is the 2016 winner of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) and [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft) [Research Prize in Algebra and Number Theory](wikipedia://en/59776675).[^o15] In 2022 she was awarded a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship).[^o16] In 2025 she was awarded a [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program).[^o4]
