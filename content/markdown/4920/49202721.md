# Edward Carey Francis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edward Carey Francis"}

**Edward Carey Francis** [OBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OBE), (13 September 1897 – 27 July 1966)[^o2] was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and Anglican missionary to [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya), where he became "arguably the most influential educationist in Kenya's modern history".[^o3]

:::

He was born in [Hampstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead), [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London). He was educated first at [William Ellis School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ellis_School), becoming head-boy of the school and captain of the [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket), [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football), [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis) and [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_of_athletics) teams.[^o2] After school he enlisted in the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army), serving in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) with the [Royal Field Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Field_Artillery) and being [mentioned in despatches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentioned_in_despatches).[^o5] On completion of the war he took up a scholarship to read mathematics at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge).[^o2]

After graduating, in 1922 he became a fellow of [Peterhouse, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge) and Director of Studies in Mathematics. He left Cambridge in 1928 when he joined the [Church Missionary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Missionary_Society).[^o2] He was posted to Kenya and became firstly a teacher and later Headmaster of [Maseno School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maseno_School) in [Nyanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyanza_Province) between 1928 and 1940. He shortly served as Mathematics teacher at Duke of York School ([Lenana School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenana_School)). He then served as Headmaster at [Alliance High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_High_School_%28Kenya%29) between 1940 and 1962. During his time at Alliance he developed a mythical-like reputation as an inspiring teacher, educating many future politicians.[^o2] In later years, between 1962 and 1966 he was Assistant Master at Pumwani High School.[^o5] In Jomo Kenyatta's first cabinet, nine out of the fifteen members had studied under Francis at Alliance.[^o6]

Francis died in Nairobi on 27 July 1966 and was buried within the grounds of Alliance High School.[^o2] On his death, a silence was held in the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_%28Kenya%29), an honour usually reserved for Heads of State.[^o2]
