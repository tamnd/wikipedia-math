# Daniel Nagin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Nagin"}

**Daniel Steven Nagin** (born November 29, 1948)[^o4] is an American criminologist, [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661), and the Teresa and H. John Heinz III University Professor of Public Policy and Statistics at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University)'s [Heinz College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_College).[^o5] He was elected a Member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2025.[^o6]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Nagin received his B.S. in Administrative and Managerial Sciences and M.S. in Industrial Administration from [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) in 1971, and his Ph.D. from what is now the university's Heinz College in 1976.[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

Nagin served as Deputy Secretary for Fiscal Policy and Analysis in the [Pennsylvania Department of Revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Department_of_Revenue) from 1981 to 1986.[^o8] He joined Carnegie Mellon University in 1986 as an associate professor of management in the School of Urban and Public Affairs. In 1990, he became professor of management in Heinz College, a position he retained until 1998.[^o10] He was also the research program area director of Carnegie Mellon University's National Consortium on Violence Research from 1997 to 2001.[^o10] In 2006, he became the Associate Dean of Faculty at Heinz College, and in 2008, he became the Teresa and H. John Heinz III University Professor of Public Policy and Statistics, positions he still held as of December 2019.[^o10]

## Research {#Research}

Nagin is known for researching the deterrence effect of criminal punishments and he chaired the [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_%28United_States%29)’s Committee on Deterrence and the Death Penalty.[^o8] In 2012, this committee released a report concluding that existing research on the deterrent effect of [capital punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment) is inconclusive.[^o12][^o13] He has also researched the use of statistical methods for analyzing longitudinal data, as well as changes in criminal behavior over the human lifetime.[^o8]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Nagin, along with [Joan Petersilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Petersilia), received the [Stockholm Prize in Criminology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Prize_in_Criminology) in 2014. He received the Edwin H. Sutherland Award from the [American Society of Criminology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_Criminology) in 2006.[^o3] In 2017, he received the NAS Award for Scientific Reviewing from the [U.S. National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o15] He is also a fellow of the American Society of Criminology, the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), and the [American Academy of Political and Social Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Political_and_Social_Science).[^o8]
