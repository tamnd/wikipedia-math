# Mordell–Weil theorem

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Mordell–Weil theorem"}

**Mordell–Weil theorem.**

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mordell–Weil theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Mordell–Weil theorem** states that for an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) $A$ over a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) $K$, the group $A(K)$ of [*K*-rational points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-rational_point) of $A$ is a [finitely-generated abelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely-generated_abelian_group), called the [Mordell–Weil group](wikipedia://en/5841813). The case with $A$ an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) $E$ and $K$ the field of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) is **Mordell's theorem**, answering a question apparently posed by [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) around 1901; it was proven by [Louis Mordell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Mordell) in 1922. It is a foundational theorem of [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205) and the [arithmetic of abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/384577).

:::

## History {#History}

The *tangent-chord process* (one form of [addition theorem](wikipedia://en/973852) on a [cubic curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_curve)) had been known as far back as the seventeenth century. The process of [infinite descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_descent) of [Fermat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat) was well known, but Mordell succeeded in establishing the finiteness of the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) $E(\mathbb{Q})/2E(\mathbb{Q})$ which forms a major step in the proof. Certainly the finiteness of this group is a [necessary condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_condition) for $E(\mathbb{Q})$ to be finitely generated; and it shows that the [rank](wikipedia://en/244541) is finite. This turns out to be the essential difficulty. It can be proved by direct analysis of the doubling of a point on *E*.

Some years later [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) took up the subject, producing the generalisation to Jacobians of higher genus curves over arbitrary number fields in his doctoral dissertation[^o4] published in 1928. More abstract methods were required, to carry out a proof with the same basic structure. The second half of the proof needs some type of [height function](wikipedia://en/3141855), in terms of which to bound the 'size' of points of $A(K)$. Some measure of the co-ordinates will do; heights are logarithmic, so that (roughly speaking) it is a question of how many digits are required to write down a set of [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316). For an abelian variety, there is no *a priori* preferred representation, though, as a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469).

Both halves of the proof have been improved significantly by subsequent technical advances: in [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) as applied to descent, and in the study of the best height functions (which are [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478)).

## Further results {#Further_results}

The theorem leaves a number of questions still unanswered:

- Calculation of the rank. This is still a demanding computational problem, and does not always have [effective solutions](wikipedia://en/250237).
- Meaning of the rank: see [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161).
- Possible torsion subgroups: [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) proved in 1978 that the Mordell–Weil group can have only finitely many torsion subgroups. This is the elliptic curve case of the [torsion conjecture](wikipedia://en/35173610).
- For a [curve](wikipedia://en/253260) $C$ in its [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) as $A$, can the intersection of $C$ with $A(K)$ be infinite? [Faltings' theorem](wikipedia://en/172199) implies this is false unless $C = A$.
- In the same context, can $C$ contain infinitely many torsion points of $A$? Because of the [Manin–Mumford conjecture](wikipedia://en/384577#Manin.E2.80.93Mumford_conjecture), proved by [Michel Raynaud](wikipedia://en/4760928), this is false unless it is the elliptic curve case.
