# List of transitive finite linear groups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of transitive finite linear groups"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in areas of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) and [finite geometry](wikipedia://en/267061), the **list of transitive finite linear groups** is an important classification of certain highly symmetric [actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) on [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370).

:::

The [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012) finite [2-transitive groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-transitive_group) were classified by [Bertram Huppert](wikipedia://en/17639456).[^o2] The [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445) made possible the complete classification of finite [doubly transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubly_transitive) [permutation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_groups). This is a result by [Christoph Hering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Hering).[^o3] A finite 2-transitive group has a [socle](wikipedia://en/3571350) that is either a vector space over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) or a non-abelian primitive [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600); groups of the latter kind are almost simple groups and described elsewhere. This article provides a complete list of the finite 2-transitive groups whose socle is [elementary abelian](wikipedia://en/4843202).

Let $p$ be a prime, and $G$ a subgroup of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) $GL(d,p)$ acting transitively on the nonzero vectors of the *d*-dimensional vector space $(F_p)^d$ over the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615)$F_p$ with *p* elements.

## Infinite classes {#Infinite_classes}

There are four infinite classes of finite transitive linear groups.

- $$G \leq \Gamma{}L(1,p^d);$$
- $$G \triangleright SL(a,q)\text{ and }p^d=q^a;$$
- $$G \triangleright Sp(2a,q)\text{ and }p^d=q^{2a};$$
- $$G \triangleright G_2(q)',\ p^d=q^6\text{ and }p=2.$$

Notice that the exceptional [group of Lie type](wikipedia://en/1691490) *G*<sub>2</sub>(*q*) is usually constructed as the automorphism groups of the split [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436). Hence, it has a natural [representation](wikipedia://en/6355948) as a subgroup of the 7-dimensional [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) O(7, *q*). If *q* is even, then the underlying [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) polarizes to a degenerate [symplectic form](wikipedia://en/292852). Factoring out with the radical, one obtains an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) between O(7, *q*) and the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) Sp(6, *q*). The subgroup of Sp(6, *q*) which corresponds to *G*<sub>2</sub>(*q*)′ is transitive.

In fact, for *q*>2, the group *G*<sub>2</sub>(*q*) = *G*<sub>2</sub>(*q*)′ is simple. If *q*=2 then *G*<sub>2</sub>(2)′ ≅ PSU(3,3) is simple with index 2 in *G*<sub>2</sub>(2).

## Sporadic finite transitive linear groups {#Sporadic_finite_transitive_linear_groups}

These groups are usually classified by some typical [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918), this normal subgroup is denoted by *G*<sub>0</sub> and are written in the third column of the table. The notation 2<sup>1+4</sup><sub>−</sub> stands for the [extraspecial group](wikipedia://en/6990619) of minus type of order 32 (i.e. the extraspecial group of order 32 with an odd number (namely one) of [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) factor).

All but one of the sporadic transitive linear groups $G$ yield a primitive permutation group $p^d:G$ of degree at most 2499. In the [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805) programs [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_computer_algebra_system) and [MAGMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma_computer_algebra_system), these groups can be accessed with the command `PrimitiveGroup(p^d,k);` where the number *k* is the *primitive identification* of $p^d:G$. This number is given in the last column of the following table.

Seven of these groups are sharply transitive; these groups were found by [Hans Zassenhaus](wikipedia://en/1376825) and are also known as the multiplicative groups of the Zassenhaus [near-fields](wikipedia://en/4033255). These groups are marked by a star in the table.

::: {#o6 .table cue=table}

| Condition on $p$ | Condition on $d$ | $G_0$ | Primitive identification of $p^d:G$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $p=5$ | $d=2$ | $SL(2,3)$ | 15\*, 18, 19 |
| $p=7$ | $d=2$ | $SL(2,3)$ | 25\*, 29 |
| $p=11$ | $d=2$ | $SL(2,3)$ | 39\*, 42 |
| $p=23$ | $d=2$ | $SL(2,3)$ | 59\* |
| $p=11$ | $d=2$ | $SL(2,5)$ | 56\*, 57 |
| $p=19$ | $d=2$ | $SL(2,5)$ | 86 |
| $p=29$ | $d=2$ | $SL(2,5)$ | 106\*, 110 |
| $p=59$ | $d=2$ | $SL(2,5)$ | 84\* |
| $p=2$ | $d=4$ | $A_6$ | 16, 17 |
| $p=2$ | $d=4$ | $A_7$ | 20 |
| $p=3$ | $d=4$ | $SL(2,5)$ | 124, 126, 127, 128 |
| $p=3$ | $d=4$ | $2^{1+4}_-$ | 71, 90, 99, 129, 130 |
| $p=2$ | $d=6$ | $PSU(3,3)$ | 62, 63 |
| $p=3$ | $d=6$ | $SL(2,13)$ | 396 |

:::

This list is not explicitly contained in Hering's paper. Many books[^o7][^o8] and papers give a list of these groups, some of them an incomplete one. For example, Cameron's book[^o9] misses the groups in line 11 of the table, that is, containing $SL(2,5)$ as a normal subgroup.
