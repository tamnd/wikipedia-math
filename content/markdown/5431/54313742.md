# Mott–Schottky equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mott–Schottky equation"}

The **Mott–Schottky equation** relates the [capacitance](wikipedia://en/140711) to the applied [voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential) across a [semiconductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor)-[electrolyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolyte) [junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_junction).[^o2]

:::

$$\frac{1}{C^2} = \frac{2}{\epsilon \epsilon_0 A^2 e N_d} (V - V_{fb} - \frac{k_B T}{e})$$

where $C$ is the differential capacitance $\frac{\partial{Q}}{\partial{V}}$, $\epsilon$ is the [dielectric constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dielectric_constant) of the semiconductor, $\epsilon_0$ is the [permittivity of free space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permittivity_of_free_space), $A$ is the area such that the [depletion region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depletion_region) volume is $w A$, $e$ is the elementary charge, $N_d$ is the density of dopants, $V$ is the applied potential, $V_{fb}$ is the [flat band potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_band_potential), $k_B$ is the [Boltzmann constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_constant), and T is the [absolute temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_temperature).

This theory predicts that a [Mott–Schottky plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mott–Schottky_plot) will be linear. The doping density $N_d$ can be derived from the slope of the plot (provided the area and dielectric constant are known). The flatband potential can be determined as well; absent the temperature term, the plot would cross the $V$-axis at the flatband potential.

## Derivation {#Derivation}

Under an applied potential $V$, the width of the depletion region is[^o4]

$$w = (\frac{2 \epsilon \epsilon_0}{e N_d} ( V - V_{fb} ) )^\frac{1}{2}$$

Using the [abrupt approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrupt_approximation),[^o4] all charge carriers except the ionized dopants have left the depletion region, so the charge density in the depletion region is $e N_d$, and the total charge of the depletion region, compensated by opposite charge nearby in the electrolyte, is

$$Q = e N_d A w = e N_d A (\frac{2 \epsilon \epsilon_0}{e N_d} ( V - V_{fb} ) )^\frac{1}{2}$$

Thus, the differential capacitance is

$$C = \frac{\partial{Q}}{\partial{V}} = e N_d A \frac{1}{2}(\frac{2 \epsilon \epsilon_0}{e N_d})^\frac{1}{2} ( V - V_{fb} )^{-\frac{1}{2}} = A (\frac{e N_d \epsilon \epsilon_0}{2(V - V_{fb})})^\frac{1}{2}$$

which is equivalent to the Mott-Schottky equation, save for the temperature term. In fact the temperature term arises from a more careful analysis, which takes [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) into account by abandoning the abrupt approximation and solving the [Poisson–Boltzmann equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson–Boltzmann_equation) for the charge density in the depletion region.[^o4]
