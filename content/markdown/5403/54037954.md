# Rui Loja Fernandes

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rui Loja Fernandes"}

**Rui António Loja Fernandes** (July 20, 1965, [Coimbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbra)) is a [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal) mathematician working in the [USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Fernandes obtained a bachelor's degree in Physics Engineering at [Instituto Superior Técnico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Superior_Técnico) ([Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon), Portugal) in 1988. He then moved to the USA and earned a master's degree in Mathematics in 1991 and a PhD in Mathematics in 1994 from the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota). His PhD thesis was entitled "*Completely Integrable bi-Hamiltonian Systems"* and has been written under the supervision of [Peter J. Olver](wikipedia://en/48942413).

In 1994 he returned to [Instituto Superior Técnico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Superior_Técnico), where he worked first as Assistant Professor (1994-2002), and then as Associated Professor (2003-2007) and Full Professor (2007-2012).[^o5][^o6]

In 2012 he moved back to the USA and since then he is the Lois M. Lackner Professor of Mathematics at [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign).[^o7] In 2016, he became a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to the study of Poisson geometry and Lie algebroids, and for service to the mathematical community."[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

Fernandes research focusses on [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), more precisely on [Poisson](wikipedia://en/882902) and [symplectic](wikipedia://en/294298) geometry. Among his most well-known results are a solution to the long-standing problem of describing the obstructions to the integrability of [Lie algebroids](wikipedia://en/310921)[^o10] and a new geometric proof of Conn's linearization theorem,[^o11] both written in collaboration with [Marius Crainic](wikipedia://en/63005657).

He is the author of more than 40 research papers in peer-reviewed journals[^o12] and has supervised 6 PhD students as of 2021.[^o4]
