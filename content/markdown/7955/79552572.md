# David A. Sánchez

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David A. Sánchez"}

**David A. Sánchez** (born January 13, 1933) is a Mexican-American[^o21][^o22] university and research administrator, mathematician, educator, and author. He held the posts of [provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provost_%28education%29) at [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University), assistant director for the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) and [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory),[^o29] and assistant [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_%28education%29) for the [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University) (TAMU) system; As a mathematician, his areas of focus include [ordinary differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_differential_equations) and [biomathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomathematics).[^o25] He has been a researcher and professor at several universities, including the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin-Madison)'s Mathematics Research Lab, [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), and the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico). He published his memoir, *Don't Forget the Accent Mark: A Memoir*, in 2011.[^o21]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

David Alan Sánchez was born on January 13, 1933, in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California) to Berta Sánchez.[^o21] At the age of three, he moved to [Mission Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Hills,_San_Diego) in [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego,_California) and was [adopted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoption) by his grandparents, Cecilio and Concepcion Sánchez.[^o21]

Sánchez earned his [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) (B.S.) in 1955 from the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico). After graduating, he served the [U.S. Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps) for three years.[^o21]

Sánchez continued with his graduate studies at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) (UM), where he worked at their Institute of Science and Technology in the Radar Laboratory. His research involved battlefield simulations for the [Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army).[^o21] He received his [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) (M.S.) in 1960. He wrote his doctoral dissertation, *[Calculus of Variations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_of_Variations) for Integrals Depending on a Convolution Product* under the direction [Lamberto Cesari](wikipedia://en/17581349); receiving his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) (Ph.D.) in 1964 from UM.[^o26]

### Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1957, Sánchez married Joan Thomas. They have two children - a son and a daughter.[^o21] After he retired from the University of New Mexico, he made his home in [Corrales, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrales,_New_Mexico).[^o22]

## Career {#Career}

### Academia {#Academia}

After completing his Ph.D., Sánchez spent two years at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) as an instructor, before traveling to [Manchester, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester,_England) as a visiting professor at [Manchester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_University).

**UCLA (1966–1977)** In 1966, Sánchez became an assistant professor at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) (UCLA). While at UCLA, he presented papers at the American Mathematical Societies 1967 meeting, and at the Conference on Qualitative Theory of Nonlinear Differential and Integral Equations in 1968, on extremals of composite functions and Ricotti equations, respectively.[^o35][^o36] After four years, spent a year at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) as a visiting professor (1973–1974); and then returned to UCLA as an associate professor.[^o29] He became a full professor at UCLA in 1976.[^o21]

**University of New Mexico (1977–1986)** In 1977, Sánchez returned to his undergraduate alma mater, the University of New Mexico, as a full professor in the Department of Mathematics and Statistics. He served as the department chairman from 1983 to 1986). He took a [sabbatical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbatical) in 1982 to teach at the [University of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wales) in [Aberystwyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth), [Ceredigion, Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceredigion,_Wales).[^o29][^o21]

In 1986, Sánchez took a nine-year detour in his academic career, into administrative roles.

**Final posts - Texas A&M and University of New Mexico** In 1995, after serving as assistant Chancellor for Texas A&M, Sánchez returned to a teaching and research role at the university.[^o24][^o33][^o25]

Sánchez' returned to the University of New Mexico (UNM) for his final university position before retiring. At UNM, he served as chairman of the Department of Mathematics and Statistics.[^o22]

### Administrative career (1986-1995) {#Administrative_career_(1986-1995)}

In 1986, Sánchez took a detour from his traditional academic path to a new career path, accepting a position as the vice president and [provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provost_%28education%29) at [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University) in [Bethlehem, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_Pennsylvania).[^o28] While at Lehigh, he restructured the promotion and tenure process; and, chaired a commission on racial diversity.[^o30][^o32] He remained at Lehigh until 1990, when he moved to the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) (NSF) to take a two-year term as an assistant director.[^o30][^o29] At the NSF, he headed the Mathematics and Physical Sciences Directorate,[^o23] the largest division at the NSF,[^o31] "setting national priorities on scientific research."[^o30][^o42] At the end of his term at the NSF, he took a position as an assistant director for the [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory).[^o30][^o29]

In November 1993, Sánchez moved to the [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University) System as Vice Chancellor for Academic Affairs, to take on long-range planning.[^o21] His foray into administration lasted for nine years, when he returned to teaching at Texas A&M.[^o33]"For nine years he acquired a severe case of administrativitis, but recovered and is happily teaching at Texas A&M University." - P. Littleton[^o33]
