# Robert Stalnaker

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Stalnaker"}

**Robert Culp Stalnaker** (born 1940) is an American [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) who is Laurance S. Rockefeller Professor Emeritus of [Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^o3] He is a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and a Corresponding Fellow of the [British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy).[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Stalnaker was born on January 22, 1940.[^o6] He earned his BA from [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University), and his PhD from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1965. His thesis advisor was [Stuart Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Hampshire), though he was strongly influenced by another faculty member, [Carl Hempel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Hempel). Stalnaker taught briefly at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) and the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois), and then for many years at the Sage School of Philosophy at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) before joining the [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) faculty in 1988.[^o3] He retired from MIT in 2016.[^o3] His many students include [Jason Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Stanley), [Zoltán Gendler Szábo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoltán_Gendler_Szábo), and [Delia Graff Fara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delia_Graff_Fara).

In 2007, Stalnaker delivered the [John Locke Lectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke_Lectures) at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) on the topic of "Our Knowledge of the Internal World".[^o7] In 2017, he delivered the Casalegno Lectures at the [University of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Milan) on "Counterfactuals and Practical Reason".[^o8]

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

His work concerns, among other things, the philosophical foundations of [semantics](wikipedia://en/29107), [pragmatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatics), philosophical logic, [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216), [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), the theory of [conditionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_sentence), [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology), and the [philosophy of mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_mind). All of these interests are in the service of addressing the problem of [intentionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intentionality), "what it is to represent the world in both speech and thought".[^o10] In his work, he seeks to provide a naturalistic account of intentionality, characterizing representation in terms of causal and modal notions.

Along with [Saul Kripke](wikipedia://en/48039), [David Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kellogg_Lewis), and [Alvin Plantinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Plantinga), Stalnaker has been one of the most influential theorists exploring philosophical aspects of [possible world semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Possible_world_semantics). According to his view of possible worlds, they are ways this world could have been, which in turn are maximal properties that this world could have had. This view distinguishes him from the influential [modal realist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_realism) Lewis, who argued that possible worlds are concrete entities just like this world.[^o11] Stalnaker's work on conditionals is reputed to have started the field of [conditional logic](wikipedia://en/81395508).[^o12]

In addition to his contributions to the metaphysics of possible worlds, he has used the apparatus of possible worlds semantics to explore many issues in the semantics of natural language, including counterfactual and indicative conditionals, and [presupposition](wikipedia://en/1965727). His view of [assertion](wikipedia://en/15014170) as narrowing the conversational common ground to exclude situations in which the asserted content is false was a major impetus in recent developments in semantics and pragmatics, in particular, the so-called "dynamic turn".[^o13]

Stalnaker is the author of four books and dozens of articles in major philosophical journals.
