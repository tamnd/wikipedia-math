::: {#o8 .subject cue=lead name="Grand antiprism"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **grand antiprism** or **pentagonal double antiprismoid** is a [uniform 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/1645899) (4-dimensional uniform [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471)) bounded by 320 [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29): 20 [pentagonal antiprisms](wikipedia://en/2860091), and 300 [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606). It is an anomalous, [non-Wythoffian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Wythoffian) uniform 4-polytope, discovered in 1965 by [John H. Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) and [Michael Guy](wikipedia://en/16480693).[^o9][^o10] Topologically, under its highest symmetry, the [pentagonal antiprisms](wikipedia://en/2860091) have *D<sub>20</sub>* symmetry (dihedral of order 20); and there are two types of regular tetrahedra, one with *C<sub>4</sub>* symmetry and one with *C<sub>2</sub>* symmetry, so constrained by their location in the 4-polytope.

:::

## Structure {#Structure}

20 stacked pentagonal antiprisms occur in two disjoint rings of 10 antiprisms each. The antiprisms in each ring are joined to each other via their pentagonal faces. The two rings are mutually perpendicular, in a structure similar to a [duoprism](wikipedia://en/2114155).

The 300 tetrahedra join the two rings to each other, and are laid out in a 2-dimensional arrangement topologically equivalent to the [2-torus](wikipedia://en/74800#The_n-torus) and the [ridge of the duocylinder](wikipedia://en/2114445#The_ridge). These can be further divided into three sets. 100 face mate to one ring, 100 face mate to the other ring, and 100 are centered at the exact midpoint of the duocylinder and edge mate to both rings. This latter set forms a [flat torus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_torus) and can be "unrolled" into a flat 10×10 square array of tetrahedra that meet only at their edges and vertices. See figure below.

*Image: 100 tets.jpg.* 100 tetrahedra in a 10×10 array forming the Clifford torus boundary in the 600-cell and grand antiprism.

In addition the 300 tetrahedra can be partitioned into 10 disjoint [Boerdijk–Coxeter helices](wikipedia://en/19172682) of 30 cells each that close back on each other. The two pentagonal antiprism tubes, plus the 10 BC helices, form an irregular discrete [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384) of the grand antiprism that Hopf maps to the faces of a pentagonal antiprism. The two tubes map to the two pentagonal faces and the 10 BC helices map to the 10 triangular faces.

The structure of the grand antiprism is analogous to that of the 3-dimensional [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851). However, the grand antiprism is the only convex uniform analogue of the antiprism in 4 dimensions (although the [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) may be regarded as a regular analogue of the *digonal [antiprism](wikipedia://en/1851)*). The only nonconvex uniform 4-dimensional antiprism analogue uses [pentagrammic crossed-antiprisms](wikipedia://en/4157338) instead of pentagonal antiprisms, and is called the *[pentagrammic double antiprismoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagrammic_double_antiprismoid)*.

## Vertex figure {#Vertex_figure}

The vertex figure of the grand antiprism is a [sphenocorona](wikipedia://en/1142750) or *dissected regular icosahedron*: a regular icosahedron with two adjacent vertices removed. In their place 8 triangles are replaced by a pair of trapezoids, edge lengths φ, 1, 1, 1 (where φ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386)), joined together along their edge of length φ, to give a [tetradecahedron](wikipedia://en/12786864) whose faces are the 2 [trapezoids](wikipedia://en/201826) and the 12 remaining [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285).

## Construction {#Construction}

*Image: 600-cell grand antiprism net.png.* The regular [600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447) can be decomposed with the symmetry of the grand antiprism, with each of the 20 blue pentagonal antiprisms being divided into 15 regular tetrahedra.

The grand antiprism can be constructed by [*diminishing* the 600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447#Diminished_600-cells): subtracting 20 pyramids whose bases are three-dimensional pentagonal antiprisms. Conversely, the two rings of pentagonal antiprisms in the grand antiprism may be triangulated by 10 tetrahedra joined to the triangular faces of each antiprism, and a circle of 5 tetrahedra between every pair of antiprisms, joining the 10 tetrahedra of each, yielding 150 tetrahedra per ring. These combined with the 300 tetrahedra that join the two rings together yield the 600 tetrahedra of the 600-cell.

This diminishing may be realized by removing two rings of 10 vertices from the 600-cell, each lying in mutually orthogonal planes. Each ring of removed vertices creates a stack of pentagonal antiprisms on the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634). This relationship is analogous to how a [pentagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860091) can be constructed from an [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) by removing two opposite vertices, thereby removing 5 triangles from the opposite 'poles' of the icosahedron, leaving the 10 equatorial triangles and two pentagons on the top and bottom.

(The [snub 24-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756) can also be constructed by another diminishing of the 600-cell, removing 24 icosahedral pyramids. Equivalently, this may be realized as taking the convex hull of the vertices remaining after 24 vertices, corresponding to those of an inscribed [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485), are removed from the 600-cell.)

Alternatively, it can also be constructed from the [decagonal ditetragoltriate](wikipedia://en/2114155#Ditetragoltriates) (the convex hull of two perpendicular nonuniform [10-10 duoprisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10-10_duoprism) where the ratio of the two decagons are in the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386)) via an [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) process. The [decagonal prisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decagonal_prism) alternate into [pentagonal antiprisms](wikipedia://en/2860091), the [rectangular trapezoprisms](wikipedia://en/6285) alternate into [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) with two new regular [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) (representing a non-corealmic [triangular bipyramid](wikipedia://en/646933)) created at the deleted vertices. This is the only uniform solution for the p-gonal double antiprismoids alongside its conjugate, the pentagrammic double antiprismoid from the decagrammic ditetragoltriate.
