# Mixture (probability)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mixture"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **mixture** is a probabilistic combination of two or more probability distributions.[^o2] The concept arises mostly in two contexts:

:::

- A mixture defining a new [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) from some existing ones, as in a [mixture distribution](wikipedia://en/708242) or a [compound distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_distribution). Here a major problem often is to derive the properties of the resulting distribution.
- A mixture used as a [statistical model](wikipedia://en/27576) such as is often used for [statistical classification](wikipedia://en/1579244). The model may represent the population from which observations arise as a [mixture](wikipedia://en/708242) of several components, and the problem is that of a [mixture model](wikipedia://en/871681), in which the task is to infer from which of a *discrete* set of sub-populations each observation originated.
