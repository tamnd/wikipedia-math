# Fermat cubic

*Image: 3D model of Fermat cubic.stl.* 3D model of Fermat cubic (real points)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fermat cubic"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Fermat cubic**, named after [Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966), is a [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) defined by

:::

$$x^3 + y^3 + z^3 = 1. \$$

Methods of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) provide the following parameterization of Fermat's cubic:

$$x(s,t) = {3 t - {1\over 3} (s^2 + s t + t^2)^2 \over t (s^2 + s t + t^2) - 3}$$

$$y(s,t) = {3 s + 3 t + {1\over 3} (s^2 + s t + t^2)^2 \over t (s^2 + s t + t^2) - 3}$$

$$z(s,t) = {-3 - (s^2 + s t + t^2) (s + t) \over t (s^2 + s t + t^2) - 3}.$$

In [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) the Fermat cubic is given by

$$w^3+x^3+y^3+z^3=0.$$

The 27 lines lying on the Fermat cubic are easy to describe explicitly: they are the 9 lines of the form (*w* : *aw* : *y* : *by*) where *a* and *b* are fixed numbers with cube −1, and their 18 conjugates under permutations of coordinates.
