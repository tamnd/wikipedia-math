# Biregular graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Biregular graph"}

In [graph-theoretic mathematics](wikipedia://en/12401), a **biregular graph**[^o2] or **semiregular bipartite graph**[^o3] is a [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) $G=(U,V,E)$ for which every two vertices on the same side of the given bipartition have the same [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) as each other. If the degree of the vertices in $U$ is $x$ and the degree of the vertices in $V$ is $y$, then the graph is said to be $(x,y)$-biregular.

:::

*Image: Rhombicdodecahedron.jpg.* The graph of the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) is biregular.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Every [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{a,b}$ is $(b,a)$-biregular.[^o10] The [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) is another example; it is (3,4)-biregular.[^o6]

:::

## Vertex counts {#Vertex_counts}

An $(x,y)$-biregular graph $G=(U,V,E)$ must satisfy the equation $x|U|=y|V|$. This follows from a simple [double counting argument](wikipedia://en/296942): the number of endpoints of edges in $U$ is $x|U|$, the number of endpoints of edges in $V$ is $y|V|$, and each edge contributes the same amount (one) to both numbers.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetry"}

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

Every [regular](wikipedia://en/85821) bipartite graph is also biregular. Every [edge-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/584406) (disallowing graphs with [isolated vertices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolated_vertex)) that is not also [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581175) must be biregular.[^o10] In particular every edge-transitive graph is either regular or biregular.

:::

## Configurations {#Configurations}

The [Levi graphs](wikipedia://en/1396888) of [geometric configurations](wikipedia://en/7200539) are biregular; a biregular graph is the Levi graph of an (abstract) configuration if and only if its [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) is at least six.[^o12]
