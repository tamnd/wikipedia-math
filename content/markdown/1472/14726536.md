# Israel Lyons

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Israel Lyons"}

**Israel Lyons the Younger** (1739 – 1 May 1775) was a mathematician and botanist. He was born in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), the son of Israel Lyons the elder (died 1770). He was regarded as a prodigy, especially in mathematics, and [Robert Smith](wikipedia://en/447816), master of [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), took him under his wing and paid for his attendance.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was the son of Israel Lyons the elder, a teacher of Hebrew in the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), who wrote a Hebrew Grammar together with the Rev. Richard Grey, D. D., rector of [Hinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinton,_Northamptonshire) in Northamptonshire, in 1763.

Due to his Ashkenazi [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) origins, Lyons was not permitted to become an official member of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). Nevertheless, his brilliance resulted in his publication *Treatise on Fluxions* at the age of 19, and his enthusiasm for botany resulted in a published survey of Cambridge flora a few years later. An Oxford undergraduate, [Joseph Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Banks), paid Lyons to deliver a series of botany lectures at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford). Lyons was selected by the [Astronomer Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer_Royal) to compute astronomical tables for the [Nautical Almanac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nautical_Almanac). Later, Banks secured Lyons a position as the astronomer for the [1773 North Pole voyage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1773_Phipps_expedition_towards_the_North_Pole) led by [Constantine Phipps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_Phipps,_2nd_Baron_Mulgrave).

Lyons married, in March 1774, Phoebe Pearson, daughter of Newman Pearson of Over, Cambridgeshire, and settled in Rathbone Place, London. There he died of measles on 1 May 1775, at the age of only 36, while preparing a complete edition of [Edmond Halley](wikipedia://en/36858805)'s works sponsored by the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society).
