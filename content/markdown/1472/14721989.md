# Field arithmetic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Field arithmetic"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **field arithmetic** is a subject that studies the interrelations between arithmetic properties of a field\_(mathematics) and its [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781). It is an interdisciplinary subject as it uses tools from [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), the theory of [finite groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_groups) and of [profinite groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profinite_groups).

:::

## Fields with finite absolute Galois groups {#Fields_with_finite_absolute_Galois_groups}

Let *K* be a field and let *G* = Gal(*K*) be its absolute Galois group. If *K* is [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed), then *G* = 1. If *K* = **R** is the real numbers, then

$$G=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf{C}/\mathbf{R})=\mathbf{Z}/2 \mathbf{Z}.$$

Here **C** is the field of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) and **Z** is the ring of integer numbers. A [theorem of Artin and Schreier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin–Schreier_theorem) asserts that (essentially) these are all the possibilities for finite absolute Galois groups.

**Artin–Schreier theorem.** Let *K* be a field whose absolute Galois group *G* is finite. Then either *K* is separably closed and *G* is trivial or *K* is [real closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_closed) and *G* = **Z**/2**Z**.

## Fields that are defined by their absolute Galois groups {#Fields_that_are_defined_by_their_absolute_Galois_groups}

Some profinite groups occur as the absolute Galois group of non-isomorphic fields. A first example for this is

$$\hat{\mathbf{Z}}=\lim_{\longleftarrow}\mathbf{Z}/n \mathbf{Z}.$$

This group is isomorphic to the absolute Galois group of an arbitrary [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615). Also the absolute Galois group of the field of [formal Laurent series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_Laurent_series) **C**((*t*)) over the complex numbers is isomorphic to that group.

To get another example, we bring below two non-isomorphic fields whose absolute Galois groups are free (that is [free profinite group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_profinite_group)).

- Let *C* be an [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed) field and *x* a variable. Then Gal(*C*(*x*)) is free of rank equal to the cardinality of *C*. (This result is due to [Adrien Douady](wikipedia://en/1604604) for 0 characteristic and has its origins in [Riemann's existence theorem](wikipedia://en/78698035). For a field of arbitrary characteristic it is due to [David Harbater](wikipedia://en/3521313) and [Florian Pop](wikipedia://en/24789740), and was also proved later by [Dan Haran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Haran) and [Moshe Jarden](wikipedia://en/48527380).)
- The absolute Galois group Gal(**Q**) (where **Q** are the rational numbers) is compact, and hence equipped with a normalized [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645). For a Galois automorphism *s* (that is an element in Gal(**Q**)) let *N<sub>s</sub>* be the maximal Galois extension of ***Q*** that *s* fixes. Then with probability 1 the absolute Galois group Gal(*N*<sub>*s*</sub>) is free of countable rank. (This result is due to [Moshe Jarden](wikipedia://en/48527380).)

In contrast to the above examples, if the fields in question are finitely generated over ***Q***, [Florian Pop](wikipedia://en/24789740) proves that an isomorphism of the absolute Galois groups yields an isomorphism of the fields:

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.** Let *K*, *L* be finitely generated fields over ***Q*** and let *a*: Gal(*K*) → Gal(*L*) be an isomorphism. Then there exists a unique isomorphism of the algebraic closures, *b*: *K*<sub>alg</sub> → *L*<sub>alg</sub>, that induces *a*.

This generalizes an earlier work of [Jürgen Neukirch](wikipedia://en/17477195) and [Koji Uchida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koji_Uchida) on number fields.

:::

## Pseudo algebraically closed fields {#Pseudo_algebraically_closed_fields}

A [pseudo algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/3696313) (in short PAC) *K* is a field satisfying the following geometric property. Each [absolutely irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_irreducible) algebraic variety *V* defined over *K* has a *K*-[rational point](wikipedia://en/2960200).

Over PAC fields there is a firm link between arithmetic properties of the field and group theoretic properties of its absolute Galois group. A nice theorem in this spirit connects [Hilbertian fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbertian_field) with ω-free fields (*K* is ω-free if any [embedding problem](wikipedia://en/12692795) for *K* is properly solvable).

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.** Let *K* be a PAC field. Then *K* is Hilbertian if and only if *K* is ω-free.

[Peter Roquette](wikipedia://en/41199245) proved the right-to-left direction of this theorem and conjectured the opposite direction. [Michael Fried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fried_%28mathematician%29) and [Helmut Völklein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Völklein) applied algebraic topology and complex analysis to establish Roquette's conjecture in characteristic zero. Later Pop proved the Theorem for arbitrary characteristic by developing "[rigid patching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_patching)".

:::
