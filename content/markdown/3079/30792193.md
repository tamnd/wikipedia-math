# Roberto Marcolongo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Roberto Marcolongo"}

**Roberto Marcolongo** (August 28, 1862 in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) – May 16, 1943 in Rome) was an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his research in [vector calculus](wikipedia://en/32640) and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Marcolongo graduated in 1886, and later he was an assistant of Valentino Cerruti in Rome. In 1895 he became professor of [rational mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586) at the [University of Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Messina). In 1908 he moved to the [University of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples), where he remained until retirement in 1935.

He worked on [vector calculus](wikipedia://en/32640) together with [Cesare Burali-Forti](wikipedia://en/2452025), which was then known as "Italian notation". In 1906 he wrote an early work which used the [four-dimensional formalism](wikipedia://en/28758) to account for relativistic invariance under [Lorentz transformations](wikipedia://en/18404).

In 1921 he published to [Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messina) one of the first treaties on the special relativity and general, where he used the absolute differential calculus without coordinates, developed with Burali-Forti, as opposed to the absolute differential calculus with coordinates of [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180) and [Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro](wikipedia://en/499223).

He was a member of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) and other Italian academies.
