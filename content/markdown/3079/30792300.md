# Joel Shapiro (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Joel Shapiro"}

**Joel H. Shapiro** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), active in the field of [composition operators](wikipedia://en/2702039). He is the author of the book *Composition Operators and Classical Function Theory* (), and the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) memoir "Cyclic Phenomena for Composition Operators" (Memoirs of the American Math. Society #596, Vol. 125, 1997, pp. 1–105), with Paul Bourdon.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Shapiro completed his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) entitled "Linear Functionals on Non-Locally Convex Spaces" under the supervision of [Allen Shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Shields) in 1969 at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). Upon graduating, he became a research associate at [Queen's University, Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University,_Canada), then from 1970 onwards was at [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University), becoming a full [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in 1979. He stayed at Michigan State until 2006, when he retired and became an adjunct professor at [Portland State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_State_University) in [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon).

Shapiro discovered some of the properties of [composition operators](wikipedia://en/2702039), including a study of the cyclic properties of such operators[^o5] and the first calculations of the essential norm [^o6] for [composition operators](wikipedia://en/2702039) on the [Hardy spaces](wikipedia://en/314780) of the [Unit disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_disc).
