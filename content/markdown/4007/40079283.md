# Convex position

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Convex position"}

In [discrete](wikipedia://en/386468) and [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), a set of points in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) or a higher-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) is said to be in **convex position** or **convex independent** if none of the points can be represented as a [convex combination](wikipedia://en/794534) of the others. A [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742) of points is in convex position if all of the points are [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) of their [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634). More generally, a [family](wikipedia://en/723043) of [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292) is said to be in convex position if they are pairwise disjoint and none of them is contained in the convex hull of the others.

:::

An assumption of convex position can make certain computational problems easier to solve. For instance, the [traveling salesman problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling_salesman_problem), NP-hard for arbitrary sets of points in the plane, is trivial for points in convex position: the optimal tour is the convex hull. Similarly, the [minimum-weight triangulation](wikipedia://en/22231180) of planar point sets is NP-hard for arbitrary point sets,[^o8] but solvable in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) by [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297) for points in convex position.

The [Erdős–Szekeres theorem](wikipedia://en/3115543) guarantees that every set of $n$ points in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736) (no three in a line) in two or more dimensions has at least a logarithmic number of points in convex position. If $n$ points are chosen uniformly at random in a [unit square](wikipedia://en/285301), the probability that they are in convex position is

$$\left(\binom{2n-2}{n-1}/n!\right)^2.$$

The [McMullen problem](wikipedia://en/35695532) asks for the maximum number $\nu(d)$ such that every set of $\nu(d)$ points in general position in a $d$-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) has a [projective transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_transformation) to a set in convex position. Known bounds are $2d+1\le\nu(d)\le 2d+\lceil(d+1)/2\rceil$.
