# Disjunctive graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Disjunctive graph"}

In the [mathematical modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_modeling) of [job shop scheduling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_shop_scheduling) problems, **disjunctive graphs** are a way of modeling a system of tasks to be scheduled and timing constraints that must be respected by the schedule. They are [mixed graphs](wikipedia://en/38713429), in which [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) (representing tasks to be performed) may be connected by both directed and undirected edges (representing timing constraints between tasks). The two types of edges represent constraints of two different types:

:::

- If one task *x* must be performed earlier than a second task *y*, this constraint is represented by a directed edge from *x* to *y*.
- If, on the other hand, two tasks *x* and *y* can be performed in either order, but not simultaneously (perhaps because they both demand the use of the same equipment or other resource), this non-simultaneity constraint is represented by an undirected edge connecting *x* and *y*.

Pairs of tasks that have no constraint on their ordering – they can be performed in either order or even simultaneously – are disconnected from each other in the graph.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

A valid schedule for the disjunctive graph may be obtained by finding an [acyclic orientation](wikipedia://en/36619881) of the undirected edges – that is, deciding for each pair of non-simultaneous tasks which is to be first, without introducing any [circular dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_dependency) – and then ordering the resulting [directed acyclic graph](wikipedia://en/204002). In particular, suppose that all tasks have equal length and the goal is to find a schedule that minimizes the makespan, the total time until all tasks have been completed. In this case, the makespan can be computed from the [longest path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longest_path) in the oriented graph, which can be found in polynomial time for directed acyclic graphs. However, the orientation stage of the solution is much more difficult: it is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) to find the acyclic orientation that minimizes the length of the longest path. In particular, by the [Gallai–Hasse–Roy–Vitaver theorem](wikipedia://en/36620043), if all edges are initially undirected, then orienting them to minimize the longest path is equivalent to finding an optimal [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) of the initial undirected graph.
