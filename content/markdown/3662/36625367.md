# N = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="N = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory"}

***N* = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills** (**SYM**) **theory** is a [relativistic](wikipedia://en/26962) [conformally invariant](wikipedia://en/671875) [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/40752010) [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155) describing the interactions of [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion) via [gauge field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_field) exchanges. In *D*=4 [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) dimensions, *N*=4 is the maximal number of [supersymmetries](wikipedia://en/224636) or supersymmetry charges.[^o2]

:::

SYM theory is a [toy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toy_theory) based on [Yang–Mills theory](wikipedia://en/672202); it does not model the real world, but it is useful because it can act as a proving ground for approaches for attacking problems in more complex theories.[^o3] It describes a universe containing [boson fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosonic_field) and [fermion fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion_field) which are related by four [supersymmetries](wikipedia://en/224636) (this means that transforming bosonic and fermionic fields in a certain way leaves the theory invariant). It is one of the simplest (in the sense that it has no free parameters except for the [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group)) and one of the few [ultraviolet finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet_divergence) [quantum field theories](wikipedia://en/25267) in 4 dimensions. It can be thought of as the most symmetric field theory that does not involve gravity.

Like all supersymmetric field theories, SYM theory may equivalently be formulated as a [superfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfield) theory on an extended [superspace](wikipedia://en/432630) in which the spacetime variables are augmented by a number of [Grassmann variables](wikipedia://en/3117887) which, for the case *N*=4, consist of 4 [Dirac spinors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_spinor), making a total of 16 independent anticommuting generators for the extended [ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_%28algebra%29) of superfunctions. The [field equations](wikipedia://en/1410595) are equivalent to the geometric condition that the [supercurvature 2-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curvature_2-form) vanish identically on all super [null lines](wikipedia://en/1453977).[^o4][^o5] This is also known as the [super-ambitwistor correspondence](wikipedia://en/260836).

A similar super-ambitwistor characterization holds for *D*=10, *N*=1 dimensional super Yang–Mills theory,[^o6][^o7] and the lower dimensional cases *D*=6, *N*=2 and *D*=4, *N*=4 may be derived from this via [dimensional reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang-Mills_equations#Dimensional_reduction_and_other_moduli_spaces).

## Meaning of *N* and numbers of fields {#Meaning_of_N_and_numbers_of_fields}

In *N* supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory, *N* denotes the number of independent supersymmetric operations that transform the [spin](wikipedia://en/19593829)-1 gauge field into spin-1/2 fermionic fields.[^o9] In an analogy with symmetries under rotations, *N* would be the number of independent rotations, *N* = 1 in a plane, *N* = 2 in 3D space, etc... That is, in a *N* = 4 SYM theory, the gauge boson can be "rotated" into *N* = 4 different supersymmetric fermion partners. In turns, each fermion can be rotated into four different bosons: one corresponds to the rotation back to the spin-1 gauge field, and the three others are spin-0 boson fields. Because in 3D space one may use different rotations to reach a same point (or here the same spin-0 boson), each spin-0 boson is superpartners of two different spin-1/2 fermions, not just one.[^o9] So in total, one has only 6 spin-0 bosons, not 16.

Therefore, *N* = 4 SYM has 1 + 4 + 6 = 11 fields, namely: one vector field (the spin-1 gauge boson), four spinor fields (the spin-1/2 fermions) and six scalar fields (the spin-0 bosons). *N* = 4 is the maximum number of independent supersymmetries: starting from a spin-1 field and using more supersymmetries, e.g., *N* = 5, only rotates between the 11 fields. To have *N* > 4 independent supersymmetries, one needs to start from a gauge field of spin higher than 1, e.g., a spin-2 [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965) field such as that of the [graviton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graviton). This is the [*N* = 8 supergravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_=_8_supergravity) theory.

## Lagrangian {#Lagrangian}

The [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/40752010) for the theory is[^o2][^o11]

$$L = \operatorname{tr} \left\{-\frac{1}{2g^2}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\frac{\theta_I}{8\pi^2}F_{\mu\nu}\bar{F}^{\mu\nu}- i \overline{\lambda}^a\overline{\sigma}^\mu D_\mu \lambda_a -D_\mu X^i D^\mu X^i +g C^{ab}_i \lambda_a[X^i,\lambda_b] + g \overline{C}_{iab}\overline{\lambda}^a[X^i,\overline{\lambda}^b]+\frac{g^2}{2}[X^i,X^j]^2 \right\},$$

where $g$ and $\theta_I$ are [coupling constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling_constant) (specifically $g$ is the gauge coupling and $\theta_I$ is the [instanton](wikipedia://en/296077) angle), the [field strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_strength_tensor) is $F^k_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A^k_\nu-\partial_\nu A^k_\mu+f^{klm}A^l_\mu A^m_\nu$ with $A^k_\nu$ the gauge field and indices *i*,*j* = 1, ..., 6 as well as *a*, *b* = 1, ..., 4, and $f$ represents the [structure constants](wikipedia://en/20285142) of the particular gauge group. The $\lambda^a$ are left [Weyl fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_fermion), $\sigma^\mu$ are the [Pauli matrices](wikipedia://en/24868), $D_\mu$ is the [gauge covariant derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_covariant_derivative), $X^i$ are real scalars, and $C_i^{ab}$ represents the structure constants of the [R-symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-symmetry) group SU(4), which rotates the four supersymmetries. As a consequence of the [nonrenormalization theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonrenormalization_theorems), this supersymmetric field theory is in fact a [superconformal field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconformal_algebra).

## Ten-dimensional Lagrangian {#Ten-dimensional_Lagrangian}

The above Lagrangian can be found by beginning with the simpler ten-dimensional Lagrangian

$$L = \operatorname{tr} \left\{ \frac{1}{g^2} F_{IJ} F^{IJ} - i \bar{\lambda} \Gamma^I D_I \lambda \right\},$$

where I and J are now run from 0 through 9 and $\Gamma^I$ are the 32 by 32 gamma matrices $( 32=2^{10/2} )$, followed by adding the term with $\theta_I$ which is a [topological term](wikipedia://en/294349).

The components $A_i$ of the gauge field for *i* = 4 to 9 become scalars upon eliminating the extra dimensions. This also gives an interpretation of the SO(6) R-symmetry as rotations in the extra compact dimensions.

By compactification on a *T*<sup>6</sup>, all the [supercharges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercharges) are preserved, giving *N* = 4 in the 4-dimensional theory.

A [Type IIB string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_IIB_string_theory) interpretation of the theory is the worldvolume theory of a stack of [D3-branes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-brane).

## S-duality {#S-duality}

The coupling constants $\theta_I$ and $g$ naturally pair together into a single coupling constant

$$\tau := \frac{\theta_I}{2\pi}+\frac{4\pi i}{g^2}.$$

The theory has symmetries that shift $\tau$ by integers. The [S-duality conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montonen–Olive_duality) says there is also a symmetry which sends $\tau \mapsto \frac{-1}{n_G\tau}$ as well as switching the group $G$ to its [Langlands dual group](wikipedia://en/6789133).

## AdS/CFT correspondence {#AdS/CFT_correspondence}

This theory is also important[^o2] in the context of the [holographic principle](wikipedia://en/14286). There is a [duality](wikipedia://en/609737) between [Type IIB string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_IIB_string_theory) on AdS<sub>5</sub> × **S**<sup>5</sup> space (a product of 5-dimensional AdS space with a 5-dimensional [sphere](wikipedia://en/39782)) and *N* = 4 super Yang–Mills on the 4-dimensional boundary of AdS<sub>5</sub>. However, this particular realization of the AdS/CFT correspondence is not a realistic model of gravity, since gravity in our universe is 4-dimensional. Despite this, the AdS/CFT correspondence is the most successful realization of the holographic principle, a speculative idea about [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312) originally proposed by [Gerard 't Hooft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_'t_Hooft), who was expanding on work on black hole thermodynamics, and was improved and promoted in the context of string theory by [Leonard Susskind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Susskind).

## Integrability {#Integrability}

There is evidence that *[N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_supersymmetry)* = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory has an integrable structure in the planar [large *N* limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_N_limit) (see below for what "planar" means in the present context).[^o17] As the number of colors (also denoted *N*) goes to infinity, the amplitudes scale like $N^{2-2g}$, so that only the [genus 0 (planar graph)](wikipedia://en/24314) contribution survives. **Planar [Feynman diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_diagrams)** are graphs in which no [propagator](wikipedia://en/698759) cross over another one, in contrast to **non-planar Feynman graphs** where one or more propagator goes over another one.[^o18] A non-planar graph has a smaller number of possible gauge loops compared to a similar planar graph. Non-planar graphs are thus suppressed by factors $1/N^{2-2g}$ compared to planar ones which therefore dominate in the large *N* limit. Consequently, a **planar Yang–Mills theory** denotes a theory in the large *N* limit, with *N* usually the number of [colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge). Likewise, a **planar limit** is a limit in which scattering amplitudes are dominated by [Feynman diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_diagrams) which can be given the structure of planar graphs.[^o19] In the [large *N* limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_N_limit), the coupling $g$ vanishes and a [perturbative formalism](wikipedia://en/297069) is therefore well-suited for large *N* calculations. Therefore, planar graphs are associated to the domain where perturbative calculations converge well.

[Beisert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niklas_Beisert) et al. [^o20] give a review article demonstrating how in this situation local operators can be expressed via certain states in [spin chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_chain) (in particular the [Heisenberg spin chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_spin_chain)), but based on a larger [Lie superalgebra](wikipedia://en/297501) rather than $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ for ordinary spin. These spin chains are integrable in the sense that they can be solved by the [Bethe ansatz](wikipedia://en/4327884) method. They also construct an [action](wikipedia://en/854978) of the associated [Yangian](wikipedia://en/15272567) on [scattering amplitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_amplitudes).

[Nima Arkani-Hamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nima_Arkani-Hamed) et al. have also researched this subject. Using [twistor theory](wikipedia://en/260836), they find a description (the [amplituhedron](wikipedia://en/40564571) formalism) in terms of the positive [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810).[^o21]

:::

## Relation to 11-dimensional M-theory {#Relation_to_11-dimensional_M-theory}

*N* = 4 super Yang–Mills can be derived from a simpler 10-dimensional theory, and yet [supergravity](wikipedia://en/77139118) and [M-theory](wikipedia://en/20406) exist in 11 dimensions. The connection is that if the gauge group U(*N*) of SYM becomes infinite as $N\rightarrow \infty$ it becomes equivalent to an 11-dimensional theory known as [matrix theory](wikipedia://en/432648).

## Topologically twisted N = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory {#Topologically_twisted_N_=_4_supersymmetric_Yang–Mills_theory}

N = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory can be topologically twisted. Such a twist is a choice for how the four-dimensional [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231) $\operatorname{Spin}(4)=\operatorname{SU}(2)\times\operatorname{SU}(2)$ acts on the canonical inclusion [representation](wikipedia://en/19378200) $\mathbf{4}\colon\operatorname{SU}(4)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})$ of the [R-symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-symmetry) group, which relates the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) to the supersymmetry. Three inequivalent twists exists, which are the [Donaldson–Witten twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaldson–Witten_twist)$(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2})\oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})\oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})$, the [Vafa–Witten twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vafa–Witten_twist) $(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2})\oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2})$ and the [Kapustin–Witten twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapustin–Witten_twist)$(\mathbf{2},\mathbf{1})\oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2})$.[^o24]
