# Alessandro Figà Talamanca

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Alessandro Figà Talamanca"}

**Alessandro Figà Talamanca** (25 May 1938 – 27 November 2023) was an Italian mathematician who had been given several prestigious tasks, both in Italy and abroad. Several times, he took part in managing the [Italian University system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Italy) and shared his opinions in newspapers, such as *[La Repubblica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Repubblica).*He was a close friend of Carlo Pucci, a mathematician who spent most of his energy in improving the method of teaching maths in Italy, and the management of Italian Maths Departments. (Pucci was, especially, the re-founder of the [Istituto Nazionale di Alta Matematica Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/34249626).) From 1995 to 2003, Figà Talamanca, successor to Pucci, was President of the Istituto, and he continued what Pucci had set up. He was also Vice-President of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470).

:::

He was a member of the [Consiglio Universitario Nazionale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consiglio_Universitario_Nazionale) and, from 1999 to 2004, of the Comitato nazionale per la valutazione del sistema universitario.

## Career {#Career}

Figà Talamanca did research, and got valuable results, in the field of [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), on aleatory [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) and the diffusion process, mostly in Rome - at [La Sapienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Sapienza) -, but also in the US, especially in the Sixties. During that period on a [Fulbright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.-Italy_Fulbright_Commission) scholarship, he joined research activities in California, [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA), where he got his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.), in 1964, and, in the same year, was Acting Assistant Professor, and met the well-known French-American mathematician [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991), and also in Boston, Moore Instructor at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) from 1966 to 1968.

While teaching at MIT, he read [Tom M. Apostol](wikipedia://en/2196529)'s *[Calculus](wikipedia://en/81437851),* a two-volume book, and decided to bring it back to Italy - when he was to become professor at the [University of Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Università_di_Genova) - with him: he was the one who proposed an Italian edition of Apostol's work to a printer in Turin, and he himself overwatched the translation,[^o5] in 1977.

Talamanca was a lecturer at Berkeley, from 1968 to 1969, then at [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), from 1969 to 1970, then a visiting professor at [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_University), in [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Wales) and [Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney).

Talamanca taught calculus for 50 years, including both Italian and American universities. Mostly, he referred to La Sapienza: in 2007, he became General Director of the Maths Department there, but he was to leave in 2009 because he was 71.

Talamanca was able to speak English and [Modern Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Greek).

Talamanca had always been up against [Eugene Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Garfield)'s [Impact factor system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_factor) in Science.[^o6]

## Death {#Death}

Talamanca died on 27 November 2023, at the age of 85.[^o8]
