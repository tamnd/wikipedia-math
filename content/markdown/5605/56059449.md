# Polynomial mapping

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polynomial mapping"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **polynomial map** or **polynomial mapping** $P: V \to W$ between [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) over an infinite [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *k* is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) in [linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) with coefficients in *k*; i.e., it can be written as

$$P(v) = \sum_{i_1, \dots, i_n} \lambda_{i_1}(v) \cdots \lambda_{i_n}(v) w_{i_1, \dots, i_n}$$

where the $\lambda_{i_j}: V \to k$ are linear functionals and the $w_{i_1, \dots, i_n}$ are vectors in *W*. For example, if $W = k^m$, then a polynomial mapping can be expressed as $P(v) = (P_1(v), \dots, P_m(v))$ where the $P_i$ are (scalar-valued) [polynomial functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_function) on *V*. (The abstract definition has an advantage that the map is manifestly free of a choice of basis.)

:::

When *V*, *W* are [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) vector spaces and are viewed as [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties), then a polynomial mapping is precisely a [morphism of algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/24457573).
