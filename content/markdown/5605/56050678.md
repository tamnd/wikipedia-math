# Henry Heaton

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Henry Heaton"}

**Henry Heaton** (or **Henry C Heaton**) (1846–1927) was a North-American amateur [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who contributed problems and solutions to the then-new journals *The Analyst* (now *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)*) and *The [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)*. The *Annals* eventually became a leading research journal and the *Monthly* famous for its problems section.

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Heaton was a son of a millwright. In 1852 the family moved to [Greenfields, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenfields,_Pennsylvania), where Heaton attended the school four months every winter until he was fourteen years old. At the age of eighteen, he began his two careers, as a carpenter and as a teacher. He studied also for his BS in the [Mount Union College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Union_College) in Ohio in the course 1866–1867. In 1869 he moved to [Taylor, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Township,_Appanoose_County,_Iowa). After, he moved to [Des Moines, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines,_Iowa) where he met [Joel E. Hendricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_E._Hendricks), the founder and editor of *The Analyst* who encouraged him to publish mathematical problems and solutions in the journal.[^o4] In 1877 he and his family were living in [Sabula, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabula,_Iowa); in 1879 in [Atlantic, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic,_Iowa); in 1881 in [Lewis, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis,_Iowa); in 1906 in [Belfield, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfield,_North_Dakota); and, finally, he died in Biddle, Montana in 1927

From 1874 to 1918, Heaton published about one hundred solutions to mathematical problems in *The Analyst* and in *The American Mathematical Monthly*.[^o5]
