# Kazuya Kato

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kazuya Kato"}

**Kazuya Kato**[^o3] is a Japanese mathematician who works at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), as a professor, and specializes in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177). He is a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, elected in 2011. He received the 2005 Imperial Prize of the Japan Academy.[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Kazuya Kato grew up in the prefecture of [Wakayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakayama_Prefecture) in [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan). He attended college at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo), from which he also obtained his master's degree in 1975, and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in 1980.[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

Kato lectured, as a professor, at [Tokyo University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_University) in 1982, then from 1997 to 2001. He also taught at [Tokyo Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Institute_of_Technology) in 1992. He later became a faculty member at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 2009.[^o7]

A special volume of *[Documenta Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documenta_Mathematica)* was published in honor of his 50th birthday, together with research papers written by leading number theorists and former students it contains Kato's song on Prime Numbers.[^o8]

## Research and contributions {#Research_and_contributions}

Kato has researched and contributed to various areas in number theory and geometry, some of which include: the "Galois theory of fields, Iwasawa theory, p-adic cohomology, L-functions, K-theory and Hodge theory." He started the use of logarithmic structure to redefine Hodge structure as well.[^o4]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 2005, Kato received the [Imperial Prize of the Japan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Prize_of_the_Japan_Academy) for "Research on Arithmetic Geometry".[^o11]

## Books {#Books}

Kato has published several books in Japanese, of which some have already been translated into English.

He wrote a book on [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) and is also the coauthor of two volumes of the trilogy on Number Theory, which have been translated into English.[^o13]
