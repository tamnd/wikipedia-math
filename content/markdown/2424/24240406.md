# Holt graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Holt graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Holt graph** or **Doyle graph** is the smallest [half-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/24229305), that is, the smallest example of a [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581175) and [edge-transitive](wikipedia://en/584406) graph which is not also [symmetric](wikipedia://en/1577896).[^o3][^o4] Such graphs are not common.[^o5] It is named after Peter G. Doyle and Derek F. Holt, who discovered the same graph independently in 1976[^o6] and 1981[^o7] respectively.

:::

The Holt graph has [diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_diameter) 3, radius 3 and [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) 5, [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) 3, [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) 5 and is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph) with 98472 distinct Hamiltonian cycles. It is also a 4-[vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) and a 4-[edge-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-edge-connected_graph) graph. It has [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 3 and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 3.[^o9] The graph is not [1-planar](wikipedia://en/38249632).[^o10]

It has an [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/15094186) of order 54.[^o8] This is a smaller group than a symmetric graph with the same number of vertices and edges would have. The graph drawing on the right highlights this, in that it lacks reflectional symmetry.

The characteristic polynomial of the Holt graph is

$$(x^3-6x+2)^6(x+2)^4(x-1)^4(x-4).\$$
