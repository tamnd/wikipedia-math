# Zhu algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zhu algebra"}

In mathematics, the **Zhu algebra** and the closely related **C<sub>2</sub>-algebra**, introduced by Yongchang Zhu in his PhD thesis, are two [associative algebras](wikipedia://en/2112) canonically constructed from a given [vertex operator algebra](wikipedia://en/1287577).[^o4] Many important [representation theoretic](wikipedia://en/19378200) properties of the vertex algebra are logically related to properties of its Zhu algebra or C<sub>2</sub>-algebra.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let $V = \bigoplus_{n \ge 0} V_{(n)}$ be a graded [vertex operator algebra](wikipedia://en/1287577) with $V_{(0)} = \mathbb{C}\mathbf{1}$ and let $Y(a, z) = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} a_n z^{-n-1}$ be the vertex operator associated to $a \in V$. Define $C_2(V)\subset V$ to be the subspace spanned by elements of the form $a_{-2} b$ for $a,b \in V$. An element $a \in V$ is homogeneous with $\operatorname{wt} a = n$ if $a \in V_{(n)}$. There are two [binary operations](wikipedia://en/3948) on $V$ defined by

$$a * b = \sum_{i \ge 0} \binom{\operatorname{wt} a}{i} a_{i-1}b$$

,

$$a \circ b = \sum_{i \ge 0} \binom{\operatorname{wt}a}{i} a_{i-2} b$$

for homogeneous elements and extended linearly to all of $V$. Define $O(V)\subset V$ to be the span of all elements $a\circ b$.

The algebra $A(V) := V/O(V)$ with the binary operation induced by $*$ is an associative algebra called the *Zhu algebra* of $V$ [^o4].

The algebra $R_V := V/C_2(V)$ with multiplication $a\cdot b = a_{-1}b \mod C_2(V)$ is called the *C<sub>2</sub>-algebra* of $V$.

:::

## Main properties {#Main_properties}

- The multiplication of the C<sub>2</sub>-algebra is commutative and the additional binary operation $\{a,b\} = a_{0}b\mod C_2(V)$ is a [Poisson bracket](wikipedia://en/294340) on $R_V$which gives the C<sub>2</sub>-algebra the structure of a [Poisson algebra](wikipedia://en/345704).[^o4]
- (Zhu's *C*<sub>2</sub>-cofiniteness condition) If $R_V$is finite dimensional then $V$ is said to be *C<sub>2</sub>-cofinite.* There are two main representation theoretic properties related to C<sub>2</sub>-cofiniteness. A vertex operator algebra $V$ is *rational* if the category of admissible modules is semisimple and there are only finitely many irreducibles. It was conjectured that rationality is equivalent to C<sub>2</sub>-cofiniteness and a stronger condition *regularity,* however this was disproved in 2007 by Adamovic and Milas who showed that the triplet vertex operator algebra is C<sub>2</sub>-cofinite but not rational*.* [^o6][^o7][^o8] Various weaker versions of this conjecture are known, including that regularity implies C<sub>2</sub>-cofiniteness[^o6] and that for C<sub>2</sub>-cofinite $V$ the conditions of rationality and regularity are equivalent.[^o9] This conjecture is a vertex algebras analogue of [Cartan's criterion](wikipedia://en/3052668#Cartan's_criterion_for_semisimplicity) for [semisimplicity](wikipedia://en/1337587) in the theory of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) because it relates a structural property of the algebra to the semisimplicity of its representation [category](wikipedia://en/53759).
- The grading on $V$ induces a filtration $A(V) = \bigcup_{p \ge 0} A_p(V)$ where $A_p(V) = \operatorname{im}(\oplus_{j = 0}^p V_p\to A(V))$ so that $A_p(V) \ast A_q(V) \subset A_{p+q}(V).$ There is a surjective morphism of Poisson algebras $R_V \to \operatorname{gr}(A(V))$.[^o10]

## Associated variety {#Associated_variety}

Because the C<sub>2</sub>-algebra $R_V$ is a [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) it may be studied using the language of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). The *associated scheme* $\widetilde{X}_V$ and *associated variety* $X_V$ of $V$ are defined to be

$$\widetilde{X}_V := \operatorname{Spec}(R_V)$$

,

$$X_V := (\widetilde{X}_V)_{\mathrm{red}}$$

which are an [affine scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme) and an [affine algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/320468) respectively. [^o12] Moreover, since $L(-1)$ acts as a derivation on $R_V$[^o4] there is an [action](wikipedia://en/12781) of $\mathbb{C}^\ast$ on the associated scheme making *$\widetilde{X}_V$* a [conical](wikipedia://en/50394182) Poisson scheme and $X_V$ a conical Poisson variety. In this language, C<sub>2</sub>-cofiniteness is equivalent to the property that $X_V$ is a point.

::: {#o13 .example cue=inline-head name="Example"}

**Example:** If $W^k(\widehat{\mathfrak g}, f)$ is the affine [W-algebra](wikipedia://en/4791570) associated to [affine Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1350865) $\widehat{\mathfrak g}$ at level $k$ and nilpotent element $f$ then $\widetilde{X}_{W^k(\widehat{\mathfrak g}, f)} = \mathcal{S}_f$is the [Slodowy slice](wikipedia://en/10198492) through $f$.[^o14]

:::
