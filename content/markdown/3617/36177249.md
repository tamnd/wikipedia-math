# 3D4

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="3D4"}

In mathematics, the Steinberg triality [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) of type **<sup>3</sup>D<sub>4</sub>** form a family of [Steinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinberg_group_%28Lie_theory%29) or [twisted Chevalley groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twisted_Chevalley_group). They are [quasi-split](wikipedia://en/34287621) forms of D<sub>4</sub>, depending on a cubic [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* ⊂ *L*, and using the [triality](wikipedia://en/683109) automorphism of the [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109) D<sub>4</sub>. Unfortunately the notation for the group is not standardized, as some authors write it as <sup>3</sup>D<sub>4</sub>(*K*) (thinking of <sup>3</sup>D<sub>4</sub> as an [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) taking values in *K*) and some as <sup>3</sup>D<sub>4</sub>(*L*) (thinking of the group as a subgroup of D<sub>4</sub>(*L*) fixed by an [outer automorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_automorphism) of order 3). The group <sup>3</sup>D<sub>4</sub> is very similar to an [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/173954) or [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231) in dimension 8.

:::

Over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) these groups form one of the 18 infinite families of [finite simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group), and were introduced by (Steinberg 1959). They were independently discovered by [Jacques Tits](wikipedia://en/219570) in (Tits 1958) and (Tits 1959).

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) split algebraic group of type D<sub>4</sub> has a triality automorphism σ of order 3 coming from an order 3 [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) of its Dynkin diagram. If *L* is a field with an automorphism τ of order 3, then this induced an order 3 automorphism τ of the group D<sub>4</sub>(*L*). The group <sup>3</sup>D<sub>4</sub>(*L*) is the subgroup of D<sub>4</sub>(*L*) of points fixed by στ. It has three 8-dimensional representations over the field *L*, [permuted](wikipedia://en/44027) by the outer automorphism τ of order 3.

:::

## Over finite fields {#Over_finite_fields}

The group <sup>3</sup>D<sub>4</sub>(*q*<sup>3</sup>) has order *q*<sup>12</sup> (*q*<sup>8</sup> + *q*<sup>4</sup> + 1) (*q*<sup>6</sup> − 1) (*q*<sup>2</sup> − 1). For comparison, the split spin group D<sub>4</sub>(*q*) in dimension 8 has order *q*<sup>12</sup> (*q*<sup>8</sup> − 2*q*<sup>4</sup> + 1) (*q*<sup>6</sup> − 1) (*q*<sup>2</sup> − 1) and the quasisplit spin group <sup>2</sup>D<sub>4</sub>(*q*<sup>2</sup>) in dimension 8 has order *q*<sup>12</sup> (*q*<sup>8</sup> − 1) (*q*<sup>6</sup> − 1) (*q*<sup>2</sup> − 1).

The group <sup>3</sup>D<sub>4</sub>(*q*<sup>3</sup>) is always [simple](wikipedia://en/47600). The [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587) is always trivial. The [outer automorphism group](wikipedia://en/628466) is [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) of order *f* where *q*<sup>3</sup> = *p<sup>f</sup>* and *p* is [prime](wikipedia://en/23666).

This group is also sometimes called <sup>3</sup>*D*<sub>4</sub>(*q*), *D*<sub>4</sub><sup>2</sup>(*q*<sup>3</sup>), or a twisted Chevalley group.

## <sup>3</sup>D<sub>4</sub>(2<sup>3</sup>) {#3D4(23)}

The smallest member of this family of groups has several exceptional properties not shared by other members of the family. It has order 211341312 = 2<sup>12</sup>⋅3<sup>4</sup>⋅7<sup>2</sup>⋅13 and outer automorphism group of order 3.

The automorphism group of <sup>3</sup>D<sub>4</sub>(2<sup>3</sup>) is a maximal subgroup of the [Thompson sporadic group](wikipedia://en/614080), and is also a subgroup of the compact [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) of type F<sub>4</sub> of dimension 52. In particular it acts on the 26-dimensional representation of F<sub>4</sub>. In this representation it fixes a 26-dimensional lattice that is the unique 26-dimensional even lattice of determinant 3 with no norm 2 vectors, studied by (Elkies Gross 1996). The dual of this lattice has 819 pairs of vectors of norm 8/3, on which <sup>3</sup>D<sub>4</sub>(2<sup>3</sup>) acts as a rank 4 [permutation group](wikipedia://en/24634).
