# Hahn polynomials

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hahn polynomials"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Hahn polynomials** are a family of [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) in the [Askey scheme](wikipedia://en/32649081) of hypergeometric orthogonal polynomials, introduced by [Pafnuty Chebyshev](wikipedia://en/149568)[^n1] and rediscovered by [Wolfgang Hahn](wikipedia://en/32782993).[^n2] The **Hahn class** is a name for special cases of Hahn polynomials, such as [Meixner polynomials](wikipedia://en/19609650), [Krawtchouk polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krawtchouk_polynomials), and [Charlier polynomials](wikipedia://en/19585960). Sometimes the Hahn class is taken to include [limiting cases](wikipedia://en/40400729) of these polynomials, in which case it also includes the [classical orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/336568).

:::

Hahn polynomials are defined in terms of [generalized hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/361184) by[^n3]

$$Q_n(x;\alpha,\beta,N)= {}_3F_2(-n,-x,n+\alpha+\beta+1;\alpha+1,-N+1;1)$$

for $n=0,1,\dots,N$. If $\alpha = \beta = 0$, these polynomials are identical to the [discrete Chebyshev polynomials](wikipedia://en/26893761) up to a scale factor.

Hahn polynomials have [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_Hahn_polynomials), [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_Hahn_polynomials) and [continuous dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_Hahn_polynomials) versions. These polynomials all have [*q*-analogs](wikipedia://en/1619396). Hahn polynomials are a limiting case of [Racah polynomials](wikipedia://en/19595494).[^n4]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Orthogonality {#Orthogonality}

Hahn polynomials satisfy the orthogonality relations

$$\sum_{x=0}^{N-1} Q_n(x)Q_m(x)\rho(x)=\frac{1}{\pi_n}\delta_{m,n},\quad \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}Q_n(x)Q_n(y)\pi_n=\frac{1}{\rho(x)}\delta_{x,y},$$

where $\delta_{x,y}$ is the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890) and the [weight functions](wikipedia://en/245523) are

$$\rho(x;\alpha;\beta,N)=\binom{\alpha+x}{x}\binom{\beta+N-1-x}{N-1-x}\bigg/\binom{N+\alpha+\beta}{N-1}$$

and

$$\pi_n(\alpha,\beta,N)=\binom{N-1}{n}\frac{2n+\alpha+\beta+1}{\alpha+\beta+1} \frac{\Gamma(\beta+1,n+\alpha+1,n+\alpha+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1,\alpha+\beta+1,n+\beta+1,n+1)}\bigg/\binom{N+\alpha+\beta+n}{n}.$$

:::

## Dual version {#Dual_version}

Hahn polynomials have a dual version, the **dual Hahn polynomials**, which are also a family of [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) in the [Askey scheme](wikipedia://en/32649081) of hypergeometric orthogonal polynomials. They are defined on a non-uniform [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) $x(s)=s(s+1)$ by

$$w_n^{(c)} (s,a,b)=\frac{(a-b+1)_n(a+c+1)_n}{n!}\cdot {}_3F_2\left( \begin{array}{c} -n\;\quad a-s\;\quad a+s+1 \\ -b\;\quad a+c+1 \end{array} ;1\right)$$

for $n=0,1,...,N-1$, and the parameters $a,b,c$ are restricted to

$$-\frac{1}{2}<a<b,\quad |c|<1+a,\quad b=a+N.$$

Here $(u)_k$ is the [rising factorial](wikipedia://en/230193), and ${}_3F_2$ is a [generalized hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/361184).

(txt, 14) give a detailed list of their properties.

### Orthogonality {#Orthogonality}

The dual Hahn polynomials satisfy the orthogonality condition

$$\sum^{b-1}_{s=a}w_n^{(c)}(s,a,b)w_m^{(c)}(s,a,b)\rho(s)\Delta x\Big(s-\frac{1}{2}\Big)=\delta_{nm}d_n^2$$

for $n,m=0,1,...,N-1$, where $\Delta x(s)=x(s+1)-x(s)$,

$$\rho(s)=\frac{\Gamma(a+s+1)\Gamma(c+s+1)}{\Gamma(s-a+1)\Gamma(b-s)\Gamma(b+s+1)\Gamma(s-c+1)}$$

and

$$d_n^2=\frac{\Gamma(a+c+n+a)}{n!(b-a-n-1)!\Gamma(b-c-n)}.$$

### Numerical instability {#Numerical_instability}

As $n$ increases, the values that the discrete polynomials attain increase. As a result, obtaining [numerical stability](wikipedia://en/233807) in calculating the polynomials requires the use of renormalized dual Hahn polynomials, defined as

$$\hat w_n^{(c)}(s,a,b)=w_n^{(c)}(s,a,b)\sqrt{\frac{\rho(s)}{d_n^2}\Delta x\Big(s-\frac{1}{2}\Big)}$$

for $n=0,1,...,N-1$. Here the orthogonality condition becomes

$$\sum^{b-1}_{s=a}\hat w_n^{(c)}(s,a,b)\hat w_m^{(c)}(s,a,b)=\delta_{m,n}$$

for $n,m=0,1,...,N-1.$

## Continuous version {#Continuous_version}

Hahn polynomials have a continuous version, the **continuous Hahn polynomials**, which are also a family of [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) in the [Askey scheme](wikipedia://en/32649081) of hypergeometric orthogonal polynomials. They are defined in terms of [generalized hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/361184) by

$$p_n(x;a,b,c,d)= i^n\frac{(a+c)_n(a+d)_n}{n!} \cdot {}_3F_2\left( \begin{array}{c} -n\;\quad n+a+b+c+d-1\;\quad a+ix \\ a+c\;\quad a+d \end{array} ; 1\right)$$

(txt, 14) give a detailed list of their properties.

### Orthogonality {#Orthogonality}

The continuous Hahn polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the [weight function](wikipedia://en/245523)

$$w(x)=\Gamma(a+ix)\,\Gamma(b+ix)\,\Gamma(c-ix)\,\Gamma(d-ix).$$

In particular, they satisfy the orthogonality relation[^n5][^o17][^n6]

$$\begin{aligned}&\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Gamma(a+ix)\,\Gamma(b+ix)\,\Gamma(c-ix)\,\Gamma(d-ix)\,p_m(x;a,b,c,d)\,p_n(x;a,b,c,d)\,dx\\ &\qquad\qquad=\frac{\Gamma(n+a+c)\,\Gamma(n+a+d)\,\Gamma(n+b+c)\,\Gamma(n+b+d)}{n!(2n+a+b+c+d-1)\,\Gamma(n+a+b+c+d-1)}\,\delta_{n m}\end{aligned}$$

for $\Re(a)>0$, $\Re(b)>0$, $\Re(c)>0$, $\Re(d)>0$, $c = \overline{a}$, $d = \overline{b}$.

### Recurrence and difference relations {#Recurrence_and_difference_relations}

The sequence of continuous Hahn polynomials satisfies the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)[^n5]

$$(a+ix)\tilde{p}_n(x)=A_n\tilde{p}_{n+1}(x) - (A_n+C_n)\tilde{p}_{n}(x) + C_n\tilde{p}_{n-1}(x),$$

where

$$\begin{aligned} &\tilde{p}_n(x)=\tilde{p}_n(x;a,b,c,d)=\frac{n!}{i^n(a+c)_n(a+d)_n}p_n(x;a,b,c,d),\\[6pt] &A_n=\frac{-(n+a+b+c+d-1)(n+a+c)(n+a+d)}{(2n+a+b+c+d-1)(2n+a+b+c+d)}, \end{aligned}$$

and

$$C_n=\frac{n(n+b+c-1)(n+b+d-1)}{(2n+a+b+c+d-2)(2n+a+b+c+d-1)}.$$

::: {#o22 .statement cue=named-heading name="Rodrigues formula"}

### Rodrigues formula {#Rodrigues_formula}

The continuous Hahn polynomials are given by the Rodrigues-type formula[^n7]

$$\begin{aligned}&\Gamma(a+ix)\,\Gamma(b+ix)\,\Gamma(c-ix)\,\Gamma(d-ix)\,p_n(x;a,b,c,d)\\[5pt] &\quad=\frac{(-1)^n}{n!}\frac{d^n}{dx^n}\left(\Gamma\left(a+\frac{n}{2}+ix\right)\,\Gamma\left(b+\frac{n}{2}+ix\right)\,\Gamma\left(c+\frac{n}{2}-ix\right)\,\Gamma\left(d+\frac{n}{2}-ix\right)\right).\end{aligned}$$

:::

### Generating functions {#Generating_functions}

The continuous Hahn polynomials have the following generating function:[^n7]

$$\begin{aligned} &\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n+a+b+c+d)\,\Gamma(a+c+1)\,\Gamma(a+d+1)}{\Gamma(a+b+c+d)\,\Gamma(n+a+c+1)\,\Gamma(n+a+d+1)}(-it)^n p_n(x;a,b,c,d)\\[4pt] &\qquad=(1-t)^{1-a-b-c-d}{}_3F_2\left( \begin{array}{c} \frac12(a+b+c+d-1), \frac12(a+b+c+d), a+ix\\ a+c, a+d\end{array} ; -\frac{4t}{(1-t)^2} \right).\end{aligned}$$

A second, distinct generating function is given by

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a+c+1)\,\Gamma(b+d+1)}{\Gamma(n+a+c+1)\,\Gamma(n+b+d+1)}t^n p_n(x;a,b,c,d)=\,_1F_1\left( \begin{array}{c} a + ix \\ a + c\end{array} ; -it\right)\,_1F_1\left( \begin{array}{c} d - ix \\ b + d\end{array} ; it\right).$$

### Relation of continuous Hahn polynomials to other polynomials {#Relation_of_continuous_Hahn_polynomials_to_other_polynomials}

- The [Wilson polynomials](wikipedia://en/19585496) are a generalization of the continuous Hahn polynomials.
- The [Bateman polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bateman_polynomials) *F*<sub>*n*</sub>(x) are related to the special case *a*=*b*=*c*=*d*=1/2 of the continuous Hahn polynomials by

$$p_n\left(x;\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12\right) = i^n n!F_n\left(2ix\right).$$

- The [Jacobi polynomials](wikipedia://en/30863587) *P*<sub>*n*</sub><sup>(α,β)</sup>(x) can be obtained as a limiting case of the continuous Hahn polynomials:[^o27]

$$P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}=\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-n}p_n\left(\tfrac12xt; \tfrac12(\alpha+1-it), \tfrac12(\beta+1+it), \tfrac12(\alpha+1+it), \tfrac12(\beta+1-it)\right).$$

## Continuous dual Hahn polynomials {#Continuous_dual_Hahn_polynomials}

In mathematics, the **continuous dual Hahn polynomials** are a family of [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) in the [Askey scheme](wikipedia://en/32649081) of hypergeometric orthogonal polynomials. They are defined in terms of [generalized hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/361184) by

*Image: Continuous dual Hahn 1.gif.* Continuous Dual Hahn polynomials

$$S_n(x^2;a,b,c)= {}_3F_2(-n,a+ix,a-ix;a+b,a+c;1).\$$

*Image: Complex plot of Continuous Duall Hahn Polynomials.gif.* Continuous Dual Hahn Polynomials, complex3d plot

(txt, 14) give a detailed list of their properties.

Closely related polynomials include the [dual Hahn polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_Hahn_polynomials) *R*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*;γ,δ,*N*), the [continuous Hahn polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_Hahn_polynomials) *p*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*,*a*,*b*, $\overline{a}$, $\overline{b}$), and the [Hahn polynomials](wikipedia://en/19585658). These polynomials all have *q*-analogs with an extra parameter *q*, such as the [q-Hahn polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-Hahn_polynomials) *Q*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*;α,β, *N*;*q*), and so on.

- [Wilson polynomials](wikipedia://en/19585496) are a generalization of continuous dual Hahn polynomials

## *q*-Hahn polynomials {#q-Hahn_polynomials}

In mathematics, the ***q*-Hahn polynomials** are a family of basic hypergeometric [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) in the basic [Askey scheme](wikipedia://en/32649081). (txt, 14) give a detailed list of their properties.

::: {#o35 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

The polynomials are given in terms of [basic hypergeometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_hypergeometric_function) by

$$Q_n(q^{-x};a,b,N;q)={}_3\phi_2\left[\begin{matrix} q^{-n},abq^{n+1},q^{-x}\\ aq,q^{-N}\end{matrix} ;q,q\right].$$

:::

### Dual *q*-Hahn polynomials {#Dual_q-Hahn_polynomials}

In mathematics, the **dual *q*-Hahn polynomials** are a family of basic [hypergeometric](wikipedia://en/2055223) [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) in the basic [Askey scheme](wikipedia://en/32649081). (txt, 14) give a detailed list of their properties.

::: {#o39 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

#### Definition {#Definition}

The polynomials are given in terms of [basic hypergeometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_hypergeometric_function). $R_n(q^{-x}+\gamma\delta q^{x+1},\gamma,\delta,N|q)={}_3\phi_2\left[\begin{matrix} q^{-n},q^{-x},\gamma\delta q^{x+1}\\ \gamma q,q^{-N}\end{matrix} ;q,q\right],\quad n=0,1,2,...,N$

:::

### Continuous *q*-Hahn polynomials {#Continuous_q-Hahn_polynomials}

In mathematics, the **continuous *q*-Hahn polynomials** are a family of basic hypergeometric [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) in the basic [Askey scheme](wikipedia://en/32649081). (txt, 14) give a detailed list of their properties.

::: {#o43 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

#### Definition {#Definition}

The polynomials are given in terms of [basic hypergeometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_hypergeometric_function) and the [q-Pochhammer symbol](wikipedia://en/11149717) by [^o44]

$$p_n(x;a,b,c,d|q)=a^{-n}e^{-inu}(abe^{2iu},ac,ad;q)_n{}_4\phi_3(q^{-n},abcdq^{n-1},ae^{i{(t+2u)}},ae^{-it};abe^{2iu},ac,ad;q;q)$$

$$x=\cos(t+u)$$

:::

### Relation of *q*-Hahn polynomials to other polynomials {#Relation_of_q-Hahn_polynomials_to_other_polynomials}

q-Hahn polynomials→ [Quantum q-Krawtchouk polynomials](wikipedia://en/32848716)：

$$\lim_{a \to \infty}Q_{n}(q^{-x};a;p,N|q)=K_{n}^{qtm}(q^{-x};p,N;q)$$

q-Hahn polynomials→ [Hahn polynomials](wikipedia://en/19585658)

make the substitution$\alpha=q^{\alpha}$,$\beta=q^{\beta}$ into definition of q-Hahn polynomials, and find the limit q→1, we obtain

${}_3F_2(-n,\alpha+\beta+n+1,-x,\alpha+1,-N,1)$，which is exactly [Hahn polynomials](wikipedia://en/19585658).

### Continuous dual *q*-Hahn polynomials {#Continuous_dual_q-Hahn_polynomials}

In mathematics, the **continuous dual *q*-Hahn polynomials** are a family of basic hypergeometric [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) in the basic [Askey scheme](wikipedia://en/32649081). (txt, 14) give a detailed list of their properties.

::: {#o50 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

#### Definition {#Definition}

The polynomials are given in terms of [basic hypergeometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_hypergeometric_function) and the [q-Pochhammer symbol](wikipedia://en/11149717) by [^o51]

$$p_n(x;a,b,c\mid q)=\frac{(ab,ac;q)_n}{a^n}{_3\phi_2}(q^{-n},ae^{i\theta},ae^{-i\theta}; ab, ac \mid q;q)$$

In which $x=\cos(\theta)$
