# Universally Baire set

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Universally Baire set"}

In the mathematical field of [descriptive set theory](wikipedia://en/396619), a set of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) (or more generally a subset of the [Baire space](wikipedia://en/2078245) or [Cantor space](wikipedia://en/352376)) is called **universally Baire** if it has a certain strong regularity property. Universally Baire sets play an important role in [Ω-logic](wikipedia://en/13772472), a very strong logical system invented by [W. Hugh Woodin](wikipedia://en/2275071) and the centerpiece of his argument against the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705) of [Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A subset *A* of the Baire space is universally Baire if it has the following equivalent properties:

1. For every notion of [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205), there are [trees](wikipedia://en/2190732) *T* and *U* such that *A* is the projection of the set of all branches through *T*, and it is forced that the projections of the branches through *T* and the branches through *U* are [complements](wikipedia://en/54347) of each other.
1. For every [compact Hausdorff space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Hausdorff_space) Ω, and every [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) *f* from Ω to the Baire space, the [preimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimage) of *A* under *f* has the [property of Baire](wikipedia://en/2213768) in Ω.
1. For every cardinal λ and every continuous function *f* from λ<sup>ω</sup> to the Baire space, the preimage of *A* under *f* has the property of Baire.
