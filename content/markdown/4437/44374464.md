# Kadison transitivity theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kadison transitivity theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Kadison transitivity theorem** is a result in the theory of [C\*-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*-algebras) that, in effect, asserts the equivalence of the notions of topological irreducibility and [algebraic irreducibility](wikipedia://en/385982) of representations of C\*-algebras. It implies that, for irreducible representations of C\*-algebras, the only non-zero linear invariant subspace is the whole space.

:::

The theorem, proved by [Richard Kadison](wikipedia://en/25798650), was surprising as *a priori* there is no reason to believe that all topologically irreducible representations are also algebraically irreducible.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

A family $\mathcal{F}$ of [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $\mathcal{H}$ is said to act *topologically irreducibly* when $\{0\}$ and $\mathcal{H}$ are the only closed stable subspaces under $\mathcal{F}$. The family $\mathcal{F}$ is said to act *algebraically irreducibly* if $\{0\}$ and $\mathcal{H}$ are the only linear manifolds in $\mathcal{H}$ stable under $\mathcal{F}$.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**. [^o5] If the C\*-algebra $\mathfrak{A}$ acts topologically irreducibly on the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}, \{ y_1, \cdots, y_n \}$ is a set of vectors and $\{x_1, \cdots, x_n \}$ is a linearly independent set of vectors in $\mathcal{H}$, there is an $A$ in $\mathfrak{A}$ such that $Ax_j = y_j$. If $Bx_j = y_j$ for some [self-adjoint operator](wikipedia://en/187408) $B$, then $A$ can be chosen to be self-adjoint.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=inline-head name="Corollary"}

**Corollary**. If the C\*-algebra $\mathfrak{A}$ acts topologically irreducibly on the Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$, then it acts algebraically irreducibly.

:::

:::
