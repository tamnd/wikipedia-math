# Choquet integral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Choquet integral"}

A **Choquet integral** is a [subadditive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subadditive) or [superadditive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superadditive) integral created by the French mathematician [Gustave Choquet](wikipedia://en/17301193) in 1953.[^o2] It was initially used in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) and [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342),[^o3] but found its way into [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216) in the 1980s,[^o4] where it is used as a way of measuring the expected [utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_theory) of an uncertain event. It is applied specifically to [membership functions](wikipedia://en/1808639) and [capacities](wikipedia://en/14368398). In [imprecise probability theory](wikipedia://en/3255074), the Choquet integral is also used to calculate the lower expectation induced by a 2-monotone [lower probability](wikipedia://en/3011778), or the upper expectation induced by a 2-alternating [upper probability](wikipedia://en/3011778).

:::

Using the Choquet integral to denote the expected utility of belief functions measured with capacities is a way to reconcile the [Ellsberg paradox](wikipedia://en/1912480) and the [Allais paradox](wikipedia://en/3223960).[^o5][^o6]

[Multiobjective optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiobjective_optimization) problems seek [Pareto optimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_optimal) solutions, but the Pareto set of such solutions can be extremely large, especially with multiple objectives. To manage this, optimization often focuses on a specific function, such as a [weighted sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_sum), which typically results in solutions forming a [convex envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_envelope) of the feasible set. However, to capture non-convex solutions, alternative [aggregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggregation_problem) operators like the Choquet integral can be used.[^o7]

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The following notation is used:

- $S$ – a set.
- $\mathcal{F}$ – a collection of subsets of $S$.
- $f : S\to \mathbb{R}$ – a function.
- $\nu : \mathcal{F}\to \mathbb{R}^+$ – a monotone [set function](wikipedia://en/25911217).

Assume that $f$ is measurable with respect to $\mathcal{F}$, that is

$$\forall x\in\mathbb{R}\colon \{s \in S \mid f (s) \geq x\}\in\mathcal{F}$$

Then the Choquet integral of $f$ with respect to $\nu$ is defined by:

$$(C)\int f d\nu := \int_{-\infty}^0 (\nu (\{s | f (s) \geq x\})-\nu(S))\, dx + \int^\infty_0 \nu (\{s | f (s) \geq x\})\, dx$$

where the integrals on the right-hand side are the usual [Riemann integral](wikipedia://en/26390) (the integrands are integrable because they are monotone in $x$).

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

In general the Choquet integral does not satisfy additivity. More specifically, if $\nu$ is not a probability measure, it may hold that

$$\int f \,d\nu + \int g \,d\nu \neq \int (f + g)\, d\nu.$$

for some functions $f$ and $g$.

The Choquet integral does satisfy the following properties.

### Monotonicity {#Monotonicity}

If $f\leq g$ then

$$(C)\int f\, d\nu \leq (C)\int g\, d\nu$$

### Positive homogeneity {#Positive_homogeneity}

For all $\lambda\ge 0$ it holds that

$$(C)\int \lambda f \,d\nu = \lambda (C)\int f\, d\nu,$$

### Comonotone additivity {#Comonotone_additivity}

If $f,g : S \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ are comonotone functions, that is, if for all $s,s' \in S$ it holds that

$$(f(s) - f(s')) (g(s) - g(s')) \geq 0.$$

which can be thought of as $f$ and $g$ rising and falling together

then

$$(C)\int\, f d\nu + (C)\int g\, d\nu = (C)\int (f + g)\, d\nu.$$

### Subadditivity {#Subadditivity}

If $\nu$ is 2-alternating, then

$$(C)\int\, f d\nu + (C)\int g\, d\nu \ge (C)\int (f + g)\, d\nu.$$

### Superadditivity {#Superadditivity}

If $\nu$ is 2-monotone, then

$$(C)\int\, f d\nu + (C)\int g\, d\nu \le (C)\int (f + g)\, d\nu.$$

:::

## Alternative representation {#Alternative_representation}

Let $G$ denote a [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) such that $G^{-1}$ is $d H$ integrable. Then this following formula is often referred to as Choquet Integral:

$$\int_{-\infty}^\infty G^{-1}(\alpha) d H(\alpha) = -\int_{-\infty}^a H(G(x))dx+ \int_a^\infty \hat{H}(1-G(x)) dx,$$

where $\hat{H}(x)=H(1)-H(1-x)$.

- choose $H(x):=x$ to get $\int_0^1 G^{-1}(x)dx = E[X]$,
- choose $H(x):=1_{[\alpha,x]}$ to get $\int_0^1 G^{-1}(x)dH(x)= G^{-1}(\alpha)$

## Applications {#Applications}

The Choquet integral was applied in image processing, video processing and computer vision. In behavioral decision theory, [Amos Tversky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Tversky) and [Daniel Kahneman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kahneman) use the Choquet integral and related methods in their formulation of cumulative prospect theory.[^o20]
