# Clarke generalized derivative

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clarke generalized derivative"}

In mathematics, the **Clarke generalized derivatives** are generalized types of [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921) that allow for the differentiation of nonsmooth functions. The Clarke derivatives were introduced by [Francis Clarke](wikipedia://en/61726385) in 1975.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

For a [locally Lipschitz continuous](wikipedia://en/51731) function $f: \mathbb{R}^{n} \rightarrow \mathbb{R},$ the *Clarke generalized directional derivative* of $f$ at $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ in the direction $v \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is defined as

$$f^{\circ} (x, v)= \limsup_{y \rightarrow x, h \downarrow 0} \frac{f(y+ hv)-f(y)}{h},$$

where $\limsup$ denotes the [limit supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_supremum).

Then, using the above definition of $f^{\circ}$, the *Clarke generalized gradient* of $f$ at $x$ (also called the *Clarke [subdifferential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdifferential)*) is given as

$$\partial^{\circ}\! f(x):=\{\xi \in \mathbb{R}^{n}: \langle\xi, v\rangle \leq f^{\circ}(x, v), \forall v \in \mathbb{R}^{n}\},$$

where $\langle \cdot, \cdot\rangle$ represents an [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) of vectors in $\mathbb{R}.$ Note that the Clarke generalized gradient is set-valued—that is, at each $x \in \mathbb{R}^n,$ the function value $\partial^{\circ}\! f(x)$ is a set.

More generally, given a Banach space $X$ and a subset $Y \subset X,$ the Clarke generalized directional derivative and generalized gradients are defined as above for a [locally Lipschitz continuous](wikipedia://en/51731) function $f : Y \to \mathbb{R}.$
