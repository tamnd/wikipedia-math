# Implicit surface

*Image: Torus-40-15.svg.* Implicit surface torus $(R = 40, a = 15)$.

*Image: Impl-flaeche-geschl2.svg.* Implicit surface of genus 2.

*Image: Impl-flaeche-weinglas.svg.* Implicit non-algebraic surface (*wineglass*).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Implicit surface"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **implicit surface** is a [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_%28geometry%29) in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) defined by an equation

:::

$$F(x,y,z)=0.$$

An *implicit surface* is the set of [zeros](wikipedia://en/264210) of a [function of three variables](wikipedia://en/39783039). *[Implicit](wikipedia://en/250896)* means that the equation is not solved for $x$ or $y$ or $z$.

The graph of a function is usually described by an equation $z=f(x,y)$ and is called an *explicit* representation. The third essential description of a surface is the *[parametric](wikipedia://en/576108)* one: $(x(s,t),y(s,t), z(s,t))$, where the $x$-, $y$- and $z$-coordinates of surface points are represented by three functions $x(s,t)\, , y(s,t)\, , z(s,t)$ depending on common parameters $s,t$. Generally the change of representations is simple only when the explicit representation $z=f(x,y)$ is given: $z-f(x,y)=0$ (implicit), $(s,t,f(s,t))$ (parametric).

*Examples*:

1. The [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) $x+2y-3z+1=0.$
1. The [sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_%28geometry%29) $x^2+y^2+z^2-4=0.$
1. The [torus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torus_%28mathematics%29) $(x^2+y^2+z^2+R^2-a^2)^2-4R^2(x^2+y^2)=0.$
1. A surface of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) 2: $2y(y^2-3x^2)(1-z^2)+(x^2+y^2)^2-(9z^2-1)(1-z^2)=0$ (see diagram).
1. The [surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549) $x^2+y^2-(\ln(z+3.2))^2-0.02=0$ (see diagram *wineglass*).

For a plane, a sphere, and a torus there exist simple parametric representations. This is not true for the fourth example.

The [implicit function theorem](wikipedia://en/581005) describes conditions under which an equation $F(x,y,z)=0$ can be solved (at least implicitly) for $x$, $y$ or $z$. But in general the solution may not be made explicit. This theorem is the key to the computation of essential geometric features of a surface: [tangent planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_plane), [surface normals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_normals), [curvatures](wikipedia://en/285623) (see below). But they have an essential drawback: their visualization is difficult.

If $F(x,y,z)$ is polynomial in $x$, $y$ and $z$, the surface is called [algebraic](wikipedia://en/648311). Example 5 is *non*-algebraic.

Despite difficulty of visualization, implicit surfaces provide relatively simple techniques to generate theoretically (e.g. [Steiner surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steiner_surface)) and practically (see below) interesting surfaces.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formulas"}

## Formulas {#Formulas}

Throughout the following considerations the implicit surface is represented by an equation $F(x,y,z)=0$ where function $F$ meets the necessary conditions of differentiability. The [partial derivatives](wikipedia://en/52565) of $F$ are $F_x,F_y,F_z,F_{xx},\ldots$.

### Tangent plane and normal vector {#Tangent_plane_and_normal_vector}

A surface point $(x_0, y_0,z_0)$ is called **regular** [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461) of $F$ at $(x_0, y_0,z_0)$ is not the zero vector $(0, 0, 0)$, meaning

$$(F_x(x_0,y_0,z_0),F_y(x_0,y_0,z_0),F_z(x_0,y_0,z_0))\ne (0,0,0).$$

If the surface point $(x_0, y_0,z_0)$ is *not* regular, it is called **singular**.

The equation of the tangent plane at a regular point $(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ is

$$F_x(x_0,y_0,z_0)(x-x_0)+F_y(x_0,y_0,z_0)(y-y_0)+F_z(x_0,y_0,z_0)(z-z_0)=0,$$

and a *normal vector* is

$$\mathbf n(x_0,y_0,z_0)=(F_x(x_0,y_0,z_0),F_y(x_0,y_0,z_0),F_z(x_0,y_0,z_0))^T.$$

### Normal curvature {#Normal_curvature}

In order to keep the formula simple the arguments $(x_0,y_0,z_0)$ are omitted:

$$\kappa_n = \frac{\mathbf v^\top H_F\mathbf v}{\|\operatorname{grad} F\|}$$

is the normal curvature of the surface at a regular point for the unit tangent direction $\mathbf v$. $H_F$ is the [Hessian matrix](wikipedia://en/412108) of $F$ (matrix of the second derivatives).

The proof of this formula relies (as in the case of an implicit curve) on the implicit function theorem and the formula for the normal curvature of a [parametric surface](wikipedia://en/3400953).

:::

## Applications of implicit surfaces {#Applications_of_implicit_surfaces}

As in the case of implicit curves it is an easy task to generate implicit surfaces with desired shapes by applying algebraic operations (addition, multiplication) on simple primitives.

*Image: Epotfl-4ladung.svg.* Equipotential surface of 4 point charges

### Equipotential surface of point charges {#Equipotential_surface_of_point_charges}

The electrical potential of a point charge $q_i$ at point $\mathbf p_i=(x_i,y_i,z_i)$ generates at point $\mathbf p=(x,y,z)$ the potential (omitting physical constants)

$$F_i(x,y,z)=\frac{q_i}{\|\mathbf p -\mathbf p_i\|}.$$

The equipotential surface for the potential value $c$ is the implicit surface $F_i(x,y,z)-c=0$ which is a sphere with center at point $\mathbf p_i$.

The potential of $4$ point charges is represented by

$$F(x,y,z)=\frac{q_1}{\|\mathbf p -\mathbf p_1\|}+ \frac{q_2}{\|\mathbf p -\mathbf p_2\|}+ \frac{q_3}{\|\mathbf p -\mathbf p_3\|}+\frac{q_4}{\|\mathbf p -\mathbf p_4\|}.$$

For the picture the four charges equal 1 and are located at the points $(\pm 1,\pm 1,0)$. The displayed surface is the equipotential surface (implicit surface) $F(x,y,z)-2.8=0$.

### Constant distance product surface {#Constant_distance_product_surface}

A Cassini oval can be defined as the point set for which the product of the distances to two given points is constant (in contrast, for an ellipse the *sum* is constant). In a similar way implicit surfaces can be defined by a constant distance product to several fixed points.

In the diagram *metamorphoses* the upper left surface is generated by this rule: With

$$\begin{aligned} F(x,y,z) = {} & \sqrt{(x-1)^2+y^2+z^2}\cdot \sqrt{(x+1)^2+y^2+z^2} \\ & \quad \cdot \sqrt{x^2+(y-1)^2+z^2}\cdot\sqrt{x^2+(y+1)^2+z^2} \end{aligned}$$

the constant distance product surface $F(x,y,z)-1.1=0$ is displayed.

*Image: Metamorphose-torus-4ppfl.svg.* Metamorphoses between two implicit surfaces: a torus and a constant distance product surface.

### Metamorphoses of implicit surfaces {#Metamorphoses_of_implicit_surfaces}

A further simple method to generate new implicit surfaces is called *metamorphosis* or *[homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250)* of implicit surfaces:

For two implicit surfaces $F_0(x,y,z)=0, F_1(x,y,z)=0$ (in the diagram: a constant distance product surface and a torus) one defines new surfaces using the design parameter $\mu \in [0,1]$:

$$F_\mu (x,y,z)=\mu F_1(x,y,z)+(1-\mu)\,F_0(x,y,z)=0$$

In the diagram the design parameter is successively $\mu=0, \, 0.33, \, 0.66, \, 1$ .

*Image: Approx-3tori-pov.png.* [POV-Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POV-Ray) image (central projection) of an approximation of three tori.

### Smooth approximations of several implicit surfaces {#Smooth_approximations_of_several_implicit_surfaces}

$\Pi$-surfaces [^o11] can be used to approximate any given smooth and bounded object in $R^3$ whose surface is defined by a single polynomial as a product of subsidiary polynomials. In other words, we can design any smooth object with a single algebraic surface. Let us denote the defining polynomials as $f_i\in\mathbb{R}[x_1,\ldots,x_n](i=1,\ldots,k)$. Then, the approximating object is defined by the polynomial

$F(x,y,z) = \prod_i f_i(x,y,z) - r$[^o11]

where $r\in\mathbb{R}$ stands for the blending parameter that controls the approximating error.

Analogously to the smooth approximation with implicit curves, the equation

$$F(x,y,z)=F_1(x,y,z)\cdot F_2(x,y,z)\cdot F_3(x,y,z) -r= 0$$

represents for suitable parameters $c$ smooth approximations of three intersecting tori with equations

$$\begin{aligned} F_1=(x^2+y^2+z^2+R^2-a^2)^2-4R^2(x^2+y^2)=0, \\[3pt] F_2=(x^2+y^2+z^2+R^2-a^2)^2-4R^2(x^2+z^2)=0, \\[3pt] F_3=(x^2+y^2+z^2+R^2-a^2)^2-4R^2(y^2+z^2)=0. \end{aligned}$$

(In the diagram the parameters are $R=1, \, a=0.2, \, r=0.01.$)

## Visualization of implicit surfaces {#Visualization_of_implicit_surfaces}

There are various algorithms for [rendering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendering_%28computer_graphics%29) implicit surfaces,[^o14] including the [marching cubes algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marching_cubes_algorithm).[^o15] Essentially there are two ideas for visualizing an implicit surface: One generates a net of polygons which is visualized (see [surface triangulation](wikipedia://en/41819435)) and the second relies on [ray tracing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_tracing_%28graphics%29) which determines intersection points of rays with the surface.[^o16] The intersection points can be approximated by *sphere tracing*, using a [signed distance function](wikipedia://en/5660713) to find the distance to the surface.[^o17]

### Implicit surface software {#Implicit_surface_software}

#### Free implicit surface software {#Free_implicit_surface_software}

Open-source or free software supporting algebraic implicit surface modelling:

- [K3DSurf](https://k3dsurf.sourceforge.net/) — A program to visualize and manipulate Mathematical models in 3-6 dimensions. K3DSurf supports Parametric equations and Isosurfaces
- **[CGAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CGAL)** (Computational Geometry Algorithms Library), written in C++, has strong support for implicit surface modeling (Boolean operations on implicit surfaces, Surface meshing for visualization, Implicit curve arrangements).
- **[PyVista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PyVista)**[^o20], a Python wrapper around [VTK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTK) for easier handling of implicit surfaces. Simplified API for rendering and manipulating implicit surfaces. It can integrate with [numpy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numpy).
- Some **[Blender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blender_%28software%29)** add-ons (metaballs and volumetric modeling for implicit surfaces, and scripting support for custom implicit functions).
- **[SculptsFEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SculptsFEM)** (for solving PDEs on implicit surfaces, Implicit curve generation)
- ImpliSolid[^o21] (open-source), supports sharp edges.
- **[Houdini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houdini_%28software%29)** (supports implicit surface modeling using SDFs and procedural techniques). Houdini Apprentice License is free.[^o22]
- **[POV-Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POV-Ray)** (Persistence of Vision Raytracer) has built-in support for defining complex implicit surfaces.[^o23]
- Vision-based surface reconstruction use implicit functions for statistical modelling of surfaces: SDFStudio,[^o24] Geo-Neus,[^o25] PointSDF,[^o26] etc.
- Various other software exist for polygonization of implicit surfaces, in context of [Marching cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marching_cubes), and in general [Image-based meshing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image-based_meshing) and, but they are not necessarily based on an algebraic closed-form field.

#### Industrial or commercial software using implicit surface software {#Industrial_or_commercial_software_using_implicit_surface_software}

- **[Altair Inspire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altair_Inspire)**[^o28][^o29]
- **Leapfrog**, implicit geological modelling software by [Seequent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seequent), widely used in mining and environmental applications.
- **Studio RM**, [geologic modelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologic_modelling) software by [Datamine Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datamine_Software), supports implicit surface generation for mineral resource estimation.
- **K-MINE**, mining and geological modelling software that uses implicit surfaces for orebody modelling.
- **[Maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_%28software%29)** has a library for plotting implicit surfaces.
