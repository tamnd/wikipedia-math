# SAT Subject Test in Mathematics Level 1

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="SAT Subject Test in Mathematics Level 1"}

The **SAT Subject Test in Mathematics Level 1** (formerly known as **Math I** or **MathIC** (the "C" representing the use of a calculator)) was the name of a one-hour [multiple choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_choice) [test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standardized_test) given on [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), basic [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261), [algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/974169), elementary [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and basic foundations of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176)[^o2] by [The College Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_College_Board). A student chose whether to take the test depending upon [college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College) entrance requirements for the schools in which the student is planning to apply. Until 1994, the [SAT Subject Tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT_Subject_Tests) were known as **Achievement Tests**; and from 1995 until January 2005, they were known as **SAT IIs**. Mathematics Level 1 was taken 109,048 times in 2006.[^o3] The [SAT Subject Test in Mathematics Level 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT_Subject_Test_in_Mathematics_Level_2) covered more advanced content.

:::

Generally you need to have completed a semester of a pre-calculus class with a solid “B” or better to feel comfortable on the Math 1, whereas the content of the Math 2 test extends through Algebra II and basic trigonometry, precalculus, and basic calculus.On January 19, 2021, [the College Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Board) discontinued all [SAT Subject tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT_Subject_Tests), including the SAT Subject Test in Mathematics Level 1. This was effective immediately in the United States, and the tests were to be phased out by the following summer for international students.[^o5][^o6] This was done as a response to changes in college admissions due to the [impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic).[^o7]

## Format {#Format}

The test had 50 multiple choice questions that were to be answered in one hour.[^o9] All questions had five answer choices. Students received 1 point for every correct answer, lost ¼ of a point for each incorrect answer, and received 0 points for questions left blank.

The questions covered a broad range of topics. Approximately 10-14% of questions focused on [Numbers](wikipedia://en/21690) and [Operations](wikipedia://en/4140245), 38-42% focused on [Algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [functions](wikipedia://en/185427), 38-42% focused on [Geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) (including [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9417), [coordinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_geometry), [three-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-dimensional_geometry), and [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261)), and 6-10% focused on [Data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954), [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), and [probability](wikipedia://en/22934).[^o9]

## Calculator Use {#Calculator_Use}

The College Board stated that a calculator "may be useful or necessary" for about 40-50% of the questions on the test. The College Board also encouraged the use of a [graphing calculator](wikipedia://en/335004) over a [scientific calculator](wikipedia://en/439711).[^o2] It also said that this test was "developed with the expectation that most students are using graphing calculators."[^o11]

Students were not permitted to use calculators on the Mathematics Level 1 test that have a [QWERTY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QWERTY) format keyboard, that require an [electrical outlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_outlet), that make noise, use paper tape, that have non-traditional methods of input (such as a [stylus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stylus_%28computing%29)), or those that are part of a communication device (such as [PDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_digital_assistant)'s, [laptops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laptop), or [cell phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_phone)). Calculators that had slanted screens or large displays (numbers 1 in. or more) are seated at the discretion of the test supervisor.[^o11]

## Preparation {#Preparation}

The College Board suggested as preparation for the test three years of mathematics, including two years of algebra, and one year of geometry.[^o9]

## Scoring {#Scoring}

For each of the 50 multiple choice questions, students received 1 point for every correct answer, lost ¼ of a point for each incorrect answer, and received 0 points for questions left blank. This created a *raw score*, which was then converted into a *scaled score*. The conversion between these numbers varied depending on the difficulty of a particular test administration. The *scaled score* was the only score reported to either students or colleges, and ranged from 200 to 800, with 800 being the best possible score. The [standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590) between test scores in 2006 was 102.[^o3]

Less than one percent of the 2006 College-Bound Seniors taking the test received a perfect score of 800. None got a score lower than 260. The mean score was 593.[^o3]
