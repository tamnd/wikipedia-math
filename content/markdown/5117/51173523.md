# Sara Zahedi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sara Zahedi"}

**Sara Zahedi** (born 1981 in [Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran))[^o4] is an [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_peoples)-[Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedes) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works in [computational fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/305924) and holds a professorship in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) at the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology) (KTH) in [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden). She is one of ten winners and the only female winner of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470) Prize for 2016 "for her outstanding research regarding the development and analysis of [numerical algorithms](wikipedia://en/21506) for [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) with a focus on applications to problems with dynamically changing [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446)". The topic of Zahedi's EMS Prize lecture was her recent research on the CutFEM method of solving [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034) problems with changing boundary geometry, such as arise when simulating the dynamics of systems of two immiscible liquids.[^o2] This method combines [level set methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_set_method) to represent the domain boundaries as cuts through an underlying uniform grid, together with numerical simulation techniques that can adapt to the complex geometries of grid cells cut by these boundaries.[^o3]

:::

When Zahedi was ten years old, with her father having been killed by the regime after the [Iranian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Revolution), her mother sent her on her own as a refugee to Sweden, and only rejoined her some years later.[^o4][^o5] She was drawn to mathematics in part because she understood mathematics better than the Swedish language,[^o5] and to [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988) because of its real-world applications.[^o4] She earned a master's degree from KTH in 2006, and a doctorate in 2011;[^o2] her dissertation, *Numerical Methods for Fluid Interface Problems*, was supervised by [Gunilla Kreiss](wikipedia://en/68485669).[^o6] After postdoctoral studies at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University), she returned to KTH as an assistant professor in 2014.[^o2]
