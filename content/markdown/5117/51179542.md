# Quasi-abelian category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-abelian category"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a **quasi-abelian category** is a [pre-abelian category](wikipedia://en/62775) in which the [pushout](wikipedia://en/582024) of a [kernel](wikipedia://en/142616) along arbitrary morphisms is again a kernel and, dually, the [pullback](wikipedia://en/841689) of a [cokernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cokernel_%28category_theory%29) along arbitrary morphisms is again a cokernel.

:::

A quasi-abelian category is an [exact category](wikipedia://en/10987985).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathcal A$ be a [pre-abelian category](wikipedia://en/62775). A morphism $f$ is **a kernel** (**a cokernel**) if there exists a morphism $g$ such that $f$ is a kernel (cokernel) of $g$. The category $\mathcal A$ is **quasi-abelian** if for every kernel $f: X\rightarrow Y$ and every morphism $h: X\rightarrow Z$ in the pushout diagram

$$\begin{array}{ccc} X & \xrightarrow{f} & Y \\ \downarrow_{h} & & \downarrow_{h'}\\ Z & \xrightarrow{f'} & Q \end{array}$$

the morphism $f'$ is again a kernel and, dually, for every cokernel $g: X\rightarrow Y$ and every morphism $h: Z\rightarrow Y$ in the pullback diagram

$$\begin{array}{ccc} P & \xrightarrow{g'} & Z \\ \downarrow_{h'} & & \downarrow_{h}\\ X & \xrightarrow{g} & Y \end{array}$$

the morphism $g'$ is again a cokernel.

Equivalently, a quasi-abelian category is a pre-abelian category in which the system of all kernel-cokernel pairs forms an [exact structure](wikipedia://en/10987985).

Given a pre-abelian category, those kernels, which are stable under arbitrary pushouts, are sometimes called the **semi-stable kernels**. Dually, cokernels, which are stable under arbitrary pullbacks, are called **semi-stable cokernels**.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Let $f$ be a morphism in a quasi-abelian category. Then the induced morphism $\overline{f} : \operatorname{cok} \ker f \to \ker \operatorname{cok} f$ is always a [bimorphism](wikipedia://en/27716891#Monomorphisms_and_epimorphisms), i.e., a [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538) and an [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539). A quasi-abelian category is therefore always [semi-abelian](wikipedia://en/51183962).

:::

## Examples and non-examples {#Examples_and_non-examples}

Every *[abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063)* is quasi-abelian. Typical non-abelian examples arise in functional analysis.[^o8]

- The category of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) is quasi-abelian.
- The category of [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801) is quasi-abelian.
- The category of ([Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637)) [locally convex spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_space) is quasi-abelian.

Contrary to the claim by Beilinson,[^o9] the category of complete separated topological vector spaces with [linear topology](wikipedia://en/35938255) is not quasi-abelian.[^o10] On the other hand, the category of (arbitrary or Hausdorff) topological vector spaces with linear topology is quasi-abelian.[^o10]

::: {#o12 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Raikov conjecture"}

## History {#History}

The concept of quasi-abelian category was developed in the 1960s. The history is involved.[^o13] This is in particular due to **Raikov's conjecture**, which stated that the notion of a [semi-abelian category](wikipedia://en/51183962) is equivalent to that of a quasi-abelian category. Around 2005 it turned out that the conjecture is false.[^o14]

:::

## Left and right quasi-abelian categories {#Left_and_right_quasi-abelian_categories}

By dividing the two conditions in the definition, one can define **left quasi-abelian categories** by requiring that cokernels are stable under pullbacks and **right quasi-abelian categories** by requiring that kernels stable under pushouts.[^o16]
