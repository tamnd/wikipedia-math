# Bedaux Unit

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bedaux Unit"}

The **Bedaux Unit** emerged from the U.S. [scientific management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_management) movement. It remains in daily use in measuring and comparing manual labor to this day. The Bedaux unit is a universal measure for manual work and is used both to compare the work done by individuals for measuring and driving efficiency in industry.

:::

## History {#History}

### F. W. Taylor's time studies (1909) {#F._W._Taylor's_time_studies_(1909)}

While [F. W. Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Winslow_Taylor) remains famous for conducting [time studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_and_motion_studies) on employees,[^o4][^o5] these studies' influence on subsequent workplaces have long been harder to determine.[^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9]

Recent research has revealed that the core purpose of Taylor's time studies was to produce *Unit-Times* data, as espoused in his *Shop Management* (1903)[^o10] and *Concrete Costs* (1912), which he co-authored with [Sanford E. Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanford_E._Thompson).[^o11][^o12]

### Emerson Consulting (1900-1930) {#Emerson_Consulting_(1900-1930)}

The key individual who spotted Taylor's focus on *Unit-Times* was [Harrington Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrington_Emerson) and his firm *Emerson Consulting*.[^o14][^o15] Emerson's circle was the basis for several innovations with *Unit-Times* by his followers [Charles E. Knoeppel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Edward_Knoeppel), [Charles E. Bedaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Bedaux) and Earl K. Wennerlund.[^o12][^o20]

### The Bedaux *B* (1920 - 1950s) {#The_Bedaux_B_(1920_-_1950s)}

Building on Taylor's *Unit-Times*, and his experience at *Emerson Consulting*, Bedaux introduced the practice of *rating assessment*. Through *rating* Bedaux developed the "[Bedaux System of Human Power Measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedaux_System)" which arrived at a universal measure for all manual work, the *Bedaux Unit* or *B*.[^o12][^o17][^o21][^o18][^o19]

The Bedaux *B*, and units derived from it such as the [Rowntree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowntree's) *Mark* and the [Urwick, Orr & Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndall_Urwick) *Point*, led to improvements in the comparability of employee and departmental efficiency,[^o20] as well as labor and activist disputes about the purpose and practices of [time studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_and_motion_studies) and the *B*.[^o12]

Bedaux's claims to originality in making this innovation remain a topic of debate.[^o12][^o21][^o22][^o23]

### British Standard 3138 (1959) {#British_Standard_3138_(1959)}

The Bedaux consultancy, its offshoots, and the [Bedaux System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedaux_System) were particularly influential in Britain well into the 1950s.[^o17][^o25]

In 1959, the [British Standards Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSI_Group) issued [British Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Standard) 3138 *(Work Study)*, which was based on the Bedaux *B*.[^o12] British Standard 3138 was reissued in 1979 and 1992, and remains in daily use in [job evaluations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_evaluation) today.[^o12]
