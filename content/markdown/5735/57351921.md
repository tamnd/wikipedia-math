# Burton Howard Camp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Burton Howard Camp"}

**Burton Howard Camp** (September 30, 1880 – March 1, 1980)[^o2] was an American mathematician and mathematical statistician. For most of his career, he was a professor of Mathematics at [Wesleyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Burton Howard Camp was born in [Hartford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Connecticut), to Howard Alexander Camp and Alice Amelia (Parsons) Camp.

He graduated from [Wesleyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University) in 1901, where he was a member of the [Eclectic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclectic_Society_%28fraternity%29) and [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa). He studied under Professor of English literature, [Caleb Thomas Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleb_Thomas_Winchester), and gave an address at the Commencement. He earned a B.A. from [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard) in 1903, an M.A. in 1907, and a Ph.D. from [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale) in 1911. His thesis was on the Convergence of Singular Integrals and his advisor was [James Pierpont](wikipedia://en/2594993).

## Career {#Career}

He taught at Oak Grove Seminary in [Vassalboro, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassalboro,_Maine), from 1902–1903, was an instructor in Mathematics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) from 1903–1904, at [Wesleyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University) from 1904–1905, at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard) from 1906–1907, and again at Wesleyan from 1907–1909. He was named an Associate Professor of Mathematics at Wesleyan in 1909, and a Professor in 1914. He was also chair of the department. He was acting director of the [Van Vleck Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Vleck_Observatory) from 1918–1920.[^o5] He remained involved with Mathematics at Wesleyan until 1948.

In the early 1920s, Camp switched from analysis to mathematical statistics and spent a year in London studying with [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695).[^o6] He was a founder and early [President of the Institute of Mathematical Statistics](wikipedia://en/15315059), Vice President of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), and a member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7] Camp was named a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 1936.[^o8]

He also served as Secretary of the Wesleyan University Alumni Association.

## Family life {#Family_life}

He married **Rachel Caroline Rice** (1889–1978) on June 30, 1915. She was a daughter of the Rev. [Charles Francis Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Francis_Rice) and Miriam Owen Jacobs. She attended [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University), and was a member of [Gamma Phi Beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_Phi_Beta) sorority, for which she wrote a speech entitled "Knowledge is now no more a fountain sealed."

They had four children: [Miriam Rice Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miriam_Camp) (1916–1994), [Paul Rice Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Rice_Camp) (1919–2012), Charles Rice Camp (1924), and Margaret Camp (1926–2021).
