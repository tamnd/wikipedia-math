# Maier's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maier's theorem"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Maier's theorem** is a theorem due to [Helmut Maier](wikipedia://en/6592450) about the numbers of [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) in short intervals for which [Cramér's probabilistic model of primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cramér_model) gives a wrong answer.

:::

The theorem states[^n1] that if $\pi$ is the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826) and $\lambda > 1$, then

$$\frac{\pi(x+(\log x)^\lambda)-\pi(x)}{(\log x)^{\lambda-1}}$$

does not have a limit as $x$ tends to infinity; more precisely the [limit superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_superior) is greater than 1, and the limit inferior is less than 1. The Cramér model of primes predicts incorrectly that it has limit 1 when $\lambda \geq 2$ (using the [Borel–Cantelli lemma](wikipedia://en/44987)).

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proofs"}

## Proofs {#Proofs}

Maier proved his theorem using [Buchstab](wikipedia://en/45421432)'s equivalent for the counting function of quasi-primes (set of numbers without prime factors lower to bound $z = x^{1/u}$, with $u$ fixed). He also used an equivalent of the number of primes in arithmetic progressions of sufficient length due to [Gallagher](wikipedia://en/59895532).

[János Pintz](wikipedia://en/22191053) gave another proof,[^n2] and also showed that most probabilistic models of primes incorrectly predict the [mean square error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_square_error)

$$\int_2^Y\!\bigg(\sum_{2<p\le x} \log p -\!\sum_{2<n\le x}1\bigg)^2dx$$

of one version of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692).
