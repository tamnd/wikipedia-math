# Leo Perutz

Leo Perutz

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Leo Perutz"}

**Leopold Perutz** (2 November 1882 – 25 August 1957) was an Austrian [novelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novelist) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Born in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague), he lived in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866) until the [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) *[Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss)* in 1938, when he emigrated to [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine).

:::

According to the biographical note on the [Arcade Publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_Publishing) editions of the English translations of his novels, Leo was a mathematician who formulated an [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) [equation](wikipedia://en/9284) which is named after him; he worked as a [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) for an [insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance) company. He was related to the [biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist) [Max Perutz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Perutz).[^o3]

During the 1950s he returned occasionally to Austria, spending the summer and autumn months in the market town of [St. Wolfgang im Salzkammergut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Wolfgang_im_Salzkammergut) in the [Salzkammergut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzkammergut) resort region and in Vienna. He died in the Austrian spa town of [Bad Ischl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Ischl) in 1957. He wrote his first novel, *The Third Bullet*, in 1915 while recovering from a wound sustained in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War). In all Perutz wrote eleven novels, which gained the admiration of [Jorge Luis Borges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Luis_Borges), [Italo Calvino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo_Calvino), [Ian Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Fleming), [Karl Edward Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Edward_Wagner) and [Graham Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Greene). Wagner cited Perutz's novel *The Master of the Day of Judgment* as one of the thirteen best non-supernatural horror novels.[^o4]

## Overview {#Overview}

Perutz was born in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague) to a largely non-religious family of Jewish ancestry.[^n1] They moved to [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), where he attended various schools and colleges before studying [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), statistics, actuarial science, and economics.[^n2]

Perutz's novels are short and are usually [historical novels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_novels) combining fast-paced adventure with a [metaphysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) twist. They are influenced by the work of [E. T. A. Hoffmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._T._A._Hoffmann), [Arthur Schnitzler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Schnitzler) and [Victor Hugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Hugo) (whose work Perutz translated into German).[^o8] Austrian fellow novelist [Friedrich Torberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Torberg) once characterized Perutz's literary style as the possible result of a little infidelity of [Franz Kafka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Kafka) and [Agatha Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agatha_Christie). *The Marquis of Bolibar* (1920) features the [Wandering Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandering_Jew) appearing during Napoleon's campaign in Spain.[^o9] *The Master of the Day of Judgement* (1921) is a decidedly different [mystery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_fiction) story about the circumstances surrounding an actor's death in the early twentieth century, and *Little Apple* (1928) concerns a [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) soldier's obsessive quest for revenge. *By Night Under the Stone Bridge* (1952) is an [episodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episode) work whose separate stories are bound together by the illicit love shared, in their dreams, by a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) woman and the Emperor [Rudolf II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). In the [posthumously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posthumous_work)-published *Leonardo's Judas* (1959), [da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci)'s quest for an appropriate face to give the betrayer in his *[Last Supper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Supper_%28Leonardo%29)* is interwoven with the squabble between a usurer and the merchant to whom he owes money.

The title of his 1933 novel *Saint Peter's Snow* (published in English in 1935 as *The Virgin's Brand*[^n1]), which is set in what was then the present day, refers to a drug which induces religious fervour; the Nazis, understandably, did not care for it. Critic Alan Piper considered it "a psychological detective story", although it has varyingly been categorised as [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction) or fantasy.[^n1] Piper believed that the novel was decades ahead of its time due to the description of a hallucinogenic drug derived from an [ergot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergot) fungus 10 years before the discovery of [LSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSD).[^n1] He also thought it astonishing that it discussed the experimental use of this hallucinogen on an isolated village 20 years before the [1951 Pont-Saint-Esprit mass poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Pont-Saint-Esprit_mass_poisoning), and proposed that ergot was the psychoactive ingredient used in the ancient mystery cults 40 years before this was put forward as a serious proposal.[^n1]

In his discussion of German language fantastic literature, critic [Franz Rottensteiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Rottensteiner) describes Perutz as "undoubtedly the finest fantasy author of his time".[^o9] Literary scholar Alan Piper described Perutz's work as typically containing "an element of the fantastic, with dramatic plots featuring confusing and conflicting interpretations of events".[^n1]
