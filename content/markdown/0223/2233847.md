# Richard Rusczyk

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Rusczyk"}

**Richard Rusczyk** (; born September 21, 1971) is an American mathematician. He was the founder and [chief executive officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_executive_officer) of Art of Problem Solving Inc. and a co-author of the *Art of Problem Solving* textbooks. Rusczyk was a national [Mathcounts](wikipedia://en/530021) participant in 1985, and he won the [USA Math Olympiad (USAMO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America_Mathematical_Olympiad) in 1989. He is one of the co-creators of the [Mandelbrot Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandelbrot_Competition), and a former director of the [USA Mathematical Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAMTS) (USAMTS). He also founded the **San Diego Math Circle**.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Richard Rusczyk was born in [Idaho Falls, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Falls,_Idaho) in 1971. [^o4] He signed up for the [MathCounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MathCounts) program when he was in middle school. As a high schooler, Rusczyk was a part of his high school math team and took part in the [American Mathematics Competitions](wikipedia://en/665435).[^o5] Rusczyk would later go on to win the [USA Math Olympiad (USAMO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America_Mathematical_Olympiad) and make the alternates for the USA team for the [International Math Olympiad (IMO)](wikipedia://en/82208).[^o6] Rusczyk attended [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), graduating in 1993 with a Bachelor's degree in chemical engineering.[^o7] After graduating from college, Rusczyk went to work as a bond trader at [D. E. Shaw & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._E._Shaw_&_Co.) for four years, before pursuing becoming a high school teacher.[^o8]

## Art of Problem Solving {#Art_of_Problem_Solving}

**Art of Problem Solving (AoPS)** is an educational organization founded in 2003 by mathematician Richard Rusczyk. It provides textbooks, online courses, and community resources designed to help middle and high school students strengthen their mathematics and problem-solving skills, particularly in preparation for mathematics competitions.[^o11]

### History {#History}

Between 1993 and 1994, Richard Rusczyk and [Sandor Lehoczky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandor_Lehoczky) authored the first two volumes of the *Art of Problem Solving* series. These books were designed to prepare students for mathematical competitions by teaching concepts and problem-solving techniques not typical in standard school curricula.[^o13] The series later gave its name to the educational company Rusczyk founded in 2003.[^o14]

After completing his undergraduate studies at Princeton University, Rusczyk worked for several years as a bond trader at [D. E. Shaw & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._E._Shaw_&_Co.) He later founded Art of Problem Solving in 2003, launching an online platform that offers instructional courses, discussion forums, a collaborative wiki, and interactive games such as *For the Win* (FTW) and *Reaper* and "Alcumus".[^o11]

### Initiatives {#Initiatives}

Rusczyk founded the nonprofit **Art of Problem Solving Initiative**, which administers the *United States of America Mathematical Talent Search (USAMTS)* and supports local mathematics programs across the United States.[^o16]

In 2012, Rusczyk received the **Mathcounts Distinguished Alumnus Award**. In 2014, he was awarded the **[Paul Erdős Award](wikipedia://en/28001680)** by the World Federation of National Mathematics Competitions.[^o17]

### Leadership {#Leadership}

On March 24, 2025, Rusczyk retired as chief executive officer of Art of Problem Solving. He was succeeded by Ben Kornell as CEO, with Andrew Sutherland appointed as chief product officer. However, as of February 9th, 2026, Rusczyk is returning as CEO to guide new initiatives. He continues to serve on the company’s board of directors.[^o19]

### Textbooks {#Textbooks}

Richard Rusczyk has authored and co-authored several mathematics textbooks for students in grades 5–12, including *Prealgebra*, *Introduction to Algebra*, and *Introduction to Geometry*.[^o21]

After his first year at Princeton University, Rusczyk collaborated with Sandor Lehoczky and Sam Vandervelde to create the Mandelbrot Competition. Following the competition’s limited success, Rusczyk and Lehoczky began writing the first volumes of the *Art of Problem Solving* series.[^o22]
