# Exponential object

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exponential object"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), an **exponential object** or **map object** is the [categorical](wikipedia://en/5869) generalization of a [function space](wikipedia://en/340630) in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553). [Categories](wikipedia://en/53759) with all [finite products](wikipedia://en/237868) and exponential objects are called [cartesian closed categories](wikipedia://en/237037). Categories (such as [subcategories](wikipedia://en/310959) of **[Top](wikipedia://en/435051)**) without adjoined products may still have an **exponential law**.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathbf{C}$ be a category, let $Z$ and $Y$ be [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) of $\mathbf{C}$, and let $\mathbf{C}$ have all [binary products](wikipedia://en/237868) with $Y$. An object $Z^Y$ together with a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) $\mathrm{eval}\colon (Z^Y \times Y) \to Z$ is an *exponential object* if for any object $X$ and morphism $g \colon X\times Y \to Z$ there is a unique morphism $\lambda g\colon X\to Z^Y$ (called the *transpose* of $g$) such that the following diagram [commutes](wikipedia://en/138484):

*Image: ExponentialObject-01.svg.* Universal property of the exponential object

This assignment of a unique $\lambda g$ to each $g$ establishes an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) ([bijection](wikipedia://en/3942)) of [hom-sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hom-set), $\mathrm{Hom}(X\times Y,Z) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(X,Z^Y).$

If $Z^Y$exists for all objects $Z, Y$ in $\mathbf{C}$, then the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $(-)^Y \colon \mathbf{C}\to \mathbf{C}$ defined on objects by $Z \mapsto Z^Y$ and on arrows by $(f\colon X\to Z) \mapsto (f^Y\colon X^Y \to Z^Y)$, is a [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint) to the product functor $-\times Y$. For this reason, the morphisms $\lambda g$ and $g$ are sometimes called *exponential adjoints* of one another.[^o6]

### Equational definition {#Equational_definition}

Alternatively, the exponential object may be defined through equations:

- Existence of $\lambda g$ is guaranteed by existence of the operation $\lambda -$.
- Commutativity of the diagrams above is guaranteed by the equality $\forall g \colon X \times Y \to Z,\ \mathrm{eval} \circ (\lambda g \times \mathrm{id}_Y) = g$.
- Uniqueness of $\lambda g$ is guaranteed by the equality $\forall h \colon X \to Z^Y, \ \lambda (\mathrm{eval} \circ (h \times \mathrm{id}_Y)) = h$.

### Universal property {#Universal_property}

The exponential $Z^Y$ is given by a [universal morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_morphism) from the product functor $- \times Y$ to the object $Z$. This universal morphism consists of an object $Z^Y$ and a morphism $\mathrm{eval}\colon (Z^Y \times Y) \to Z$.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

In the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), an exponential object $Z^Y$ is the set of all [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) $Y \to Z$.[^o11] The map $\mathrm{eval}\colon (Z^Y \times Y) \to Z$ is just the [evaluation map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apply), which sends the pair $(f, y)$ to $f(y)$. For any map $g\colon X \times Y \to Z$ the map $\lambda g\colon X \to Z^Y$ is the [curried](wikipedia://en/6600) form of $g$:

$$\lambda g(x) = y \mapsto g(x,y).\,$$

A [Heyting algebra](wikipedia://en/309343) $H$ is just a bounded [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) that has all exponential objects. Heyting implication, $Y \Rightarrow Z$, is an alternative notation for $Z^Y$. The above adjunction results translate to implication ($\Rightarrow : H \times H \to H$) being [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint) to [meet](wikipedia://en/3956618) ($\wedge : H \times H \to H$). This adjunction can be written as $(- \wedge Y) \dashv (Y \Rightarrow -)$, or more fully as:

$$(- \wedge Y): H \stackrel {\longrightarrow} {\underset {\longleftarrow}{\top}} H: (Y \Rightarrow -)$$

In the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051), the exponential object $Z^Y$ exists provided that $Y$ is a [locally compact](wikipedia://en/48632) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637). In that case, the space $Z^Y$ is the set of all [continuous functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) from $Y$ to $Z$ together with the [compact-open topology](wikipedia://en/726335). The evaluation map is the same as in the category of sets; it is continuous with the above topology.[^o12] If $Y$ is not locally compact Hausdorff, the exponential object may not exist (the space $Z^Y$ still exists, but it may fail to be an exponential object since the evaluation function need not be continuous). For this reason the category of topological spaces fails to be [cartesian closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_closed). However, the category of locally compact topological spaces is not cartesian closed either, since $Z^Y$ need not be locally compact for locally compact spaces $Z$ and $Y$. A cartesian closed category of spaces is, for example, given by the [full subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959#Formal_definition) spanned by the [compactly generated](wikipedia://en/899382) Hausdorff spaces.

In [functional programming languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_programming_language), the morphism $\operatorname{eval}$ is often [called $\operatorname{apply}$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apply), and the syntax $\lambda g$ is often [written $\operatorname{curry}(g)$](wikipedia://en/20576545#Representation). The morphism $\operatorname{eval}$ should not be confused with the [`eval`](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eval) function in some [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015), which evaluates quoted expressions.
