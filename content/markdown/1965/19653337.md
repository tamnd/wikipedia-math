# Parametric family

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Parametric family"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and its applications, a **parametric family** or a **parameterized family** is a [family](wikipedia://en/23624339) of objects (a set of related objects) whose differences depend only on the chosen values for a set of [parameters](wikipedia://en/25065).[^o2]

:::

Common examples are parametrized (families of) [functions](wikipedia://en/185427), [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543), curves, shapes, etc.

## In probability and its applications {#In_probability_and_its_applications}

*Image: Probability distribution functions for normal distribution.svg.* A graph of the probability density functions of several [normal distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_distributions) (from the same parametric family).

For example, the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) $f_{X}$ of a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) $X$ may depend on a parameter $\theta$. In that case, the function may be denoted $f_X( \cdot \, ; \theta)$ to indicate the dependence on the parameter $\theta$. $\theta$ is not a formal argument of the function as it is considered to be fixed. However, each different value of the parameter gives a different probability density function. Then the *parametric family* of densities is the set of functions $\{ f_X( \cdot \, ; \theta) \mid \theta \in \Theta \}$, where $\Theta$ denotes the [parameter space](wikipedia://en/2882813), the set of all possible values that the parameter $\theta$ can take. As an example, the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) is a family of similarly shaped distributions parametrized by their [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) and their [variance](wikipedia://en/32344).[^o4][^o5]

In [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216), [two-moment decision models](wikipedia://en/27834453) can be applied when the decision-maker is faced with random variables drawn from a [location-scale family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location-scale_family) of probability distributions.

## In algebra and its applications {#In_algebra_and_its_applications}

*Image: Cobb douglas.png.* A three-dimensional graph of a [Cobb–Douglas production function](wikipedia://en/350668).

In [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), the [Cobb–Douglas production function](wikipedia://en/350668) is a family of [production functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_function) parametrized by the [elasticities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28economics%29) of output with respect to the various [factors of production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factors_of_production).

*Image: Quadratic equation coefficients.png.* Graphs of several [quadratic polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_polynomial), varying each of the three coefficients independently.

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the [quadratic equation](wikipedia://en/25175), for example, is actually a family of equations parametrized by the [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) of the variable and of its square and by the [constant term](wikipedia://en/79861).
