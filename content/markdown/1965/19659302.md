# Hexagonal pyramid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hexagonal pyramid"}

*Image: Hexagonal pyramid.stl.* 3D model of a hexagonal pyramid

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **hexagonal pyramid** is a [pyramid](wikipedia://en/3307045) with a [hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) base upon which are erected six [isosceles triangular](wikipedia://en/315428) faces that meet at a point (the apex). Like any pyramid, it is self-[dual](wikipedia://en/8815).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A hexagonal pyramid has seven vertices, twelve edges, and seven faces. One of its faces is [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733), a [base](wikipedia://en/9579379) of the pyramid; six others are triangles. Six of the edges make up the hexagon by connecting its six vertices, and the other six edges are known as the *lateral edges* of the pyramid, meeting at the seventh vertex called the [apex](wikipedia://en/6022079).

Like other right pyramids with a regular polygon as a base, this pyramid has [pyramidal symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramidal_symmetry) of [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) $C_{6\mathrm{v}}$: the pyramid is left invariant by rotations of one, two, three, four, and five in six of a full turn around its [axis of symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_symmetry), the line connecting the apex to the center of the base. It is also [mirror symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_symmetric) relative to any perpendicular plane passing through a bisector of the base. It can be represented as the [wheel graph](wikipedia://en/1655876) $W_6$; more generally, a wheel graph $W_n$ is the representation of the [skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton_%28topology%29) of a $n$-sided pyramid. It is [self-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual_polyhedron), meaning its [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) is the hexagonal pyramid itself.
