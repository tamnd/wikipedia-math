# Ladder graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ladder graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **ladder graph** $L_{n}$ is a [planar](wikipedia://en/24314), [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) with $2n$ [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and $3n - 2$ edges.

:::

The ladder graph can be obtained as the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/2856852) of two [path graphs](wikipedia://en/4587190), one of which has only one edge: $L_{n} = P_{n} \square P_{2}$.[^o4][^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

By construction, the ladder graph L<sub>*n*</sub> is isomorphic to the [grid graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_graph) *G*<sub>2,*n*</sub> and looks like a ladder with *n* rungs. It is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph) with girth 4 (if *n>1*) and [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) 3 (if *n>2*).

The [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of the ladder graph is 2 and its [chromatic polynomial](wikipedia://en/1460126) is $(x-1)x(x^2-3x+3)^{(n-1)}$.

*Image: Ladder graphs.svg.* The ladder graphs *L*<sub>1</sub>, *L*<sub>2</sub>, *L*<sub>3</sub>, *L*<sub>4</sub> and *L*<sub>5</sub>.

:::

::: {#o9 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Ladder rung graph"}

## Ladder rung graph {#Ladder_rung_graph}

Sometimes the term "ladder graph" is used for the *nP*<sub>2</sub> **ladder rung graph**, which is the graph union of *n* copies of the path graph *P*<sub>2</sub>.

*Image: Ladder rung graphs.svg.* The ladder rung graphs *LR*<sub>1</sub>, *LR*<sub>2</sub>, *LR*<sub>3</sub>, *LR*<sub>4</sub>, and *LR*<sub>5</sub>.

:::

## Circular ladder graph {#Circular_ladder_graph}

The **circular ladder graph** *CL*<sub>*n*</sub> is constructible by connecting the four 2-degree vertices in a *straight* way, or by the Cartesian product of a cycle of length *n* ≥ 3 and an edge.[^o11] In symbols, *CL*<sub>*n*</sub> = *C*<sub>*n*</sub> □ *P*<sub>2</sub>. It has 2*n* nodes and 3*n* edges. Like the ladder graph, it is [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph), [planar](wikipedia://en/24314) and [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle), but it is [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431) if and only if *n* is even.

Circular ladder graph are the [polyhedral graphs](wikipedia://en/24656270) of prisms, so they are more commonly called **prism graphs**.

## Möbius ladder {#Möbius_ladder}

Connecting the four 2-degree vertices of a standard ladder graph *crosswise* creates a [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) called a Möbius ladder.
