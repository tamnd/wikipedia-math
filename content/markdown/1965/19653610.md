# Larry V. Hedges

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Larry V. Hedges"}

**Larry Vernon Hedges** is a researcher in [statistical methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_methods) for [meta-analysis](wikipedia://en/62329) and [evaluation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaluation) of [education policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_policy). He is Professor of Statistics and Education and Social Policy, Institute for Policy Research, [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University). Previously, he was the Stella M. Rowley Distinguished Service Professor of Education, Sociology, Psychology, and Public Policy Studies at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o3][^o4] He is a member of the [National Academy of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Education) and a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), the [American Educational Research Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Educational_Research_Association), the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association), and the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988).[^o5] In 2018, he received the [Yidan Prize for Education Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yidan_Prize), one of the world's largest education prizes, which carries an award of HK\$30 million (about US\$3.9 million).[^o6][^o7]

:::

He has authored a number of articles and books on statistical methods for [meta-analysis](wikipedia://en/62329), which is the use of statistical methods for combining results from different studies. He also suggested several estimators for [effect sizes](wikipedia://en/437276) and derived their properties. He carried out research on the relation of resources available to schools and student achievement, most notably the relation between class size and achievement.

## Hedges' g {#Hedges'_g}

In 1981, Hedges published a paper describing the unbiased standardized mean difference, the [g statistic](wikipedia://en/437276#Hedges'_g).[^o9] "It turns out that \[Cohen's\] d has a slight bias, tending to overestimate the absolute value of in small samples. This bias can be removed by a simple correction that yields an unbiased estimate of, with the unbiased estimate sometimes called Hedges’ g."[^o10]
