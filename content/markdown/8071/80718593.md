# Perla Sousi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perla Sousi"}

**Perla Sousi** (born 1984) is a Greek mathematician specialising in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a professor in the Department of Pure Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics (DPMSS) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), and a Fellow of [Emmanuel College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_College,_Cambridge).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Sousi was born in 1984 in Athens. After a 2005 bachelor's degree in mathematics from the [University of Patras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Patras), she first came to the University of Cambridge in 2006 for [Part III of the Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_III_of_the_Mathematical_Tripos). After study in 2006 and 2007 in France at the [École normale supérieure (Paris)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29) and the [French Institute for Research in Computer Science and Automation](wikipedia://en/318413) (Inria), she completed her Ph.D. at Cambridge in 2011. Her dissertation, *Collisions and detection for random walks and Brownian motion*, was supervised by [James R. Norris](wikipedia://en/22849173).

She continued at Cambridge as a Junior Research Fellow of Emmanuel College from 2011 to 2014, while also doing postdoctoral research in 2012 at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California). She became a Fellow of Emmanuel College and a research associate of DPMMS in 2014. A readership at Cambridge was established in 2019, and she was made a full professor in 2024.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Sousi received a 2025 [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) "for her innovative contributions to the study of mixing and cutoff phenomena for Markov chains; to the study of random walks and Brownian motion in fixed and changing environments; and to the development of the potential theory of branching random walks on d-dimensional lattices".
