# Lev Aleksandrovich Shuvalov

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lev Aleksandrovich Shuvalov"}

**Lev Aleksandrovich Shuvalov** (Шувалов Лев Александрович; 15 November 1923 – 6 December 2004) was a Soviet crystallographer and mathematician. Shuvalov is notable for his work on symmetry considerations in [ferroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferroelectricity) crystal phase changes.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Shuvalov was born on 15 November 1923. In 1941 he entered the army directly from school. Shuvalov participated in the battles of [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moscow), [Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad) and [Kursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk). He served until the end of the war in 1945 and was awarded the [Order of the Red Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Red_Star).[^o4][^o5]

In 1946 Shuvalov entered [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) to study physics, graduating in 1951. He then worked for the [Hydroproject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroproject) Institute for five years. In 1956 Shuvalov joined the [Institute of Crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shubnikov_Institute_of_Crystallography_RAS) studying under [Alexei Vasilievich Shubnikov](wikipedia://en/70215429); he gained his doctorate in 1971. He was then appointed a professor at the Institute of Crystallography. In 1972 Shuvalov created the Laboratory of Phase Transitions of which he was the head for over 20 years.[^o6]

Shuvalov's work was in the fields of mathematics and crystallography, specifically the use of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) to analyse the [phase changes](wikipedia://en/54423) occurring in [ferroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferroelectricity) and [ferroelastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferroelasticity) crystals. He published more than 700 papers during his scientific career and held over 20 patents.[^o6]

Between 1956 and 1974, Shuvalov was a leader in the field of symmetry considerations in the crystallography and crystal physics of ferroelectrics. Shuvalov investigated symmetry changes during ferroelectric, ferroelastic and ferromagnetic phase transitions based on [Curie's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curie's_principle).[^o4][^o5] He established a classification of ferroelectrics based on their crystal symmetry.[^o6] In the 1980s and 1990s Shuvalov led a team that researched new families of ferroelectrics and [antiferroelectrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiferroelectricity) such as the alkali trihydrosulfates and trihydroselenites.[^o5][^o7] He also discovered a new class of crystals with superionic conductivity (superprotonic conductors).[^o6]

Shuvalov was editor of *[Kristallografija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristallografija)* from 1997 to 2004.[^o8] He also served on the editorial boards of international journals such as *Condensed Matter News*, *[Crystallography Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography_Reviews)*, *[Ferroelectrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferroelectrics_%28journal%29)*, *Ferroelectrics Letters*, and *[Zeitschrift für Kristallographie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeitschrift_für_Kristallographie_–_Crystalline_Materials)*.[^o7]

A special issue of *Ferroelectrics* was dedicated to Shuvalov in 1989.[^o9][^o10][^o11] Shuvalov died on 6 December 2004 in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow).

A symposium in honour of the centenary of Shuvalov's birth was held at [MIREA – Russian Technological University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIREA_–_Russian_Technological_University) in 2024.[^o12]

## Publications {#Publications}

Selected publications available in English:

- 1956: Ferroelectric phase transitions and crystal symmetry[^o14]
- 1960: Ferromagnetic phase transitions and the symmetry of crystals[^o15]
- 1963: [Antisymmetry](wikipedia://en/73498718) and its concrete modifications[^o16]
- 1963: Limit groups of [double antisymmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_antisymmetry)[^o17]
- 1964: Crystallographic classification of ferroelectrics, ferroelectric phase transformations and peculiarities of domain structure, and some physical properties of ferroelectrics of the different classes[^o18]
- 1970: Symmetry aspects of ferroelectricity[^o19]
- 1988: Modern Crystallography IV: Physical properties of crystals[^o20]
