# Analytical regularization

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Analytical regularization"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), **analytical regularization** is a technique used to convert [boundary value problems](wikipedia://en/436779) which can be written as [Fredholm integral equations](wikipedia://en/363377) of the first kind involving [singular operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_integral_operator) into equivalent Fredholm integral equations of the second kind. The latter may be easier to solve analytically and can be studied with [discretization](wikipedia://en/330017) schemes like the [finite element method](wikipedia://en/18233581) or the [finite difference method](wikipedia://en/6054681) because they are [pointwise convergent](wikipedia://en/340198). In [computational electromagnetics](wikipedia://en/3849994), it is known as the **method of analytical regularization**. It was first used in mathematics during the development of [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) before acquiring a name.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Method"}

## Method {#Method}

Analytical regularization proceeds as follows. First, the boundary value problem is formulated as an integral equation. Written as an operator equation, this will take the form

$$G X= Y$$

with $Y$ representing boundary conditions and [inhomogeneities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhomogeneous_differential_equation), $X$ representing the field of interest, and $G$ the integral operator describing how Y is given from X based on the physics of the problem. Next, $G$ is split into $G_1 + G_2$, where $G_1$ is invertible and contains all the singularities of $G$ and $G_2$ is regular. After splitting the operator and multiplying by the inverse of $G_1$, the equation becomes

$$X + G_1^{-1} G_2 X= G_1^{-1} Y$$

or

$$X + A X = B$$

which is now a Fredholm equation of the second type because by construction $A$ is [compact](wikipedia://en/577441) on the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) of which $B$ is a member.

In general, several choices for $\mathbf{G}_1$ will be possible for each problem.[^o2]
