# Kelmans–Seymour conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kelmans–Seymour conjecture"}

*Image: K5-in-Crown12.svg.* $K_{5}$ subdivision of the 12-vertex [crown graph](wikipedia://en/21062705)

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Kelmans–Seymour conjecture** states that every [5-vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) graph that is not [planar](wikipedia://en/24314) contains a [subdivision](wikipedia://en/353021) of the 5-vertex [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_{5}$. It is named for [Paul Seymour](wikipedia://en/1575708) and Alexander Kelmans, who independently described the conjecture; Seymour in 1977 and Kelmans in 1979.[^o2][^o3] A proof was announced in 2016, and published in four papers in 2020.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

A graph is 5-vertex-connected when there are no five vertices that removed leave a disconnected graph. The complete graph is a graph with an edge between every five vertices, and a subdivision of a complete graph modifies this by replacing some of its edges by longer paths. So a graph $G$ contains a subdivision of $K_{5}$ if it is possible to pick out five vertices of $G$, and a set of ten paths connecting these five vertices in pairs without any of the paths sharing vertices or edges with each other.

In any [drawing of the graph](wikipedia://en/325813) on the Euclidean plane, at least two of the ten paths must cross each other, so a graph *G* that contains a *K*<sub>5</sub> subdivision cannot be a [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314). In the other direction, by [Kuratowski's theorem](wikipedia://en/54493), a graph that is not planar necessarily contains a subdivision of either $K_{5}$ or of the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{3,3}$. The Kelmans–Seymour conjecture refines this theorem by providing a condition under which only one of these two subdivisions, the subdivision of $K_{5}$, can be guaranteed to exist. It states that, if a non-planar graph is 5-vertex-connected, then it contains a subdivision of $K_{5}$.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Related results"}

## Related results {#Related_results}

A related result, [Wagner's theorem](wikipedia://en/3126130), states that every 4-vertex-connected nonplanar graph contains a copy of $K_{5}$ as a [graph minor](wikipedia://en/353042). One way of restating this result is that, in these graphs, it is always possible to perform a sequence of [edge contraction](wikipedia://en/3758115) operations so that the resulting graph contains a $K_{5}$ subdivision. The Kelmans–Seymour conjecture states that, with a higher order of connectivity, these contractions are not required.

An earlier conjecture of [Gabriel Andrew Dirac](wikipedia://en/1643806) (1964), proven in 2001 by Wolfgang Mader, states that every $n$-vertex graph with at least $3n - 5$ edges contains a subdivision of $K_{5}$. Because planar graphs have at most $3n - 6$ edges, the graphs with at least $3n - 5$ edges must be nonplanar. However, they need not be 5-connected, and 5-connected graphs can have as few as $2.5n$ edges.[^o8][^o9][^o10]

:::

## Claimed proof {#Claimed_proof}

In 2016, a proof of the Kelmans–Seymour conjecture was claimed by Xingxing Yu of the [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology) and his Ph.D. students Dawei He and Yan Wang.[^o2] A sequence four papers proving this conjecture appeared in *[Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Combinatorial_Theory,_Series_B)*.[^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15]
