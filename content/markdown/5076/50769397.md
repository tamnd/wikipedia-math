# Jean-Michel Bony

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jean-Michel Bony"}

**Jean-Michel Bony** (; born 1 February 1942 in Paris) is a French mathematician, specializing in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). He is known for his work on [microlocal analysis](wikipedia://en/6108841) and [pseudodifferential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudodifferential_operator).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Bony completed his undergraduate and graduate studies at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure), where he received his Ph.D. in 1972 with thesis advisor [Gustave Choquet](wikipedia://en/17301193). Bony became a professor at the [University of Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris-Sud) and is now a professor at the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique).

His doctoral students include [Jean-Yves Chemin](wikipedia://en/68418903).

## Research {#Research}

Bony's research deals with [microlocal analysis](wikipedia://en/6108841), [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342). In 1981 he published important results on paradifferential operators, extending the theory of pseudifferential operators published by [Ronald Coifman](wikipedia://en/1558980) and [Yves Meyer](wikipedia://en/2096465) in 1979.[^o6][^o7] Bony applied his theory to the propagation of singularities in solutions of semilinear wave equations.[^o8]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1980, Bony received the [Prix Paul Doistau–Émile Blutet](wikipedia://en/63735602). He was elected a corresponding member and a full member of the French Academy of Sciences in 1990 and 2000 respectively.

He was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1970 in Nice[^o10] and in 1983 in Warsaw.[^o11]
