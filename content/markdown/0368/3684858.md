# DIMACS

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="DIMACS"}

The **Center for [Discrete Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_Mathematics) and [Theoretical Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_Computer_Science)** (**DIMACS**) is a collaboration between [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), and the research firms [AT&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T), [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs), Applied Communication Sciences, and [NEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEC_Corporation). It was founded in 1989 with money from the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation). Its offices are located on the Rutgers campus, and 250 members from the six institutions form its permanent members.

:::

DIMACS is devoted to both theoretical development and practical applications of discrete mathematics and theoretical computer science. It engages in a wide variety of evangelism including encouraging, inspiring, and facilitating researchers in these subject areas, and sponsoring conferences and workshops.

Fundamental research in discrete mathematics has applications in diverse fields including Cryptology, Engineering, Networking, and Management Decision Support.

Past directors have included [Fred S. Roberts](wikipedia://en/26225243), [Daniel Gorenstein](wikipedia://en/1569478), [András Hajnal](wikipedia://en/21765779), and [Rebecca N. Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_N._Wright).[^o2]

## The DIMACS challenges {#The_DIMACS_challenges}

DIMACS sponsors implementation challenges to determine practical algorithm performance on problems of interest. There have been eleven DIMACS challenges so far.

- 1990−1991: Network flows and matching
- 1992−1992: [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) problems: [Max Clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_clique_problem), [Graph Coloring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_coloring_problem), and [SAT](wikipedia://en/4715)
- 1993−1994: Parallel algorithms for combinatorial problems
- 1994−1995: Computational giology: fragment assembly and genome rearrangement
- 1995−1996: Priority queues, dictionaries, and multidimensional point sets
- 1998−1998: Near-neighbor searches
- 2000−2000: Semidefinite and related optimization problems
- 2001−2001: The [traveling salesman problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling_salesman_problem)
- 2005−2005: The [shortest-path problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortest_path)
- 2011−2012: [Graph partitioning](wikipedia://en/11973947) and [graph clustering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_structure) <http://www.cc.gatech.edu/dimacs10/>
- 2013−2014: [Steiner tree problems](wikipedia://en/498304)
- 2020−2021: [Vehicle routing problems](wikipedia://en/7799668)
