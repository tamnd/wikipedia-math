# Simon Brendle

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Simon Brendle"}

**Simon Brendle** (born June 1981) is a German-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and nonlinear [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations). At the age of 19,[^o4] he received his [Dr. rer. nat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._rer._nat.) from [Tübingen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tübingen_University) under the supervision of [Gerhard Huisken](wikipedia://en/39508202) (2001). He was a professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) (2005–2016), and is currently a professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). He has held visiting positions at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich), [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), and [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University).

:::

## Contributions to mathematics {#Contributions_to_mathematics}

Simon Brendle has solved major open problems regarding the [Yamabe equation](wikipedia://en/11757994) in conformal geometry. This includes his counterexamples to the compactness conjecture for the Yamabe problem, and the proof of the convergence of the [Yamabe flow](wikipedia://en/14629108) in all dimensions (conjectured by [Richard Hamilton](wikipedia://en/758911)). In 2007, he proved the [differentiable sphere theorem](wikipedia://en/4685719) (in collaboration with [Richard Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen)), a fundamental problem in global differential geometry. In 2012, he proved the [Hsiang–Lawson's conjecture](wikipedia://en/221183), a longstanding problem in minimal surface theory. He has also worked on singularity formation in the [mean curvature flow](wikipedia://en/13110176) and [Ricci flow](wikipedia://en/288291), solving a question concerning the uniqueness of self-similar solutions to the Ricci flow which arose in the context of [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266)'s work.

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

He received an [Alfred P. Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) in 2006. For his contributions to [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) he was awarded an [EMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMS_Prize) in 2012. He delivered the 2012 [Euler Lecture](wikipedia://en/75774672) and the 2011 Takagi Lectures. He was named as the recipient of the 2014 [Bôcher Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bôcher_Prize)[^o7] of the American Mathematical Society. In 2017, he received a [Simons Investigator Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Investigator_Award)[^o8] and the [Fermat Prize](wikipedia://en/10919195). In 2023, he received the [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933). In 2026 he was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o9]
