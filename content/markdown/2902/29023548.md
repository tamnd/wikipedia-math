# Aise Johan de Jong

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aise Johan de Jong"}

de Jong

**Aise Johan de Jong** (born 30 January 1966)[^o14] is a Dutch mathematician and professor of mathematics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). His research interests include [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). He maintains the [Stacks Project](wikipedia://en/48211685).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

De Jong was born in [Bruges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruges), Belgium on 30 January 1966.[^o14] He attended the [Christelijk Gymnasium Sorghvliet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christelijk_Gymnasium_Sorghvliet) in [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague), Netherlands.[^o14] He obtained his master's degree at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) in 1987, under the supervision of Antonius Van de Ven.[^o14] He earned his Ph.D. *cum laude* at the [Radboud University Nijmegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radboud_University_Nijmegen) in 1992, under the supervision of [Frans Oort](wikipedia://en/58171100) and [Joseph H. M. Steenbrink](wikipedia://en/58181629).[^o14]

## Career {#Career}

De Jong spent 1 year as a visitor at the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1386960), 3 months as a visitor at [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University), and then 3 years as a fellow at the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) stationed at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University).[^o14][^o16] He was a Benjamin Peirce Assistant Professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) from 1995 to 1996.[^o16] He was a professor of mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1996 to 1998 and then worked at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) from 1998 to 2005.[^o16] He moved to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) as a professor of mathematics in 2005.[^o16]

## Work {#Work}

In 1996, de Jong developed his theory of [alterations](wikipedia://en/9914115#De_Jong.27s_method) which was used by [Fedor Bogomolov](wikipedia://en/8823369) and Tony Pantev (1996) and [Dan Abramovich](wikipedia://en/53880775) and de Jong (1997) to prove [resolution of singularities](wikipedia://en/9914115) in [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) 0 and to prove a weaker result for varieties of all dimensions in characteristic *p* which is strong enough to act as a substitute for resolution for many purposes.[^o7][^o8][^o9]

In 2005, de Jong started the [Stacks Project](wikipedia://en/48211685), "an open source textbook and reference work on [algebraic stacks](wikipedia://en/717377) and the [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) needed to define them."[^o10] The book that the project has generated currently runs to more than 7500 pages as of July 2022.[^o11]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1998 he was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o13] He won the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in 2000 for his theory of alterations.[^o14] In the same year, De Jong became a correspondent of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o15] In 2022 he received the [Leroy P. Steele Prize for Mathematical Exposition](wikipedia://en/1252550).[^o16]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

De Jong lives in New York City with his wife, [Cathy O'Neil](wikipedia://en/40066499), and their three sons.[^o18]
