# Cameron Leigh Stewart

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cameron Leigh Stewart"}

**Cameron Leigh Stewart** [FRSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) is a Canadian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is a professor of pure mathematics at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo).

:::

## Contributions {#Contributions}

He has made numerous contributions to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), in particular to work on the [*abc* conjecture](wikipedia://en/174108). In 1976 he obtained, with [Alan Baker](wikipedia://en/18950800), an effective improvement to [Liouville's Theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liouville's_theorem_on_diophantine_approximation). In 1991 he proved that the number of solutions to a [Thue equation](wikipedia://en/1151323) $f(x,y) = h$ is at most $2800(1 + 1/4\epsilon \deg f)(\deg f)^{1 + \omega(g)}$, where $\epsilon$ is a pre-determined positive real number and $\omega(g)$ is the number of distinct primes dividing a large divisor $g$ of $h$. This improves on an earlier result of [Enrico Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475) and [Wolfgang M. Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_M._Schmidt) and is close to the best possible result. In 1995 he obtained, along with [Jaap Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaap_Top), the existence of infinitely many quadratic, cubic, and sextic twists of [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves) of large rank. In 1991 and 2001 respectively, he obtained, along with Kunrui Yu, the best unconditional estimates for the [abc conjecture](wikipedia://en/174108). In 2013, he solved an old problem of [Erdős](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős) (so his [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) is 1) involving Lucas and Lehmer numbers. In particular, he proved that the largest prime divisor $P(n)$ of $2^n - 1$ satisfies $\lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} P(n)/n = \infty$.

## Education {#Education}

Stewart completed a [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) in 1971 and a [M.Sc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.Sc) in 1972 from [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University). He earned his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1976, under the supervision of [Alan Baker](wikipedia://en/18950800).[^o5]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1974, while at Cambridge, he was awarded the J.T. Knight Prize.

He was elected Fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) in 1989. He was appointed Fellow of the [Fields Institute](wikipedia://en/2495768) in 2008. Since 2003 he has held a [Canada Research Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Research_Chair) (tier 1).[^o7] Since 2005 he has been appointed University Professor at the University of Waterloo.[^o8] He was selected to give the annual [Isidore and Hilda Dressler Lecture](https://web.archive.org/web/20130620202326/http://www.math.ksu.edu/group/numbertheory/dressler.html) at [Kansas State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_State_University) in 2015.

He was elected as a fellow of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) in 2019.[^o9]
