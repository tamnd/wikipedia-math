# Fern Hunt

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Fern Hunt"}

**Fern Yvette Hunt** (born January 14, 1948) is an African American[^o3] [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for her work in applied mathematics and [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology).[^o4] She currently works as a researcher at the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology), where she conducts research on the [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986) of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems).[^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hunt was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) on January 14, 1948, to Daphne Lindsay and Thomas Edward Hunt. Her sister, [Erica Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erica_Hunt), is a published poet and author. Hunt's grandparents immigrated to the United States from Jamaica before [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I).[^o7][^o4] Her family lived in a primarily black housing project in Hampton. Her father did not graduate from high school, and though her mother attended [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College) for two years, she did not earn a degree. When Hunt was 9 years old, her mother gifted her a chemistry set for Christmas, which sparked her early interest in science. Hunt's middle school science teacher, Charles Wilson, further encouraged Hunt to pursue math and science.[^o4][^o8] Hunt attended the [Bronx High School of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_High_School_of_Science), and it was during her time in high school that her primary focus shifted from science to mathematics.[^o16] After graduating high school, Hunt attended [Bryn Mawr College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_College) following the encouragement of her mother, earning an [A.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.B.) in mathematics in 1969.[^o7] She went on to earn a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) and [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in mathematics from the [Courant Institute of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) in 1978.[^o9] Her PhD thesis (1978) *Genetic and Spatial Variation in some Selection-Migration Models* was advised by [Frank Hoppensteadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Hoppensteadt).[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

Hunt began her academic career at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah), and in 1978, she accepted a job as an assistant professor at [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University). She remained a member of the Department of Mathematics at Howard until 1993. While at Howard, she also worked for the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health) in the Laboratory of Mathematical Biology (1981-1982) and the [National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Standards) (1986-1991).[^o7] Additionally, from 1988 to 1991, she was a member of the [GRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_Record_Examinations) Mathematics Advisory Board at [Educational Testing Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Testing_Service) (ETS). In 1993, she left Howard and began working for the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology), where she worked on mathematical problems from physics and chemistry research. While working at NIST she continued her research on the ergodic theory of dynamical systems.[^o9]

Fern also lectures at colleges and universities to encourage students in mathematics. She uses her experiences of the setbacks she experienced as a black woman in mathematics to mentor minority students interested in mathematics. In 1998 she was an instructor at a summer workshop for women entering Ph.D. programs in mathematics run by the [EDGE Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDGE_Foundation) ([Enhancing Diversity in Graduate Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhancing_Diversity_in_Graduate_Education)).[^o9]

## Awards and achievements {#Awards_and_achievements}

In 2000, Hunt received the [Arthur S. Flemming Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_S._Flemming_Award) for her contributions to probability and [stochastic modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_modeling), mathematical biology, computational geometry, nonlinear dynamics, computer graphics, and [parallel computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing).[^o12] She has been a member of the Bryn Mawr College board of trustees since 1992 and the Biological and Environmental Research Advisory Committee for the [United States Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy) since 1994.[^o9]

Hunt was included in the 2019 class of fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for outstanding applications of mathematics to science and technology, exceptional service to the US government, and for outreach and mentoring".[^o13] In 2005, the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) awarded her the [Ella Z. Falconer award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecture), where she delivered the lecture "Techniques for Visualizing Frequency Patterns in DNA."[^o14] The AWM has included her in the 2020 class of AWM Fellows for "her exceptional commitment to outreach and mentoring; for her sustained efforts to make the AWM organization more inclusive; for her service to higher education and government; and for inspiring those underrepresented in mathematics with her work in ergodic theory, probability, and computation".[^o15]

Hunt's work earned her recognition by [Mathematically Gifted & Black](wikipedia://en/64552006) as a Black History Month 2017 Honoree.[^o16]
