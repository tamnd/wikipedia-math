# Harborth's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harborth's conjecture"}

::: {#o2 .problem cue=unsolved}

Does every planar graph have an integral Fáry embedding?

:::

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Harborth's conjecture** states that every [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) has a planar [drawing](wikipedia://en/325813) in which every edge is a straight [segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) of [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) length.[^o3][^o4][^o5] This [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) is named after [Heiko Harborth](wikipedia://en/22801588), and (if true) would strengthen [Fáry's theorem](wikipedia://en/6805386) on the existence of straight-line drawings for every planar graph. For this reason, a drawing with integer edge lengths is also known as an **integral Fáry embedding**.[^o6] Despite much subsequent research, Harborth's conjecture remains [unsolved](wikipedia://en/1333335).[^o7]

:::

## Special classes of graphs {#Special_classes_of_graphs}

Although Harborth's conjecture is not known to be true for all planar graphs, it has been [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) for several special kinds of planar graph.

One class of graphs that have integral Fáry embeddings are the graphs that can be reduced to the [empty graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_graph) by a sequence of operations of two types:

- Removing a vertex of [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) at most two.
- Replacing a vertex of degree three by an edge between two of its [neighbors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28graph_theory%29). (If such an edge already exists, the degree three vertex can be removed without adding another edge between its neighbors.)

For such a graph, a [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) Fáry embedding can be constructed incrementally by reversing this removal process, re-inserting the vertices that were removed. Re-inserting a degree-two vertex uses the fact that the set of points that are at a rational distance from two given points are [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) in the plane. Re-inserting a degree-three vertex uses the fact that, if three points have rational distance between one pair and square-root-of-rational distance between the other two pairs, then the points at rational distances from all three are again dense in the plane.[^o9][^o10] The distances in such an embedding can be made into integers by scaling the embedding by an appropriate factor. Based on this construction, the graphs known to have integral Fáry embeddings include the [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431) planar graphs, [(2,1)-sparse](wikipedia://en/2777098#Sparse_and_tight_graphs) planar graphs, planar graphs of [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) at most 3, and graphs of degree at most four that either contain a [diamond](wikipedia://en/20796238) subgraph or are not [4-edge-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-edge-connected_graph).[^o6][^o11][^o12]

In particular, the graphs that can be reduced to the empty graph by the removal only of vertices of degree at most two (the [2-degenerate](wikipedia://en/27545816) planar graphs) include both the [outerplanar graphs](wikipedia://en/352343) and the [series–parallel graphs](wikipedia://en/9938559). However, for the outerplanar graphs a more direct construction of integral Fáry embeddings is possible, based on the existence of infinite subsets of the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) in which all distances are rational.[^o13]

Additionally, integral Fáry embeddings are known for each of the five [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905).[^o5]

## Related conjectures {#Related_conjectures}

A stronger version of Harborth's conjecture, posed by (Kleber 2008), asks whether every planar graph has a planar drawing in which the vertex coordinates as well as the edge lengths are all integers.[^o17] It is known to be true for [3-regular graphs](wikipedia://en/980508),[^o18] for graphs that have maximum degree 4 but are not 4-regular,[^o19] and for [planar 3-trees](wikipedia://en/31104610).[^o19]

Another unsolved problem in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the [Erdős–Ulam problem](wikipedia://en/52483865), concerns the existence of dense subsets of the plane in which all distances are rational numbers. If such a subset existed, it would form a [universal point set](wikipedia://en/38864413) that could be used to draw all planar graphs with rational edge lengths (and therefore, after scaling them appropriately, with integer edge lengths). However, Ulam conjectured that dense rational-distance sets do not exist.[^o20] According to the [Erdős–Anning theorem](wikipedia://en/12600251), infinite non-collinear point sets with all distances being integers cannot exist. This does not rule out the existence of sets with all distances rational, but it does imply that in any such set the denominators of the rational distances must grow arbitrarily large.
