# Center of curvature

*Image: Concave mirror qwertyxp2000.png.* A concave mirror with light rays

*Image: Radius of curvature.svg.* Center of curvature

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Center of curvature"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **center of curvature** of a curve is a point located at a distance from the [curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curve_%28mathematics%29) equal to the [radius of curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radius_of_curvature_%28mathematics%29) lying on the [curve normal vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curve_normal_vector).[^o2] It is the [point at infinity](wikipedia://en/403139) if the curvature is zero. The [osculating circle](wikipedia://en/1896705) to the curve is centered at the centre of curvature. [Cauchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy) defined the center of curvature *C* as the intersection point of two [infinitely close](wikipedia://en/160990) normal lines to the curve.[^o3] The [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of centers of curvature for each point on the curve comprise the [evolute](wikipedia://en/842387) of the curve. This term is generally used in physics regarding [the study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics) of [lenses](wikipedia://en/18320) and [mirrors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror) (see [radius of curvature (optics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radius_of_curvature_%28optics%29)).

:::

It lies on the principal axis of a mirror or lens. In case of a convex mirror it lies behind the polished, or reflecting, surface and it lies in front of the reflecting surface in case of a concave mirror.[^o5]
