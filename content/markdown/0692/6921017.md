# Algebraic Geometry (book)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Algebraic Geometry"}

***Algebraic Geometry*** is an [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) written by [Robin Hartshorne](wikipedia://en/5693379) and published by [Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer-Verlag) in 1977.[^o3]

:::

## Importance {#Importance}

Although shorter synopses introducing [scheme theory](wikipedia://en/364754) like [Macdonald](wikipedia://en/3100378)'s *Algebraic Geometry: Introduction to Schemes* (1968) and [Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594)'s widely circulated but unpublished *Red Book* (1967) were available earlier, Hartshorne's *Algebraic Geometry* was the first comprehensive treatment of scheme theory written as a text intended to be accessible to graduate students and is considered to be the standard reference.[^n1][^n2][^n3] Even so, it is considered to be notoriously difficult for beginners; reading it and working through the challenging exercises have been considered by some to be a rite-of-passage for serious students and future researchers of algebraic geometry.[^o8] This book was cited when Hartshorne was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for mathematical exposition in 1979.

## Contents {#Contents}

The first chapter, titled "Varieties", gives a brief, formal introduction to the classical algebraic geometry of [varieties](wikipedia://en/248808) over [algebraically closed fields](wikipedia://en/1018). This chapter recalls many classical results from [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) (e.g., [Hilbert's Nullstellensatz](wikipedia://en/149215)), with the books by [Atiyah–Macdonald](wikipedia://en/8122079), Matsumura, and Zariski–Samuel cited as references for proofs. The second and the third chapters, "Schemes" and "Cohomology", form the technical heart of the book. The last two chapters, "Curves" and "Surfaces", respectively explore the geometry of 1- and 2-dimensional objects, using the tools developed in the chapters 2 and 3.
