# Sigma-ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sigma-ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a nonempty collection of [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) is called a **\-ring** (pronounced *sigma-ring*) if it is [closed](wikipedia://en/293802) under countable [union](wikipedia://en/47949) and [relative complementation](wikipedia://en/54347).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $\mathcal{R}$ be a nonempty [collection of sets](wikipedia://en/723043). Then $\mathcal{R}$ is a **\-ring** if:

1. Closed under countable [unions](wikipedia://en/47949): $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty} A_{n} \in \mathcal{R}$ if $A_{n} \in \mathcal{R}$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$
1. Closed under [relative complementation](wikipedia://en/54347): $A \setminus B \in \mathcal{R}$ if $A, B \in \mathcal{R}$

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

These two properties imply:

$$\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty} A_n \in \mathcal{R}$$

whenever $A_1, A_2, \ldots$ are elements of $\mathcal{R}.$

This is because

$$\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty A_n = A_1 \setminus \bigcup_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(A_1 \setminus A_n\right).$$

Every -ring is a [δ-ring](wikipedia://en/6939387) but there exist δ-rings that are not -rings.

:::

## Similar concepts {#Similar_concepts}

If the first property is weakened to closure under finite union (that is, $A \cup B \in \mathcal{R}$ whenever $A, B \in \mathcal{R}$) but not countable union, then $\mathcal{R}$ is a [ring](wikipedia://en/2686481) but not a -ring.

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

\-rings can be used instead of [\-fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra) (-algebras) in the development of [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) and [integration](wikipedia://en/15532) theory, if one does not wish to require that the [universal set](wikipedia://en/296838) be measurable. Every -field is also a -ring, but a -ring need not be a -field.

A -ring $\mathcal{R}$ that is a collection of subsets of $X$ induces a [\-field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra) for $X.$ Define $\mathcal{A} = \{ E \subseteq X : E \in \mathcal{R} \ \text{or} \ E^c \in \mathcal{R} \}.$ Then $\mathcal{A}$ is a -field over the set $X$ - to check closure under countable union, recall a $\sigma$-ring is closed under countable intersections. In fact $\mathcal{A}$ is the minimal -field containing $\mathcal{R}$ since it must be contained in every -field containing $\mathcal{R}.$

:::
