# Hans Heilbronn

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hans Heilbronn"}

**Hans Arnold Heilbronn** [^o3] (8 October 1908 – 28 April 1975) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o5]

:::

## Education {#Education}

He was born into a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans)-[Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family. He was a student at the universities of [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen), where he met [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230), who supervised his doctorate. In his thesis, he improved a result of [Hoheisel](wikipedia://en/5384175) on the size of [prime gaps](wikipedia://en/5075259).

## Life {#Life}

Heilbronn fled Germany for Britain in 1933 due to the rise of [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism). He arrived in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), then found accommodation in [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester) and eventually was offered a position at [Bristol University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_University), where he stayed for about one and a half years. There he proved[^o9] that the [class number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_number_%28number_theory%29) of the [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ tends to plus infinity as $d$ does, as well as, in collaboration with [Edward Linfoot](wikipedia://en/33238736), that there are at most ten[^o10] quadratic [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) of the form $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, $d$ a natural number, with class number 1. On invitation of [Louis Mordell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Mordell) he moved back to [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester) in 1934, but left again only one year later, accepting the Bevan Fellowship at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). In Cambridge Heilbronn published several joint papers with [Harold Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111), in one of which they devised a new variant of the [Hardy–Littlewood circle method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Littlewood_circle_method), now sometimes referred to as the Davenport-Heilbronn method, proving that for any indefinite diagonal form $f$ of degree $k$ in more than $n=2^k$ variables whose coefficients are not all in rational ratio there exists $x$ in $\mathbb{Z}^n$ such that $|f(x)|$ is arbitrarily small. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) he was briefly [interned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment) as an enemy alien but released to serve in the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army). In 1946 he returned to Bristol, becoming [Henry Overton Wills Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Overton_Wills_Professor_of_Mathematics). He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1951 and was president of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) from 1959 to 1961.

Heilbronn and his wife moved to North America in 1964. He stayed at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) for a while, then moved on to [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto), where he was Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) from 1964 to 1975. He became a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians) citizen in 1970.

His PhD students include Inder Chowla, Tom Callahan and [Albrecht Fröhlich](wikipedia://en/1096590).[^o4]

The [Heilbronn Institute for Mathematical Research](wikipedia://en/61922775) is named in his honour.
