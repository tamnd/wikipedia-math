# Arthur Erdélyi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur Erdélyi"}

**Arthur Erdélyi** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society),[^o3] [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (2 October 1908 – 12 December 1977) was a [Hungarian-born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Erdélyi was a leading expert on [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110), particularly [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) and [hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/2055223).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born Arthur Diamant in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) to Ignác Josef Armin Diamant and Frederike Roth. His name was changed to Erdélyi when his mother remarried to Paul Erdélyi. He attended the primary and secondary schools there from 1914 to 1926. His interest in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) dates back to this time. Erdélyi was a [Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew), and so it was difficult for him to receive a university education in his native Hungary. He travelled to [Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno), [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), to obtain a degree in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering). However, after his flair for mathematics was discovered (he won several prizes in a competition in his first year), he was persuaded to study the subject.

He soon after began to conduct theoretical research into mathematics, and his first paper was published in 1930. At the end of 1936, he had already published 18 papers, and 11 more appeared in 1937. However, due to the German occupation of Czechoslovakia and neighbouring countries, Erdélyi was forced to flee the country.

Erdélyi contacted [Edmund Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Whittaker), a fellow expert in hypergeometric functions, asking for his assistance and soon after, Erdélyi travelled to [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland), after receiving [£](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_sterling)400 for a visa from Whittaker. He joined the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), and after 2 years there, became a lecturer in the Department of Mathematics (after gaining a DSc in 1940 based on his already extensive published work).[^o7]

In 1946, after [Harry Bateman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Bateman) died, Whittaker was asked to recommend a mathematician who could start the task of publishing Bateman's manuscripts: the [Bateman Manuscript Project](wikipedia://en/3053039). Erdélyi was chosen, and in 1947, after becoming a naturalised British [citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen), travelled to [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California) as a Visiting Professor. He returned to Edinburgh in 1948, only to resign in 1949 to assume the position of Professor of Mathematics at the California Institute of Technology. This was a post he was to hold for the next 15 years, and he retained his citizenship of Britain while in the United States.

In 1964, he returned to Edinburgh as a Professor of Mathematics, a position he held until his death in 1977.

He died in Edinburgh.[^o8]

## Family {#Family}

He married Eva Neuburg in 1942. They did not have children.

## Research {#Research}

Erdélyi was primarily an expert in special functions, in particular, [Lamé functions](wikipedia://en/17212216), hypergeometric functions and orthogonal polynomials. He also contributed to the field of [asymptotic analysis](wikipedia://en/641995), [fractional integration](wikipedia://en/229939) and [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations). He introduced the [Erdélyi–Kober operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdélyi–Kober_operator) for [fractional integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_integration). He wrote two books of high standing - *Asymptotic Expansions (1955)* (reprinted by [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover_Publications)) and *Operational Calculus and Generalised Functions (1962)*

## Awards {#Awards}

Erdélyi received several honours, including being elected to the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) as a Fellow in 1975.[^o3] He also became a fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh in 1945, and was also elected a member of the Academy of Sciences of Turin.
