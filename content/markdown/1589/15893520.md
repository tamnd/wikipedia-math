# Chudnovsky algorithm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chudnovsky algorithm"}

The **Chudnovsky algorithm** is a fast method for calculating the digits of [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601), based on [Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan)'s [$\pi$ formulae](wikipedia://en/4659082#Efficient_infinite_series). Published by the [Chudnovsky brothers](wikipedia://en/1705790) in 1988,[^o2] it was used to calculate $\pi$ to a billion decimal places.[^o3]

:::

It was used in the [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology_of_computation_of_π) calculations of approx. 2.7 trillion digits of $\pi$ in December 2009,[^o4] 10 trillion digits in October 2011,[^o5][^o6] around 22.4 trillion digits in November 2016,[^o7] 31.4 trillion digits in September 2018–January 2019,[^o8] 50 trillion digits on January 29, 2020,[^o9] 62.8 trillion digits on August 14, 2021,[^o10] 100 trillion digits on March 21, 2022,[^o11] 105 trillion digits on March 14, 2024,[^o12] and 202 trillion digits on June 28, 2024.[^o13] Recently, the record was broken yet again on November 18, 2025 with 314 trillion digits of pi.[^o14][^o15] This was done through the usage of the algorithm on [y-cruncher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-cruncher).

::: {#o17 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Algorithm"}

## Algorithm {#Algorithm}

The algorithm is based on the negated [Heegner number](wikipedia://en/522147) $d = -163$, the [*j*-function](wikipedia://en/451988) $j \left(\tfrac{1 + i\sqrt{163}}{2}\right) = -640320^3$, and on the following rapidly convergent [generalized hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_hypergeometric_series):[^o18]

$$\frac{1}{\pi} = \frac{\sqrt{10005}}{4270934400} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} {\frac{(-1)^k (6k)! (545140134k + 13591409)}{(3k)! (k!)^3(640320)^{3k}}}$$

This identity is similar to some of [Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan)'s formulas involving $\pi$,[^o18] and is an example of a [Ramanujan–Sato series](wikipedia://en/42185330).

The [time complexity](wikipedia://en/405944) of the algorithm is $O\left(n (\log n)^3\right)$, where n is the number of digits desired.[^o19] Each term produces around 14 correct decimal digits of $\pi$.[^o20]

:::

## Optimizations {#Optimizations}

The optimization technique used for the world record computations is called [binary splitting](wikipedia://en/4226251).[^o22]
