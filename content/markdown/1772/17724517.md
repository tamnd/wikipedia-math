# Singular isothermal sphere profile

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Singular isothermal sphere profile"}

The **singular isothermal sphere (SIS) profile** is the simplest [parametrization](wikipedia://en/453198) of the spatial distribution of [matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter) in an [astronomical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) system (e.g. [galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy), [clusters of galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy_groups_and_clusters), etc.).[^o2]

:::

## Density distribution {#Density_distribution}

$$\rho(r) = \frac{\sigma_{V}^{2}}{2\pi G r^{2}}$$

where σ<sub>V</sub><sup>2</sup> is the [velocity dispersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocity_dispersion) and G is the [gravitational constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_constant). The SIS profile is [unphysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unphysicality) because of the [singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity) at zero radius and the fact that the total [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass) calculated by integrating the function out to infinite radius does not converge (i.e., is [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824)). However, it is commonly utilized in the literature due to the simplicity of its form.
