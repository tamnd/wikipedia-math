# AF+BG theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="AF+BG theorem"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) the **AF+BG theorem** (also known as **Max Noether's fundamental theorem**) is a result of [Max Noether](wikipedia://en/2482337) that asserts that, if the equation of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) in the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155) belongs locally (at each intersection point) to the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) generated by the equations of two other algebraic curves, then it belongs globally to this ideal.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $F$, $G$, and $H$ be [homogeneous polynomials](wikipedia://en/1698977) in three variables, with $H$ having higher degree than $F$ and $G$; let $a = \deg H - \deg F$ and $b = \deg H - \deg G$ (both positive integers) be the differences of the degrees of the polynomials. Suppose that the [greatest common divisor](wikipedia://en/9927028) of $F$ and $G$ is a constant, which means that the [projective curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_curve) that they define in the projective plane $\mathbb P^2$ have an intersection consisting in a finite number of points. For each point $P$ of this intersection, the polynomials $F$ and $G$ generate an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) $(F, G)_{P}$ of the [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) of $\mathbb P^2$ at $P$ (this local ring is the ring of the fractions $\tfrac n d,$ where $n$ and $d$ are polynomials in three variables and $d(P) \ne 0$). The theorem asserts that, if $H$ lies in $(F, G)_{P}$ for every intersection point $P$, then $H$ lies in the ideal $(F, G)$; that is, there are homogeneous polynomials $A$ and $B$ of degrees $a$ and $b$, respectively, such that $H = AF + BG$. Furthermore, any two choices of $A$ differ by a multiple of $G$, and similarly any two choices of $B$ differ by a multiple of $F$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Related results"}

## Related results {#Related_results}

This theorem may be viewed as a generalization of [Bézout's identity](wikipedia://en/4947), which provides a condition under which an integer or a univariate polynomial $h$ may be expressed as an element of the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) generated by two other integers or univariate polynomials $f$ and $g$: such a representation exists exactly when $h$ is a multiple of the [greatest common divisor](wikipedia://en/12354) of $f$ and $g$. The AF+BG condition expresses, in terms of [divisors](wikipedia://en/1693055) (sets of points, with multiplicities), a similar condition under which a [homogeneous polynomial](wikipedia://en/1698977) $H$ in three variables can be written as an element of the ideal generated by two other polynomials $F$ and $G$.

This theorem is also a refinement, for this particular case, of [Hilbert's Nullstellensatz](wikipedia://en/149215), which provides a condition expressing that some power of a polynomial $h$ (in any number of variables) belongs to the ideal generated by a finite set of polynomials.

:::
