# Arul Shankar

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arul Shankar"}

**Arul Shankar** is an Indian mathematician at the [University of Toronto Mississauga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto_Mississauga) specializing in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), particularly [arithmetic statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_statistics).

:::

## Education {#Education}

He received his B.Sc. (honours) in mathematics and computer science from the [Chennai Mathematical Institute](wikipedia://en/3124675) in 2007. During his undergraduate studies, he was awarded the CMI Medal of Excellence in Mathematics for outstanding academic performance at the institute’s first convocation as a deemed university.[^o3] Through CMI’s exchange programme with the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure), Paris, he undertook an eight-week summer research visit at ENS in May–June 2007.[^o3]

He obtained his PhD from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 2012 under [Manjul Bhargava](wikipedia://en/5102088).[^o4] Shankar is known for his work, with Bhargava, establishing unconditionally that the [average rank of elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_rank_of_elliptic_curves) is bounded when ordered by naive height by $1.5$[^o5] and $1.17$[^o6] respectively, thus proving the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161) for a positive proportion of elliptic curves.

In 2018 he was awarded a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship),[^o7] one of the most prestigious early career research fellowships available to mathematicians.[^o8] He was named a [Simons Fellow in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Fellows_in_Mathematics) in 2024, a fellowship awarded by the Simons Foundation to support research leave for established mathematicians.[^o9]
