# NewHope

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="NewHope"}

In [post-quantum cryptography](wikipedia://en/26605226), **NewHope** is a [key-agreement protocol](wikipedia://en/63944) by Erdem Alkim, Léo Ducas, Thomas Pöppelmann, and Peter Schwabe that is designed to resist [quantum computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computer) attacks.[^o2][^o3]

:::

NewHope is based on a mathematical problem [ring learning with errors](wikipedia://en/45588368) (RLWE) that is believed to be difficult to solve. NewHope has been selected as a round-two contestant in the [NIST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIST) [Post-Quantum Cryptography Standardization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Quantum_Cryptography_Standardization) competition,[^o4] and was used in [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google)'s [CECPQ1](wikipedia://en/51628519) experiment as a quantum-secure algorithm, alongside the classical [X25519](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X25519) algorithm.[^o5][^o6]

## Design choices {#Design_choices}

The designers of NewHope made several choices in developing the algorithm:[^o8]

- *Binomial Sampling*: Although sampling to high-quality discrete Gaussian distribution is important in post-quantum lattice-based *compact* signature scheme such as Falcon (GPV-style Hash-and-Sign paradigm) and [BLISS](wikipedia://en/50701148) (GLP-style [Fiat–Shamir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat–Shamir_heuristic) paradigm) to prevent signature from leaking information about the private key, it's otherwise not so essential to key exchange schemes. The author chose to sample error vectors from [binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/3876).
- *Error Reconciliation*: What distinguishes NewHope from its predecessors is its method for error reconciliation. Previous [ring learning with error key exchange schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RLWE-KEX) correct errors one coefficient at a time, whereas NewHope corrects errors 2 or 4 coefficients at a time based on high-dimension geometry. This allows for lower decryption failure rate and higher security.
- *Base Vector Generation*: The authors of NewHope proposed deriving the base "generator" vector (commonly denoted as **A** or $a$) from the output of the XOF function [SHAKE-128](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHA3) in order to prevent "back-doored" values from being used, as may happen with traditional [Diffie–Hellman](wikipedia://en/7903) through [Logjam attack](wikipedia://en/46759583).
- *Security Levels*: In the early versions of the papers describing NewHope, authors proposed using 1024-degree polynomial for 128-bit "post-quantum" security level, and a 512-degree polynomial as "toy" instance for cryptanalysis challenge.[^o9] In the version submitted to NIST, the 512-degree version is codified to provide 128-bit "classical" security level.
