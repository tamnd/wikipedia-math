# Invertible module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Invertible module"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), an **invertible module** is intuitively a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) that has an [inverse](wikipedia://en/45199) with respect to the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844). Invertible modules form the foundation for the definition of [invertible sheaves](wikipedia://en/581859) in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

:::

Formally, a [finitely generated module](wikipedia://en/326430) *M* over a ring *R* is said to be invertible if it is locally a [free module](wikipedia://en/326454) of [rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rank_of_a_free_module) 1. In other words, $M_P\cong R_P$ for all [primes](wikipedia://en/60692) *P* of *R*. Now, if *M* is an invertible *R*-module, then its [dual](wikipedia://en/609737#Dual_objects) *M*<sup>\*</sup> = Hom(*M*,*R*) is its inverse with respect to the tensor product, i.e. $M\otimes _R M^*\cong R$.

The theory of invertible modules is closely related to the theory of [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149) one [varieties](wikipedia://en/248808) including the theory of [divisors](wikipedia://en/1693055).
