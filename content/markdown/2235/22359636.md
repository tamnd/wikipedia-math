# Simplicial sphere

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simplicial sphere"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), a **simplicial** (or **combinatorial**) ***d*-sphere** is a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686) [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the [*d*-dimensional sphere](wikipedia://en/39782). Some simplicial spheres arise as the boundaries of [convex polytopes](wikipedia://en/1817885), however, in higher dimensions most simplicial spheres cannot be obtained in this way.

:::

One important open problem in the field was the **g-conjecture**, formulated by [Peter McMullen](wikipedia://en/40964822), which asks about possible numbers of faces of different dimensions of a simplicial sphere. In December 2018, the g-conjecture was proven by [Karim Adiprasito](wikipedia://en/47874333) in the more general context of rational homology spheres.[^o2][^o8]

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- For any *n* ≥ 3, the [simple *n*-cycle](wikipedia://en/878962) *C*<sub>*n*</sub> is a **simplicial circle**, i.e. a simplicial sphere of dimension 1. This construction produces all simplicial circles.
- The boundary of a convex [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) in **R**<sup>3</sup> with triangular faces, such as an [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) or [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084), is a simplicial 2-sphere.
- More generally, the boundary of any (*d*+1)-dimensional [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) (or [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258)) simplicial [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885) in the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) is a simplicial *d*-sphere.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

It follows from [Euler's formula](wikipedia://en/148420) that any simplicial 2-sphere with *n* vertices has 3*n* − 6 edges and 2*n* − 4 faces. The case of *n* = 4 is realized by the tetrahedron. By repeatedly performing the [barycentric subdivision](wikipedia://en/583651), it is easy to construct a simplicial sphere for any *n* ≥ 4. Moreover, [Ernst Steinitz](wikipedia://en/3966685) gave a [characterization of 1-skeleta](wikipedia://en/19762817) (or edge graphs) of convex polytopes in **R**<sup>3</sup> implying that any simplicial 2-sphere is a boundary of a convex polytope.

[Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) constructed an example of a non-polytopal simplicial sphere (that is, a simplicial sphere that is not the boundary of a polytope). [Gil Kalai](wikipedia://en/19709056) proved that, in fact, "most" simplicial spheres are non-polytopal. The smallest example is of dimension *d* = 4 and has *f*<sub>0</sub> = 8 vertices.

The [upper bound theorem](wikipedia://en/40964551) gives upper bounds for the numbers *f*<sub>*i*</sub> of *i*-faces of any simplicial *d*-sphere with *f*<sub>0</sub> = *n* vertices. This conjecture was proved for simplicial convex polytopes by [Peter McMullen](wikipedia://en/40964822) in 1970[^o7] and by [Richard Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397) for general simplicial spheres in 1975.

The ***g*-conjecture**, formulated by McMullen in 1970, asks for a complete characterization of *f*-vectors of simplicial *d*-spheres. In other words, what are the possible sequences of numbers of faces of each dimension for a simplicial *d*-sphere? In the case of polytopal spheres, the answer is given by the ***g*-theorem**, proved in 1979 by Billera and Lee (existence) and Stanley (necessity). It has been conjectured that the same conditions are necessary for general simplicial spheres. The conjecture was proved by [Karim Adiprasito](wikipedia://en/47874333) in December 2018.[^o2][^o8]
