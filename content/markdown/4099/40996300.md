# Central differencing scheme

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Central differencing scheme"}

*Image: CentralDifference.svg.* Figure 1.Comparison of different schemes

In [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), the **central differencing scheme** is a [finite difference method](wikipedia://en/6054681) that optimizes the approximation for the [differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886) in the central node of the considered patch and provides numerical solutions to differential equations.[^o2] It is one of the schemes used to solve the integrated [convection–diffusion equation](wikipedia://en/19662199) and to calculate the transported property Φ at the e and w faces, where *e* and *w* are short for *east* and *west* (compass directions being customarily used to indicate directions on computational grids). The method's advantages are that it is easy to understand and implement, at least for simple material relations; and that its convergence rate is faster than some other finite differencing methods, such as forward and backward differencing. The right side of the convection-diffusion equation, which basically highlights the diffusion terms, can be represented using central difference approximation. To simplify the solution and analysis, linear interpolation can be used logically to compute the cell face values for the left side of this equation, which is nothing but the convective terms. Therefore, cell face values of property for a uniform grid can be written as:[^o3]

:::

$$\Phi_e = \tfrac 1 2 (\Phi_P + \Phi_E)$$

$$\Phi_w = \tfrac 1 2 (\Phi_W + \Phi_P)$$

## Steady-state convection diffusion equation {#Steady-state_convection_diffusion_equation}

The [convection–diffusion equation](wikipedia://en/19662199) is a collective representation of diffusion and convection equations, and describes or explains every physical phenomenon involving convection and diffusion in the transference of particles, energy and other physical quantities inside a [physical system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_system):[^o3]

$$\operatorname{div}(\rho u\varphi) = \operatorname{div}(\Gamma\nabla\varphi)+S_\varphi; \,$$

where $\text{Г}$ is [diffusion coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_coefficient) and $\Phi$ is the [property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property).

## Formulation of steady-state convection diffusion equation {#Formulation_of_steady-state_convection_diffusion_equation}

Formal [integration](wikipedia://en/15532) of steady-state convection–diffusion equation over a [control volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_volume) gives

::: {#o6 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\int_A \, n\cdot(\rho u\varphi)\,dA = \int_A \,n \cdot (\Gamma\nabla\varphi)\,dA + \int_{CV}\,S_\varphi \,dV \tag{Equation (1}$$

:::

This equation represents flux balance in a control volume. The left side gives the net convective flux, and the right side contains the net diffusive flux and the generation or destruction of the property within the control volume.

In the absence of source term equation, one becomes

::: {#o7 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$\frac{d}{dx} (\rho u\varphi) = \frac{d}{dx}\left( \frac{d\varphi}{dx}\right) \tag{Equation (2}$$

:::

[Continuity equation](wikipedia://en/410793):

::: {#o8 .equation cue=numblk name="3"}

$${d \over dx}(\rho u)=0 \tag{Equation (3}$$

:::

*Image: Interpolation value.png.* Figure 2. Interpolation method

Assuming a control volume and integrating equation 2 over control volume gives:

::: {#o9 .equation cue=numblk name="Integrated convection–diffusion equation"}

$$(\rho u\varphi A)_e - (\rho u\varphi A)_w = (\Gamma A d\varphi /dx)_e - (\Gamma A d\varphi /dx)_w \tag{Integrated convection–diffusion equation}$$

:::

Integration of equation 3 yields:

::: {#o10 .equation cue=numblk name="Integrated continuity equation"}

$$(\rho uA)_e - (\rho uA)_w = 0 \tag{Integrated continuity equation}$$

:::

It is convenient to define two variables to represent the convective [mass flux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_flux) per unit area and diffusion conductance at cell faces, for example:

$$F = \rho u$$

$$D = \Gamma / \delta x$$

Assuming $A_e = A_w$, we can write integrated convection–diffusion equation as:

$$F_e \varphi_e - F_w \varphi_w = D_e( \varphi_E - \varphi_P ) - D_w(\varphi_P - \varphi_W)$$

And integrated continuity equation as:

$$F_e - F_w = 0$$

In a central differencing scheme, we try linear interpolation to compute cell face values for convection terms.

For a uniform grid, we can write cell face values of property $\Phi$ as

$$\varphi_e = \tfrac 1 2 (\varphi_E + \varphi_P), \quad \varphi_w = \tfrac 1 2 (\varphi_P + \varphi_W)$$

On substituting this into integrated convection-diffusion equation, we obtain:

$$F_e\frac{\varphi_E + \varphi_P}2 - F_w\frac{\varphi_W + \varphi_P}2 = D_e(\varphi_E - \varphi_P) - D_w(\varphi_P - \varphi_W)$$

And on rearranging:

$$\left[\left(D_w + \frac{F_w}2\right) + \left(D_e - \frac{F_e}2\right) + (F_e - F_w)\right]\varphi_P = \left(D_w + \frac{F_w}2 \right)\varphi_W + \left(D_e - \frac{F_e}2 \right)\varphi_E$$

$$a_P \varphi_P = a_W \varphi_W + a_E\varphi_E$$

## Different aspects of central differencing scheme {#Different_aspects_of_central_differencing_scheme}

### Conservativeness {#Conservativeness}

Conservation is ensured in central differencing scheme since overall flux balance is obtained by summing the net flux through each control volume taking into account the boundary fluxes for the control volumes around nodes 1 and 4.

*Image: Typical Illustration.png.* Figure 3.Typical illustration

Boundary flux for control volume around node 1 and 4

$$\begin{aligned} & \left[\frac{\Gamma_{e_1} (\varphi_2 - \varphi_1)}{ \delta x} - q_A\right] + \left[ \frac {\Gamma_{e_2} (\varphi_3 - \varphi_2)}{ \delta x} - \frac{ \Gamma_{w_2} (\varphi_2 - \varphi_1)}{ \delta x}\right] \\[10pt] + {} & \left[ \frac{ \Gamma_{e_3} (\varphi_4 - \varphi_3)}{\delta x} - \frac{\Gamma_{w_3} (\varphi_3 - \varphi_2)}{\delta x}\right] + \left[q_B - \frac{\Gamma_{w_4} (\varphi_4 - \varphi_3)}{\delta x}\right] = q_B - q_A \end{aligned}$$

because $\Gamma_{e_1} = \Gamma_{w_2} , \Gamma_{e_2} = \Gamma_{w_3} , \Gamma_{e_3} = \Gamma_{w_4}$

### Boundedness {#Boundedness}

Central differencing scheme satisfies first condition of [boundedness](wikipedia://en/48258).

Since $F_e - F_w = 0$ from continuity equation, therefore; $a_P \varphi_P = a_W \varphi_W + a_E\varphi_E$

Another essential requirement for boundedness is that all coefficients of the discretised equations should have the same sign (usually all positive). But this is only satisfied when ([peclet number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peclet_number)) $F_e/D_e < 2$ because for a unidirectional flow ($F_e > 0, F_w > 0$) $a_E = (D_e - F_e/2)$ is always positive if $D_e > F_e/2$

### Transportiveness {#Transportiveness}

It requires that transportiveness changes according to magnitude of peclet number i.e. when pe is zero $\varphi$ is spread in all directions equally and as Pe increases (convection > diffusion) $\varphi$ at a point largely depends on upstream value and less on downstream value. But central differencing scheme does not possess transportiveness at higher pe since Φ at a point is average of neighbouring nodes for all Pe.

### Accuracy {#Accuracy}

The [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) [truncation error](wikipedia://en/9366097) of the central differencing scheme is second order. Central differencing scheme will be accurate only if Pe \< 2. Owing to this limitation, central differencing is not a suitable discretisation practice for general purpose flow calculations.

## Applications of central differencing schemes {#Applications_of_central_differencing_schemes}

- They are currently used on a regular basis in the solution of the [Euler equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_equations) and [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395).
- Results using central differencing approximation have shown noticeable improvements in accuracy in smooth regions.
- [Shock wave](wikipedia://en/146253) representation and [boundary-layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary-layer) definition can be improved on coarse meshes.[^o17]

## Advantages {#Advantages}

- Simpler to program, requires less computer time per step, and works well with multigrid [acceleration](wikipedia://en/2443) techniques
- Has a [free parameter](wikipedia://en/12829161) in conjunction with the fourth-difference dissipation, which is needed to approach a steady state.
- More accurate than the first-order [upwind scheme](wikipedia://en/14645977) if the [Peclet number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peclet_number) is less than 2.[^o17]

## Disadvantages {#Disadvantages}

- Somewhat more dissipative
- Leads to [oscillations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillations) in the solution or divergence if the local Peclet number is larger than 2.[^o20]
