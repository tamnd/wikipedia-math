# Nef line bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nef line bundle"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) on a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) is **nef** if it has nonnegative degree on every [curve](wikipedia://en/253260) in the variety. The classes of nef line bundles are described by a [convex cone](wikipedia://en/3558266), and the possible contractions of the variety correspond to certain faces of the nef cone. In view of the correspondence between line bundles and [divisors](wikipedia://en/1693055) (built from [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149)-1 subvarieties), there is an equivalent notion of a **nef divisor**.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

More generally, a line bundle *L* on a [proper](wikipedia://en/603782) [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X* over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *k* is said to be **nef** if it has nonnegative degree on every (closed [irreducible](wikipedia://en/2652476)) curve in *X*.[^o4] (The **[degree](wikipedia://en/1693055#Divisor_class_group)** of a line bundle *L* on a proper curve *C* over *k* is the degree of the divisor (*s*) of any nonzero rational section *s* of *L*.) A line bundle may also be called an [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859).

The term "nef" was introduced by [Miles Reid](wikipedia://en/5863755) as a replacement for the older terms "arithmetically effective" (Zariski 1962, definition 7.6) and "numerically effective", as well as for the phrase "numerically eventually free".[^o6] The older terms were misleading, in view of the examples below.

Every line bundle *L* on a proper curve *C* over *k* which has a [global section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_section) that is not identically zero has nonnegative degree. As a result, a [basepoint-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basepoint-free) line bundle on a proper scheme *X* over *k* has nonnegative degree on every curve in *X*; that is, it is nef.[^o7] More generally, a line bundle *L* is called **semi-ample** if some positive [tensor power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_product_of_line_bundles) $L^{\otimes a}$ is basepoint-free. It follows that a semi-ample line bundle is nef. Semi-ample line bundles can be considered the main geometric source of nef line bundles, although the two concepts are not equivalent; see the examples below.

A [Cartier divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartier_divisor) *D* on a proper scheme *X* over a field is said to be nef if the [associated line bundle](wikipedia://en/1693055) *O*(*D*) is nef on *X*. Equivalently, *D* is nef if the [intersection number](wikipedia://en/384327) $D\cdot C$ is nonnegative for every curve *C* in *X*.

To go back from line bundles to divisors, the **first Chern class** is the isomorphism from the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) of line bundles on a variety *X* to the group of Cartier divisors modulo [linear equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_equivalence). Explicitly, the first Chern class $c_1(L)$ is the divisor (*s*) of any nonzero rational section *s* of *L*.[^o8]

:::

## The nef cone {#The_nef_cone}

To work with inequalities, it is convenient to consider **R**-divisors, meaning finite [linear combinations](wikipedia://en/55632) of Cartier divisors with [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) coefficients. The **R**-divisors modulo [numerical equivalence](wikipedia://en/25384599) form a real [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $N^1(X)$ of finite dimension, the [Néron–Severi group](wikipedia://en/1873706) [tensored](wikipedia://en/2993836) with the real numbers.[^o10] (Explicitly: two **R**-divisors are said to be numerically equivalent if they have the same intersection number with all curves in *X*.) An **R**-divisor is called nef if it has nonnegative degree on every curve. The nef **R**-divisors form a closed convex cone in $N^1(X)$, the **nef cone** Nef(*X*).

The **[cone of curves](wikipedia://en/15098681)** is defined to be the convex cone of linear combinations of curves with nonnegative real coefficients in the real vector space $N_1(X)$ of 1-cycles modulo numerical equivalence. The vector spaces $N^1(X)$ and $N_1(X)$ are [dual](wikipedia://en/7988) to each other by the intersection pairing, and the nef cone is (by definition) the [dual cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_cone) of the cone of curves.[^o11]

A significant problem in algebraic geometry is to analyze which line bundles are [ample](wikipedia://en/1186896), since that amounts to describing the different ways a variety can be embedded into projective space. One answer is **Kleiman's criterion** (1966): for a projective scheme *X* over a field, a line bundle (or **R**-divisor) is ample if and only if its class in $N^1(X)$ lies in the interior of the nef cone.[^o12] (An **R**-divisor is called ample if it can be written as a positive linear combination of ample Cartier divisors.) It follows from Kleiman's criterion that, for *X* projective, every nef **R**-divisor on *X* is a limit of ample **R**-divisors in $N^1(X)$. Indeed, for *D* nef and *A* ample, *D* + *cA* is ample for all real numbers *c* > 0.

## Metric definition of nef line bundles {#Metric_definition_of_nef_line_bundles}

Let *X* be a [compact complex manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_complex_manifold) with a fixed [Hermitian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_metric), viewed as a positive [(1,1)-form](wikipedia://en/2497815) $\omega$. Following [Jean-Pierre Demailly](wikipedia://en/53350424), Thomas Peternell and Michael Schneider, a [holomorphic line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_line_bundle) *L* on *X* is said to be **nef** if for every $\epsilon > 0$ there is a [smooth](wikipedia://en/1408000) Hermitian metric $h_\epsilon$ on *L* whose [curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_vector_bundle) satisfies $\Theta_{h_\epsilon}(L)\geq -\epsilon\omega$. When *X* is projective over **C**, this is equivalent to the previous definition (that *L* has nonnegative degree on all curves in *X*).[^o14]

Even for *X* projective over **C**, a nef line bundle *L* need not have a Hermitian metric *h* with curvature $\Theta_h(L)\geq 0$, which explains the more complicated definition just given.[^o15]

## Examples {#Examples}

- If *X* is a smooth projective surface and *C* is an (irreducible) curve in *X* with self-intersection number $C^2\geq 0$, then *C* is nef on *X*, because any two *distinct* curves on a surface have nonnegative intersection number. If $C^2<0$, then *C* is effective but not nef on *X*. For example, if *X* is the [blow-up](wikipedia://en/1817790) of a smooth projective surface *Y* at a point, then the exceptional curve *E* of the blow-up $\pi\colon X\to Y$ has $E^2=-1$.
- Every effective divisor on a [flag manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_manifold) or [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) is nef, using that these varieties have a [transitive action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_action) of a connected [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924).[^o18]
- Every line bundle *L* of degree 0 on a smooth complex projective curve *X* is nef, but *L* is semi-ample if and only if *L* is [torsion](wikipedia://en/3039330) in the Picard group of *X*. For *X* of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) *g* at least 1, most line bundles of degree 0 are not torsion, using that the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/489734) of *X* is an abelian variety of dimension *g*.
- Every semi-ample line bundle is nef, but not every nef line bundle is even numerically equivalent to a semi-ample line bundle. For example, [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594) constructed a line bundle *L* on a suitable [ruled surface](wikipedia://en/705158#Ruled_surfaces_in_algebraic_geometry) *X* such that *L* has positive degree on all curves, but the intersection number $c_1(L)^2$ is zero.[^o19] It follows that *L* is nef, but no positive multiple of $c_1(L)$ is numerically equivalent to an effective divisor. In particular, the space of global sections $H^0(X,L^{\otimes a})$ is zero for all positive integers *a*.

:::

## Contractions and the nef cone {#Contractions_and_the_nef_cone}

A **[contraction](wikipedia://en/52622473)** of a [normal](wikipedia://en/2629423) projective variety *X* over a field *k* is a surjective morphism $f\colon X\to Y$ with *Y* a normal projective variety over *k* such that $f_*\mathcal{O}_X=\mathcal{O}_Y$. (The latter condition implies that *f* has [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) fibers, and it is equivalent to *f* having connected fibers if *k* has [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero.[^o21]) A contraction is called a **fibration** if dim(*Y*) \< dim(*X*). A contraction with dim(*Y*) = dim(*X*) is automatically a [birational morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birational_morphism).[^o22] (For example, *X* could be the blow-up of a smooth projective surface *Y* at a point.)

A **face** *F* of a convex cone *N* means a convex subcone such that any two points of *N* whose sum is in *F* must themselves be in *F*. A contraction of *X* determines a face *F* of the nef cone of *X*, namely the intersection of Nef(*X*) with the [pullback](wikipedia://en/1665392) $f^*(N^1(Y))\subset N^1(X)$. Conversely, given the variety *X*, the face *F* of the nef cone determines the contraction $f\colon X\to Y$ up to isomorphism. Indeed, there is a semi-ample line bundle *L* on *X* whose class in $N^1(X)$ is in the interior of *F* (for example, take *L* to be the pullback to *X* of any ample line bundle on *Y*). Any such line bundle determines *Y* by the [Proj construction](wikipedia://en/2285000):[^o23]

$$Y=\text{Proj }\bigoplus_{a\geq 0}H^0(X,L^{\otimes a}).$$

To describe *Y* in geometric terms: a curve *C* in *X* maps to a point in *Y* if and only if *L* has degree zero on *C*.

As a result, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the contractions of *X* and some of the faces of the nef cone of *X*.[^o24] (This correspondence can also be formulated dually, in terms of faces of the cone of curves.) Knowing which nef line bundles are semi-ample would determine which faces correspond to contractions. The [cone theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_theorem) describes a significant class of faces that do correspond to contractions, and the [abundance conjecture](wikipedia://en/56768957) would give more.

Example: Let *X* be the blow-up of the complex projective plane $\mathbb{P}^2$ at a point *p*. Let *H* be the pullback to *X* of a line on $\mathbb{P}^2$, and let *E* be the exceptional curve of the blow-up $\pi\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^2$. Then *X* has Picard number 2, meaning that the real vector space $N^1(X)$ has dimension 2. By the geometry of convex cones of dimension 2, the nef cone must be spanned by two rays; explicitly, these are the rays spanned by *H* and *H* − *E*.[^o25] In this example, both rays correspond to contractions of *X*: *H* gives the birational morphism $X\to\mathbb{P}^2$, and *H* − *E* gives a fibration $X\to\mathbb{P}^1$ with fibers isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1$ (corresponding to the lines in $\mathbb{P}^2$ through the point *p*). Since the nef cone of *X* has no other nontrivial faces, these are the only nontrivial contractions of *X*; that would be harder to see without the relation to convex cones.
