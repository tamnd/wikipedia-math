# Ton of refrigeration

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ton of refrigeration"}

A **ton of refrigeration** (**TR** or **TOR**), also called a **refrigeration ton** (**RT**), is a [unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_measurement) of [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_%28physics%29) used in some countries (especially in North America) to describe the heat-extraction rate of [refrigeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrigeration) and [air conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioning) equipment. It was originally defined as the rate of [heat transfer](wikipedia://en/184726) that results in the freezing or melting of 1 ST of pure [ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice) at 0 °C in 24 hours.[^o3][^o4]

:::

The modern definition is exactly 12,000 [Btu<sub>IT</sub>/h](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_thermal_unit#Btu/h) (12,000). Air-conditioning and refrigeration equipment capacity in the U.S. is often specified in "tons" (of refrigeration). Many manufacturers also specify capacity in Btu/h, especially when specifying the performance of smaller equipment.

## History {#History}

The ton of refrigeration is equivalent to the consumption of one [short ton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_ton) of ice per day and originated during the transition from [stored natural ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_cutting) to [mechanical refrigeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrigeration). Just as [horsepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower) and [candlepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlepower) were intuitive units of measure for people living through the transition from [horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse) to steam power[^o6] and from flame-based to [electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_light) lighting, so was the ton of refrigeration an intuitive unit of measure during a [technological change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_change), as the [ice trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_trade) gradually included growing percentages of artificial ice (ice from ice-making [plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_plant)) in addition to its natural ice supplies. The TR unit was developed during the 1880s. Its definition was set at the level of an industry [standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_standard) in 1903, when Thomas Shipley of the [York Manufacturing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_International) led the formation of an [industry association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_association) (the Ice Machine Builders Association of the United States) along with [standardization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standardization) of several equipment specifications.[^o7] In 1904 these efforts led to the founding of the American Society of Refrigerating Engineers (ASRE),[^o7] which was one of the predecessors of [ASHRAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASHRAE).
