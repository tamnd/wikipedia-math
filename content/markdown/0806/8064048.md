# Abu'l-Hasan ibn Ali al-Qalasadi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Abu'l-Hasan ibn Ali al-Qalasadi"}

**Abu'l-Hasan Ali ibn Muhammad al-Qalasadi** (أبو الحسن علي بن محمد بن علي القرشي البسطي; 1412–1486[^o4]) was a [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab)[^o3] [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematics) from [Al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus) specializing in [Islamic inheritance jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_inheritance_jurisprudence). [Franz Woepcke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Woepcke) stated that al-Qalaṣādī was known as one of the most influential voices in [algebraic notation](wikipedia://en/277184) for taking "the first steps toward the introduction of [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) symbolism''. He wrote numerous books on [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) and algebra, including *al-Tabsira fi'lm al-hisab* (التبصير في علم الحساب "*Clarification of the science of arithmetic*").

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Al-Qalaṣādī was born in [Baza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baza,_Granada), an outpost of the [Emirate of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Granada). He received education in [Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada), but continued to support his family in Baza. He published many works and eventually retired to his native Baza.

*Image: Granada by Piri Reis.jpg.* Map of the coasts of the kingdom of Granada by [Piri Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piri_Reis) (16th century).

His works dealt with Algebra and contained the precise mathematical answers to problems in everyday life, such as the composition of medicaments, the calculation of the drop of irrigation canals and the explanation of frauds linked to instruments of measurement. The second part belongs to the already ancient tradition of judicial and cultural mathematics and joins a collection of arithmetical problems presented in the form of poetical riddles.

In 1480 the [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians) forces of [Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Aragon) and [Isabella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_I_of_Castile), "The Catholic Monarchs", raided and often pillaged the city. Al-Qalasādī served in the mountain citadels which were erected in the vicinity of Baza. Al-Qalasādī eventually left his homeland and took refuge with his family in [Béja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Béja), [Tunisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisia), where he died in 1486. Baza was eventually besieged by the forces of Ferdinand and Isabella and then sacked.

Al-Qalasadi spent 8 years studying in [Tlemcen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlemcen), where he wrote his first arithmetic treatise, *The Introduction to the Dust Letters*.[^o6]

## Symbolic algebra {#Symbolic_algebra}

Like his predecessors, al-Qalaṣādī used an [algebraic notation](wikipedia://en/277184). While the 19th century writer Franz Woepcke believed that this algebraic symbolism was created by al-Qalaṣādī, these symbols had actually been used by other mathematicians in North Africa 100 years earlier.[^o4] Al-Qalaṣādī represented [mathematical symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_of_mathematical_symbols) using characters from the [Arabic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_alphabet), where:[^o4]

- ﻭ (*[wa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waw_%28letter%29)*) means "and" for [addition](wikipedia://en/61338) (+)
- إلا (*[illa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illa_%28Arabic%29)*) literally, "except"; means "less" for [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141) (−)
- في (*fi*) literally, "in"; means "times" for [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) (\*)
- على (*ala*) means "over" for [division](wikipedia://en/53696) (/)
- ﺝ (*[j](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ǧīm)*) represents *jadah* meaning "[root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function)"
- ﺵ (*[sh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin_%28letter%29)*) represents *shay**meaning "thing" for a [variable](wikipedia://en/3728109) (x)***
- ﻡ (*[m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mīm)*) represents *maal* for a [square](wikipedia://en/659942) (x<sup>2</sup>)
- ﻙ (*[k](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kāf)*) represents *moka'aab* for a [cube](wikipedia://en/451445) (x<sup>3</sup>)
- ﻝ (*[l](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lām)*) represents *ya'adilu* for [equality](wikipedia://en/90446) (=)
