# Petr Vaníček

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Petr Vaníček"}

**Petr Vaníček** (born 18 July 1935) is a [Czech Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Canadian) [geodesist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesist) and theoretical [geophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_geophysicists) who has made important breakthroughs in theory of [spectrum analysis](wikipedia://en/13070117#Overview) and [geoid](wikipedia://en/176244) computation.

:::

## Main contributions {#Main_contributions}

### Research {#Research}

One of Vaníček's main contributions of general relevance is [least-squares spectral analysis](wikipedia://en/13609399),[^o6] also called the Vaníček method[^o7] and the Gauss-Vaniček method[^o8] — a [frequency spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_spectrum) computation method published in 1969[^o9] and 1971.[^o10] It is based on a [least-squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least-squares) fit of [sinusoids](wikipedia://en/324749) to the data samples, and mitigates the drawbacks of applying [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659) for analyzing long incomplete data records such as most [natural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural) [datasets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dataset).[^o11] Unlike with Fourier analysis, data need not be equally spaced to use Vaníček analysis.

His discoveries in theoretical [geophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysics), the "precise geoid solution"[^o12] in particular, enable millimetre-to-centimetre [accuracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accuracy) in geoid [computation](wikipedia://en/5926), an-[order-of-magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order-of-magnitude) improvement from previous solutions.[^o13][^o14][^o15]

### Service {#Service}

Vaníček initiated the establishing of the [Canadian Geophysical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Geophysical_Union) in 1974, and served as the [Union's president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Canadian_Geophysical_Union) between 1987 and 1989.

He was the first [chairperson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chair_%28official%29) of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) [committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee) for Geodetic Aspects of the [Law of the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Convention_on_the_Law_of_the_Sea) (GALOS), founded in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland), by the [International Association of Geodesy](https://web.archive.org/web/20160926061652/http://www.gfy.ku.dk/~iag/) (IAG) in [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_in_science).[^o17][^o18]

This and other activities led to creation of the technical supplement to the Law of the Sea, TALOS (*Manual on Technical Aspects of the United Nations' Convention on the Law of the Sea*) in 1982, which is on a regular re-issuing schedule by the UN. The *Geodetic Commentary to the TALOS Manual*, largely prepared by Vaníček and published by the [International Hydrographic Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Hydrographic_Organization) in 1996, was incorporated into the Manual.[^o19]

The book *Geodesy: The Concepts*,[^o20] by Vaníček and Krakiwsky, now translated into several languages, is a standard text for both undergraduate and graduate courses in [geodesy](wikipedia://en/12608) worldwide.[^o21][^o22]

Vaníček also served as [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) and [peer-reviewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_Review) for several [scientific journals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_journal) as well as on numerous scientific boards and committees.[^o23]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

### International {#International}

Petr Vaníček is a member of the [International Union of Geodesy and Geophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_of_Geodesy_and_Geophysics), of the [American Geophysical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Geophysical_Union), and of the [Czechoslovak Society of Arts and Sciences (SVU)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Society_of_Arts_and_Sciences_%28SVU%29). He was awarded the Senior Distinguished Scientist Fellowship by the [Alexander von Humboldt Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation), and was a Senior Visiting Scientist with the U.S. [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o31]

Over the course of his career, he taught or performed research at universities and labs across six continents, including the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology) and the [USGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USGS).[^o31]

In 2022 he received the Medal of the [Learned Society of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_Society_of_the_Czech_Republic).[^o26][^o27]

### National {#National}

Petr Vaníček was awarded the [J. Tuzo Wilson Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Tuzo_Wilson_Medal) in 1996, the highest recognition by the [Canadian Geophysical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Geophysical_Union), for his outstanding contributions to Canadian geophysics.[^o29]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Since he was born into a typical [bourgeois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeois) family, Petr Vaníček's wife and children requested to leave [Communist Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Czechoslovakia) during the brief but liberal times of [Prague Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring). They were granted [exit visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit_visa) just before the [Soviet invasion of 1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact_invasion_of_Czechoslovakia). The family reunited in England where he was staying on a 1967 Senior Research Fellowship at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool). Together, they immigrated to Canada in 1969. He has one daughter and two sons.[^o31]

He retired as [Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus) in 2002, after more than thirty years of teaching at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) and the [University of New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Brunswick). He lives in [Fredericton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredericton), Canada.[^o31]
