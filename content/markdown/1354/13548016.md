# Orthogonal Procrustes problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthogonal Procrustes problem"}

The **orthogonal Procrustes problem**[^o2] is a [matrix approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_approximation) problem in [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422). In its classical form, one is given two [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) $A$ and $B$ and asked to find an [orthogonal matrix](wikipedia://en/105620) $\Omega$ which most closely maps $A$ to $B$.[^o3][^o4] Specifically, the orthogonal Procrustes problem is an [optimization problem](wikipedia://en/1126536) given by

:::

$$\begin{aligned} \underset{\Omega}{\text{minimize}}\quad & \|\Omega A-B\|_F \\ \text{subject to}\quad & \Omega^T \Omega = I, \end{aligned}$$

where $\|\cdot\|_F$ denotes the [Frobenius norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_norm). This is a special case of [Wahba's problem](wikipedia://en/31998221) (with identical weights; instead of considering two matrices, in Wahba's problem the columns of the matrices are considered as individual vectors). Another difference is that Wahba's problem tries to find a proper rotation matrix instead of just an orthogonal one.

The name [Procrustes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procrustes) refers to a bandit from Greek mythology who made his victims fit his bed by either stretching their limbs or cutting them off.

## Solution {#Solution}

This problem was originally solved by [Peter Schönemann](wikipedia://en/12807112) in a 1964 thesis, and shortly after appeared in the journal Psychometrika.[^o6]

This problem is equivalent to finding the nearest orthogonal matrix to a given matrix $M=BA^{T}$, i.e. solving the *closest orthogonal approximation problem*

$$\min_R\|R-M\|_F \quad\mathrm{subject\ to}\quad R^T R=I.$$

To find matrix $R$, one uses the [singular value decomposition](wikipedia://en/142207) (for which the entries of $\Sigma$ are non-negative)

$$M=U\Sigma V^T\,\!$$

to write

$$R=UV^T.\,\!$$

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of Solution"}

### Proof of Solution {#Proof_of_Solution}

One proof depends on the basic properties of the [Frobenius inner product](wikipedia://en/52203645) that induces the [Frobenius norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_norm):

$$\begin{aligned} R &= \arg\min_\Omega ||\Omega A-B\|_F^2 \\ &= \arg\min_\Omega \langle \Omega A-B, \Omega A-B \rangle_F \\ &= \arg\min_\Omega \|\Omega A\|_F^2 + \|B\|_F^2 - 2 \langle \Omega A , B \rangle_F \\ &= \arg\min_\Omega \|A\|_F^2 + \|B\|_F^2 - 2 \langle \Omega A , B \rangle_F \\ &= \arg\max_\Omega \langle \Omega A , B \rangle_F \\ &= \arg\max_\Omega \langle \Omega , B A^T \rangle_F \\ &= \arg\max_\Omega \langle \Omega, U\Sigma V^T \rangle_F \\ &= \arg\max_\Omega \langle U^T \Omega V , \Sigma \rangle_F \\ &= \arg\max_\Omega \langle S , \Sigma \rangle_F \quad \text{where } S = U^T \Omega V \\ \end{aligned}$$

This quantity $S$ is an orthogonal matrix (as it is a product of orthogonal matrices) and thus the expression is maximised when $S$ equals the identity matrix $I$. Thus

$$\begin{aligned} I &= U^T R V \\ R &= U V^T \\ \end{aligned}$$

where $R$ is the solution for the optimal value of $\Omega$ that minimizes the norm squared $||\Omega A-B\|_F^2$.

:::

## Generalized/constrained Procrustes problems {#Generalized/constrained_Procrustes_problems}

There are a number of related problems to the classical orthogonal Procrustes problem. One might generalize it by seeking the closest matrix in which the columns are [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal), but not necessarily [orthonormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthonormal).[^o10]

Alternately, one might constrain it by only allowing [rotation matrices](wikipedia://en/856005) (i.e. orthogonal matrices with [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) 1, also known as [special orthogonal matrices](wikipedia://en/105620)). In this case, one can write (using the above decomposition $M=U\Sigma V^T$)

$$R=U\Sigma'V^T,\,\!$$

where $\Sigma'\,\!$ is a modified $\Sigma\,\!$, with the smallest singular value replaced by $\det(UV^T)$ (+1 or -1), and the other singular values replaced by 1, so that the determinant of R is guaranteed to be positive.[^o11] For more information, see the [Kabsch algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabsch_algorithm).
