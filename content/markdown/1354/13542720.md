# Multiplier algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multiplier algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **multiplier algebra**, denoted by *M*(*A*), of a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) *A* is a unital C\*-algebra that is the largest unital C\*-algebra that contains *A* as an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) in a "non-degenerate" way. It is the [noncommutative](wikipedia://en/662624) generalization of [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897). Multiplier algebras were introduced by (Busby 1968).

:::

For example, if *A* is the C\*-algebra of [compact operators on a separable Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/6690773), *M*(*A*) is *B*(*H*), the C\*-algebra of all [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on *H*.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

An ideal *I* in a C\*-algebra *B* is said to be **essential** if *I* ∩ *J* is non-trivial for every ideal *J*. An ideal *I* is essential if and only if *I*<sup>⊥</sup>, the "[orthogonal complement](wikipedia://en/1058833)" of *I* in the [Hilbert C\*-module](wikipedia://en/16059132) *B* is {0}.

Let *A* be a C\*-algebra. Its multiplier algebra *M*(*A*) is any C\*-algebra satisfying the following [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245): for any C\*-algebra *D* containing *A* as an ideal, there exists a unique \*-homomorphism φ: *D* → *M*(*A*) such that *φ* extends the identity homomorphism on *A* and *φ*(*A*<sup>⊥</sup>) = {0}.

Uniqueness up to [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) is specified by the universal property. When *A* is unital, *M*(*A*) = *A*. It also follows from the definition that for any *D* containing *A* as an essential ideal, the multiplier algebra *M*(*A*) contains *D* as a C\*-subalgebra.

The existence of *M*(*A*) can be shown in several ways.

A **double centralizer** of a C\*-algebra *A* is a pair (*L*, *R*) of bounded linear maps on *A* such that *aL*(*b*) = *R*(*a*)*b* for all *a* and *b* in *A*. This implies that ||*L*|| = ||*R*||. The set of double centralizers of *A* can be given a C\*-algebra structure. This C\*-algebra contains *A* as an essential ideal and can be identified as the multiplier algebra *M*(*A*). For instance, if *A* is the compact operators *K*(*H*) on a separable Hilbert space, then each *x* ∈ *B*(*H*) defines a double centralizer of *A* by simply multiplication from the left and right.

Alternatively, *M*(*A*) can be obtained via representations. The following fact will be needed:

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=inline-head name="Lemma"}

**Lemma.** If *I* is an ideal in a C\*-algebra *B*, then any faithful nondegenerate representation *π* of *I* can be extended *uniquely* to *B*.

Now take any faithful nondegenerate representation *π* of *A* on a Hilbert space *H*. The above lemma, together with the universal property of the multiplier algebra, yields that *M*(*A*) is isomorphic to the [idealizer](wikipedia://en/24575820) of *π*(*A*) in *B*(*H*). It is immediate that *M*(*K*(*H*)) = *B*(*H*).

Lastly, let *E* be a Hilbert C\*-module and *B*(*E*) (resp. *K*(*E*)) be the adjointable (resp. compact) operators on *E* *M*(*A*) can be identified via a \*-homomorphism of *A* into *B*(*E*). Something similar to the above lemma is true:

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=inline-head name="Lemma"}

**Lemma.** If *I* is an ideal in a C\*-algebra *B*, then any faithful nondegenerate \*-homomorphism *π* of *I* into *B*(*E*) can be extended *uniquely* to *B*.

Consequently, if *π* is a faithful nondegenerate \*-homomorphism of *A* into *B*(*E*), then *M*(*A*) is isomorphic to the idealizer of *π*(*A*). For instance, *M*(*K*(*E*)) = *B*(*E*) for any Hilbert module *E*.

The C\*-algebra *A* is isomorphic to the compact operators on the Hilbert module *A*. Therefore, *M*(*A*) is the adjointable operators on *A*.

:::

:::

## Strict topology {#Strict_topology}

Consider the topology on *M*(*A*) specified by the [seminorms](wikipedia://en/292067) {*l<sub>a</sub>*, *r<sub>a</sub>*}<sub>*a* ∈ *A*</sub>, where

$$l_a (x) = \|ax\|, \; r_a(x) = \| xa \|.$$

The resulting topology is called the **strict topology** on *M*(*A*). *A* is strictly dense in *M*(*A*) .

When *A* is unital, *M*(*A*) = *A*, and the strict topology coincides with the [norm topology](wikipedia://en/246722). For *B*(*H*) = *M*(*K*(*H*)), the strict topology is the [σ-strong\* topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologies_on_the_set_of_operators_on_a_Hilbert_space). It follows from above that *B*(*H*) is complete in the σ-strong\* topology.

## Commutative case {#Commutative_case}

Let *X* be a [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637), *A* = *C*<sub>0</sub>(*X*), the commutative C\*-algebra of continuous functions that [vanish at infinity](wikipedia://en/461986). Then *M*(*A*) is *C*<sub>*b*</sub>(*X*), the continuous bounded functions on *X*. By the [Gelfand–Naimark theorem](wikipedia://en/315414), one has the isomorphism of C\*-algebras

$$C_b(X) \simeq C(Y)$$

where *Y* is the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/918414) of *C*<sub>*b*</sub>(*X*). *Y* is in fact homeomorphic to the [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897) *βX* of *X*.

## Corona algebra {#Corona_algebra}

The **corona** or **corona algebra** of *A* is the quotient *M*(*A*)/*A*. For example, the corona algebra of the algebra of compact operators on a Hilbert space is the [Calkin algebra](wikipedia://en/945565).

The corona algebra is a noncommutative analogue of the [corona set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona_set) of a topological space.
