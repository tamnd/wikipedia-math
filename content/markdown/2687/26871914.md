# Welington de Melo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Welington de Melo"}

**Welington Celso de Melo** (17 November 1946 – 21 December 2016) was a Brazilian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Known for his contributions to [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632), he served as full professor at [Instituto Nacional de Matemática Pura e Aplicada](wikipedia://en/2334708) from 1980 to 2016. Melo wrote numerous papers, one being a complete description of the topological behavior of 1-dimensional real dynamical systems (co-authored with Marco Martens and Sebastian van Strien).[^o3] He proved the global hyperbolicity of renormalization for $C$<sup>*r*</sup> unimodal maps (co-authored with [Alberto Pinto](wikipedia://en/41312567) and Edson de Faria).[^o4] He was a recipient of the 2003 [TWAS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWAS_Prize).[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born on November 17, 1946, in [Guapé, Minas Gerais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guapé), Welington studied [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) at the [Federal University of Minas Gerais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_University_of_Minas_Gerais) (1969). He adopted mathematics after attending a course in the researcher of the [Elon Lages Lima](wikipedia://en/13531757) Institute in a colloquium in [Poços de Caldas, Minas Gerais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poços_de_Caldas). Invited by Elon to study at [Instituto Nacional de Matemática Pura e Aplicada](wikipedia://en/2334708) he moved to Rio de Janeiro with his wife Gilza in 1970, with [Jacob Palis](wikipedia://en/1540070) his advisor, where Welington completed his doctorate in two years, with a thesis published in the prestigious [Inventiones Mathematicae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventiones_Mathematicae) in 1972. He did his post-doctorate in mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1972. He returned two years later to [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil) to make a career as a researcher at IMPA, where he stayed ever since.[^o7]

Appreciated by different generations of mathematicians, Welington was known and respected for his academic rigor and scientific productivity. Besides mathematics, he was passionate about sailing. On 1987 [Steve Smale](wikipedia://en/40294), [Charles Pugh](wikipedia://en/55128667) and Welington sailed the Pacific in a long journey on Smale's 43-foot *Stardust*.[^o8] On his 30-foot *Doisdu* boat, he sailed at least a hundred days a year, mostly along [Angra dos Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angra_dos_Reis)'s coast. He interpreted the wind with mastery, a phenomenon that can be modeled by dynamic systems, his mathematical specialty. He was proud to have taken on board the *Doisdu* six [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) recipients and many friends of IMPA.

He died on December 21, 2016, at age 70, of complications of a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack).[^o9]
