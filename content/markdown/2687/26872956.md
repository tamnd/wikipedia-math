# Trope (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trope"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), ***trope*** is an archaic term for a singular (meaning special) tangent space of a [variety](wikipedia://en/247152), often a [quartic surface](wikipedia://en/20133775). The term may have been introduced by (txt, p. 202), who defined it as "the reciprocal term to node". It is not easy to give a precise definition, because the term is used mainly in older books and papers on [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), whose definitions are vague and different, and use archaic terminology. The term *trope* is used in the theory of quartic surfaces in [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135), where it is sometimes defined as a tangent space meeting the quartic surface in a conic; for example [Kummer's surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kummer's_surface) has 16 tropes.

:::

(txt, p. 14), describes a trope as a tangent plane where the envelope of nearby tangent planes forms a conic, rather than a plane [pencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil_%28mathematics%29) which we would expect for a generic point. The tangent plane would be tangent to the quartic along the conic, implying that the [Gauss map](wikipedia://en/378881) would have a singular point. (Dolgachev 2012, p. 437)
