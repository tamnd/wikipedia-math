# Gerhard Kowalewski

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gerhard Kowalewski"}

**Gerhard Kowalewski** (27 March 1876 – 21 February 1950) was a German mathematician who introduced the [matrices notation](wikipedia://en/8434205).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Waldemar Hermann Gerhard Kowalewski was born on March 27, 1876, in [Alt Järshagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alt_Järshagen) in [Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pomerania_%281815–1945%29), then part of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire).[^o4] He was the son of Julius Leonard Kowalewski, a school teacher. In 1893 he left home to attend the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg) where his brother Christian Kowalewski was a professor of philosophy and mathematics. There he studied mathematics and philosophy before enrolling at the [University of Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greifswald), before eventually settling at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig), where he earned his doctorate with the thesis *Über eine Kategorie von Transformationsgruppen einer vierdimensionalen Mannigfaltigkeit* in 1898.[^o4] At Leipzig he was a student of [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010), where he was considered one of Lie's most elite and gifted students.[^o5]

## Academia {#Academia}

In 1901 he became an associate professor at the [University of Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greifswald) before moving on to the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) in 1904.[^o4] In 1909 he left for [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague) to assume an appointment to a German-based school, before taking a position with the [German University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University_in_Prague) of Prague in 1912.[^o4] From there he took a position at the [Technical University of Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Dresden), culminating in his appointment as Rector in 1935.[^o4] During 1937—1941, he was an editor of the journal *[Deutsche Mathematik](wikipedia://en/31018614)*. In 1937 he requested leave and was granted such from the Reich Ministry of Education.[^o7] He returned to the German University of Prague in 1939 where he held a position until 1945, which following the expulsion of Germans following the war, fled to [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich) where he established himself at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich).[^o4]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Kowalewski is known for the introduction of the [matrices notation](wikipedia://en/8434205) in 1909 with his work titled *Determinantentheorie*.[^o4] He researched theories of [determinants](wikipedia://en/8468), [transformation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_group), natural and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), and approximation and interpolation, publishing over 100 works, including *Vorlesungen über natürliche Geometrie*, the German translation of [Ernesto Cesàro](wikipedia://en/605144)'s work, *Das Integral und seine geometrischen Anwendungen* in 1910, *Über Bolzanos Nichtdifferenzierbare Stetige Funktion*, *Der Keplersche Korper und andere Bauspiele* in 1938, and his memoir in 1950, *Bestand und Wandel*, as well as 24 mathematical textbooks.[^o4] He was awarded the Lobachevsky Diploma in 1927.[^o4]

Kowalewski was a member of the Saxon Academy of Sciences, the [Société Mathématique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_Mathématique_de_France), and socially associated with members of the Louvre Circle and Prague intellectual elite, which included Berta Fanta, [Oskar Kraus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Kraus), [Franz Kafka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Kafka), [Hugo Bergmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Bergmann), [Philipp Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Frank), [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736), and [Christian von Ehrenfels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_von_Ehrenfels).[^o9]

Kowalewski died in 1950 in [Gräfelfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gräfelfing).

### Nazi party membership {#Nazi_party_membership}

Kowalewski was a member of the Nazi party,[^o11] who said about [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) that "he has been sent to us by Providence."[^o11] This helped him secure his position as Rector at the Technical University of Dresden.
