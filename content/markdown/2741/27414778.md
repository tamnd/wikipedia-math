# Graph bandwidth

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph bandwidth"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **graph bandwidth problem** may be visualized as placing the vertices of a given [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) at distinct integer positions along the [number line](wikipedia://en/287188) so that the length of the longest edge is minimized. Such placement is called **linear graph arrangement**, **linear graph layout** or **linear graph placement**.[^o18] It may be formalized as labeling the $n$ [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) $v_i$ of a graph $G$ with distinct [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $f(v_i)$ so that the quantity $\max\{\,| f(v_i) - f(v_j)| : v_iv_j \in E \,\}$ is minimized, where $E$ is the edge set of $G$.[^o2]

:::

The **weighted graph bandwidth problem** is a [generalization](wikipedia://en/12746) wherein the edges are assigned [weights](wikipedia://en/325806#Weighted_graph) $w_{ij}$ and the [cost function](wikipedia://en/442137) to be minimized is the product of weight with length, $\max\{\, w_{ij} |f(v_i) - f(v_j)| : v_iv_j \in E \,\}$.

In terms of matrices, the (unweighted) graph bandwidth is the minimal [bandwidth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandwidth_%28matrix_theory%29) of a [symmetric matrix](wikipedia://en/126474) which is an [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of the graph. The bandwidth may also be defined as one less than the [maximum clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_clique) size in a [proper interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_interval_graph) supergraph of the given graph, chosen to minimize its clique size.[^o3]

## Bandwidth formulas for some graphs {#Bandwidth_formulas_for_some_graphs}

For several families of graphs, the bandwidth $\varphi(G)$ is given by an explicit formula.

The bandwidth of a [path graph](wikipedia://en/4587190) $P_n$ on $n$ vertices is 1, and the bandwidth of a complete graph $K_m$ is $\varphi(K_n)=n-1$. For the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{m,n}$,

$$\varphi(K_{m,n}) = \lfloor (m-1)/2\rfloor+n,$$

assuming $m \ge n\ge 1$. As a special case of this formula, the [star graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_graph) $S_k = K_{k,1}$ on $k+1$ vertices has bandwidth $\varphi(S_{k}) = \lfloor (k-1)/2\rfloor+1$.[^o6]

For the [hypercube graph](wikipedia://en/6706815) $Q_n$ on $2^n$ vertices the bandwidth is[^o7]

$$\varphi(Q_n)=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1} \binom{m}{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}.$$

The bandwidth of the $m\times n$ [square grid graph](wikipedia://en/19635706) $P_m \times P_n$, that is, the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/2856852) of two path graphs on $m$ and $n$ vertices, is equal to $\min\{m,n\}$.[^o8]

:::

## Bounds {#Bounds}

The bandwidth of a graph can be bounded in terms of various other graph parameters. For instance, letting χ(*G*) denote the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of *G*,

$$\varphi(G) \ge \chi(G) - 1;$$

letting diam(*G*) denote the [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) of *G*, the following inequalities hold:[^o2]

$$\lceil (n-1)/\operatorname{diam}(G) \rceil \le \varphi(G) \le n - \operatorname{diam}(G),$$

where $n$ is the number of vertices in $G$.

If a graph *G* has bandwidth *k*, then its [pathwidth](wikipedia://en/5133142) is at most *k*,[^o3] and its [tree-depth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree-depth) is at most *k* log(*n*/*k*).[^o10] In contrast, as noted in the previous section, the star graph *S*<sub>*k*</sub>, a structurally very simple example of a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560), has comparatively large bandwidth. Observe that the [pathwidth](wikipedia://en/5133142) of *S*<sub>*k*</sub> is 1, and its tree-depth is 2.

Some graph families of bounded degree have sublinear bandwidth: if *T* is a tree of maximum degree at most ∆, then [^o11]

$$\varphi(T) \le \frac{5n}{\log_\Delta n}.$$

More generally, for [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314) of bounded maximum degree at most *∆*, a similar bound holds:[^o12]

$$\varphi(G) \le \frac{20n}{\log_\Delta n}.$$

## Computing the bandwidth {#Computing_the_bandwidth}

Both the unweighted and weighted versions are special cases of the [quadratic bottleneck assignment problem](wikipedia://en/27414761). The bandwidth problem is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard), even for some special cases.[^o14] Regarding the existence of efficient [approximation algorithms](wikipedia://en/563105), it is known that the bandwidth is [NP-hard to approximate](wikipedia://en/20677277) within any constant, and this even holds when the input graphs are restricted to [caterpillar trees](wikipedia://en/27258886) with maximum hair length 2.[^o15] For arbitrary graphs with $n$ vertices, the best [approximation ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_ratio) known is $O(\log^3 n\sqrt{\log\log n})$, using [semidefinite programming](wikipedia://en/4993539).[^o16] For the case of dense graphs, a 3-approximation algorithm is known.[^o17] On the other hand, a number of polynomially-solvable special cases are known.[^o18] A [heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristic) algorithm for obtaining linear graph layouts of low bandwidth is the [Cuthill–McKee algorithm](wikipedia://en/1019406). Fast multilevel algorithm for graph bandwidth computation was proposed in.[^o19]

## Applications {#Applications}

The interest in this problem comes from some application areas.

One area is [sparse matrix](wikipedia://en/341015)/[band matrix](wikipedia://en/1451476) handling, and general algorithms from this area, such as [Cuthill–McKee algorithm](wikipedia://en/1019406), may be applied to find approximate solutions for the graph bandwidth problem.

Another application domain is in [electronic design automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_design_automation). In [standard cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_cell) design methodology, typically standard cells have the same height, and their [placement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placement_%28EDA%29) is arranged in a number of rows. In this context, graph bandwidth problem models the problem of placement of a set of standard cells in a single row with the goal of minimizing the maximal [propagation delay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propagation_delay) (which is assumed to be proportional to wire length).
