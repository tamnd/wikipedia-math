# Łukasiewicz–Moisil algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Łukasiewicz–Moisil algebra"}

**Łukasiewicz–Moisil algebras** (**LM<sub>*n*</sub> algebras**) were introduced in the 1940s by [Grigore Moisil](wikipedia://en/4000394) (initially under the name of **Łukasiewicz algebras**[^o2]) in the hope of giving [algebraic semantics](wikipedia://en/2848519) for the *n*-valued [Łukasiewicz logic](wikipedia://en/11001950). However, in 1956 Alan Rose discovered that for *n* ≥ 5, the Łukasiewicz–Moisil algebra does not [model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_%28mathematical_logic%29) the Łukasiewicz logic. A faithful model for the ℵ<sub>0</sub>-valued (infinitely-many-valued) [Łukasiewicz–Tarski logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łukasiewicz–Tarski_logic) was provided by [C. C. Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._C._Chang)'s [MV-algebra](wikipedia://en/2483815), introduced in 1958. For the axiomatically more complicated (finite) *n*-valued Łukasiewicz logics, suitable algebras were published in 1977 by [Revaz Grigolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revaz_Grigolia) and called [MV<sub>*n*</sub>-algebras](wikipedia://en/2483815#MVn-algebras).[^o3] MV<sub>*n*</sub>-algebras are a subclass of LM<sub>*n*</sub>-algebras, and the inclusion is strict for *n* ≥ 5.[^o4] In 1982 [Roberto Cignoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Cignoli) published some additional constraints that added to LM<sub>*n*</sub>-algebras produce proper models for *n*-valued Łukasiewicz logic; Cignoli called his discovery **proper Łukasiewicz algebras**.[^o5]

:::

Moisil however, published in 1964 a logic to match his algebra (in the general *n* ≥ 5 case), now called **Moisil logic**.[^o3] After coming in contact with [Zadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotfi_A._Zadeh)'s [fuzzy logic](wikipedia://en/49180), in 1968 Moisil also introduced an infinitely-many-valued logic variant and its corresponding **LM<sub>*θ*</sub> algebras**.[^o6] Although the [Łukasiewicz implication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łukasiewicz_implication) cannot be defined in a LM<sub>*n*</sub> algebra for *n* ≥ 5, the [Heyting implication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heyting_implication) can be, i.e. LM<sub>*n*</sub> algebras are [Heyting algebras](wikipedia://en/309343); as a result, Moisil logics can also be developed (from a purely logical standpoint) in the framework of Brower's [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262).[^o7]

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A LM<sub>*n*</sub> algebra is a [De Morgan algebra](wikipedia://en/8495580) (a notion also introduced by Moisil) with *n*-1 additional unary, "modal" operations: $\nabla_1, \ldots, \nabla_{n-1}$, i.e. an algebra of [signature](wikipedia://en/7788156) $(A, \vee, \wedge, \neg, \nabla_{j \in J}, 0, 1)$ where *J* = { 1, 2, ... *n*-1 }. (Some sources denote the additional operators as $\nabla^n_{j \in J}$ to emphasize that they depend on the order *n* of the algebra.[^o10]) The additional unary operators ∇<sub>*j*</sub> must satisfy the following axioms for all *x*, *y* ∈ *A* and *j*, *k* ∈ *J*:[^o4]

1. $$\nabla_j(x \vee y) = (\nabla_j\; x) \vee (\nabla_j\; y)$$
1. $$\nabla_j\;x \vee \neg \nabla_j\;x = 1$$
1. $$\nabla_j (\nabla_k\;x) = \nabla_k\;x$$
1. $$\nabla_j \neg x = \neg \nabla_{n-j}\;x$$
1. $$\nabla_1\;x\leq \nabla_2\;x\cdots\leq \nabla_{n-1}\;x$$
1. if $\nabla_j\;x = \nabla_j\;y$ for all *j* ∈ *J*, then *x* = *y*.

(The adjective "modal" is related to the \[ultimately failed\] program of Tarksi and Łukasiewicz to axiomatize [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) using [many-valued logic](wikipedia://en/38024).)

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Elementary properties"}

## Elementary properties {#Elementary_properties}

The duals of some of the above axioms follow as properties:[^o4]

- $$\nabla_j(x \wedge y) = (\nabla_j\; x) \wedge (\nabla_j\; y)$$
- $$\nabla_j\;x \wedge \neg \nabla_j\;x = 0$$

Additionally: $\nabla_j\;0 =0$ and $\nabla_j\;1 =1$.[^o4] In other words, the unary "modal" operations $\nabla_j$ are lattice [endomorphisms](wikipedia://en/9569).[^o7]

:::

::: {#o14 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

LM<sub>2</sub> algebras are the [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959). The canonical Łukasiewicz algebra $\mathcal{L}_n$ that Moisil had in mind were over the set $L_{n} = \{ 0,\ \frac{1} {n-1}, \frac{2} {n-1}, ... , \frac{n-2} {n-1}\ , 1 \}$ with negation $\neg x = 1-x$ conjunction $x \wedge y = \min\{x, y \}$ and disjunction $x \vee y = \max\{x, y \}$ and the unary "modal" operators:

$$\nabla_j\left(\frac{i}{n-1}\right)= \; \begin{cases} 0 & \mbox{if } i+j < n \\ 1 & \mbox{if } i+j \geq n \\ \end{cases} \quad i \in \{0\} \cup J,\; j \in J.$$

If *B* is a Boolean algebra, then the algebra over the set *B*<sup>\[2\]</sup> ≝ {(*x*, *y*) ∈ *B*×*B* | *x* ≤ *y*} with the lattice operations defined [pointwise](wikipedia://en/4786797) and with ¬(*x*, *y*) ≝ (¬*y*, ¬*x*), and with the unary "modal" operators ∇<sub>2</sub>(*x*, *y*) ≝ (*y*, *y*) and ∇<sub>1</sub>(*x*, *y*) = ¬∇<sub>2</sub>¬(*x*, *y*) = (*x*, *x*) \[derived by axiom 4\] is a three-valued Łukasiewicz algebra.[^o10]

:::

::: {#o16 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Representation"}

## Representation {#Representation}

Moisil proved that every LM<sub>*n*</sub> algebra can be [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119#Universal_algebra_and_model_theory) in a [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887) (of copies) of the canonical $\mathcal{L}_n$ algebra. As a corollary, every LM<sub>*n*</sub> algebra is a [subdirect product](wikipedia://en/17395221) of [subalgebras](wikipedia://en/2057747) of $\mathcal{L}_n$.[^o4]

The Heyting implication can be defined as:[^o7]

$$x \Rightarrow y\; \overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\;y \vee \bigwedge_{j\in J}(\neg\nabla_j\;x) \vee (\nabla_j\;y)$$

[Antonio Monteiro](wikipedia://en/43620657) showed that for every [monadic Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/1018197) one can construct a trivalent Łukasiewicz algebra (by taking certain equivalence classes) and that any trivalent Łukasiewicz algebra is isomorphic to a Łukasiewicz algebra thus derived from a monadic Boolean algebra.[^o10][^o17] Cignoli summarizes the importance of this result as: "Since it was shown by Halmos that monadic Boolean algebras are the algebraic counterpart of classical first order monadic calculus, Monteiro considered that the representation of three-valued Łukasiewicz algebras into monadic Boolean algebras gives a proof of the consistency of Łukasiewicz [three-valued logic](wikipedia://en/249904) relative to classical logic."[^o10]

:::
