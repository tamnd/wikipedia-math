# Timeline of computational mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Timeline of computational mathematics"}

This is a timeline of key developments in [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833).

:::

## 18th century {#18th_century}

- [Anton Felkel](wikipedia://en/3011543) and [Carl Hindenburg](wikipedia://en/545047) (independently of Felkel) build and use mechanical [prime sieves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_sieve) based on the stencil method[^o3][^o4]

## 1890s {#1890s}

- [F. W. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Pethick-Lawrence,_1st_Baron_Pethick-Lawrence) provides a high-level design for a mechanical, automated [prime sieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_sieve) in his paper "Factorisation of numbers".[^o6][^o7][^o8]

## 1910s {#1910s}

- Lawrence's "Factorisation of numbers" is republished in French,[^o10] sparking efforts to build a mechanical prime sieve. Eugène-Olivier Carissan builds his *machine à congruences*, a working mechanical sieve, in 1919.[^o6][^o7][^o11]

## 1920s {#1920s}

- [D. H. Lehmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._H._Lehmer) builds an electromechanical [number sieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_sieve), the "bicycle" [Lehmer sieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehmer_sieve), in 1926[^o3][^o4][^o7]

## 1930s {#1930s}

- D. H. Lehmer builds further Lehmer sieves: the "gear sieve" in 1932 and the "film strip sieve" in 1936[^o3][^o4][^o7]

## 1940s {#1940s}

- Monte Carlo simulation (voted one of the top 10 [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) of the 20th century) invented at Los Alamos by [von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), [Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Ulam) and Metropolis.[^o15][^o16][^o17]
- Dantzig introduces the [simplex algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplex_method) (voted one of the top 10 algorithms of the 20th century).[^o18]
- First [hydro simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_Fluid_Dynamics) at Los Alamos occurred.[^o19][^o20]
- Ulam and von Neumann introduce the notion of cellular automata.[^o21]
- [A routine for the Manchester Baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Small-Scale_Experimental_Machine#First_programs) written to factor a large number (2^18), one of the first in [computational number theory](wikipedia://en/511466).[^o22] The Manchester group would make several other breakthroughs in [this area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_primes).[^o23]
- LU decomposition technique first discovered.

## 1950s {#1950s}

- [Hestenes](wikipedia://en/12223917), [Stiefel](wikipedia://en/12223876), and [Lanczos](wikipedia://en/1657748), all from the Institute for Numerical Analysis at the [National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIST), initiate the development of [Krylov subspace iteration methods](wikipedia://en/15237).[^o25][^o26][^o27][^o28] Voted one of the top 10 algorithms of the 20th century.
- *[Equations of State Calculations by Fast Computing Machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equations_of_State_Calculations_by_Fast_Computing_Machines)* introduces the [Metropolis–Hastings algorithm](wikipedia://en/56107).[^o29] Also, important earlier independent work by Alder and S. Frankel.[^o30][^o31]
- [Enrico Fermi](wikipedia://en/10264), [Stanislaw Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Ulam), [John Pasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Pasta), and [Mary Tsingou](wikipedia://en/37784250), discover the [Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou problem](wikipedia://en/2864857).[^o32]
- In network theory, Ford & Fulkerson compute [a solution to the maximum flow problem](wikipedia://en/53777).[^o33]
- Householder invents his [eponymous matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Householder_matrix) and [transformation method](wikipedia://en/485424) (voted one of the top 10 algorithms of the 20th century).[^o34]
- Molecular dynamics invented by Alder and Wainwright[^o35]
- [John G.F. Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_G.F._Francis)[^o36] and [Vera Kublanovskaya](wikipedia://en/18387851)[^o37] invent [QR factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QR_factorization) (voted one of the top 10 algorithms of the 20th century).

## 1960s {#1960s}

- [First recorded use](wikipedia://en/18233581#History) of the term "finite element method" by [Ray Clough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_W._Clough),[^o39] to describe the methods of Courant, Hrenikoff and Zienkiewicz, among others. See also [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_analysis#Timeline).
- Using computational investigations of the [3-body problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-body_problem), Minovitch formulates the [gravity assist](wikipedia://en/155758) method.[^o40][^o41]
- Molecular dynamics was invented independently by [Aneesur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aneesur_Rahman).[^o42]
- Cooley and Tukey re-invent the [Fast Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/11512) (voted one of the top 10 algorithms of the 20th century), an algorithm first discovered by [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss).
- [Edward Lorenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Lorenz) discovers the [butterfly effect](wikipedia://en/4024) on a computer, attracting interest in [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295).[^o43]
- [Kruskal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kruskal) and [Zabusky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Zabusky) follow up the [Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou problem](wikipedia://en/2864857) with further numerical experiments, and coin the term "soliton".[^o44][^o45]
- [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161) formulated through investigations on a computer.[^o46]
- Grobner bases and Buchberger's algorithm invented for algebra[^o47]
- Frenchman Verlet (re)discovers [a numerical integration algorithm](wikipedia://en/825735),[^o48] (first used in 1791 by Delambre, by Cowell and Crommelin in 1909, and by [Carl Fredrik Störmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredrik_Carl_Størmer) in 1907,[^o49] hence the alternative names Störmer's method or the Verlet-Störmer method) for dynamics.[^o48]
- Risch invents algorithm for symbolic integration.[^o50]

## 1970s {#1970s}

- Mandelbrot, from studies of the [Fatou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatou_set), [Julia](wikipedia://en/56434) and [Mandelbrot sets](wikipedia://en/19562), coined and popularized the term 'fractal' to describe these structures' [self-similarity](wikipedia://en/28782).[^o52][^o53]
- Kenneth Appel and Wolfgang Haken prove the [four colour theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_colour_theorem), the [first theorem to be proved by computer](wikipedia://en/2840305#Theorems_proved_with_the_help_of_computer_programs).[^o54][^o55][^o56]

## 1980s {#1980s}

- [Fast multipole method](wikipedia://en/8280771) invented by Rokhlin and Greengard (voted one of the top 10 algorithms of the 20th century).[^o58][^o59][^o60]

## 1990s {#1990s}

- The appearance of the first research grids using [volunteer computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteer_computing) – [GIMPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIMPS) (1996) and [distributed.net](wikipedia://en/337011) (1997).
- [Kepler conjecture](wikipedia://en/368311) is [almost all but certainly proved](wikipedia://en/359970) algorithmically by [Thomas Hales](wikipedia://en/2388356) in 1998.

## 2000s {#2000s}

- In computational group theory, [God's Number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_solutions_for_Rubik's_Cube) for the [Rubik's Cube](wikipedia://en/25971) is shown to be 20.[^o63][^o64]
- Mathematicians completely map the E8-group.[^o65][^o66][^o67]

## 2010s {#2010s}

- Hales completes the proof of Kepler's conjecture.[^o69][^o70][^o71]
