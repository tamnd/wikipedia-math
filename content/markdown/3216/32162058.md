# Jacquet module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacquet module"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Jacquet module** is a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) used in the study of [automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representations). The **Jacquet functor** is the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) that sends a [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) to its Jacquet module. They are both named after [Hervé Jacquet](wikipedia://en/25600925).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Jacquet module *J*(*V*) of a representation (*π*,*V*) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *N* is the space of co-invariants of *N*; or in other words the largest quotient of *V* on which *N* acts trivially, or the zeroth [homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) H<sub>0</sub>(*N*,*V*). In other words, it is the quotient *V*/*V<sub>N</sub>* where *V<sub>N</sub>* is the subspace of *V* generated by elements of the form *π*(*n*)*v* - *v* for all *n* in *N* and all *v* in *V*.

The Jacquet functor *J* is the functor taking *V* to its Jacquet module *J*(*V*).

:::

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Jacquet modules are used to classify admissible irreducible representations of a [reductive algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_algebraic_group) *G* over a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375), and *N* is the [unipotent radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unipotent_radical) of a [parabolic subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) of *G*. In the case of *p*-adic groups, they were studied by (txt).

For the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) GL(2), the Jacquet module of an admissible irreducible representation has [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) at most two. If the dimension is zero, then the representation is called a [supercuspidal representation](wikipedia://en/6043458). If the dimension is one, then the representation is a [special representation](wikipedia://en/5862365). If the dimension is two, then the representation is a [principal series representation](wikipedia://en/10620358).

:::
