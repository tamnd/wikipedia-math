# Baer–Specker group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Baer–Specker group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Baer–Specker group**, or **Specker group**, named after [Reinhold Baer](wikipedia://en/1612114) and [Ernst Specker](wikipedia://en/22209158), is an example of an [infinite](wikipedia://en/4587030) [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) which is a building block in the structure theory of such groups.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Baer–Specker group is the group $B=\mathbb{Z}^\mathbb{N}$ of all [integer sequences](wikipedia://en/51421) with componentwise addition, that is, the [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of [countably infinitely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite) many copies of $\mathbb Z$. It can equivalently be described as the additive group of [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) with integer [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

[Reinhold Baer](wikipedia://en/1612114) proved in 1937 that this group is *not* [free abelian](wikipedia://en/247296); Specker proved in 1950 that every countable [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of $B$ is free abelian.

The group of [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/12396) from the Baer–Specker group to a free abelian group of finite rank is a free abelian group of countable rank. This provides another proof that the group is not free.[^o6]

:::

[^o6]: (Blass Göbel 1996) attribute this result to (Specker 1950). They write it in the form $P^*\cong S$ where $P$ denotes the Baer-Specker group, the star operator gives the dual group of homomorphisms to $\mathbb{Z}$, and $S$ is the free abelian group of countable rank. They continue, "It follows that $P$ has no direct summand isomorphic to $S$", from which an immediate consequence is that $P$ is not free abelian.
