# David George Kendall

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David George Kendall"}

**David George Kendall** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o4] (15 January 1918 – 23 October 2007)[^o12] was an English [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his work on probability, statistical shape analysis, ley lines and [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578). He spent most of his academic life in the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) (1946–1962) and the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) (1962–1985). He worked with [M. S. Bartlett](wikipedia://en/865429) during World War II, and visited [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) after the war.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

David George Kendall was born on 15 January 1918 in [Ripon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripon), [West Riding of Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Riding_of_Yorkshire), and attended [Ripon Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripon_Grammar_School) before attending [Queen's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_College,_Oxford), graduating in 1939.[^o4][^o7][^o8]

He worked on [rocketry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket) at the [Ministry of Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Supply)'s Projectile Development Establishment during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), before moving to [Magdalen College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford), [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), in 1946.

In 1962 he was appointed the first [Professor of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professorship_of_Mathematical_Statistics,_University_of_Cambridge) in the [Statistical Laboratory, University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_Laboratory,_University_of_Cambridge); in which post he remained until his retirement in 1985. He was elected to a professorial fellowship at [Churchill College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge), and he was a founding trustee of the [Rollo Davidson Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollo_Davidson_Prize). In 1986, he was awarded an Honorary Degree (Doctor of Science) by the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath).[^o9]

Kendall was an expert in [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and data analysis, and pioneered [statistical shape analysis](wikipedia://en/8707155) including the study of [ley lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ley_lines). He defined [Kendall's notation](wikipedia://en/7345405) for [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578).

The [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) awarded him the [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Silver in 1955, followed in 1981 by the Guy Medal in Gold. In 1980 the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) awarded Kendall their [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize), and in 1989 their [De Morgan Medal](wikipedia://en/1096532).[^o10] He was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1964. Kendall also played a key role in founding the [Bernoulli Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_Society) in 1975, and was its initial president.[^o11]

He was married to Diana Fletcher from 1952 until his death. They had two sons and four daughters, including [Wilfrid Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfrid_Kendall), professor in the Department of Statistics at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick), and journalist [Bridget Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridget_Kendall) [MBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire).[^o12]
