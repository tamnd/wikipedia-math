# Tonal system (Nystrom)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tonal system"}

*Image: Hexadecimal Clock by Nystrom.jpg.* Proposed clock-face with noon on topThe proposed figures on the right are based on rotations of those on the left (assigning value 10 to symbol 9).

The **tonal system** is a [base 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_16) system of notation (predating the widespread use of [hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263) in computing), [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118), and [metrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrology) proposed in 1859 by [John W. Nystrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Nystrom).[^o2] In addition to new weights and measures, his proposal included a new [calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar) with sixteen months, a new system of [coinage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency), and a [clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexadecimal_clock) with sixteen major divisions of the day (called tims). Nystrom advocated his system thus:

:::

I am not afraid, or do not hesitate, to advocate a binary system of arithmetic and metrology. I know I have nature on my side; if I do not succeed to impress upon you its utility and great importance to mankind, it will reflect that much less credit upon our generation, upon scientific men and philosophers.[^o3]

## Names for the numbers {#Names_for_the_numbers}

He proposed names for the digits, calling zero "noll" and counting (from one to sixteen):

"An, de, ti, go, su, by, ra, me, ni, ko, hu, vy, la, po, fy, ton." (Therefore, *ton*al system.)

Because hexadecimal requires sixteen digits, Nystrom supplemented the existing decimal digits 0 through 9 with his own invented characters (shown on his clockface above) and changed the value of 9 to ten. (Unicode approximation: ⬯𐑑߶ƷႷ5678𝓈9꒹🝣𐐁ꯖⳠ) Later, the hexadecimal notation overcame this same obstacle by using the digits 0 through 9 followed by the letters A through F.

The numbers 11<sub>16</sub> and 12<sub>16</sub> would be said "tonan", "tonde", etc. The table below shows Nystrom's names for successive powers of 10<sub>16</sub>.

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

| Base 16 Number | Tonal Name | Decimal Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | ton | 16 |
| 100 | san | 256 |
| 1000 | mill | 4,096 |
| 1,0000 | bong | 65,536 |
| 10,0000 | tonbong | 1,048,576 |
| 100,0000 | sanbong | 16,777,216 |
| 1000,0000 | millbong | 268,435,456 |
| 1,0000,0000 | tam | 4,294,967,296 |
| 1,0000,0000,0000 | song | 16^12 |
| 1,0000,0000,0000,0000 | tran | 16^16 |
| 1,0000,0000,0000,0000,0000 | bongtran | 16^20 |

:::

Thus, the hexadecimal number 1510,0000 would be "mill-susanton-bong". This first hexadecimal system, proposed in the 19th century, has thus far not achieved widespread usage.

Although Nystrom did not propose a language for tonal fractions, his nomenclature for units of measure does provide one: the name of a power of sixteen *before* the base unit's name multiplies it by that number, but a power of sixteen *after* the base unit's name divides it by that number. Thus, *de timtons* means $\tfrac{1}{8}$ *tim*.

## Geography {#Geography}

*Image: Hexadecimal compass by Nystrom.jpg.* Nystrom's hexadecimal compass

For latitudes he put 0 at the [North Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pole), 4 at the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator) and 8 at the [South Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pole). The units were called *tims*. They are the same as the [colatitudes](wikipedia://en/1828681) measured in [turns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_%28geometry%29) times 16.

## Music {#Music}

*Image: Nystrom tonal system.jpg.* A page in the tonal system book

In his book he made a reference to music notation, where binary division is already in use for time. He also discussed the problem of [pitch inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_pitch#Pitch_inflation), which he proposed to solve by setting the A below middle C to a frequency of san per timmill (194 Hz).
