# Bihari–LaSalle inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bihari–LaSalle inequality"}

The **Bihari–LaSalle inequality** was proved by the American mathematician [Joseph P. LaSalle](wikipedia://en/47624922) (1916–1983) in 1949[^o2] and by the Hungarian mathematician Imre Bihari (1915–1998) in 1956.[^o3] It is the following nonlinear generalization of [Grönwall's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grönwall's_lemma).

:::

Let *u* and *ƒ* be non-negative [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) defined on the half-infinite [ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_%28mathematics%29) \[0, ∞), and let *w* be a continuous [non-decreasing function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-decreasing_function) defined on \[0, ∞) and *w*(*u*) > 0 on (0, ∞). If *u* satisfies the following [integral](wikipedia://en/15532) [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489),

$$u(t)\leq \alpha+ \int_0^t f(s)\,w(u(s))\,ds,\qquad t\in[0,\infty),$$

where $\alpha$ is a non-negative [constant](wikipedia://en/24758132), then

$$u(t)\leq G^{-1}\left(G(\alpha)+\int_0^t\,f(s) \, ds\right),\qquad t\in[0,T],$$

where the function *G* is defined by

$$G(x)=\int_{x_0}^x \frac{dy}{w(y)},\qquad x \geq 0,\,x_0>0,$$

and *G*<sup>−1</sup> is the [inverse function](wikipedia://en/14907) of *G* and *T* is chosen so that

$$G(\alpha)+\int_0^t\,f(s)\,ds\in \operatorname{Dom}(G^{-1}),\qquad \forall \, t \in [0,T].$$
