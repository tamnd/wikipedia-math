# James Lockhart (banker)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Lockhart"}

**James Lockhart** (1763–1852) was an English banker who wrote on [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).[^o2]

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was one of three children and two sons of James Lockhart (1735–1814), a Scottish banker, and his wife Mary Harriot Gray or Grey, from a Quaker family; [John Ingram Lockhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ingram_Lockhart) was his brother, and the daughter was Mary Harriett, married name Greenwollers. He was educated at [Reading School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_School) and [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College).[^o2][^o4][^o5][^o6]

Lockhart worked as a banker in [Pall Mall, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pall_Mall,_London), in his father's firm Lockhart, Wallace, & Co. He became a partner, and set up the Cattle Insurance Company.[^o2][^o7][^o8] Retiring from the bank in 1799, he moved north to the [Windermere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windermere) area after the death of his first wife.[^o2]

Lockhart knew [Walking Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walking_Stewart), and stated he had learned from him. He knew also [William Combe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Combe), and the idea for Combe's "Dr Syntax" has been attributed to him. He taught mathematics to his own children, and pupils including [Joshua King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_King_%28mathematician%29).[^o2]

For about 15 years Lockhart and his family resided in the Netherlands, from 1819, in [Leyden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyden) and then [Haarlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haarlem). He returned to England on the death in 1835 of his brother John, who left no heir.[^o2]

Living first at [Aylesbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aylesbury), Lockhart moved to the rectory at [Cowley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowley,_Oxford) in 1837. After a legal case on his brother's will, the estate at Cowley was sold in 1841. He moved to [Brompton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brompton,_London).[^o2] In 1847 his address was given as Lanhams, near [Braintree, Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braintree,_Essex).[^o9]

Taking a lease on shooting on the Scottish island of [Raasay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raasay), Lockhart spent time from 1845 on the [Isle of Skye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Skye). He then lived in an [Argyllshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argyllshire) mansion. He moved in 1851 to Clitsome House near [Washford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washford) in Somerset.[^o2] He died there the following year.[^o10]

## Works {#Works}

Lockhart proposed challenge problems on the separation of the roots of equations of degree five and six, some being published in 1841.[^o12] He was still writing on the theory of equations while in Argyllshire.[^o13] The challenges were considered by [Florian Cajori](wikipedia://en/542738) to have influenced the work of [John Radford Young](wikipedia://en/39667884);[^o14] [William Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Rutherford_%28mathematician%29) noted an analysis by Young of an equation proposed by Lockhart, in a book of 1849.[^o15]

Detailed analysis of a quintic using [Budan's theorem](wikipedia://en/35083603) for separation of roots was given in 1842, by James R. Christie;[^o16] it was noted by Young.[^o17] In 1843 Young commented that Budan's approach, and Lockhart's own ideas, could now be simplified on the basis of recent developments, which had led to [Sturm's theorem](wikipedia://en/1221919).[^o18] Young also commended books by Lockhart in discussing a problem on roots proposed by [John Pell](wikipedia://en/491570) to [Silius Titus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silius_Titus).[^o19] Lockhart and Young then influenced the subsequent work of [Rehuel Lobatto](wikipedia://en/23918641).[^o20]

Lockhart's books were mostly self-published:

- *A Method of Approximating Towards the Roots of Cubic Equations Belonging to the Irreducible Case* (1813)[^o21]
- *Nieuwe en algemeene leerwijze om biquadraten op te lossen waarbij de systemas van Descartes en Euler tot biquadraten met derzelver tweede termen worden voortgezet* (1823)[^o22]
- *Nieuwe oplossing van cubiek-vergelykingen door juiste uitdrukkingen, en ook bij nadering, zonder beproeving of gissing* (1825)[^o23]
- *Extension of the celebrated theorem of C. Sturm, whereby the roots of numeral equations may be separated from each other, with copious examples* (1839)[^o24]
- *Resolution of Two Equations* (1839)[^o25]
- *Resolution of Equations by Means of Inferior and Superior Limits* (1842)[^o26]
- *The Nature of the Roots of Numerical Equations* (1850)[^o27]

## Family {#Family}

Lockhart married twice. With his first wife, Mary Coxe, daughter of Leonard Coxe of [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia), a dispossessed loyalist of the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution), he had three daughters and a son.[^o2][^o29] With his second wife Elizabeth, whom he married in 1805, he had six sons and two daughters; she died in 1843 at age 56.[^o2][^o30] Lockhart's children included:

- James, the eldest son, a barrister and poet;[^o31][^o32] James Augustus Lockhart, officer in the [41st Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/41st_%28Welsh%29_Regiment_of_Foot), killed at age 21 on 8 September 1855 in the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) was his son.[^o33][^o34]
- [John Ingram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ingram_Lockhart_%28writer%29) (1812–1889), a writer and friend of [Nicolaas Beets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaas_Beets).[^o35]
- Miles, who married Anna Rebecca Charlotte, daughter of Major Robert Stewart of the [94th Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/94th_Regiment_of_Foot);[^o36] [James Haldane Stewart Lockhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Haldane_Stewart_Lockhart) was their son.[^o37]
- Maria, who married John Smith of [Ellingham Hall, Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellingham_Hall,_Norfolk) in 1828.[^o38][^o39]
