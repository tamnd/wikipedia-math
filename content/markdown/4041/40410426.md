# Cohn-Vossen's inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cohn-Vossen's inequality"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), **Cohn-Vossen's inequality**, named after [Stefan Cohn-Vossen](wikipedia://en/3204307), relates the integral of [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) of a non-compact [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_%28differential_geometry%29) to the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420). It is akin to the [Gauss–Bonnet theorem](wikipedia://en/139229) for a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) surface.

:::

A **divergent path** within a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) is a smooth curve in the manifold that is not contained within any [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) subset of the manifold. A **complete manifold** is one in which every divergent path has infinite [length](wikipedia://en/1898401) with respect to the Riemannian metric on the manifold. Cohn-Vossen's inequality states that in every complete Riemannian 2-manifold *S* with finite [total curvature](wikipedia://en/19172363) and finite Euler characteristic, we have[^o2]

$$\iint_S K \, dA \le 2\pi\chi(S),$$

where *K* is the Gaussian curvature, *dA* is the element of area, and *χ* is the Euler characteristic.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If *S* is a compact surface (without boundary), then the inequality is an equality by the usual Gauss–Bonnet theorem for compact manifolds.
- If *S* has a boundary, then the Gauss–Bonnet theorem gives

$$\iint_S K\, dA = 2\pi\chi(S) - \int_{\partial S}k_g\,ds$$

where $k_g$ is the [geodesic curvature](wikipedia://en/483526) of the boundary, and its integral the [total curvature](wikipedia://en/19172363) which is necessarily positive for a boundary curve, and the inequality is strict. (A similar result holds when the boundary of *S* is piecewise smooth.)

- If *S* is the plane **R**<sup>2</sup>, then the curvature of *S* is zero, and *χ*(*S*) = 1, so the inequality is strict: 0 \< 2$\pi$.

:::
