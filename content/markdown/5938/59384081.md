# Isotropy representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isotropy representation"}

In differential geometry, the **isotropy representation** is a natural [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) of a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945), that is [acting](wikipedia://en/39904936) on a manifold, on the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) to a fixed point.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Given a [Lie group action](wikipedia://en/39904936) $(G, \sigma)$ on a manifold *M*, if *G*<sub>*o*</sub> is the [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_%28group_theory%29) of a point *o* (isotropy subgroup at *o*), then, for each *g* in *G*<sub>*o*</sub>, $\sigma_g: M \to M$ fixes *o* and thus taking the derivative at *o* gives the map $(d\sigma_g)_o: T_o M \to T_o M.$ By the [chain rule](wikipedia://en/6113),

$$(d \sigma_{gh})_o = d (\sigma_g \circ \sigma_h)_o = (d \sigma_g)_o \circ (d \sigma_h)_o$$

and thus there is a representation:

$$\rho: G_o \to \operatorname{GL}(T_o M)$$

given by

$$\rho(g) = (d \sigma_g)_o.$$

It is called the isotropy representation at *o*. For example, if $\sigma$ is a [conjugation](wikipedia://en/61839) action of *G* on itself, then the isotropy representation $\rho$ at the identity element *e* is the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) of $G = G_e$.

:::
