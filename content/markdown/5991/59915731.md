# Davenport–Erdős theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Davenport–Erdős theorem"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **Davenport–Erdős theorem** states that, for sets of multiples of integers, several different notions of [density](wikipedia://en/1819983) are equivalent.

:::

Let $A=a_1,a_2,\dots$ be a sequence of [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer). Then the multiples of $A$ are another set $M(A)$ that can be defined as the set $M(A)=\{ka\mid k\in\mathbb{N}, a\in A\}$ of numbers formed by multiplying members of $A$ by arbitrary positive integers.

According to the Davenport–Erdős theorem, for a set $M(A)$, the following notions of density are equivalent, in the sense that they all produce the same number as each other for the density of $M(A)$:

- The lower [natural density](wikipedia://en/1819983), the [inferior limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_superior_and_limit_inferior) as $n$ goes to infinity of the proportion of members of $M(A)$ in the interval $[1,n]$.
- The [logarithmic density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_density) or multiplicative density, the weighted proportion of members of $M(A)$ in the interval $[1,n]$, again in the limit, where the weight of an element $a$ is $1/a$.
- The sequential density, defined as the limit (as $i$ goes to infinity) of the densities of the sets $M(\{a_1,\dots a_i\})$ of multiples of the first $i$ elements of $A$. As these sets can be decomposed into finitely many disjoint [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389), their densities are well defined without resort to limits.

A [Behrend sequence](wikipedia://en/59915725) is defined as a sequence $A$ for which the three densities $M(A)$ described by this theorem equal one. In this case, the upper natural density (taken using the [superior limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_superior_and_limit_inferior) in place of the inferior limit) and the natural density itself (the limit of the same sequence of values) must also equal one. However, there exist other sequences $A$ and their sets of multiples $M(A)$ for which the upper natural density differs from the lower density, and for which the natural density itself does not exist.

The theorem is named after [Harold Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111) and [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), who published it in 1936. Their original proof used the [Hardy–Littlewood Tauberian theorem](wikipedia://en/22846993); later, they published another, elementary proof.
