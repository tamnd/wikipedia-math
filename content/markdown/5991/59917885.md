# Nick Patterson (scientist)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nick Patterson"}

**Nicholas James Patterson** (born 9 June 1947)[^o3] is a mathematician working as a staff scientist at the [Broad Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Institute) with notable contributions to the area of computational genomics.[^o4][^o5] His work has appeared in scientific journals such as *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29)*, *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_%28journal%29)* and *[Nature Genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Genetics)*.[^o6][^o7] His research has brought a better understanding of [early human migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_human_migrations).[^o8][^o9] He is among the group of scientists who have sequenced the [Neanderthal genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal_genome_project) in 2010.[^o10] This was followed by the sequencing of a much higher quality [Neanderthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal) genome, where the subject was from the [Altai Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altai_Mountains), in 2014.[^o11] These studies have uncovered some unexpected facts about the [interbreeding between archaic and modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbreeding_between_archaic_and_modern_humans).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Patterson was an only child who grew up in the [Bayswater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayswater) section of central London.[^o13] Patterson received his B.A. and Ph.D. in mathematics at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University).[^o5] His doctoral advisor was [John G. Thompson](wikipedia://en/862189).[^o14] He initially worked for the British code-breaking agency [GCHQ](wikipedia://en/12884) and the [Center for Communications Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses) in Princeton.[^o15]

Following this [cryptology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptology) work, Patterson worked at the quantitative trading firm [Renaissance Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Technologies).[^o16]

In 2001, Patterson joined the Center for Genome Research at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), the [Whitehead Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehead_Institute). He briefly worked on gene expression data applied to cancer before switching to the study of human genetics.[^o5]

In 2012, Patterson developed [ADMIXTOOLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADMIXTOOLS), a software package written in [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29) that is currently widely used by population geneticists.[^o17]

### Chess {#Chess}

Nick Patterson was a child [chess prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_prodigy).[^o4] He won the [Irish Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Chess_Championship) in 1969. In a long game in the [Chess Olympiad of Siegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Chess_Olympiad) in 1970, where he played top board for Ireland, he met the Danish grandmaster [Bent Larsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bent_Larsen), who was one of the top 10 players in the world at the time, and managed to make a draw as black after 93 moves.[^o3]
