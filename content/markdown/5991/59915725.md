# Behrend sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Behrend sequence"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **Behrend sequence** is an [integer sequence](wikipedia://en/51421) whose multiples include [almost all](wikipedia://en/1309) integers. The sequences are named after [Felix Behrend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Behrend).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If $A$ is a sequence of integers greater than one, and if $M(A)$ denotes the set of positive integer multiples of members of $A$, then $A$ is a Behrend sequence if $M(A)$ has [natural density](wikipedia://en/1819983) one. This means that the proportion of the integers from 1 to $n$ that belong to $M(A)$ converges, in the limit of large $n$, to one.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) form a Behrend sequence, because every integer greater than one is a multiple of a prime number. More generally, a subsequence $A$ of the prime numbers forms a Behrend sequence if and only if the sum of [reciprocals](wikipedia://en/229940) of $A$ diverges.

The [semiprimes](wikipedia://en/339542), the products of two prime numbers, also form a Behrend sequence. The only integers that are not multiples of a semiprime are the [prime powers](wikipedia://en/1630483). But as the prime powers have density zero, their complement, the multiples of the semiprimes, have density one.

:::

## History {#History}

The problem of characterizing these sequence was described as "very difficult" by [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) in 1979.

These sequences were named "Behrend sequences" in 1990 by Richard R. Hall, with a definition using [logarithmic density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_density) in place of natural density. Hall chose their name in honor of [Felix Behrend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Behrend), who proved that for a Behrend sequence $A$, the sum of [reciprocals](wikipedia://en/229940) of $A$ must diverge. Later, Hall and [Gérald Tenenbaum](wikipedia://en/36258646) used natural density to define Behrend sequences in place of logarithmic density. This variation in definitions makes no difference in which sequences are Behrend sequences, because the [Davenport–Erdős theorem](wikipedia://en/59915731) shows that, for sets of multiples, having natural density one and having logarithmic density one are equivalent.

## Derived sequences {#Derived_sequences}

When $A$ is a Behrend sequence, one may derive another Behrend sequence by omitting from $A$ any finite number of elements.

Every Behrend sequence may be decomposed into the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of infinitely many Behrend sequences.
