# Bevan point

*Image: Bevan punkt.svg.* solid black solid black 1px solid red

*Image: Bevan2 punkt.svg.* solid black solid black 1px solid red solid black 1px Other points: [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) $I$, [Nagel point](wikipedia://en/9489914) $N$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bevan point"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Bevan point**, named after [Benjamin Bevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Bevan), is a [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488). It is defined as center of the **Bevan circle**, that is the circle through the centers of the three [excircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) of a triangle.

:::

The Bevan point of a triangle is the reflection of the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) across the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) of the triangle. Bevan posed the problem of proving this in 1804, in a mathematical problem column in *The Mathematical Repository*. The problem was solved in 1806 by John Butterworth.

The Bevan point $M$ of triangle $\triangle ABC$ has the same distance from its [Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622) $e$ as its incenter $I$. Their distance is

$$\overline{MI} = 2\sqrt{R^2-\frac{abc}{a+b+c}}$$

where $R$ denotes the radius of the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) and $a, b, c$ the sides of $\triangle ABC$.

The Bevan is point is also the midpoint of the line segment $\overline{NL}$ connecting the [Nagel point](wikipedia://en/9489914) $N$ and the [de Longchamps point](wikipedia://en/14593776) $L$. The radius of the Bevan circle is $2R$, that is twice the radius of the circumcircle.
