# Violet Cane

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Violet Cane"}

**Violet Rosina Cane** (31 January 1916 – 27 June 2008) was a Cambridge-educated [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who was the first woman appointed as a professor in the Faculty of Science at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester). She is known for her work in classical statistics and stochastic processes.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Cane was born in Camberwell, London, the daughter of Tubal George Cane and Anne Louisa (Lansdell) Cane.[^o3]

- 1938: Graduated from [Newnham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newnham_College)
- 1942-45: Joined the Foreign Office and worked at [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037)
- 1947: Elected Fellow of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664)
- 1948: Awarded a diploma in mathematical statistics from Cambridge, and appointed statistician to the MRC Applied Psychology Unit at Cambridge.
- 1957: Elected Fellow of [Newnham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newnham_College)
- 1959: Founding member of the [Experimental Psychology Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_Psychology_Society)
- 1960: Appointed University Lecturer in the Statistical Laboratory at Cambridge.
- 1971: Appointed Professor of Mathematical Statistics at [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester)

She retired from Manchester in 1981. She has been described as "one of the biggest names in the subject \[of mathematics\]" at Manchester in the 20th century.[^o5]
