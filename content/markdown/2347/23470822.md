# Slash distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Slash distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **slash distribution** is the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of a standard [normal](wikipedia://en/21462) variate divided by an independent [standard uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29#Standard_uniform) variate.[^o2] In other words, if the [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) *Z* has a normal distribution with zero mean and unit [variance](wikipedia://en/32344), the random variable *U* has a uniform distribution on \[0,1\] and *Z* and *U* are [statistically independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistically_independent), then the random variable *X* = *Z* / *U* has a slash distribution. The slash distribution is an example of a [ratio distribution](wikipedia://en/9519371). The distribution was named by William H. Rogers and [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974) in a paper published in 1972.[^o3]

:::

The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) (pdf) is

$$f(x) = \frac{\varphi(0) - \varphi(x)}{x^2}.$$

where $\varphi(x)$ is the probability density function of the standard normal distribution.[^o4] The quotient is undefined at *x* = 0, but the [discontinuity is removable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Removable_discontinuity):

$$\lim_{x\to 0} f(x) = \frac{\varphi(0)}{2} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}$$

The most common use of the slash distribution is in [simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation) studies. It is a useful distribution in this context because it has [heavier tails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_tail) than a normal distribution, but it is not as [pathological](wikipedia://en/372742) as the [Cauchy distribution](wikipedia://en/7003).[^o4]
