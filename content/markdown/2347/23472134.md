# Overfull graph

*Image: Overfull graph.svg.* This graph is **overfull** because its [size](wikipedia://en/325806#Graph) is larger than the product of its maximum [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) and the number of vertices in the graph (its [order](wikipedia://en/325806#Graph)) divided by 2 and rounded down. In this case, 22 $>$ 20.

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

**Conjecture:** A graph *G* with $\Delta (G) > n/3$ is class 2 [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it has an overfull subgraph S such that $\displaystyle \Delta (G) = \Delta (S)$.

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Overfull graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **overfull graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) whose [size](wikipedia://en/325806#Graph) is greater than the product of its maximum [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) and half of its [order](wikipedia://en/325806#Graph) [floored](wikipedia://en/54267), i.e. $|E| > \Delta (G) \lfloor |V|/2 \rfloor$ where $|E|$ is the size of *G*, $\displaystyle\Delta(G)$ is the maximum degree of *G*, and $|V|$ is the order of *G*. The concept of an **overfull subgraph**, an overfull graph that is a [subgraph](wikipedia://en/325802#Subgraph), immediately follows. An alternate, stricter definition of an overfull subgraph *S* of a graph *G* requires $\displaystyle\Delta (G) = \Delta (S)$.

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Every [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) of length three or more is overfull. The product of its degree (two) and half its length (rounded down) is one less than the number of edges in the cycle. More generally, every [regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) with an odd number $n$ of vertices is overfull, because its number of edges, $\Delta n/2$ (where $\Delta$ is its degree), is larger than $\Delta\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A few properties of overfull graphs:

1. Overfull graphs are of odd order.
1. Overfull graphs are [class 2](wikipedia://en/690647#Properties). That is, they require at least $\Delta + 1$ colors in any [edge coloring](wikipedia://en/690647).
1. A graph *G*, with an overfull subgraph *S* such that $\displaystyle\Delta (G) = \Delta (S)$, is of class 2.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Overfull conjecture"}

## Overfull conjecture {#Overfull_conjecture}

In 1986, [Amanda Chetwynd](wikipedia://en/61062118) and [Anthony Hilton](wikipedia://en/42746762) posited the following [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) that is now known as the **overfull conjecture**.[^o9]

A graph *G* with $\Delta (G) > n/3$ is class 2 [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it has an overfull subgraph S such that $\displaystyle \Delta (G) = \Delta (S)$.

This conjecture, if true, would have numerous implications in graph theory, including the [1-factorization conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-factorization_conjecture).[^o10]

:::

::: {#o12 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Algorithms"}

## Algorithms {#Algorithms}

For graphs in which $\Delta \ge n/3$, there are at most three [induced](wikipedia://en/1655188) overfull subgraphs, and it is possible to find an overfull subgraph in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time). When $\Delta \ge n/2$, there is at most one induced overfull subgraph, and it is possible to find it in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time).[^o13]
