# I. Richard Savage

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="I. Richard Savage"}

*Image: I.\_Richard\_Savage\_(October\_1966).jpg.* I. Richard Savage in 1966

**I. Richard Savage** (October 26, 1925 – June 4, 2004) was an American statistician who specialized in [population censuses](wikipedia://en/6889). He was [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) and former chair of statistics at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). He was an elected fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) and of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Savage earned his BS from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) and his MSc from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). He earned a PhD in statistics from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1954 where he studied under the tutelage of [Howard Levene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Levene). His thesis was titled: *Contributions to the Theory of Rank Order Statistics.* His early research continued his work on [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369), especially [rank order statistics](wikipedia://en/59091992).

## Career {#Career}

Savage's first position after completing his PhD was as a mathematical statistician at the National Bureau of Standards (now the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology)), where he published his PhD thesis.[^n1] He then had a number of university faculty positions, including [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), [Harvard Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_School), [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London), [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), and [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University). In 1974, he joined the Department of Statistics at Yale University where he stayed until his retirement in 1990. He was chair of the department from 1976 to 1978.

## Recognition and service {#Recognition_and_service}

Savage was elected a fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/51140705), the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) and of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics). He was co-editor of both the [Journal of the American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/16179313) and the [Annals of Statistics](wikipedia://en/22160768). In 1984, he was [president of the American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/14833025). In 1993, he was presented with the [American Statistical Association Founders Award](wikipedia://en/81174224).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

While studying for his PhD, Savage met JoAnn Osherow. They married in 1950 and had two daughters. Savage was the brother of [Leonard Jimmie Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Jimmie_Savage).

Savage contracted [polio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polio) in 1945 that left him legally blind and needing the use of a wheelchair. He died from [post-polio syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-polio_syndrome) in 2004. He was 78 years old.
