# Flexible algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Flexible algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) • on a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) is **flexible** if it satisfies the **flexible identity**:

:::

$$a \bullet \left(b \bullet a\right) = \left(a \bullet b\right) \bullet a$$

for any two elements *a* and *b* of the set. A [magma](wikipedia://en/141916) (that is, a set equipped with a binary operation) is flexible if the binary operation with which it is equipped is flexible. Similarly, a [nonassociative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonassociative_algebra) is flexible if its multiplication operator is flexible.

Every [commutative](wikipedia://en/294390) or [associative](wikipedia://en/1335) operation is flexible, so flexibility becomes important for binary operations that are neither commutative nor associative, e.g. for the [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) of [sedenions](wikipedia://en/51434), which are not even [alternative](wikipedia://en/3160).

In 1954, [Richard D. Schafer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_D._Schafer) examined the algebras generated by the [Cayley–Dickson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley–Dickson_process) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) and showed that they satisfy the flexible identity.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Besides [associative algebras](wikipedia://en/2112), the following classes of nonassociative algebras are flexible:

- [Alternative algebras](wikipedia://en/3160)
- [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944)
- [Jordan algebras](wikipedia://en/936371) (which are commutative)
- [Okubo algebras](wikipedia://en/39255698)

In the world of [magmas](wikipedia://en/141916), there is only a binary multiplication operation with no addition or scaling with from a base ring or field like in algebras. In this setting, [alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_magma) and [commutative](wikipedia://en/7741863) magmas are all flexible - the alternative and commutative laws all imply flexibility. This includes many important classes of magmas: all [groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_Theory), [semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroups) and [moufang loops](wikipedia://en/755329) are flexible.

The [sedenions](wikipedia://en/51434) and [trigintaduonions](wikipedia://en/25474499), and all algebras constructed from these by iterating the [Cayley–Dickson construction](wikipedia://en/175609), are also flexible.
