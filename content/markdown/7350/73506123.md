# Sphericity (graph theory)

*Image: Disk graph in the plane.svg.* A graph with sphericity $2$: A space graph of the vertices of a pentagon, realized as the intersection graph of the unit $2$-disks in the plane, centered on these points. This is also known as a unit disk graph.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sphericity"}

In the mathematical field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **sphericity** of a [graph](wikipedia://en/12401) is a [graph invariant](wikipedia://en/1655142) defined to be the smallest [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) required to realize the graph as the [intersection graph](wikipedia://en/7726759) of [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859).[^o2][^o3] The sphericity of a graph is one of several notions of graph dimension based on intersection graphs; others include [boxicity](wikipedia://en/11167471) and [cubicity](wikipedia://en/73506350).[^o4][^o5] The concept of sphericity was first introduced by Hiroshi Maehara in 1980[^o6] (and also used by Timothy F. Havel in 1982).[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

This article only considers [undirected graphs](wikipedia://en/325806), with finite and non-empty vertex sets, with no loop and no multiple edge.[^o10][^o11]

The *sphericity* of a graph $G$, denoted by $\operatorname{sph}(G)$, is the smallest [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $k \geq 0$ such that $G$ can be realized as the intersection graph of [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) unit-diameter spheres,[^o12][^o13] in the $k$-dimensional Euclidean space, $\mathbb{R}^k$.

Sphericity can also be defined using the language of space graphs as follows. For a [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742) of points in the $k$-dimensional Euclidean space, a *space graph* $\mathbb G$ is built by connecting pairs of points with a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) if and only if their [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) is less than some specified constant (called the *adjacency limit* of $\mathbb G$).[^o14] Then, the sphericity of a graph $G$ is the minimum integer $k \geq 0$ such that $G$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to a space graph in $\mathbb{R}^k$.[^o15] Indeed: A space graph in $k$-space is, as an abstract graph, nothing but the intersection graph of a family of equiradial $k$-disks in $k$-space.[^o16] Remark: Maehara takes these disks to be [open](wikipedia://en/39358).[^o17] (The final result is the same.)

*Image: Indifference graph = unit interval graph.svg.* A space graph with sphericity $1$, formed from a set of points on the real [number line](wikipedia://en/287188), by connecting pairs of points whose distance is at most $1$. Also the intersection graph of the set of unit $1$-disks, i.e. unit intervals, centered on the points.

Graphs of sphericity $1$ are known as [unit interval graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_interval_graph)[^o18] or [indifference graphs](wikipedia://en/42535190). Graphs of sphericity $2$ are known as [unit disk graphs](wikipedia://en/3686677).

:::

## Values on certain graph classes, bounds {#Values_on_certain_graph_classes,_bounds}

The sphericity on certain graph classes can be computed exactly. The following sphericities were given by Maehara in his original paper on the topic[^o20] ($n$ denotes the [graph order](wikipedia://en/325806)).

::: {#o21 .table cue=table}

| Graph | Description | Sphericity | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| $K_1$ | [Complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) | $0$ |  |
| $K_n$ | Complete graph | $1$ | $n > 1$ |
| $P_n$ | [Path graph](wikipedia://en/4587190) | $1$ | $n > 1$ |
| $C_n$ | [Circuit graph](wikipedia://en/878962) | $2$ | $n > 3$ |
| $K_{p(2)}$ | [Complete multipartite graph](wikipedia://en/39469099) on $p$ parts of [cardinal](wikipedia://en/6173) $2$ | $2$ | $p > 1.$[^o22] |

:::

However, Fishburn claims that $\operatorname{cub}(G) = \operatorname{sph}(G) = 0$ if and only if $G$ is a complete graph (where $\operatorname{cub}(G)$ denotes the [cubicity](wikipedia://en/73506350) of $G$; by convention, $0$-space is the [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798) $\{ 0 \}$ and any (closed) $0$-disk = any (closed) $0$-cube = $\{ 0 \}$), and that $\operatorname{cub}(G) = \operatorname{sph}(G) = 1$ if and only if $G$ is a unit interval graph that is not complete.[^o23] Indeed, his definition of cubicity / sphericity allows adjacent distinct vertices with same closed neighborhood to be assigned the same cube / sphere.[^o24][^o25]

The following sphericities were given by Maehara in his paper on semiregular polyhedra.

::: {#o26 .table cue=table}

| Graph of polyhedron | Description | Sphericity | Note | Ref. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $A_m$ | $m$-Antiprism | $2$ | $m \geq 3$ | [^o27] |
| $P_m$ | $m$-Prism | $2$ | $m = 3$ or $m \geq 10$ | [^o28] |
| $P_m$ | $m$-Prism | $3$ | $m = 5$ or $m = 6$ | [^o29] |
| $C, D, I$ | Cube, regular dodecahedron, regular icosahedron | $3$ |  | [^o30] |
|  | Archimedean solid | $3$ |  | [^o31] |

:::

Maehara conjectured that the graphs of the $7$-prism, $8$-prism, and $9$-prism have sphericity $3$.[^o32]

The most general upper bound on sphericity that is known is as follows: If a graph $G$ is not [complete](wikipedia://en/85816), then $\operatorname{sph}(G) \leq |G| - \mathbb{\text{ω}}(G)$, where $|G|$ and $\mathbb{\text{ω}}(G)$ respectively denote the order and the [clique number](wikipedia://en/524466) of $G$.[^o33][^o34]

For certain graphs, a slightly smaller upper bound is known: If $G$ is a [split graph](wikipedia://en/9928681) and $|G| - \mathbb{\mbox{ω}}(G) > 2$, then $\operatorname{sph}(G) \leq |G| - \mathbb{\mbox{ω}}(G) - 1$.[^o35]

For every [positive integer](wikipedia://en/21474) $m$, there exists a split graph $G$ such that $\operatorname{sph}(G) = |G| - \mathbb{\text{ω}}(G) - 1 = m$.[^o36]

For $1 \leq m \leq n$, $~ \operatorname{sph}(K_{m,n}) \leq m - 1 + \lceil n/2 \rceil$, where $K_{m,n}$ denotes the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) with part cardinals $m$ and $n$.[^o37][^o38]
