# Arto Salomaa

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Arto Salomaa"}

**Arto Kustaa Salomaa** (6 June 1934 – 26 January 2025) was a Finnish mathematician and computer scientist. His research career, which spanned over 40 years, was focused on [formal languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_languages) and [automata theory](wikipedia://en/103356).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Salomaa was born in [Turku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turku), Finland on 6 June 1934. He earned a [Bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) from the [University of Turku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turku) in 1954 and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) from the same university in 1960. Salomaa's father was a professor of philosophy at the [University of Turku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turku).[^o4] Salomaa was introduced to the theory of automata and formal languages during seminars at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) given by [John Myhill](wikipedia://en/2611636) in 1957.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

In 1965 Salomaa became a professor of mathematics at the [University of Turku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turku), a position he retired from in 1999. He also spent two years in the late 1960s at the [University of Western Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Ontario) in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_Ontario), Ontario, Canada, and two years in the 1970s at [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University) in [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus), Denmark.[^o4][^o7]

Salomaa was president of the [European Association for Theoretical Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EATCS) from 1979 until 1985.[^o8]

## Publications {#Publications}

Salomaa authored or co-authored 46 textbooks, including *[Theory of Automata](wikipedia://en/103356)* (1969), *[Formal Languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_languages)* (1973), *The Mathematical Theory of [L-Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_Systems)* (1980, with [Grzegorz Rozenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grzegorz_Rozenberg)), *Jewels of Formal Language Theory* (1981) *[Public-Key Cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_key_cryptography)* (1990) and *[DNA Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_computing)* (1998, with [Grzegorz Rozenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grzegorz_Rozenberg) and [Gheorghe Paun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Paun)). With [Rozenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grzegorz_Rozenberg), Salomaa edited the *Handbook of Formal Languages* (1997), a 3-volume, 2000-page reference on [formal language theory](wikipedia://en/10939).[^o10] These books have often become standard references in their respective areas. For example, *Formal Languages* was reported in 1991 to be among the 100 most cited texts in mathematics.[^o4]

Salomaa published over 400 articles in scientific journals during his professional career.[^o11] He also authored non-scientific articles such as "What computer scientists should know about sauna".[^o12] From his retirement until 2014, Salomaa published over 100 scientific articles.[^o11]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Salomaa married in 1959.[^o14] He had two children, Kirsti and [Kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Salomaa),[^o14] the latter of whom is a professor of Computer Science at [Queen's University at Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston) and also works in the field of formal languages and automata theory.[^o15]

Salomaa died on 26 January 2025, at the age of 90.[^o16] The [Research Council of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Council_of_Finland) reported his death two days later in a press release, on 28 January.[^o17]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

Salomaa was awarded the title of [Academician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academician) by the [Academy of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Finland),[^o19][^o20] one of twelve living Finnish individuals awarded the title. He also received the [EATCS Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EATCS_Award) in 2004.[^o21] Salomaa received seven honorary degrees.[^o21] On 13 June 2013, Salomaa was awarded a Doctor Honoris Causa from the [University of Western Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Ontario).[^o22]
