# Anafunctor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anafunctor"}

In mathematics, an **anafunctor** is a notion introduced by (Makkai 1996) for ordinary categories that is a generalization of functors. In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), some statements require the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840), but the axiom of choice can sometimes be avoided when using an anafunctor.[^o3] For example, the statement "every fully faithful and [essentially surjective functor](wikipedia://en/4557668) is an [equivalence of categories](wikipedia://en/379051)" is equivalent to the axiom of choice, but we can usually follow the same statement without the axiom of choice by using anafunctor instead of functor.[^o4]

:::

## Definition {#Definition}

### Span formulation of anafunctors {#Span_formulation_of_anafunctors}

*Image: Anafunctor (span).svg.* Anafunctor (span)

Let $X$ and $A$ be [categories](wikipedia://en/53759). An anafunctor $F$ with domain ([source](wikipedia://en/27716891#Definition)) $X$ and [codomain](wikipedia://en/50264) ([target](wikipedia://en/27716891#Definition)) $A$, and between categories $X$ and $A$ is a category $|F|$, in a notation $F:X \xrightarrow{a} A$, is given by the following conditions:[^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12]

- $F_0$ is surjective on [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_of_a_category).
- Let pair $F_0:|F| \rightarrow X$ and $F_1:|F| \rightarrow A$ be [functors](wikipedia://en/10987), a [span](wikipedia://en/10318351) of ordinary functors ($X \leftarrow |F| \rightarrow A$), where $F_0$ is [fully faithful](wikipedia://en/876534).

### Set-theoretic definition {#Set-theoretic_definition}

An anafunctor $F: X \xrightarrow{a} A$ following condition:[^o14][^o15]

1. A set $|F|$ of specifications of $F$, with maps $\sigma : |F| \to \mathrm{Ob} (X)$ (source), $\tau : |F| \to \mathrm{Ob} (A)$ (target). $|F|$ is the set of specifications, $s \in |F|$ specifies the value $\tau (s)$ at the argument $\sigma (s)$. For $X \in \mathrm{Ob} (X)$, we write $|F| \; X$ for the class $\{s \in |F| : \sigma (s) = X\}$ and $F_{s} (X)$ for $\tau (s)$ the notation $F_{s} (X)$ presumes that $s \in |F| \; X$.
1. For each $X, \; Y \in \mathrm{Ob} (X)$, $x \in |F| \; X$, $y \in |F| \; Y$ and $f : X \to Y$ in the class of all arrows $\mathrm{Arr (X)}$ an [arrows](wikipedia://en/27716891) $F_{x,y} (f) : F_{x} (X) \to F_{y} (Y)$ in $A$.
1. For every $X \in \mathrm{Ob} (X)$, such that $|F| \; X$ is [inhabited](wikipedia://en/5473033) (non-[empty](wikipedia://en/9566)).
1. $F$ hold [identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_morphism). For all $X \in \mathrm{Ob} (X)$ and $x \in |F| \; X$, we have $F_{x,x} (\mathrm{id}_x) = \mathrm{id}_{F_{x}X}$
1. $F$ hold [composition](wikipedia://en/27716891#Definition). Whenever $X, Y, Z \in \mathrm{Ob} (X)$, $x \in |F| \; X$, $y \in |F| \; Y$, $z \in |F| \; Z,$ and $F_{x,z} (gf) = F_{y,z} (g) \circ F_{x,y} (f)$.
