# Milnor conjecture (Ricci curvature)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Milnor conjecture"}

In 1968 [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632) conjectured[^o11] that the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a [complete manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_manifold) is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) if its [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582) stays nonnegative. In an oversimplified interpretation, such a manifold has a finite number of "[holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hole_%28topology%29)". A version for [almost-flat manifolds](wikipedia://en/933946) holds from work of [Gromov](wikipedia://en/771562).[^o2][^o10]

:::

In two dimensions $M^2$ has finitely generated fundamental group as a consequence that if $\operatorname{Ric}>0$ for noncompact $M^2$, then it is [flat](wikipedia://en/12756616) or [diffeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphic) to $\mathbb{R}^2$, by work of [Cohn-Vossen](wikipedia://en/3204307) from 1935.[^o3][^o8]

In three dimensions the conjecture holds due to a noncompact $M^3$ with $\operatorname{Ric}>0$ being diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^3$ or having its [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) [isometrically](wikipedia://en/254777) split. The diffeomorphic part is due to [Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen)-[Yau](wikipedia://en/303137) (1982)[^o4][^o8] while the other part is by Liu (2013).[^o5][^o8] Another proof of the full statement has been given by Pan (2020).[^o6][^o8]

In 2023 Bruè, [Naber](wikipedia://en/77589855) and Semola disproved in two preprints the conjecture for six[^o9] or more[^o8] dimensions by constructing [counterexamples](wikipedia://en/143151) that they described as "smooth fractal snowflakes". The status of the conjecture for four or five dimensions remains open.[^o10]
