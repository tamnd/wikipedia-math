# Petrus Bertius

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Petrus Bertius"}

*Image: Portret van Petrus Bertius, hoogleraar te Leiden BN 116.tiff.* Petrus Bertius, engraving by [Willem Isaacsz. van Swanenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Isaacsz._van_Swanenburg).

**Petrus Bertius** (also Peter Bertius; Pieter de Bert) (14 November 1565 – 13 October 1629) was a [Flemish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_people) philosopher, theologian, historian, geographer and cartographer. Bertius published much in mathematics, and historical and theological works, but he is now best known as a cartographer with his edition of the *[Geographia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_%28Ptolemy%29)* of [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979), which includes [Mercator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardus_Mercator)'s Ptolemaic atlas from 1578 as well as parallel Greek and Latin texts.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in [Beveren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alveringem) (Alveringem), the son of a Flemish preacher Pieter Michielszoon Bardt, who left Flanders for religious exile in London around 1568, with his family. In 1577 Petrus Bertius returned to the Netherlands, to study at the [University of Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leiden). He supported himself by tutoring younger students and continued travelling in Europe. In 1593 he was appointed to subregent of the Leiden *Statencollege*, marrying in the same year Maritgen, daughter of [Johannes Kuchlinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kuchlinus), the first regent of the *Statencollege*, whom he would succeed after his death in 1606 as a regent. He was also connected by marriage to [Jodocus Hondius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodocus_Hondius) and [Pieter van den Keere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_van_den_Keere), his brothers-in-law and both cartographers, and this would influence his later life.[^o3]

## Philosophy & theology {#Philosophy_&_theology}

Bertius wrote an introduction to [Boethius](wikipedia://en/195684)'s [Consolations of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolations_of_Philosophy), which was published in [Giovanni Antonio Volpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Antonio_Volpi)'s edition of the text in 1721.[^o5]

Bertius was a friend of [Jacobus Arminius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobus_Arminius), and spoke at his funeral in 1609. A controversy arose with [Francis Gomarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Gomarus), the chief opponent of [Arminianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arminianism), on the basis of the published version of the funeral oration. Bertius held views on essential doctrines that were unwelcome even to his friends.[^n1] He was accused of [Semipelagianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semipelagianism).[^n2]

In 1612 Bertius published *Hymenaeus desertor*, a Latin work that went further than Arminius in theology, against the advice of fellow [Remonstrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remonstrant). The *Hymenaeus desertor* was widely circulated, but was unpopular: [William Louis, Count of Nassau-Dillenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Louis,_Count_of_Nassau-Dillenburg) disliked it, and [Johan van Oldenbarnevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_van_Oldenbarnevelt) with other politicians felt that Bertius had gone against them. Bertius persisted, however, in 1613 with a Dutch translation, which he reprinted in 1615 in Leiden. Eventually Bertius lost his teaching position, and was also forbidden to give private lessons.

## Later life {#Later_life}

In 1618 Bertius was given an appointment as a cosmographer to the court of [Louis XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIII). His career in cartography had started in 1598, with the publication of a Latin edition of a miniature atlas *Caert Thresoor* by [Barent Langenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barent_Langenes),[^o9] which he translated as *Tabulae contractae* (1600). His *Theatrum geographiae veteris* had commended him to King Louis.[^o3] When his academic prospects in the Netherlands were cut down, he went to France.

In 1620 he converted to the Catholic Church, and became professor of rhetoric at the [Collège de Boncourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_Boncourt), a part of the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). In 1622 Louis XIII set up a new personal chair in mathematical sciences in the royal college for Bertius, and granted him the title of royal historian. He died in Paris.
