# ST type theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="ST type theory"}

The following system is Mendelson's (1997, 289–293) **ST** [type theory](wikipedia://en/40282). ST is equivalent with Russell's ramified theory plus the [Axiom of reducibility](wikipedia://en/10591072). The [domain of quantification](wikipedia://en/229180) is partitioned into an ascending hierarchy of types, with all [individuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual) assigned a type. Quantified variables range over only one type; hence the underlying logic is [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983). **ST** is "simple" (relative to the type theory of *[Principia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/24133)*) primarily because all members of the [domain](wikipedia://en/22517558) and [codomain](wikipedia://en/22517558) of any [relation](wikipedia://en/22517558) must be of the same type. There is a lowest type, whose individuals have no members and are members of the second lowest type. Individuals of the lowest type correspond to the [urelements](wikipedia://en/643648) of certain set theories. Each type has a next higher type, analogous to the notion of [successor](wikipedia://en/325637) in [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic). While **ST** is silent as to whether there is a maximal type, a [transfinite number](wikipedia://en/189734) of types poses no difficulty. These facts, reminiscent of the Peano axioms, make it convenient and conventional to assign a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) to each type, starting with 0 for the lowest type. But type theory does not require a prior definition of the naturals.

:::

The symbols peculiar to **ST** are primed variables and [infix operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infix_operator) $\in$. In any given formula, unprimed variables all have the same type, while primed variables ($x'$) range over the next higher type. The [atomic formulas](wikipedia://en/4472066) of **ST** are of two forms, $x=y$ ([identity](wikipedia://en/298428)) and $y\in x'$. The infix-operator symbol $\in$ suggests the intended [interpretation](wikipedia://en/19858), set membership.

All variables appearing in the definition of identity and in the axioms *Extensionality* and *Comprehension*, range over individuals of one of two consecutive types. Only unprimed variables (ranging over the "lower" type) can appear to the left of '$\in$', whereas to its right, only primed variables (ranging over the "higher" type) can appear. The first-order formulation of **ST** rules out quantifying over types. Hence each pair of consecutive types requires its own axiom of Extensionality and of Comprehension, which is possible if *Extensionality* and *Comprehension* below are taken as [axiom schemata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_scheme) "ranging over" types.

- **Identity**, defined by $x=y\leftrightarrow\forall z' [x\in z'\leftrightarrow y\in z']$.
- **[Extensionality](wikipedia://en/52376)**. An [axiom schema](wikipedia://en/764613). $\forall x[x\in y' \leftrightarrow x\in z'] \rightarrow [y'=z']$.

Let $\Phi(x)$ denote any [first-order formula](wikipedia://en/10983) containing the [free variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_variable) $x$.

- **Comprehension**. An [axiom schema](wikipedia://en/764613). $\exists z'\forall x[x\in z'\leftrightarrow \Phi(x)]$.

*Remark*. Any collection of elements of the same type may form an object of the next higher type. Comprehension is schematic with respect to $\Phi(x)$ as well as to types.

- **[Infinity](wikipedia://en/246449)**. There exists a nonempty [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) $R$ over the individuals of the lowest type, that is [irreflexive](wikipedia://en/200458), [transitive](wikipedia://en/200463), and strongly connected: $\forall x,y [x\neq y\rightarrow[xRy\vee yRx]]$ and with codomain contained in domain.

*Remark*. Infinity is the only true axiom of **ST** and is entirely mathematical in nature. It asserts that $R$ is a strict [total order](wikipedia://en/30330), with a [codomain](wikipedia://en/22517558) contained in its [domain](wikipedia://en/22517558). If 0 is assigned to the lowest type, the type of $R$ is 3. Infinity can be satisfied only if the (co)domain of $R$ is [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824), thus forcing the existence of an infinite set. If relations are defined in terms of [ordered pairs](wikipedia://en/22362), this axiom requires a prior definition of ordered pair; the Kuratowski definition, adapted to **ST**, will do. The literature does not explain why the usual [axiom of infinity](wikipedia://en/246449) (there exists an [inductive set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_set_%28axiom_of_infinity%29)) of [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) of other set theories could not be married to **ST**.

**ST** reveals how type theory can be made very similar to [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory). Moreover, the more elaborate [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_%28information_science%29) of **ST**, grounded in what is now called the "iterative conception of set," makes for axiom (schemata) that are far simpler than those of conventional set theories, such as [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC), with simpler ontologies. Set theories whose point of departure is type theory, but whose axioms, [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_%28information_science%29), and terminology differ from the above, include [New Foundations](wikipedia://en/945957) and [Scott&ndash;Potter set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott&ndash;Potter_set_theory).

## Formulations based on equality {#Formulations_based_on_equality}

Church's type theory has been extensively studied by two of Church's students, [Leon Henkin](wikipedia://en/356165) and [Peter B. Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_B._Andrews_%28mathematician%29). Since **ST** is a [higher order logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_order_logic), and in higher order logics one can define propositional connectives in terms of [logical equivalence](wikipedia://en/169324) and quantifiers, in 1963 Henkin developed a formulation of **ST** based on equality, but in which he restricted attention to propositional types. This was simplified later that year by Andrews in his [theory **Q<sub>0</sub>**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q_zero).[^o3] In this respect **ST** can be seen as a particular kind of a higher-order logic, classified by [P.T. Johnstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.T._Johnstone) in *Sketches of an Elephant*, as having a [lambda-signature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambda-signature), that is a higher-order [signature](wikipedia://en/7788156) that contains no relations, and uses only products and arrows (function types) as [type constructors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_constructors). Furthermore, as Johnstone put it, **ST** is "logic-free" in the sense that it contains no logical connectives or quantifiers in its formulae.[^o4]
