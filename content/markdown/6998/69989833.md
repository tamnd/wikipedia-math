# Viveka Erlandsson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Viveka Erlandsson"}

**Viveka Erlandsson** is a Swedish mathematician specialising in [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774) and geometry, and known in particular for extending the work of [Maryam Mirzakhani](wikipedia://en/6409655) on counting [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) on [hyperbolic manifolds](wikipedia://en/1589135). She is a lecturer at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol) and in a part position an associate professor at [UiT The Arctic University of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UiT_The_Arctic_University_of_Norway).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Erlandsson earned a bachelor's degree in applied mathematics from [San Francisco State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_State_University) in 2004, and continued at the same university for a master's degree in 2006. She became a lecturer at [Baruch College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_College) and [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College) in the [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York) system, while pursuing a doctorate in mathematics through the [Graduate Center of the City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_Center_of_the_City_University_of_New_York), which she completed in 2013. Her dissertation, *The Margulis region in hyperbolic 4-space*, was supervised by Ara Basmajian.

After postdoctoral research at [Aalto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalto_University) and the [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki) in Finland, she became a lecturer in mathematics at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol) in 2017.

## Book {#Book}

Erlandsson is the coauthor of the book *Geodesic Currents and Mirzakhani’s Curve Counting*, with Juan Souto, to be published by Springer in 2022.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Erlandsson is the 2021 winner of the [Anne Bennett Prize](wikipedia://en/61347778) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097), given to her "for her outstanding achievements in geometry and topology and her inspirational active role in promoting women mathematicians". She was a 2024 recipient of the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913), "for her outstanding work on curve counting on surfaces" and for her "extraordinary rigidity theorem for bounce sequences associated to billiard tables".
