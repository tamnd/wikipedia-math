# Ailsa Keating

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ailsa Keating"}

**Ailsa Macgregor Keating** is a mathematician specialising in [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298) and [homological mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/2164886).[^o3] She is a professor in the Department of Pure Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Keating grew up in [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse), France. She read mathematics in [Clare College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge) from 2005 to 2009, earning a master's degree through [Part III of the Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_III_of_the_Mathematical_Tripos). She went on to graduate study at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), completing her dissertation in 2014 with the dissertation *Symplectic properties of Milnor fibres* supervised by [Paul Seidel](wikipedia://en/27678785).

She returned to Cambridge as a Junior Research Fellow in [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in 2014, at the same time doing postdoctoral research as a [Simons Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Foundation) Junior Fellow[^o5] at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). She became a lecturer at Cambridge in 2017 and was promoted to professor in 2023.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Keating is the winner of the 2021 [Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prize) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097), for her research using [Dehn twists](wikipedia://en/765970) to study the symmetries of [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356).
