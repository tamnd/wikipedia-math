# William Edward Story

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="William Edward Story"}

**William Edward Story** (April 29, 1850 – April 10, 1930) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who taught at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) and [Clark University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_University).

:::

William was born in Boston to Isaac Marion Story (1818-1901) and Elizabeth Bowen Woodberry (1817-1888). He attended high school in Somerville, Massachusetts, and entered [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in the fall of 1867. He graduated with honors in mathematics and began graduate study in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) in September 1871.[^o4] In [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) he attended lectures of [Weierstrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass), [Ernst Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117), [Helmholtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmholtz) and Dove. In [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig) he heard [Karl Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Neumann_%28mathematician%29), Bruhns, Mayer, Van der Müll, and Engelmann. He earned a Ph.D. in Leipzig in 1875 with a dissertation *On the algebraic relations existing between the polars of a binary quantic*.[^o5]

W.E. Story began his teaching career at Harvard as a [tutor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutor). With the establishment of [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) in 1876, Story was recruited by [Daniel Coit Gilman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Coit_Gilman) as an Associate. [J. J. Sylvester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Sylvester) led the program in mathematics. Until 1879, Story was the only other instructor in mathematics besides Sylvester.[^o6] Story was instrumental in starting two publication projects: *The Johns Hopkins University Circulars* was a student paper detailing classes and attendees. *[American Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Mathematics)* was also started as a joint effort of Sylvester and Story, but soon Story was replaced by [Thomas Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Craig_%28mathematician%29) as managing editor. In 1893, Story became an [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor); he taught courses on [quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternions), [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939), [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762), mathematical astronomy and mathematical elasticity. Story also introduced introductory courses for graduate students, surveying the entire field. The monthly Mathematical Seminary became a weekly mathematical society, divided into three parts; Story oversaw the section on geometry and quaternions.[^o7]

When [Clark University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_University) was established in 1889, President [G. Stanley Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._Stanley_Hall) hired [Oskar Bolza](wikipedia://en/10217078) and Story to lead the mathematics department.[^o8] [Henry Taber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Taber) was hired as [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent), he had studied with Story at Johns Hopkins.[^o9] [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384) and other mathematicians contributed to making Clark the leading site for mathematics in the USA until 1892 when [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) eclipsed it.[^o10]

Clark University ceased its graduate program in 1919 and Story retired in 1921. He died in 1930 in [Worcester, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester,_Massachusetts).[^o11]
