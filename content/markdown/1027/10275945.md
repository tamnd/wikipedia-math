::: {#o9 .subject cue=lead name="6-demicube"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **6-demicube**, **demihexeract** or **hemihexeract** is a [uniform 6-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821728), constructed from a *6-cube* ([hexeract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexeract)) with [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) vertices removed. It is part of a dimensionally infinite family of [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392) called [demihypercubes](wikipedia://en/6833695). Acronym: **hax**.

:::

[E. L. Elte](wikipedia://en/27806942) identified it in 1912 as a semiregular polytope, labeling it as HM<sub>6</sub> for a 6-dimensional *half measure* polytope.

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) named this polytope as **1<sub>31</sub>** from its [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram), with a ring on one of the 1-length branches, . It can named similarly by a 3-dimensional exponential [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\left\{3 \begin{array}{l}3, 3, 3\\3\end{array}\right\}$ or {3,3<sup>3,1</sup>}.

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

[Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) for the vertices of a demihexeract centered at the origin are alternate halves of the [hexeract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexeract):

(±1,±1,±1,±1,±1,±1)

with an odd number of plus signs.

## As a configuration {#As_a_configuration}

This [configuration matrix](wikipedia://en/18801624#As_configurations) represents the 6-demicube. The rows and columns correspond to vertices, edges, faces, cells, 4-faces and 5-faces. The diagonal numbers say how many of each element occur in the whole 6-demicube. The nondiagonal numbers say how many of the column's element occur in or at the row's element.[^o12][^n1]

The diagonal f-vector numbers are derived through the [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777), dividing the full group order of a subgroup order by removing one mirror at a time.

## Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

There are 47 uniform polytopes with D<sub>6</sub> symmetry, 31 are shared by the B<sub>6</sub> symmetry, and 16 are unique:

The 6-demicube, 1<sub>31</sub> is third in a dimensional series of uniform polytopes, expressed by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) as *k*<sub>31</sub> series. The fifth figure is a Euclidean honeycomb, [3<sub>31</sub>](wikipedia://en/19593724), and the final is a noncompact hyperbolic honeycomb, 4<sub>31</sub>. Each progressive [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392) is constructed from the previous as its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719).

It is also the second in a dimensional series of uniform polytopes and honeycombs, expressed by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) as 1<sub>3*k*</sub> series. The fourth figure is the Euclidean honeycomb [1<sub>33</sub>](wikipedia://en/19593765) and the final is a noncompact hyperbolic honeycomb, 1<sub>34</sub>.

### Skew icosahedron {#Skew_icosahedron}

Coxeter identified a subset of 12 vertices that form a [regular skew](wikipedia://en/19193037) [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) {3, 5} with the same symmetries as the icosahedron itself, but at different angles. He dubbed this the **regular skew icosahedron**.[^o19][^o20]
