# Octonion algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Octonion algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **octonion algebra** or **Cayley algebra** over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F* is a [unital](wikipedia://en/191788#Unital_algebra) [composition algebra](wikipedia://en/3861635) over *F* that has [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) 8 over *F*. In other words, it is a 8-dimensional [unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra) [non-associative algebra](wikipedia://en/191788#Non-associative_algebra) *A* over *F* with a [non-degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952) [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) *N* (called the *norm form*) such that

:::

$$N(xy) = N(x)N(y)$$

for all *x* and *y* in *A*.

The most well-known example of an octonion algebra is the classical [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436), which are an octonion algebra over **R**, the field of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438). The [split-octonions](wikipedia://en/3861649) also form an octonion algebra over **R**. Up to **R**-algebra [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828), these are the only octonion algebras over the reals. The algebra of [bioctonions](wikipedia://en/51005149) is the octonion algebra over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) **C**.

The octonion algebra for *N* is a [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625) if and only if the form *N* is [anisotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropic_quadratic_space). A **split octonion algebra** is one for which the quadratic form *N* is [isotropic](wikipedia://en/10917170) (i.e., there exists a non-zero vector *x* with *N*(*x*) = 0). Up to *F*-algebra isomorphism, there is a unique split octonion algebra over any field *F*.[^o2] When *F* is [algebraically closed](wikipedia://en/1018) or a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), these are the only octonion algebras over *F*. For instance, when *F* is the field of real numbers, the algebra is [split-octonions](wikipedia://en/3861649).

Octonion algebras are always non-associative. They are, however, [alternative algebras](wikipedia://en/3160), alternativity being a weaker form of associativity. Moreover, the [Moufang identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moufang_identities) hold in any octonion algebra. It follows that the invertible elements in any octonion algebra form a [Moufang loop](wikipedia://en/755329), as do the elements of unit norm.

The construction of general octonion algebras over an arbitrary field *k* was described by [Leonard Dickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Dickson) in his book *Algebren und ihre Zahlentheorie* (1927) (Seite 264) and repeated by [Max Zorn](wikipedia://en/19740).[^o3] The product depends on selection of a γ from *k*. Given *q* and *Q* from a [quaternion algebra](wikipedia://en/2776890) over *k*, the octonion is written *q* + *Q*e. Another octonion may be written *r* + *R*e. Then with \* denoting the conjugation in the quaternion algebra, their product is

$$(q + Qe)(r + Re) = (qr + \gamma R^* Q) + (Rq + Q r^* )e .$$

Zorn's [German language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) description of this [Cayley–Dickson construction](wikipedia://en/175609) contributed to the persistent use of this [eponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym) describing the construction of [composition algebras](wikipedia://en/3861635).

[Cohl Furey](wikipedia://en/70611641) has proposed that octonion algebras can be utilized in an attempt to reconcile components of the [Standard Model](wikipedia://en/47641).[^o4]

Note that octonion algebras are specifically unital composition algebras. However, non-unital 8 dimensional composition algebras also exist, such as [Okubo algebras](wikipedia://en/39255698).

## Classification {#Classification}

It is a theorem of [Adolf Hurwitz](wikipedia://en/771690) that the *F*-[isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of the norm form are in one-to-one correspondence with the isomorphism classes of octonion *F*-algebras. Moreover, the possible norm forms are exactly the [Pfister 3-forms](wikipedia://en/7204602) over *F*.[^o6]

Since any two octonion *F*-algebras become isomorphic over the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of *F*, one can apply the ideas of non-[abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315). In particular, by using the fact that the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the split octonions is the split [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) [G<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/292864), one sees the correspondence of isomorphism classes of octonion *F*-algebras with isomorphism classes of G<sub>2</sub>-[torsors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsor) over *F*. These isomorphism classes form the non-abelian Galois cohomology set $H^1(F, G_2)$.[^o7]
