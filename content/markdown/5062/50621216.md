# Empirical characteristic function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Empirical characteristic function"}

Let $(X_1,...,X_n)$ be [independent, identically distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent,_identically_distributed) real-valued random variables with common [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401) $\varphi(t)$. The empirical characteristic function (ECF) defined as

:::

$$\varphi_{n}(t)= \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} e^{i tX_j},$$

is an unbiased and [consistent estimator](wikipedia://en/1497569) of the corresponding population characteristic function $\varphi(t)$, for each $t\in\mathbb R$.

## History {#History}

The ECF apparently made its debut in page 342 of the classical textbook of [Cramér](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cramér) (1946),[^o3] and then as part of the auxiliary tools for [density estimation](wikipedia://en/554671) in Parzen (1962).[^o4] Nearly a decade later the ECF features as the main object of research in two separate lines of application: In Press (1972)[^o5] for [parameter estimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parameter_estimation) and in Heathcote (1972)[^o6] for [goodness-of-fit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodness-of-fit) testing.

Since that time there has subsequently been a vast expansion of [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577) methods based on the ECF. For reviews of estimation methods based on the ECF the reader is referred to Csörgő (1984a),[^o7] Rémillard and Theodorescu (2001),[^o8] Yu (2004),[^o9] and Carrasco and Kotchoni (2017),[^o10] while testing procedures are surveyed by Csörgő (1984b),[^o11] Hušková and Meintanis (2008a),[^o12] Hušková and Meintanis (2008b),[^o13] and Meintanis (2016).[^o14] Ushakov (1999)[^o15] and Prakasa Rao (1987)[^o16] (chapter 8) are also good sources of information on the limit properties of the ECF process, as well as on estimation and goodness-of-fit testing via the ECF.

One of the lines of research that deserves special mention is ECF testing for independence by means of [distance correlation](wikipedia://en/28217722) as originally suggested by Székely et al. (2007).[^o17] This approach has become extremely popular and is currently under vigorous development. We refer to Edelmann et al. (2019)[^o18] and Székely and Rizzo (2023)[^o19] for a recent survey on distance correlation methods.

Another popular line of research is goodness-of-fit testing for multivariate distributions, with special emphasis on testing for multivariate normality; for more information on this topic the reader is referred to the review by Ebner and Henze (2020). [^o20] This approach of ECF-based goodness-of-fit testing has been generalized by Chen et al. (2022) [^o21] to testing fit for arbitrary elliptical distributions by means of modification of the two-sample test initially suggested by Meintanis (2005). [^o22]

A recent account of the basic ECF-based goodness-of-fit methods for testing symmetry, homogeneity and independence may be found in Chen et al. (2019).[^o23]
