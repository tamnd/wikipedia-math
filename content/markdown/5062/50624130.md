# Robert William Genese

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert William Genese"}

**Robert William Genese** (1848, Dublin – 1928) was an Irish mathematician whose career was spent in Wales.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Genese was born on [Westland Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Row) a street on the south side of Dublin on 8 May 1848. From [St John's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge) of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), Genese received in 1871 his bachelor's degree (with rank eighth [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in the [Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripos)) and in 1874 his master's degree.

## Professional life {#Professional_life}

Following an unsuccessful application for the Chair of Mathematics at [Aberystwyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth_University) in 1872, he taught at the Training College in [Carmarthen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmarthen). He finally secured the professorship at Aberystwyth in 1879, and held it until 1919. Along the way his title became Professor of Mathematics and Astronomy.[^o4]

Genese introduced into the United Kingdom the ideas of [Hermann Grassmann](wikipedia://en/99242) (advancing the use of vector analysis). In his 1941 book *The calculus of extensions*, [Henry Forder](wikipedia://en/43469245) published numerous examples in vector analysis taken from Genese's posthumous notes. (Genese's notes were left to the [Mathematical Association](wikipedia://en/2742403) and then given in 1929 to Forder by [E. H. Neville](wikipedia://en/27393555).)[^o5]

Genese was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1904 in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg) with talk *On some useful theorems in the continued multiplication of a regressive product in real four-point space*[^o6] and in 1908 in Rome with talk *The method of reciprocal polars applied to forces in space*.

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- "Suggestions for the Practical Treatment of the Standard Cubic Equation, and a Contribution to the Theory of Substitution." The Mathematical Gazette 9, no. 129 (1917): 65–69.
- "On the Theory of the Plane Complex with Simple Geometrical and Kinematical Illustrations." The Mathematical Gazette 11, no. 164 (1923): 293–301.
- "A Simple Exposition of Grassmann's Methods." The Mathematical Gazette 13, no. 189 (1927): 373–391.
