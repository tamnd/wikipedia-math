# Askey scheme

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Askey scheme"}

In mathematics, the **Askey scheme** is a way of organizing [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) of hypergeometric or basic hypergeometric type into a hierarchy. For the classical orthogonal polynomials discussed in (Andrews Askey 1985),[^o3] the Askey scheme was first drawn by (Labelle 1985)[^o5] and by (txt),[^o7] and has since been extended by (Koekoek Swarttouw 1998)[^o9] and Koekoek, Lesky & Swarttouw (2010)[^o10] to cover basic orthogonal polynomials.

:::

## Askey scheme for hypergeometric orthogonal polynomials {#Askey_scheme_for_hypergeometric_orthogonal_polynomials}

Koekoek, Lesky & Swarttouw (2010)[^o12] give the following version of the Askey scheme:

**$${}_4F_3(4)$$**

[Wilson](wikipedia://en/19585496) | [Racah](wikipedia://en/19595494)

**$${}_3F_2(3)$$**

[Continuous dual Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_Hahn_polynomials) | [Continuous Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_Hahn_polynomials) | [Hahn](wikipedia://en/19585658) | [dual Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_Hahn_polynomials)

**$${}_2F_1(2)$$**

[Meixner–Pollaczek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meixner–Pollaczek_polynomials) | [Jacobi](wikipedia://en/30863587) | [Pseudo Jacobi](wikipedia://en/32740146) | [Meixner](wikipedia://en/19609650) | [Krawtchouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krawtchouk_polynomials)

**$${}_2F_0(1)\ \ / \ \ {}_1F_1(1)$$**

[Laguerre](wikipedia://en/943917) | [Bessel](wikipedia://en/6523885) | [Charlier](wikipedia://en/19585960)

**$${}_2F_0(0)$$**

[Hermite](wikipedia://en/195984)

Here ${}_pF_q(n)$ indicates a [hypergeometric series](wikipedia://en/361184) representation with $n$ parameters

## Completeness {#Completeness}

While there are several approaches to constructing still more general families of orthogonal polynomials, it is usually not possible to extend the Askey scheme by reusing hypergeometric functions of the same form. For instance, one might naively hope to find new examples given by

$$p_n(x) = {}_{q + 1}F_q \left ( \begin{array}{c} -n, n + \mu, a_1(x), \dots, a_{q - 1}(x) \\ b_1, \dots, b_q \end{array} ; 1 \right )$$

above $q = 3$ which corresponds to the Wilson polynomials. This was ruled out in (Cheikh Lamiri Ouni 2009)[^o17] under the assumption that the $a_i(x)$ are degree 1 polynomials such that

$$\prod_{i = 1}^{q - 1} (a_i(x) + r) = \prod_{i = 1}^{q - 1} a_i(x) + \pi(r)$$

for some polynomial $\pi(r)$.
