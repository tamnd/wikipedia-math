# William J. LeVeque

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William J. LeVeque"}

**William Judson LeVeque** (August 9, 1923 – December 1, 2007) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administration_%28academic%29) who worked primarily in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He was [executive director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_director) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) during the 1970s and 1980s when that organization was growing rapidly and greatly increasing its use of computers in [academic publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_publishing).

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

LeVeque was born August 9, 1923, in [Boulder, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulder,_Colorado). He received his [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree from the [University of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_system) in 1944, and a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in 1945 and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1947 from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).[^o4]

He was an instructor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) from 1947 to 1949, then started at [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) as an instructor and rose to professor. In 1970 he moved to the [Claremont Graduate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claremont_Graduate_School). In 1977 he became [executive director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_director) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and remained there until his retirement in 1988.[^o4]

After retirement LeVeque and his wife, Ann, took up [sailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing) and lived on their [sailboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailboat) for three years while they traveled from [Narragansett Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narragansett_Bay) to [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada). They then moved to [Bainbridge Island, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bainbridge_Island,_Washington), where he kept active in volunteer activities for the rest of his life.[^o5] He died December 1, 2007.[^o6] His son [Randall J. LeVeque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randall_J._LeVeque) is a well known applied mathematician.

## Work {#Work}

LeVeque's research interest was [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), specifically [transcendental numbers](wikipedia://en/30325), [uniform distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28discrete%29), and [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558).[^o6]

He wrote a number of number theory textbooks and reference books, which influenced the development of number theory in the United States. A long-term project was to update [Leonard Eugene Dickson](wikipedia://en/1790247)'s *[History of the Theory of Numbers](wikipedia://en/37174589)*. This project eventually produced a six-volume collection titled *Reviews in Number Theory*.[^o6] The [Special Libraries Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Libraries_Association)'s Physics-Astronomy-Mathematics Division awarded LeVeque its Division Award in 1978 for his contributions to the [bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliography) of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).[^o8]

The [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) grew rapidly during LeVeque's time as [executive director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_director) (1977–1988). Revenues tripled from \$5 million in 1977 to \$14.9 million in 1988. The Society began computerizing at a rapid rate during this period, with [Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646) first becoming available electronically through existing academic dial-up services; this system later evolved into [MathSciNet](wikipedia://en/2580683). Most of the headquarters staff received computer terminals for use in the new operations.[^o6]
