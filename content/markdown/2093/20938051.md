# Jiří Matoušek (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jiří Matoušek"}

**Jiří (Jirka) Matoušek** (10 March 1963 – 9 March 2015) was a [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927) and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). He was a professor at [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague) and the author of several textbooks and research monographs.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Matoušek was born in Prague. In 1986, he received his [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) at Charles University under [Miroslav Katětov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miroslav_Katětov).[^o4] From 1986 until his death he was employed at the Department of Applied Mathematics of Charles University, holding a professor position since 2000. He was also a visiting and later full professor at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich).[^o12]

In 1996, he won the [European Mathematical Society prize](wikipedia://en/969470#Prizes)[^o5] and in 2000 he won the Scientist award of the [Learned Society of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_Society_of_the_Czech_Republic).[^o6] In 1998 he was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o7] He became a fellow of the Learned Society of the Czech Republic in 2005.[^o8]

Matoušek's paper on computational aspects of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) won the Best Paper award at the 2012 ACM [Symposium on Discrete Algorithms](wikipedia://en/44632347).[^o9]

Aside from his own academic writing, he has translated the popularization book *Mathematics: A Very Short Introduction* by [Timothy Gowers](wikipedia://en/365715) into Czech.[^o10] He was a supporter and signatory of the [Cost of Knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_of_Knowledge) protest.[^o11]

Matoušek died in 2015, aged 51.[^o12][^o13] In 2021, a lecture hall at the [Faculty of Mathematics and Physics, Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_of_Mathematics_and_Physics,_Charles_University), was named after him.[^o14]
