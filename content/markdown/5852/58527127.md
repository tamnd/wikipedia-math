# Percival Symonds

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Percival Symonds"}

**Percival Mallon Symonds** (April 18, 1893 – August 6, 1960) was an American educational psychologist. He was known for his development of several tests in the fields of [educational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_psychology), [clinical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_psychology), and [school psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_psychology), including the Foreign Language Prognosis Test, the Personality Survey,[^o3] and the Symonds picture-study test, a [projective test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_test) administered to adolescents.[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Percival Mallon Symonds was born on April 18, 1893, in [Newtonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtonville,_Massachusetts), [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts), to Joseph Ainsworth Symonds and Abbie Kendall Mallon.[^o6]

He received preliminary education in the public schools in [West Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Newton,_Massachusetts). He then received his [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1915, followed by an [A.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1920 and 1923, respectively.[^o3][^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Symonds first taught at [Punchard High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punchard_High_School) in [Andover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andover,_Massachusetts) during 1915 to 1917 and then at [Worcester Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester_Academy) from 1917 to 1918. During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), he did statistical work on [ballistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistics) at the [Aberdeen Proving Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Proving_Ground) by the [U.S. Army Ordnance Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army_Ordnance_Department) from 1918 to 1919. He would later work at an [actuarial department](wikipedia://en/189373) of a [life insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_insurance) company.[^o6]

After a year of advanced study in Mathematics in [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), he taught during the summer session in the University in 1920. He became assistant to [Edward Thorndike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Thorndike) in [Teachers College, Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachers_College,_Columbia_University), in 1921 to 1922.[^o6]

He served as the first chairman of the [American Association of Applied Psychologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_Applied_Psychologists)' Education Section and, from 1947 to 1948, as president of the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association)'s Division of Educational Psychology. He was also president of the [American Educational Research Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Educational_Research_Association) from 1956 to 1957.[^o3][^o8]

He was a professor of education and psychology at the [University of Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawaiʻi_at_Mānoa) from 1922 to 1924.[^o9] In 1924, he began teaching at the [Teachers College, Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachers_College,_Columbia_University), where he remained a faculty member until his retirement in 1958. He died on August 6, 1960, in [Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem,_Massachusetts), Massachusetts.[^o3]

## Research {#Research}

Symonds researched the relationship between [personality traits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personality_trait) in [teachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher) and their teaching abilities.[^o11][^o12] His work, which included twenty-one books and over two hundred articles, emphasized the importance of [dynamic psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_psychology).[^o13]
