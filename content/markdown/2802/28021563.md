# Abel's irreducibility theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abel's irreducibility theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Abel's irreducibility theorem**, a [field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_%28mathematics%29) result described in 1829 by [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573),[^o2] asserts that if *f*(*x*) is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F* that shares a [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_polynomial) with a polynomial *g*(*x*) that is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/188725) over *F*, then every root of *g*(*x*) is a root of *f*(*x*). Equivalently, if *f*(*x*) shares at least one root with *g*(*x*) then *f* is divisible evenly by *g*(*x*), meaning that *f*(*x*) can be factored as *g*(*x*)*h*(*x*) with *h*(*x*) also having [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) in *F*.[^o3][^o4]

:::

[Corollaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corollaries) of the theorem include:[^o3]

- If *f*(*x*) is irreducible, there is no lower-[degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) polynomial (other than the [zero polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_polynomial)) that shares any root with it. For example, *x*<sup>2</sup> − 2 is irreducible over the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) and has $\sqrt{2}$ as a root; hence there is no linear or constant polynomial over the rationals having $\sqrt{2}$ as a root. Furthermore, there is no same-degree polynomial that shares any roots with *f*(*x*), other than constant multiples of *f*(*x*).
- If *f*(*x*) ≠ *g*(*x*) are two different irreducible [monic polynomials](wikipedia://en/314730), then they share no roots.
