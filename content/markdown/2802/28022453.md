# Diplomatic Wireless Service

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Diplomatic Wireless Service"}

*Image: View\_of\_ASPI\_and\_DWS\_logos\_at\_Aspidistra\_site.jpg.* View of the ASPI and DWS logos in a remaining safe building near Crowborough in 2003

The **Diplomatic Wireless Service** (**DWS**) was the name of the communications system set up for the British [Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Office) by Brigadier [Richard Gambier-Parry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gambier-Parry), the first Foreign Office Director of Communications, in the latter part of 1945. It grew out of the Special Communication Units (SCU) which were responsible for communications for [MI6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MI6) during the war. Its original base was at [Whaddon Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whaddon_Hall) in [Buckinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckinghamshire), but it moved to [Hanslope Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanslope_Park) in the winter of 1946/47.[^o2][^o3] The primary role of the DWS was communications between British embassies and the UK, this part of its operations being based at Hanslope Park, which is still the HQ of its successor, [His Majesty's Government Communications Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty's_Government_Communications_Centre) (HMGCC).

:::

It also operated and maintained transmitters at home and abroad on behalf of the Foreign Office for the broadcasting of the European Service of the BBC and the BBC Overseas Service, which were combined as the [BBC World Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_World_Service) in 1988.[^o4] The main UK broadcast operation was based under the [Ashdown Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashdown_Forest) near [Crowborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowborough) in [East Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Sussex). The main transmitter was called ['Aspi 1'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspidistra_%28transmitter%29). [Crowborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowborough) was also the engineering base for the overseas relay stations at [Zygi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygi), [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus) known as the British Eastern Mediterranean Relay Station (BEMRS) and [Perim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perim) and later on [Masirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masirah) both called the British Middle East Relay Station. This section of the DWS was renamed the Foreign and Commonwealth Office Communications Engineering Division in the mid-1970s and was ultimately transferred to the control of the BBC in 1985.[^o5]

DWS operators were also involved in radio eavesdropping, the gathering of [signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence) (SIGINT) for [GCHQ](wikipedia://en/12884), from within the compounds of embassies. The first of these undercover stations was established at [Ankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankara) in 1943;[^o6] another important station was at [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm), a location ideally suited for the monitoring of radio traffic from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).[^o7]
