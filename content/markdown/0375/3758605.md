# Martingale representation theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Martingale representation theorem"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **martingale representation theorem** states that a random variable with finite variance that is [measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable) with respect to the [filtration](wikipedia://en/57854817) generated by a [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/149984) can be written in terms of an [Itô integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itô_integral) with respect to this Brownian motion.

:::

The theorem only asserts the existence of the representation and does not help to find it explicitly; it is possible in many cases to determine the form of the representation using [Malliavin calculus](wikipedia://en/837875).

Similar theorems also exist for [martingales](wikipedia://en/274536) on filtrations induced by [jump processes](wikipedia://en/2180750), for example, by [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876).

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $B_t$ be a [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/149984) on a standard [filtered probability space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_probability_space) $(\Omega, \mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_t, P )$ and let $\mathcal{G}_t$ be the [augmented filtration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmented_filtration) generated by $B$. If *X* is a [square integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_integrable) random variable measurable with respect to $\mathcal{G}_\infty$, then there exists a [predictable process](wikipedia://en/33414015) *C* which is [adapted](wikipedia://en/6583420) with respect to $\mathcal{G}_t,$ such that

$$X = E(X) + \int_0^\infty C_s\,dB_s.$$

Consequently,

$$E(X| \mathcal{G}_t) = E(X) + \int_0^t C_s \, d B_s.$$

:::

## Application in finance {#Application_in_finance}

The martingale representation theorem can be used to establish the existence of a [hedging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_%28finance%29) strategy. Suppose that $\left ( M_t \right )_{0 \le t < \infty}$ is a Q-martingale process, whose [volatility](wikipedia://en/11930108) $\sigma_t$ is always non-zero. Then, if $\left ( N_t \right )_{0 \le t < \infty}$ is any other Q-martingale, there exists an $\mathcal{F}$-previsible process $\varphi$, unique up to sets of measure 0, such that $\int_0^T \varphi_t^2 \sigma_t^2 \, dt < \infty$ with probability one, and *N* can be written as:

$$N_t = N_0 + \int_0^t \varphi_s\, d M_s.$$

The replicating strategy is defined to be:

- hold $\varphi_t$ units of the stock at the time *t*, and
- hold $\psi_t B_t = C_t - \varphi_t Z_t$ units of the bond.

where $Z_t$ is the stock price discounted by the bond price to time $t$ and $C_t$ is the expected payoff of the option at time $t$.

At the expiration day *T*, the value of the portfolio is:

$$V_T = \varphi_T S_T + \psi_T B_T = C_T = X$$

and it is easy to check that the strategy is self-financing: the change in the value of the portfolio only depends on the change of the asset prices $\left ( dV_t = \varphi_t \, dS_t + \psi_t\, dB_t \right )$.
