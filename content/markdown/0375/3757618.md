::: {#o7 .subject cue=lead name="Tetragonal disphenoid honeycomb"}

The **tetragonal disphenoid tetrahedral honeycomb** is a space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in [Euclidean 3-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_3-space) made up of identical [tetragonal disphenoidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetragonal_disphenoid) cells. Cells are [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive) with 4 identical [isosceles triangle](wikipedia://en/315428) faces. [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls it an *oblate tetrahedrille* or shortened to *obtetrahedrille*.[^o8]

:::

A cell can be seen as 1/12 of a translational cube, with its vertices centered on two faces and two edges. Four of its edges belong to 6 cells, and two edges belong to 4 cells.

*Image: oblate tetrahedrille cell.png.*

The tetrahedral disphenoid honeycomb is the dual of the uniform [bitruncated cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3736299).

Its vertices form the A\* / D\* lattice, which is also known as the [body-centered cubic](wikipedia://en/666697) lattice.

## Geometry {#Geometry}

This honeycomb's [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is a [tetrakis cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrakis_cube): 24 disphenoids meet at each vertex. The union of these 24 disphenoids forms a [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631). Each edge of the tessellation is surrounded by either four or six disphenoids, according to whether it forms the base or one of the sides of its adjacent isosceles triangle faces respectively. When an edge forms the base of its adjacent isosceles triangles, and is surrounded by four disphenoids, they form an irregular [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635). When an edge forms one of the two equal sides of its adjacent isosceles triangle faces, the six disphenoids surrounding the edge form a special type of [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975) called a [trigonal trapezohedron](wikipedia://en/3504388).

*Image: Disphenoid tetrah hc.png.*

An orientation of the tetragonal disphenoid honeycomb can be obtained by starting with a [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318), subdividing it at the planes $x=y$, $x=z$, and $y=z$ (i.e. subdividing each cube into [path-tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/20740340)), then squashing it along the main diagonal until the distance between the points (0, 0, 0) and (1, 1, 1) becomes the same as the distance between the points (0, 0, 0) and (0, 0, 1).

The **hexakis cubic honeycomb** is a uniform space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in Euclidean 3-space. [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls it a *pyramidille*.[^o12]

Cells can be seen in a translational cube, using 4 vertices on one face, and the cube center. Edges are colored by how many cells are around each of them.

*Image: Cubic square pyramid.png.*

It can be seen as a [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318) with each cube subdivided by a center point into 6 [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455) cells.

There are two types of planes of faces: one as a [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809), and flattened [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858) with half of the triangles removed as *holes*.

The **square bipyramidal honeycomb** is a uniform space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in Euclidean 3-space. [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls it an *oblate octahedrille* or shortened to *oboctahedrille*.[^o8]

A cell can be seen positioned within a translational cube, with 4 vertices mid-edge and 2 vertices in opposite faces. Edges are colored and labeled by the number of cells around the edge.

*Image: Cubic square bipyramid.png.*

It can be seen as a [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318) with each cube subdivided by a center point into 6 [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455) cells. The original cubic honeycomb walls are removed, joining pairs of square pyramids into square bipyramids (octahedra). Its vertex and edge framework is identical to the [hexakis cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexakis_cubic_honeycomb).

There is one type of plane with faces: a flattened [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858) with half of the triangles as *holes*. These cut face-diagonally through the original cubes. There are also [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809) plane that exist as nonface *holes* passing through the centers of the octahedral cells.

The **phyllic disphenoidal honeycomb** is a uniform space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in Euclidean 3-space. [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls this an *Eighth pyramidille*.[^o35]

A cell can be seen as 1/48 of a translational cube with vertices positioned: one corner, one edge center, one face center, and the cube center. The edge colors and labels specify how many cells exist around the edge. It is one 1/6 of a smaller cube, with 6 phyllic disphenoidal cells sharing a common diagonal axis.

*Image: Eighth pyramidille cell.png.*
