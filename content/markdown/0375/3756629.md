# Imre Leader

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Imre Leader"}

**Imre Bennett Leader** (born 30 October 1963) is a British mathematician, a professor in [DPMMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPMMS) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) working in the field of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). He is also known as an [Othello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reversi) player.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He is the son of the physicist Elliot Leader and his first wife Ninon Neményi (his mother was previously married to the poet Endre Kövesi); [Darian Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darian_Leader) is his brother.[^o4] [Imre Lakatos](wikipedia://en/38455) was a family friend and his godfather.[^o5]

Leader was educated at [St Paul's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Paul's_School_%28London%29) in London, from 1976 to 1980.[^o6] He won a silver medal on the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) team at the 1981 [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) (IMO) for pre-undergraduates. He later acted as the official leader of the British IMO team, taking over from Adam McBride in 1999, to 2001.[^o8][^o9][^o10] He was the IMO's Chief Coordinator and Problems Group Chairman in 2002.[^o11]

Leader went on to [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where he graduated B.A. in 1984, M.A. in 1989, and Ph.D. in 1989.[^o12] His Ph.D. in mathematics was for work on combinatorics, supervised by [Béla Bollobás](wikipedia://en/614956). From 1989 to 1996 he was [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow#In_ancient_universities) at [Peterhouse, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge), then was Reader at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) from 1996 to 2000. He was a lecturer at Cambridge from 2000 to 2002, and Reader there from 2002 to 2005.[^o10] In 2000 he became a Fellow of Trinity College.[^o14]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In 1999 Leader was awarded a [Junior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Whitehead_Prize) for his contributions to combinatorics. Cited results included the proof, with Reinhard Diestel, of the bounded graph conjecture of [Rudolf Halin](wikipedia://en/38581524).[^o16][^o17]

## Othello {#Othello}

Leader in an interview in 2016 stated that he began to play Othello in 1981, with his friend [Jeremy Rickard](wikipedia://en/57801041).[^o19] In the years from 1982 and 2022 he was 16 times the British Othello champion.[^o20] In 1983 he came second in the world individual championship, and in 1988 he played on the British team that won the world team championship.[^o21]

As of , Leader has won the European Grand Prix Championship six times, most recently in 2023.[^o22]
