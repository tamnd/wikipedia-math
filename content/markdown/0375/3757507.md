::: {#o8 .subject cue=lead name="Quarter cubic honeycomb"}

The **quarter cubic honeycomb**, **quarter cubic cellulation** or **bitruncated alternated cubic honeycomb** is a space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in [Euclidean 3-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_3-space). It is composed of [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) and [truncated tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/279651) in a ratio of 1:1. It is called "quarter-cubic" because its symmetry unit – the minimal block from which the pattern is developed by reflections – is four times that of the [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318).

:::

It is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) with 6 [truncated tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/279651) and 2 [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) around each vertex.

It is one of the 28 [convex uniform honeycombs](wikipedia://en/2961).

The faces of this honeycomb's cells form four families of parallel planes, each with a [\3.6.3.6 tiling](wikipedia://en/2864397).

Its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is an isosceles [antiprism](wikipedia://en/1851): two [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) joined by six [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428).

[John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) calls this honeycomb a **truncated tetrahedrille**, and its dual [oblate cubille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblate_cubille).

The vertices and edges represent a **[Kagome lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagome_lattice)** in three dimensions,[^o9] which is the **[pyrochlore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrochlore)** lattice.

## Construction {#Construction}

The quarter cubic honeycomb can be constructed in slab layers of truncated tetrahedra and tetrahedral cells, seen as two [trihexagonal tilings](wikipedia://en/2864397). Two tetrahedra are stacked by a vertex and a [central inversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_inversion). In each [trihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864397), half of the triangles belong to tetrahedra, and half belong to truncated tetrahedra. These slab layers must be stacked with tetrahedra triangles to truncated tetrahedral triangles to construct the uniform *quarter cubic honeycomb*. Slab layers of hexagonal prisms and triangular prisms can be alternated for *elongated* honeycombs, but these are also not uniform.

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

Cells can be shown in two different symmetries. The reflection generated form represented by its [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) has two colors of [truncated cuboctahedra](wikipedia://en/285879). The symmetry can be doubled by relating the pairs of ringed and unringed nodes of the Coxeter-Dynkin diagram, which can be shown with one colored tetrahedral and truncated tetrahedral cells.
