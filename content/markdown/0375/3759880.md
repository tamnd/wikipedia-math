# Gerhard Hessenberg

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gerhard Hessenberg"}

**Gerhard Hessenberg** (; 16 August 1874 – 16 November 1925) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), and [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Hessenberg received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) in 1899 under the guidance of [Hermann Schwarz](wikipedia://en/3165834) and [Lazarus Fuchs](wikipedia://en/2817087).

His name is usually associated with [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), where he is known for proving that [Desargues' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desargues'_theorem) is a consequence of [Pappus's hexagon theorem](wikipedia://en/1493395),[^o4] and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) where he is known for introducing the concept of a [connection](wikipedia://en/308815).[^o5][^o6] He was also a set theorist: the [Hessenberg sum and product](wikipedia://en/2139226#Natural_operations) of [ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932) are named after him. However, [Hessenberg matrices](wikipedia://en/519204) are named for [Karl Hessenberg](wikipedia://en/14699638), a near relative.

In 1908 Gerhard Hessenberg was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Rome.[^o7]
