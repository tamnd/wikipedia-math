# Snub polyhedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Snub polyhedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **snub polyhedron** is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) obtained by performing a [snub](wikipedia://en/9428917) operation: [alternating](wikipedia://en/4349117) a corresponding [omnitruncated](wikipedia://en/7083690) or [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) polyhedron, depending on the definition. Some, but not all, authors include [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851) as snub polyhedra, as they are obtained by this construction from a degenerate "polyhedron" with only two faces (a [dihedron](wikipedia://en/6109457)).

:::

[Chiral](wikipedia://en/1170160) snub polyhedra do not always have [reflection symmetry](wikipedia://en/1390873) and hence sometimes have two *enantiomorphous* (left- and right-handed) forms which are reflections of each other. Their [symmetry groups](wikipedia://en/28186) are all [point groups](wikipedia://en/2694525#The_seven_remaining_point_groups).

Snub polyhedra have [Wythoff symbol](wikipedia://en/3691548) $| p q r$ and by extension, [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) $3.p.3.q.3.r$. Retrosnub polyhedra (a subset of the snub polyhedron, containing the [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898), [small retrosnub icosicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157694), and [great retrosnub icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157697)) still have this form of Wythoff symbol, but their vertex configurations are instead $\frac{(3.-p.3.-q.3.-r)}{2}.$

## List of snub polyhedra {#List_of_snub_polyhedra}

### Uniform {#Uniform}

There are 12 uniform snub polyhedra, not including the antiprisms, the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) as a snub [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606), the [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898) as a retrosnub [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) and the [great disnub dirhombidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4145225), also known as **Skilling's figure**.

When the [Schwarz triangle](wikipedia://en/2069325) of the snub polyhedron is [isosceles](wikipedia://en/315428), the snub polyhedron is not chiral. This is the case for the antiprisms, the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084), the [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898), the [small snub icosicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157567), and the [small retrosnub icosicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157694).

In the pictures of the snub derivation (showing a distorted snub polyhedron, topologically identical to the uniform version, arrived at from geometrically alternating the parent uniform omnitruncated polyhedron) where green is not present, the faces derived from alternation are coloured red and yellow, while the snub triangles are blue. Where green is present (only for the [snub icosidodecadodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157687) and [great snub dodecicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157674)), the faces derived from alternation are red, yellow, and blue, while the snub triangles are green.

***Notes:***

- The [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084), [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249) and [snub dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/287137) are the only three [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) ones. They are obtained by snubification of the [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654), [truncated cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/285879) and the [truncated icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/286534) - the three convex truncated [quasiregular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/8810651).
- The only snub polyhedron with the [chiral](wikipedia://en/1170160) [octahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedral_group) of symmetries is the [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249).
- Only the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) and the [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898) are also [regular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/333299). They are also [deltahedra](wikipedia://en/493995).
- Only the icosahedron, great icosahedron, [small snub icosicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157567), [small retrosnub icosicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157694), [great dirhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2910030), and [great disnub dirhombidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4145225) also have reflective symmetries.

There is also the infinite set of [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851). They are formed from [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), which are truncated [hosohedra](wikipedia://en/2316192), *[degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952)* [regular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/333299). Those up to hexagonal are listed below. In the pictures showing the snub derivation, the faces derived from alternation (of the prism bases) are coloured red, and the snub triangles are coloured yellow. The exception is the tetrahedron, for which all the faces are derived as red snub triangles, as alternating the square bases of the cube results in degenerate [digons](wikipedia://en/5442728) as faces.

### Non-uniform {#Non-uniform}

Two [Johnson solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_solids) are snub polyhedra: the [snub disphenoid](wikipedia://en/1133565) and the [snub square antiprism](wikipedia://en/1133918). Neither is chiral.
