# Witness (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Witness"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), a **witness** is a specific value $t$ to be substituted for variable $x$ of an [existential statement](wikipedia://en/91420) of the form $\exists x \varphi(x)$ such that $\varphi(t)$ is true.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For example, a theory *T* of arithmetic is said to be inconsistent if there exists a proof in *T* of the formula "0 = 1". The formula I(*T*), which says that *T* is inconsistent, is thus an existential formula. A witness for the inconsistency of *T* is a particular proof of "0 = 1" in *T*.

Boolos, Burgess, and Jeffrey (2002:81) define the notion of a witness with the example, in which *S* is an *n*-place relation on natural numbers, *R* is an *(n+1)*-place [recursive relation](wikipedia://en/332264), and ↔ indicates [logical equivalence](wikipedia://en/169324) (if and only if):

*S*(*x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub>) ↔ ∃*y* *R*(*x*<sub>1</sub>, . . ., *x*<sub>*n*</sub>, *y*)

"A *y* such that *R* holds of the *x<sub>i</sub>* may be called a 'witness' to the relation *S* holding of the *x<sub>i</sub>* (provided we understand that when the witness is a number rather than a person, a witness only testifies to what is true)."

In this particular example, the authors defined *s* to be *(positively) recursively semidecidable*, or simply *semirecursive*.

:::

## Henkin witnesses {#Henkin_witnesses}

In [predicate calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_calculus), a **Henkin witness** for a sentence $\exists x\, \varphi(x)$ in a theory *T* is a [term](wikipedia://en/24885593) *c* such that *T* proves *φ*(*c*) (Hinman 2005:196). The use of such witnesses is a key technique in the proof of [Gödel's completeness theorem](wikipedia://en/12450) presented by [Leon Henkin](wikipedia://en/356165) in 1949.

## Relation to game semantics {#Relation_to_game_semantics}

The notion of witness leads to the more general idea of [game semantics](wikipedia://en/617121). In the case of sentence $\exists x\, \varphi(x)$ the winning strategy for the verifier is to pick a witness for $\varphi$. For more complex formulas involving [universal quantifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_quantifier), the existence of a winning strategy for the verifier depends on the existence of appropriate [Skolem functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skolem_function). For example, if *S* denotes $\forall x \, \exists y\, \varphi(x,y)$ then an [equisatisfiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equisatisfiable) statement for *S* is $\exists f \,\forall x \, \varphi(x,f(x))$. The Skolem function *f* (if it exists) actually codifies a winning strategy for the verifier of *S* by returning a witness for the existential sub-formula for every choice of *x* the falsifier might make.
