# Restricted Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Restricted Lie algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **restricted Lie algebra** (or ***p*-Lie algebra**) is a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p*>0 together with an additional "*p*th power" operation. Most naturally occurring Lie algebras in characteristic *p* come with this structure, because the Lie algebra of a [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) over a field of characteristic *p* is restricted.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a Lie algebra over a field *k* of characteristic *p*>0. The [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188#Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_algebra) of $\mathfrak{g}$ is defined by $(\text{ad }X)(Y)=[X,Y]$ for $X,Y\in \mathfrak{g}$. A *p*-**mapping** on $\mathfrak{g}$ is a function from $\mathfrak{g}$ to itself, $X \mapsto X^{[p]}$, satisfying:[^o4]

- $\mathrm{ad}(X^{[p]}) = (\mathrm{ad}\; X)^p$ for all $X \in \mathfrak{g}$,
- $(tX)^{[p]} = t^pX^{[p]}$ for all $t \in k$ and $X \in \mathfrak{g}$,
- $(X+Y)^{[p]} = X^{[p]} + Y^{[p]} + \sum_{i=1}^{p-1} s_i(X,Y)$ for all $X,Y \in \mathfrak{g}$, where $s_i(X,Y)$ is $1/i$ times the coefficient of $t^{i-1}$ in the formal expression $(\mathrm{ad}\; (tX+Y))^{p-1}(X)$.

[Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson) (1937) defined a **restricted Lie algebra** over *k* to be a Lie algebra over *k* together with a *p*-mapping. A Lie algebra is said to be **restrictable** if it has at least one *p*-mapping. By the first property above, in a restricted Lie algebra, the derivation $(\mathrm{ad}\; X)^p$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ is [inner](wikipedia://en/17944#Derivations) for each $X\in \mathfrak{g}$. In fact, a Lie algebra is restrictable if and only if the derivation $(\mathrm{ad}\; X)^p$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ is inner for each $X\in \mathfrak{g}$.[^o5]

For example:

- For *p* = 2, a restricted Lie algebra has $(X+Y)^{[2]}=X^{[2]}+[Y,X]+Y^{[2]}$.
- For *p* = 3, a restricted Lie algebra has $(X+Y)^{[3]}=X^{[3]}+2[X,[Y,X]]+[Y,[Y,X]]+Y^{[3]}$.

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) *A* over a field *k* of characteristic *p*>0, the commutator $[X,Y] := XY-YX$ and the *p*-mapping $X^{[p]} := X^p$ make *A* into a restricted Lie algebra.[^o4] In particular, taking *A* to be the [ring of *n* x *n* matrices](wikipedia://en/487541) shows that the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{gl}(n)$ of *n* x *n* matrices over *k* is a restricted Lie algebra, with the *p*-mapping being the *p*th power of a matrix. This "explains" the definition of a restricted Lie algebra: the complicated formula for $(X+Y)^{[p]}$ is needed to express the *p*th power of the sum of two matrices over *k*, $(X+Y)^p$, given that *X* and *Y* typically do not commute.

Let *A* be an algebra over a field *k*. (Here *A* is a possibly [non-associative algebra](wikipedia://en/6533841).) Then the [derivations](wikipedia://en/17944#Derivations) of *A* over *k* form a Lie algebra $\text{Der}_k(A)$, with the Lie bracket being the commutator, $[D_1,D_2]:=D_1D_2-D_2D_1$. When *k* has characteristic *p*>0, then iterating a derivation *p* times yields a derivation, and this makes $\text{Der}_k(A)$ into a restricted Lie algebra.[^o4] If *A* has finite [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) as a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), then $\text{Der}_k(A)$ is the Lie algebra of the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) scheme of *A* over *k*; that indicates why spaces of derivations are a natural way to construct Lie algebras.

Let *G* be a group scheme over a field *k* of characteristic *p*>0, and let $\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ be the [Zariski tangent space](wikipedia://en/510207) at the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) of *G*. Then $\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ is a restricted Lie algebra over *k*.[^o8] This is essentially a special case of the previous example. Indeed, each element *X* of $\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ determines a left-invariant [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) on *G*, and hence a left-invariant derivation on the ring of [regular functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_function) on *G*. The *p*th power of this derivation is again a left-invariant derivation, hence the derivation associated to an element $X^{[p]}$ of $\mathrm{Lie}(G)$. Conversely, every restricted Lie algebra of finite dimension over *k* is the Lie algebra of a group scheme. In fact, $G\mapsto\mathrm{Lie}(G)$ is an [equivalence of categories](wikipedia://en/379051) from finite group schemes *G* of height at most 1 over *k* (meaning that $f^p=0$ for all regular functions *f* on *G* that vanish at the identity element) to restricted Lie algebras of finite dimension over *k*.[^o9]

In a sense, this means that Lie theory is less powerful in positive characteristic than in characteristic zero. In characteristic *p*>0, the [multiplicative group](wikipedia://en/510523#Examples) $G_m$ (of dimension 1) and its finite subgroup scheme $\mu_p=\{ x\in G_m:x^p=1\}$ have the same restricted Lie algebra, namely the vector space *k* with the *p*-mapping $a^{[p]}=a^p$. More generally, the restricted Lie algebra of a group scheme *G* over *k* only depends on the kernel of the [Frobenius homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_homomorphism) on *G*, which is a subgroup scheme of height at most 1.[^o10] For another example, the Lie algebra of the [additive group](wikipedia://en/510523#Examples) $G_a$ is the vector space *k* with *p*-mapping equal to zero. The corresponding Frobenius kernel is the subgroup scheme $\alpha_p=\{x\in G_a:x^p=0\}.$

For a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X* over a field *k* of characteristic *p*>0, the space $H^0(X,TX)$ of vector fields on *X* is a restricted Lie algebra over *k*. (If *X* is [affine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme), so that $X=\text{Spec}(A)$ for a commutative *k*-algebra *A*, this is the Lie algebra of derivations of *A* over *k*. In general, one can informally think of $H^0(X,TX)$ as the Lie algebra of the automorphism group of *X* over *k*.) An [action](wikipedia://en/12781) of a group scheme *G* on *X* determines a homomorphism $\text{Lie}(G)\to H^0(X,TX)$ of restricted Lie algebras.[^o11]

:::

## The choice of a *p*-mapping {#The_choice_of_a_p-mapping}

Given two *p*-mappings on a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, their difference is a *p*-linear function from $\mathfrak{g}$ to the [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_a_Lie_algebra) $\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})$. (*p*-linearity means that $f(X+Y)=f(X)+f(Y)$ and $f(tX)=t^pf(X)$.) Thus, if the center of $\mathfrak{g}$ is zero, then $\mathfrak{g}$ is a restricted Lie algebra in *at most* one way.[^o5] In particular, this comment applies to any [simple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/59500649) of characteristic *p*>0.

## The restricted enveloping algebra {#The_restricted_enveloping_algebra}

The functor that takes an associative algebra *A* over *k* to *A* as a restricted Lie algebra has a [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint) $\mathfrak{g}\mapsto u(\mathfrak{g})$, called the **restricted enveloping algebra**. To construct this, let $U(\mathfrak{g})$ be the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) of $\mathfrak{g}$ over *k* (ignoring the *p*-mapping of $\mathfrak{g}$). Let *I* be the two-sided [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) generated by the elements $X^p - X^{[p]}$ for $X\in\mathfrak{g}$; then the restricted enveloping algebra is the quotient ring $u(\mathfrak{g}) = U(\mathfrak{g}) / I$. It satisfies a form of the [Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem](wikipedia://en/762696): if $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28vector_space%29) for $\mathfrak{g}$ as a *k*-vector space, then a basis for $u(\mathfrak{g})$ is given by all ordered products $e_1^{i_1}\cdots e_n^{i_n}$ with $0\leq i_j\leq p-1$ for each *j*. In particular, the map $\mathfrak{g}\to u(\mathfrak{g})$ is injective, and if $\mathfrak{g}$ has dimension *n* as a vector space, then $u(\mathfrak{g})$ has dimension $p^n$ as a vector space.[^o14]

A **restricted representation** *V* of a restricted Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is a [representation](wikipedia://en/318712) of $\mathfrak{g}$ as a Lie algebra such that $X^{[p]}(v)=X^p(v)$ for all $X\in \mathfrak{g}$ and $v\in V$. Restricted representations of $\mathfrak{g}$ are equivalent to [modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_%28algebra%29) over the restricted enveloping algebra.

## Classification of simple Lie algebras {#Classification_of_simple_Lie_algebras}

The simple Lie algebras of finite dimension over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) of characteristic zero were classified by [Wilhelm Killing](wikipedia://en/1531824) and [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) in the 1880s and 1890s, using [root systems](wikipedia://en/277087). Namely, every simple Lie algebra is of type A<sub>*n*</sub>, B<sub>*n*</sub>, C<sub>*n*</sub>, D<sub>*n*</sub>, E<sub>6</sub>, E<sub>7</sub>, E<sub>8</sub>, F<sub>4</sub>, or G<sub>2</sub>.[^o16] (For example, the simple Lie algebra of type A<sub>*n*</sub> is the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}(n+1)$ of (*n*+1) x (*n*+1) matrices of [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) zero.)

In characteristic *p*>0, the classification of [simple algebraic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_algebraic_group) is the same as in characteristic zero. Their Lie algebras are simple in most cases, and so there are simple Lie algebras A<sub>*n*</sub>, B<sub>*n*</sub>, C<sub>*n*</sub>, D<sub>*n*</sub>, E<sub>6</sub>, E<sub>7</sub>, E<sub>8</sub>, F<sub>4</sub>, G<sub>2</sub>, called (in this context) the **classical** simple Lie algebras. (Because they come from algebraic groups, the classical simple Lie algebras are restricted.) Surprisingly, there are also many other finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras in characteristic *p*>0. In particular, there are the simple Lie algebras of **Cartan type**, which are finite-dimensional analogs of infinite-dimensional Lie algebras in characteristic zero studied by Cartan. Namely, Cartan studied the Lie algebra of vector fields on a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) of dimension *n*, or the subalgebra of vector fields that preserve a [volume form](wikipedia://en/1855811), a [symplectic form](wikipedia://en/28356), or a [contact structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_structure). In characteristic *p*>0, the simple Lie algebras of Cartan type include both restrictable and non-restrictable examples.[^o17]

[Richard Earl Block](wikipedia://en/8907509) and Robert Lee Wilson (1988) classified the restricted simple Lie algebras over an algebraically closed field of characteristic *p*>7. Namely, they are all of classical or Cartan type. Alexander Premet and Helmut Strade (2004) extended the classification to Lie algebras which need not be restricted, and to a larger range of characteristics. (In characteristic 5, Hayk Melikyan found another family of simple Lie algebras.) Namely, every simple Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field of characteristic *p*>3 is of classical, Cartan, or Melikyan type.[^o18]

## Jacobson's Galois correspondence {#Jacobson's_Galois_correspondence}

[Jacobson's Galois correspondence](wikipedia://en/4770099#Galois_correspondence_for_purely_inseparable_extensions) for purely inseparable field extensions is expressed in terms of restricted Lie algebras.
