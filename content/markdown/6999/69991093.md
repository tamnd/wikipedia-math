# Kiepert conics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kiepert conics"}

In [triangle geometry](wikipedia://en/69736905), the **Kiepert conics** are two special [conics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic) associated with the reference triangle. One of them is a [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052), called the **Kiepert hyperbola** and the other is a [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231), called the **Kiepert parabola**. The Kiepert conics are defined as follows:

:::

If the three triangles $A^\prime BC$, $AB^\prime C$ and $ABC^\prime$, constructed on the sides of a triangle $ABC$ as bases, are similar, isosceles and similarly situated, then the triangles $ABC$ and $A^\prime B^\prime C^\prime$ are in [perspective](wikipedia://en/14909067). As the base angle of the isosceles triangles varies between $-\pi/2$ and $\pi/2$, the [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of the [center of perspectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_perspectivity) of the triangles $ABC$ and $A^\prime B^\prime C^\prime$ is a hyperbola called the Kiepert hyperbola and the [envelope](wikipedia://en/880235) of their [axis of perspectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_perspectivity) is a parabola called the Kiepert parabola.

It has been proved that the Kiepert hyperbola is the hyperbola passing through the vertices, the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) and the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) of the reference triangle and the Kiepert parabola is the parabola [inscribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inconic) in the reference triangle having the [Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622) as [directrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directrix_%28conic_section%29) and the [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488) X<sub>110</sub> as [focus](wikipedia://en/1032607).[^o2] The following quote from a paper by R. H. Eddy and R. Fritsch is enough testimony to establish the importance of the Kiepert conics in the study of triangle geometry:[^o3]

"If a visitor from Mars desired to learn the geometry of the triangle but could stay in the earth's relatively dense atmosphere only long enough for a single lesson, earthling mathematicians would, no doubt, be hard-pressed to meet this request. In this paper, we believe that we have an optimum solution to the problem. The Kiepert conics ...."

## Kiepert hyperbola {#Kiepert_hyperbola}

The Kiepert hyperbola was discovered by [Ludvig Kiepert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_August_Ludwig_Kiepert) while investigating the solution of the following problem proposed by [Emile Lemoine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emile_Lemoine) in 1868: "Construct a triangle, given the peaks of the equilateral triangles constructed on the sides." A solution to the problem was published by [Ludvig Kiepert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_August_Ludwig_Kiepert) in 1869 and the solution contained a remark which effectively stated the locus definition of the Kiepert hyperbola alluded to earlier.[^o3]

### Basic facts {#Basic_facts}

Let $a, b, c$ be the side lengths and $A,B,C$ the vertex angles of the reference triangle $ABC$.

#### Equation {#Equation}

The equation of the Kiepert hyperbola in barycentric coordinates $x:y:z$ is

$$\frac{b^2-c^2}{x}+\frac{c^2-a^2}{y}+\frac{a^2-b^2}{z}=0.$$

#### Center, asymptotes {#Center,_asymptotes}

- The centre of the Kiepert hyperbola is the triangle center X(115). The barycentric coordinates of the center are

$$(b^2-c^2)^2 : (c^2-a^2)^2 : (a^2-b^2)^2.$$

- The asymptotes of the Kiepert hyperbola are the [Simson lines](wikipedia://en/3307757) of the intersections of the [Brocard axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocard_axis) with the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432).
- The Kiepert hyperbola is a [rectangular hyperbola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectangular_hyperbola) and hence its eccentricity is $\sqrt{2}$.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

1. The center of the Kiepert hyperbola lies on the [nine-point circle](wikipedia://en/161243). The center is the midpoint of the [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) joining the isogonic centers of triangle $ABC$ which are the triangle centers X(13) and X(14) in the [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593).
1. The image of the Kiepert hyperbola under the [isogonal transformation](wikipedia://en/2083100) is the [Brocard axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocard_axis) of triangle $ABC$ which is the line joining the [symmedian point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmedian_point) and the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter).
1. Let $P$ be a point in the plane of a nonequilateral triangle $ABC$ and let $p$ be the trilinear polar of $P$ with respect to $ABC$. The locus of the points $P$ such that $p$ is perpendicular to the Euler line of $ABC$ is the Kiepert hyperbola.

:::

## Kiepert parabola {#Kiepert_parabola}

The Kiepert parabola was first studied in 1888 by a German mathematics teacher Augustus Artzt in a "school program".[^o3][^o11]

### Basic facts {#Basic_facts}

- The equation of the Kiepert parabola in barycentric coordinates $x:y:z$ is

$f^2x^2+g^2y^2+h^2z^2 - 2fgxy - 2ghyz - 2 hfzx=0$ where $f=(b^2-c^2)/a, g=(c^2-a^2)/b, h=(a^2-b^2)/c$.

- The focus of the Kiepert parabola is the triangle center X(110). The barycentric coordinates of the focus are

$$a^2/(b^2-c^2) : b^2/(c^2-a^2) : c^2/(a^2-b^2)$$

- The directrix of the Kiepert parabola is the Euler line of triangle $ABC$.
