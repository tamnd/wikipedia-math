# List of largest known primes and probable primes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of largest known primes and probable primes"}

The table below lists the largest currently known [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) and [probable primes](wikipedia://en/160560) (PRPs) as tracked by the [PrimePages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PrimePages) and by Henri & Renaud Lifchitz's PRP Records. Numbers with more than 2,000,000 digits are shown.

:::

## Largest known primes {#Largest_known_primes}

These numbers have been proved prime by computer with a [primality test](wikipedia://en/183751) for their form, for example the [Lucas–Lehmer primality test](wikipedia://en/325739) for [Mersenne numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_number). “!” is the [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606), “#” is the [primorial](wikipedia://en/422786), and $\Phi_3(x)$ is the third [cyclotomic polynomial](wikipedia://en/171992), defined as $x^2 + x + 1$.

## Largest known probable primes (PRPs) {#Largest_known_probable_primes_(PRPs)}

These are [probable primes](wikipedia://en/160560). Primality has not been proven because it is too hard for general numbers of this size but they are expected to be primes. F(*n*) is the *n*th [Fibonacci number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number).
