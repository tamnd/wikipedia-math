# Drinfeld upper half plane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Drinfeld upper half plane"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Drinfeld upper half plane** is a [rigid analytic space](wikipedia://en/5828941) analogous to the usual [upper half plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_half_plane) for function fields, introduced by (txt). It is defined to be the set difference **P**<sup>1</sup>(**C**) \\ **P**<sup>1</sup>(**F**<sub>∞</sub>), where **F** is a function field of a curve over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), **F**<sub>∞</sub> its completion at ∞, and **C** the completion of the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of **F**<sub>∞</sub>.

:::

The analogy with the usual upper half plane arises from the fact that the [global function field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_function_field) **F** is analogous to the rational numbers **Q**. Then, **F**<sub>∞</sub> is the real numbers **R** and the algebraic closure of **F**<sub>∞</sub> is the complex numbers **C** (which are already complete). Finally, **P**<sup>1</sup>(**C**) is the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799), so **P**<sup>1</sup>(**C**) \\ **P**<sup>1</sup>(**R**) is the upper half plane *together with* the lower half plane.
