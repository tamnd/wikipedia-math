# Categorical trace

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Categorical trace"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **categorical trace** is a generalization of the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The trace is defined in the context of a [symmetric monoidal category](wikipedia://en/3865249) *C*, i.e., a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) equipped with a suitable notion of a product $\otimes$. (The notation reflects that the product is, in many cases, a kind of a [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844).) An [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) *X* in such a category *C* is called [dualizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualizable_object) if there is another object $X^\vee$ playing the role of a dual object of *X*. In this situation, the trace of a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) $f: X \to X$ is defined as the composition of the following morphisms: $\mathrm{tr}(f) : 1 \ \stackrel{coev}{\longrightarrow}\ X \otimes X^\vee \ \stackrel{f \otimes \operatorname{id}}{\longrightarrow}\ X \otimes X^\vee \ \stackrel{twist}{\longrightarrow}\ X^\vee \otimes X \ \stackrel{eval}{\longrightarrow}\ 1$ where 1 is the monoidal unit and the extremal morphisms are the coevaluation and evaluation, which are part of the definition of dualizable objects.[^o4]

The same definition applies, to great effect, also when *C* is a symmetric monoidal ∞-category.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If *C* is the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces) over a fixed [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *k*, the dualizable objects are precisely the [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), and the trace in the sense above is the morphism

$$k \to k$$

which is the multiplication by the trace of the [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) *f* in the usual sense of [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422).

- More generally, in the [category of modules](wikipedia://en/11231812) over a ring *R*, the dualizable objects are the [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488). The dual of such a module *M* is $M^*=\operatorname{Hom}(M,R)$, and the evaluation map $M^*\otimes_RM\to R$, $\phi\otimes x\mapsto\phi(x)$ (extended linearly), allows the identification $M^*\otimes_RM=\operatorname{End}_R(M)$, under which the trace of an endomorphism is, again, given by multiplication with the [trace](wikipedia://en/2993836#Trace), the value of the map $M^*\otimes_RM\to R$ above.[^o7] Similarly, one can define a trace for endomorphisms of locally free sheaves of finite rank on a [ringed space](wikipedia://en/248014), see Sheaf of modules.
- If *C* is the [∞-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∞-category) of [chain complexes](wikipedia://en/138404) of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) (over a fixed [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R*), dualizable objects *V* in *C* are precisely the [perfect complexes](wikipedia://en/46630707). The trace in this setting captures, for example, the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420), which is the alternating sum of the ranks of its terms:

$\mathrm{tr}(\operatorname{id}_V) = \sum_i (-1)^i \operatorname {rank} V_i.$[^o8]

:::

## Further applications {#Further_applications}

(Kondyrev Prikhodko 2018) have used categorical trace methods to [prove](wikipedia://en/82285) an [algebro-geometric](wikipedia://en/1997) version of the [Atiyah–Bott fixed point formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiyah–Bott_fixed_point_formula), an extension of the [Lefschetz fixed point formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefschetz_fixed_point_formula).
