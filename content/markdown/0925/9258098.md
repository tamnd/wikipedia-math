# Glen Van Brummelen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glen Van Brummelen"}

**Glen Robert Van Brummelen** (born May 20, 1965) is a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians) historian of mathematics specializing in the [history of trigonometry](wikipedia://en/6785051) and historical applications of mathematics to [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy).

:::

He is president of the [Canadian Society for History and Philosophy of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1806503),[^o3] and was a co-editor of *Mathematics and the Historian's Craft: The Kenneth O. May Lectures* (Springer, 2005).

He has been involved in the summer program [Mathpath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathpath) since 2004 and is the creator of "Glensheep" and their evolution. [^o4]

## Life {#Life}

Van Brummelen earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree from [Simon Fraser University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fraser_University) in 1993, and served as a professor of mathematics at [Bennington College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennington_College) from 1999 to 2006. He then transferred to [Quest University Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quest_University_Canada) as a founding faculty member. In 2020, he became the dean of the Faculty of Natural and Applied Sciences at [Trinity Western University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Western_University) in [Langley, BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langley,_British_Columbia_%28district_municipality%29).[^o7]

Glen Van Brummelen has published the first major history in English of the origins and early development of trigonometry, *The Mathematics of the Heavens and the Earth: The Early History of Trigonometry*.[^o8] His second book, *Heavenly Mathematics: The Forgotten Art of Spherical Trigonometry*, concerns [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405).[^o9][^o10]

In 2016 he received a [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics).[^o11]

As of 2026 he is on sabbatical[^o12] as an Erskine Fellow at the [University of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Canterbury) in Christchurch, New Zealand.[^o13] He was previously awarded the [Erskine Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erskine_Fellowship) in 2010, 2017 and 2019.[^o12]
