# Univariate distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Univariate distribution"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **univariate distribution** is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of only one [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685).[^o2] This is in contrast to a [multivariate distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_distribution), the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of a [random vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_vector) (consisting of multiple random variables). Univariate distributions can be characterized by their [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725) for discrete variables, [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) for continuous variables, or their [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793), which applies to all types of random variables.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

A univariate distribution can be described by different functions depending on whether the [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) is discrete or continuous.

- The [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725) gives the probability that a [discrete random variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_random_variable) is equal to a specific value.[^o5]
- For a [continuous random variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_random_variable), the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) represents the density of probability. Its integral over a range gives the probability that the variable will fall within that range.[^o6]
- The [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) is defined for any kind of random variable (discrete, continuous, and mixed) and gives the probability that the variable *X* will take a value less than or equal to *x*.[^o7]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Uniform\_Distribution\_PDF\_SVG.svg.* [Continuous uniform distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29)

One of the simplest examples of a [discrete univariate distribution](wikipedia://en/23543#Discrete_probability_distribution) is the [discrete uniform distribution](wikipedia://en/1699214), where all elements of a finite set are equally likely. It is the probability model for the outcomes of tossing a fair coin, rolling a fair die, etc. The univariate [continuous uniform distribution](wikipedia://en/1699223) on an interval \[*a*, *b*\] has the property that all sub-intervals of the same length are equally likely.

*Image: Binomial Distribution.svg.* Binomial distribution with normal approximation for *n* = 6 and *p* = 0.5

Other examples of discrete univariate distributions include the [binomial](wikipedia://en/3876), [geometric](wikipedia://en/45922), [negative binomial](wikipedia://en/45177), and [Poisson distributions](wikipedia://en/23009144).[^o10] At least 750 univariate discrete distributions have been reported in the literature.[^o11]

Examples of commonly applied [continuous univariate distributions](wikipedia://en/23543#Continuous_probability_distribution)[^o12] include the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462), [Student's t distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student's_t_distribution), [chisquare distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chisquare_distribution), [F distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F_distribution), [exponential](wikipedia://en/45906) and [gamma](wikipedia://en/207079) distributions.
