# James Hann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Hann"}

**James Hann** (1799–1856) was an English mathematician, teacher and textbook writer.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Hann was born at [Washington, County Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_County_Durham), where his father was a colliery smith. After working as a fireman at a pumping-station at [Hebburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebburn), he was for several years on one of the steamers used on the [Tyne River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyne_River) for towing vessels. He studied mathematics, in particular the works of [William Emerson](wikipedia://en/172568) the fluxionist.[^o3]

Hann then became a teacher, and kept a school at Friar's Goose, near [Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne). An acquaintanceship with [Wesley S. B. Woolhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_S._B._Woolhouse) the mathematician led to Hann's obtaining a situation as calculator in the [Nautical Almanac Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Nautical_Almanac_Office). A few years later he was appointed writing-master, and then shortly mathematical master at [King's College School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_School), London; this post he held till his death. Among his pupils was [Henry Fawcett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Fawcett).[^o3]

Hann was elected a member of the [Institution of Civil Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institution_of_Civil_Engineers) in 1843, and was an honorary member of the [Philosophical Society of Newcastle-on-Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_Society_of_Newcastle-on-Tyne). He died in King's College Hospital 17 August 1856, aged 57 years.[^o3]

## Works {#Works}

In applied mathematics Hann wrote:[^o3]

- *Mathematics for Practical Men*, published 1833, with [Isaac Dodds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Dodds_and_Son) of Gateshead, a friend and owner of one of the steamers on which Hann worked;[^o5]
- *The Theory of Bridges*, 1843, with [William Hosking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hosking);[^o5]
- *Treatise on the Steam Engine, with Practical Rules*, 1847, for [John Weale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Weale_%28publisher%29);[^o5]
- *Principles and Practice of the Machinery of Locomotive Engines*, 1850, for Weale.[^o5]

In 1841, with [Olinthus Gregory](wikipedia://en/849126), Hann drew up and published *Tables for the Use of Nautical Men*. He also contributed papers to the *Diaries* and other mathematical periodicals.[^o3]

Hann published on mechanics and pure mathematics, works in these areas being for Weale's *Rudimentary Series*:[^o3][^o5]

- *Analytical Geometry* (later reworked by [John Radford Young](wikipedia://en/39667884));
- *Treatise on Plane Trigonometry*;
- *Spherical Trigonometry*;
- *Examples of the Integral Calculus*;
- *Examples of the Differential Calculus*.

## Family {#Family}

Hann married as a young Man, and had several Children.[^o3]
