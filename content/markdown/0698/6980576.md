# M. S. Narasimhan

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="M. S. Narasimhan"}

**Mudumbai Seshachalu Narasimhan** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (7 June 1932 – 15 May 2021) was an Indian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). His focus areas included [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). He was a pioneer in the study of [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) of [holomorphic vector bundles](wikipedia://en/4794668) on [projective varieties](wikipedia://en/320469). His work is considered the foundation for [Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence](wikipedia://en/37856668) that links [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and algebraic geometry of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) over [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742). He was also known for his collaboration with mathematician [C. S. Seshadri](wikipedia://en/5633765), for their proof of the [Narasimhan–Seshadri theorem](wikipedia://en/35098864) which proved the necessary conditions for [stable vector bundles](wikipedia://en/18909129) on a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181).

:::

He was a recipient of the [Padma Bhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bhushan), India's third highest civilian honor, in 1990, and the [Ordre national du Mérite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_national_du_Mérite) from France in 1989. He was an elected Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society), London. He was also the recipient of [Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanti_Swarup_Bhatnagar_Prize_for_Science_and_Technology) in 1975 and was the only Indian to receive the [King Faisal International Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_International_Prize) in the field of science.

## Early life {#Early_life}

Narasimhan was born on 7 June 1932 into a rural family in [Tandarai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tandarai) in present day Tamil Nadu, as the eldest among five children.[^o15][^o4] His family hailed from the [North Arcot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Arcot) district. After his early education in rural part of the country, he joined [Loyola College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyola_College,_Chennai) in Madras for his undergraduate education. Here he studied under Father Charles Racine, a French Jesuit professor, who in turn had studied under the French mathematician and [geometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometer) [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274).[^o5] He joined the [Tata Institute of Fundamental Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Institute_of_Fundamental_Research) (TIFR), [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay), for his graduate studies in 1953. He obtained his Ph.D. from the [University of Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mumbai) in 1960 where his advisor was the mathematician [K. S. Chandrasekharan](wikipedia://en/22907213), who was known for his work on number theory.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Narasimhan started his career in 1960 when he joined the faculty of the [Tata Institute of Fundamental Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Institute_of_Fundamental_Research) (TIFR); he later went on to become an honorary fellow.[^o21][^o7] His areas of focus while at TIFR included studying [partial differential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_operator) and [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137).[^o5] During this time, he visited France under the invitation of [Laurent Schwartz](wikipedia://en/317485) and was exposed to the works of other French mathematicians including [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), [Claude Chevalley](wikipedia://en/366040), [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274), and [Jean Leray](wikipedia://en/245430).[^o5] He contracted [pleurisy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleurisy) during his time in France and was hospitalized. He would later recount the incident as exposing him to the "real France" and further strengthening his [leftist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_politics) sympathies which were already triggered by his interactions with the [Trotskyist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotskyist) Schwartz.[^o5]

During his time in France he also collaborated with Japanese mathematician [Takeshi Kotake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeshi_Kotake) working on the analyticity theorems for determining specific types of [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137) that satisfied [Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities](wikipedia://en/38128). His work with Kotake was known as the [Kotake–Narasimhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotake–Narasimhan) theorem for elliptic operators in the setting of ultradifferentiable functions.[^o5][^o8]

He collaborated with Indian mathematician [C. S. Seshadri](wikipedia://en/5633765) for the ground-breaking [Narasimhan–Seshadri theorem](wikipedia://en/35098864) which has been at the core of algebraic geometry and number theory for over half a century.[^o5][^o9] The theorem derived the relation between the purely algebraic notion of [stable vector bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_bundle) on [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181).[^o9] The theorem made a connection between two areas of modern geometry viz. [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).[^o7] Both Seshadri and Narasimhan were elected [Fellows of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) for their work on this topic. He also collaborated with mathematician [R. R. Simha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._R._Simha) on proving the existence of moduli of general type complex structures on a real [analytic manifold](wikipedia://en/3183798). These measures were called [Simha–Narasimhan measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simha_–_Narasimhan_measures) on [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181).[^o10]

For his work, Narasimhan was considered a pioneer in the study of [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) of [holomorphic vector bundles](wikipedia://en/4794668) on [projective varieties](wikipedia://en/320469).[^o15] His work is considered the foundation for [Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence](wikipedia://en/37856668) that links [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) over [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742).[^o15]

When the National Board of Higher Mathematics was established in India, Narasimhan was the first chairman of the board.[^o5] In 1992, Narasimhan retired from TIFR, and became the head of the research group in Mathematics at the [International Centre for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Theoretical_Physics) in [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste).[^o21][^o7] He had also served as a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) in 1968.[^o11] After retiring from ICTP, he settled in [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore).[^o7]

He was a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellowship_of_the_Royal_Society), London as well as recipient of [French National Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Order_of_Merit_%28France%29) in 1989.[^o12] He was awarded the [Padma Bhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bhushan), India's third highest civilian honor, in 1990.[^o13] He was also the recipient of the [Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanti_Swarup_Bhatnagar_Prize) in 1975, [Third World Academy of Sciences Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWAS_Prize) for Mathematics in 1987, and the [Srinivasa Ramanujan Medal](wikipedia://en/32501943) in 1988.[^o14] He was also the recipient of the [King Faisal International Prize for Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_International_Prize_for_Science) in 2006, an award that he won jointly with mathematician [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305), Imperial College.[^o15][^o13][^o16] As of 2021, he was the only Indian to have won the King Faisal International Prize for Science.[^o17][^o18] Also in 2021, he became a laureate of the [Asian Scientist 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Scientist_100) by the *[Asian Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Scientist)*.[^o19]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Narasimhan was married to [Sakuntala Narasimhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakuntala_Narasimhan), a classical musician, journalist and a consumer rights activist. The couple had a daughter, [Shobhana Narasimhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shobhana_Narasimhan), a scientist and professor at [Jawaharlal Nehru Centre for Advanced Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru_Centre_for_Advanced_Scientific_Research), and a son.[^o7] Narasimhan was interested in [Indian classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_music), contemporary art and painting, as well as [Tamil literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_literature).[^o5]

Narasimhan died on 15 May 2021, in [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore) at the age of 88. He had been undergoing treatment for [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer) for the previous year.[^o21][^o7]
