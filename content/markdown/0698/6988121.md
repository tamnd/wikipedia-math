# Arithmetic and geometric Frobenius

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetic and geometric Frobenius"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the [Frobenius endomorphism](wikipedia://en/1088270) is defined in any [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $R$ that has [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) $p$, where $p$ is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666). Namely, the mapping $\phi:r\mapsto r^p$ is a [ring endomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_endomorphism) of $R$.

:::

The image of $\phi$ is then $R^p$, the [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of $R$ consisting of $p$-th powers. In some important cases, for example [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615), $\phi$ is [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective). Otherwise, $\phi$ is an endomorphism but not a ring automorphism.

The terminology of **geometric Frobenius** arises by applying the [spectrum of a ring](wikipedia://en/37291) construction to $\phi$. This gives a mapping

$$\phi^*:\operatorname{Spec}(R^p)\to\operatorname{Spec}(R)$$

of [affine schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme). Even in cases where $R^p=R$ this is not the identity, unless $R$ is the [prime field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_field).

Mappings created by [fibre product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibre_product) with $\phi^*$, i.e. [base changes](wikipedia://en/1816636), tend in [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory) to be called *geometric Frobenius*. The reason for a careful terminology is that the [Frobenius automorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_automorphism) in [Galois groups](wikipedia://en/12673), or defined by [transport of structure](wikipedia://en/6129269), is often the [inverse mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_mapping) of the geometric Frobenius. As in the case of a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) in which a generator is also the inverse of a generator, there are in many situations two possible definitions of Frobenius, and without a consistent convention some problem of a [minus sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minus_sign) may appear.
