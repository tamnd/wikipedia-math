# Bitangents of a quartic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bitangents of a quartic"}

*Image: TrottCurveBiTangents7.svg.* The Trott curve and seven of its bitangents. The others are symmetric with respect to 90° rotations through the origin.

*Image: TrottCurveBiTangents28.svg.* The Trott curve with all 28 bitangents.

In the theory of algebraic [plane curves](wikipedia://en/971691), a general [quartic plane curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_plane_curve) has 28 [bitangent](wikipedia://en/2605212) lines, lines that are tangent to the curve in two places. These lines exist in the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155), but it is possible to define quartic curves for which all 28 of these lines have [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) as their coordinates and therefore belong to the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410).

:::

An explicit quartic with twenty-eight real bitangents was first given by (txt).[^o3] As Plücker showed, the number of real bitangents of any quartic must be 28, 16, or a number less than 9. Another quartic with 28 real bitangents can be formed by the [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of centers of [ellipses](wikipedia://en/9277) with fixed axis lengths, tangent to two non-parallel lines.[^o4] (Shioda 1995) gave a different construction of a quartic with twenty-eight bitangents, formed by projecting a [cubic surface](wikipedia://en/526126); twenty-seven of the bitangents to Shioda's curve are real while the twenty-eighth is the [line at infinity](wikipedia://en/403142) in the projective plane.

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The **Trott curve**, another curve with 28 real bitangents, is the set of points (*x*,*y*) satisfying the [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) four [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) equation

$$\displaystyle 144(x^4+y^4)-225(x^2+y^2)+350x^2y^2+81=0.$$

These points form a nonsingular quartic curve that has [genus](wikipedia://en/3115807) three and that has twenty-eight real [bitangents](wikipedia://en/2605212).[^o8]

Like the examples of Plücker and of Blum and Guinand, the Trott curve has four separated ovals, the maximum number for a curve of degree four, and hence is an [M-curve](wikipedia://en/5209604). The four ovals can be grouped into six different pairs of ovals; for each pair of ovals there are four bitangents touching both ovals in the pair, two that separate the two ovals, and two that do not. Additionally, each oval bounds a nonconvex region of the plane and has one bitangent spanning the nonconvex portion of its boundary.

:::

## Connections to other structures {#Connections_to_other_structures}

The [dual curve](wikipedia://en/3127321) to a quartic curve has 28 real ordinary double points, dual to the 28 bitangents of the primal curve.

The 28 bitangents of a quartic may also be placed in correspondence with symbols of the form

$$\begin{bmatrix} a & b & c \\ d & e & f \\ \end{bmatrix}$$

where $a, b, c, d, e, f$ are all zero or one and where

$ad + be + cf = 1\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 2).$[^o10]

There are 64 choices for $a, b, c, d, e, f$, but only 28 of these choices produce an odd sum. One may also interpret $a, b, c$ as the [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316) of a point of the [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404) and $d, e, f$ as the coordinates of a line in the same finite projective plane; the condition that the sum is odd is equivalent to requiring that the point and the line do not touch each other, and there are 28 different pairs of a point and a line that do not touch.

The points and lines of the Fano plane that are disjoint from a non-incident point-line pair form a triangle, and the bitangents of a quartic have been considered as being in correspondence with the 28 triangles of the Fano plane.[^o11] The [Levi graph](wikipedia://en/1396888) of the Fano plane is the [Heawood graph](wikipedia://en/1396870), in which the triangles of the Fano plane are represented by 6-cycles. The 28 6-cycles of the Heawood graph in turn correspond to the 28 vertices of the [Coxeter graph](wikipedia://en/5358570).[^o12]

The 28 bitangents of a quartic also correspond to pairs of the 56 lines on a degree-2 [del Pezzo surface](wikipedia://en/732323),[^o11] and to the 28 odd [theta characteristics](wikipedia://en/7193470).

The 27 lines on the cubic and the 28 bitangents on a quartic, together with the 120 tritangent planes of a canonic [sextic curve](wikipedia://en/5918099) of genus 4, form a "[trinity](wikipedia://en/648042#Trinities)" in the sense of [Vladimir Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490), specifically a form of [McKay correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKay_correspondence),[^o13][^o14][^o15] and can be related to many further objects, including E<sub>7</sub> and E<sub>8</sub>, as discussed at *[trinities](wikipedia://en/648042#Trinities).*
