# Harnack's curve theorem

*Image: ECClines-3.svg.* The [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) (smooth degree 3) on the left is an M-curve, as it has the maximum (2) components, while the curve on the right has only 1 component.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harnack's curve theorem"}

In [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **Harnack's curve theorem**, named after [Axel Harnack](wikipedia://en/1925276), gives the possible numbers of [connected components](wikipedia://en/6233) that an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) can have, in terms of the degree of the curve. For any algebraic curve of degree $m$ in the real [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350), the number of components $c$ is bounded by

:::

$$\frac{1-(-1)^m}{2} \le c \le \frac{(m-1)(m-2)}{2}+1.\$$

The maximum number is one more than the maximum [genus](wikipedia://en/3115807) of a curve of degree $m$, attained when the curve is [nonsingular](wikipedia://en/581763). Moreover, any number of components in this range of possible values can be attained.

*Image: Trott bitangents.png.* The [Trott curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trott_curve), shown here with 7 of its [bitangents](wikipedia://en/2605212), is a [quartic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_plane_curve) (degree 4) M-curve, attaining the maximum (4) components for a curve of that degree.

A curve which attains the maximum number of real components is called an **M-curve** (from "maximum") – for example, an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) with two components, such as $y^2=x^3-x,$ or the [Trott curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trott_curve), a [quartic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_plane_curve) with four components, are examples of M-curves.

This theorem formed the background to [Hilbert's sixteenth problem](wikipedia://en/381750).

In a recent development a [Harnack curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harnack_curve) is shown to be a curve whose [amoeba](wikipedia://en/8864768) has area equal to the [Newton polygon](wikipedia://en/742477) of the [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) $P$, which is called the characteristic curve of [dimer models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimer_model), and every Harnack curve is the spectral curve of some dimer model.(Mikhalkin 2001)((Kenyon Okounkov Sheffield 2006))
