# LIBOR market model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="LIBOR market model"}

The **LIBOR market model**, also known as the **BGM Model** (**Brace Gatarek Musiela Model**, in reference to the names of some of the inventors) is a [financial model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_model) of [interest rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate).[^o2] It is used for pricing [interest rate derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate_derivative), especially exotic derivatives like Bermudan swaptions, ratchet caps and floors, target redemption notes, autocaps, zero coupon swaptions, [constant maturity swaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constant_maturity_swap) and spread options, among many others. The quantities that are modeled, rather than the [short rate](wikipedia://en/1219048) or instantaneous forward rates (like in the [Heath–Jarrow–Morton framework](wikipedia://en/2876834)) are a set of [forward rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_rates) (also called forward [LIBORs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIBOR)), which have the advantage of being directly observable in the market, and whose volatilities are naturally linked to traded contracts. Each forward rate is modeled by a [lognormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lognormal_distribution) process under its [forward measure](wikipedia://en/9795255), i.e. a [Black model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_model) leading to a Black formula for [interest rate caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate_cap). This formula is the market standard to quote cap prices in terms of implied volatilities, hence the term "market model". The LIBOR market model may be interpreted as a collection of forward LIBOR dynamics for different forward rates with spanning tenors and maturities, each forward rate being consistent with a Black interest rate caplet formula for its canonical maturity. One can write the different rates' dynamics under a common pricing [measure](wikipedia://en/19873), for example the [forward measure](wikipedia://en/9795255) for a preferred single maturity, and in this case forward rates will not be lognormal under the unique measure in general, leading to the need for numerical methods such as [Monte Carlo simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_simulation) or approximations like the frozen drift assumption.

:::

## Model dynamic {#Model_dynamic}

The LIBOR market models a set of $n$ forward rates $L_{j}$, $j=1,\ldots,n$ as [lognormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lognormal_distribution) processes. Under the respective $T_j$ -Forward measure $Q_{T_{j+1}}$[^o4]

$$dL_j(t) = \mu_j(t) L_j(t) dt + \sigma_j(t) L_j(t) dW^{Q_{T_{j+1}}}(t) .$$

Here we can consider that $\mu_j(t) = 0, \forall t$ (centered process). Here, $L_{j}$ is the forward rate for the period $[T_{j},T_{j+1}]$. For each single forward rate the model corresponds to the Black model.

The novelty is that, in contrast to the [Black model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_model), the LIBOR market model describes the dynamic of a whole family of forward rates under a common measure. The question now is how to switch between the different $T$-Forward measures. By means of the multivariate [Girsanov's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girsanov's_theorem) one can show[^o5][^o6] that

$$dW^{Q_{T_j}}(t) = \begin{cases} dW^{Q_{T_p}}(t) - \sum\limits_{k=j+1}^{p} \frac{\delta L_k(t)}{1 + \delta L_k(t)} {\sigma}_k(t) {\rho}_{jk} dt & j < p \\ dW^{Q_{T_p}}(t) & j = p \\ dW^{Q_{T_p}}(t) + \sum\limits_{k=p+1}^{j} \frac{\delta L_k(t)}{1 + \delta L_k(t)} {\sigma}_k(t) {\rho}_{jk} dt & j > p \end{cases}$$

and

$$dL_j(t) = \begin{cases} L_j(t){\sigma}_j(t)dW^{Q_{T_{p}}}(t) - L_j(t)\sum\limits_{k=j+1}^{p} \frac{\delta L_k(t)}{1 + \delta L_k(t)} {\sigma}_j(t){\sigma}_k(t){\rho}_{jk}dt & j <p\\ L_j(t){\sigma}_j(t)dW^{Q_{T_{p}}}(t) & j = p \\ L_j(t){\sigma}_j(t)dW^{Q_{T_{p}}}(t) + L_j(t)\sum\limits_{k=p+1}^{j} \frac{\delta L_k(t)}{1 + \delta L_k(t)} {\sigma}_j(t){\sigma}_k(t){\rho}_{jk}dt & j > p\\ \end{cases}$$
