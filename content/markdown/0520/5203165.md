# Butterfly curve (algebraic)

*Image: Butterfly curve.png.* The butterfly curve $x^6 + y^6 = x^2$

*Image: Butterlink.png.* The butterfly curve singularity [link](wikipedia://en/1161784)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Butterfly curve"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **algebraic butterfly curve** is a [plane algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) of [degree](wikipedia://en/2776558) six given by the equation

:::

$$x^6 + y^6 = x^2.$$

The butterfly curve has a single [singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity) with [delta invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_invariant) three, which means it is a curve of [genus](wikipedia://en/3115807) seven. The only plane curves of genus seven are singular, since seven is not a [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915), and the minimum degree for such a curve is six.

The butterfly curve has branching number and multiplicity two, and hence the singularity [link](wikipedia://en/1161784) has two components.

The area of the algebraic butterfly curve is given by

$$4 \int_0^1 (x^2 - x^6)^{\frac{1}{6}} dx = \frac{ \Gamma\big(\frac{1}{6}\big) \cdot \Gamma\big(\frac{1}{3}\big)}{3 \sqrt{\pi}} \approx 2.804,$$

where $\Gamma$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316), and the [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of the curve is $s \approx 9.017$.
