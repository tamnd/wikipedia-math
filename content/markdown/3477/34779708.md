# Richard Palais

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Palais"}

**Richard Sheldon Palais** (May 22, 1931 – May 13, 2026) was an American mathematician working in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Palais studied at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he obtained a B.A. in 1952, an M.A. in 1954 and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1956. His PhD thesis, entitled *A Global Formulation of the Lie Theory of Transformation Groups,* was supervised by [Andrew M. Gleason](wikipedia://en/4244871) and [George Mackey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mackey).

Palais was a postdoctoral researcher at [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) from 1956 to 1958 and at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 1958 to 1960. He moved then to [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University), where he worked as assistant professor in 1960–1962, as associate professor in 1962-1965 and as full professor from 1965 until his retirement in 2003. From 2004 he was an [adjunct professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunct_professor) at the [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine).

Palais was awarded a [Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) in 1965.[^o5] In 1970, he was an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065#1970,_Nice) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice).[^o6] From 1965 to 1982 he was an editor for the *[Journal of Differential Geometry](wikipedia://en/25823636)* and from 1966 to 1969 an editor for the *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)*. In 1979 he co-founded the [TeX Users Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeX_Users_Group) and became its first president.[^o7]

In 1980, Palais was elected a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science)[^o8] and in 2012 a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o9] In 2006 he was awarded the first prize, together with Luc Bénard, for the NSF/[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_%28journal%29) Visualization Challenge[^o10] and in 2010 he received a [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award).[^o11]

Palais died on May 13, 2026, at the age of 94.[^o12]

## Research {#Research}

Palais' research interests included [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the theory of [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [differentiable groups](wikipedia://en/17945) of transformations, the geometry of [submanifolds](wikipedia://en/525095), [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560) and non-linear [global analysis](wikipedia://en/42027498). In particular, he is known for the [principle of symmetric criticality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_symmetric_criticality), the [Mostow–Palais theorem](wikipedia://en/34779593), the [Lie–Palais theorem](wikipedia://en/37647580), the [Morse–Palais lemma](wikipedia://en/9610679), and the [Palais–Smale compactness condition](wikipedia://en/4408934). Since the 1990s he worked on the theory of [solitons](wikipedia://en/51654) and [mathematical visualization](wikipedia://en/25948250).

His doctoral students include [Edward Bierstone](wikipedia://en/33946193), [Leslie Lamport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Lamport), [Jill P. Mesirov](wikipedia://en/45477104), [Chuu-lian Terng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuu-lian_Terng), and [Karen Uhlenbeck](wikipedia://en/4384796).[^o4]
