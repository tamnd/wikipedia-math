# Schmidt decomposition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schmidt decomposition"}

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), the **Schmidt decomposition** (named after its originator [Erhard Schmidt](wikipedia://en/672191)) refers to a particular way of expressing a [vector](wikipedia://en/879358) in the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of two [inner product spaces](wikipedia://en/14856). It has numerous applications in [quantum information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_information_theory), for example in [entanglement](wikipedia://en/25336) characterization and in [state purification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purification_of_quantum_state), and [plasticity](wikipedia://en/240123).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

Let $H_1$ and $H_2$ be [Hilbert spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_spaces) of [dimensions](wikipedia://en/8398) *n* and *m* respectively. Assume $n \geq m$. For any vector $w$ in the tensor product $H_1 \otimes H_2$, there exist orthonormal sets $\{ u_1, \ldots, u_m \} \subset H_1$ and $\{ v_1, \ldots, v_m \} \subset H_2$ such that $w= \sum_{i =1} ^m \alpha _i u_i \otimes v_i$, where the scalars $\alpha_i$ are real, non-negative, and unique up to re-ordering.

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

The Schmidt decomposition is essentially a restatement of the [singular value decomposition](wikipedia://en/142207) in a different context. Fix orthonormal bases $\{ e_1, \ldots, e_n \} \subset H_1$ and $\{ f_1, \ldots, f_m \} \subset H_2$. We can identify an elementary tensor $e_i \otimes f_j$ with the matrix $e_i f_j ^\mathsf{T}$, where $f_j ^\mathsf{T}$ is the [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844) of $f_j$. A general element of the tensor product

$$w = \sum _{1 \leq i \leq n, 1 \leq j \leq m} \beta _{ij} e_i \otimes f_j$$

can then be viewed as the *n* × *m* matrix

$$\; M_w = (\beta_{ij}) .$$

By the [singular value decomposition](wikipedia://en/142207), there exist an *n* × *n* unitary *U*, *m* × *m* unitary *V*, and a [positive semidefinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-semidefinite_matrix) diagonal *m* × *m* matrix Σ such that

$$M_w = U \begin{bmatrix} \Sigma \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} V^* .$$

Write $U =\begin{bmatrix} U_1 & U_2 \end{bmatrix}$ where $U_1$ is *n* × *m* and we have

$$\; M_w = U_1 \Sigma V^* .$$

Let $\{ u_1, \ldots, u_m \}$ be the *m* column vectors of $U_1$, $\{ v_1, \ldots, v_m \}$ the column vectors of *$\overline{V}$*, and $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_m$ the diagonal elements of Σ. The previous expression is then

$$M_w = \sum _{k=1} ^m \alpha_k u_k v_k ^\mathsf{T} ,$$

Then

$$w = \sum _{k=1} ^m \alpha_k u_k \otimes v_k ,$$

which proves the claim.

:::

:::

## Some observations {#Some_observations}

Some properties of the Schmidt decomposition are of physical interest.

### Spectrum of reduced states {#Spectrum_of_reduced_states}

Consider a vector $w$ of the tensor product

$$H_1 \otimes H_2$$

in the form of Schmidt decomposition

$$w = \sum_{i =1} ^m \alpha _i u_i \otimes v_i.$$

Form the rank 1 matrix $\rho = w w^*$. Then the [partial trace](wikipedia://en/814148) of $\rho$, with respect to either system *A* or *B*, is a [diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/174080) whose non-zero diagonal elements are $| \alpha_i|^2$. In other words, the Schmidt decomposition shows that the reduced states of $\rho$ on either subsystem have the same spectrum.

::: {#o9 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Schmidt rank"}

### Schmidt rank and entanglement {#Schmidt_rank_and_entanglement}

The strictly positive values *$\alpha_i$* in the Schmidt decomposition of $w$ are its **Schmidt coefficients**, or **Schmidt numbers**. The total number of Schmidt coefficients of $w$, counted with multiplicity, is called its **Schmidt rank**.

If $w$ can be expressed as a product

$$u \otimes v$$

then $w$ is called a [separable state](wikipedia://en/2833933). Otherwise, $w$ is said to be an [entangled state](wikipedia://en/25336). From the Schmidt decomposition, we can see that $w$ is entangled if and only if $w$ has Schmidt rank strictly greater than 1. Therefore, two subsystems that partition a [pure state](wikipedia://en/30876419) are entangled if and only if their reduced states are mixed states.

:::

### Von Neumann entropy {#Von_Neumann_entropy}

A consequence of the above comments is that, for pure states, the [von Neumann entropy](wikipedia://en/2515425) of the reduced states is a well-defined measure of [entanglement](wikipedia://en/25336). For the von Neumann entropy of both reduced states of $\rho$ is $-\sum_i |\alpha_i|^2 \log\left(|\alpha_i|^2\right)$, and this is zero if and only if $\rho$ is a product state (not entangled).

## Schmidt-rank vector {#Schmidt-rank_vector}

The Schmidt rank is defined for bipartite systems, namely quantum states

$$|\psi\rangle \in H_A \otimes H_B$$

The concept of Schmidt rank can be extended to quantum systems made up of more than two subsystems.[^o12]

Consider the tripartite quantum system:

$$|\psi\rangle \in H_A \otimes H_B \otimes H_C$$

There are three ways to reduce this to a bipartite system by performing the [partial trace](wikipedia://en/814148) with respect to $H_A, H_B$ or $H_C$

$$\begin{cases} \hat{\rho}_A = Tr_A(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\\ \hat{\rho}_B = Tr_B(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\\ \hat{\rho}_C = Tr_C(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|) \end{cases}$$

Each of the systems obtained is a bipartite system and therefore can be characterized by one number (its Schmidt rank), respectively $r_A, r_B$ and $r_C$. These numbers capture the "amount of entanglement" in the bipartite system when respectively A, B or C are discarded. For these reasons the tripartite system can be described by a vector, namely the Schmidt-rank vector

$$\vec{r} = (r_A, r_B, r_C)$$

### [Multipartite systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multipartite_entanglement) {#Multipartite_systems}

The concept of Schmidt-rank vector can be likewise extended to systems made up of more than three subsystems through the use of [tensors](wikipedia://en/29965).

::: {#o15 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

Source:[^o16]

Take the tripartite quantum state $|\psi_{4, 2, 2}\rangle = \frac{1}{2}\big(|0, 0, 0\rangle + |1, 0, 1\rangle + |2, 1, 0\rangle + |3, 1, 1\rangle \big)$

This kind of system is made possible by encoding the value of a [qudit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qudit) into the [orbital angular momentum (OAM)](wikipedia://en/33190076) of a photon rather than its [spin](wikipedia://en/19593829), since the latter can only take two values.

The Schmidt-rank vector for this quantum state is $(4, 2, 2)$.

:::
