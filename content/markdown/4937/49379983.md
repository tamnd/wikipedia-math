# Ben J. Winer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ben J. Winer"}

**Ben James Winer** (June 18, 1917 – May 30, 1984) was an American research psychologist and academic. He served as a psychology professor at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) and was president of the [Psychometric Society](wikipedia://en/20788500). He has been listed as one of the most highly cited psychologists in the United States, having authored a well-known textbook on statistical analysis.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in Oregon, Winer attended the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon) and he served as a personnel research bureau assistant for the psychology department head, Howard Taylor.[^o4] He earned a master's degree in psychology from Oregon in 1940 before serving in the military for five years. He held positions with the [United States Civil Service Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Civil_Service_Commission) and [The Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) while he took evening graduate courses in statistics at [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University).[^o5]

After briefly attending graduate school at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), Winer went to [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University), where he earned a Ph.D. in [industrial psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_and_organizational_psychology) in 1951.[^o5] He was a postdoctoral fellow at the [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina) and he received joint teaching appointments in statistics and psychology at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) in 1954. He had a full-time appointment in the psychology department by 1956, but statistical analysis remained a strong area of emphasis for him.[^o5] Working with Andrew Halpin, Winer studied behavioral approaches to leadership. They concluded that two concepts, *initiating structure* and *consideration*, characterized the behavior of successful leadership.[^o6]

Winer was the 1967-68 president of the [Psychometric Society](wikipedia://en/20788500).[^o7] In 1983, he received a Quantitative Methods Teaching Award from the [American Psychological Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association). He authored an influential textbook, *Statistical Principles in Experimental Design*.[^o8] The book was reviewed in journals including *[Educational and Psychological Measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_and_Psychological_Measurement)*,[^o9][^o10] *Ergonomics*[^o11] and the *[Journal of the American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/16179313)*.[^o12] In 2002, Winer was ranked fourth on a list of American psychologists most frequently cited in the professional literature;[^o13] *Statistical Principles in Experimental Design* remains his most cited work by far.[^o14]

Upon his death, Winer left a gift to Purdue to further the study of [mathematical psychology](wikipedia://en/2081516). The university established a memorial lecture series in his honor.[^o15] A distinguished professorship at the school was named after him in 1998.[^o16]
