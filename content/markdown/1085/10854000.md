# Statistical study of energy data

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Statistical study of energy data"}

**Energy statistics** refers to collecting, compiling, analyzing and disseminating data on commodities such as [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [crude oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crude_oil), [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas), [electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity), or renewable energy sources ([biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass), [geothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power), [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power) or [solar energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power)), when they are used for the energy they contain. [Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) is the capability of some substances, resulting from their physico-chemical properties, to do [work](wikipedia://en/3616613) or produce [heat](wikipedia://en/19593167). Some energy commodities, called [fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuels), release their [energy content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_content) as heat when they [burn](wikipedia://en/5638). This heat could be used to run an [internal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine) or [external combustion engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_combustion_engine).

:::

The need to have [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) on energy commodities became obvious during the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis) that brought tenfold increase in [petroleum prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_prices). Before the crisis, to have accurate data on global energy supply and demand was not deemed critical. Another concern of energy statistics today is a huge gap in energy use between [developed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) and [developing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country) countries. As the gap narrows (*see picture*), the pressure on energy supply increases tremendously.

*Image: Global energy consumption.jpg.* Global energy consumption per capita, 1950-2004

The data on energy and electricity come from three principal sources:

- [Energy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_industry)
- Other industries ("self-producers")
- Consumers

The flows of and trade in energy commodities are measured both in physical units (e.g., [metric tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_ton)), and, when [energy balances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_accounting) are calculated, in energy units (e.g., [terajoules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terajoule) or [tons of oil equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ton_of_oil_equivalent)). What makes energy statistics specific and different from other fields of [economic statistics](wikipedia://en/340363) is the fact that energy commodities undergo greater number of transformations (flows) than other commodities. In these transformations energy is conserved, as defined by and within the limitations of the [first](wikipedia://en/166404) and [second](wikipedia://en/133017) laws of thermodynamics.
