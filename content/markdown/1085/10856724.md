# Jacob Cohen (statistician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacob Cohen"}

**Jacob Cohen** (April 20, 1923 – January 20, 1998)[^o5] was an American [psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychologist) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) best known for his work on [statistical power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_power) and [effect size](wikipedia://en/437276), which helped to lay foundations for current statistical [meta-analysis](wikipedia://en/62329)[^o10][^o4][^o5] and the methods of [estimation statistics](wikipedia://en/40703875). He gave his name to such measures as [Cohen's kappa](wikipedia://en/1701650), [Cohen's *d*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen's_d), and [Cohen's *h*](wikipedia://en/47072912).[^o6][^o7]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Cohen grew up in a Yiddish-speaking household and learned English at school. He attended the [Townsend Harris High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townsend_Harris_High_School) and graduated at the age of 15. He entered the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York), where he first studied mathematics but was interrupted by a service in the [US Army intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army_intelligence) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) in Europe.[^o3] He eventually received a BA in psychology in 1947 from the City College.[^o10] He then obtained his Master's degree in 1948 and PhD in clinical psychology at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) (NYU) in 1950. His PhD advisor was Avrum Ben-Avi.[^o9] Cohen worked in the hospitals affiliated with the [Veterans Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_Administration) after graduation, first at Bronx, then at Montrose VA Medical Center, where he was a director of research.[^o3] Between 1959 and retirement in 1993, he worked in the psychology department at NYU, latterly as the head of the [quantitative psychology](wikipedia://en/3067624) group.[^o10] In 1969 Cohen married Patricia Ruth Childs (1936-2018), later Patricia Cohen, who was a former PhD student in psychology at NYU.[^o11]

After retirement from NYU, Cohen continued working as a statistical consultant at the [Columbia Presbyterian Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Presbyterian_Medical_Center), [New York State Psychiatric Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Psychiatric_Institute), and at their joint H.I.V. Center for Clinical and Behavioral Studies. Cohen passed away in 1998 at the [St. Vincent's Hospital and Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Vincent's_Hospital_and_Medical_Center) in New York City.[^o10]

Cohen was president of the [Society of Multivariate Experimental Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Multivariate_Experimental_Psychology) in 1969. He was awarded the Distinguished Lifetime Achievement Award by the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association) in 1997 and was a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), the American Psychological Association and the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988).[^o10]

## Power analysis and significance testing {#Power_analysis_and_significance_testing}

In addition to being an advocate of power analysis and effect size, Cohen was a critic of reliance on, and lack of understanding of, [significance testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Significance_testing) procedures used in statistics, especially misunderstandings of [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) significance testing.[^o13] In particular, he identified the "near universal misinterpretation of p as the probability that H<sub>0</sub> is false, the misinterpretation that its complement is the probability of successful replication, and the mistaken assumption that if one rejects H<sub>0</sub> one thereby affirms the theory that led to the test".[^o14] He encouraged instead a recognition of single studies as exploratory and a reliance on [replication](wikipedia://en/10306262) for support. Cohen developed known interpretation thresholds for the correlation *r* and the standardized mean difference *d*.[^o15]
