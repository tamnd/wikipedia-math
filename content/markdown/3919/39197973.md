# Perfectoid space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perfectoid space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **perfectoid spaces** are [adic spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adic_space) of special kind, which occur in the study of problems of "[mixed characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_characteristic)", such as [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero which have [residue fields](wikipedia://en/10789680) of characteristic [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*.

:::

A **perfectoid field** is a complete [topological field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_field) *K* whose [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) is induced by a nondiscrete [valuation](wikipedia://en/347049) of rank 1, such that the [Frobenius endomorphism](wikipedia://en/1088270) Φ is [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) on *K*°/*p* where *K*° denotes the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of [power-bounded elements](wikipedia://en/79549898).

Perfectoid spaces may be used to (and were invented in order to) compare mixed characteristic situations with purely finite characteristic ones. Technical tools for making this precise are the tilting equivalence and the almost purity theorem. The notions were introduced in 2012 by [Peter Scholze](wikipedia://en/39409306).[^o2]

## Tilting equivalence {#Tilting_equivalence}

For any perfectoid field *K* there is a **tilt** *K*<sup>♭</sup>, which is a perfectoid field of finite characteristic *p*. As a [set](wikipedia://en/26691), it may be defined as

$$K^\flat = \varprojlim_{x \mapsto x^p} K.$$

Explicitly, an element of *K*<sup>♭</sup> is an infinite [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) (*x*<sub>0</sub>, *x*<sub>1</sub>, *x*<sub>2</sub>, ...) of elements of *K* such that *x*<sub>*i*</sub> = *x* *p* . The multiplication in *K*<sup>♭</sup> is defined termwise, while the addition is more complicated. If *K* has finite characteristic, then *K* ≅ *K*<sup>♭</sup>. If *K* is the *p*-adic [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28algebra%29) of $\mathbb{Q}_p(p^{1/p^\infty})$, then *K*<sup>♭</sup> is the *t*-adic completion of $\mathbb{F}_p((t))(t^{1/p^\infty})$.

There are notions of **perfectoid algebras** and **perfectoid spaces** over a perfectoid field *K*, roughly analogous to [commutative algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29) and [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603). The tilting operation extends to these objects. If *X* is a perfectoid space over a perfectoid field *K*, then one may form a perfectoid space *X*<sup>♭</sup> over *K*<sup>♭</sup>. The **tilting equivalence** is a theorem that the tilting [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) (-)<sup>♭</sup> induces an [equivalence of categories](wikipedia://en/379051) between perfectoid spaces over *K* and perfectoid spaces over *K*<sup>♭</sup>. Note that while a perfectoid field of finite characteristic may have several non-[isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) "untilts", the categories of perfectoid spaces over them would all be equivalent.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Almost purity theorem"}

### Almost purity theorem {#Almost_purity_theorem}

This equivalence of categories respects some additional properties of morphisms. Many properties of [morphisms of schemes](wikipedia://en/7482797) have analogues for morphisms of adic spaces. The **almost purity theorem** for perfectoid spaces is concerned with [finite](wikipedia://en/2139357) [étale morphisms](wikipedia://en/3022771). It's a generalization of [Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226)'s almost purity theorem in [*p*-adic Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/26086853). The name is alluding to [almost mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_mathematics), which is used in a proof, and a distantly related classical theorem on [purity of the branch locus](wikipedia://en/25534996).[^o6]

The statement has two parts. Let *K* be a perfectoid field.

- If *X* → *Y* is a finite étale morphism of adic spaces over *K* and *Y* is perfectoid, then *X* also is perfectoid;
- A morphism *X* → *Y* of perfectoid spaces over *K* is finite étale [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the tilt *X*<sup>♭</sup> → *Y*<sup>♭</sup> is finite étale over *K*<sup>♭</sup>.

Since finite étale maps into a field are exactly finite [separable](wikipedia://en/363340) [field extensions](wikipedia://en/11634), the almost purity theorem implies that for any perfectoid field *K* the [absolute Galois groups](wikipedia://en/2996781) of *K* and *K*<sup>♭</sup> are isomorphic.

:::
