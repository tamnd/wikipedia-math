# G2-structure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="G2-structure"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **$G_2$-structure** is an important type of [G-structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-structure) that can be defined on a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold). If *M* is a smooth manifold of dimension seven, then a G<sub>2</sub>-structure is a reduction of structure group of the [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550) of *M* to the [compact](wikipedia://en/750326), exceptional [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) [G<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/292864).

:::

## Equivalent conditions {#Equivalent_conditions}

The existence of a $G_2$ structure on a 7-manifold $M$ is equivalent to either of the following conditions:

- The first and second [Stiefel–Whitney classes](wikipedia://en/877761) of *M* vanish.
- *M* is [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable) and admits a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303).

It follows that the existence of a $G_2$-structure is much weaker than the existence of a metric of holonomy $G_2$, because a compact 7-manifold of holonomy $G_2$ must also have finite fundamental group and non-vanishing first [Pontrjagin class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontrjagin_class).

## History {#History}

The fact that there might be certain Riemannian 7-manifolds manifolds of holonomy $G_2$ was first suggested by [Marcel Berger](wikipedia://en/12149363)'s 1955 classification of possible Riemannian holonomy groups. Although still working in a complete absence of examples, [Edmond Bonan](wikipedia://en/39597334) then forged ahead in 1966, and investigated the properties that a manifold of holonomy $G_2$ would necessarily have; in particular, he showed that such a manifold would carry a parallel 3-form and a parallel 4-form, and that the manifold would necessarily be Ricci-flat.[^o4] However, it remained unclear whether such metrics actually existed until [Robert Bryant](wikipedia://en/24204031) proved a local existence theorem for such metrics in 1984. The first complete (although non-compact) 7-manifolds with holonomy $G_2$ were constructed by Bryant and Simon Salamon in 1989.[^o5] The first compact 7-manifolds with holonomy $G_2$ were constructed by [Dominic Joyce](wikipedia://en/1120202) in 1994, and compact $G_2$ manifolds are sometimes known as "Joyce manifolds", especially in the physics literature.[^o6] In 2013, it was shown by M. Firat Arikan, Hyunjoo Cho, and Sema Salur that any manifold with a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303), and, hence, a $G_2$-structure, admits a compatible almost contact metric structure, and an explicit compatible almost contact structure was constructed for manifolds with $G_2$-structure.[^o7] In the same paper, it was shown that certain classes of [$G_2$-manifolds](wikipedia://en/648062) admit a [contact structure](wikipedia://en/476398#Contact_forms_and_structures).

## Remarks {#Remarks}

The property of being a [$G_2$-manifold](wikipedia://en/648062) is much stronger than that of admitting a $G_2$-structure. Indeed, being a $G_2$-manifold is equivalent to admitting a $G_2$-structure that is [torsion-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-structure#Torsion_of_a_G-structure).

The letter "G" occurring in the phrases "G-structure" and "$G_2$-structure" refers to different things. In the first case, G-structures take their name from the fact that arbitrary Lie groups are typically denoted with the letter "G". On the other hand, the letter "G" in "$G_2$" comes from the fact that its Lie algebra is the seventh type ("G" being the seventh letter of the alphabet) in the classification of complex simple Lie algebras by [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274).
