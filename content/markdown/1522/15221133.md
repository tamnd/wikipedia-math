# Euler force

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Euler force"}

In [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586), the **Euler force** is the [fictitious](wikipedia://en/1082841) [tangential force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangential_force)[^o2] that appears when a non-uniformly [rotating reference frame](wikipedia://en/1209823) is used for analysis of motion and there is variation in the [angular velocity](wikipedia://en/65927) of the [reference frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_of_reference)'s axes. The **Euler acceleration** (named for [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902)), also known as **azimuthal acceleration**[^o3] or **transverse acceleration**,[^o4] is that part of the absolute acceleration that is caused by the variation in the [angular velocity](wikipedia://en/65927) of the [reference frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_of_reference).[^o5]

:::

## Intuitive example {#Intuitive_example}

The Euler force will be felt by a person riding a [merry-go-round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merry-go-round). As the ride starts, the Euler force will be the apparent force pushing the person to the back of the horse; and as the ride comes to a stop, it will be the apparent force pushing the person towards the front of the horse. A person on a horse close to the perimeter of the merry-go-round will perceive a greater apparent force than a person on a horse closer to the axis of rotation. Of the three [fictitious forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fictitious_forces) that appear in a rotating reference frame, only the Euler force results from rotation speeding up or slowing down.

## Mathematical description {#Mathematical_description}

The direction and magnitude of the Euler acceleration is given, in the rotating reference frame, by:

$$\mathbf{a}_\mathrm{Euler} = - \frac{d\boldsymbol\omega}{dt} \times \mathbf{r},$$

where **ω** is the angular velocity of rotation of the reference frame and **r** is the vector position of the point in the reference frame. The Euler force on an object of mass *m* in the rotating reference frame is then

$$\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{Euler} = m \mathbf{a}_\mathrm{Euler} = - m \frac{d\boldsymbol\omega}{dt} \times \mathbf{r}.$$
