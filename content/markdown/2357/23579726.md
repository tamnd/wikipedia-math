# Imre Simon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Imre Simon"}

**Imre Simon** (August 14, 1943 – August 13, 2009) was a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary)-born [Brazilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist).

:::

His research mainly focused on theoretical computer science, [automata theory](wikipedia://en/103356), and [tropical mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_mathematics), a subject he founded, and which was so named because he lived in Brazil.[^o3][^o4] He was a professor of mathematics at the [University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_São_Paulo) in Brazil. In 2005, the journal RAIRO-ITA published an issue dedicated to his life and work.[^o4] He was also actively interested in questions about the impact of computing and information networks on human collaboration and the production of intellectual commons, and was an enthusiastic advocate for open collaborative information systems, of which [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia) is an example.[^o5]

## Biography {#Biography}

Simon came to Brazil with his parents after the [Hungarian Revolution of 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956). He studied electrical engineering at the University of São Paulo, received his diploma in 1966, and his Ph.D. at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) in 1972, under [Janusz Brzozowski](wikipedia://en/23616030) with the thesis: *Hierarchies of Events with Dot-Depth One*.[^o7]

He died of lung cancer in [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/São_Paulo), [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil) on August 13, 2009, one day before his 66th birthday.[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

Simon was a Full Professor of the Instituto de Matemática e Estatística's Department of Computer Science and received the [Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) title, post-mortem.[^o9]
