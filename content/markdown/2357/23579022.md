# Artur Avila

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Artur Avila"}

Avila

**Artur Avila Cordeiro de Melo** (; born 29 June 1979) is a Brazilian and naturalized French[^o3] mathematician working primarily in the fields of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) and [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713). He is one of the winners of the 2014 [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859),[^o4] being the first Latin American and [lusophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusophone) to win such award. He has been a researcher at both the [IMPA](wikipedia://en/2334708) and the [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) (working a half-year in each one). He has been a professor at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich) since September 2018.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

At the age of 16, Avila won a gold medal at the 1995 [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208)[^o6] and received a scholarship for the [Instituto Nacional de Matemática Pura e Aplicada](wikipedia://en/2334708) (IMPA) to start an M.S. degree while still attending high school in [Colégio de São Bento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colégio_de_São_Bento) and Colégio Santo Agostinho in Rio de Janeiro.[^o7] He completed his M.S. degree in 1997.[^o8] Later he enrolled in the [Federal University of Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_University_of_Rio_de_Janeiro) (UFRJ), earning his B.S in mathematics.[^o9]

At the age of 19, Avila began writing his doctoral thesis on the theory of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087). In 2001 he finished it and received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from IMPA. That same year he moved abroad to France to do postdoctoral research.[^o10] He works with one-dimensional dynamics and [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110).[^o11] Since 2003 he has worked as a researcher for the [Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_National_de_la_Recherche_Scientifique) (CNRS) in France, later becoming a research director in 2008. His post-doctoral supervisor was [Jean-Christophe Yoccoz](wikipedia://en/589307).[^o12]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Much of Artur Avila's work has been in the field of dynamical systems. In March 2005, at age 26, Avila and [Svetlana Jitomirskaya](wikipedia://en/46271961) proved the "[conjecture of the ten martinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_martini_problem)," a problem proposed by the American [mathematical physicist](wikipedia://en/173416#Mathematically_rigorous_physics) [Barry Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Simon).[^o14] [Mark Kac](wikipedia://en/1062425) promised a reward of ten [martinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martini_%28cocktail%29) to whoever solved the problem: whether or not the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137) of a [particular type of operator](wikipedia://en/16628707) is a [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172), given certain conditions on its parameters. The problem had been unsolved for 25 years when Avila and Jitomirskaya answered it affirmatively.[^o15][^o16] Later that year, Avila and [Marcelo Viana](wikipedia://en/44610530) proved the [Zorich–Kontsevich conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorich–Kontsevich_conjecture) that the non-trivial [Lyapunov exponents](wikipedia://en/166441) of the Teichmüller flow on the moduli space of [Abelian differentials](wikipedia://en/1138912) on compact Riemann surfaces are all distinct.[^o17][^o18] Other than that, Avila also did notable research on [dynamical billiards](wikipedia://en/2160429).[^o19]

## Honours and recognition {#Honours_and_recognition}

*Image: Four\_Fields\_medallists\_plus\_epsilon.jpg.* Four Fields medallists left to right (Artur Avila, Martin Hairer (at back), Maryam Mirzakhani, with Maryam's daughter Anahita) and [Manjul Bhargava](wikipedia://en/5102088) at the ICM 2014 in Seoul

Later, as a research mathematician, he received in 2006 a [CNRS Bronze Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS_Bronze_Medal) as well as the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize), and was a [Clay Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Research_Fellow). He became the youngest professorial fellow (*directeur de recherches*) at the [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) in 2008. The same year, he was awarded one of the ten prestigious [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470) prizes, and in 2009 he won the [Grand Prix Jacques Herbrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_Jacques_Herbrand) from the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences#Medals,_awards_and_prizes).[^o34] In 2017 he gave the [Łojasiewicz Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łojasiewicz_Lecture) (on the "One-frequency Schrödinger operators and the almost reducibility conjecture") at the [Jagiellonian University in Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University).[^o21]

He was a [plenary speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2010.[^o22] In 2011, he was awarded the [Michael Brin Prize in Dynamical Systems](wikipedia://en/47522601). He received the Early Career Award from the [International Association of Mathematical Physics](wikipedia://en/23897340) in 2012,[^o23] [TWAS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWAS_Prize) in 2013[^o24] and the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 2014.[^o25]

He was elected a foreign associate of the US [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in April 2019.[^o26]

Avila is a member of [World Minds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World.minds).
