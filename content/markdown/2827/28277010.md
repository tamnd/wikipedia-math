# Poincaré complex

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Poincaré complex"}

In mathematics, and especially [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a **Poincaré complex** (named after the mathematician [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740)) is an abstraction of the [singular chain complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_chain_complex) of a [closed](wikipedia://en/669475), [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable_manifold) [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470).

:::

The singular [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) and [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) groups of a closed, orientable manifold are related by [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732), an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) between its homology and cohomology groups. A [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) is called a Poincaré complex if its homology and cohomology groups have the abstract properties of Poincaré duality.

A [Poincaré space](wikipedia://en/17094227) is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) whose singular chain complex is a Poincaré complex. These are used in [surgery theory](wikipedia://en/5961115) to analyze manifolds algebraically.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $C = \{C_i\}$ be a [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974), and assume that the homology groups of $C$ are [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/99945). Assume that there exists a map $\Delta\colon C\to C\otimes C$, called a chain-diagonal, with the property that $(\varepsilon \otimes 1)\Delta = (1\otimes \varepsilon)\Delta$. Here the map $\varepsilon\colon C_0\to \mathbb{Z}$ denotes the [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) known as the [augmentation map](wikipedia://en/2224774), which is defined as follows: if $n_1\sigma_1 + \cdots + n_k\sigma_k\in C_0$, then $\varepsilon(n_1\sigma_1 + \cdots + n_k\sigma_k) = n_1+ \cdots + n_k\in \mathbb{Z}$.[^o5]

Using the diagonal as defined above, we are able to form pairings, namely:

$$\rho \colon H^k(C)\otimes H_n(C) \to H_{n-k}(C), \ \text{where} \ \ \rho(x\otimes y) = x \frown y,$$

where $\scriptstyle \frown$ denotes the [cap product](wikipedia://en/5230810).[^o6]

A chain complex *C* is called **geometric** if a [chain homotopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_homotopy) exists between $\Delta$ and $\tau\Delta$, where $\tau \colon C\otimes C\to C\otimes C$ is the transposition/flip given by $\tau (a\otimes b) = b\otimes a$.

A geometric chain complex is called an algebraic **Poincaré complex**, of dimension *n*, if there exists an infinite-[ordered](wikipedia://en/493527) element of the *n*-dimensional homology group, say $\mu \in H_n(C)$, such that the maps given by

$$(\frown\mu) \colon H^k(C) \to H_{n-k}(C)$$

are group [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828) for all $0 \le k \le n$. These isomorphisms are the isomorphisms of Poincaré duality.[^o7][^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

- The [singular](wikipedia://en/431041) [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) of an orientable, closed *n*-dimensional manifold $M$ is an example of a Poincaré complex, where the duality isomorphisms are given by capping with the fundamental class $[M] \in H_{n}(M; \mathbb{Z})$.[^o2]

:::
