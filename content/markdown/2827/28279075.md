# Steenrod problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Steenrod problem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and particularly [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory), **Steenrod's Problem** (named after mathematician [Norman Steenrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Steenrod)) is a problem concerning the realisation of [homology classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_class) by singular manifolds.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Let $M$ be a [closed](wikipedia://en/669475), [oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriented_manifold) manifold of dimension $n$, and let $[M] \in H_n(M)$ be its orientation class. Here $H_n(M)$ denotes the integral, $n$-dimensional [homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) of $M$. Any [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) $f\colon M\to X$ defines an induced [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) $f_*\colon H_n(M)\to H_n(X)$.[^o5] A homology class of $H_n(X)$ is called realisable if it is of the form $f_*[M]$ for some manifold $M$ and map $f:M \to X$. The Steenrod problem is concerned with describing the realisable homology classes of $H_n(X)$.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement cue=heading name="Results"}

## Results {#Results}

All elements of $H_k(X)$ are realisable by smooth manifolds provided $k\le 6$. Moreover, any cycle can be realized by the mapping of a [pseudo-manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-manifold).[^o6]

The assumption that *M* be orientable can be relaxed. In the case of non-orientable manifolds, every homology class of $H_n(X,\mathbb{Z}_2)$, where $\mathbb{Z}_2$ denotes the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) 2, can be realized by a non-oriented manifold, $f\colon M^n\to X$.[^o6]

:::

## Conclusions {#Conclusions}

For smooth manifolds *M* the problem reduces to finding the form of the homomorphism $\Omega_n(X) \to H_n(X)$, where $\Omega_n(X)$ is the oriented [bordism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordism) group of *X*.[^o10] The connection between the bordism groups $\Omega_*$ and the [Thom spaces](wikipedia://en/1243451) MSO(*k*) clarified the Steenrod problem by reducing it to the study of the homomorphisms $H_*(\operatorname{MSO}(k)) \to H_*(X)$.[^o6][^o11] In his landmark paper from 1954,[^o11] [René Thom](wikipedia://en/61633) produced an example of a non-realisable class, $[M] \in H_7(X)$, where *M* is the [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) $K(\mathbb{Z}_3\oplus \mathbb{Z}_3,1)$.
