# Canonical ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Canonical ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **pluricanonical ring** of an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *V* (which is [nonsingular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities)), or of a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742), is the [graded ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_commutative_ring)

:::

$$R(V,K)=R(V,K_V) \,$$

of sections of powers of the [canonical bundle](wikipedia://en/734893) *K*. Its *n*th graded component (for $n\geq 0$) is:

$$R_n := H^0(V, K^n),\$$

that is, the space of [sections](wikipedia://en/367621) of the *n*-th [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) *K*<sup>*n*</sup> of the canonical bundle *K*.

The 0th graded component $R_0$ is sections of the trivial bundle, and is one-dimensional as *V* is projective. The projective variety defined by this graded ring is called the **canonical model** of *V*, and the dimension of the canonical model is called the [Kodaira dimension](wikipedia://en/1866873) of *V*.

One can define an analogous ring for any [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) *L* over *V*; the analogous dimension is called the **[Iitaka dimension](wikipedia://en/13024295)**. A line bundle is called **big** if the Iitaka dimension equals the dimension of the variety.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Birational invariance {#Birational_invariance}

The canonical ring and therefore likewise the Kodaira dimension is a [birational invariant](wikipedia://en/2605247): Any birational map between smooth compact complex manifolds induces an isomorphism between the respective canonical rings. As a consequence one can define the Kodaira dimension of a singular space as the Kodaira dimension of a [desingularization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desingularization). Due to the birational invariance this is well defined, i.e., independent of the choice of the desingularization.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Fundamental conjecture of birational geometry"}

### Fundamental conjecture of birational geometry {#Fundamental_conjecture_of_birational_geometry}

A basic conjecture is that the pluricanonical ring is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/11985661). This is considered a major step in the [Mori program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mori_program). (txt) proved this conjecture.

## The plurigenera {#The_plurigenera}

The dimension

$$P_n = h^0(V, K^n) = \operatorname{dim}\ H^0(V, K^n)$$

is the classically defined *n*-th ***plurigenus*** of *V*. The pluricanonical divisor $K^n$, via the corresponding [linear system of divisors](wikipedia://en/1186886), gives a map to projective space $\mathbf{P}(H^0(V, K^n)) = \mathbf{P}^{P_n - 1}$, called the *n*-canonical map.

The size of *R* is a basic invariant of *V*, and is called the Kodaira dimension.

:::
