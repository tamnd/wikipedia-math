# Hans Rademacher

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hans Rademacher"}

**Hans Adolph Rademacher** (; 3 April 1892 – 7 February 1969) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany)-born American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for work in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Rademacher received his Ph.D. in 1916 from [Georg-August-Universität Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg-August-Universität_Göttingen); [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625) supervised his dissertation.[^o4] In 1919, he became *[privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent)* under [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625) at [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin). In 1922, he became an assistant professor at the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg), where he supervised budding mathematicians like [Theodor Estermann](wikipedia://en/4872148).[^o5] He was dismissed from his position at the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau) by the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) in 1933 due to his public support of the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic),[^o6] and emigrated from Europe in 1934.

After leaving Germany, he moved to [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia) and worked at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) until his retirement in 1962; he held the [Thomas A. Scott Professorship of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Scott_Professorship_of_Mathematics) at Pennsylvania from 1956 to 1962. Rademacher had a number of well-known students, including [George Andrews](wikipedia://en/3356273), [Paul T. Bateman](wikipedia://en/26642296), [Theodor Estermann](wikipedia://en/4872148) and [Emil Grosswald](wikipedia://en/21442284).

## Research {#Research}

Rademacher performed research in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), mathematical [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics), the [theory of functions of a real variable](wikipedia://en/26478), and [quantum theory](wikipedia://en/25202). Most notably, he developed the theory of [Dedekind sums](wikipedia://en/1122242). In 1937 Rademacher discovered an exact convergent series for the [partition function](wikipedia://en/312250) P(n), the number of integer partitions of a number, improving upon [Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan)'s asymptotic non-convergent series and validating Ramanujan's supposition that an exact series representation existed.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

With his retirement from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), a group of mathematicians provided the seed funding for The Hans A. Rademacher Instructorships, and honored him with an honorary degree as Doctor of Science.

Rademacher is the co-author (with [Otto Toeplitz](wikipedia://en/547682)) of the popular mathematics book *The Enjoyment of Mathematics*, published in German in 1930 and still in print.[^o9][^o10]
