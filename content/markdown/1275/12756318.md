# Feit–Thompson conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Feit–Thompson conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Feit–Thompson conjecture** is a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), suggested by (). The conjecture states that there are no distinct [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) *p* and *q* such that

:::

$\frac{p^{q} - 1}{p - 1}$ divides $\frac{q^{p} - 1}{q - 1}$.

If the conjecture were true, it would greatly simplify the final chapter of the [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) (Feit Thompson 1963) of the [Feit–Thompson theorem](wikipedia://en/1656473) that every [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) of odd [order](wikipedia://en/493527) is [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012). A stronger conjecture that the two numbers are always [coprime](wikipedia://en/6556) was disproved by (Stephens 1971) with the [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) *p* = 17 and *q* = 3313 with common [factor](wikipedia://en/49492) 2*pq* + 1 = 112643.

It is known that the conjecture is true for *q* = 2 (Stephens 1971) and *q* = 3 (Le 2012).

Informal [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) arguments suggest that the "expected" number of counterexamples to the Feit–Thompson conjecture is very close to 0, suggesting that the Feit–Thompson conjecture is likely to be true.
