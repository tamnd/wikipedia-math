# Freiheitssatz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Freiheitssatz"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Freiheitssatz** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): "freedom/independence theorem": *Freiheit* + *Satz*) is a result in the [presentation theory](wikipedia://en/99494) of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), stating that certain subgroups of a [one-relator group](wikipedia://en/51159172) are [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Consider a group presentation

$$G = \langle x_{1}, \dots, x_{n} | r = 1 \rangle$$

given by $n$ generators $x_{i}$ and a single [cyclically reduced](wikipedia://en/2105607) relator $r$. If $x_{1}$ appears in $r$, then (according to the freiheitssatz) the [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of $G$ generated by $x_{2}, ..., x_{n}$ is a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735), freely generated by $x_{2}, ..., x_{n}$. In other words, the only relations involving $x_{2}, ..., x_{n}$ are the trivial ones.

:::

## History {#History}

The result was proposed by the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Max Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602) and proved by his student, [Wilhelm Magnus](wikipedia://en/8337666), in his doctoral thesis.[^o5] Although Dehn expected Magnus to find a [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) proof,[^o6] Magnus instead found a proof based on [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881)[^o9] and [amalgamated products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalgamated_product) of groups.[^o13] Different induction-based proofs were given later by (Lyndon 1972) and (Weinbaum 1972).[^o9][^o10][^o11]

## Significance {#Significance}

The Freiheitssatz has become "the cornerstone of one-relator group theory", and motivated the development of the theory of [amalgamated products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalgamated_product). It also provides an analogue, in non-commutative group theory, of certain results on [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) and other commutative groups.
