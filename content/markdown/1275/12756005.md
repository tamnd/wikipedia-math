# Euclidean ordered field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Euclidean ordered field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Euclidean field** is an [ordered field](wikipedia://en/22430) $K$ for which every non-negative element is a square: that is, $x \ge 0$ in $K$ implies that $x = y^{2}$ for some $y$ in $K$.

:::

The [constructible numbers](wikipedia://en/7439) form a Euclidean field. It is the smallest Euclidean field, as every Euclidean field contains it as an ordered subfield. In other words, the constructible numbers form the Euclidean closure of the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Every Euclidean field is an ordered [Pythagorean field](wikipedia://en/31032774), but the converse is not true.[^o4]
- If *E*/*F* is a finite [extension](wikipedia://en/11634), and *E* is Euclidean, then so is *F*. This "going-down theorem" is a consequence of the [Diller–Dress theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diller–Dress_theorem).[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The real [constructible numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructible_numbers), those (signed) lengths which can be constructed from a rational segment by [ruler and compass constructions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruler_and_compass_constructions), form a Euclidean field.[^o8]

Every [real closed field](wikipedia://en/1066093) is a Euclidean field. The following examples are also real closed fields.

- The [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\mathbb{R}$ with the usual operations and ordering form a Euclidean field.
- The field of real [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158) $\mathbb{R}\cap\mathbb{\overline Q}$ is a Euclidean field.
- The field of [hyperreal numbers](wikipedia://en/51429) is a Euclidean field.

:::

::: {#o10 .example kind=example/counterexample cue=heading name="Counterexamples"}

## Counterexamples {#Counterexamples}

- The [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) $\mathbb Q$ with the usual operations and ordering do not form a Euclidean field. For example, 2 is not a square in $\mathbb Q$ since the [square root of 2](wikipedia://en/341682) is [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689).[^o11] By the going-down result above, no [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) can be Euclidean.[^o5]
- The [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $\mathbb C$ do not form a Euclidean field since they cannot be given the structure of an ordered field.

:::

::: {#o13 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Euclidean closure"}

## Euclidean closure {#Euclidean_closure}

The **Euclidean closure** of an ordered field $K$ is an extension of $K$ in the [quadratic closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_closure) of $K$ which is maximal with respect to being an ordered field with an order extending that of $K$.[^o14] It is also the smallest subfield of the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of $K$ that is a Euclidean field and is an ordered [extension](wikipedia://en/11634) of $K$.

:::
