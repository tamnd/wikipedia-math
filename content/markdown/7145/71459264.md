# Black hole stability conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Black hole stability conjecture"}

The **black hole stability conjecture** is the conjecture that a perturbation of a [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650) solution of the [Einstein field equations](wikipedia://en/424540) will settle back down to a black hole state. The relevant black hole solutions are described by metrics such as the [Kerr metric](wikipedia://en/456715), which describes isolated rotating black holes, or the [Kerr–Newman–de–Sitter metric](wikipedia://en/74620623), which describes charged rotating black holes in an expanding universe. The mathematical foundation for the conjecture is work by the French mathematician [Yvonne Choquet-Bruhat](wikipedia://en/22081008) from 1952,[^o2][^o3] which showed that the [initial value problem](wikipedia://en/498383) for the Einstein field equations is well-posed. The conjecture then states that small perturbations of the initial conditions of a black hole disperse over time, while the parameters of the black hole (mass, angular momentum and charge) settle down to some new values.

:::

The stability of the Schwarzschild metric under infinitesimal perturbations was first investigated by [Tullio Regge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullio_Regge) and [John Archibald Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Archibald_Wheeler) in 1957.[^o4] and Frank Zerilli, who discovered the [Regge–Wheeler–Zerilli equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regge–Wheeler–Zerilli_equations). Following the discovery of the [Kerr metric](wikipedia://en/456715) by [Roy Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Kerr) in 1963, these investigations were extended to rotating black holes by [Saul Teukolsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Teukolsky), [C. V. Vishveshwara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._V._Vishveshwara), [Robert Wald](wikipedia://en/5635914), [Bernard Whiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Whiting) and others.

The conjecture was proved for Kerr black holes by [Peter Hintz](wikipedia://en/81320042) in 2026.[^o5] This result applies to all subextremal Kerr black holes, which are those that possess an event horizon. In terms of the parameters $M$ (mass) and $a$ (angular momentum) of the Kerr metric, subextremality is the condition $|a|<M$. The [superextremal](wikipedia://en/456715#Overextreme_Kerr_solutions) (sometimes called overextreme) Kerr spacetime, for which $|a|>M$, does not possess an event horizon and does not describe a black hole anymore, but is an example of a [naked singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naked_singularity). A series of papers culminating in 2022 was published by Elena Giorgi, [Sergiu Klainerman](wikipedia://en/25808826) and [Jérémie Szeftel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jérémie_Szeftel) which gave a proof of the conjecture for slowly rotating Kerr black holes, for which $|a|\ll M$.[^o6][^o7][^o8] In 2021, [Mihalis Dafermos](wikipedia://en/23535913), [Gustav Holzegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Holzegel), Martin Taylor and [Igor Rodnianski](wikipedia://en/33944172) proved the stability of the Schwarzschild spacetime for all perturbations that ensure that the black hole that the spacetime settles down to has vanishing angular momentum.[^o9] A stability result for the [Schwarzschild spacetime](wikipedia://en/264606) among solutions with special symmetries was published by Klainerman and Szeftel in 2017.[^o10][^o3]

A 2016 paper by [Peter Hintz](wikipedia://en/81320042) and [András Vasy](wikipedia://en/52321031) proved the stability of slowly rotating Kerr black holes in [de Sitter space](wikipedia://en/301666).[^o11][^o3]
