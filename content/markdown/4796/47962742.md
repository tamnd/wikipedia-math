# Tally marks

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tally marks"}

*Image: Strike symbol (49778996432).jpg.* A "five-barred gate" on a [chalkboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalkboard)

*Image: NYW-Tally.png.* Tally marks incrementing the number of tallies by 1

**Tally marks**, also called **hash marks**, are used as a basic [numeral system](wikipedia://en/21170) for [counting](wikipedia://en/275768); they can be thought of as a [unary numeral system](wikipedia://en/32316).

:::

They are most useful in counting (*tallying*) ongoing results, such as the [score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Score_%28game%29) in a game or sport, as no intermediate results need to be erased or discarded. However, because of the length of large numbers, tallies are not commonly used for static text. Notched sticks, known as [tally sticks](wikipedia://en/30872676), were also historically used for this purpose.

## Early history {#Early_history}

Counting aids other than body parts appear in the [Upper Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic). The oldest [tally sticks](wikipedia://en/30872676) date to between 35,000 and 25,000 years ago, in the form of notched bones found in the context of the [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Stone_Age) [Aurignacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurignacian) to [Gravettian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravettian) and in Africa's [Late Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Stone_Age).

The so-called *[Wolf bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_bone)* is a prehistoric artifact discovered in 1937 in [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) during excavations at [Dolní Věstonice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolní_Věstonice_%28archaeology%29), [Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravia), led by [Karl Absolon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Absolon). Dated to the [Aurignacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurignacian), approximately 30,000 years ago, the bone is marked with 55 marks which may be tally marks. The head of an ivory [Venus figurine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_figurine) was excavated close to the bone.[^o3]

The [Ishango bone](wikipedia://en/5508956), found in the [Ishango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishango) region of the present-day [Democratic Republic of Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Congo), is dated to over 20,000 years old. Upon discovery, it was thought to portray a series of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666). In the book *How Mathematics Happened: The First 50,000 Years*, Peter Rudman argues that the development of the concept of prime numbers could only have come about after the concept of division, which he dates to after [10,000 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10,000_BC), with prime numbers probably not being understood until about 500 BC. He also writes that "no attempt has been made to explain why a tally of something should exhibit multiples of two, [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_numbers) between 10 and 20, and some numbers that are almost multiples of 10."[^o4] [Alexander Marshack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Marshack) examined the Ishango bone microscopically, and concluded that it may represent a six-month [lunar calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_calendar).[^o5]

Artifacts in [Little Salt Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Salt_Spring) demonstrate likely use of a tally system for [calendric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar) purposes about 10,000 years ago. Markings on reindeer antler are shown to be consistent with the theory of their usage to track the [lunar cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_phase).[^o6]

## Clustering {#Clustering}

*Image: Unary8.svg.* Various ways to cluster the number eight (8). The third example shows how the first or fifth mark in each group may be written at an angle to the others for easier distinction. In the fourth example, the fifth stroke "closes out" a group of five, forming a "herringbone". In the fifth example the fifth mark crosses diagonally, forming a "five-bar gate".

Tally marks are typically clustered in groups of five for legibility. The cluster size 5 has the advantages of (a) easy conversion into decimal for higher arithmetic operations and (b) avoiding error, as humans can far more easily [correctly identify](wikipedia://en/734854) a cluster of 5 than one of 10.

## Writing systems {#Writing_systems}

[Roman numerals](wikipedia://en/25657), the [Brahmi](wikipedia://en/1903587) and [Chinese numerals](wikipedia://en/5781) for one through three (一 二 三), and [rod numerals](wikipedia://en/26661047) were derived from tally marks, as possibly was the [ogham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogham) script.[^o12]

[Base 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_1) arithmetic [notation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notation_system) is a [unary](wikipedia://en/32316) [positional system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positional_system) similar to tally marks. It is rarely used as a practical base for [counting](wikipedia://en/275768) due to its difficult readability. Under base 1, the numbers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 ... would be represented as:[^o13]

1, 11, 111, 1111, 11111, 111111 ...

Base 1 notation is widely used in [type numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flour#Type_numbers) of flour; the higher number represents a higher grind.

### Unicode {#Unicode}

In 2015, [Ken Lunde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Lunde) and Daisuke Miura submitted a proposal to encode various systems of tally marks in the [Unicode Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode_Standard).[^o15] However, the box tally and dot-and-dash tally characters were not accepted for encoding, and only the five [ideographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideogram) tally marks (正 scheme) and two Western tally digits were added to the Unicode Standard in the [Counting Rod Numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counting_Rod_Numerals_%28Unicode_block%29) block in Unicode version 11.0 (June 2018). Only the tally marks for the numbers 1 and 5 are encoded, and tally marks for the numbers 2, 3 and 4 are intended to be composed from sequences of tally mark 1 at the font level.
