# Frank Forelli

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Frank Forelli"}

**Frank John Forelli**, Jr. (8 April 1932, [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego) – 5 September 1994, [Madison, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin)) was an American mathematician, specializing in the functional analysis of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110).[^o3]

:::

Forelli received his bachelor's degree from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and then, after 3 years as an officer in the U. S. Navy, returned to Berkeley.[^o3] He received there in 1961 his Ph.D. under [Henry Helson](wikipedia://en/35258187) with thesis *Marcel Riesz's theorem on conjugate functions*. In 1961 Forelli joined the faculty of the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison), where he remained for the remainder of his life.[^o3] The main focus of his research was in the properties of holomorphic functions. In particular, he used [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) methods applied to the boundary values of such functions. His contributions to the field were recognized early in his career by an invitation to give an [invited lecture at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) in 1970.[^o3]

Upon his death, he was survived by his wife and two daughters.[^o3]
