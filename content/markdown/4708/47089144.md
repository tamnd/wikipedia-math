# Regular embedding

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Regular embedding"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a [closed immersion](wikipedia://en/1623986) $i: X \hookrightarrow Y$ of schemes is a **regular embedding** of codimension *r* if each point *x* in *X* has an open affine neighborhood *U* in *Y* such that the ideal of $X \cap U$ is generated by a [regular sequence](wikipedia://en/505119) of length *r*. A regular embedding of codimension one is precisely an [effective Cartier divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_Cartier_divisor).

:::

## Examples and usage {#Examples_and_usage}

For example, if *X* and *Y* are [smooth](wikipedia://en/7837041) over a scheme *S* and if *i* is an *S*-morphism, then *i* is a regular embedding. In particular, every section of a smooth morphism is a regular embedding.[^o3] If $\operatorname{Spec}B$ is regularly embedded into a [regular scheme](wikipedia://en/43016199), then *B* is a [complete intersection ring](wikipedia://en/3116851).[^o4]

The notion is used, for instance, in an essential way in Fulton's approach to [intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602). The important fact is that when *i* is a regular embedding, if *I* is the ideal sheaf of *X* in *Y*, then the [normal sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_sheaf), the dual of $I/I^2$, is locally free (thus a vector bundle) and the natural map $\operatorname{Sym}(I/I^2) \to \oplus_0^\infty I^n/I^{n+1}$ is an isomorphism: the [normal cone](wikipedia://en/4959227) $\operatorname{Spec}(\oplus_0^\infty I^n/I^{n+1})$ coincides with the normal bundle.

::: {#o6 .example kind=example/counterexample cue=heading name="Non-examples"}

### Non-examples {#Non-examples}

One non-example is a scheme which isn't equidimensional. For example, the scheme

$$X = \text{Spec}\left( \frac{\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]}{(xz,yz)}\right)$$

is the union of $\mathbb{A}^2$ and $\mathbb{A}^1$. Then, the embedding $X \hookrightarrow \mathbb{A}^3$ isn't regular since taking any non-origin point on the $z$-axis is of dimension $1$ while any non-origin point on the $xy$-plane is of dimension $2$.

:::

## Local complete intersection morphisms and virtual tangent bundles {#Local_complete_intersection_morphisms_and_virtual_tangent_bundles}

A morphism of finite type $f:X \to Y$ is called a **(local) complete intersection morphism** if each point *x* in *X* has an open affine neighborhood *U* so that *f* |<sub>*U*</sub> factors as $U \overset{j}\to V \overset{g}\to Y$ where *j* is a regular embedding and *g* is [smooth](wikipedia://en/7837041). [^o9] For example, if *f* is a morphism between [smooth varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety), then *f* factors as $X \to X \times Y \to Y$ where the first map is the [graph morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_morphism_%28algebraic_geometry%29) and so is a complete intersection morphism. Notice that this definition is compatible with the one in EGA IV for the special case of [flat morphisms](wikipedia://en/542520).[^o10]

Let $f: X \to Y$ be a local-complete-intersection morphism that admits a global factorization: it is a composition $X \overset{i}\hookrightarrow P \overset{p}\to Y$ where $i$ is a regular embedding and $p$ a smooth morphism. Then the **virtual tangent bundle** is an element of the [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790) of vector bundles on *X* given as:[^o11]

$$T_f = [i^* T_{P/Y}] - [N_{X/P}],$$

where $T_{P/Y}=\Omega_{P/Y}^{\vee}$ is the relative tangent sheaf of $p$ (which is [locally free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_free_sheaf) since $p$ is smooth) and $N$ is the normal sheaf $(\mathcal{I}/\mathcal{I}^2)^{\vee}$ (where $\mathcal{I}$ is the ideal sheaf of $X$ in $P$), which is locally free since $i$ is a regular embedding.

More generally, if $f \colon X \rightarrow Y$ is a *any* local complete intersection morphism of schemes, its [cotangent complex](wikipedia://en/17346860) $L_{X/Y}$ is [perfect](wikipedia://en/46630707) of Tor-amplitude \[-1,0\]. If moreover $f$ is locally of finite type and $Y$ locally Noetherian, then the converse is also true.[^o12]

These notions are used for instance in the [Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/1532606).

:::

## Non-Noetherian case {#Non-Noetherian_case}

[SGA 6 Exposé VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Séminaire_de_géométrie_algébrique_du_Bois_Marie) uses the following slightly weaker form of the notion of a regular embedding, which agrees with the one presented above for Noetherian schemes:

First, given a [projective module](wikipedia://en/364488) *E* over a commutative ring *A*, an *A*-linear map $u: E \to A$ is called **Koszul-regular** if the [Koszul complex](wikipedia://en/519992) determined by it is [acyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acyclic_complex) in dimension > 0 (consequently, it is a resolution of the cokernel of *u*).[^o14] Then a closed immersion $X \hookrightarrow Y$ is called **Koszul-regular** if the ideal sheaf determined by it is such that, locally, there are a finite free *A*-module *E* and a Koszul-regular surjection from *E* to the ideal sheaf.[^o15]

It is this Koszul regularity that was used in SGA 6 [^o16] for the definition of local complete intersection morphisms; it is indicated there that Koszul-regularity was intended to replace the definition given earlier in this article and that had appeared originally in the already published EGA IV.[^o17]

(This questions arises because the discussion of zero-divisors is tricky for non-Noetherian rings in that one cannot use the theory of associated primes.)
