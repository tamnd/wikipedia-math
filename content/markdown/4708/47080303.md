# Benjamin Bramer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Benjamin Bramer"}

**Benjamin Bramer** (15 February 1588 – 17 March 1652) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [architect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architect), [inventor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventor), and [adviser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adviser).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Bramer was born on 15 February 1588 in [Felsberg, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felsberg,_Germany) to a Protestant minister father. The minister later died when Bramer was three years old. This led him to be adopted by his brother-in-law, [Jost Bürgi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jost_Bürgi), who was a prominent mathematician at the time. He moved to Bürgi's home in [Kassel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassel) after being adopted. Bürgi educated Bramer from a young age, particularly in the fields of mathematics and architecture. When Bramer was 16 he stayed in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague) with his foster father after Bürgi was appointed to the imperial court. Bramer would stay there for five years before returning to Kassel to begin his career.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

*Image: Title page of Bramer's book Problema, Marburg, 1614.jpg.* Title page of the Benjamin Bramer's booklet Problema wie aus bekannt gegebenem sinu (...), Marburg, 1614

One of his first jobs was as an architectural adviser to [Count Christian von Waldeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_Christian_von_Waldeck). He advised him to construct a new church in the town of [Widungen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widungen), but the out break of the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War) caused him to begin focusing on the design of military fortifications. One such fortification was the [Rheinfels Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheinfels_Castle) which he improved for the [Count of Solms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_of_Solms).[^o4] In 1635 he became the master builder of [Ziegenhain Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziegenhain_Fortress).[^o6]

Bramer's first scientific publication was in 1614. Titled *Problema wie aus bekannt gegebenem sinu eines Grades, Minuten oder Sekunden alle folgenden sinus aufs leichtests zu finden und der canon sinuum zu absolvieren sei* he described various measures to calculate sines. He also published a work on [vacuums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuums) titled *Kurze Meldung vom Vacuo oder leerem Orte, neben anderen wunderbaren und subtilen Quaestionen, desgleichen Nic Cusani Dialogus von Waag und Gewicht*.[^o4]

In 1630, he constructed an early [pantograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantograph) enabling him to draw accurate [geometric perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_perspective) which he described in an earlier 1617 publication titled *Trigonometrica planorum mechanica oder Unterricht und Beschreibung eines neuen und sehr bequemen geometrischen Instrumentes zu allerhand Abmessung*. Although he is typically not credited with the invention of the pantograph, his early design is superior to the pantograph invented by [Christoph Scheiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Scheiner).[^o4][^o7]
