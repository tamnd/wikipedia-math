# Analysis of molecular variance

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Analysis of molecular variance"}

**Analysis of molecular variance** (**AMOVA**), is a [statistical model](wikipedia://en/27576) for the molecular algorithm in a single [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species), typically [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology).[^o2] The name and model are inspired by [ANOVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANOVA). The method was developed by [Laurent Excoffier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Excoffier), [Peter Smouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Smouse) and [Joseph Quattro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Quattro) at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) in 1992.

:::

Since developing AMOVA, Excoffier has written a [program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_program) for running such analyses. This program, which runs on [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows), is called [Arlequin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlequin_%28software%29) and is freely available on Excoffier's website. There are also implementations in [R language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_language) in the ade4 and the pegas packages, both available on CRAN (Comprehensive R Archive Network). Another implementation is in [Info-Gen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Info-Gen), which also runs on [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows). The student version is free and fully functional. Native language of the application is Spanish but an English version is also available.

An additional free statistical package, GenAlEx,[^o3] is geared toward teaching as well as research and allows for complex genetic analyses to be employed and compared within the commonly used Microsoft Excel interface. This software allows for calculation of analyses such as AMOVA, as well as comparisons with other types of closely related statistics including F-statistics and Shannon's index, and more.
