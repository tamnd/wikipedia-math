# Graph Fourier transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph Fourier transform"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **graph Fourier transform** is a [mathematical transform](wikipedia://en/1904373) which [eigendecomposes](wikipedia://en/13576645) the [Laplacian matrix](wikipedia://en/1448472) of a graph into [eigenvalues and eigenvectors](wikipedia://en/2161429). Analogously to the [classical Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247), the eigenvalues represent [frequencies](wikipedia://en/370346) and eigenvectors form what is known as a graph [Fourier basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_basis).

:::

The Graph Fourier transform is important in [spectral graph theory](wikipedia://en/534914). It is widely applied in the recent study of graph structured [learning algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_algorithms), such as the widely employed [convolutional networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convolutional_neural_networks).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given an [undirected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) [weighted graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_graph) $G = (V,E)$, where $V$ is the set of nodes with $|V| = N$ ($N$ being the number of nodes) and $E$ is the set of edges, a graph signal $f: V \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a function defined on the vertices of the graph $G$. The signal $f$ maps every vertex $\{v_i\}_{i=1,\ldots,N}$ to a [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) $f(i)$. Any graph signal can be projected on the [eigenvectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvectors) of the [Laplacian matrix](wikipedia://en/1448472) $L$.[^o4] Let $\lambda_l$ and $\mu_l$ be the $l_\text{th}$ eigenvalue and eigenvector of the [Laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian) matrix $L$ (the eigenvalues are sorted in an increasing order, i.e., $0= \lambda_0\leq\lambda_1\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N-1}$[^o5]), the graph Fourier transform (GFT) $\hat{f}$ of a graph signal $f$ on the vertices of $G$ is the expansion of $f$ in terms of the [eigenfunctions](wikipedia://en/228601) of $L$.[^o6] It is defined as:[^o4][^o7]

$$\mathcal{G F}[f](\lambda_{l})=\hat{f}\left(\lambda_{l}\right)=\langle f, \mu_{l}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{N} f(i) \mu_{l}^*(i),$$

where $\mu_l^* = \mu_l^\text{T}$.

Since $L$ is a [real symmetric matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_symmetric_matrix), its eigenvectors $\{\mu_l\}_{l=0,\cdots, N-1}$ form an [orthogonal basis](wikipedia://en/755400). Hence an inverse graph Fourier transform (IGFT) exists, and it is written as:[^o7]

$$\mathcal{I} \mathcal{G} \mathcal{F}[\hat{f}](i)=f(i)=\sum_{l=0}^{N-1} \hat{f}(\lambda_l) \mu_l(i)$$

Analogously to the classical [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247), graph Fourier transform provides a way to represent a signal in two different domains: the vertex domain and the [graph spectral domain](wikipedia://en/534914). Note that the definition of the graph Fourier transform and its inverse depend on the choice of Laplacian eigenvectors, which are not necessarily unique.[^o6] The eigenvectors of the normalized Laplacian matrix are also a possible base to define the forward and inverse graph Fourier transform.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

::: {#o11 .statement cue=named-heading name="Parseval's identity"}

### Parseval's identity {#Parseval's_identity}

The [Parseval relation](wikipedia://en/706435) holds for the graph Fourier transform,[^o12] that is, for any $f,h\in \mathbb{R}^N$

$$\langle f, h\rangle=\langle\hat{f}, \hat{h}\rangle.$$

This gives us [Parseval's identity](wikipedia://en/418615):[^o6]

$$\sum_{i=1}^N |f(i)|^{2}=\|f\|_2^2=\langle f, f\rangle=\langle\hat{f}, \hat{f}\rangle=\|\hat{f}\|_2^2=\sum_{l=0}^{N-1} \left|\hat{f}\left(\lambda_l\right)\right|^2.$$

:::

### Generalized convolution operator {#Generalized_convolution_operator}

The definition of [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) between two functions $f$ and $g$ cannot be directly applied to graph signals, because the [signal translation](wikipedia://en/467384) is not defined in the context of graphs.[^o7] However, by replacing the complex [exponential shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_shift_theorem) in classical Fourier transform with the graph Laplacian eigenvectors, convolution of two graph signals can be defined as:[^o6]

$$(f * g)=\mathcal{I} \mathcal{G} \mathcal{F}[\mathcal{G} \mathcal{F}[f] \cdot \mathcal{G} \mathcal{F}[g]],$$

$$(f * g)(i)=\sum_{l=0}^{N-1} \hat{f}(\lambda_l) \hat{g}(\lambda_l) \mu_l(i).$$

#### Properties of the convolution operator {#Properties_of_the_convolution_operator}

The generalized convolution operator satisfies the following properties:[^o6]

- Generalized convolution in the vertex domain is multiplication in the graph spectral domain: $\widehat{f * g}=\hat{f} \hat{g}.$
- [Commutativity](wikipedia://en/294390): $f * g=g * f$
- [Distributivity](wikipedia://en/103118): $f * (g+h)=f*g + f*h$
- [Associativity](wikipedia://en/1335): $(f*g)*h = f*(g*h)$
- Associativity with [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373): $\alpha(f * g)=(\alpha f) * g=f *(\alpha g)$, for any $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$.
- [Multiplicative identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicative_identity): $f * g_0=f$, where $g_0(i)=\sum_{l=0}^{N-1} \mu_{l}(i)$ is an identity for the generalized convolution operator.
- The sum of the generalized convolution of two signals is a constant times the product of the sums of the two signals:

$$\sum_{i=1}^N(f * g)(i)=\sqrt{N} \hat{f}(0) \hat{g}(0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^N f(i)\right] \left[\sum_{i=1}^N g(i)\right].$$

### Generalized translation operator {#Generalized_translation_operator}

As previously stated, the classical translation operator $T_v$ cannot be generalized to the graph setting. One way to define a generalized translation operator is through generalized convolution with a delta function centered at vertex $n$:[^o5]$\left(T_{n} f\right)(i)=\sqrt{N}\left(f * \delta_{n}\right)(i){=} \sqrt{N} \sum_{l=0}^{N-1} \hat{f}\left(\lambda_{l}\right) u_{l}^{*}(n) u_{l}(i),$

where $\delta_i(n)=\begin{cases} 1, & \text {if } i=n, \\ 0, & \text {otherwise.} \end{cases}$

The normalization constant $\sqrt{N}$ ensures that the translation operator preserves the signal mean,[^o7] i.e.,

$$\sum_{i=1}^N(T_n f)(i)=\sum_{i=1}^N f(i).$$

#### Properties of the translation operator {#Properties_of_the_translation_operator}

The generalized convolution operator satisfies the following properties:[^o6]

For any $f, g \in \mathbb{R}^N$, and $j,k\in \{1,2,\dots,N\}$,

- $$T_j(f * g)=(T_j f) * g=f *(T_{j} g)$$
- $$T_j T_k f=T_k T_j f$$
- $$\sum_{i=1}^N (T_j f)(i)=\sqrt{N} \hat{f}(0)=\sum_{i=1}^N f(i)$$
- $$\left\|T_j f\right\| \neq \| f \|$$

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

### Image compression {#Image_compression}

Representing signals in [frequency domain](wikipedia://en/370346) is a common approach to [data compression](wikipedia://en/8013). As graph signals can be sparse in their graph spectral domain, the graph Fourier transform can also be used for [image compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_compression).[^o20][^o21]

### Graph noise reduction {#Graph_noise_reduction}

Similar to classical [noise reduction](wikipedia://en/476836) of signals based on Fourier transform, [graph filters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_kernel) based on the graph Fourier transform can be designed for graph signal denoising.[^o23]

### Data classification {#Data_classification}

As the graph Fourier transform enables the definition of convolution on graphs, it makes possible to adapt the conventional [convolutional neural networks (CNN)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convolutional_neural_network) to work on graphs. Graph structured [semi-supervised learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-supervised_learning) algorithms such as graph [convolutional network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convolutional_neural_network) (GCN), are able to propagate the labels of a graph signal throughout the graph with a small subset of labeled nodes, theoretically operating as a first order approximation of spectral graph convolutions without computing the graph Laplacian and its eigendecomposition.[^o25]

:::

## Toolbox {#Toolbox}

GSPBOX[^o27][^o28] is a toolbox for [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) of graphs, including the graph Fourier transform. It supports both [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) and [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412) languages.
