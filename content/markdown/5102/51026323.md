# Proximal operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Proximal operator"}

In [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033), the **proximal** operator is an [operator](wikipedia://en/22213) associated with a proper,[^o2] [lower semi-continuous](wikipedia://en/57824) [convex function](wikipedia://en/245568) $f$ from a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $\mathcal{X}$ to $[-\infty,+\infty]$, and is defined by:[^o3]

:::

$$\operatorname{prox}_f(v) = \arg \min_{x\in\mathcal{X}} \left(f(x) + \frac 1 2 \|x - v\|_\mathcal{X}^2\right).$$

For any function in this class, the minimizer of the right-hand side above is unique, hence making the proximal operator well-defined. The proximal operator is used in proximal gradient methods, which is frequently used in optimization algorithms associated with non-[differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206) optimization problems such as [total variation denoising](wikipedia://en/26792343).

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The $\text{prox}$ of a proper, lower semi-continuous convex function $f$ enjoys several useful properties for optimization.

- Fixed points of $\text{prox}_f$ are minimizers of $f$: $\{x\in \mathcal{X}\ |\ \text{prox}_fx = x\} = \arg \min f$.
- Global convergence to a minimizer is defined as follows: If $\arg \min f \neq \varnothing$, then for any initial point $x_0 \in \mathcal{X}$, the recursion $(\forall n \in \mathbb{N})\quad x_{n+1} = \text{prox}_f x_n$ yields convergence $x_n \to x \in \arg \min f$ as $n \to +\infty$. This convergence may be weak if $\mathcal{X}$ is infinite dimensional.[^o6]
- The proximal operator can be seen as a generalization of the [projection operator](wikipedia://en/519182). Indeed, in the specific case where $f$ is the [0-$\infty$ characteristic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_function_%28convex_analysis%29) $\iota_C$ of a nonempty, closed, convex set $C$ we have that

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{prox}_{\iota_C}(x) &= \operatorname{argmin}\limits_y \begin{cases} \frac{1}{2} \left\| x-y \right\|_2^2 & \text{if } y \in C \\ + \infty & \text{if } y \notin C \end{cases} \\ &=\operatorname{argmin}\limits_{y \in C} \frac{1}{2} \left\| x-y \right\|_2^2 \end{aligned}$$

showing that the proximity operator is indeed a generalisation of the projection operator.

- The proximal operator is [firmly non-expansive](wikipedia://en/6239#Firmly_non-expansive_mapping): $(\forall (x,y) \in \mathcal{X}^2) \quad \|\text{prox}_fx - \text{prox}_fy\|^2 \leq \langle x-y\ , \text{prox}_fx - \text{prox}_fy\rangle$.
- The proximal operator of a function is related to the gradient of the [Moreau envelope](wikipedia://en/72529865) $M_{\lambda f}$ of a function $\lambda f$ by the following identity: $\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x) = \frac{1}{\lambda} (x - \mathrm{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))$.
- The proximity operator of $f$ is characterized by inclusion $p=\operatorname{prox}_f(x) \Leftrightarrow x-p \in \partial f(p)$, where $\partial f$ is the [subdifferential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdifferential) of $f$, given by

$\partial f(x) = \{ u \in \mathbb{R}^N \mid \forall y \in \mathbb{R}^N, (y-x)^\mathrm{T}u+f(x) \leq f(y)\}$ In particular, If $f$ is differentiable then the above equation reduces to $p=\operatorname{prox}_f(x) \Leftrightarrow x-p = \nabla f(p)$.

:::
