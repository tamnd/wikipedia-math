# Blasius theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Blasius theorem"}

In [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), **Blasius theorem** states that [^o2][^o3][^o4] *the force experienced by a two-dimensional fixed body in a steady irrotational flow is given by*

:::

$$F_x-iF_y = \frac{i\rho}{2} \oint_C \left(\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}z}\right)^2\mathrm{d}z$$

*and the moment about the origin experienced by the body is given by*

$$M=\Re\left\{-\frac{\rho}{2}\oint_C z \left(\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}z}\right)^2\mathrm{d}z\right\}.$$

Here,

- $(F_x,F_y)$ is the force acting on the body,
- $\rho$ is the [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) of the fluid,
- $C$ is the contour flush around the body,
- $w=\phi+ i\psi$ is the complex potential ($\phi$ is the [velocity potential](wikipedia://en/2448955), $\psi$ is the [stream function](wikipedia://en/218091)),
- ${\mathrm{d}w}/{\mathrm{d}z} = u_x-i u_y$ is the complex velocity ($(u_x,u_y)$ is the velocity vector),
- $z=x+iy$ is the [complex variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variable) ($(x,y)$ is the position vector),
- $\Re$ is the real part of the [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826), and
- $M$ is the moment about the coordinate origin acting on the body.

The first formula is sometimes called *Blasius–Chaplygin formula*.[^o5]

The theorem is named after [Paul Richard Heinrich Blasius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Richard_Heinrich_Blasius), who derived it in 1911.[^o6] The [Kutta–Joukowski theorem](wikipedia://en/8606325) directly follows from this theorem.
