# Levitzky's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Levitzky's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424) and the theory of [nil ideals](wikipedia://en/3100140), **Levitzky's theorem**, named after [Jacob Levitzki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Levitzki), states that in a right [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129), every nil one-sided [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) is necessarily [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/6389159).[^o2][^o3] Levitzky's theorem is one of the many results suggesting the veracity of the [Köthe conjecture](wikipedia://en/3100105), and indeed provided a solution to one of [Köthe's](wikipedia://en/27641787) questions as described in (Levitzki 1945). The result was originally submitted in 1939 as (Levitzki 1950), and a particularly simple [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) was given in (Utumi 1963).

:::

## Proof {#Proof}

This is Utumi's argument as it appears in (Lam 2001, p. 164-165)

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=inline-head name="Lemma"}

**Lemma[^n1]**

Assume that *R* satisfies the [ascending chain condition](wikipedia://en/3189) on [annihilators](wikipedia://en/328684) of the form $\{r\in R\mid ar=0\}$ where *a* is in *R*. Then

1. Any nil one-sided ideal is contained in the lower nil radical Nil<sub>\*</sub>(*R*);
1. Every nonzero nil right ideal contains a nonzero nilpotent right ideal.
1. Every nonzero nil left ideal contains a nonzero nilpotent left ideal.

**Levitzki's Theorem [^n2]**

Let *R* be a right Noetherian ring. Then every nil one-sided ideal of *R* is nilpotent. In this case, the upper and lower nilradicals are equal, and moreover this ideal is the largest nilpotent ideal among nilpotent right ideals and among nilpotent left ideals.

*Proof*: In view of the previous [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634), it is sufficient to show that the lower nilradical of *R* is nilpotent. Because *R* is right Noetherian, a maximal nilpotent ideal *N* exists. By maximality of *N*, the [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) *R*/*N* has no nonzero nilpotent ideals, so *R*/*N* is a [semiprime ring](wikipedia://en/7739701). As a result, *N* contains the lower nilradical of *R*. Since the lower nilradical contains all nilpotent ideals, it also contains *N*, and so *N* is equal to the lower nilradical. [Q.E.D.](wikipedia://en/25228)
