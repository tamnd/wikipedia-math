# Freeman law

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Freeman law"}

The **Freeman law** is a statement in astronomy which says that [disk galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disk_galaxy) have the same [surface brightness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_brightness), Σ at the center. It was described in 1970 by [Ken Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Freeman_%28astronomer%29).[^o2][^o3]

:::

More recently it has been argued that the Freeman law is an effect of [selection bias](wikipedia://en/394392).[^o2][^o4] However, after accounting for selection effects, the Freeman law was confirmed in the year 2010 for a sample of 30000 [Sloan Digital Sky Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Digital_Sky_Survey) galaxy images.[^o5] It can be used with the [Tully–Fisher relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tully–Fisher_relation) to determine the [luminosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminosity) and therefore distance of an observed galaxy.

The Freeman law is sometimes called the Fish-Freeman effect.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .statement cue=named-heading name="Fish law"}

## Fish law {#Fish_law}

The **Fish law** (or Fish's law) in astronomy is the analogue for the Freeman law with disk galaxies replaced by [elliptical galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptical_galaxy). It was described in 1964 by Robert A. Fish based on photometric results for 29 elliptical galaxies.[^o9]

:::

## Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) {#Modified_Newtonian_Dynamics_(MOND)}

According to Brada and [Milgrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordehai_Milgrom),[^o11] The acceleration constant of the modified dynamics ([MOND](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOND)), *a<sub>0</sub>*, appears in various predicted regularities pertinent to galaxies. For example, it features as an upper cutoff to the mean surface density (or mean surface brightness translated with *[M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_common_astronomy_symbols#Mass_comparison)/[L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_common_astronomy_symbols#Luminosity_comparison)*) of galaxies, as observed and formulated in the Freeman law for disks, and of the Fish law for ellipticals.
