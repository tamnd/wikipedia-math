# Pinsky phenomenon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pinsky phenomenon"}

In mathematics, the **Pinsky phenomenon** is a result in [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659).[^o2] This phenomenon was discovered by [Mark Pinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Pinsky) of [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University). It involves the spherical inversion of the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247). The phenomenon involves a lack of convergence at a point due to a discontinuity at boundary. This lack of convergence in the Pinsky phenomenon happens far away from the boundary of the discontinuity, rather than at the discontinuity itself seen in the [Gibbs phenomenon](wikipedia://en/373986). This non-local phenomenon is caused by a lensing effect.

:::

## Prototypical example {#Prototypical_example}

Let a function *g*(*x*) = 1 for |*x*| \< *c* in 3 dimensions, with *g*(*x*) = 0 elsewhere. The jump at |*x*| = *c* will cause an oscillatory behavior of the spherical partial sums, which prevents convergence at the center of the ball as well as the possibility of Fourier inversion at *x* = 0. Stated differently, spherical partial sums of a [Fourier integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_integral) of the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) of a [ball](wikipedia://en/160556) are divergent at the center of the [ball](wikipedia://en/160556) but convergent elsewhere to the desired indicator function. This prototype example was coined the ”Pinsky phenomenon” by [Jean-Pierre Kahane](wikipedia://en/6949961), CRAS, 1995.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

This prototype example can be suitably generalized to Fourier integral expansions in higher dimensions, both in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) and other non-compact rank-one [symmetric spaces](wikipedia://en/1642877). Also related are [eigenfunction](wikipedia://en/228601) expansions on a [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) ball in a rank-one symmetric space, but one must consider boundary conditions. Pinsky and others also represent some results on the [asymptotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic) behavior of the [Fejér approximation](wikipedia://en/693816) in one dimension, inspired by work of Bump, [Persi Diaconis](wikipedia://en/65467), and J. B. Keller.

:::
