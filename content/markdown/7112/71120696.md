# William Lloyd Garrison Williams

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William Lloyd Garrison Williams"}

**William Lloyd Garrison Williams** (October 3, 1888 – January 31, 1976) was an American-Canadian [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for the founding of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) and overseeing [Elbert Frank Cox](wikipedia://en/2814552)'s doctorate in mathematics.

:::

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Williams was born in Friendship, [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas) to Amanda Dunreath Truex and Nathan Williams.[^o4] After the death of his mother, he was taken in by his father's first wife's family, the Tomlinsons in Indiana.

Williams married Anne Skyes, a pianist from Cincinnati, in 1917.[^o5] Together, they had two daughters, Hester and mathematician [Christine Ayoub](wikipedia://en/79846305).[^o6]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

After attending the Quaker Academy, Williams taught in North Dakota.[^o5] He then studied [Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics) at [Haverford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College).[^o4] Subsequently, he was awarded a [Rhodes Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Scholarship), where he studied mathematics at [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) from 1910 to 1913.[^o8] Once obtaining an M.A. he took on a faculty position at the [Miami University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_University).[^o5] During the summers, he did Ph.D. work at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o8] He wrote his Ph.D. on *Fundamental Systems of Formal Modular Seminvariants of the Binary Cubic*, published in 1920. He then taught briefly at [Gettysburg College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_College) and [William and Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_William_&_Mary), before relocating to [Cornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).[^o4]

In 1924, Williams moved again, this time to teach at [McGill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University), to develop their graduate program. He remained there until his retirement in 1954.[^o9] He was awarded honorary degrees by the [University of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Montréal), [University of Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manitoba), [Dalhousie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalhousie_University), and [Mount Allison University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Allison_University).[^o4]

## Elbert Frank Cox {#Elbert_Frank_Cox}

Williams supervised the Ph.D. of [Elbert Frank Cox](wikipedia://en/2814552), the first African American to get a mathematics doctorate, during his time at Cornell. When Williams took up his position at McGill, Cox followed him. Cox was awarded his degree in 1925, utilizing the Erastus Brooks Fellowship.[^o4]

Unsatisfied with the limited amount of recognition Cox received, Williams petitioned international universities to recognise his student, eventually convincing the [Sendai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendai_University), Japan.[^o11]

## Canadian Mathematical Congress {#Canadian_Mathematical_Congress}

Williams founded the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055), formerly the Canadian Mathematical Congress, in 1945. Williams dreamed of a forum to bring Canadian mathematicians together, regardless of race or creed, especially after attending the Toronto Congress in 1924 where all mathematicians of the Central Powers had been excluded.[^o6] He worked as the treasurer from its founding in 1945 to 1963.[^o4] He was particularly successful in achieving support from insurance companies, although he was noted by many to be quite zealous and friendly and found support from many.[^o13]

Currently the [Jeffrey-Williams Prize](wikipedia://en/18739069) is named in his honour, and awarded to mathematicians who have made outstanding contributions to mathematical research by the Canadian Mathematical Society.[^o9]

## Religious beliefs {#Religious_beliefs}

A devoted Quaker, Williams was instrumental in the Montreal Quaker community during the 1930s and beyond.[^o15] These beliefs influenced his views of racial equality and hard work. Williams pioneered Quaker causes. He was a member of the Board of the [Canadian Friends Service Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Friends_Service_Committee) and chairman of the committee from 1959 until 1963.[^o6] He founded the Montreal “Save the Children” Fund in 1944.[^o16] He additionally helped with the purchasing of a building for Montreal friends to use as a meeting house, although it is no longer in use.[^o5] He is noted for being an official of the Quakers of Montreal.[^o6]
