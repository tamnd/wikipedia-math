# Mapping torus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mapping torus"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), the **mapping torus** of a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) *f* of some [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* to itself is a particular geometric construction with *f*. Take the [cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of *X* with a [closed interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_interval) *I*, and glue the boundary components together by the static homeomorphism:

:::

$$M_f =\frac{(I \times X)}{(1,x)\sim (0,f(x))}$$

The result is a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) whose base is a circle and whose fiber is the original space *X*.

If *X* is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470), *M<sub>f</sub>* will be a manifold of dimension one higher, and it is said to ["fiber over the circle"](wikipedia://en/276526).

As a simple example, let $X$ be the circle, and $f$ be the inversion $e^{ix} \mapsto e^{-ix}$, then the mapping torus is the Klein bottle.

Mapping tori of surface homeomorphisms play a key role in the theory of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) and have been intensely studied. If *S* is a closed surface of [genus](wikipedia://en/27865#Classification_of_closed_surfaces) *g* ≥ 2 and if *f* is a self-homeomorphism of *S*, the mapping torus *M<sub>f</sub>* is a [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) that [fibers](wikipedia://en/276526) over the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) with fiber *S*. A [deep result](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_result) of [Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190) states that in this case the [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) *M<sub>f</sub>* is [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/1589135) if and only if *f* is a [pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/2327355) of *S*.[^o2]
