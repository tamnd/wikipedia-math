# Guillaume Carlier

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Guillaume Carlier"}

**Guillaume Carlier** is a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Most of his work lies in the field of [calculus of variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_of_variation) and [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization). He is a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at [Paris Dauphine University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University) and a researcher at Mokaplan, a joint [INRIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INRIA)-[CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS)-Université Paris-Dauphine team dedicated to research in the field of [optimal transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_transport).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Carlier's work mainly focuses on applied mathematics, in particular in the fields of [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization), [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201), and [transportation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_transport) as well as their application to [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and traffic modelling.[^o3][^o6][^o7]

He graduated in mathematics and mathematical economics from [ENSAE ParisTech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENSAE_ParisTech), [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University), and Paris-Dauphine University in 1996. He then completed his PhD at Paris-Dauphine University in 2000, with a dissertation on the applications of calculus of variations to contract theory, under the supervision of [Ivar Ekeland](wikipedia://en/31377247).[^o8] After his studies, he was an assistant professor at the [University of Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bordeaux) and then moved to Paris-Dauphine University, where he is now a professor.[^o3] He is a member of Mokaplan,[^o4] a joint research unit co-sponsored by Paris-Dauphine University, the [National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research), and the [French Institute for Research in Computer Science and Automation](wikipedia://en/318413).[^o9]
