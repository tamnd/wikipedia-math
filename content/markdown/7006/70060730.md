# Ernst Snapper

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ernst Snapper"}

**Ernst Snapper** (December 2, 1913, [Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groningen) – February 5, 2011, [Chapel Hill, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina)) was a Dutch-American mathematician, known for his research in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [cohomology of groups](wikipedia://en/248635), [character theory](wikipedia://en/691741), and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Ernst Snapper, born to a Jewish family in the Netherlands, received in 1936 the equivalent of a master's degree from the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam). In 1938 his father, Isidore Snapper, an internationally known physician and medical researcher, accepted an offer to become the director of medical research at the [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation)'s [Peking Union Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_Union_Medical_College). Acting on a suggestion from [Abraham Flexner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Flexner), Isidore Snapper encouraged Ernst Snapper to apply to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) to become a graduate student. As a doctoral student of [Joseph Wedderburn](wikipedia://en/2969494), Ernst Snapper graduated with a Ph.D. from Princeton University in 1941. In China, his father and mother were interned by the Japanese, but were later released in an exchange.[^o4] Ernst Snapper was an instructor from 1941 to 1945 at Princeton University. He was a professor of mathematics from 1945 to 1955 at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California), from 1955 to 1958 at [Miami University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_University) of Ohio, from 1958 to 1963 at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University), and from 1963 to 1979 at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College), where he retired as professor emeritus. He was a visiting professor for the academic years 1949–1950 and 1954–1955 at Princeton University[^o5] and for the academic year 1953–1954 at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o2]

An early sequence of papers extended the [Steinitz](wikipedia://en/3966685) [field theory](wikipedia://en/10603) to completely primary [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) using ideas from the work of [Krull](wikipedia://en/964328). During his visits at Princeton and Harvard, Snapper studied algebraic geometry and the [homological](wikipedia://en/139410) and [sheaf-theoretic](wikipedia://en/245466) methods of [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) and [Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042). Later he applied those methods in several important papers on the polynomial properties of the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) associated with divisor classes of an irreducible normal [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469). He continued using homological methods in a sequence of papers in which he extended the classical cohomology of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) to the cohomology of arbitrary [permutation representations](wikipedia://en/59487764) of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707). Snapper then applied these methods to obtain a classical result on [Frobenius kernels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_kernel). In the area of combinatorial mathematics, Snapper extended [de Bruijn](wikipedia://en/3166399)’s theory of the cycle index of a finite group to that of an arbitrary permutation representation. A subsequent paper coauthored with [Arunas Rudvalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunas_Rudvalis) extended this cycle index to a generalized cycle index of a permutation representation paired with a class function. They then obtained the theorem of Frobenius that every simple character of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) is an [integral](wikipedia://en/14563) linear combination of transitive permutation characters.[^o2]

His doctoral students include [Arunas Rudvalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunas_Rudvalis). Snapper's paper *The Three Crises in Mathematics: Logicism, Intuitionism and formalism* won the 1980 [Carl B. Allendoerfer Award](wikipedia://en/40343255).[^o2][^o6]

He was married to Ethel K. Snapper (1917–1995) for nearly 60 years. Upon his death he was survived by his two sons, John and James, both of whom were graduates of Princeton University, and two granddaughters.[^o5] John Snapper received his Ph.D. in philosophy from the University of Chicago and became a professor at [Illinois Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Institute_of_Technology). James Robert Snapper received his M.D. from [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School) in 1974 and became a pulmonologist and consulting professor in the department of medicine of [Duke University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University_School_of_Medicine).

Ernst Snapper corresponded with [Leo Vroman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Vroman), who was his cousin.[^o7]
