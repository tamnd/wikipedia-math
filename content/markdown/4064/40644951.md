# Bishop–Phelps theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bishop–Phelps theorem"}

In mathematics, the **Bishop–Phelps theorem** is a theorem about the topological properties of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) named after [Errett Bishop](wikipedia://en/321995) and [Robert Phelps](wikipedia://en/31368502), who published its proof in 1961.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Bishop–Phelps theorem"}

**Bishop–Phelps theorem.**

Let $B \subseteq X$ be a bounded, closed, convex subset of a real [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) $X.$ Then the set of all [continuous linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_linear_functional) $f$ that achieve their supremum on $B$ (meaning that there exists some $b_0 \in B$ such that $|f(b_0)| = \sup_{b \in B} |f(b)|$)

$$\left\{f \in X^* : f \text{ attains its supremum on } B\right\}$$

is [norm](wikipedia://en/13525027)-dense in the [continuous dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space) $X^*$ of $X.$

:::

Importantly, this theorem fails for complex Banach spaces.[^o6] However, for the special case where $B$ is the closed unit ball then this theorem does hold for complex Banach spaces.[^o2][^o6]

:::
