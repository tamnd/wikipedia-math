# Geraldine Darden

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geraldine Darden"}

**Geraldine Claudette Darden** (born July 22, 1936) is an American mathematician. She was the fourteenth African American woman to earn a Ph.D. in mathematics.[^o2]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Darden was born in [Nansemond County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nansemond_County,_Virginia). Darden earned a bachelor's degrees in mathematics in 1957 from the [Hampton Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Institute), a historically black institute, and took a teaching position at S.H. Clarke Junior High School in [Portsmouth, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth,_Virginia). In the summer of 1958, Darden saw an opportunity for aspiring mathematicians created by the launch of Russian satellite [Sputnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik) and ensuing US interest in mathematics and science a year earlier, and she applied for and received a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) grant to attend the Summer Institute in Mathematics held at [North Carolina Central University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Central_University). Here she met [Marjorie Lee Browne](wikipedia://en/8459839), the mathematician who directed the institute, who would encourage Darden to go on to graduate school.

Darden earned a master's degree in 1960 at the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign), and a second master's degree in 1965 and Ph.D. in 1967 from [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University). Her dissertation was completed under the supervision of James Reid, "On the Direct Sums of Cyclic Groups".[^o4]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In addition to teaching, Darden also co-wrote *Selected Papers on Pre-calculus*, with textbook author [Tom Apostol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Apostol), Gulbank D. Chakerian, and John D. Neff.[^o6]
