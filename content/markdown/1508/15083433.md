# Karl Christian von Langsdorf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Karl Christian von Langsdorf"}

*Image: Langsdorf Karl Christian1.jpg.* Karl Christian von Langsdorf

**Karl Christian von Langsdorf**, also known as **Carl Christian von Langsdorff** (18 May 1757 in [Nauheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Nauheim)[^o2] – 10 June 1834 in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg)), was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [geologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologist), [natural scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_scientist) and [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Langsdorf was the son of Georg Melchior Langsdorff and Maria Margarethe Koch. His father was saltworks archivist and [Hesse-Kassel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landgraviate_of_Hesse-Kassel) *Rentmeister* (master of the bursary) (saltworks of Nauheim). He had a twin brother named Daniel Isaak. After finishing *[Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29)* (secondary school) in [Idstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idstein) in 1773, Langsdorf studied, among other things, [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law), and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) from 1774 until autumn of 1776 with [Abraham Gotthelf Kästner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Gotthelf_Kästner) and then until 1777 at the [University of Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen). He then interned at the saltworks in [Salzhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidda,_Hesse), near Nidda, and subsequently devoted himself in [Nidda, Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidda,_Hesse) to the study of saltworks. He finished his doctorate in 1781 in [Erfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erfurt).

In the summer semester of 1781, he was an outside lecturer at Giessen. For health reasons however, he decided against an academic career. He pursued a career in administration and became *Rentmeister* and land judge in [Mülheim an der Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mülheim). Starting in 1784, he was active as a saltworks inspector in [Gerabronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerabronn) (near [Rothenburg ob der Tauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothenburg_ob_der_Tauber)), which belonged to the [margravate of Ansbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Ansbach) at the time.

In 1798, he was given a full professorship in mechanical engineering at [Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich-Alexander-University,_Erlangen-Nuremberg). He taught there until 1804 and he subjected the 15-year-old [Georg Ohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Ohm) to a thorough examination of his knowledge of mathematics. Ohm and his younger brother were instructed in mathematics by their father.

He refused an offer from Heidelberg in 1803 and accepted an offer to teach mathematics and technology at [Vilnius University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_University). In the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), he and his family were elevated into the hereditary aristocracy.[^o4] In 1806, he returned with the aristocratic descriptor [von](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von) and became (with the help of his older brother Gottlieb, the governor of [Dilsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neckargemünd)) full professor in Heidelberg.

The mathematical physics class of the [Bavarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Academy_of_Sciences) admitted him to its ranks as a non-local member in 1808. Langsdorf left the University of Erlangen in early 1809 to take up a post in the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruprecht_Karl_University_of_Heidelberg). Karl Christian von Langsdorff had been a professor of mathematics at Heidelberg from 1806 to 1834.(He is relative with mathematician Christian Hugo Eduard Study) Langsdorf was also very interested in theological questions and published several works on this topic. His brother, Johann Wilhelm, made a name for himself as a specialist in the area of saltworks engineering.
