# Secondary research

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Secondary research"}

**Secondary research** involves the summary, collation and/or synthesis of existing research. Secondary research is contrasted with primary research in that primary research involves the generation of data, whereas secondary research uses primary research sources as a source of data for analysis.[^o2] A notable marker of primary research is the inclusion of a "methods" section, where the authors describe how the data was generated.

:::

Common examples of secondary research include [textbooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbooks), [encyclopedias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopedia), [news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News) articles, [review articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_articles), and [meta analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_analysis).[^o3][^o4]

When conducting secondary research, authors may draw data from published academic papers, government documents, [statistical databases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_database), and historical records.[^o2][^o5]

## Fields {#Fields}

The term is widely used in fields such as history, [legal research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_research), [market research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_research), and Wikipedia editing. The principal methodology in health secondary research is the [systematic review](wikipedia://en/2994579), commonly using meta-analytic statistical techniques. Other methods of synthesis, like realist reviews and meta-[narrative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrative) reviews, have been developed in the 21st century.[^o7]

Secondary market research includes the reuse by a second party of any data collected from a first party such as telephone interviews or surveys. Secondary market research can be broken up into two categories: information from internal sources such as an agency or company, and information from external sources held outside an organization or agency.[^o8] Secondary market research uses information from the past, reuses data already collected, and is more economical.

## Primary research vs secondary research {#Primary_research_vs_secondary_research}

[Primary research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research#Forms_of_research) is research that is collected firsthand and is original to the person using it.[^o10] When conducting primary research, the goal is to answer questions that have not been answered in the published literature.[^o10] Additionally, the research has to be verified by others to help eliminate one's own biases.[^o11] Primary research can be a survey, observation, or an interview. This type of research tends to be more time consuming and can be costly.[^o12] If possible, secondary research should be done before primary research.

Secondary research is based on already published data and information gathered from other conducted studies.[^o11] It is a common practice by researchers to conduct secondary research before primary research in order to determine what information is not already available.[^o12] Secondary research is an easy place to start when starting a new research project. Secondary research can vary in credibility depending on where the data is coming from and who is sharing research.[^o13]
