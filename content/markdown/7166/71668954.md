# John Truss

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="John Truss"}

**John Kenneth Truss** (born April 1947) is an English mathematician. He is an [emeritus professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_professor) of [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds) where he specialises in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), infinite [permutation groups](wikipedia://en/24634), homogeneous structures and [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858).[^o3] Truss began his career as a junior research fellow at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) before holding a series of academic positions and lastly joining the University of Leeds. He has written books on discrete mathematics (1991) and mathematical analysis (1997) and was co-editor in chief of the *[Journal of the London Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_London_Mathematical_Society)* until June 2003. He is the father of the former [Prime Minister of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom), [Liz Truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Truss).

:::

## Early life and family {#Early_life_and_family}

John Truss was born in April 1947.[^o5][^o6] He graduated from [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), in 1968 and earned his PhD at the University of Leeds in 1973 for a dissertation titled "Some Results about Cardinal Numbers without the Axiom of Choice" which was supervised by Frank Drake.[^o7] In 1969, he married Priscilla Mary Grasby, a nurse,[^o8] who he had met while they were students at Cambridge.[^o8] Together, they have a daughter, [Liz Truss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Truss), and three sons.[^o9] Liz Truss has described her parents' politics as "to the left of [Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_%28UK%29)".[^o10] Truss and his wife were both supporters of the [Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_for_Nuclear_Disarmament).[^o11] They divorced in 2003.[^o8]

Truss refused to campaign with his daughter on her selection for [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_UK) candidate for [South West Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_Norfolk) in the [2010 UK general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_UK_general_election).[^o12]

## Career {#Career}

Truss's first academic position was as a junior research fellow at the [Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Institute) of the University of Oxford.[^o14] He then taught at a school in [Kidderminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidderminster),[^o15] Worcestershire, before lecturing at [Paisley College of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paisley_College_of_Technology) from 1979 to 1985.[^o8] In 1987, he worked at [Simon Fraser University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fraser_University)[^o16] in British Columbia, Canada, and later at the University of Leeds where in 1988 with Frank Drake he edited the collected papers of Logic Colloquium '86, held at the [University of Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hull) in 1986.[^o17]

In 1990, [Peter Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Cameron_%28mathematician%29) paid tribute to Truss in his notes on *Oligomorphic Permutation Groups* in the London Mathematical Society Lecture Notes Series No. 152, for saving him from "making some rash [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) (by disproving them)", and "notably" for his contribution to the question of what are the possible cycle structures of [automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphisms) of *M*?[^o18] In 1991, Truss published *Discrete Mathematics for Computer Scientists* which [John Bayliss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bayliss_%28mathematician%29) described in *[The Mathematical Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Gazette)* as "masterful and thorough" and getting "rapidly to the heart of some very exciting topics" but felt that it was more of a mathematician's book than a book for computer scientists as claimed by the author. Nonetheless, Bayliss felt that the approach taken by Truss in organising and presenting his material was highly successful in condensing different strands of mathematics so that the author had shown that "[discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) has come of age and is no longer a collection of disparate topics."[^o19]

In 1999, Truss and [S. Barry Cooper](wikipedia://en/5573572), also of the University of Leeds, jointly edited two volumes of papers in the [London Mathematical Society Lecture Notes Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Mathematical_Society_Lecture_Notes_Series) arising from the European meeting of the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438) in Leeds in July 1997 on sets and proofs[^o20][^o21] and models and computability.[^o20] The volumes were welcomed by philosopher [Graham Priest](wikipedia://en/3659382) of the [University of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Queensland) who noted that they concentrated on logic as practiced in mathematics departments with little content of a philosophical or computer science nature, but, possibly as a result, were more coherent than usual for collections of conference papers.[^o20] By then, Truss and [Jonathan Partington](wikipedia://en/5074250) were co-editors of the *[Journal of the London Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_London_Mathematical_Society)*.[^o22] They were succeeded on 6 June 2003 by [Francis Burstall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Burstall) and [John Toland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Toland_%28mathematician%29).[^o23]
