# DeFries–Fulker regression

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="DeFries–Fulker regression"}

In [behavioural genetics](wikipedia://en/24235330), **DeFries–Fulker (DF) regression**, also sometimes called **DeFries–Fulker extremes analysis**, is a type of [multiple regression analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_regression_analysis) designed for estimating the magnitude of genetic and environmental effects in [twin studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_studies).[^o2] It is named after [John C. DeFries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._DeFries) and [David Fulker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fulker), who first proposed it in 1985.[^o3] It was originally developed to assess [heritability](wikipedia://en/155624) of [reading disability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_disability) in twin studies, but it has since been used to assess the heritability of other cognitive traits, and has also been applied to non-twin methodologies.[^o12][^o4]

:::

## Approach {#Approach}

DeFries–Fulker regression analysis is based on the differences in the magnitude of [regression to the mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regression_to_the_mean) in a genetic trait between [monozygotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monozygotic_twins) (MZ) and [dizygotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizygotic_twins) (DZ) twins. In DF regression, the first step is to select [probands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proband) in a twin study with extreme scores on the trait being studied, and to regression to the mean among their co-twins depending on whether the probands are part of a MZ or DZ twin pair. DF regression is thus based on the assumption that, to the extent that genetic factors play a role in causing the extreme variation in the studied trait, the magnitude of regression to the mean should be greater in DZ co-twins of probands than in their MZ counterparts.[^o6] This should result in MZ co-twins being more similar to their probands on the trait under study than DZ twins are to theirs.[^o12]

### Modifications {#Modifications}

Since the DF regression approach was first proposed in 1985, other researchers have proposed modified versions of the DF model that take additional factors into account, including observed covariates[^o8] and opposite-sex DZ twins.[^o9]

## Regression analysis {#Regression_analysis}

The probands are chosen with scores that fall below a "cutoff" for what is considered "extreme", and regression is then used to predict the co-twin scores based on those of the probands and a term reflecting whether the twin pair is MZ (1.0) or DZ (0.5).[^o11] The formula used for DF regression is:

$$C = B_{1}P + B_{2}R + K$$

where *C* = expected co-twin score, *P* = proband score, *R* = coefficient of relationship (0.5 for DZ twins, 1.0 for MZ twins), and *K* = regression constant. *B*<sub>1</sub> represents a measure of co-twin relatedness separate from that of zygosity, while *B*<sub>2</sub> can be converted into an estimate of the [heritability](wikipedia://en/155624) of extreme scores on the trait.[^o6] By comparing MZ and DZ co-twins in this manner, an estimate of "group heritability" is generated.[^o12]
