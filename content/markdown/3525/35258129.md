# John Jay Gergen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Jay Gergen"}

**John Jay Gergen** (April 17, 1903 – January 16, 1967) was an American mathematician who introduced the Lebesgue–Gergen criterion for convergence of a Fourier series.[^o2]

:::

Gergen was born in [Saint Paul, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota), the son of Cora (Johnson) and John Andrew Gergen.[^o3] He received a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) from the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) in 1925 and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) in 1928. His doctoral advisors were [Griffith C. Evans](wikipedia://en/20086983) and [Szolem Mandelbrojt](wikipedia://en/1070245). From 1928 to 1930, as a National Research fellow, Gergen visited [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University), the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), and the [University of Clermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Clermont_Auvergne). From 1930 to 1933 he was a Benjamin Peirce Instructor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), and from 1933 to 1936 he was an assistant professor at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester). He was the chairman of the Department of Mathematics at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) from 1937 to July 1966. His doctoral students include [Walter Rudin](wikipedia://en/5491766). He married Aubigne Lermond during 1931, and had four sons: John Andrew Gergen, Stephen Lermond Gergen, presidential adviser and [Harvard Kennedy School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Kennedy_School) professor [David Richmond Gergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gergen), and [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College) psychology professor [Kenneth Jay Gergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_J._Gergen).
