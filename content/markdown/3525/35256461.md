# Catalan's triangle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Catalan's triangle"}

In [combinatorial mathematics](wikipedia://en/5170), **Catalan's triangle** is a [number triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_triangle) whose entries $C(n,k)$ give the number of strings consisting of *n* X's and *k* Y's such that no initial segment of the string has more Y's than X's. It is a generalization of the [Catalan numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_numbers), and is named after [Eugène Charles Catalan](wikipedia://en/639553). Bailey[^o2] shows that $C(n,k)$ satisfy the following properties:

:::

1. $$C(n,0)=1 \text{ for } n\geq 0.$$
1. $$C(n,1)=n \text{ for } n\geq 1.$$
1. $$C(n+1,k)=C(n+1,k-1)+C(n,k) \text{ for } 1<k<n+1$$
1. $$C(n+1,n+1)=C(n+1,n) \text{ for } n\geq 1.$$

Formula 3 shows that the entry in the triangle is obtained recursively by adding numbers to the left and above in the triangle. The earliest appearance of the Catalan triangle along with the recursion formula is in page 214 of the treatise on Calculus published in 1800[^o3] by [Louis François Antoine Arbogast](wikipedia://en/91156).

Shapiro[^o4] introduces another triangle which he calls the Catalan triangle that is distinct from the triangle being discussed here.

::: {#o6 .statement cue=named-heading name="General formula"}

## General formula {#General_formula}

The general formula for $C(n,k)$ is given by[^o2][^o7]

$$C(n,k)= \binom{n+k}{k} - \binom{n+k}{k-1}$$

So

$$C(n,k) = \frac{n-k+1}{n+1}\binom{n+k}{k}$$

When $k=n$, the diagonal $C(n, n)$ is the $n$-th [Catalan number](wikipedia://en/216488).

The row sum of the $n$-th row is the $(n + 1)$-th [Catalan number](wikipedia://en/216488), using the [hockey-stick identity](wikipedia://en/51935974) and an [alternative expression](wikipedia://en/216488#Properties) for Catalan numbers.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- Formula 3 from the first section can be used to prove both

$$C(n,k) = \sum_{i=0}^k C(n-1,i) = \sum_{i=k}^n C(i,k-1)$$

That is, an entry is the partial sum of the above row and also the partial sum of the column to the left (except for the entry on the diagonal).

- If $k>n$, then at some stage there must be more $Y$'s than $X$'s, so $C(n,k)=0$.
- A combinatorial interpretation of the $(n,k-1)$-th value is the number of non-decreasing partitions with exactly $n$ parts with maximum part $k$ such that each part is less than or equal to its index. So, for example, $(4,2) = 9$ counts

$$1111, 1112, 1113, 1122, 1123, 1133, 1222, 1223, 1233$$

## Generalization {#Generalization}

**Catalan's trapezoids** are a [countable set](wikipedia://en/6026) of number trapezoids which generalize Catalan’s triangle. Catalan's trapezoid of order $m = 1, 2, 3, ...$ is a number trapezoid whose entries $C_{m}(n,k)$ give the number of strings consisting of $n$ X-s and $k$ Y-s such that in every initial segment of the string the number of Y-s does not exceed the number of X-s by $m$ or more.[^o21] By definition, Catalan's trapezoid of order $m = 1$ is Catalan's triangle, i.e., $C_{1}(n,k)=C(n,k)$.

A general formula for $C_{m}(n,k)$ is given by

$$C_{m}(n,k)=\begin{cases} \left(\begin{array}{c} n+k\\ k \end{array}\right) & \,\,\,0\leq k<m\\ \\ \left(\begin{array}{c} n+k\\ k \end{array}\right)-\left(\begin{array}{c} n+k\\ k-m \end{array}\right) & \,\,\, m\leq k\leq n+m-1\\ \\ 0 & \,\,\, k>n+m-1 \end{cases}$$

( $n = 0, 1, 2, ...$, $k = 0, 1, 2, ...$, $m = 1, 2, 3, ...$).

## Proofs of the general formula {#Proofs_of_the_general_formula}

::: {#o17 .proof cue=heading name="Proof 1"}

### Proof 1 {#Proof_1}

This proof involves an extension of [Andre's reflection method](wikipedia://en/1043036#Bertrand's_and_André's_proofs) as used in the second proof for the [Catalan number](wikipedia://en/216488) to different diagonals. The following shows how every path from the bottom left $(0,0)$ to the top right $(k, n)$ of the diagram that crosses the constraint $n-k+m-1 = 0$ can also be reflected to the end point $(n + m, k - m)$.

*Image: General catalan number proof.png.*

We consider three cases to determine the number of paths from $(0,0)$ to $(k, n)$ that do not cross the constraint:

(1) when $m > k$ the constraint cannot be crossed, so all paths from $(0,0)$ to $(k, n)$ are valid, i.e. $C_{m}(n,k)=\left(\begin{array}{c} n+k\\ k \end{array}\right)$.

(2) when $k - m + 1 > n$ it is impossible to form a path that does not cross the constraint, i.e. $C_{m}(n,k)= 0$.

(3) when $m\leq k\leq n+m-1$, then $C_{m}(n,k)$ is the number of 'red' paths $\left(\begin{array}{c} n+k\\ k \end{array}\right)$ minus the number of 'yellow' paths that cross the constraint, i.e. $\left(\begin{array}{c} (n+m)+(k-m)\\ k-m \end{array}\right) = \left(\begin{array}{c} n+k\\ k-m \end{array}\right)$.

Therefore the number of paths from $(0,0)$ to $(k, n)$ that do not cross the constraint $n - k + m - 1 = 0$ is as indicated in the formula in the previous section "*Generalization*".

:::

::: {#o19 .proof cue=heading name="Proof 2"}

### Proof 2 {#Proof_2}

Firstly, we confirm the validity of the recurrence relation $C_{m}(n,k)=C_{m}(n-1,k)+C_{m}(n,k-1)$ by breaking down $C_{m}(n,k)$ into two parts, the first for XY combinations ending in X and the second for those ending in Y. The first group therefore has $C_{m}(n-1,k)$ valid combinations and the second has $C_{m}(n,k-1)$. Proof 2 is completed by verifying the solution satisfies the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806) and obeys initial conditions for $C_{m}(n,0)$ and $C_{m}(0,k)$.

:::
