# Nikolay Krylov (mathematician, born 1941)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nikolay Krylov"}

**Nicolai Vladimirovich Krylov** (Никола́й Влади́мирович Крыло́в; born 5 June 1941) is a Russian mathematician specializing in [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), particularly [stochastic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/12473239) and [diffusion processes](wikipedia://en/1667059). Krylov studied at [Lomonosov University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov_University), where he in 1966 under [E. B. Dynkin](wikipedia://en/1351540) attained a doctoral candidate title (similar to a PhD) and in 1973 a Russian doctoral degree (somewhat more prestigious than a PhD). He taught from 1966 to 1990 at the Lomonosov University and is since 1990 a professor at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota). At the beginning of his career (starting from 1963) he, in collaboration with Dynkin, worked on nonlinear stochastic [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039), making advances in the study of convex,[^o3] nonlinear partial equations of 2nd order (*i.e.* [Bellman](wikipedia://en/730173) equations), which were examined with stochastic methods. This led to the Evans-Krylov theory,[^o4] for which he received with [Lawrence C. Evans](wikipedia://en/2790063) in 2004 the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550)[^o5] of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (for work done simultaneously and independently by both Krylov and Evans). They proved the second order differentiability ([Hölder continuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hölder_continuity) of the second derivative) of the solutions of convex, completely nonlinear, second order elliptical partial differential equations and thus the existence of "classical solutions" (Theorem of Evans-Krylov). He was in 1978 at [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) and in 1986 at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California) an invited speaker for the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842). He received the Humboldt Research Award in 2001. In 1993 he was elected a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (1993). He should not be confused with the mathematician [Nikolay M. Krylov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Mitrofanovich_Krylov).

:::
