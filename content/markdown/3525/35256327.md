# Jacqueline Ferrand

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacqueline Ferrand"}

**Jacqueline Ferrand** (17 February 1918 – 26 April 2014) was a French mathematician who worked on [conformal](wikipedia://en/50627) [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342), and [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652). She taught at universities in Caen, Lille, and Paris.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Marie Odile Jacqueline Ferrand was born in [Alès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alès), in southern France. The daughter of a classics teacher, she attended the [lycée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée) in [Nîmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nîmes). Excelling in mathematics, she won the first prize in a national mathematics competition while there.[^o8]

In 1936 the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in Paris began admitting women, and she was one of the first to apply and be admitted to study mathematics and science. In 1939 she and [Roger Apéry](wikipedia://en/1421250) placed first in the mathematics [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation).[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

She began teaching at the [École normale supérieure de jeunes filles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_jeunes_filles), an elite teacher training college for girls in [Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sèvres), while continuing to do mathematics research under the supervision of [Arnaud Denjoy](wikipedia://en/1485520).[^o8] She published three papers in 1941 and defended a doctoral thesis in 1942.[^o7][^o8]

In June 1942, in connection with her doctorate (doctorat-es-sciences), she published two papers: "Étude de la correspondence entre les frontières dans la représentation conforme" and "Étude de la représentation conforme au voisinage de la frontière".[^o8] As a result, in 1943 she won the Girbal-Baral Prize of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences), and obtained a faculty position at the [University of Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bordeaux). She moved to the [University of Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Caen) in 1945 and was given a chair at the [University of Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lille) in 1948, which she held until 1956. After a sabbatical at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), she was then appointed a profession at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), becoming one of the first female full professors in the science faculty.[^o8] She retired in 1984.[^o7][^o8][^o11]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Ferrand undertook research in the areas of analysis and geometry, especially [conformal representation](wikipedia://en/50627) (from 1942), [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342) (from 1947), and from 1955 [Riemann manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) and [harmonic forms](wikipedia://en/501590).[^o8][^o13]

The thesis she defended in 1942, as well as later papers, addressed [conformal transformations](wikipedia://en/295907) in relation to a neighbouring [boundary point](wikipedia://en/72824). In subsequent papers, she developed a concept she called "preholomorphic functions" as the basis of a new methodology for [mathematical proofs](wikipedia://en/82285).[^o13][^o15]

Researching [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342), she was able to undertake work on theorems such as the lemmas of [Julia Wolf](wikipedia://en/58446958) and [Phragmén-Lindelöf](wikipedia://en/1377241) by generalising them to *n* dimensions. She went on to tackle the field's most difficult problems, once again on the basis of the new concepts she created.[^o13]

From 1958 to 1968, Ferrand was less active in the research field, devoting most of her time to raising her four children. She nevertheless prepared a number of university courses. Initially simply duplicated, as a result of their popularity they were later published as books. They included a course on differential geometry, published by Masson in 1983, as well as undergraduate courses published by [Armand Colin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_Colin) (1964) and Dunod (1967).[^o15]

In 1955, she turned from research to writing, producing a comprehensive treatise titled "Représentation conforme et transformations à intégrale de Dirichlet bornée" published in the Collections de Cahiers Scientifiques.[^o13]

Ferrand had nearly 100 mathematical publications, including ten books,[^o8] and was active in mathematical research into her late 70s.[^o7] One of her accomplishments, in 1971, was to prove the [compactness](wikipedia://en/750326) of the group of conformal mappings of a non-spherical compact [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652), resolving a conjecture of [André Lichnerowicz](wikipedia://en/2389188), and on the basis of this work she became an invited speaker at the 1974 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver).[^o7][^o11]

While Ferrand's work has significantly influenced various areas of mathematics, her research has not been widely recognised in France. As a result of international collaboration, she has nevertheless been appreciated abroad, especially in Finland where her work has enjoyed considerable interest.[^o15]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Ferrand married mathematician [Pierre Lelong](wikipedia://en/34789789) in 1947, taking his surname alongside hers in her subsequent publications[^o8] until their separation in 1977.[^o7][^o11] They had four children, born in 1949, 1951, 1952 and 1958.[^o8][^o15]

Ferrand died at her home on 26 April 2014 age 96.[^o16]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

In 2026, Jacqueline Ferrand was announced as one of 72 historical women in STEM whose names have been proposed to be added to the [72 men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_72_names_on_the_Eiffel_Tower) already celebrated on the [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower). The idea was conceived by a student and tour guide named Bernard Rigaud and then championed by [Nathalie Drach-Temam](wikipedia://en/83485079), the President of Sorbonne University.[^o18] The plan was announced by the Mayor of Paris, [Anne Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hidalgo) following the recommendations of a committee led by [Isabelle Vauglin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabelle_Vauglin) of [Femmes et Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femmes_et_Sciences) and Jean-François Martins, representing the operating company which runs the Eiffel Tower.[^o19][^o20][^o21][^o22]
