# Thomas Muirhead Flett

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Muirhead Flett"}

**Thomas Muirhead Flett** (28 July 1923 – 13 February 1976) was an English mathematician at [Sheffield University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield) working on [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).[^o5]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Thomas Muirhead Flett was born on 28 July 1923, in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), when his parents moved from [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland) to [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London). At age 11, he won a [scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship) by the County of [Middlesex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex) from a state primary school to [University College School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_School) (U.C.S.), Hampstead.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Flett was first employed as a laboratory assistant in the [Post Office Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_Office_Research_Station) at [Dollis Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollis_Hill), where the electronic computers used for code-breaking at [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037) were being assembled.[^o5][^o14] While working, he studied part-time at Acton Technical College and obtained first class Honours in the [London University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) B.Sc. general Degree in Mathematics and Physics. After working in the Post Office Research Station for three years, Flett worked as a research physicist at [Simmonds Aerocessories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simmonds_Aerocessories) in [Brentford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentford) for two years. In 1945, pursuing his interest in Mathematics at [Chelsea Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_College_of_Arts), he obtained class Honours in the B.Sc. Special Degree in Mathematics. In 1946, he obtained a mark in optional subjects, and in 1947, he was awarded for M.Sc. in Mathematics of London University.[^o5]

After having received his early degrees as a part-time student, Flett entered [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1947. Under the guidance of Prof. [John Edensor Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371), he completed a Ph.D. degree in mathematics with a 1950 thesis entitled *Some applications of estimates of exponential sums to the theory of functions*.[^o3][^o14]

In 1950, Flett joined [Liverpool University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool) as a lecturer. While there, he authored the undergraduate textbook *Mathematical Analysis: Theory, Methods, and Applications*.[^o14][^o7] In 1966, Flett spent a year at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in Seattle. In 1967, he joined the University of Sheffield as a professor of pure mathematics. Flett contracted [multiple myeloma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_myeloma)[^o14] and died in Sheffield on 13 February 1976 at the age of 52.[^o5][^o8]

At the time of his death, Flett had almost completed his second textbook *Differential Analysis: Differentiation, Differential Equations and Differential Inequalities*. It was edited and published by his colleague at Sheffield, Prof. John Sydney Pym.[^o14][^o9]

## Family {#Family}

In 1948, Flett married Joan Frances Ayers[^o14][^o11] (1923–2016).[^o12][^o13] They had two daughters.[^o14]

Tom and Joan Flett were avid Scottish dancers. In 1964, they co-authored *Traditional Dancing in Scotland*.[^o14] In 1996, Joan Flett updated their book as *Traditional Step-dancing in Scotland*.[^o15]
