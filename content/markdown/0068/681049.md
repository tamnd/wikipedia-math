# H-cobordism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="H-cobordism"}

In [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732) and [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), an (*n* + 1)-dimensional [cobordism](wikipedia://en/391832) *W* between *n*-dimensional [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* and *N* is an ***h*-cobordism** (the *h* stands for [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence)) if the inclusion maps

:::

$$M \hookrightarrow W \quad\mbox{and}\quad N \hookrightarrow W$$

are homotopy equivalences.

The ***h*-cobordism theorem** gives sufficient conditions for an *h*-cobordism to be trivial, i.e., to be **C**-isomorphic to the cylinder *M* × \[0, 1\]. Here **C** refers to any of the categories of **[smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold)**, **[piecewise linear](wikipedia://en/2174184)**, or **[topological](wikipedia://en/2119193)** manifolds.

The theorem was first proved by [Stephen Smale](wikipedia://en/40294) for which he received the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) and is a fundamental result in the theory of high-dimensional manifolds. For a start, it almost immediately proves the [generalized Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/8536216).

## Background {#Background}

Before Smale proved this theorem, mathematicians became stuck while trying to understand manifolds of dimension 3 or 4, and assumed that the higher-dimensional cases were even harder. The *h*-cobordism theorem showed that (simply connected) manifolds of dimension at least 5 are much easier than those of dimension 3 or 4. The proof of the theorem depends on the "[Whitney trick](wikipedia://en/477578)" of [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794), which geometrically untangles homologically untangled spheres of complementary dimension in a manifold of dimension >4. An informal reason why manifolds of dimension 3 or 4 are unusually hard is that [the trick fails to work](wikipedia://en/1362795#Failure_of_the_Whitney_trick_in_dimension_4) in lower dimensions, which have no room for entanglement.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Precise statement of the h-cobordism theorem"}

## Precise statement of the *h*-cobordism theorem {#Precise_statement_of_the_h-cobordism_theorem}

Let *n* be at least 5 and let *W* be a compact (*n* + 1)-dimensional *h*-cobordism between *M* and *N* in the category **C**=**[Diff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold)**, **[PL](wikipedia://en/2174184)**, or **[Top](wikipedia://en/2119193)** such that *W*, *M* and *N* are [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected). Then *W* is **C**-isomorphic to *M* × \[0, 1\]. The isomorphism can be chosen to be the identity on *M* × {0}.

This means that the homotopy equivalence between *M* and *N* (or, between *M* × \[0, 1\], *W* and *N* × \[0, 1\]) is homotopic to a **C**-isomorphism.

:::

## Lower dimensional versions {#Lower_dimensional_versions}

For *n* = 4, the topological *h*-cobordism theorem was proved by [Michael Freedman](wikipedia://en/658970). The smooth 4-dimensional *h*-cobordism theorem was disproved by [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305).

For *n* = 3, the *h*-cobordism theorem for smooth manifolds has not been proved and, due to the 3-dimensional [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798), is equivalent to the hard open question of whether the 4-sphere has non-standard ([exotic](wikipedia://en/1114931)) [smooth structures](wikipedia://en/1542238).

For *n* = 2, the *h*-cobordism theorem is equivalent to the Poincaré conjecture stated by [Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) in 1904 (one of the [Millennium Problems](wikipedia://en/27599234)[^o6]) and was proved by [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266) in a series of three papers in 2002 and 2003,[^o7][^o8][^o9] where he follows [Richard S. Hamilton](wikipedia://en/758911)'s program using [Ricci flow](wikipedia://en/288291).

For *n* = 1, the *h*-cobordism theorem is vacuously true, since there is no closed simply-connected 1-dimensional manifold.

For *n* = 0, the *h*-cobordism theorem is trivially true: the interval is the only connected cobordism between connected 0-manifolds.

::: {#o11 .proof cue=heading name="A proof sketch"}

## A proof sketch {#A_proof_sketch}

A [Morse function](wikipedia://en/391832#Morse_functions) $f:W\to[a,b]$ induces a [handle decomposition](wikipedia://en/902592) of *W*, i.e., if there is a single critical point of index *k* in $f^{-1}([c,c'])$, then the ascending cobordism $W_{c'}$ is obtained from $W_c$ by attaching a *k*-handle. The goal of the proof is to find a handle decomposition with no handles at all so that integrating the non-zero gradient vector field of *f* gives the desired diffeomorphism to the trivial cobordism.

This is achieved through a series of techniques.

**\1) Handle rearrangement**

First, we want to rearrange all handles by order so that lower order handles are attached first. The question is thus when can we slide an *i*-handle off of a *j*-handle? This can be done by a radial isotopy so long as the *i* attaching sphere and the *j* belt sphere do not intersect. We thus want $(i-1)+(n-j)\leq\dim\partial W-1=n-1$ which is equivalent to $i\leq j$.

We then define the handle chain complex $(C_*,\partial_*)$ by letting $C_k$ be the free abelian group on the *k*-handles and defining $\partial_k:C_k\to C_{k-1}$ by sending a *k*-handle $h_{\alpha}^k$ to $\sum_\beta \langle h_\alpha^k\mid h_\beta^{k-1}\rangle h_\beta^{k-1}$, where $\langle h_\alpha^k\mid h_\beta^{k-1}\rangle$ is the intersection number of the *k*-attaching sphere and the (*k* − 1)-belt sphere.

**\2) Handle cancellation**

Next, we want to "cancel" handles. The idea is that attaching a *k*-handle $h_\alpha^k$ might create a hole that can be filled in by attaching a (*k* + 1)-handle $h_\beta^{k+1}$. This would imply that $\partial_{k+1}h_\beta^{k+1}=\pm h_\alpha^k$ and so the $(\alpha,\beta)$ entry in the matrix of $\partial_{k+1}$ would be $\pm 1$. However, when is this condition sufficient? That is, when can we geometrically cancel handles if this condition is true? The answer lies in carefully analyzing when the manifold remains simply-connected after removing the attaching and belt spheres in question, and finding an embedded disk using the [Whitney trick](wikipedia://en/477578). This analysis leads to the requirement that *n* must be at least 5. Moreover, during the proof one requires that the cobordism has no 0-,1-,*n*-, or (*n* + 1)-handles which is obtained by the next technique.

**\3) Handle trading**

The idea of handle trading is to create a cancelling pair of (*k* + 1)- and (*k* + 2)-handles so that a given *k*-handle cancels with the (*k* + 1)-handle leaving behind the (*k* + 2)-handle. To do this, consider the core of the *k*-handle which is an element in $\pi_k(W,M)$. This group is trivial since *W* is an *h*-cobordism. Thus, there is a disk $D^{k+1}$ which we can fatten to a cancelling pair as desired, so long as we can embed this disk into the boundary of *W*. This embedding exists if $\dim\partial W-1=n-1\geq 2(k+1)$. Since we are assuming *n* is at least 5 this means that *k* is either 0 or 1. Finally, by considering the negative of the given Morse function, −*f*, we can turn the handle decomposition upside down and also remove the *n*- and (*n* + 1)-handles as desired.

**\4) Handle sliding**

Finally, we want to make sure that doing row and column operations on $\partial_k$ corresponds to a geometric operation. Indeed, it isn't hard to show (best done by drawing a picture) that sliding a *k*-handle $h_\alpha^k$ over another *k*-handle $h_{\beta}^k$ replaces $h_\alpha^k$ by $h_\alpha^k\pm h_\beta^k$ in the basis for $C_k$.

The proof of the theorem now follows: the handle [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) is exact since $H_*(W,M;\mathbb{Z})=0$. Thus $C_k\cong \operatorname{coker} \partial_{k+1}\oplus\operatorname{im} \partial_{k+1}$ since the $C_k$ are free. Then $\partial_k$, which is an [integer matrix](wikipedia://en/4500787), restricts to an invertible morphism which can thus be diagonalized via elementary row operations (handle sliding) and must have only $\pm 1$ on the diagonal because it is invertible. Thus, all handles are paired with a single other cancelling handle yielding a decomposition with no handles.

:::

## The *s*-cobordism theorem {#The_s-cobordism_theorem}

If the assumption that *M* and *N* are simply connected is dropped, *h*-cobordisms need not be cylinders; the obstruction is exactly the [Whitehead torsion](wikipedia://en/681186) τ (*W*, *M*) of the inclusion $M \hookrightarrow W$.

Precisely, the ***s*-cobordism theorem** (the *s* stands for [simple-homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/20897472)), proved independently by [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679), [John Stallings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stallings), and [Dennis Barden](wikipedia://en/7705698), states (assumptions as above but where *M* and *N* need not be simply connected):

An *h*-cobordism is a cylinder if and only if [Whitehead torsion](wikipedia://en/681186) τ (*W*, *M*) vanishes.

The torsion vanishes [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the inclusion $M \hookrightarrow W$ is not just a homotopy equivalence, but a [simple homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_homotopy_equivalence).

Note that one need not assume that the other inclusion $N \hookrightarrow W$ is also a simple homotopy equivalence—that follows from the theorem.

Categorically, *h*-cobordisms form a [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543).

Then a finer statement of the *s*-cobordism theorem is that the isomorphism classes of this groupoid (up to **C**-isomorphism of *h*-cobordisms) are [torsors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsor) for the respective[^o14] [Whitehead groups](wikipedia://en/681186) Wh(π), where $\pi \cong \pi_1(M) \cong \pi_1(W) \cong \pi_1(N).$

:::

## Wall theorems {#Wall_theorems}

[Wall's theorem on h-cobordisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall's_theorem_on_h-cobordisms) claims, that [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) smooth [4-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1362795) with isomorphic [intersection form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_form) are even [h-cobordant](wikipedia://en/681049).[^o17][^o18] [Wall's theorem on stabilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall's_theorem_on_stabilization) claims, that for h-cobordant simply connected closed smooth 4-manifolds $M$ and $N$, there exists a natural number $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) $M\#n(S^2\times S^2)\cong N\#n(S^2\times S^2)$.[^o19][^o20]

:::
