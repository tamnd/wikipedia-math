# Torus bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Torus bundle"}

A **torus bundle**, in the sub-field of [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), is a kind of [surface bundle over the circle](wikipedia://en/2011690), which in turn is a class of [three-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-manifold).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

To obtain a torus bundle: let $f$ be an [orientation](wikipedia://en/187446)-preserving [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) of the two-dimensional [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) $T$ to itself. Then the three-manifold $M(f)$ is obtained by

- taking the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of $T$ and the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) and
- gluing one component of the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of the resulting manifold to the other boundary component via the map $f$.

Then $M(f)$ is the torus bundle with [monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250) $f$.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For example, if $f$ is the identity map (i.e., the map which fixes every point of the torus) then the resulting torus bundle $M(f)$ is the [three-torus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-torus): the Cartesian product of three [circles](wikipedia://en/6220).

Seeing the possible kinds of torus bundles in more detail requires an understanding of [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190)'s [geometrization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurston's_geometrization_conjecture) program. Briefly, if $f$ is [finite order](wikipedia://en/249268), then the manifold $M(f)$ has [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417). If $f$ is a power of a [Dehn twist](wikipedia://en/765970) then $M(f)$ has [Nil geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nil_geometry). Finally, if $f$ is an [Anosov map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anosov_map) then the resulting three-manifold has [Sol geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sol_geometry).

These three cases exactly correspond to the three possibilities for the absolute value of the trace of the action of $f$ on the [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) of the torus: either less than two, equal to two, or greater than two.

:::
