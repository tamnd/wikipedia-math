# Compression (functional analysis)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Compression"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **compression** of a [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) *T* on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) to a [subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) *K* is the operator

:::

$$P_K T \vert_K : K \rightarrow K,$$

where $P_K : H \rightarrow K$ is the [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) onto *K*. This is a natural way to obtain an operator on *K* from an operator on the whole Hilbert space. If *K* is an [invariant subspace](wikipedia://en/585826) for *T*, then the compression of *T* to *K* is the [restricted](wikipedia://en/1589032) operator *K→K* sending *k* to *Tk*.

More generally, for a linear operator *T* on a Hilbert space $H$ and an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) *V* on a subspace $W$ of $H$, define the **compression** of *T* to $W$ by

$$T_W = V^*TV : W \rightarrow W,$$

where $V^*$ is the [adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175) of *V*. If *T* is a [self-adjoint operator](wikipedia://en/187408), then the compression $T_W$ is also self-adjoint. When *V* is replaced by the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516) $I: W \to H$, $V^* = I^*=P_K : H \to W$, and we acquire the special definition above.
