# Subbayya Sivasankaranarayana Pillai

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Subbayya Sivasankaranarayana Pillai"}

**Subbayya Sivasankaranarayana Pillai** (5 April 1901 – 31 August 1950) was an Indian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specialising in number theory. His contribution to [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569) was described in 1950 by [K. S. Chandrasekharan](wikipedia://en/22907213) as "almost certainly his best piece of work and one of the very best achievements in Indian Mathematics since [Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717)".[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Subbayya Sivasankaranarayana Pillai was born to parents Subbayya Pillai and Gomati Ammal. His mother died a year after his birth and his father when Pillai was in his last year at school.[^o3]

Pillai did his intermediate course and B.Sc. Mathematics in the [Scott Christian College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Christian_College) at [Nagercoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagercoil)[^o3] and managed to earn a B.A. degree from [Maharaja's college, Trivandrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Trivandrum).[^o5]

In 1927, Pillai was awarded a research fellowship at the [University of Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Madras) to work among professors [K. Ananda Rau](wikipedia://en/35226529) and [Ramaswamy S. Vaidyanathaswamy](wikipedia://en/11498709). He was from 1929 to 1941 at [Annamalai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annamalai_University) where he worked as a lecturer. It was in [Annamalai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annamalai_University) that he did his major work in [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569).[^o5] In 1941, he went to the [University of Travancore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Travancore) and a year later to the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta) as a lecturer (where he was at the invitation of [Friedrich Wilhelm Levi](wikipedia://en/3752306)).[^o6]

For his achievements he was invited in August 1950, for a year to visit the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), Princeton, United States. He was also invited to participate in the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) as a delegate of the [Madras University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras_University) but he died during the crash of [TWA Flight 903](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWA_Flight_903) in Egypt on the way to the conference.[^o7]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

He proved the [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569) for $k\ge 6$ in 1935[^o9] under the further condition of $(3^k + 1)/(2^k - 1)\le [1.5^k] + 1$ ahead of [Leonard Eugene Dickson](wikipedia://en/1790247) who around the same time proved it for $k\ge 7.$[^o10]

He showed that $g(k) = 2^k + l - 2$ where $l$ is the largest natural number $\le (3/2)^k$ and hence computed the precise value of $g(6) = 73$.[^o9]

The [Pillai sequence](wikipedia://en/56121084) 1, 4, 27, 1354, ..., is a quickly growing [integer sequence](wikipedia://en/51421) in which each term is the sum of the previous term and a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) whose following [prime gap](wikipedia://en/5075259) is larger than the previous term. It was studied by Pillai in connection with representing numbers as sums of prime numbers.
