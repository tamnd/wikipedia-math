# Helicoid

*Image: Helicoid.svg.* A helicoid with *α* = 1, −1 ≤ *ρ* ≤ 1 and −$\pi$ ≤ *θ* ≤ $\pi$.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helicoid"}

The **helicoid**, also known as **helical surface**, is a smooth [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_%28differential_geometry%29) embedded in [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853). It is the surface traced by an infinite line that is simultaneously being rotated and lifted along its [fixed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_around_a_fixed_axis) axis of rotation. It is the third [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734) to be known, after the [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) and the [catenoid](wikipedia://en/273329).

:::

## Description {#Description}

It was described by [Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler) in 1774 and by [Jean Baptiste Meusnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Baptiste_Meusnier) in 1776. Its [name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomenclature) derives from its similarity to the [helix](wikipedia://en/179924): for every [point](wikipedia://en/593693) on the helicoid, there is a helix contained in the helicoid which passes through that point.

The helicoid is also a [ruled surface](wikipedia://en/705158) (and a [right conoid](wikipedia://en/23756785)), meaning that it is a trace of a line. Alternatively, for any point on the surface, there is a line on the surface passing through it. Indeed, [Catalan](wikipedia://en/639553) proved in 1842 that the helicoid and the plane were the only ruled [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734).[^o3][^o4]

A helicoid is also a [translation surface](wikipedia://en/59182989) in the sense of differential geometry.

The helicoid and the [catenoid](wikipedia://en/273329) are parts of a family of helicoid-catenoid minimal surfaces.

The helicoid is shaped like [Archimedes screw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes_screw), but extends infinitely in all directions. It can be described by the following [parametric equations](wikipedia://en/576108) in [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates):

$$x = \rho \cos (\alpha \theta), \$$

$$y = \rho \sin (\alpha \theta), \$$

$$z = \theta, \$$

where $\rho$ and $\theta$ range from negative [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824) to [positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_number) infinity, while $\alpha$ is a constant. If $\alpha$ is positive, then the helicoid is right-handed as shown in the figure; if negative then left-handed.

The helicoid has [principal curvatures](wikipedia://en/2072472) $\pm \alpha /(1+ \alpha^2 \rho ^2) \$. The sum of these quantities gives the [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210) (zero since the helicoid is a minimal surface) and the product gives the [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623).

The helicoid is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to the plane $\mathbb{R}^2$. To see this, let $\alpha$ decrease [continuously](wikipedia://en/6122) from its given value down to [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28number%29). Each intermediate value of $\alpha$ will describe a different helicoid, until $\alpha = 0$ is reached and the helicoid becomes a vertical [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852).

Conversely, a plane can be turned into a helicoid by choosing a line, or *axis*, on the plane, then twisting the plane around that axis.

If a helicoid of radius $R$ revolves by an angle of $\theta$ around its axis while rising by a height $h$, the area of the surface is given by

$$\frac{\theta}{2} \left[R \sqrt{R^2+c^2}+c^2 \ln \left(\frac{R + \sqrt{R^2+c^2}} c\right) \right], \ c = \frac{h}{\theta}.$$
