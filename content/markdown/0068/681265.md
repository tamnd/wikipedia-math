# Hopf–Rinow theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hopf–Rinow theorem"}

The **Hopf–Rinow theorem** is a set of statements about the [geodesic completeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_manifold) of [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652). It is named after [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082) and his student [Willi Rinow](wikipedia://en/38412624), who published it in 1931.[^o2] [Stefan Cohn-Vossen](wikipedia://en/3204307) extended part of the Hopf–Rinow theorem to the context of certain types of [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $(M, g)$ be a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) and smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary. Then the following statements are equivalent:

1. The [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) and [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258) [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of $M$ are [compact](wikipedia://en/6042);
1. $M$ is a [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018);
1. $M$ is geodesically complete; that is, for every $p \in M,$ the [exponential map](wikipedia://en/276548) exp<sub>*p*</sub> is defined on the entire [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) $\mathrm{T}_p M.$

Furthermore, any one of the above implies that given any two points $p, q \in M,$ there exists a length-minimizing [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) connecting these two points (geodesics are in general [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424) for the [length](wikipedia://en/1898401) functional, and may or may not be minima).

In the Hopf–Rinow theorem, the first characterization of completeness deals purely with the topology of the manifold and the boundedness of various sets; the second deals with the existence of minimizers to a certain problem in the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) (namely minimization of the length functional); the third deals with the nature of solutions to a certain system of [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753).

:::

## Variations and generalizations {#Variations_and_generalizations}

- The Hopf–Rinow theorem is generalized to [length-metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Length-metric_space) the following way:
  - If a [length-metric space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Length-metric_space) is [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) and [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) then any two points can be connected by a [minimizing geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096), and any bounded [closed set](wikipedia://en/47279) is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042).

In fact these properties characterize completeness for locally compact length-metric spaces.

- The theorem does not hold for infinite-dimensional manifolds. The unit sphere in a [separable Hilbert space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_Hilbert_space) can be endowed with the structure of a [Hilbert manifold](wikipedia://en/2504412) in such a way that antipodal points cannot be joined by a length-minimizing geodesic. It was later observed that it is not even automatically true that two points are joined by any geodesic, whether minimizing or not.[^o6]
- The theorem also does not generalize to [Lorentzian manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold): the [Clifton–Pohl torus](wikipedia://en/34242725) provides an example (diffeomorphic to the two-dimensional torus) that is compact but not complete.
