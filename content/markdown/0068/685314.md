# Juliusz Schauder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Juliusz Schauder"}

**Juliusz Paweł Schauder** ('; 21 September 1899 – October 1943) was a Polish [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mathematicians) known for his work in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). A representative of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328), in 1923, he received his [doctoral degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_degree) from the [Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kazimierz_University) in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów). In 1943, during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he was murdered by Germans under unclear circumstances.[^o3]

:::

Concepts in mathematical literature named after him include [Schauder basis](wikipedia://en/3460662), [Schauder fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/638200), [Schauder estimates](wikipedia://en/23547303), [Banach–Schauder theorem](wikipedia://en/17395276), [Leray–Schauder degree](wikipedia://en/70573417), and [Faber-Schauder system](wikipedia://en/50905#Haar_system_on_the_unit_interval_and_related_systems).

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

### Early life and career {#Early_life_and_career}

Born on 21 September 1899 in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów), then part of the [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria), to Samuel, a lawyer father of Jewish descent, and mother Regina,[^o3] he was drafted into the [Austro-Hungarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army) right after his graduation from school and saw action on the [Italian front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_front_%28World_War_I%29).[^o6] He was captured and imprisoned in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy). In 1918, Schauder enlisted in the Polish [Blue Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Army_%28Poland%29) organized by general [Józef Haller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Haller) in France. The following year, he returned with Haller's army to his hometown of Lwów, which had been besieged by Ukrainians during the [Polish-Ukrainian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-Ukrainian_War).[^o7] The Ukrainian forces were subsequently repulsed by Poles.[^o8][^o9]

Although Schauder remained in military service in Lwów until the autumn of 1919, he began taking part in the activities of the Mathematical‑Physical Society of the [Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kazimierz_University) (now the [University of Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv), Ukraine) in March 1919, attending its meetings in the blue uniform of a sergeant in Haller’s army.[^o3] In the same year, he officially entered the Jan Kazimierz University. In 1923, he received his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) based on his dissertation entitled *The Theory of Surface Measure* written under [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735).[^o3] Apart from Steinhaus, his other notable teachers included [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646), [Stanisław Ruziewicz](wikipedia://en/1821290), and [Eustachy Żyliński](wikipedia://en/73170532).[^o3] In 1927, he obtained his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) based on his work *Zur Theorie stetiger Abbildngen in Funktionalräume*.[^o10]

He got no appointment at the university and continued his research while working as a teacher at secondary schools in Lwów. As one of his pupils, [Roman S. Ingarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Stanisław_Ingarden), noted, his students quickly realized that the German word "Schauder" means "shudder" and gave him that nickname, although he in fact gained a reputation as a kind and well-liked teacher.[^o13][^o11]

Due to his outstanding results, he obtained a [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation) scholarship[^o11] in 1932 that allowed him to spend several years in [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig) (1932–34), where he collaborated with [Leon Lichtenstein](wikipedia://en/31433458)[^o10] and, especially, Paris. In Paris, he worked with [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675) and started a very successful collaboration with [Jean Leray](wikipedia://en/245430). Upon returning to [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów) in 1934, he also collaborated with fellow Polish mathematician [Józef Marcinkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Marcinkiewicz).[^o12] In 1935, Schauder obtained the position of a senior assistant in the [Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kazimierz_University) and the same year went to [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) to attend an International Conference in Topology.[^o13] Schauder, along with [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162), was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1936 in Oslo.[^o14]

In 1938, Schauder was awarded, jointly with Leray, the inaugural Grand Prix Internationaux de Mathématiques Malaxa, established by [Nicolae Malaxa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Malaxa), for their 1934 paper *Topologie et équations fonctionelles* devoted to the application of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and theory of [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_spaces).[^o11] The international jury members who conferred the award included [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180), [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg), and [Gheorghe Țițeica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Țițeica).[^o3] In 1939, he received a nomination for the position of "extraordinary professor" at the Jan Kazimierz University.[^o10]

### World War II and death {#World_War_II_and_death}

When the Soviet troops took control of Lwów, the Jan Kazimierz University was transformed into the Ivan Franko University and Schauder became professor there as well as a member of the Ukrainian Academy of Sciences.[^o10] Schauder was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish), and after the [invasion of German troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) in Lwów 1941, it was impossible for him to continue his work. Even before the Lwów ghetto was established he wrote to [Ludwig Bieberbach](wikipedia://en/616186) pleading for his support. Instead, Bieberbach passed his letter to the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo) and Schauder was arrested. In his letters to Swiss mathematicians, he wrote that he had important new results, but no paper to write them down. He was executed by the Gestapo, probably in October 1943.[^o16]

After Schauder’s death, his wife Emilia (Löwenthal) and their daughter Ewa went into hiding, including in the sewers of Lwów. Emilia was captured by the Germans and died in the [Majdanek concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majdanek_concentration_camp) while his daughter survived the war and was taken care of by her uncle Marian who lived in Italy.[^o10]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Most of his mathematical work is in the field of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), being part of a large Polish group of mathematicians, i.e. the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328). They were pioneers in this area with wide applications in all parts of modern [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).

Schauder is best known for the [Schauder fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/638200), an extension of the [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/4101) to [locally convex topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/518844), which is a major tool to prove the existence of solutions in various problems. His other important result is the [Schauder basis](wikipedia://en/3460662), which is a generalization of an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) from [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932) to [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989). The [Leray−Schauder principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leray−Schauder_principle) is a way to establish solutions of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) from [a priori estimates](wikipedia://en/23547303).[^o10]

The [Banach–Schauder theorem](wikipedia://en/17395276) is a fundamental result in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) asserting that if a [bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) or [continuous linear operator](wikipedia://en/1802169) between [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) is [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) then it is an [open map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_map).

The [Schauder estimates](wikipedia://en/23547303) are a set of results established by Schauder concerning the regularity of solutions to linear, uniformly [elliptic](wikipedia://en/550137) [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). They assert that when both the equation’s coefficients and its solutions possess sufficient [smoothness](wikipedia://en/330206), the [Hölder norm](wikipedia://en/4005892) of the solution can be bounded in terms of the Hölder norms of the coefficient and source terms.[^o18][^o19]

## Remembrance {#Remembrance}

The Schauder Medal[^o21] is awarded by the J.P. Schauder Center for Nonlinear Studies at the [Nicolaus Copernicus University in Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus_University_in_Toruń), Poland, to individuals for their significant achievements related to [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) methods in [nonlinear analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_analysis).
