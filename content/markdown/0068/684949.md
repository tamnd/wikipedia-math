# Gérard de Vaucouleurs

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gérard de Vaucouleurs"}

**Gérard Henri de Vaucouleurs** (; 25 April 1918 – 7 October 1995)[^o4][^o5] was a French [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) best known for his studies of galaxies.[^o6]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Gérard de Vaucouleurs was born on April 25, 1918 in Paris, he took the maiden name of his mother as his last name.[^o8] He had an early interest in [amateur astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_astronomy) and received his undergraduate degree in 1939 at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) in that city.

After military service in World War II, he resumed his pursuit of astronomy. He was married to fellow astronomer [Antoinette de Vaucouleurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoinette_de_Vaucouleurs) on October 31, 1944, and the couple would frequently collaborate on astronomical research.[^o9]

He was fluent in English and spent 1949-51 in England and 1951–57 in Australia at [Mount Stromlo Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Stromlo_Observatory). He was at [Lowell Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowell_Observatory) in [Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona) from 1957-1958 and at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College_Observatory) from 1958-60.

In 1960 he was appointed to the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin), where he spent the rest of his career. He was one of the first five faculty in the newly formed astronomy department there.[^o6][^o9] His wife Antoinette died in 1987. In 1995 he died of a heart attack in his home in Austin at the age of 77.[^o5] At the time of his death he had a second wife named Elysabeth.[^o5][^o8]

## Research {#Research}

His earliest work had concerned the planet Mars and while at Harvard he used telescope observations from 1909 to 1958 to study the [areographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areography) coordinates of features on the surface of Mars.[^o11] His later work focused on the study of [galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy) and he co-authored the *Third Reference Catalogue of Bright Galaxies* with his wife Antoinette (1921-1987), a fellow UT Austin astronomer and lifelong collaborator.[^o9]

His specialty included reanalyzing Hubble and Sandage's galaxy atlas and recomputing the distance measurements utilizing a method of averaging many different kinds of metrics such as luminosity, the diameters of ring galaxies, brightest star clusters, etc., in a method he called "spreading the risks." During the 1950s he promoted the idea that galactic clusters are grouped into superclusters.[^o5]

The *[de Vaucouleurs modified Hubble sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy_morphological_classification)* is a widely used variant of the standard [Hubble sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_sequence).

De Vaucouleurs was awarded the [Henry Norris Russell Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Norris_Russell_Lectureship) by the [American Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Astronomical_Society) in 1988. He was awarded the [Prix Jules Janssen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Jules_Janssen) of the [Société astronomique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_astronomique_de_France) (Astronomical Society of France) in the same year. He and his wife and longtime collaborator, Antoinette, together produced 400 research and technical papers, 20 books and 100 articles for laymen.[^o5]
