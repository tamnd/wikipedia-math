# Cupola (geometry)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Cupola"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **cupola** (plural: **cupolae**) is a [solid](wikipedia://en/23470) formed by joining two [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621), one (the base) with twice as many [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) as the other, by an alternating band of [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428) and [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048). If the triangles are [equilateral](wikipedia://en/173285) and the rectangles are [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29), while the base and its opposite face are [regular polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polygons), the [triangular](wikipedia://en/1128465), [square](wikipedia://en/1128479), and [pentagonal](wikipedia://en/1131073) cupolae all count among the [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034), and can be formed by taking sections of the [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280), [rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213), and [rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/285486), respectively. Cupolae are a subclass of the [prismatoids](wikipedia://en/23917).

:::

A cupola can also be seen as a [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) where one of the polygons has been collapsed in half by merging alternate vertices.

A cupola can be given an extended [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\{n\} || t\{n\},$ representing a [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306) $\{n\}$ joined by a parallel of its [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429#Truncation_of_polygons), $t\{n\}$ or $\{2n\}.$

The dual of a cupola contains a shape that is sort of a weld between half of an $n$-sided [trapezohedron](wikipedia://en/719845) and a $2n$-sided [pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid).

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Tile 3464.svg.* Plane "[hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) cupolae" in the [rhombitrihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864311)

The triangular, square, and pentagonal cupolae are the only non-trivial convex cupolae with regular faces: The "[hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) cupola" is a plane figure, and the [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584) might be considered a "cupola" of degree 2 (the cupola of a line segment and a square). However, cupolae of higher-degree polygons may be constructed with [irregular](wikipedia://en/333306#Taxonomic_classification) triangular and rectangular faces.

## Coordinates of the vertices {#Coordinates_of_the_vertices}

*Image: Cupola\_40.png.* A tetracontagonal cupola has: blue yellow red and a bottom regular [octacontagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octacontagon) (hidden).

The definition of the cupola does not require the base (or the side opposite the base, which can be called the top) to be a regular polygon, but it is convenient to consider the case where the cupola has its maximal symmetry, $C_{nv}$. In that case, the top is a regular $n$-gon, while the base is either a regular $2n$-gon or a $2n$-gon which has two different side lengths alternating and the same angles as a regular $2n$-gon. It is convenient to fix the coordinate system so that the base lies in the $xy$-plane, with the top in a plane parallel to the $xy$-plane. The $z$-axis is the $n$-fold axis, and the mirror planes pass through the $z$-axis and bisect the sides of the base. They also either bisect the sides or the angles of the top polygon, or both. (If $n$ is even, half of the mirror planes bisect the sides of the top polygon and half bisect the angles, while if $n$ is odd, each mirror plane bisects one side and one angle of the top polygon.) The vertices of the base can be designated $V_1$ through $V_{2n},$ while the vertices of the top polygon can be designated $V_{2n+1}$ through $V_{3n}.$ With these conventions, the coordinates of the vertices can be written as:

$$\begin{array}{rllcc} V_{2j-1} :& \biggl( r_b \cos\left(\frac{2\pi(j-1)}{n} + \alpha\right), & r_b \sin\left(\frac{2\pi(j-1)}{n} + \alpha\right), & 0 \biggr) \\[2pt] V_{2j} :& \biggl( r_b \cos\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n} - \alpha\right), & r_b \sin\left(\frac{2\pi j}{n} - \alpha\right), & 0 \biggr) \\[2pt] V_{2n+j} :& \biggl( r_t \cos\frac{\pi j}{n}, & r_t \sin\frac{\pi j}{n}, & h \biggr) \end{array}$$

for $j = 1, 2, ..., n$.

Since the polygons $V_1 V_ 2 V_{2n+2} V_{2n+1},$ etc. are rectangles, this puts a constraint on the values of $r_b, r_t, \alpha.$ The distance $\bigl|V_1 V_2 \bigr|$ is equal to

$$\begin{aligned} & r_b \sqrt{ \left[\cos\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{n} - \alpha\right) - \cos \alpha\right]^2 + \left[\sin\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{n} - \alpha\right) - \sin\alpha\right]^2} \\[5pt] =\ & r_b \sqrt{ \left[\cos^2 \left(\tfrac{2\pi}{n} - \alpha\right) - 2\cos\left(\tfrac{2pi}{n} - \alpha\right)\cos\alpha + \cos^2 \alpha \right] + \left[\sin^2 \left(\tfrac{2\pi}{n} - \alpha\right) - 2\sin\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{n} - \alpha\right) \sin\alpha + \sin^2 \alpha \right] } \\[5pt] =\ & r_b \sqrt{ 2\left[1 - \cos\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{n} - \alpha\right) \cos\alpha - \sin\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{n} - \alpha\right)\sin\alpha \right]} \\[5pt] =\ & r_b \sqrt{ 2\left[1 - \cos\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{n} - 2\alpha\right)\right]} \end{aligned}$$

while the distance $\bigl| V_{2n+1}V_{2n+2} \bigr|$ is equal to

$$\begin{aligned} & r_t \sqrt{ \left[ \cos\tfrac{\pi}{n} - 1 \right]^2 + \sin^2 \tfrac{\pi}{n} } \\[5pt] =\ & r_t \sqrt{ \left[ \cos^2\tfrac{\pi}{n} - 2\cos\tfrac{\pi}{n} + 1 \right] + \sin^2\tfrac{\pi}{n} } \\[5pt] =\ & r_t \sqrt{2 \left[1 - \cos\tfrac{\pi}{n} \right]} \end{aligned}$$

These are to be equal, and if this common edge is denoted by $s$,

$$\begin{aligned} r_b &= \frac{s}{ \sqrt{2\left[1 - \cos\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{n} - 2\alpha \right) \right] }} \\[4pt] r_t &= \frac{s}{ \sqrt{2\left[1 - \cos\tfrac{\pi}{n} \right] }} \end{aligned}$$

These values are to be inserted into the expressions for the coordinates of the vertices given earlier.

## Star-cupolae {#Star-cupolae}

Star cupolae exist for any top base $\{n/d\}$ where $6/5 < n/d < 6$ and $d$ is odd. At these limits, the cupolae collapse into plane figures. Beyond these limits, the triangles and squares can no longer span the distance between the two base polygons (it can still be made with non-equilateral [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428) and non-square rectangles). If $d$ is even, the bottom base $\{2n/d\}$ becomes degenerate; then we can form a *cupoloid* or *semicupola* by withdrawing this degenerate face and letting the triangles and squares connect to each other here (through single edges) rather than to the late bottom base (through its double edges). In particular, the [tetrahemihexahedron](wikipedia://en/2851834) may be seen as a $\{3/2\}$-cupoloid.

The cupolae are all [orientable](wikipedia://en/187446), while the cupoloids are all non-orientable. For a cupoloid, if $n/d > 2$, then the triangles and squares do not cover the entire (single) base, and a small membrane is placed in this base $\{n/d\}$-gon that simply covers empty space. Hence the $\{5/2\}$- and $\{7/2\}$-cupoloids pictured above have membranes (not filled in), while the $\{5/4\}$- and $\{7/4\}$-cupoloids pictured above do not.

The height $h$ of an $\{n/d\}$-cupola or cupoloid is given by the formula:

$$h = \sqrt{1 - \frac{1}{4 \sin^{2} \left( \frac{\pi d}{n} \right)}}.$$

In particular, $h = 0$ at the limits $n/d = 6$ and $n/d = 6/5$, and $h$ is maximized at $n/d = 2$ (in the [digonal cupola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digonal_cupola): the triangular prism, where the triangles are upright).[^o8][^o9]

In the images above, the star cupolae have been given a consistent colour scheme to aid identifying their faces: the base $\{n/d\}$-gon is red, the base $\{2n/d\}$-gon is yellow, the squares are blue, and the triangles are green. The cupoloids have the base $\{n/d\}$-gon red, the squares yellow, and the triangles blue, as the base $\{2n/d\}$-gon has been withdrawn.

:::

## Hypercupolae {#Hypercupolae}

The **hypercupolae** or **polyhedral cupolae** are a family of convex nonuniform [polychora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychora) (here four-dimensional figures), analogous to the cupolas. Each one's bases are a [Platonic solid](wikipedia://en/23905) and its [expansion](wikipedia://en/6341070).[^o11]
