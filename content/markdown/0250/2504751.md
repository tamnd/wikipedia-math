# CertCo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CertCo"}

**CertCo, Inc.**, was a [financial cryptography](wikipedia://en/222858) [startup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company) spun out of [Bankers Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankers_Trust) in the 1990s. The company pioneered a risk management approach to cryptographic services. It had offices in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) and [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts), [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts). It offered three main [public key infrastructure](wikipedia://en/194112) (PKI) based products: an Identity Warranty system (tracking and insuring reliance on identity assertions in financial transactions); an electronic payment system (internally known as Acquire); and an [Online Certificate Status Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Certificate_Status_Protocol) (OCSP) responder for validating [X.509](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X.509) [public key certificates](wikipedia://en/223796). It went out of business in Spring 2002 never having found a wide [market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_%28economics%29) for its products despite filing a number of patents and developing new technology.

:::

## History {#History}

### Early history {#Early_history}

CertCo was founded in March 1994 by Frank Sudia and Peter Freund as an internal bank department known as BT Electronic Commerce (BTEC). It spun out in November 1996 as CertCo with a number of outside strategic and financial investors in a transaction managed by [Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs).[^o5]

Some of its better known early employees included Rich Ankney, Ed Appel, Alan Asay, Ernest Brickell, David Kravitz (inventor of the [Digital Signature Algorithm](wikipedia://en/59470)), Yair Frankel, [Dan Geer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Geer), C.T. Montgomery, Jay Simmons, Nanette Di Tosto, Paul Turner, Mark Jefferson and [Moti Yung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moti_Yung).

Early on it licensed the "Fair Cryptosystem" [key escrow](wikipedia://en/17114) patents of [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) Professor [Silvio Micali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Micali) and announced plans to implement a "Commercial Key Escrow System". Thereafter the policy climate for [key escrow](wikipedia://en/17114) turned negative, market interest waned, and the system was never built.[^o6][^o7][^o8]

### Vision {#Vision}

CertCo and [Bankers Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankers_Trust) promoted the creation of a bank consortium to serve as a PKI [certificate authority](wikipedia://en/296370) for global commerce, leading to the 1999 launch of Identrus, later renamed [Identrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identrust). The banks, however, declined to license CertCo's technology, opting instead for a vendor-neutral approach. Unlike the vendor-neutral approach, Certco promoted a risk management approach to PKI with transaction level insurance, and pioneered novel visionary approach to authentication in the financial sector: First, a distributed proactively secured [certificate authority](wikipedia://en/296370) was designed and built (had it become a standard, it would have avoided a single control point over certificate authorities, and would have avoided coercion by that control point, and would have been further used to prevent attacks on the trust infrastructure, like the one on [DigiNotar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DigiNotar)). Secondly, strong authentication of clients employing PKI and digital signatures was promoted, and if it had been widely used this would have reduced the effect of [Phishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phishing) attacks, also envisioned as a possible threat to financial transactions).[^o10][^o11] Currently, practice employing [U2F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2F) devices employs such strong authentication measures at the user side.

CertCo's most notable commercial customer was SETCo,[^o12] the operating company for the [Visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_%28company%29)-[MasterCard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MasterCard) [Secure electronic transaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_electronic_transaction) credit card security protocol, to which it provided [certificate authority](wikipedia://en/296370) technology, which was the first implementation of distributed [Threshold cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threshold_cryptography) based signing.[^o13] Currently, Threshold Cryptography is widely employed, say in the [Cryptocurrency exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_exchange) ecosystem.[^o14]

### Business failure {#Business_failure}

Despite developing new technology, CertCo did not find a wide [market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_%28economics%29) for its products, and went out of business in Spring 2002, following substantial [reductions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layoff) in technical staff in November and December 2001, due, partially, to unavailability of investors after the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks).

## Technical contributions {#Technical_contributions}

CertCo made various contributions to the fields of [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) and [public key infrastructure](wikipedia://en/194112) via scientific publications and patents. Its most heavily cited patents by subject are:

- 5659616 Attribute certificates
- 5825880 [Certificate authority](wikipedia://en/296370)
- 5995625 [Digital rights management](wikipedia://en/18938226)
- 5903882 Identity warranty
- 5799086 [Key escrow](wikipedia://en/17114)
- 6029150 Payment system

Other patent filings include

- 6411716 Changing key fragments in a digital signature system
- 8321348B2 Computer-based method and system for aiding transactions

## Standards and policy {#Standards_and_policy}

CertCo personnel contributed to a number of standards bodies and policy projects, including:

- [IETF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IETF) [Online Certificate Status Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Certificate_Status_Protocol) [OCSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OCSP)
- [American Bar Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bar_Association) Digital Signature Guidelines\][^o18]
- [ANSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANSI) X9.30 The Digital Signature Algorithm (DSA)[^o19]
- [ANSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANSI) X9.31 Reversible Public Key Cryptography (rDSA) (better known as [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29))[^o20]
- [ANSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANSI) X9.45 Attribute Certificates[^o21]
- [ANSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANSI) X9.57 Certificate Management for Financial Services[^o22]
