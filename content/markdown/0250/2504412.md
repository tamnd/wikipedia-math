# Hilbert manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hilbert manifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Hilbert manifold** is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) modeled on [Hilbert spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_spaces). Thus it is a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) in which each point has a neighbourhood [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to an infinite dimensional Hilbert space. The concept of a Hilbert manifold provides a possibility of extending the theory of manifolds to infinite-dimensional setting. Analogous to the finite-dimensional situation, one can define a *differentiable* Hilbert manifold by considering a maximal atlas in which the transition maps are differentiable.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Many basic constructions of manifold theory, such as the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) of a manifold and a [tubular neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubular_neighbourhood) of a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) (of finite codimension), carry over from the finite-dimensional situation to the Hilbert setting with little change. However, in statements involving maps between manifolds one often has to restrict consideration to *Fredholm maps*, that is, maps whose differential at every point is [Fredholm](wikipedia://en/279722). The reason for this is that [Sard's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sard's_lemma) holds for Fredholm maps, but not in general. Notwithstanding this difference, Hilbert manifolds have several very nice properties:

- **[Kuiper's theorem](wikipedia://en/2993692)**: If $X$ is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) or has the [homotopy type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_type) of a [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022), then every (real or complex) Hilbert space [bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) over $X$ is trivial. In particular, every Hilbert manifold is [parallelizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallelizable).
- Every smooth Hilbert manifold can be smoothly embedded onto an open subset of the model Hilbert space.
- Every [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence) between two Hilbert manifolds is homotopic to a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564). In particular every two homotopy equivalent Hilbert manifolds are already diffeomorphic. This stands in contrast to [lens spaces](wikipedia://en/885725) and [exotic spheres](wikipedia://en/1114931), which demonstrate that in the finite-dimensional situation, homotopy equivalence, homeomorphism, and diffeomorphism of manifolds are distinct properties.
- Although Sard’s Theorem does not hold in general, every continuous map $f \colon X \to \mathbb{R}^n$ from a Hilbert manifold can be arbitrarily closely approximated by a smooth map $g \colon X \to \mathbb{R}^n$ that has no [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424).

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any Hilbert space $H$ is a Hilbert manifold with a single global chart given by the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069) on $H.$ Moreover, since $H$ is a vector space, the tangent space $\mathrm{T}_p H$ to $H$ at any point $p \in H$ is canonically isomorphic to $H$ itself, and so has a natural inner product, the “same” as the one on $H.$ Thus $H$ can be given the structure of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) with metric $$g(v, w)(p) := \langle v, w \rangle_H \text{ for } v, w \in \mathrm{T}_p H,$$ where $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_H$ denotes the inner product in $H.$
- Similarly, any [open subset](wikipedia://en/39358) of a Hilbert space is a Hilbert manifold and a Riemannian manifold under the same construction as for the whole space.
- There are several [mapping spaces](wikipedia://en/340630) between manifolds which can be viewed as Hilbert spaces by only considering maps of suitable [Sobolev class](wikipedia://en/611964). For example we can consider the space $\operatorname{L}(M)$ of all $H^1$ maps from the unit circle $S^1$ into a manifold $M.$ This can be topologized via the [compact open topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_open_topology) as a subspace of the space of all continuous mappings from the circle to $M,$ that is, the [free loop space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_loop_space) of $M.$ The Sobolev kind mapping space $\operatorname{L}(M)$ described above is homotopy equivalent to the free loop space. This makes it suited to the study of algebraic topology of the free loop space, especially in the field of [string topology](wikipedia://en/28725806). We can do an analogous Sobolev construction for the [loop space](wikipedia://en/2174332), making it a [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149)-$d$ Hilbert submanifold of $\operatorname{L}(M),$ where $d$ is the dimension of $M.$
