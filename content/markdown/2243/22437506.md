# Group family

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Group family"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), especially as it is used in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **group family** of [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) is one obtained by subjecting a random variable with a fixed distribution to a suitable transformation, such as a [location–scale family](wikipedia://en/2680667), or otherwise one of probability distributions [acted upon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) by a [group](wikipedia://en/19447).[^o2] Considering a family of distributions as a group family can, in [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579), lead to identifying [ancillary statistics](wikipedia://en/670398).[^o3]

:::

## Types {#Types}

A group family can be generated by subjecting a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with a fixed distribution to some suitable [transformations](wikipedia://en/1904373).[^o2] Different types of group families are as follows :

### Location {#Location}

This family is obtained by adding a constant to a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685). Let $X$ be a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) and $a \in R$ be a constant. Let $Y = X + a$ . Then

$$F_Y(y) = P(Y\leq y) = P(X+a \leq y) = P(X \leq y-a) = F_X(y-a)$$

For a fixed distribution, as $a$ varies from $-\infty$ to $\infty$, the distributions that we obtain constitute the location family.

### Scale {#Scale}

This family is obtained by multiplying a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with a constant. Let $X$ be a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) and $c \in R^+$ be a constant. Let $Y = cX$ . Then

$$F_Y(y) = P(Y\leq y) = P(cX \leq y) = P(X \leq y/c) = F_X(y/c)$$

### Location–scale {#Location–scale}

This family is obtained by multiplying a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with a constant and then adding some other constant to it. Let $X$ be a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685), $a \in R$ and $c \in R^+$be constants. Let $Y = cX + a$. Then

$$F_Y(y) = P(Y\leq y) = P(cX+a \leq y) = P(X \leq (y-a)/c) = F_X((y-a)/c)$$

Note that it is important that $a \in R$ and $c \in R^+$ in order to satisfy the properties mentioned in the following section.

## Transformation {#Transformation}

The [transformation](wikipedia://en/1904373) applied to the random variable must satisfy the properties of closure under composition and inversion.[^o2]
