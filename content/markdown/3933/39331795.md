# Linear Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Linear Lie algebra"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), a **linear Lie algebra** is a subalgebra $\mathfrak{g}$ of the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak{gl}(V)$ consisting of [endomorphisms](wikipedia://en/9569) of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) *V*. In other words, a linear Lie algebra is the image of a [Lie algebra representation](wikipedia://en/318712).

:::

Any Lie algebra is a linear Lie algebra in the sense that there is always a faithful representation of $\mathfrak{g}$ (in fact, on a [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) vector space by [Ado's theorem](wikipedia://en/3422249) if $\mathfrak{g}$ is itself finite-dimensional.)

Let *V* be a finite-dimensional vector space over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero and $\mathfrak{g}$ a subalgebra of $\mathfrak{gl}(V)$. Then *V* is semisimple as a module over $\mathfrak{g}$ if and only if (i) it is a direct sum of the center and a semisimple ideal and (ii) the elements of the center are [diagonalizable](wikipedia://en/246325) (over some [extension field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extension_field)).[^o2]
