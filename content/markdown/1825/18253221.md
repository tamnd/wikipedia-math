# Brun–Titchmarsh theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brun–Titchmarsh theorem"}

In [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), the **Brun–Titchmarsh theorem**, named after [Viggo Brun](wikipedia://en/40647) and [Edward Charles Titchmarsh](wikipedia://en/1709459), is an [upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) on the distribution of [prime numbers in arithmetic progression](wikipedia://en/11740178).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $\pi(x;q,a)$ count the number of primes *p* congruent to *a* modulo *q* with *p* ≤ *x*. Then

$$\pi(x;q,a) \le {2x \over \varphi(q)\log(x/q)}$$

for all *q* \< *x*.

:::

## History {#History}

The result was proven by [sieve methods](wikipedia://en/1035915) by Montgomery and Vaughan; an earlier result of Brun and Titchmarsh obtained a weaker version of this inequality with an additional multiplicative factor of $1+o(1)$.

## Improvements {#Improvements}

If *q* is relatively small, e.g., $q\le x^{9/20}$, then there exists a better bound:

$$\pi(x;q,a)\le{(2+o(1))x\over\varphi(q)\log(x/q^{3/8})}$$

This is due to Y. Motohashi (1973). He used a bilinear structure in the error term in the [Selberg sieve](wikipedia://en/18739942), discovered by himself. Later this idea of exploiting structures in sieving errors developed into a major method in Analytic Number Theory, due to [H. Iwaniec](wikipedia://en/9338319)'s extension to combinatorial sieve.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Comparison with Dirichlet's theorem"}

## Comparison with Dirichlet's theorem {#Comparison_with_Dirichlet's_theorem}

By contrast, [Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/101453) gives an asymptotic result, which may be expressed in the form

$$\pi(x;q,a) = \frac{x}{\varphi(q)\log(x)} \left({1 + O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right)}\right)$$

but this can only be proved to hold for the more restricted range *q* \< (log *x*)<sup>*c*</sup> for constant *c*: this is the [Siegel–Walfisz theorem](wikipedia://en/5075154).

:::
