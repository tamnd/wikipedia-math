# Rudolf Ahlswede

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rudolf Ahlswede"}

*Image: RudolfAhlswede.jpg.* Rudolf Ahlswede

**Rudolf F. Ahlswede** (15 September 1938 – 18 December 2010) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Born in [Dielmissen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dielmissen), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), he studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy). He wrote his Ph.D. thesis in 1966, at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), with the topic "Contributions to the [Shannon information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_information_theory) in case of non-stationary channels". He dedicated himself in his further career to [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773) and became one of the leading representatives of this area worldwide.

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

In 1977, he joined and held a Professorship at the [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University), Bielefeld, Germany. In 1988, he received together with [Imre Csiszár](wikipedia://en/13008077) the Best Paper Award of the IEEE Information Theory Society for work in the area of the hypothesis testing as well as in 1990 together with Gunter Dueck for a new theory of message identification. He has been awarded this prize twice. As an emeritus of Bielefeld University, Ahlswede received the 2006 [Claude E. Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_E._Shannon) [Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_E._Shannon_Award), one of the first few non-US citizens to receive it. Ahlswede's work began the field of [Network coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_coding).[^o3]

Rudolf Ahlswede died on 18 December 2010, at the age of 72.
