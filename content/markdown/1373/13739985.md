# Lehmer's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lehmer's conjecture"}

**Lehmer's conjecture**, also known as the **Lehmer's Mahler measure problem,** is a problem in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) raised by [Derrick Henry Lehmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Henry_Lehmer).[^o2] The conjecture asserts that there is an absolute constant $\mu>1$ such that every [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) with integer coefficients $P(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ satisfies one of the following properties:

:::

- The [Mahler measure](wikipedia://en/1088425)[^o3] $\mathcal{M}(P(x))$ of $P(x)$ is greater than or equal to $\mu$.
- $P(x)$ is an integral multiple of a product of cyclotomic polynomials or the monomial $x$, in which case $\mathcal{M}(P(x))=1$. (Equivalently, every complex root of $P(x)$ is a root of unity or zero.)

There are a number of definitions of the Mahler measure, one of which is to factor $P(x)$ over $\mathbb{C}$ as

$$P(x)=a_0 (x-\alpha_1)(x-\alpha_2)\cdots(x-\alpha_D),$$

and then set

$$\mathcal{M}(P(x)) = |a_0| \prod_{i=1}^{D} \max(1,|\alpha_i|).$$

The smallest known Mahler measure (greater than 1) is for "Lehmer's polynomial"

$$P(x)= x^{10}+x^9-x^7-x^6-x^5-x^4-x^3+x+1 \,,$$

for which the Mahler measure is the [Salem number](wikipedia://en/1066694)[^o4]

$$\mathcal{M}(P(x))=1.176280818\dots \ .$$

It is widely believed that this example represents the true minimal value: that is, $\mu=1.176280818\dots$ in Lehmer's conjecture.[^o5][^o6]

## Motivation {#Motivation}

Consider Mahler measure for one variable and [Jensen's formula](wikipedia://en/6054639) shows that if $P(x)=a_0 (x-\alpha_1)(x-\alpha_2)\cdots(x-\alpha_D)$ then

$$\mathcal{M}(P(x)) = |a_0| \prod_{i=1}^{D} \max(1,|\alpha_i|).$$

In this paragraph denote $m(P)=\log(\mathcal{M}(P(x))$ , which is also called [Mahler measure](wikipedia://en/1088425).

If $P$ has integer coefficients, this shows that $\mathcal{M}(P)$ is an [algebraic number](wikipedia://en/1158) so $m(P)$ is the logarithm of an [algebraic integer](wikipedia://en/98981). It also shows that $m(P)\ge 0$ and that if $m(P)=0$ then $P$ is a product of [cyclotomic polynomials](wikipedia://en/171992) i.e. monic polynomials whose all roots are roots of unity, or a monomial polynomial of $x$ i.e. a power $x^n$ for some $n$ .

Lehmer noticed[^o2][^o8] that $m(P)=0$ is an important value in the study of the integer sequences $\Delta_n=\text{Res}(P(x), x^n-1)=\prod^D_{i=1}(\alpha_i^n-1)$ for monic $P$ . If $P$ does not vanish on the circle then $\lim|\Delta_n|^{1/n}=\mathcal{M}(P)$. If $P$ does vanish on the circle but not at any root of unity, then the same convergence holds by [Baker's theorem](wikipedia://en/28751817) (in fact an earlier result of [Gelfond](wikipedia://en/4542389) is sufficient for this, as pointed out by Lind in connection with his study of quasihyperbolic toral automorphisms[^o9]).[^o10] As a result, Lehmer was led to ask

whether there is a constant $c>0$ such that $m(P)>c$ provided $P$ is not cyclotomic?,

or

given $c>0$, are there $P$ with integer coefficients for which $0<m(P)<c$?

Some positive answers have been provided as follows, but Lehmer's conjecture is not yet completely proved and is still a question of much interest.

## Partial results {#Partial_results}

Let $P(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$ be an irreducible [monic polynomial](wikipedia://en/314730) of degree $D$.

Smyth[^o12] proved that Lehmer's conjecture is true for all polynomials that are not [reciprocal](wikipedia://en/1106042), i.e., all polynomials satisfying $x^DP(x^{-1})\ne P(x)$.

Blanksby and [Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Montgomery_%28mathematician%29)[^o13] and Stewart[^o14] independently proved that there is an absolute constant $C>1$ such that either $\mathcal{M}(P(x))=1$ or[^o15]

$$\log\mathcal{M}(P(x))\ge \frac{C}{D\log D}.$$

Dobrowolski[^o16] improved this to

$$\log\mathcal{M}(P(x))\ge C\left(\frac{\log\log D}{\log D}\right)^3.$$

Dobrowolski obtained the value *C* ≥ 1/1200 and asymptotically C > 1-ε for all sufficiently large *D*. Voutier in 1996 obtained *C* ≥ 1/4 for *D* ≥ 2.[^o17]

## Elliptic analogues {#Elliptic_analogues}

Let $E/K$ be an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) defined over a number field $K$, and let $\hat{h}_E:E(\bar{K})\to\mathbb{R}$ be the [canonical height](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_height) function. The canonical height is the analogue for elliptic curves of the function $(\deg P)^{-1}\log\mathcal{M}(P(x))$. It has the property that $\hat{h}_E(Q)=0$ if and only if $Q$ is a [torsion point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_point) in $E(\bar{K})$. The **elliptic Lehmer conjecture** asserts that there is a constant $C(E/K)>0$ such that

$\hat{h}_E(Q) \ge \frac{C(E/K)}{D}$ for all non-torsion points $Q\in E(\bar{K})$,

where $D=[K(Q):K]$. If the elliptic curve *E* has [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183), then the analogue of Dobrowolski's result holds:

$$\hat{h}_E(Q) \ge \frac{C(E/K)}{D} \left(\frac{\log\log D}{\log D}\right)^3 ,$$

due to Laurent.[^o20] For arbitrary elliptic curves, the best known result is

$$\hat{h}_E(Q) \ge \frac{C(E/K)}{D^3(\log D)^2},$$

due to [Masser](wikipedia://en/2172777).[^o21] For elliptic curves with non-integral [j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988), this has been improved to

$$\hat{h}_E(Q) \ge \frac{C(E/K)}{D^2(\log D)^2},$$

by Hindry and [Silverman](wikipedia://en/2288703).[^o22]

:::

## Restricted results {#Restricted_results}

Stronger results are known for restricted classes of polynomials or algebraic numbers.

If *P*(*x*) is not reciprocal then

$$M(P) \ge M(x^3 - x - 1) \approx 1.3247$$

and this is clearly best possible.[^o24] If further all the coefficients of *P* are odd then[^o25]

$$M(P) \ge M(x^2 -x - 1) \approx 1.618 .$$

For any algebraic number *α*, let $M(\alpha)$ be the Mahler measure of the minimal polynomial $P_\alpha$ of *α*. If the field **Q**(*α*) is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of **Q**, then Lehmer's conjecture holds for $P_\alpha$.[^o25]

## Relation to structure of compact group automorphisms {#Relation_to_structure_of_compact_group_automorphisms}

The [measure-theoretic entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure-theoretic_entropy) of an ergodic [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) of a compact metrizable [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) is known to be given by the [logarithmic Mahler measure](wikipedia://en/1088425) of a polynomial with integer coefficients if it is finite.[^o27] As pointed out by Lind, this means that the set of possible values of the entropy of such actions is either all of $(0,\infty]$ or a countable set depending on the solution to Lehmer's problem.[^o28] Lind also showed that the infinite-dimensional torus either has [ergodic](wikipedia://en/258986) automorphisms of finite positive entropy or only has automorphisms of infinite entropy depending on the solution to Lehmer's problem. Since an ergodic compact group automorphism is [measurably isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable) to a [Bernoulli shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_shift), and the Bernoulli shifts are classified up to measurable isomorphism by their entropy by [Ornstein's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornstein's_theorem), this means that the moduli space of all ergodic compact group automorphisms up to measurable isomorphism is either countable or uncountable depending on the solution to Lehmer's problem.
