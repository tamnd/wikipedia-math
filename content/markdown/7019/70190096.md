# Price-Jones curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Price-Jones curve"}

A **Price-Jones curve** is a graph showing the [distribution](wikipedia://en/13266) of diameters of [red blood cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_blood_cells). Higher diameter may be seen in [pernicious anaemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernicious_anemia), while lower diameter may be seen after [haemorrhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haemorrhage).

:::

## Medical uses {#Medical_uses}

A Price-Jones curve can be used in the diagnosis of [anaemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemia).[^o3][^o4] Price-Jones curves usually vary both by average red blood cell size, and the distribution of sizes.[^o5]

### Interpretation of results {#Interpretation_of_results}

Higher red blood cell diameter and wider variation in size are often seen in [pernicious anaemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernicious_anaemia).[^o5] Lower diameter with normal variation in size are often seen after [haemorrhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haemorrhage).[^o5] A higher variation in size is known as [anisocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisocytosis).[^o7][^o8]

::: {#o10 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Procedure"}

## Procedure {#Procedure}

A [blood smear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_smear) can be used to view individual [red blood cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_blood_cell). The diameter of each red blood cell can be measured, which is usually analogous to volume.[^o4] This is usually performed automatically by particle counters.[^o4][^o7] Data is then converted into a [histogram](wikipedia://en/13266).[^o3] This can be used to assess [red blood cell distribution width](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_blood_cell_distribution_width) (RDW).[^o3][^o7]

:::

## History {#History}

Cecil Price-Jones first proposed using the Price-Jones curve in a 1922 paper.[^o12] It has been used for assessing [red blood cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_blood_cell) since then.[^o13]
