# Clifford Martin Will

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Clifford Martin Will"}

**Clifford Martin Will** (born 1946) is a Canadian-born [theoretical physicist](wikipedia://en/19594028) noted for his contributions to [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024).[^o3]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Will was born in [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario). In 1968, he earned a B.Sc. from [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University). At [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), he studied under [Kip Thorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kip_Thorne), earning his Ph.D. in 1971.[^o6] He was an Enrico Fermi postdoctoral fellow under [Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar](wikipedia://en/145319) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) and an Assistant Professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and in 1981 joined the faculty of [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis). He served 10 years as Chair of Physics (1991 - 2002), and in 2005 was named James S. McDonnell Professor of Space Sciences. In 2012, he moved to a position as Distinguished Professor of Physics at the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida).[^o3]

Will's theoretical work has centered on [post-Newtonian expansions](wikipedia://en/4383472) of approximate solutions to the [Einstein field equations](wikipedia://en/424540), a notoriously difficult area which forms the theoretical underpinnings essential for such achievements as the indirect verification by [Russell Hulse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Hulse) and [Joseph Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Hooton_Taylor,_Jr.) in 1979 of the existence of [gravitational radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_radiation) from observations of a [binary pulsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_pulsar),[^o3] and the direct detection of gravitational radiation in 2015 by the [LIGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIGO)-Virgo laser-interferometric detectors. He also made contributions to the theory of quasinormal modes of oscillation of black holes, to elucidating properties of the supermassive black hole at the center of the Milky Way, and to the "three-body problem" of Newtonian gravity.

Will's book reviewing experimental tests of general relativity is widely regarded as the essential resource for research in this area; his popular book on the same subject was listed by *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* as one of the 200 best books published in 1986,[^o3] and was translated into French, German, Italian, Japanese, Portuguese, Spanish, Korean, Greek, Persian, and Chinese. ## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Will was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship)[^o9] and a [J. William Fulbright Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) for the academic year 1996–1997. From 2009 to 2018, Will was the editor-in-chief of [IOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics) Publishing's journal [Classical and Quantum Gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_and_Quantum_Gravity).[^o3]

He was elected a [Fellow of the American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Physical_Society) in 1989 [^o10] and elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2002. He was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2007.[^o3]

In 2019, Will received the [Albert Einstein Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_Medal), awarded each year since 1979 by the [Albert Einstein Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_Society) in Bern, Switzerland, for his "important contributions to General Relativity, in particular including the Post-Newtonian expansions of approximate solutions of the Einstein field equations and their confrontation with experiments."[^o11] In 2021 he shared the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society) Einstein Prize with Saul Teukolsky, for "outstanding contributions to observational tests of general relativity with theories of gravitational waves, astrophysical black holes and neutron stars."
