# Arthur Geoffrey Walker

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur Geoffrey Walker"}

**Arthur Geoffrey Walker** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London) [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (17 July 1909 in [Watford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watford), [Hertfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertfordshire), England – 31 March 2001)[^o3][^o4] was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and professor of the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield) who made important contributions to [physical cosmology](wikipedia://en/5378). Although he was an accomplished [geometer](wikipedia://en/8625), he is best remembered today for two important contributions to [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), namely, the [Robertson-Walker metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robertson-Walker_metric) and the [Fermi–Walker transport](wikipedia://en/3598939).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in [Watford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watford) on 17 July 1909 the son of Arthur John Walker (b.1879), a coach builder, and his wife, Eleanor Joanna Gosling.[^o4]

Walker attended [Watford Grammar School for Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watford_Grammar_School_for_Boys) and won a scholarship to [Balliol College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford), where he graduated with first class honours in Mathematics.[^o6] He then studied at [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford). He then went as a postgraduate to [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), studying for his doctorate (PhD) under Professor [Edmund Taylor Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Taylor_Whittaker). Professor [Arthur Stanley Eddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Stanley_Eddington) was his external examiner.[^o4][^o6] His exposure to differential geometry, general relativity, and cosmology created the background for his future work.[^o3]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

[Luther Pfahler Eisenhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther_P._Eisenhart)'s 1926 text, *Riemannian Geometry*, proved to a great influence on Walker, who referred to it as his "Bible" and cited it in many of his papers.[^o3] The notion of [parallel transport](wikipedia://en/285595) was introduced by [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180) and again by [Enrico Fermi](wikipedia://en/10264), who also applied it to [world lines](wikipedia://en/165487) in spacetime. In 1932, Walker developed this idea further, creating the technique of parallel transport for a [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965). The Fermi-Walker transport is now of common use in general relativity.[^o3]

Walker took up a post as Lecturer at [Imperial College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College) in 1935; the following year he was appointed as Lecturer in Pure Mathematics at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool), a post he held until 1947, when he moved to the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield) as Professor of Pure Mathematics.[^o4][^o6] In 1935, Walker and Robertson demonstrated that the isotropic and homogeneous cosmological models previously constructed by [Georges Lemaître](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Lemaître) and [Alexander Friedmann](wikipedia://en/728892) all shared the same general form, the Robertson-Walker metric. They also showed that this was the most general model possible that featured [isotropy](wikipedia://en/14865) and [homogeneity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneity_%28physics%29) (see the [Copernican principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernican_principle)).[^o3] During the mid 1930s, he collaborated with the Department of Mechanical Engineering on problems involving tidal flow.[^o3]

In 1946 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were [Harold Stanley Ruse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Stanley_Ruse), Sir Edmund Taylor Whittaker, [David Gibb](wikipedia://en/50969533) and [William Edge](wikipedia://en/3996306). He won the Society's [Keith Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Medal) for the period 1947/49.[^o10]

In 1952 he returned to Liverpool University, in 1962 becoming Dean of its Faculty of Science.[^o6] Having been elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1955, he served as a member of the organisation's council from 1961 to 1962.[^o6] He served as president of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) from 1962 to 1963.[^o10] Walker retired from Liverpool University in 1974.[^o4]

## Publications {#Publications}

- *Harmonic Spaces* (1962)
- *An Introduction to Geometrical Cosmology* (1975)

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Walker married Phyllis Ashcroft Freeman in 1939;[^o6] the couple were accomplished ballroom dancers.[^o4] He died in [Chichester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chichester) on 31 March 2001, aged 91.[^o4][^o10]
