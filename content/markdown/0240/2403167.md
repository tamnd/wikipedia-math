# William H. Tucker (psychologist)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William H. Tucker"}

**William H. Tucker** (1940–2022), also known as **Bill Tucker**, was an American psychologist. He was an Emeritus Professor of [Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) and the author of several books critical of [race science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_racism). He retired from Rutgers in 2009.[^o2] Tucker died in 2022.[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Tucker received his bachelor's degree from [Bates College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bates_College) in 1967, and his master's and doctorate from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). He joined the faculty at Rutgers University in 1970 and was there since.[^o5] During his time as a student at Princeton, he was a member of the [Students for a Democratic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students_for_a_Democratic_Society) and participated in taking over buildings on campus.[^o2]

## Career {#Career}

Tucker was a Psychometric Fellow for three years at Princeton, a position subsidized by [Educational Testing Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Testing_Service). The majority of Tucker's scholarship was about [psychometrics](wikipedia://en/24982), not in it. He sat on the advisory board of the [Institute for the Study of Academic Racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Study_of_Academic_Racism).[^o7]

Tucker wrote critical commentaries on several [hereditarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditarian) psychologists known for their controversial work on [race and intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_intelligence). He won the [Anisfield-Wolf Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisfield-Wolf_Book_Award) in 1995, and received a fellowship from the [National Endowment for the Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Endowment_for_the_Humanities) in 2006.[^o2]

According to his website, "My research interests concern the use—or more properly the misuse—of social science to support oppressive social policies, especially in the area of race. I seek to explore how scientists in general, and psychologists in particular, have become involved with such issues and what effect their participation has produced."
