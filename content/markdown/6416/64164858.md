# Jaap Murre

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jaap Murre"}

*Image: Murre jacob.jpg.* Murre in 2004

**Jacob Pieter "Jaap" Murre** (18 September 1929 – 9 April 2023) was a Dutch mathematician specializing in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). He was a professor of mathematics at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) from 1961 to 1994.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Murre was born on 18 September 1929 in [Baarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baarland).[^o3] At his small primary school one of his classmates and friends was later botanist Jan Zeevaart.[^o4] Murre started studying mathematics at [Delft University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology) in 1947, he however switched to [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) where he obtained his degree in 1952.[^o5] With the [Italian school of algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/383424) being in fashion he was inspired by his promotor to do doctoral research under [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), which he was able to do because of the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan).[^o5] In 1957 Murre obtained his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) under [Hendrik Kloosterman](wikipedia://en/6022800) with a thesis titled: *Over multipliciteiten van maximaal samenhangende bossen*.[^o3][^o6] By 1958 Murre became inspired by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) and managed to collaborate with him.[^o5] In 1959 he was appointed associate professor (lector) at Leiden University. In 1961 he became professor of mathematics at the same institute, with a teaching assignment in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).[^o3] [Frans Oort](wikipedia://en/58171100) was the first doctoral student he supervised, but Oort had to formally promote under Wil van Est as his promotion came one month before Murre's appointment.[^o5]

During his career Murre traveled frequently. He amongst others went to India to study with [C. S. Seshadri](wikipedia://en/5633765) and to the United Kingdom where he met [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594), who inspired him to work on [algebraic cycles](wikipedia://en/9520954).[^o5] In Italy he collaborated with Alberto Conte, who became a friend. He also befriended American mathematician [Spencer Bloch](wikipedia://en/25794633). In the 1990s he published on the [Chow group](wikipedia://en/4282051).[^o5] Murre retired as professor in 1994.[^o6]

Murre was elected a member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1971.[^o7] In 2002 he obtained an honorary doctorate from the [University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin).[^o5] Murre was elected a foreign member of the Accademia delle Scienze di Torino in 2004.[^o8]

Murre died on 9 April 2023 at the age of 93.[^o5]
