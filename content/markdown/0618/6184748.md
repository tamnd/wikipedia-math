# Nathan Mantel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nathan Mantel"}

**Nathan Mantel** (February 16, 1919 – May 25, 2002)[^o3] was an American [biostatistician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biostatistician) best known for his work with [William Haenszel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Haenszel) that led to the [Mantel–Haenszel test](wikipedia://en/29622014) and its associated estimate, the Mantel–Haenszel [odds ratio](wikipedia://en/406880). The Mantel–Haenszel procedure and its extensions allow data from several sources or groups to be combined while avoiding [confounding](wikipedia://en/3105999).[^o4]

:::

He spent much of his career working for the [National Cancer Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cancer_Institute). During his career, he published over 380 academic papers. Later in his life, Mantel was known for defending the [tobacco industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_industry) against claims that [passive smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_smoking) was harmful.[^o5]
