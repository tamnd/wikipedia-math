# Weak dimension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weak dimension"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the **weak dimension** of a [nonzero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_module) right [module](wikipedia://en/276410) *M* over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* is the largest number *n* such that the [Tor group](wikipedia://en/856347) $\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(M,N)$ is [nonzero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_group) for some left *R*-module *N* (or infinity if no largest such *n* exists), and the weak dimension of a left *R*-module is defined similarly. The weak dimension was introduced by (txt, p.122). The weak dimension is sometimes called the **flat dimension** as it is the shortest length of the [resolution](wikipedia://en/19745659) of the module by [flat modules](wikipedia://en/492616). The weak dimension of a module is, at most, equal to its [projective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_dimension).

:::

The **weak global dimension** of a ring is the largest number *n* such that $\operatorname{Tor}_n^R(M,N)$ is nonzero for some right *R*-module *M* and left *R*-module *N*. If there is no such largest number *n*, the weak global dimension is defined to be infinite. It is at most equal to the left or right [global dimension](wikipedia://en/2291739) of the ring *R*.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The module $\mathbb{Q}$ of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) over the ring $\mathbb{Z}$ of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) has weak dimension 0, but projective dimension 1.
- The module $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ over the ring $\mathbb{Z}$ has weak dimension 1, but [injective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_dimension) 0.
- The module $\mathbb{Z}$ over the ring $\mathbb{Z}$ has weak dimension 0, but injective dimension 1.
- A [Prüfer domain](wikipedia://en/12367835) has weak global dimension at most 1.
- A [Von Neumann regular ring](wikipedia://en/907524) has weak global dimension 0.
- A [product](wikipedia://en/25063) of infinitely many [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) has weak global dimension 0 but its global dimension is nonzero.
- If a ring is right [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129), then the right global dimension is the same as the weak global dimension, and is at most the left global dimension. In particular if a ring is right and left Noetherian then the left and right global dimensions and the weak global dimension are all the same.
- The [triangular matrix ring](wikipedia://en/30536078) $\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Q} \\0&\mathbb{Q} \end{bmatrix}$ has right global dimension 1, weak global dimension 1, but left global dimension 2. It is right Noetherian, but not left Noetherian.

:::
