# Hardy–Ramanujan theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hardy–Ramanujan theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hardy–Ramanujan theorem**, proved by [Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717) and checked by [Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599)[^o2] states that the [normal order](wikipedia://en/18747595) of the number $\omega(n)$ of distinct [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) of a number $n$ is $\log\log n$.

:::

Roughly speaking, this means that most numbers have about this number of distinct prime factors.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Precise statement"}

## Precise statement {#Precise_statement}

A more precise version[^o5] states that for every real-valued function $\psi(n)$ that tends to infinity as $n$ tends to infinity

$$|\omega(n)-\log\log n|<\psi(n)\sqrt{\log\log n}$$

or more traditionally

$$|\omega(n)-\log\log n|<{(\log\log n)}^{\frac12 +\varepsilon}$$

for *[almost all](wikipedia://en/1309)* (all but an [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) proportion of) integers. That is, let $g(x)$ be the number of positive integers $n$ less than $x$ for which the above inequality fails: then $g(x)/x$ converges to zero as $x$ goes to infinity.

:::

## History {#History}

A simple proof to the result was given by [Pál Turán](wikipedia://en/614998), who used the [Turán sieve](wikipedia://en/18747602) to prove that[^o7]

$$\sum_{n \le x} | \omega(n) - \log\log x|^2 \ll x \log\log x .$$

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The same results are true of $\Omega(n)$, the number of prime factors of $n$ counted with [multiplicity](wikipedia://en/19211790#Multiplicity_of_a_prime_factor). This theorem is generalized by the [Erdős–Kac theorem](wikipedia://en/6046005), which shows that $\omega(n)$ is essentially [normally distributed](wikipedia://en/21462). There are many proofs of this, including the method of moments (Granville & Soundararajan)[^o10] and [Stein's method](wikipedia://en/12933953) (Harper).[^o11] It was shown by Durkan that a modified version of Turán's result allows one to prove the Hardy–Ramanujan Theorem with any even moment.[^o12]

:::
