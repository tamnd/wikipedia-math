# Kurt Hensel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kurt Hensel"}

**Kurt Wilhelm Sebastian Hensel** (29 December 1861 – 1 June 1941) was a German mathematician born in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg), best known for introducing the [p-adic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_numbers).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Hensel was born in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg), [Province of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Prussia) (today [Kaliningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad), Russia), the son of Julia (née von Adelson) and landowner and entrepreneur [Sebastian Hensel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Hensel). He was the brother of philosopher [Paul Hensel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Hensel). Kurt and Paul's paternal grandparents were painter [Wilhelm Hensel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Hensel) and composer [Fanny Hensel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanny_Hensel). Fanny was the sister of [Felix Mendelssohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Mendelssohn), daughter of [Abraham Mendelssohn Bartholdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Mendelssohn_Bartholdy), and granddaughter of philosopher [Moses Mendelssohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Mendelssohn), and entrepreneur [Daniel Itzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Itzig). Both of Hensel's grandmothers and his mother were from Jewish families that had converted to Christianity.

Hensel studied mathematics in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) and [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), under the mathematicians [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450) and [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459).

Later in his life Hensel was a professor at the [University of Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Marburg) until 1930. He was also an editor of the mathematical *[Crelle's Journal](wikipedia://en/973547)*. He edited the five-volume collected works of Leopold Kronecker.

Hensel is well known for his introduction of [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423). First described by him in 1897,[^o4] they became increasingly important in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and other fields during the twentieth century.[^o5]
