# Gorenstein scheme

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gorenstein scheme"}

In algebraic geometry, a **Gorenstein scheme** is a [locally Noetherian](wikipedia://en/35457783#local) [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) whose local rings are all [Gorenstein](wikipedia://en/515613).[^o2] The [canonical line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_line_bundle) is defined for any Gorenstein scheme over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), and its properties are much the same as in the special case of [smooth schemes](wikipedia://en/35033819).

:::

## Related properties {#Related_properties}

For a Gorenstein scheme *X* of [finite type](wikipedia://en/35457783#finite_type_%28locally%29) over a field, *f*: *X* → Spec(*k*), the [dualizing complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualizing_complex) *f*<sup>!</sup>(*k*) on *X* is a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/581859) (called the **canonical bundle** *K*<sub>*X*</sub>), viewed as a complex in degree −dim(*X*).[^o4] If *X* is smooth of dimension *n* over *k*, the canonical bundle *K*<sub>*X*</sub> can be identified with the line bundle Ω<sup>*n*</sup> of top-degree [differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahler_differentials#Use_in_algebraic_geometry).[^o5]

Using the canonical bundle, [Serre duality](wikipedia://en/359175) takes the same form for Gorenstein schemes as it does for smooth schemes.

Let *X* be a [normal scheme](wikipedia://en/2629423) of finite type over a field *k*. Then *X* is [regular](wikipedia://en/43016199) outside a closed subset of [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149) at least 2. Let *U* be the open subset where *X* is regular; then the canonical bundle *K*<sub>*U*</sub> is a line bundle. The restriction from the [divisor class group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor_class_group) Cl(*X*) to Cl(*U*) is an isomorphism, and (since *U* is smooth) Cl(*U*) can be identified with the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) Pic(*U*). As a result, *K*<sub>*U*</sub> defines a [linear equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor_class_group) class of [Weil divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_divisor) on *X*. Any such divisor is called the **canonical divisor** *K*<sub>*X*</sub>. For a normal scheme *X*, the canonical divisor *K*<sub>*X*</sub> is said to be **Q-Cartier** if some positive multiple of the Weil divisor *K*<sub>*X*</sub> is [Cartier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartier_divisor). (This property does not depend on the choice of Weil divisor in its linear equivalence class.) Alternatively, normal schemes *X* with *K*<sub>*X*</sub> **Q**-Cartier are sometimes said to be **Q-Gorenstein**.

It is also useful to consider the normal schemes *X* for which the canonical divisor *K*<sub>*X*</sub> is **Cartier**. Such a scheme is sometimes said to be **Q-Gorenstein of index 1**. (Some authors use "Gorenstein" for this property, but that can lead to confusion.) A normal scheme *X* is Gorenstein (as defined above) if and only if *K*<sub>*X*</sub> is Cartier and *X* is [Cohen–Macaulay](wikipedia://en/497407).[^o6]

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- An [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) with [local complete intersection](wikipedia://en/3116851) singularities, for example any [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) in a smooth variety, is Gorenstein.[^o9]
- A variety *X* with quotient singularities over a field of [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_of_a_field) zero is Cohen–Macaulay, and *K*<sub>*X*</sub> is **Q**-Cartier. The quotient variety of a vector space *V* by a linear action of a finite group *G* is Gorenstein if *G* maps into the subgroup SL(*V*) of linear transformations of [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) 1. By contrast, if *X* is the quotient of **C**<sup>2</sup> by the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order *n* acting by scalars, then *K*<sub>*X*</sub> is not Cartier (and so *X* is not Gorenstein) for *n* ≥ 3.
- Generalizing the previous example, every variety *X* with [klt](wikipedia://en/22910728#Pairs) (Kawamata log terminal) singularities over a field of characteristic zero is Cohen–Macaulay, and *K*<sub>*X*</sub> is **Q**-Cartier.[^o10]
- If a variety *X* has [log canonical](wikipedia://en/22910728#Pairs) singularities, then *K*<sub>*X*</sub> is **Q**-Cartier, but *X* need not be Cohen–Macaulay. For example, any [affine cone](wikipedia://en/50394182) *X* over an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) *Y* is log canonical, and *K*<sub>*X*</sub> is Cartier, but *X* is not Cohen–Macaulay when *Y* has dimension at least 2.[^o11]
