# Predictive mean matching

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Predictive mean matching"}

**Predictive mean matching (PMM)**[^o2] is a widely used[^o3] statistical [imputation method](wikipedia://en/1309220) for missing values, first proposed by Donald B. Rubin in 1986[^o4] and R. J. A. Little in 1988.[^o5]

:::

It aims to reduce the [bias](wikipedia://en/40786) introduced in a dataset through imputation, by drawing real values sampled from the data.[^o6] This is achieved by building a small subset of observations where the outcome variable matches the outcome of the observations with missing values.[^o2]

Compared to other imputation methods, it usually imputes less implausible values (e.g. negative incomes) and takes [heteroscedastic data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteroscedasticity) into account more appropriately.[^o7]
