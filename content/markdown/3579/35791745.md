# Hall plane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hall plane"}

In mathematics, a **Hall plane** is a [non-Desarguesian projective plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Desarguesian_projective_plane) constructed by [Marshall Hall Jr.](wikipedia://en/7758584) (1943).[^o2] There are examples of order *p*<sup>2*n*</sup> for every prime *p* and every positive integer *n* provided *p*<sup>2*n*</sup> > 4.[^o3]

:::

## Algebraic construction via Hall systems {#Algebraic_construction_via_Hall_systems}

The original construction of Hall planes was based on the Hall [quasifield](wikipedia://en/4029839) (also called a *Hall system*), *H* of order *p*<sup>2*n*</sup> for *p* a prime. The creation of the plane from the quasifield follows the standard construction (see [quasifield](wikipedia://en/4029839#Projective_planes) for details).

To build a Hall quasifield, start with a [Galois field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_field), *F* = GF(*p*<sup>*n*</sup>) for *p* a prime and a quadratic irreducible polynomial *f*(*x*) = *x*<sup>2</sup> − *rx* − *s* over *F*. Extend *H* = *F* × *F*, a two-dimensional vector space over *F*, to a quasifield by defining a multiplication on the vectors by (*a*, *b*) ∘ (*c*, *d*) = (*ac* − *bd*<sup>−1</sup>*f*(*c*), *ad* − *bc* + *br*) when *d* ≠ 0 and (*a*, *b*) ∘ (*c*, 0) = (*ac*, *bc*) otherwise.

Writing the elements of *H* in terms of a basis $\langle 1, \lambda\rangle$, that is, identifying (*x*, *y*) with *x* + *λy* as *x* and *y* vary over *F*, we can identify the elements of *F* as the ordered pairs (*x*, 0), i.e. *x* + *λ*0. The properties of the defined multiplication which turn the right vector space *H* into a quasifield are:

1. every element *α* of *H* not in *F* satisfies the quadratic equation *f*(*α*) = 0;
1. *F* is in the kernel of *H* (meaning that (*α* + *β*)*c* = *αc* + *βc*, and (*αβ*)*c* = *α*(*βc*) for all *α*, *β* in *H* and all *c* in *F*); and
1. every element of *F* commutes (multiplicatively) with all the elements of *H*.[^o5]

## Derivation {#Derivation}

Another construction that produces Hall planes is obtained by applying derivation to [Desarguesian planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desarguesian_plane).

A process, due to T. G. Ostrom, which replaces certain sets of lines in a projective plane by alternate sets in such a way that the new structure is still a projective plane is called *derivation*. We give the details of this process.[^o7] Start with a [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350#Finite_projective_planes) *π* of order *n*<sup>2</sup> and designate one line ℓ as its [line at infinity](wikipedia://en/403142). Let *A* be the [affine plane](wikipedia://en/24350#Affine_planes) *π* ∖ ℓ. A set *D* of *n* + 1 points of ℓ is called a *derivation set* if for every pair of distinct points *X* and *Y* of *A* which determine a line meeting ℓ in a point of *D*, there is a [Baer subplane](wikipedia://en/24350#Subplanes) containing *X*, *Y* and *D* (we say that such Baer subplanes *belong* to *D*.) Define a new affine plane D(*A*) as follows: The points of D(*A*) are the points of *A*. The lines of D(*A*) are the lines of *π* which do not meet ℓ at a point of *D* (restricted to *A*) and the Baer subplanes that belong to *D* (restricted to *A*). The set D(*A*) is an affine plane of order *n*<sup>2</sup> and it, or its projective completion, is called a *derived plane*.[^o8]

## Properties {#Properties}

1. Hall planes are [translation planes](wikipedia://en/2628762).
1. All finite Hall planes of the same order are isomorphic.
1. Hall planes are not [self-dual](wikipedia://en/539925).
1. All finite Hall planes contain subplanes of order 2 ([Fano subplanes](wikipedia://en/390404)).
1. All finite Hall planes contain subplanes of order different from 2.
1. Hall planes are [André planes](wikipedia://en/21121170).

:::

## Hall plane of order 9 {#Hall_plane_of_order_9}

The **Hall plane of order 9** is the smallest Hall plane, and one of the three smallest examples of a finite [non-Desarguesian projective plane](wikipedia://en/21092112), along with its [dual](wikipedia://en/539925) and the [Hughes plane](wikipedia://en/21116300) of order 9.[^o13]

::: {#o15 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

While usually constructed in the same way as other Hall planes, the Hall plane of order 9 was actually found earlier by [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen) and [Joseph Wedderburn](wikipedia://en/2969494) in 1907.[^o16] There are four quasifields of order nine which can be used to construct the Hall plane of order nine. Three of these are Hall systems generated by the irreducible polynomials *f*(*x*) = *x*<sup>2</sup> + 1, *g*(*x*) = *x*<sup>2</sup> − *x* − 1 or *h*(*x*) = *x*<sup>2</sup> + *x* − 1.[^o17] The first of these produces an associative quasifield,[^o18] that is, a [near-field](wikipedia://en/4033255), and it was in this context that the plane was discovered by Veblen and Wedderburn. This plane is often referred to as the nearfield plane of order nine.

:::

::: {#o20 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

#### Automorphism Group {#Automorphism_Group}

The Hall plane of order 9 is the unique projective plane, finite or infinite, which has [Lenz–Barlotti class](wikipedia://en/21092112#Classification) IVa.3.[^o22] Its [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) acts on its (necessarily unique) [translation line](wikipedia://en/2628762) [imprimitively](wikipedia://en/2739252), having 5 pairs of points that the group preserves set-wise; the automorphism group acts as S<sub>5</sub> on these 5 pairs.[^o23]

#### Unitals {#Unitals}

The Hall plane of order 9 admits four inequivalent embedded [unitals](wikipedia://en/41285965).[^o25] Two of these unitals arise from Buekenhout's[^o26] constructions: one is *parabolic*, meeting the translation line in a single point, while the other is *hyperbolic*, meeting the translation line in 4 points. The latter of these two unitals was shown by Grüning[^o27] to also be embeddable in the dual Hall plane. Another of the unitals arises from the construction of Barlotti and Lunardon.[^o28] The fourth has an automorphism group of order 8 isomorphic to the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440), and is not part of any known infinite family.

:::
