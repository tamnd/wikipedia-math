# Mordellic variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mordellic variety"}

In mathematics, a **Mordellic variety** is an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) which has only finitely many points in any finitely generated field. The terminology was introduced by [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991) to enunciate a [range of conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lang_conjecture_on_analytically_hyperbolic_varieties) linking the geometry of varieties to their Diophantine properties.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Formally, let *X* be a variety defined over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) of [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero): hence *X* is defined over a finitely generated field *E*. If the set of points *X*(*F*) is finite for any [finitely generated field extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_generated_field_extension) *F* of *E*, then *X* is Mordellic.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Lang's conjectures"}

## Lang's conjectures {#Lang's_conjectures}

The *special set* for a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) *V* is the [Zariski closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski_closure) of the union of the images of all non-trivial maps from [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) into *V*. Lang conjectured that the complement of the special set is Mordellic.

A variety is *algebraically hyperbolic* if the special set is empty. Lang conjectured that a variety *X* is Mordellic if and only if *X* is algebraically hyperbolic and that this is in turn equivalent to *X* being [pseudo-canonical](wikipedia://en/35797504).

For a complex algebraic variety *X* we similarly define the *analytic special* or *exceptional set* as the Zariski closure of the union of images of non-trivial [holomorphic maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_map) from **C** to *X*. Brody's definition of a hyperbolic variety is that there are no such maps. Again, Lang conjectured that a hyperbolic variety is Mordellic and more generally that the complement of the analytic special set is Mordellic.

:::
