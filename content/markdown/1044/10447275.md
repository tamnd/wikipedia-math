# Graph center

*Image: Graphcenter.svg.* A graph with central points colored red. These are the three vertices *A* such that *d*(*A*, *B*) ≤ 3 for all vertices *B*. Each black vertex is a distance of at least 4 from some other vertex.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph center"}

The **center** (or [Jordan](wikipedia://en/355931) center[^o2]) of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) is the set of all vertices of minimum [eccentricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eccentricity_%28graph_theory%29),[^o3] that is, the set of all vertices *u* where the greatest distance *d*(*u*,*v*) to other vertices *v* is minimal. Equivalently, it is the set of vertices with eccentricity equal to the graph's [radius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radius_%28graph_theory%29). Thus vertices in the center (**central points**) minimize the maximal distance from other points in the graph.

:::

This is also known as the **vertex 1-center problem** and can be extended to the [vertex k-center problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_k-center_problem).

Finding the center of a graph is useful in [facility location problems](wikipedia://en/10469986) where the goal is to minimize the worst-case distance to the facility. For example, placing a hospital at a central point reduces the longest distance the ambulance has to travel.

The center can be found using the [Floyd–Warshall algorithm](wikipedia://en/230401).[^o5][^o6] Another algorithm has been proposed based on matrix calculus.[^o7]

The concept of the center of a graph is related to the [closeness centrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closeness_centrality) measure in [social network analysis](wikipedia://en/325726), which is the reciprocal of the mean of the distances *d*(*A*,*B*).[^o2]
