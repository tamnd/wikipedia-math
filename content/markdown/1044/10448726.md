# Johann Gustav Hermes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Johann Gustav Hermes"}

*Image: Das Fotoalbum für Weierstraß 025 (Johann Gustav Hermes).jpg.* Johann Gustav Hermes

**Johann Gustav Hermes** (20 June 1846 – 8 June 1912) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Hermes is known for constructing a [polygon with 65,537 sides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/65,537-gon).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

On 20 June 1846, Hermes was born in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg), a former German city (presently [Kaliningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad), Russia). Hermes was educated at the Kneiphöfischen [Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29). He undertook his *Abitur* (final examination) at the school in 1866. After completing his secondary education, he studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from 1866 to 1870, mostly in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg). His studies were interrupted due to his participation in the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War) between 1870 and 1871.

## Education {#Education}

On 14 December 1872, Hermes completed his studies and earned a degree in mathematics. On 5 April 1879, Hermes received a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) degree and his [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) was on the "Reduction of the problem of [cyclotomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotomy) on linear equations (for [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) of the form 2<sup>*m*</sup>+1)" (German: "*Zurückführung des Problems der Kreistheilung auf lineare Gleichungen (für Primzahlen von der Form* 2<sup>*m*</sup>+1*)*").[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

After a probationary year at the [Chernyakhovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernyakhovsk) Realgymnasium in 1873, Hermes worked as a teacher at the Progymnasium of the Royal Orphanage of Königsberg in Prussia (German: *Königliches Waisenhaus zu Königsberg in Preußen*). Beginning in 1883 he was an *Oberlehrer* (or upper teacher). In 1893 he became a professor at the Georgianum Gymnasium in [Lingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingen). Finally, on 1 April 1899, he became a professor and director at the [Osnabrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabrück) Realgymnasium (now named the Ernst-Moritz-Arndt-Gymnasium).

On 31 December 1906 he asked for an early retirement from his position due to illness.

### Achievements {#Achievements}

In 1894, Hermes completed his decade-long effort to find and write down a procedure for the [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge_constructions) of the [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) [65537-gon](wikipedia://en/5024281) exclusively with a compass and a straightedge. His manuscript, with over 200 pages, is today located at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen).

In his maiden speech as the director at the Osnabrück Realgymnasium on 11 April 1899, he praised the [concept of duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_imperative) of the influential [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant), who was also a resident of Königsberg. He finished with the words "*Geduld ist die Pforte der Freude.*" ("Patience is the gate to joy.")

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Hermes became ill in December 1906, and died on 8 June 1912. He is buried in Osnabrück, Germany.
