# Affine Hecke algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Affine Hecke algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **affine Hecke algebra** is the [algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) associated to an [affine Weyl group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_Weyl_group), and can be used to [prove](wikipedia://en/82285) [Macdonald's constant term conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macdonald's_constant_term_conjecture) for [Macdonald polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macdonald_polynomial).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $V$ be a Euclidean space of a finite dimension and $\Sigma$ an [affine root system](wikipedia://en/32702474) on $V$. An **affine Hecke algebra** is a certain [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) that deforms the [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) $\mathbb{C}[W]$ of the [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) $W$ of $\Sigma$ (the [affine Weyl group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_Weyl_group)). It is usually denoted by $H(\Sigma,q)$, where $q:\Sigma\rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ is [multiplicity function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicity_function) that plays the role of deformation parameter. For $q\equiv 1$ the affine Hecke algebra $H(\Sigma,q)$ indeed reduces to $\mathbb{C}[W]$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

[Ivan Cherednik](wikipedia://en/33333323) introduced generalizations of affine Hecke algebras, the so-called [double affine Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/32034345) (usually referred to as DAHA). Using this he was able to give a proof of Macdonald's constant term conjecture for Macdonald polynomials (building on work of [Eric Opdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Opdam)). Another main inspiration for Cherednik to consider the double affine Hecke algebra was the [quantum KZ equations](wikipedia://en/25525656).

:::
