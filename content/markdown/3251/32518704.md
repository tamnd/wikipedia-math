# Braided vector space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Braided vector space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **braided** vector space$\;V$ is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) together with an additional structure map $\tau$ symbolizing **interchanging** of two vector [tensor copies](wikipedia://en/30844):

:::

$$\tau:\; V\otimes V\longrightarrow V\otimes V$$

such that the [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810) is fulfilled. Hence drawing [tensor diagrams](wikipedia://en/2973987) with $\tau$ an **overcrossing** the corresponding composed morphism is unchanged when a [Reidemeister move](wikipedia://en/1152374) is applied to the tensor diagram and thus they present a representation of the [braid group](wikipedia://en/550138).

As first example, every vector space is braided via the trivial braiding (simply flipping). A [superspace](wikipedia://en/432630) has a braiding with negative sign in braiding two **odd** vectors. More generally, a **diagonal braiding** means that for a $V$-base $x_i$ we have

$$\tau(x_i\otimes x_j)=q_{ij}(x_j\otimes x_i)$$

A good source for braided vector spaces entire [braided monoidal categories](wikipedia://en/1470466) with braidings between any objects $\tau_{V,W}$, most importantly the modules over [quasitriangular Hopf algebras](wikipedia://en/3727020) and [Yetter–Drinfeld modules](wikipedia://en/10497038) over [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) (such as $\mathbb{Z}_2$ above)

If $V$ additionally possesses an [algebra structure inside the braided category](wikipedia://en/10504214) ("braided algebra") one has a **braided commutator** (e.g. for a [superspace](wikipedia://en/432630) the [anticommutator](wikipedia://en/7193)):

$$\;[x,y]_\tau:=\mu((x\otimes y)-\tau(x\otimes y))\qquad \mu(x\otimes y):=xy$$

Examples of such braided algebras (and even [Hopf algebras](wikipedia://en/10504214)) are the [Nichols algebras](wikipedia://en/32370851), that are by definition generated by a given braided vectorspace. They appear as quantum Borel part of [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678) and often (e.g. when finite or over an abelian group) possess an [arithmetic root system](wikipedia://en/277087), multiple [Dynkin diagrams](wikipedia://en/277109) and a [PBW-basis](wikipedia://en/762696) made up of braided commutators just like the ones in [semisimple Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1337587).
