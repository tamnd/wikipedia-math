# Hartley Rogers Jr.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hartley Rogers Jr."}

**Hartley Rogers Jr.** (July 6, 1926 – July 17, 2015) was an American mathematician who worked in [computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414), and was a professor in the [Mathematics Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Mathematics_Department) of the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in 1926 in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), Rogers studied English as an undergraduate at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), graduating in 1946. After visiting the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) under a [Henry Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Fellowship), he returned to Yale for a master's degree in physics, which he completed in 1950. He studied mathematics under [Alonzo Church](wikipedia://en/49731) at [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), earned a second master's degree in 1951,[^o7] and received his Ph.D. there in 1952.

## Career {#Career}

He was a Benjamin Peirce Lecturer at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) from 1952 to 1955. After holding a visiting position at MIT, he became a professor in the [MIT Mathematics Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Mathematics_Department) in 1956.[^o7] His doctoral students included [Patrick Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_C._Fischer), [Louis Hodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Hodes), [Carl Jockusch](wikipedia://en/9859617), [Andrew Kahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Kahr), [David Luckham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Luckham), [Rohit Parikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohit_Jivanlal_Parikh), [David Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Park_%28computer_scientist%29), and [John Stillwell](wikipedia://en/15066763).[^o4] He chaired the MIT faculty senate from 1971 to 1973 and served as associate provost of the university from 1974 to 1980.[^o7]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Beyond teaching and research, Rogers was an avid rower and rowing competitor.[^o7]

He retired as a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) in 2009, and died on July 17, 2015.[^o7]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Rogers worked in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), particularly [recursion theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion_theory), and wrote the classic text *Theory of Recursive Functions and Effective Computability*.[^o9] The [Rogers equivalence theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger's_equivalence_theorem) is named after him.

Rogers won the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) in 1965 for his expository article *Information Theory*.[^o10]
