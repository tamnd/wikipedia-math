# Regge calculus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Regge calculus"}

In [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), **Regge calculus** is a formalism for producing [simplicial approximations](wikipedia://en/2446477) of spacetimes that are solutions to the [Einstein field equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_field_equation). The calculus was introduced by the Italian theoretician [Tullio Regge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullio_Regge) in 1961.[^o2]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The starting point for Regge's work is the fact that every four dimensional time orientable [Lorentzian manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold) admits a [triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440970) into [simplices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplices). Furthermore, the [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) can be expressed in terms of [deficit angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defect_%28geometry%29) associated with *2-faces* where arrangements of *4-simplices* meet. These 2-faces play the same role as the [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) where arrangements of *triangles* meet in a triangulation of a *2-manifold*, which is easier to visualize. Here a vertex with a positive angular deficit represents a concentration of *positive* [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623), whereas a vertex with a negative angular deficit represents a concentration of *negative* Gaussian curvature.

The deficit angles can be computed directly from the various [edge](wikipedia://en/12158034) lengths in the triangulation, which is equivalent to saying that the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) can be computed from the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) of a Lorentzian manifold. Regge showed that the [vacuum field equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_field_equations) can be reformulated as a restriction on these deficit angles. He then showed how this can be applied to evolve an initial [spacelike hyperslice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacelike_hyperslice) according to the vacuum field equation.

The result is that, starting with a triangulation of some spacelike hyperslice (which must itself satisfy a certain [constraint](wikipedia://en/7418540) equation), one can eventually obtain a simplicial approximation to a vacuum solution. This can be applied to difficult problems in [numerical relativity](wikipedia://en/1797708) such as simulating the collision of two [black holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_holes).

The elegant idea behind Regge calculus has motivated the construction of further generalizations of this idea. In particular, Regge calculus has been adapted to study [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312).
