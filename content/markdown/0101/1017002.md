# Upper topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Upper topology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **upper topology** on a [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) *X* is the [coarsest topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coarsest_topology) in which the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of a [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798) $\{a\}$ is the order section $a] = \{x \leq a\}$ for each $a\in X.$ If $\leq$ is a partial order, the upper topology is the least [order consistent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialization_%28pre%29order#Important_properties) topology in which all [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358) are [up-sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up-set). However, not all up-sets must necessarily be open sets. The **lower topology** induced by the preorder is defined similarly in terms of the [down-sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down-set). The preorder inducing the upper topology is its [specialization preorder](wikipedia://en/542347), but the specialization preorder of the lower topology is opposite to the inducing preorder.

:::

The real upper topology is most naturally defined on the [upper-extended real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper-extended_real_line) $(-\infty, +\infty] = \mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$ by the system $\{(a, +\infty] : a \in \mathbb{R} \cup \{\pm\infty\}\}$ of open sets. Similarly, the real lower topology $\{[-\infty, a) : a \in \mathbb{R} \cup \{\pm\infty\}\}$ is naturally defined on the lower real line $[-\infty, +\infty) = \mathbb{R} \cup \{-\infty\}.$ A real function on a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is [upper semi-continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_semi-continuous) if and only if it is lower-continuous, i.e. is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) with respect to the lower topology on the lower-extended line ${[-\infty, +\infty)}.$ Similarly, a function into the upper real line is [lower semi-continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_semi-continuous) if and only if it is upper-continuous, i.e. is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) with respect to the upper topology on ${(-\infty, +\infty]}.$
