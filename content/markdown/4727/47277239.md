# Hoàng Xuân Sính

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hoàng Xuân Sính"}

**Hoàng Xuân Sính** (born September 8, 1933)[^o3] is a Vietnamese mathematician, a student of [Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042), the first female mathematics professor in Vietnam, the founder of Thang Long University, and a recipient of the *[Ordre des Palmes Académiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_des_Palmes_Académiques)*.

:::

## Early life and career {#Early_life_and_career}

Hoàng was born in Cót, in the [Từ Liêm District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Từ_Liêm_District) of Vietnam, one of seven children of fabric merchant Hoàng Thuc Tan. Her mother died when she was eight years old, and she was raised by a stepmother. She has also frequently been said to be the granddaughter of Vietnamese mathematician [Hoàng Xuân Hãn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoàng_Xuân_Hãn). She completed a bachelor's degree in 1951 in Hanoi, studying English and French, and then traveled to Paris for a second baccalaureate in mathematics.[^o5] She stayed in France to study for an *[agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation)* at the [University of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toulouse), which she completed in 1959, before returning to Vietnam to become a mathematics teacher at the [Hanoi National University of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanoi_National_University_of_Education).[^o16] Hoàng became the first female mathematics professor in Vietnam[^o16] and at that time was one of a very small number of mathematicians there with a foreign education.[^o6]

## Work with Grothendieck {#Work_with_Grothendieck}

The French mathematician and pacifist [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) visited North Vietnam in late 1967, during the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War), and spent a month teaching mathematics to the Hanoi University mathematics department staff,[^o8] including Hoàng, who took the notes for the lectures.[^o6] Because of the war, Grothendieck's lectures were held away from Hanoi, first in the nearby countryside and later in [Đại Từ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Đại_Từ). After Grothendieck returned to France, he continued to teach Hoàng as a correspondence student.[^o8] She earned her doctorate under Grothendieck's supervision from [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) in 1975, with a handwritten thesis.[^o10] Her thesis research, on algebraic structures based on [categorical](wikipedia://en/5869) [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) but with a group law that holds only up to isomorphism, prefigured much of the modern theory of [2-groups](wikipedia://en/21368493).[^o11][^o12]

## Later accomplishments {#Later_accomplishments}

When she was promoted to full professor Hoàng became the first female full professor in Vietnam in any scientific or technical field.[^o14] In 1988 she founded the first private university in Vietnam, Thang Long University in Hanoi,[^o15] and became the president of its board of directors.[^o16][^o17]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2003 she was awarded France's *[Ordre des Palmes Académiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_des_Palmes_Académiques)* for her "contributions to boosting cooperation in culture and science between the two nations" of France and Vietnam.[^o16][^o19]
