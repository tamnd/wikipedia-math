# Diameter (group theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Diameter"}

In the area of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **diameter** of a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) is a measure of its complexity.

:::

Consider a finite group $\left(G,\circ\right)$, and any [set of generators](wikipedia://en/99945) $S$. Define $D_S$ to be the [graph diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_diameter) of the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) $\Lambda=\left(G,S\right)$. Then the diameter of $\left(G,\circ\right)$ is the largest value of $D_S$ taken over all generating sets $S$.

For instance, every finite [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order $s$, the Cayley graph for a generating set with one generator is an $s$-vertex [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962). The diameter of this graph, and of the group, is $\lfloor s/2\rfloor$.[^o2]

It is conjectured, for all non-abelian finite [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600) $G$, that[^o3]

$$\operatorname{diam}(G) \leqslant \left(\log|G|\right)^{\mathcal{O}(1)}.$$

Many partial results are known but the full conjecture remains open.[^o4]
