# Conformal bootstrap

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conformal bootstrap"}

The **conformal bootstrap** is a [non-perturbative](wikipedia://en/1719992) mathematical method to constrain and solve [conformal field theories](wikipedia://en/364774), i.e. models of [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics) or [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics) that exhibit similar properties at different levels of resolution.[^o2]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

Unlike more traditional techniques of [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), conformal bootstrap does not use the [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/40752010) of the theory. Instead, it operates with the general axiomatic parameters, such as the [scaling dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaling_dimension) of the local operators and their [operator product expansion](wikipedia://en/1877895) coefficients. A key axiom is that the product of local operators must be expressible as a sum over local operators (thus turning the product into an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788)); the sum must have a non-zero radius of convergence. This leads to decompositions of correlation functions into structure constants and [conformal blocks](wikipedia://en/58259190).

The main ideas of the conformal bootstrap were formulated in the 1970s by the Soviet physicist [Alexander Polyakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Markovich_Polyakov)[^o4] and the Italian physicists [Sergio Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Ferrara), Raoul Gatto and [Aurelio Grillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelio_Grillo).[^o5] Other early pioneers of this idea were Gerhard Mack and Ivan Todorov (physicist).

In two dimensions, the conformal bootstrap was demonstrated to work in 1983 by [Alexander Belavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Belavin), [Alexander Polyakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Markovich_Polyakov) and [Alexander Zamolodchikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zamolodchikov).[^o6] Many [two-dimensional conformal field theories](wikipedia://en/52183913) were solved using this method, notably the [minimal models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_model_%28physics%29) and the [Liouville field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liouville_field_theory).

In higher dimensions, the conformal bootstrap started to develop following the 2008 paper by [Riccardo Rattazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riccardo_Rattazzi), [Slava Rychkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slava_Rychkov), [Erik Tonni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Tonni) and [Alessandro Vichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Vichi).[^o7] The method was since used to obtain many general results about conformal and [superconformal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconformal_algebra) field theories in three, four, five and six dimensions. Applied to the conformal field theory describing the [critical point](wikipedia://en/1951419) of the three-dimensional [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744), it produced the most precise predictions for its [critical exponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ising_critical_exponents).[^o8][^o9][^o10]

## Current research {#Current_research}

The international [Simons Collaboration on the Nonperturbative Bootstrap](http://bootstrapcollaboration.com/) unites researchers devoted to developing and applying the conformal bootstrap and other related techniques in quantum field theory.[^o12]

## History of the name {#History_of_the_name}

The modern usage of the term "conformal bootstrap" was introduced in 1984 by Belavin et al.[^o6] In the earlier literature, the name was sometimes used to denote a different approach to conformal field theories, nowadays referred to as the [skeleton expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton_expansion) or the "old bootstrap". This older method is perturbative in nature,[^o14][^o15] and is not directly related to the conformal bootstrap in the modern sense of the term.
