# Ternary equivalence relation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ternary equivalence relation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **ternary equivalence relation** is a kind of [ternary relation](wikipedia://en/3784565) analogous to a [binary](wikipedia://en/3931) [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259). A ternary equivalence relation is symmetric, reflexive, and transitive, where those terms are meant in the sense defined below. The classic example is the relation of [collinearity](wikipedia://en/3189581) among three points in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). In an abstract set, a ternary equivalence relation determines a collection of equivalence classes or *[pencils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil_%28mathematics%29)* that form a [linear space](wikipedia://en/13872825) in the sense of [incidence geometry](wikipedia://en/1576323). In the same way, a binary equivalence relation on a set determines a [partition](wikipedia://en/340240).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A ternary equivalence relation on a set $X$ is a relation $E \subset X^{3}$, written $[a, b, c]$, that satisfies the following axioms:

1. Symmetry: If $[a, b, c]$ then $[b, c, a]$ and $[c, b, a]$. (Therefore also $[a, c, b]$, $[b, a, c]$, and $[c, a, b]$.)
1. Reflexivity: $[a, b, b]$. Equivalently, in the presence of symmetry, if $a$, $b$, and $c$ are not all distinct, then $[a, b, c]$.
1. Transitivity: If $a \ne b$ and $[a, b, c]$ and $[a, b, d]$ then $[b, c, d]$. (Therefore also $[a, c, d]$.)
