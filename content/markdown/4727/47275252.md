# Harald Niederreiter

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harald Niederreiter"}

*Image: Harald Niederreiter.jpg.* Harald Niederreiter in [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach), 2013

**Harald G. Niederreiter** (born June 7, 1944) is an Austrian mathematician known for his work in [discrepancy theory](wikipedia://en/1116842), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [quasi-Monte Carlo methods](wikipedia://en/487599), and [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Niederreiter was born on June 7, 1944, in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), and grew up in [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg).[^o3][^o4] He began studying mathematics at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) in 1963,[^o3][^o4] and finished his doctorate there in 1969, with a thesis on [discrepancy](wikipedia://en/1116842) in [compact](wikipedia://en/750326) [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) supervised by [Edmund Hlawka](wikipedia://en/5442007). He began his academic career as an assistant professor at the University of Vienna, but soon moved to [Southern Illinois University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois_University).[^o3][^o4] During this period he also visited the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign), [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), and [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles).[^o4] In 1978 he moved again, becoming the head of a new mathematics department at the [University of the West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_West_Indies) in [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica). In 1981 he returned to Austria for a post at the [Austrian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Academy_of_Sciences), where from 1989 to 2000 he served as director of the Institutes of Information Processing and Discrete Mathematics. In 2001 he became a professor at the [National University of Singapore](wikipedia://en/286880).[^o3][^o4] In 2009 he returned to Austria again, to the Johann Radon Institute for Computational and Applied Mathematics of the Austrian Academy of Sciences. He also worked from 2010 to 2011 as a professor at the [King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Fahd_University_of_Petroleum_and_Minerals) in [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia).[^o4]

## Research {#Research}

Niederreiter's initial research interests were in the [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) and [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615), subjects also represented by his later book *Finite Fields* (with Rudolf Lidl, 1983).[^o3] From his doctoral thesis onwards, he also incorporated [discrepancy theory](wikipedia://en/1116842) and the theory of [uniformly distributed sets](wikipedia://en/38607512) in [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018) into his study of these subjects.[^o4]

In 1970, Niederreiter began to work on [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) and [random number generation](wikipedia://en/2736939), and in 1974 he published the book *Uniform Distribution of Sequences*. Combining his work on pseudorandom numbers with the [Monte Carlo method](wikipedia://en/56098), he did pioneering research in the [quasi-Monte Carlo method](wikipedia://en/487599) in the late 1970s, and again later published a book on the topic, *Random Number Generation and Quasi-Monte Carlo Methods* (1995).[^o3][^o4]

Niederreiter's interests in pseudorandom numbers also led him to study [stream ciphers](wikipedia://en/49007) in the 1980s, and this interest branched out into other areas of [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) such as [public key cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_key_cryptography). The [Niederreiter cryptosystem](wikipedia://en/21177402), an encryption system based on [error-correcting codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error-correcting_code) that can also be used for [digital signatures](wikipedia://en/59644), was developed by him in 1986.[^o3] His work in cryptography is represented by his book *Algebraic Geometry in Coding Theory and Cryptography* (with C. P. Xing, 2009).[^o4]

Returning to pure mathematics, Niederreiter has also made contributions to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) with the discovery of many dense curves over finite fields,[^o3] and published the book *Rational Points on Curves over Finite Fields: Theory and Applications* (with C. P. Xing, 2001).[^o4]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Niederreiter is a member of the [Austrian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Academy_of_Sciences) and the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina). In 1998 he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065),[^o8] and won the [Kardinal Innitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kardinal_Innitzer_Prize).[^o3][^o4] He became a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2013.[^o4]

Niederreiter's book *Random Number Generation and Quasi-Monte Carlo Methods* won the Outstanding Simulation Publication Award.[^o3]

In 2014, a workshop in honor of Niederreiter's 70th birthday was held at the Johann Radon Institute for Computational and Applied Mathematics of the [Austrian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o9] and a [Festschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) was published in his honor.[^o10]
