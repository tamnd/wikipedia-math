# Carla Cotwright-Williams

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Carla Cotwright-Williams"}

**Carla Denise Cotwright-Williams** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works as a Technical Director and [Data Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_scientist) for the [United States Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Us_government). She was the second African-American woman to earn a doctorate in mathematics at the [University of Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mississippi).

:::

## Early life and educational {#Early_life_and_educational}

She is the daughter of a police officer and grew up in [South Central Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_South_Central_Los_Angeles). She went to [Westchester High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_High_School_%28Los_Angeles%29) and attended summer enrichment programs for underrepresented students there that included courses at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), and a field trip to see the [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle) at [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA)'s [Armstrong Flight Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armstrong_Flight_Research_Center) on [Edwards Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwards_Air_Force_Base). She graduated in 1991.

As an undergraduate at [California State University, Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_Long_Beach), Cotwright-Williams started in engineering. Then, as a math major, she struggled initially and earned low enough grades to be academically disqualified from the university, but worked hard to return as a student in good standing, eventually earning a bachelor's degree in mathematics in 2000. She then earned a master's degree in mathematics from [Southern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_University) in [Baton Rouge, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_Rouge,_Louisiana), in 2002. Originally intending to follow a science & math Ph.D. track, she was persuaded to shift to pure mathematics under the mentorship of an African-American professor, Stella R. Ashford, who became the supervisor for her master's thesis in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), *Unique Factorization in Bi-Quadratic Number Fields*.

She went on to doctoral studies at the [University of Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mississippi), where she became president of the Graduate Student Council and earned a second master's degree there along the way in 2004. She completed her Ph.D. at the University of Mississippi in 2006. Her dissertation was supervised by T. James Reid and concerned [matroid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid_theory). She was the second African-American woman to earn a doctorate in mathematics at the university, and was part of a group of four African-Americans who all graduated in the same year.

## Career {#Career}

After completing her doctorate, Cotwright-Williams worked as a tenure-track faculty member in mathematics at [Wake Forest University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_University), [Hampton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_University), and [Norfolk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_State_University). While working there, in an effort to shift her career to a government track, she began studying [public policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_policy) and working on collaborative research on [Bayesian network](wikipedia://en/203996) based drone control systems with [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA), and on a [US Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Navy) project involving [measurement uncertainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurement_uncertainty). In 2010, she completed a Graduate Certificate in Public Policy Analysis at [Old Dominion University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dominion_University). She applied for an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) Congressional Fellowship, and was turned down on her first application but succeeded in her second, in 2012.

Cotwright-Williams also became a 2012–2013 Legislative Branch Fellow, under the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) Science and Technology Policy Fellowship program.[^o5] She also worked as a science and technology Fellow for both the Senate and the House of Representatives. While a Congressional Fellow she worked as a staffer on the majority staff of the [U.S. Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senate_Homeland_Security_and_Governmental_Affairs_Permanent_Subcommittee_on_Investigations) and her responsibilities included responding to the [Boston Marathon bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Marathon_bombing) in 2013.[^o6] In 2014 she worked on [data quality](wikipedia://en/1609808) for [United States Citizenship and Immigration Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Citizenship_and_Immigration_Services), and in 2015 she became Hardy-Apfel IT Fellow at the [Social Security Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_Administration). Her work at the Social Security Administration has included [business analytics](wikipedia://en/10147369) to prevent fraud and support [data warehousing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_warehousing). In 2018, with the fellowship expiring, she moved again to the [United States Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense) as a data scientist.

Cotwright-Williams continues to hold an adjunct professorial lecturer position in mathematics and statistics at [American University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University). She serves as an at-large member of the executive committee of the [Association for Women in Mathematics (AWM)](wikipedia://en/9858698).[^o7]

Her career advice includes the following quote: "Get out and talk to people and learn new things!"[^o8]
