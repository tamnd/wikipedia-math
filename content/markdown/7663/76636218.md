# Lee's L

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lee's L"}

**Lee's *L*** is a bivariate spatial [correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/29018709) which measures the association between two sets of observations made at the same spatial sites. Standard measures of [association](wikipedia://en/157057) such as the [Pearson correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/221708) do not account for the spatial dimension of data, in particular they are vulnerable to inflation due to spatial [autocorrelation](wikipedia://en/2724). Lee's *L* is available in numerous [spatial analysis](wikipedia://en/3190431) software libraries including *spdep* [^o2] and [PySAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PySAL)[^o3] (where it is called *Spatial\_Pearson*) and has been applied in diverse applications such as studying [air pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution),[^o4] [viticulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viticulture)[^o5] and housing rent.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formula"}

## Formula {#Formula}

For spatial data $x_i$ and $y_i$ measured at $N$ locations connected with the spatial weight matrix $w_{ij}$ first define the spatially lagged vector

$$\tilde{x}_i = \sum_j w_{ij} x_j$$

with a similar definition for $\tilde{y}_i$. Then Lee's *L*[^o9] is defined as

$$L_{x,y} = \frac{N}{\sum_i \left( \sum_j w_{ij} \right)^2} \frac{\sum_{ij} (\tilde{x}_i - \bar{x})(\tilde{y}_i - \bar{y}) }{ \sqrt{ \sum_i (\tilde{x}_i - \bar{x})^2} \sqrt{ \sum_i (\tilde{y}_i - \bar{y})^2} }$$

where $\bar{x}, \bar{y}$ are the mean values of $x_i, y_i$. When the spatial weight matrix is row normalized, such that $\sum_j w_{ij} = 1$, the first factor is 1.

### Alternate definition {#Alternate_definition}

Lee also defines the *spatial smoothing scalar*

$$SSS_{x} = \frac{ \sum_i (\tilde{x}_i - \bar{x})^2}{\sum_i (x_i - \bar{x})^2}$$

to measure the spatial [autocorrelation](wikipedia://en/2724) of a variable.

It is shown by Lee[^o9] that the above definition is equivalent to

$$L_{x,y} = \sqrt{ SSS_{x} } \sqrt{ SSS_{y} } r( \tilde{x}, \tilde{y} )$$

Where $r$ is the [Pearson correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/221708)

$$r(\tilde{x}, \tilde{y}) =\frac{\sum ^n _{i=1}(\tilde{x}_i - \bar{\tilde{x}})(\tilde{y}_i - \bar{\tilde{y}})}{\sqrt{\sum ^n _{i=1}(\tilde{x}_i - \bar{\tilde{x}})^2} \sqrt{\sum ^n _{i=1}(\tilde{y}_i - \bar{\tilde{y}})^2}}$$

This means Lee's L is equivalent to the Pearson correlation of the spatially lagged data, multiplied by a measure of each data set's spatial autocorrelation.
