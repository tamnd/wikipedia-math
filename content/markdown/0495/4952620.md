# Tarski's axiomatization of the reals

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tarski's axiomatization of the reals"}

In 1936, [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) gave an [axiomatization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatization) of the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) and their arithmetic, consisting of only the eight [axioms](wikipedia://en/928) shown below and a mere four [primitive notions](wikipedia://en/23706):[^o2] the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of reals denoted **R**, a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) over **R**, denoted by [infix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infix) \<, a [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) of addition over **R**, denoted by infix +, and the constant 1.

:::

Tarski's axiomatization, which is a [second-order](wikipedia://en/341428) theory, can be seen as a version of the more usual [definition of real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438#Definition) as the unique [Dedekind-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind-complete) [ordered field](wikipedia://en/22430); it is however made much more concise by avoiding multiplication altogether and using unorthodox variants of standard algebraic axioms and other subtle tricks. Tarski did not supply a proof that his axioms are sufficient or a definition for the multiplication of real numbers in his system.

Tarski also studied the [first-order](wikipedia://en/10983) theory of the structure (**R**, +, ·, \<), leading to a set of axioms for this theory and to the concept of [real closed fields](wikipedia://en/1066093).

## The axioms {#The_axioms}

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Axioms of order (primitives: R, <)"}

### Axioms of order (primitives: R, \<) {#Axioms_of_order_(primitives:_R,_<)}

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=inline-head name="Axiom 1"}

**Axiom 1**

If *x* \< *y*, then not *y* \< *x*.

\[That is, "\<" is an [asymmetric relation](wikipedia://en/1229368). This implies that "\<" is [irreflexive](wikipedia://en/200458), i.e., for all *x*, not *x* \< *x*.\]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=inline-head name="Axiom 2"}

**Axiom 2**

If *x* \< *z*, there exists a *y* such that *x* \< *y* and *y* \< *z*.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=inline-head name="Axiom 3"}

**Axiom 3**

For all subsets *X*, *Y* ⊆ **R**, if for all *x* ∈ *X* and *y* ∈ *Y*, *x* \< *y*, then there exists a *z* such that for all *x* ∈ *X* and *y* ∈ *Y*, if *x* ≠ *z* and *y* ≠ *z*, then *x* \< *z* and *z* \< *y*.

\[In other words, "\<" is [Dedekind-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind-complete), or informally: "If a set of reals *X* precedes another set of reals *Y*, then there exists at least one real number *z* separating the two sets."

This is a second-order axiom as it refers to sets and not just elements.\]

:::

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Axioms of addition (primitives: R, <, +)"}

### Axioms of addition (primitives: R, \<, +) {#Axioms_of_addition_(primitives:_R,_<,_+)}

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=inline-head name="Axiom 4"}

**Axiom 4**

*x* + (*y* + *z*) = (*x* + *z*) + *y*.

\[Note that this is an unorthodox mixture of [associativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity) and [commutativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity).\]

:::

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=inline-head name="Axiom 5"}

**Axiom 5**

For all *x*, *y*, there exists a *z* such that *x* + *z* = *y*.

\[This allows subtraction and also gives a 0.\]

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=inline-head name="Axiom 6"}

**Axiom 6**

If *x* + *y* \< *z* + *w*, then *x* \< *z* or *y* \< *w*.

\[This is the [contrapositive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrapositive) of a standard axiom for ordered groups.\]

:::

:::

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Axioms for 1 (primitives: R, <, +, 1)"}

### Axioms for 1 (primitives: R, \<, +, 1) {#Axioms_for_1_(primitives:_R,_<,_+,_1)}

::: {#o17 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=inline-head name="Axiom 7"}

**Axiom 7**

1 ∈ **R**.

:::

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=inline-head name="Axiom 8"}

**Axiom 8**

1 \< 1 + 1.

:::

:::

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

Tarski stated, without proof, that these axioms turn the relation \< into a [total ordering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_ordering). The missing component was supplied in 2008 by Stefanie Ucsnay.[^o20]

The axioms then imply that **R** is a [linearly ordered](wikipedia://en/349014) [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) under addition with distinguished positive element 1**,** and that this group is [Dedekind-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind-complete), [divisible](wikipedia://en/472147), and [Archimedean](wikipedia://en/264169).

Tarski never proved that these axioms and primitives imply the existence of a [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) called multiplication that has the expected properties, so that **R** becomes a complete [ordered field](wikipedia://en/22430) under addition and multiplication. It is possible to define this multiplication operation by considering certain order-preserving homomorphisms of the ordered group (**R**,+,\<).[^o21]
