# Normal cone (algebraic geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Normal cone"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **normal cone** of a subscheme of a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) is a scheme analogous to the [normal bundle](wikipedia://en/2370200) or [tubular neighborhood](wikipedia://en/3192042) in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The normal cone $C_{X}Y$ or $C_{X/Y}$ of an embedding $i: X \to Y$, defined by some [sheaf of ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_of_ideals) *I*, is defined as the [relative Spec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_Spec)

$$\operatorname{Spec}_X \left(\bigoplus_{n = 0}^{\infty} I^n / I^{n+1}\right).$$

When the embedding *i* is [regular](wikipedia://en/47089144) the normal cone is the normal bundle, the [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) on *X* corresponding to the dual of the sheaf $I/I^{2}$.

If *X* is a point, then the normal cone and the normal bundle to it are also called the [tangent cone](wikipedia://en/1416915) and the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) ([Zariski tangent space](wikipedia://en/510207)) to the point. When *Y* = Spec *R* is affine, the definition means that the normal cone to *X* = Spec *R*/*I* is the Spec of the [associated graded ring](wikipedia://en/38279783) of *R* with respect to *I*.

If *Y* is the product *X* × *X* and the embedding *i* is the [diagonal embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_embedding), then the normal bundle to *X* in *Y* is the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) to *X*.

The normal cone (or rather its projective cousin) appears as a result of [blow-up](wikipedia://en/1817790). Precisely, let

$$\pi: \operatorname{Bl}_X Y = \operatorname{Proj}_Y \left(\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty} I^n\right) \to Y$$

be the blow-up of *Y* along *X*. Then, by definition, the [exceptional divisor](wikipedia://en/14271694) is the pre-image $E = \pi^{-1}(X)$; which is the [projective cone](wikipedia://en/50394182) of $\bigoplus_0^{\infty} I^n \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_Y} \mathcal{O}_X = \bigoplus_0^{\infty} I^n/I^{n+1}$. Thus,

$$E = \mathbb{P}(C_X Y).$$

The global sections of the normal bundle classify [embedded infinitesimal deformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_infinitesimal_deformation) of *Y* in *X*; there is a natural [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between the set of closed subschemes of $Y \times_{k} D$, [flat](wikipedia://en/542520) over the ring *D* of [dual numbers](wikipedia://en/42169#Generalizations) and having *X* as the special fiber, and *H*<sup>0</sup>(*X*, *N*<sub>*X*</sub> *Y*).[^n1]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

### Compositions of regular embeddings {#Compositions_of_regular_embeddings}

If $i: X \hookrightarrow Y, \, j: Y \hookrightarrow Z$ are [regular embeddings](wikipedia://en/47089144), then $j \circ i$ is a regular embedding and there is a natural [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) of vector bundles on *X*:[^n2]

$$0\to N_{X/Y} \to N_{X/Z} \to i^* N_{Y/Z} \to 0.$$

If $Y_i \hookrightarrow X$ are regular embeddings of codimensions $c_i$ and if $W := \bigcap_i Y_i \hookrightarrow X$ is a regular embedding of codimension

$$\sum c_i$$

then[^n2]

$$N_{W/X} = \bigoplus_i N_{Y_i/X}|_W.$$

In particular, if $X \to S$ is a [smooth morphism](wikipedia://en/7837041), then the normal bundle to the [diagonal embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_embedding)

$$\Delta: X \hookrightarrow X \times_S \cdots \times_S X$$

(*r*-fold) is the direct sum of $r - 1$ copies of the [relative tangent bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_tangent_bundle) $T_{X/S}$.

If $X \hookrightarrow Y$ is a [closed immersion](wikipedia://en/1623986) and if $Y' \to Y$ is a flat morphism such that $X' = X \times_Y Y'$, then[^n3]

$$C_{X'/Y'} = C_{X/Y} \times_X X'.$$

If $X \to S$ is a [smooth morphism](wikipedia://en/7837041) and $X \hookrightarrow Y$ is a regular embedding, then there is a natural exact sequence of vector bundles on *X*:[^n4]

$$0 \to T_{X/S} \to T_{Y/S}|_X \to N_{X/Y} \to 0$$

(which is a special case of an exact sequence for [cotangent sheaves](wikipedia://en/44901757)).

### Cartesian square {#Cartesian_square}

For a Cartesian square of schemes

$$\begin{matrix} X' & \to & Y' \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ X & \to & Y \end{matrix}$$

with $f:X' \to X$ the vertical map, there is a closed embedding

$$C_{X'/Y'} \hookrightarrow f^*C_{X/Y}$$

of normal cones.

### Dimension of components {#Dimension_of_components}

Let $X$ be a scheme of finite type over a field and $W \subset X$ a closed subscheme. If $X$ is of [pure dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_dimension) *r*; i.e., every irreducible component has dimension *r*, then $C_{W/X}$ is also of pure dimension *r*.[^n5] (This can be seen as a consequence of #Deformation to the normal cone.) This property is a key to an application in [intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602): given a pair of closed subschemes $V, X$ in some ambient space, while the [scheme-theoretic intersection](wikipedia://en/52580781) $V \cap X$ has irreducible components of various dimensions, depending delicately on the positions of $V, X$, the normal cone to $V \cap X$ is of pure dimension.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

Let $D \hookrightarrow X$ be an effective [Cartier divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartier_divisor). Then the normal bundle to it (or equivalently the normal cone to it) is[^n6]

$$N_{D/X} = \mathcal{O}_D(D) := \mathcal{O}_X(D)|_D.$$

### Non-regular embedding {#Non-regular_embedding}

Consider the non-regular embedding[^o19]p. 4-5

$$X = \text{Spec}\left( \frac{\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]}{(xz,yz)}\right) \to \mathbb{A}^3$$

then, we can compute the normal cone by first observing

$$\begin{aligned} I &= (xz, yz) \\ I^2 &= (x^2z^2, xyz^2, y^2z^2) \\ \end{aligned}$$

If we make the auxiliary variables $a = xz$ and $b = yz$ we get the relation

$$ya - xb = 0.$$

We can use this to give a presentation of the normal cone as the relative spectrum

$$C_X \mathbb{A}^3 = \text{Spec}_X \left( \frac{\mathcal{O}_X[a,b]}{(ya-xb)} \right)$$

Since $\mathbb{A}^3$ is affine, we can just write out the relative spectrum as the affine scheme

$$C_X \mathbb{A}^3 = \text{Spec}\left( \frac{\mathbb{C}[x,y,z][a,b]}{(xz,yz,ya-xb)} \right)$$

giving us the normal cone.

#### Geometry of this normal cone {#Geometry_of_this_normal_cone}

The normal cone's geometry can be further explored by looking at the fibers for various closed points of $X$. Note that geometrically $X$ is the union of the $xy$-plane $H$ with the $z$-axis $L$,

$$X = H \cup L$$

so the points of interest are smooth points on the plane, smooth points on the axis, and the point on their intersection. Any smooth point on the plane is given by a map

$$\begin{matrix} x \mapsto z_1 & y \mapsto z_2 & z \mapsto 0 \end{matrix}$$

for $z_1,z_2 \in \mathbb{C}$ and either $z_1 \neq 0$ or $z_2\neq 0$. Since it is arbitrary which point we take, for convenience let us assume $z_1 \neq 0, z_2 = 0$. Hence the fiber of $C_X\mathbb{A}^3$ at the point $p=(z_1,0,0)$ is isomorphic to

$$C_X \mathbb{A}^3 |_p \cong \frac{\mathbb{C}[a,b]}{(z_1b)} \cong \mathbb{C}[a]$$

giving the normal cone as a one dimensional line, as expected. For a point $q$ on the axis, this is given by a map

$$\begin{matrix} x \mapsto 0 & y \mapsto 0 & z \mapsto z_3 \end{matrix}$$

hence the fiber at the point $q = (0,0,z_3)$ is

$$C_X \mathbb{A}^3 |_q \cong \frac{\mathbb{C}[a,b]}{(0)} \cong \mathbb{C}[a,b]$$

which gives a plane. At the origin $r = (0,0,0)$, the normal cone over that point is again isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}[a,b]$.

### Nodal cubic {#Nodal_cubic}

For the nodal cubic curve $Y$ given by the polynomial $y^2 + x^2(x-1)$ over $\mathbb{C}$, and $X$ the point at the node, the cone has the isomorphism

$$C_{X/Y} \cong \text{Spec}\left(\mathbb{C}[x,y]/\left(y^2-x^2\right)\right)$$

showing the normal cone has more components than the scheme it lies over.

:::

## Deformation to the normal cone {#Deformation_to_the_normal_cone}

Suppose $i : X \to Y$ is an embedding. This can be deformed to the embedding of $X$ inside the normal cone $C_{X/Y}$ (as the zero section) in the following sense:[^o19] there is a flat family

$$\pi : M^o_{X/Y} \to \mathbb{P}^1$$

with generic fiber $Y$ and special fiber $C_{X/Y}$ such that there exists a family of closed embeddings

$$X \times \mathbb{P}^1 \hookrightarrow M^o_{X/Y}$$

over $\mathbb{P}^1$ such that

1. Over any point $t \in \mathbb{P}^1-\{0\}$ the associated embeddings are an embedding $X\times\{t\} \hookrightarrow Y$
1. The fiber over $0 \in \mathbb{P}^1$ is the embedding of $X \hookrightarrow C_{X/Y}$ given by the zero section.

This construction defines a tool analogous to [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562) where non-transverse intersections are performed in a tubular neighborhood of the intersection. Now, the intersection of $X$ with a cycle $Z$ in $Y$ can be given as the pushforward of an intersection of $X$ with the pullback of $Z$ in $C_{X/Y}$.

::: {#o24 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

One application of this is to define intersection products in the [Chow ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_ring). Suppose that *X* and *V* are closed subschemes of *Y* with intersection *W*, and we wish to define the intersection product of *X* and *V* in the Chow ring of *Y*. Deformation to the normal cone in this case means that we replace the embeddings of *X* and *W* in *Y* and *V* by their normal cones *C*<sub>*Y*</sub>(*X*) and *C*<sub>*W*</sub>(*V*), so that we want to find the product of *X* and *C*<sub>*W*</sub>*V* in *C*<sub>*X*</sub>*Y*. This can be much easier: for example, if *X* is [regularly embedded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regularly_embedded) in *Y* then its normal cone is a vector bundle, so we are reduced to the problem of finding the intersection product of a subscheme *C*<sub>*W*</sub>*V* of a vector bundle *C*<sub>*X*</sub>*Y* with the zero section *X*. However this intersection product is just given by applying the Gysin isomorphism to *C*<sub>*W*</sub>*V*.

Concretely, the deformation to the normal cone can be constructed by means of blowup. Precisely, let

$$\pi: M \to Y \times \mathbb{P}^1$$

be the blow-up of $Y \times \mathbb{P}^1$ along $X \times 0$. The exceptional divisor is $\overline{C_X Y} = \mathbb{P}(C_X Y \oplus 1)$, the projective completion of the normal cone; for the notation used here see Cone (algebraic geometry)#Properties. The normal cone $C_X Y$ is an open subscheme of $\overline{C_X Y}$ and $X$ is embedded as a zero-section into $C_X Y$.

Now, we note:

1. The map $\rho: M \to \mathbb{P}^1$, the $\pi$ followed by projection, is flat.
1. There is an induced closed embedding $$\widetilde{i}: X \times \mathbb{P}^1 \hookrightarrow M$$ that is a morphism over $\mathbb{P}^1$.
1. *M* is trivial away from zero; i.e., $\rho^{-1}(\mathbb{P}^1 - 0)= Y \times (\mathbb{P}^1 - 0)$ and $\widetilde{i}$ restricts to the trivial embedding $$X \times (\mathbb{P}^1 - 0) \hookrightarrow Y \times (\mathbb{P}^1 - 0).$$
1. $\rho^{-1}(0)$ as the divisor is the sum $$\overline{C_X Y} + \widetilde{Y}$$ where $\widetilde{Y}$ is the blow-up of *Y* along *X* and is viewed as an effective Cartier divisor.
1. As divisors $\overline{C_X Y}$ and $\widetilde{Y}$ intersect at $\mathbb{P}(C)$, where $\mathbb{P}(C)$ sits at infinity in $\overline{C_X Y}$.

Item 1 is clear (check torsion-free-ness). In general, given $X \subset Y$, we have $\operatorname{Bl}_V X \subset \operatorname{Bl}_V Y$. Since $X \times 0$ is already an effective Cartier divisor on $X \times \mathbb{P}^1$, we get

$$X \times \mathbb{P}^1 = \operatorname{Bl}_{X \times 0} X \times \mathbb{P}^1 \hookrightarrow M,$$

yielding $\widetilde{i}$. Item 3 follows from the fact the blowdown map π is an isomorphism away from the center $X \times 0$. The last two items are seen from explicit local computation. [Q.E.D.](wikipedia://en/25228)

Now, the last item in the previous paragraph implies that the image of $X \times 0$ in *M* does not intersect $\widetilde{Y}$. Thus, one gets the deformation of *i* to the zero-section embedding of *X* into the normal cone.

:::

## Intrinsic normal cone {#Intrinsic_normal_cone}

### Intrinsic normal bundle {#Intrinsic_normal_bundle}

Let $X$ be a [Deligne–Mumford stack](wikipedia://en/40639179) locally of finite type over a field $k$. If $\textbf{L}_X$ denotes the [cotangent complex](wikipedia://en/17346860) of *X* relative to $k$, then the **intrinsic normal bundle**[^o27] to $X$ is the [quotient stack](wikipedia://en/40579087)

$$\mathfrak{N}_X := h^1 / h^0(\textbf{L}_{X, \text{fppf}}^{\vee})$$

which is the stack of fppf $\textbf{L}_X^{\vee, 0}$-[torsors](wikipedia://en/41910974) on $\textbf{L}_X^{\vee, 1}$. A concrete interpretation of this stack quotient can be given by looking at its behavior locally in the étale topos of the stack $X$.

#### Properties of intrinsic normal bundle {#Properties_of_intrinsic_normal_bundle}

More concretely, suppose there is an étale morphism $U \to X$ from an affine finite-type $k$-scheme $U$ together with a locally closed immersion $f: U \to M$ into a smooth affine finite-type $k$-scheme $M$. Then one can show

$$\mathfrak{N}_X |_U = [N_{U/M}/f^* T_M]$$

meaning we can understand the intrinsic normal bundle as a stacky incarnation for the failure of the normal sequence

$$\mathcal{T}_U \to \mathcal{T}_M |_U \to \mathcal{N}_{U/M}$$

to be exact on the right hand side. Moreover, for special cases discussed below, we are now considering the quotient as a continuation of the previous sequence as a triangle in some [triangulated category](wikipedia://en/1679700). This is because the local stack quotient $[N_{U/M}/f^* T_M]$ can be interpreted as

$$B \mathcal{T}_U = \mathcal{T}_U[+1]$$

in certain cases.

### Normal cone {#Normal_cone}

The **intrinsic normal cone** to $X$, denoted as $\mathfrak{C}_X$,[^o27] is then defined by replacing the normal bundle $N_{U/M}$ with the normal cone $C_{U/M}$; i.e.,

$$\mathfrak{C}_X|_U = [C_{U/M} / f^* T_M].$$

::: {#o30 .example cue=inline-head name="Example"}

**Example**: One has that $X$ is a local complete intersection if and only if $\mathfrak{C}_X = \mathfrak{N}_X$. In particular, if $X$ is [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_stack), then $\mathfrak{C}_X = \mathfrak{N}_X = B T_X$ is the [classifying stack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classifying_stack) of the tangent bundle $T_X$, which is a commutative [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) over $X$.

More generally, let $X \to Y$ is a Deligne-Mumford Type (DM-type) morphism of Artin Stacks which is locally of finite type. Then $\mathfrak{C}_{X/Y} \subseteq \mathfrak{N}_{X/Y}$ is characterised as the closed substack such that, for any étale map $U \to X$ for which $U \to X \to Y$ factors through some smooth map $M \to Y$ (e.g., $\mathbb{A}_Y^n \to Y$), the pullback is:

$$\mathfrak{C}_{X/Y}|_U = [C_{U/M} / T_{M/Y}|_U].$$

:::
