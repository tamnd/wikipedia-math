# Bingham distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bingham distribution"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Bingham distribution**, named after [Christopher Bingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Bingham), is an [antipodally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipodal_symmetry) [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) on the [*n*-sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersphere).[^o2] It is a generalization of the Watson distribution and a special case of the [Kent](wikipedia://en/5624561) and Fisher–Bingham distributions.

:::

The Bingham distribution is widely used in [paleomagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleomagnetic) data analysis,[^o3] and has been used in the field of [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision).[^o4][^o5][^o6]

Its [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) is given by

$$f(\mathbf{x}\,;\,M,Z) \; dS^{n-1} = {}_1 F_1 \left( \tfrac12 ; \tfrac n2 ; Z \right)^{-1} \cdot \exp \left( \operatorname{tr} Z M^T \mathbf{x} \mathbf{x}^T M \right)\; dS^{n-1}$$

which may also be written

$$f(\mathbf{x}\,;\,M,Z)\; dS^{n-1} \;=\; {}_1 F_1 \left( \tfrac12 ; \tfrac n2 ;Z \right)^{-1} \cdot \exp\left( \mathbf{x}^T M Z M^T \mathbf{x} \right)\; dS^{n-1}$$

where **x** is an axis (i.e., a unit vector), *M* is an [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/105620) orientation matrix, *Z* is a diagonal concentration matrix, and ${}_{1}F_{1}(\cdot;\cdot,\cdot)$ is a confluent [hypergeometric function of matrix argument](wikipedia://en/8664461). The matrices *M* and *Z* are the result of [diagonalizing](wikipedia://en/246325) the [positive-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_matrix) covariance matrix of the [Gaussian distribution](wikipedia://en/50347) that underlies the Bingham distribution.
