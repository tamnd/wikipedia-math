# Stencil (numerical analysis)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stencil"}

*Image: Crank-Nicolson-stencil.svg.* The [Crank–Nicolson](wikipedia://en/1843447) stencil for a 1D problem

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially the areas of [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) concentrating on the [numerical solution of partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_partial_differential_equations), a **stencil** is a geometric arrangement of a nodal group that relates to the point of interest by using a numerical approximation routine. Stencils are the basis for many algorithms to numerically solve [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) (PDE). Two examples of stencils are the [five-point stencil](wikipedia://en/4342582) and the [Crank–Nicolson method](wikipedia://en/1843447) stencil.

:::

Stencils are classified into two categories: [compact](wikipedia://en/18554484) and [non-compact](wikipedia://en/18555870), the difference being the layers from the point of interest that are also used for calculation.

In the notation used for one-dimensional stencils n-1, n, n+1 indicate the time steps where timestep n and n-1 have known solutions and time step n+1 is to be calculated. The spatial location of finite volumes used in the calculation are indicated by j-1, j and j+1.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

Graphical representations of node arrangements and their coefficients arose early in the study of PDEs. Authors continue to use varying terms for these such as "relaxation patterns", "operating instructions", "lozenges", or "point patterns".[^o3][^o4] The term "stencil" was coined for such patterns to reflect the concept of laying out a [stencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stencil) in the usual sense over a computational grid to reveal just the numbers needed at a particular step.[^o4]

## Calculation of coefficients {#Calculation_of_coefficients}

The [finite difference coefficients](wikipedia://en/26835669) for a given stencil are fixed by the choice of node points. The coefficients may be calculated by taking the derivative of the [Lagrange polynomial](wikipedia://en/217523) interpolating between the node points,[^o7] by computing the [Taylor expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_expansion) around each node point and solving a linear system,[^o6] or by enforcing that the stencil is exact for [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416) up to the degree of the stencil.[^o7] For equi-spaced nodes, they may be calculated efficiently as the [Padé approximant](wikipedia://en/3090820) of $x^s \cdot (\log x)^m$, where $m$ is the order of the stencil and $s$ is the ratio of the distance between the leftmost derivative and the left function entries divided by the grid spacing.[^o8]
