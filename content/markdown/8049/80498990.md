# Hartmanis–Stearns conjecture

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

If the expansion of a real $x$ in some base $b \geq 2$ is real-time computable, must $x$ be rational or transcendental?

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hartmanis–Stearns conjecture"}

In [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hartmanis–Stearns conjecture** is an open problem named after [Juris Hartmanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Hartmanis) and [Richard E. Stearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_E._Stearns), who posed it in a 1965 paper that founded the field of [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543) (earning them the 1993 [ACM Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Turing_Award)).

:::

An [infinite word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_word) is said to be *real-time computable* when there exists a [multitape Turing machine](wikipedia://en/4631387) which (run without input) writes the successive letters of the word on its output tape, taking a bounded amount of time between two successive letters. Equivalently, there exists a multitape Turing machine which given a natural number $n$ in unary outputs the first $n$ letters of the word in time $O(n)$. The Hartmanis–Stearns conjecture states that if $x$ is a real number whose expansion in some base $b \geq 2$ (e.g., the [decimal expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_expansion) for $b = 10$) is real-time computable, then $x$ is [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) or [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325).

The conjecture has the deep implication that there is no [integer multiplication algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_multiplication_algorithm) in $O(n)$ (while an $O(n \log(n))$ algorithm is known).

A partial result was proved by Boris Adamczewski and Yann Bugeaud (a previous claimed proof by John H. Loxton and [Alfred van der Poorten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_van_der_Poorten) turned out to contain a gap): $x$ is rational or transcendental if the expansion of $x$ in some base $b \geq 2$ is an [automatic sequence](wikipedia://en/7740302). This was subsequently generalized by Boris Adamczewski, Julien Cassaigne and Marion Le Gonidec to sequences generated by [deterministic pushdown automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic_pushdown_automata).
