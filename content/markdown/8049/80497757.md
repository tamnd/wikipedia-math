# Dean G. Hoffman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dean G. Hoffman"}

**Dean Gunnar Hoffman** (May 8, 1949 – November 11, 2022) was an American mathematician who worked primarily in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [design theory](wikipedia://en/2761968), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and [puzzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle).[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hoffman was born on May 8, 1949. He was a [backgammon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backgammon) player and would commonly play with students and collaborators. In 1970, he obtained his BA in mathematics from [Union College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_College). He then went on to be advised by [David Klarner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_A._Klarner) and Ron Mullin and obtain his PhD in mathematics from the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) in 1976. [^o4]

## Career {#Career}

He began working at Auburn University in 1977 until his death in 2022.[^o4]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

He has been awarded the Marie Kraska Award for the Excellence in Teaching in 2017 and 2022.[^o7][^o8]

In 2008, he was an invited speaker for the first Istanbul Design Theory and Combinatorics Conference which was held to celebrate the 70th birthday of [Curt Lindner](wikipedia://en/80785873), one of his collaborators.[^o9] In 2014, Hoffman was invited to give the keynote address for the 8th Annual Troy MathFest at [Troy University at Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_University_at_Montgomery).[^o10]

## Puzzles {#Puzzles}

The [Hoffman's packing puzzle](wikipedia://en/62364657) is an [assembly puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_puzzle#Assembly_puzzles) which is named after Hoffman, who described it in 1978.[^o12] The puzzle consists of 27 identical rectangular [cuboids](wikipedia://en/156802), each of whose edges have three different lengths. The goal of the puzzle is to assemble them all to fit within a cube whose edge length is the sum of the three lengths.[^o13]

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

He died on November 11, 2022.

His family created the Dr. Dean G. Hoffman Endowed Graduate Award in his honor.[^o15]
