# Devil's curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Devil's curve"}

*Image: Devils curve a=0.8 b=1.svg.* Devil's curve for *a* = 0.8 and *b* = 1.

*Image: Devils curve a=0.0-1.0 b=1.svg.* Devil's curve with $a$ ranging from 0 to 1 and *b* = 1 (with the curve colour going from blue to red).

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **Devil's curve**, also known as the **Devil on Two Sticks**, is a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) defined in the [Cartesian plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_plane) by an equation of the form[^o2]

:::

$$y^4 - b^2y^2 = x^4 - a^2x^2$$

i.e. a Quartic, in terms of x and y.

The [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation) of this curve is of the form

$$r = \sqrt{\frac{b^2 \sin^2\theta-a^2 \cos^2\theta}{\sin^2\theta-\cos^2\theta}} = \sqrt{\frac{b^2 -a^2 \cot^2\theta}{1-\cot^2\theta}}$$

Devil's curves were discovered in 1750 by [Gabriel Cramer](wikipedia://en/499331), who studied them extensively.[^o3]

The name comes from the shape its central [lemniscate](wikipedia://en/5210590) takes when graphed. The shape is named after the juggling game [diabolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabolo), which was named after the Devil[^o4] and which involves two sticks, a string, and a spinning prop in the likeness of the lemniscate.[^o5]

For $|b|>|a|$, the central lemniscate, often called hour-glass, is horizontal, For $|b|<|a|$ it is vertical. If $|b|=|a|$, the shape becomes a circle. The vertical hour-glass intersects the y-axis at $-b,0,b$ . The horizontal hour-glass intersects the x-axis at $-a,0,a$ and for the four turning points, for this hour-glass,

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x(a^2-2x^2)}{y(b^2-2y^2)}=0$$

giving:

$$x=+/- \frac{\sqrt2}{2}a$$

Total Area of horizontal hour-glass

$$\approx 2.8284\int_{0}^{a}\sqrt{b^2-\sqrt{-4a^2x^2+b^4+4x^4}}\,dx$$

## Electric Motor Curve {#Electric_Motor_Curve}

A special case of the Devil's curve occurs at $\frac{a^2}{b^2}=\frac{25}{24}$, where the curve is called the **electric motor curve**.[^o7] It is defined by an equation of the form

$$y^2(y^2-96) = x^2(x^2-100)$$

The name of the special case comes from the middle shape's resemblance to the coils of wire, which rotate from forces exerted by magnets surrounding it.
