# Eduard Stiefel

*Image: Otto Volk, Eduard Stiefel.jpeg.* Eduard Stiefel (right) together with Otto Volk in [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach), 1978

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eduard Stiefel"}

**Eduard L. Stiefel** (21 April 1909 – 25 November 1978) was a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Together with [Cornelius Lanczos](wikipedia://en/1657748) and [Magnus Hestenes](wikipedia://en/12223917), he invented the [conjugate gradient method](wikipedia://en/1448821), and gave what is now understood to be a partial construction of the [Stiefel–Whitney classes](wikipedia://en/877761) of a [real vector bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_vector_bundle), thus co-founding the study of [characteristic classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_classes).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Eduard Stiefel was born on 21 April 1909 in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich). His father was the painter and graphic artist [Eduard Stiefel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Stiefel_%28painter%29), after whom he was named.

Stiefel attended schools in his hometown and from 1927 to 1931 studied mathematics and physics at the [Swiss Federal Institute of Technology (ETH Zurich)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich). He graduated with distinction in 1931 and continued his studies in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen). In 1935 he received his doctorate from ETH under [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082), with a dissertation entitled *Richtungsfelder und Fernparallelismus in n-dimensionalen Mannigfaltigkeiten* (Direction fields and distant parallelism in n-dimensional manifolds). After a period as a research assistant, Stiefel completed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation), becoming a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) in 1942 and a full professor at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) in 1943.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Stiefel's early research was in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), including work on the topology of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) and [fibre bundles](wikipedia://en/276526).[^o6] A class of [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530), the [Stiefel–Whitney class](wikipedia://en/877761), bears his name.[^o7][^o8] His classification of fibre bundles over [spheres](wikipedia://en/39782) also found application in the classification of algebras, as discussed by [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) in [Heinz-Dieter Ebbinghaus](wikipedia://en/28870090) et al., *Zahlen* (Springer, 1992).[^o9]

Besides his academic pursuits, Stiefel was also active as a military officer, rising to the rank of colonel in the Swiss army during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).

He later turned entirely to [numerical mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_mathematics). After achieving his full professorship at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) in 1943, Stiefel founded the [Institute for Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/42563941) in 1948, which he also directed. The objective of the new institute was to design and construct an electronic computer (the *Elektronische Rechenmaschine der ETH*, or [ERMETH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ERMETH)). For this, Stiefel located a [Z4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuse_Z4) computer by [Konrad Zuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Zuse) in Bavaria in 1949; the machine was leased from Zuse and restored to working order for ETH. This made ETH Zurich the first university on the European continent to operate a programmable computing machine, which was used, among other tasks, to calculate the [gravity wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_dam) of a dam. Stiefel later sent his assistants [Heinz Rutishauser](wikipedia://en/29245633) and [Ambros Speiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambros_Speiser) to the United States to study computing machines, which led to the development of ETH's own computer, [ERMETH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ERMETH), in operation from 1955 to 1963.

He also spent a year in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) commencing in August, 1951. During this time, he met [Magnus Hestenes](wikipedia://en/12223917) and other scientists at the [National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Standards) and these professional associations served him during the remainder of his career at [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich).

Stiefel also worked on [celestial mechanics](wikipedia://en/102182),[^o10] in particular the calculation of satellite orbits, and more broadly on [perturbation theory](wikipedia://en/102140).[^o11][^o12][^o13] A regularisation method developed by him and Paul Kustaanheimo, applying [spinors](wikipedia://en/29276) and a transformation in four-dimensional space, bears his name – The [Kustaanheimo–Stiefel regularization](wikipedia://en/84111349). Stiefel received research contracts from [ESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Space_Agency) (and its predecessor [ESRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESRO)) and from [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA). From 1966 to 1970 he served as president of the Swiss Committee for Space Research.

Stiefel, who edited [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522)'s lectures on representation theory at ETH Zurich in 1936,[^o14] also authored a book on [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and one on [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891).[^o15]

In 1952 he developed the [conjugate gradient method](wikipedia://en/1448821) in [numerical linear algebra](wikipedia://en/7330660) together with Magnus Hestenes.[^o16]

In 1956 he served as president of the [Swiss Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Mathematical_Society).[^o17][^o18] From 1970 to 1974 he was president of the [Society for Applied Mathematics and Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesellschaft_für_Angewandte_Mathematik_und_Mechanik) (GAMM).[^o18]

Stiefel was a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters) and the [Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_National_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina), and received honorary doctorates from [KU Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KU_Leuven) (1971), the [University of Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Würzburg) (1974), and the [University of Braunschweig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Braunschweig) (1975).[^o19] In 1968 he held a visiting professorship at the [Université libre de Bruxelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_libre_de_Bruxelles).[^o19] Stiefel also served as a colonel of artillery in the [Swiss Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Army) and was a member of the Zürich city council from 1958 to 1966, representing the [Landesring der Unabhängigen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Independents).[^o19]

In 1954 he delivered a plenary lecture at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam), entitled "Recent developments in relaxation techniques".[^o20]

*Image: Eduard Stiefel Grabstein Friedhof Witikon Zürich.jpg.* Stiefel's grave at Witikon Cemetery, [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich)

Among Stiefel's doctoral students were [Ambros Speiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambros_Speiser), [Peter Henrici](wikipedia://en/31964476), Urs Kirchgraber, Fritz-Rudolf Güntsch, [Max Rössler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Rössler), and Jörg Waldvogel.[^o21]
