# Magnus Hestenes

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Magnus Hestenes"}

**Magnus Rudolph Hestenes** (February 13, 1906 – May 31, 1991) was an American mathematician best known for his contributions to [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) and [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565).[^o3] As a pioneer in computer science, he devised the [conjugate gradient method](wikipedia://en/1448821), published jointly with [Eduard Stiefel](wikipedia://en/12223876).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Bricelyn, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bricelyn,_Minnesota), Hestenes graduated with a B.S. in 1927 from [St. Olaf College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Olaf_College) and with an M.A. in 1928 from the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison).[^o7] He earned his Ph.D. at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1932 under [Gilbert Bliss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Bliss). His dissertation was titled "Sufficient Conditions for the General Problem of Mayer with Variable End-Points." After teaching as an associate professor at Chicago, in 1947 he moved to a professorship at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles). He continued there until his retirement in 1973, and during that time he served as department chair from 1950 to 1958. While a professor, Hestenes supervised the thesis research of 34 students, among them [Glen Culler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Culler), [Richard Tapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Tapia) and [Jesse Wilkins, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Ernest_Wilkins,_Jr.)

Hestenes received [Guggenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) (1954) and [Fulbright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) awards, was a vice president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), and was an invited speaker at the 1954 International Congress of Mathematicians in Amsterdam.[^o8]

He is the father of mathematician and physicist [David Hestenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hestenes).

He died on May 31, 1991, in Los Angeles, California.
