# Glennie's identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glennie's identity"}

In mathematics, **Glennie's identity** is an identity used by Charles M. Glennie to establish some s-identities that are valid in [special Jordan algebras](wikipedia://en/936371#Special_Jordan_algebras) but not in all [Jordan algebras](wikipedia://en/936371). A Jordan s-identity ("s" for special) is a Jordan polynomial[^o2] which vanishes in all special Jordan algebras but not in all Jordan algebras. What is now known as Glennie's identity first appeared in his 1963 Yale PhD thesis with [Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson) as thesis advisor.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let • denote the product in a special Jordan algebra $A$. For all *X*, *Y*, *Z* in *A*, define the Jordan triple product

1. {*X*,*Y*,*Z*} = *X*•(*Y*•*Z*) − *Y*•(*Z*•*X*) + *Z*•(*X*•*Y*) then Glennie's identity *G*<sub>8</sub> holds in the form:
1. 2{ {*Z*,{*X*,*Y*,*X*},*Z*}, *Y*, *Z*•*X*} − {*Z*, {*X*, {*Y*, *X*•*Z*, *Y*}, *X*}, *Z*} = 2{ *X*•*Z*, *Y*, {*X*, {*Z*,*Y*,*Z*}, *X*} } − {*X*, {*Z*, {*Y*,*X*•*Z*,*Y*}, *Z*}, *X*}.[^o5]

:::

## References {#References}

[^o2]: In this context, Jordan polynomial is a polynomial operator on a Jordan algebra. Jordan polynomial has a different meaning in the context of the Jordan normal form.
