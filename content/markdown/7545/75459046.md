# Carolyn Morgan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carolyn Morgan"}

**Carolyn Bradshaw Morgan** is an American statistician and applied mathematician, one of the first [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans) undergraduates at [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University), and the former chair of the mathematics department at [Hampton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Morgan was born in [Winston-Salem, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston-Salem,_North_Carolina), the oldest of three children of a single mother. She grew up going to [segregated schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States), but with integrated [Advanced Placement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Placement) classes and summer programs; she was her high school [valedictorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian). She became a student at [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University), supported by a Rockefeller Scholarship. She majored in mathematics, and graduated in 1969 with her future husband, chemical engineer Morris Morgan. Both were among the eight African-American undergraduate students first admitted when Vanderbilt desegregated in the mid-1960s.

Morgan continued for a master's degree in mathematics at [Wright State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_State_University), and worked as a schoolteacher and as a [computer programmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programmer) for [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) before joining the [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric) research and development center in [Schenectady, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schenectady,_New_York) in 1973. While there, she completed a Ph.D. in administrative and engineering systems and statistics at [Union College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_College) in Schenectady in 1982.

She continued to work for GE until 1996, including participation in the development of the [GE Profile dishwasher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Appliances). From 1996 to 2007 she was chair of the mathematics department at [Hampton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_University), a [historically black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historically_black_colleges_and_universities) university in [Hampton, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton,_Virginia), and continued afterwards as a professor of mathematics there.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Morgan is a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association), elected to the 1995 class of fellows.
