# Olympic gel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Olympic gel"}

*Image: Classical gel vs. Olympic gel.png.* Comparison of the network structures of a classical polymer gel and an Olympic gel.[^o2]

An **Olympic gel** is a type of [material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material) made of [polymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer) molecules that are connected like the rings in the [Olympic symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_symbol). Each pair of linked molecules forms a [catenane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catenane), and Olympic gels may be considered a type of [mechanically interlocked molecular architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanically_interlocked_molecular_architecture). The name and concept originate with the work of [Pierre-Gilles de Gennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Gilles_de_Gennes),[^o3] and an illustration of an Olympic gel appears on the cover of certain editions of his book *Scaling Concepts in Polymer Physics*. They are initially of interest to the [materials physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_physics) community due to their predicted nonlinear [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29),[^o4] stretching differently than a [Hookean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hookean) material with different behavior at low and high forces.

:::

The existence of [enzymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzymes) that can modify the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) allows the construction of Olympic gels from DNA in various ways. One method uses [Topoisomerase II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topoisomerase_II) to form links between circular molecules such as [plasmids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasmids), which in high enough density allows the formation of an Olympic gel.[^o5] Another method uses [Watson-Crick base pairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson-Crick_base_pairing) with complimentary sequences on opposite ends of linear DNA molecules. If there are enough unique sequences of DNA in a sample, molecules are more likely to form loops than [concatemers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concatemers), and after [ligation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligation_%28molecular_biology%29) an Olympic gel can be formed.[^o6]

Naturally occurring Olympic gels are found in [kinetoplast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetoplast) DNA in the [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondria) of [trypanosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trypanosoma) parasites. Kinetoplasts consist of thousands of linked DNA circular DNA molecules, in which smaller molecules are transcribed into [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA) molecules that can edit the RNA transcribed from [mitochondrial DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_DNA).

There have been reports of synthetic Olympic gels created from [hydroxyethyl lipoate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxyethyl_lipoate).[^o7]
