# David Eisenbud

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Eisenbud"}

**David Eisenbud** (born 8 April 1947) is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and former director of the then [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) (MSRI), now known as Simons Laufer Mathematical Sciences Institute (SLMath). He served as director of MSRI from 1997 to 2007, and then again from 2013 to 2022.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Eisenbud is the son of [mathematical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physicist) [Leonard Eisenbud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Eisenbud), who was a student and collaborator of the renowned [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) [Eugene Wigner](wikipedia://en/10101).[^o4] Eisenbud received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1970 from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), where he was a student of [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574) and, unofficially, [James Christopher Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Christopher_Robson). He then taught at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) from 1970 to 1997, during which time he had visiting positions at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) (IHÉS), [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), and [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) (CNRS). He joined the staff at MSRI in 1997, and took a position at Berkeley at the same time.

From 2003 to 2005 Eisenbud was president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o5]

Eisenbud's mathematical interests include [commutative](wikipedia://en/245990) and [non-commutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative_algebra), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), and computational methods in these fields. He has written over 150 papers and books with over 60 co-authors. Notable contributions include the theory of matrix factorizations for [maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules](wikipedia://en/497407) over [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) rings, the [Eisenbud–Goto conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenbud–Goto_conjecture) on degrees of generators of [syzygy modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syzygy_module), and the [Buchsbaum–Eisenbud criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchsbaum–Eisenbud_criterion) for [exactness of a complex](wikipedia://en/138215). He also proposed the [Eisenbud–Evans conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenbud–Evans_conjecture), which was later settled by the [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) mathematician [Neithalath Mohan Kumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neithalath_Mohan_Kumar).[^o6]

Eisenbud has had 31 doctoral students, including [Craig Huneke](wikipedia://en/41178067), [Mircea Mustaţă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_Mustaţă), [Irena Peeva](wikipedia://en/43076151), and Gregory G. Smith (winner of the [Aisenstadt Prize](wikipedia://en/8534576) in 2007).

Eisenbud's hobbies are [juggling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juggling) (he has written two papers on the [mathematics of juggling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_juggling)) and music. He has appeared in [Brady Haran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brady_Haran)'s online video channel "[Numberphile](wikipedia://en/61716514)".[^o7]

Eisenbud was elected Fellow of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences in 2006.[^o8] He was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) in 2010. In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o9]
