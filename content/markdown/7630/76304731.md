# Deficiency (statistics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Deficiency"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **deficiency** is a measure to compare a [statistical model](wikipedia://en/27576) with another statistical model. The concept was introduced in the 1960s by the French mathematician [Lucien Le Cam](wikipedia://en/2629104), who used it to prove an approximative version of the [Blackwell–Sherman–Stein theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwell–Sherman–Stein_theorem).[^o2][^o3] Closely related is the **Le Cam distance**, a [pseudometric](wikipedia://en/68503) for the maximum deficiency between two statistical models. If the deficiency of a model $\mathcal{E}$ in relation to $\mathcal{F}$ is zero, then one says $\mathcal{E}$ is *better* or *more informative* or *stronger* than $\mathcal{F}$.

:::

## Introduction {#Introduction}

Le Cam defined the statistical model more abstract than a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) with a family of probability measures. He also didn't use the term "statistical model" and instead used the term "experiment". In his publication from 1964 he introduced the statistical experiment to a parameter set $\Theta$ as a triple $(X,E,(P_\theta)_{\theta\in\Theta})$ consisting of a set $X$, a [vector lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_lattice) $E$ with unit $I$ and a family of normalized [positive functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_functional) $(P_\theta)_{\theta \in \Theta}$ on $E$.[^o5][^o6] In his book from 1986 he omitted $E$ and $X$.[^o7] This article follows his definition from 1986 and uses his terminology to emphasize the generalization.

## Formulation {#Formulation}

### Basic concepts {#Basic_concepts}

Let $\Theta$ be a [parameter space](wikipedia://en/2882813). Given an [abstract L<sub>1</sub>-space](wikipedia://en/63990150) $(L,\|\cdot\|)$ (i.e. a [Banach lattice](wikipedia://en/54391334) such that for elements $x,y\geq 0$ also $\|x+y\|=\|x\|+\|y\|$ holds) consisting of lineare positive functionals $\{P_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\}$. An *experiment* $\mathcal{E}$ is a map $\mathcal{E}:\Theta \to L$ of the form $\theta \mapsto P_{\theta}$, such that $\|P_{\theta}\|=1$. $L$ is the band induced by $\{P_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\}$ and therefore we use the notation $L(\mathcal{E})$. For a $\mu\in L(\mathcal{E})$ denote the $\mu^{+}=\mu \vee 0=\max(\mu,0)$. The [topological dual](wikipedia://en/7988) $M$ of an L-space with the conjugated norm $\|u\|_M=\sup\{|\langle u,\mu\rangle|; \|\mu\|_L\leq 1\}$ is called an *abstract M-space*. It's also a lattice with unit defined through $I \mu=\|\mu^+\|_L-\|\mu^-\|_L$ for $\mu\in L$.

Let $L(A)$ and $L(B)$ be two L-space of two experiments $A$ and $B$, then one calls a positive, norm-preserving [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102), i.e. $\|T\mu^{+}\|=\|\mu^{+}\|$ for all $\mu\in L(A)$, a transition. The adjoint of a transitions is a positive linear map from the dual space $M_B$ of $L(B)$ into the dual space $M_A$ of $L(A)$, such that the unit of $M_A$ is the image of the unit of $M_B$ ist.[^o7]

### Deficiency {#Deficiency}

Let $\Theta$ be a parameter space and $\mathcal{E}:\theta \to P_\theta$ and $\mathcal{F}:\theta \to Q_\theta$ be two experiments indexed by $\Theta$. Le $L(\mathcal{E})$ and $L(\mathcal{F})$ denote the corresponding L-spaces and let $\mathcal{T}$ be the set of all transitions from $L(\mathcal{E})$ to $L(\mathcal{F})$.

The **deficiency** $\delta(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})$ of $\mathcal{E}$ in relation to $\mathcal{F}$ is the number defined in terms of [inf sup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inf_sup):

$\delta(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}):=\inf\limits_{T\in \mathcal{T}}\sup\limits_{\theta \in \Theta} \tfrac{1}{2}\|Q_{\theta}-TP_{\theta}\|_{\text{TV}},$[^o12]

where $\|\cdot\|_{\text{TV}}$ denoted the [total variation norm](wikipedia://en/683561#Modern_definition_of_total_variation_norm) $\|\mu\|_{\text{TV}}=\mu^{+}+\mu^{-}$. The factor $\tfrac{1}{2}$ is just for computational purposes and is sometimes omitted.

#### Explanations {#Explanations}

- $\delta(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})=0$ means that there exists a transition $T$ such that $TP_{\theta}=Q_{\theta}$ for all $\theta \in \Theta$.
- The deficiency measures how well $Q_{\theta}$ of $P_{\theta}$ can be approximated by $T$ in the sense of total variation.
- The deficiency is a norm for $Q_{\theta}-TP_{\theta}$.

::: {#o15 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Le Cam distance"}

### Le Cam distance {#Le_Cam_distance}

The **Le Cam distance** is the following pseudometric

$$\Delta(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}):= \operatorname{max}\left(\delta(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}),\delta(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})\right).$$

This induces an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) and when $\Delta(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})=0$, then one says $\mathcal{E}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ are *equivalent*. The equivalent class $C_{\mathcal{E}}$ of $\mathcal{E}$ is also called the *type of $\mathcal{E}$*.

Often one is interested in families of experiments $(\mathcal{E}_n)_{n}$ with $\{P_{n,\theta}\colon \theta \in \Theta_{n}\}$ and $(\mathcal{F}_n)_{n}$ with $\{Q_{n,\theta}\colon \theta \in \Theta_{n}\}$. If $\Delta(\mathcal{E}_n,\mathcal{F}_n)=0$ as $n\to \infty$, then one says $(\mathcal{E}_n)$ and $(\mathcal{F}_n)$ are *asymptotically equivalent*.

Let $\Theta$ be a parameter space and $E(\Theta)$ be the set of all types that are induced by $\Theta$, then the Le Cam distance $\Delta$ is complete with respect to $E(\Theta)$. The condition $\delta(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})=0$ induces a [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) on $E(\Theta)$, one says $\mathcal{E}$ is *better* or *more informative* or *stronger* than $\mathcal{F}$.[^o12]
