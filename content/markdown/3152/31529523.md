# Population Investigation Committee

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Population Investigation Committee"}

The **Population Investigation Committee** is a United Kingdom social research group founded in 1936 by the council of the [British Eugenics Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Eugenics_Society).[^o5]

:::

Its original members included [David V. Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Glass_%28sociologist%29), [Griselda Rowntree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griselda_Rowntree) and [J.W.B. Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_W._B._Douglas).[^o2] Since the second world war it has been housed at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) (LSE).[^o13]

The PIC founded a journal, *[Population Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_Studies_%28journal%29)* and also a Scholarship Fund for UK students studying for master's degrees in demography.[^o3]

## Formation {#Formation}

The origins of the PIC lie in an annual lecture of the Eugenics Society. On 16 February 1935, the Society's [Galton](wikipedia://en/49072) Lecture was delivered by [Sir Alexander Carr-Saunders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Carr-Saunders), Charles Booth Chair of Social Science at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool), and Chairman of the Eugenics Society's [Positive Eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics) Committee. Entitled "Eugenics in the Light of Population Trends", Carr-Saunders's lecture drew attention to the falling birth rate in Britain and his fears for what this meant for British society.[^o5]

Carr-Saunders argued for a more structured approach to the topic of population and the creation of a "coherent population policy".[^o6] It was towards these ends that the Council of the Eugenics Society formed a research body – the Population Investigation Committee - in 1936.[^o7]

At the first meeting of the PIC on 15 June 1936, Carr-Saunders was elected Chairman of the committee, [C.P. Blacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Blacker) the General Secretary, and [David Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Glass_%28sociologist%29) the Research Secretary. It was also resolved that the PIC would be an independent organisation to the Eugenics Society (and to other organisations who had appointed representatives to the PIC).[^o8]

Despite Carr-Saunders original call for a "coherent population policy", the PIC's originating remit was towards research, not in formulating policy. As such, the PIC played a major role in a number of national surveys which sought to investigate the "medical, economic and social factors affecting changes in the population".[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

The PIC has been involved in a number of social science research projects, the most notable were the [National Survey of Health and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Survey_of_Health_and_Development) and the [Scottish Mental Survey of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothian_birth-cohort_studies).[^o2][^o10] The [National Survey of Health and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Survey_of_Health_and_Development) developed from a Maternity Inquiry established in 1946 through a collaboration of the PIC with the Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists.[^o10][^o11] The [Scottish Mental Survey of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothian_birth-cohort_studies) was carried out by the PIC in cooperation with the [Scottish Council for Research in Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Council_for_Research_in_Education).[^o12]

Other research activities of the PIC included inquiries and surveys into fertility and birth control; marriage and divorce and the British Peerage.[^o13]

## *Population Studies* {#Population_Studies}

The PIC's journal, *Population Studies*, was established in 1947. It was the first English-language journal "exclusively concerned with [demography](wikipedia://en/45642)".[^o8] *Population Studies* was also the place of publication for much of the research carried out by the PIC.[^o15]

## Later years {#Later_years}

It has been suggested that the research outputs of the PIC slowed down after the death of its then Chairman David Glass in 1978.[^o17]

The work of the PIC remains centred around *Population Studies* even if since 2002 the journal has been published externally to the PIC. The PIC also retains interests in the postgraduate teaching of demography - it established a training programme in demography at the LSE in 1965[^o18] - and in recent years has maintained a Scholarship Fund (drawn from profits of *Population Studies*) for the support of UK masters students studying demography.[^o8]

## Chairmen {#Chairmen}

The Chairmen of the PIC have been:[^o17]

[Sir Alexander Carr-Saunders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Carr-Saunders) (1936-1958)

[David Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Glass_%28sociologist%29) (1958-1978)

[Prof. Ralph Dahrendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Dahrendorf) (1978-1984)

[Prof. Sir Tony Wrigley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Wrigley) (1984-1991)

Prof. John Hobcraft (1991-2003)

Prof. John Cleland (2003- )
