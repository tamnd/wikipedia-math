# Triply periodic minimal surface

*Image: Schwarz H Surface.png.* Schwarz H surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Triply periodic minimal surface"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **triply periodic minimal surface (TPMS)** is a [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734) in $\mathbb{R}^3$ that is invariant under a rank-3 [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) of translations.

:::

These surfaces have the symmetries of a [crystallographic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallographic_group). Numerous examples are known with cubic, [tetragonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetragonal), [rhombohedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombohedral), and [orthorhombic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthorhombic) symmetries. [Monoclinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoclinic) and [triclinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triclinic) examples are certain to exist, but have proven hard to parametrise.[^o2]

TPMS are of relevance in natural science. TPMS have been observed as biological membranes,[^o3] as [block copolymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_copolymers),[^o4] equipotential surfaces in crystals[^o5] etc. Their approximants have been used to model bone substitutes.[^o6] They have also been of interest in architecture, design and art.

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Nearly all studied TPMS are free of self-intersections (i.e. [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) in $\mathbb{R}^3$): from a mathematical standpoint they are the most interesting (since self-intersecting surfaces are trivially abundant).[^o9]

All connected TPMS have [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) ≥ 3,[^o19] and in every lattice there exist orientable embedded TPMS of every genus ≥3.[^o10]

Embedded TPMS are orientable and divide space into two disjoint sub-volumes (labyrinths). If they are congruent the surface is said to be a balance surface.[^o11]

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Schwarz P Surface.png.* Schwarz P surface

The first examples of TPMS were the [surfaces described by Schwarz](wikipedia://en/36763333) in 1865, followed by [a surface described by his student E. R. Neovius](wikipedia://en/37043957) in 1883.[^o13][^o14]

In 1970 [Alan Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Schoen) came up with 12 new TPMS based on skeleton graphs spanning crystallographic cells.[^o15][^o16] While Schoen's surfaces became popular in natural science the construction did not lend itself to a mathematical existence proof and remained largely unknown in mathematics, until H. Karcher proved their existence in 1989.[^o17]

Using [conjugate surfaces](wikipedia://en/37016612) many more surfaces were found. While [Weierstrass representations](wikipedia://en/2996193) are known for the simpler examples, they are not known for many surfaces. Instead methods from [Discrete differential geometry](wikipedia://en/17700015) are often used.[^o9]

## Families {#Families}

The classification of TPMS is an open problem.

TPMS often come in families that can be continuously deformed into each other. Meeks found an explicit 5-parameter family for genus 3 TPMS that contained all then known examples of genus 3 surfaces except the gyroid.[^o19] Members of this family can be continuously deformed into each other, remaining embedded in the process (although the lattice may change). The [gyroid](wikipedia://en/3905163) and [lidinoid](wikipedia://en/37026829) are each inside a separate 1-parameter family.[^o20]

Another approach to classifying TPMS is to examine their space groups. For surfaces containing lines the possible boundary polygons can be enumerated, providing a classification.[^o11][^o21]

## Generalisations {#Generalisations}

Periodic minimal surfaces can be constructed in **S**<sup>3</sup>[^o23] and **H**<sup>3</sup>.[^o24]

It is possible to generalise the division of space into labyrinths to find triply periodic (but possibly branched) minimal surfaces that divide space into more than two sub-volumes.[^o25]

[Quasiperiodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiperiodic) minimal surfaces have been constructed in $\mathbb{R}^2 \times \textbf{S}^1$.[^o26] It has been suggested but not been proven that minimal surfaces with a [quasicrystalline](wikipedia://en/25350) order in $\mathbb{R}^3$ exist.[^o27]
