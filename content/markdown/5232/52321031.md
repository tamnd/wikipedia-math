# András Vasy

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="András Vasy"}

**András Vasy** (born 1969) is a Hungarian–American mathematician working in the areas of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), [microlocal analysis](wikipedia://en/6108841), [scattering theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_theory), and [inverse problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_problems). He is currently a professor of mathematics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Vasy grew up in Budapest and attended Apáczai Csere János Gimnázium of the [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) (ELTE). He then became a boarding-school student at the [United World College of the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_World_College_of_the_Atlantic) in Wales.[^o2] Vasy attended [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), obtaining his BS in Physics and MS in mathematics in 1993. He received his PhD from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) under the supervision of [Richard B. Melrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Burt_Melrose) in 1997.[^o6] Following his postdoctoral appointment at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), he joined the MIT faculty as an assistant professor in 1999. He was awarded tenure at MIT in 2005[^o7] during a long-term stay at Northwestern University before moving to Stanford in 2006.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Vasy was an [Alfred P. Sloan Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) from 2002 to 2004,[^o9] and a [Clay Research Fellow](wikipedia://en/7655) from 2004 to 2006.[^o10] He was elected a Fellow of the American Mathematical Society in 2012. He was an invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Seoul in 2014.[^o11][^o12] In 2017, he was awarded the [Bôcher Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bôcher_Prize) of the American Mathematical Society.[^o13][^o2]

## Research {#Research}

The unifying feature of Vasy's work is the application of tools from microlocal analysis to problems in hyperbolic partial differential or pseudo-differential equations. He analyzed the propagation of singularities for solutions of wave equations on manifolds with corners[^o15] or more complicated boundary structures, partially in joint work with [Richard Melrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Burt_Melrose) and [Jared Wunsch](wikipedia://en/75063353).[^o16] For his paper on a unified approach to scattering theory on asymptotically hyperbolic spaces and spacetimes arising in Einstein's theory of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) such as [de Sitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Sitter) space and [Kerr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Kerr)-de Sitter spacetimes,[^o17] he was awarded the [Bôcher Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bôcher_Prize) in 2017. This paper led to further advances, including the proof, by Vasy and [Peter Hintz](wikipedia://en/81320042), of the global nonlinear stability of the Kerr-de Sitter family of black hole spacetimes,[^o18] and a new proof of [Smale](wikipedia://en/40294)'s conjecture for Anosov flows by [Semyon Dyatlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semyon_Dyatlov) and [Maciej Zworski](wikipedia://en/34110829).[^o19] Vasy has also collaborated with [Gunther Uhlmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunther_Uhlmann) on inverse problems for geodesic transforms.[^o20]
