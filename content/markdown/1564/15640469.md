# Dickson polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dickson polynomial"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Dickson polynomials**, denoted $D_{n}(x,\alpha)$, form a [polynomial sequence](wikipedia://en/195990) introduced by (). They were rediscovered by (Brewer 1961) in his study of [Brewer sums](wikipedia://en/37184509) and have at times, although rarely, been referred to as **Brewer polynomials**.

:::

Over the complex numbers, Dickson polynomials are essentially equivalent to [Chebyshev polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chebyshev_polynomial) with a change of variable, and, in fact, Dickson polynomials are sometimes called Chebyshev polynomials.

Dickson polynomials are generally studied over [finite fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_fields), where they sometimes may not be equivalent to Chebyshev polynomials. One of the main reasons for interest in them is that for fixed $\alpha$, they give many examples of *[permutation polynomials](wikipedia://en/15641015)*; polynomials acting as [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) of finite fields.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

::: {#o7 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Dickson polynomial of the first kind"}

### First kind {#First_kind}

For integer $n > 0$ and $\alpha$ in a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $R$ with identity (often chosen to be the finite field $\mathbf{F}_{q} = \operatorname{GF}(q)$) the **Dickson polynomials** (of the first kind) over $R$ are given by[^o8]

$$D_n(x,\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \right\rfloor}\frac{n}{n-i} \binom{n-i}{i} (-\alpha)^i x^{n-2i} \,.$$

The first few Dickson polynomials are

$$\begin{aligned} D_1(x,\alpha) &= x \\ D_2(x,\alpha) &= x^2 - 2\alpha \\ D_3(x,\alpha) &= x^3 - 3x\alpha \\ D_4(x,\alpha) &= x^4 - 4x^2\alpha + 2\alpha^2 \\ D_5(x,\alpha) &= x^5 - 5x^3\alpha + 5x\alpha^2 \,. \end{aligned}$$

They may also be generated by the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806) for $n \ge 2$,

$$D_n(x,\alpha) = xD_{n-1}(x,\alpha)-\alpha D_{n-2}(x,\alpha) \,,$$

with the initial conditions $D_{0}(x,\alpha) = 2$ and $D_{1}(x,\alpha) = x$.

The coefficients are given at several places in the OEIS[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12] with minute differences for the first two terms.

:::

::: {#o14 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Dickson polynomial of the second kind"}

### Second kind {#Second_kind}

The Dickson polynomials of the second kind, $E_{n}(x,\alpha)$, are defined by

$$E_n(x,\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \right\rfloor}\binom{n-i}{i} (-\alpha)^i x^{n-2i}.$$

They have not been studied much, and have properties similar to those of Dickson polynomials of the first kind. The first few Dickson polynomials of the second kind are

$$\begin{aligned} E_0(x,\alpha) &= 1 \\ E_1(x,\alpha) &= x \\ E_2(x,\alpha) &= x^2 - \alpha \\ E_3(x,\alpha) &= x^3 - 2x\alpha \\ E_4(x,\alpha) &= x^4 - 3x^2\alpha + \alpha^2 \,. \end{aligned}$$

They may also be generated by the recurrence relation for $n \ge 2$,

$$E_n(x,\alpha) = xE_{n-1}(x,\alpha)-\alpha E_{n-2}(x,\alpha) \,,$$

with the initial conditions $E_{0}(x,\alpha) = 1$ and $E_{1}(x,\alpha) = x$.

The coefficients are also given in the OEIS.[^o15][^o16]

:::

:::

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The $D_{n}$ are the unique monic polynomials satisfying the functional equation

$$D_n\left(u + \frac{\alpha}{u},\alpha\right) = u^n + \left(\frac{\alpha}{u}\right)^n,$$

where $\alpha \in \mathbf{F}_{q}$ and $u \ne 0 \in \mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}$.[^o19]

They also satisfy a composition rule,[^o19]

$$D_{mn}(x,\alpha) = D_m\bigl(D_n(x,\alpha),\alpha^n\bigr) \,= D_n\bigl(D_m(x,\alpha),\alpha^m\bigr) \, .$$

The $E_{n}$ also satisfy a functional equation[^o19]

$$E_n\left(y + \frac{\alpha}{y}, \alpha\right) = \frac{y^{n+1} - \left(\frac{\alpha}{y}\right)^{n+1}}{y - \frac{\alpha}{y}} \,,$$

for $y \ne 0$, $y^{2} \ne \alpha$, with $\alpha \in \mathbf{F}_{q}$ and $y \in \mathbf{F}_{q^{2}}$.

The Dickson polynomial $y = D_{n}$ is a solution of the [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753)

$$\left(x^2-4\alpha\right)y'' + xy' - n^2y=0 \,,$$

and the Dickson polynomial $y = E_{n}$ is a solution of the differential equation

$$\left(x^2-4\alpha\right)y'' + 3xy' - n(n+2)y=0 \,.$$

Their [ordinary generating functions](wikipedia://en/160993#Ordinary_generating_function) are

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_n D_n(x,\alpha)z^n &= \frac{2-xz}{1-xz+\alpha z^2} \\ \sum_n E_n(x,\alpha)z^n &= \frac{1}{1-xz+\alpha z^2} \,. \end{aligned}$$

:::

## Links to other polynomials {#Links_to_other_polynomials}

By the recurrence relation above, Dickson polynomials are [Lucas sequences](wikipedia://en/734635). Specifically, for $\alpha = -1$, the Dickson polynomials of the first kind are [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/483720) polynomials, and Dickson polynomials of the second kind are [Lucas polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_polynomials).

By the composition rule above, when α is [idempotent](wikipedia://en/361940), composition of Dickson polynomials of the first kind is commutative.

- The Dickson polynomials with parameter $\alpha = 0$ give [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416).

$$D_n(x,0) = x^n \, .$$

- The Dickson polynomials with parameter $\alpha = 1$ are related to [Chebyshev polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chebyshev_polynomial) $T_{n}(x) = \cos (n \arccos x)$ of the first kind by[^o8]

$$D_n(2x, 1) = 2T_n(x) \,.$$

- Since the Dickson polynomial $D_{n}(x,\alpha)$ can be defined over rings with additional idempotents, $D_{n}(x,\alpha)$ is often not related to a Chebyshev polynomial.

## Permutation polynomials and Dickson polynomials {#Permutation_polynomials_and_Dickson_polynomials}

A **permutation polynomial** (for a given finite field) is one that acts as a permutation of the elements of the finite field.

The Dickson polynomial $D_{n}(x, \alpha)$ (considered as a function of $x$ with α fixed) is a permutation polynomial for the field with $q$ elements if and only if $n$ is coprime to $q^{2} - 1$.[^o22]

(Fried 1970) proved that any integral polynomial that is a permutation polynomial for infinitely many prime fields is a composition of Dickson polynomials and linear polynomials (with rational coefficients). This assertion has become known as Schur's conjecture, although in fact Schur did not make this conjecture. Since Fried's paper contained numerous errors, a corrected account was given by (Turnwald 1995), and subsequently (Müller 1997) gave a simpler proof along the lines of an argument due to Schur.

Further, (Müller 1997) proved that any permutation polynomial over the finite field $\mathbf{F}_{q}$ whose degree is simultaneously coprime to $q$ and less than $q^{\tfrac{1}{4}}$ must be a composition of Dickson polynomials and linear polynomials.

## Generalization {#Generalization}

Dickson polynomials of both kinds over finite fields can be thought of as initial members of a sequence of generalized Dickson polynomials referred to as Dickson polynomials of the $(k + 1)$th kind.[^o29] Specifically, for $\alpha \ne 0 \in \mathbf{F}_{q}$ with $q = p^{e}$ for some prime $p$ and any integers $n \ge 0$ and $0 \le k < p$, the **$n$th Dickson polynomial of the $(k + 1)$th kind** over $\mathbf{F}_{q}$, denoted by $D_{n,k}(x,\alpha)$, is defined by[^o30]

$$D_{0,k}(x,\alpha) = 2 - k$$

and

$$D_{n,k}(x,\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \right\rfloor}\frac{n - ki}{n-i}\binom{n-i}{i} (-\alpha)^i x^{n-2i} \,.$$

$D_{n,0}(x,\alpha) = D_{n}(x,\alpha)$ and $D_{n,1}(x,\alpha) = E_{n}(x,\alpha)$, showing that this definition unifies and generalizes the original polynomials of Dickson.

The significant properties of the Dickson polynomials also generalize:[^o31]

- *Recurrence relation*: For $n \ge 2$,

$$D_{n,k}(x,\alpha) = xD_{n-1,k}(x,\alpha)-\alpha D_{n-2,k}(x,\alpha)\,,$$

with the initial conditions $D_{0,k}(x,\alpha) = 2 - k$ and $D_{1,k}(x,\alpha) = x$.

- *Functional equation*:

$$D_{n,k}\left(y + \alpha y^{-1}, \alpha\right) = \frac{y^{2n} +k\alpha y^{2n-2} + \cdots +k\alpha^{n-1}y^2 + \alpha^n}{y^n} = \frac{y^{2n} + {\alpha}^n}{y^n} + \left(\frac{k\alpha}{y^n} \right) \frac{y^{2n} - {\alpha}^{n-1}y^2}{y^2 - \alpha} \,,$$

where $y \ne 0$, $y^{2} \ne \alpha$.

- *Generating function*:

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} D_{n,k}(x,\alpha)z^n = \frac{2 - k + (k-1)xz}{1 - xz + \alpha z^2} \,.$$

:::
