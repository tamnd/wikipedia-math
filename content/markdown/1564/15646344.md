# Chernoff's distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chernoff's distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Chernoff's distribution**, named after [Herman Chernoff](wikipedia://en/1181131), is the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of the [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685)

:::

$$Z =\underset{s \in \mathbf{R}}{\operatorname{argmax}}\ (W(s) - s^2),$$

where *W* is a "two-sided" [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984) (or two-sided "[Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436)") satisfying *W*(0) = 0. If

$$V(a,c) = \underset{s \in \mathbf{R}}{\operatorname{argmax}} \ (W(s) - c(s-a)^2),$$

then *V*(0, *c*) has density

$$f_c(t) = \frac{1}{2} g_c(t) g_c(-t)$$

where *g*<sub>*c*</sub> has [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) given by

$$\hat{g}_c (s) = \frac{(2/c)^{1/3}}{\operatorname{Ai} (i (2c^2)^{-1/3} s)}, \ \ \ s \in \mathbf{R}$$

and where Ai is the [Airy function](wikipedia://en/406896). Thus *f*<sub>*c*</sub> is symmetric about 0 and the density *ƒ*<sub>*Z*</sub> = *ƒ*<sub>1</sub>. Groeneboom (1989)[^o2] shows that

$$f_Z (z) \sim \frac{1}{2} \frac{4^{4/3} |z|}{\operatorname{Ai}' (\tilde{a}_1)} \exp \left( - \frac{2}{3} |z|^3 + 2^{1/3} \tilde{a}_1 |z| \right) \text{ as }z \rightarrow \infty$$

where $\tilde{a}_1 \approx -2.3381$ is the largest zero of the Airy function Ai and where $\operatorname{Ai}' (\tilde{a}_1 ) \approx 0.7022$. In the same paper, Groeneboom also gives an analysis of the [process](wikipedia://en/47895) $\{V(a,1): a \in \mathbf{R}\}$. The connection with the statistical problem of estimating a monotone density is discussed in Groeneboom (1985).[^o3] Chernoff's distribution is now known to appear in a wide range of monotone problems including [isotonic regression](wikipedia://en/2836143).[^o4]

The Chernoff distribution should not be confused with the Chernoff geometric distribution[^o5] (called the Chernoff point in information geometry) induced by the Chernoff information.

## History {#History}

Groeneboom, Lalley and Temme[^o7] state that the first investigation of this distribution was probably by Chernoff in 1964,[^o8] who studied the behavior of a certain [estimator](wikipedia://en/10043) of a [mode](wikipedia://en/1432127). In his paper, Chernoff characterized the distribution through an analytic representation through the [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415) with suitable [boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_conditions). Initial attempts at approximating Chernoff's distribution via solving the heat equation, however, did not achieve satisfactory precision due to the nature of the boundary conditions.[^o7] The computation of the distribution is addressed, for example, in Groeneboom and Wellner (2001).[^o9]

The connection of Chernoff's distribution with Airy functions was also found independently by Daniels and Skyrme[^o10] and Temme,[^o11] as cited in Groeneboom, Lalley and Temme. These two papers, along with Groeneboom (1989), were all written in 1984.[^o7]
