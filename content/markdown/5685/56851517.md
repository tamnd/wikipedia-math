# Charles J. Mendelsohn

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles J. Mendelsohn"}

**Charles Jastrow Mendelsohn** (December 8, 1880 – September 27, 1939) was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [cryptographer](wikipedia://en/18934432) and [classicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics).[^o2] He was the only child of [Rabbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbi) [Samuel Mendelsohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Mendelsohn) and Esther Jastrow.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

He was born in [Wilmington, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_North_Carolina).[^o2]

## Education {#Education}

He graduated from the [Episcopal Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Academy), [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia) in 1896.[^o2] He was a Harrison Scholar at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), which he graduated with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1900 and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1904.[^o2] He was a member of [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa).[^o2] He joined the faculty of [College of the City of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York) as a tutor in Greek in 1905, becoming an instructor in 1907.[^o2] He was a professor of ancient languages in 1917.[^o4]

## Military service {#Military_service}

During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) he served in the censorship department of the [Post Office Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Post_Office_Department) in 1917, dealing with foreign language, postal and newspaper censorship.[^o2][^o4] His work came to the attention of [Herbert Yardley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Yardley) and he was recruited into Military Intelligence, section 8 (MI-8).[^o4] From 1918 to 1919 he was a captain in the Military Intelligence Division of the [War Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_War) General Staff.[^o2]

He led a team dealing with German diplomatic correspondence, breaking at least six diplomatic ciphers.[^o4] Two messages dealt with German attempts to get support from Mexico.[^o4]

He received an [honorable discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorable_discharge).[^o4]

## Return to civilian life {#Return_to_civilian_life}

He returned to City College in 1920 as a professor of history.[^o2][^o4] He remained interested in cryptography, writing papers and became interested in the history of the topic, collecting many books on it.[^o2]

He remained in contact with Herbert Yardley and did part time cryptographic work for the [Black Chamber](wikipedia://en/991864).[^o4] Together they published the *Universal Trade Code*, a commercial code.[^o4] He wrote several works for the Black Chamber, including *The Zimmermann Telegram of January 16, 1917, and its Cryptographic Background* and *Studies in German Diplomatic Codes Employed During the World War*.[^o4]

## Death {#Death}

In 1939, he was recalled to active duty as a result of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), but while preparing he contracted [meningitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meningitis) and died of it.[^o2][^o4]

His library, which had been called "probably the most important cryptographic library in America, if not in the world" was bequeathed to the University of Pennsylvania.[^o2]

He had never married and was survived by his mother.[^o2] He was buried in the Hebrew Cemetery at [Oakdale Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakdale_Cemetery_%28Wilmington,_North_Carolina%29), Wilmington, North Carolina.[^o2]

## Publications {#Publications}

- *Studies in the Word-Play in Plautus*[^o2]
- *The Zimmermann Telegram of January 16, 1917, and its Cryptographic Background*[^o4]
- *Studies in German Diplomatic Codes Employed During the World War*[^o4]
