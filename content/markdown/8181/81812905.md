# Fractional dominating set

*Image: Fractional dominating set.svg.* The sum of the weights of each vertex and its neighbors (its [closed neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28graph_theory%29)) is at least 1. The assignment of weights is therefore a **fractional dominating set**. One may consider the sum of all weights of the graph across all fractional dominating sets; the smallest of these is the graph's **fractional domination number**. The graph shown has an optimal set shown, with a total sum of $7/3$.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fractional dominating set"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **fractional dominating set** is a generalization of the [dominating set](wikipedia://en/1747972) concept that allows vertices to be assigned fractional weights between 0 and 1, rather than binary membership. This relaxation transforms the domination problem into a [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) problem, often yielding more precise bounds and enabling polynomial-time computation.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a graph. A **fractional dominating function** is a function $f: V \to [0, 1]$ such that for every vertex $v \in V$, the sum of $f$ over the [closed neighborhood](wikipedia://en/7309251) $N[v]$ is at least 1:[^o4][^o5]

$$\sum_{u \in N[v]} f(u) \geq 1$$

The **fractional domination number** $\gamma_f(G)$ is the minimum total weight of a fractional dominating function:

$$\gamma_f(G) = \min\left\{\sum_{v \in V} f(v)\right\}$$

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

For any graph $G$, the fractional domination number satisfies:[^o4]

$$\gamma_f(G) \leq \gamma(G) \leq \Gamma(G) \leq \Gamma_f(G)$$

where $\gamma(G)$ is the [domination number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domination_number), $\Gamma(G)$ is the upper domination number, and $\Gamma_f(G)$ is the upper fractional domination number.

The fractional domination number can be computed as the solution to a linear program by utilizing [strong duality](wikipedia://en/34390810).[^o5]

For any graph $G$ with $n$ vertices, minimum degree $\delta$, and maximum degree $\Delta$:[^o5]

$$\frac{n}{\Delta + 1} \leq \gamma_f(G) \leq \frac{n}{\delta + 1}$$

For any graph $G$, the fractional *edge* domination number equals the domination number of the [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231):[^o11]

$$\gamma'_f(G) = \gamma(L(G))$$

:::

::: {#o9 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formulas for specific graph families"}

## Formulas for specific graph families {#Formulas_for_specific_graph_families}

For a [$k$-regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) with $n$ vertices and $k \geq 1$:[^o4][^o10]

$$\gamma_f(G) = \frac{n}{k+1}$$

For the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{r,s}$:[^o5]

$$\gamma_f(K_{r,s}) = \frac{r(s-1) + s(r-1)}{rs - 1}$$

For the [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) $C_n$:[^o11]

$$\gamma_f(C_n) = \frac{n}{3}$$

For the [path graph](wikipedia://en/4587190) $P_n$:[^o11]

$$\gamma_f(P_n) = \left\lceil \frac{n}{3} \right\rceil$$

For the [crown graph](wikipedia://en/21062705) $H_{n,n}$:[^o11]

$$\gamma_f(H_{n,n}) = 2$$

For the [wheel graph](wikipedia://en/1655876) $W_n$ with $n > 3$ vertices:[^o11]

$$\gamma_f(W_n) = 1$$

Several graph classes have $\gamma_f(G) = \gamma(G)$:[^o5]

- [Trees](wikipedia://en/48560)
- [Block graphs](wikipedia://en/29106883) (graphs where every block is complete)
- [Strongly chordal graphs](wikipedia://en/24809733)

For the [strong product of graphs](wikipedia://en/11494014) $G \boxtimes H$:[^o5]

$$\gamma_f(G \boxtimes H) = \gamma_f(G) \cdot \gamma_f(H)$$

For the [Cartesian product of graphs](wikipedia://en/2856852) $G \square H$ ([Vizing's conjecture](wikipedia://en/8175877), fractional version):[^o5]

$$\gamma_f(G \square H) \geq \gamma_f(G) \cdot \gamma_f(H)$$

:::

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

Since the fractional domination number can be formulated as a linear program, it can be computed in polynomial time, unlike the standard domination number which is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) to compute.[^o5]

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variants"}

## Variants {#Variants}

A **fractional distance k-dominating function** generalizes the concept by requiring that for every vertex $v$, the sum over its distance-$k$ neighborhood $N_k[v]$ (vertices at distance at most $k$ from $v$) is at least one. The corresponding **fractional distance k-domination number** is denoted $\gamma_{kf}(G)$. [^o10]

For $k$-regular graphs and specific values of $k$, exact formulas exist. For instance, for cycles $C_n$:[^o10]

$$\gamma_{kf}(C_n) = \frac{n}{2k+1}$$

An **efficient fractional dominating function** satisfies

$$\sum_{u \in N[v]} f(u) = 1$$

for all vertices $v$. Not all graphs admit efficient fractional dominating functions.[^o5]

A **fractional total dominating function** requires that for every vertex $v$, the sum over its open neighborhood $N(v)$ (excluding $v$ itself) is at least one. The **fractional total domination number** is denoted $\gamma_{ft}(G)$.[^o5]

The **upper fractional domination number** $\Gamma_f(G)$ is the maximum weight among all minimal fractional dominating functions.[^o5]

:::
