# Group-scheme action

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Group-scheme action"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), an **action of a group scheme** is a generalization of a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) to a [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523). Precisely, given a group *S*-scheme *G*, a **left action of *G* on an *S*-scheme *X*** is an *S*-morphism

:::

$$\sigma: G \times_S X \to X$$

such that

- (associativity) $\sigma \circ (1_G \times \sigma) = \sigma \circ (m \times 1_X)$, where $m: G \times_S G \to G$ is the group law,
- (unitality) $\sigma \circ (e \times 1_X) = 1_X$, where $e: S \to G$ is the identity section of *G*.

A **right action of *G* on *X*** is defined analogously. A scheme equipped with a left or right action of a group scheme *G* is called a ***G*-scheme**. An [equivariant morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant_morphism) between *G*-schemes is a [morphism of schemes](wikipedia://en/7482797) that intertwines the respective *G*-actions.

More generally, one can also consider (at least some special case of) an action of a [group functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_functor): viewing *G* as a functor, an action is given as a natural transformation satisfying the conditions analogous to the above.[^o2] Alternatively, some authors study group action in the language of a [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543); a group-scheme action is then an example of a [groupoid scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupoid_scheme).

## Constructs {#Constructs}

The usual constructs for a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) such as orbits generalize to a group-scheme action. Let $\sigma$ be a given group-scheme action as above.

- Given a T-valued point $x: T \to X$, the [orbit map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_map) $\sigma_x: G \times_S T \to X \times_S T$ is given as $(\sigma \circ (1_G \times x), p_2)$.
- The [orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) of *x* is the image of the orbit map $\sigma_x$.
- The [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_subgroup) of *x* is the [fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme-theoretic_fiber) over $\sigma_x$ of the map $(x, 1_T): T \to X \times_S T.$

## Problem of constructing a quotient {#Problem_of_constructing_a_quotient}

Unlike a set-theoretic group action, there is no straightforward way to construct a quotient for a group-scheme action. One exception is the case when the action is free, the case of a [principal fiber bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_fiber_bundle).

There are several approaches to overcome this difficulty:

- [Level structure](wikipedia://en/47016783) - Perhaps the oldest, the approach replaces an object to classify by an object together with a level structure
- [Geometric invariant theory](wikipedia://en/1639003) - throw away bad orbits and then take a quotient. The drawback is that there is no canonical way to introduce the notion of "bad orbits"; the notion depends on a choice of [linearization](wikipedia://en/1118789). See also: [categorical quotient](wikipedia://en/39561038), [GIT quotient](wikipedia://en/41139312).
- [Borel construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_construction) - this is an approach essentially from algebraic topology; this approach requires one to work with an [infinite-dimensional space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite-dimensional_space).
- Analytic approach, the theory of [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661)
- [Quotient stack](wikipedia://en/40579087) - in a sense, this is the ultimate answer to the problem. Roughly, a "quotient prestack" is the category of orbits and one [stackify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stackification) (i.e., the introduction of the notion of a torsor) it to get a quotient stack.

Depending on applications, another approach would be to shift the focus away from a space then onto stuff on a space; e.g., [topos](wikipedia://en/21391464). So the problem shifts from the classification of orbits to that of [equivariant objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant_object).

[^o2]: In details, given a group-scheme action $\sigma$, for each morphism $T \to S$, $\sigma$ determines a group action $G(T) \times X(T) \to X(T)$; i.e., the group $G(T)$ acts on the set of *T*-points $X(T)$. Conversely, if for each $T \to S$, there is a group action $\sigma_T: G(T) \times X(T) \to X(T)$ and if those actions are compatible; i.e., they form a [natural transformation](wikipedia://en/45022), then, by the [Yoneda lemma](wikipedia://en/34359), they determine a group-scheme action $\sigma: G \times_S X \to X$.
