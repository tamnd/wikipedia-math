# Dominance drawing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dominance drawing"}

*Image: Dominance drawing.svg.* A dominance drawing

**Dominance drawing** is a style of [graph drawing](wikipedia://en/325813) of [directed acyclic graphs](wikipedia://en/204002) that makes the [reachability](wikipedia://en/2833097) relations between vertices visually apparent. In dominance drawing, vertices are placed at distinct points of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) and a vertex *v* is reachable from another vertex *u* if and only if both [Cartesian coordinates](wikipedia://en/7706) of *v* are greater than or equal to the coordinates of *u*. The edges of a dominance drawing may be drawn either as straight [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860), or, in some cases, as [polygonal chains](wikipedia://en/12056032).[^o11]

:::

## Planar graphs {#Planar_graphs}

Every [transitively reduced](wikipedia://en/3757117) [*st*-planar graph](wikipedia://en/47704642), a directed acyclic [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) with a single source and a single sink, both on the outer face of some embedding of the graph, has a dominance drawing. The **left–right algorithm** for finding these drawings sets the *x* coordinate of every vertex to be its position in a [depth-first search](wikipedia://en/97034) ordering of the graph, starting with *s* and prioritizing edges in right-to-left order, and by setting the *y* coordinate to be obtained in the same way but prioritizing edges in left-to-right order. Typical dominance drawing algorithms include another phase of compaction after this coordinate assignment, shifting vertices down and to the left as much as possible while preserving the properties of a dominance drawing. The resulting drawing lies within an *n* × *n* [integer grid](wikipedia://en/3333402), and displays many of the symmetries of the underlying topological embedding. This drawing, and more generally every dominance drawing of a transitively-reduced *st*-planar graph, is necessarily planar, with straight-line edges.[^o11][^o10]

For *st*-planar graphs that are not transitively reduced, an equivalent transitively reduced graph may be obtained by [subdividing](wikipedia://en/353021) each edge. However, a straight-line drawing of the resulting transitively reduced graph will form a drawing of the original graph in which some edges have [bends](wikipedia://en/43176911), at the dummy vertices introduced by the subdivision.[^o11][^o10] A planar dominance drawing is not necessarily an [upward planar drawing](wikipedia://en/40148474), because some edges may be horizontal, but rotating it by 45° necessarily gives an upward planar drawing.[^o11] In a comparison with other methods for drawing directed acyclic graphs, the left-right algorithm (together with a planarization preprocessing step) was found to perform well in terms of the [area](wikipedia://en/43176265) of the drawings it produces, the number of bends, and the [aspect ratio](wikipedia://en/1021) of the drawing, but less well in total edge length.[^o8]

## Nonplanar graphs {#Nonplanar_graphs}

A directed acyclic graph (regardless of planarity) has a dominance drawing if and only if the [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) of its vertices, ordered by reachability, has [order dimension](wikipedia://en/3121852) two. The (rotated) dominance drawing of a transitively reduced directed acyclic graph may be used as a [Hasse diagram](wikipedia://en/338199) of the corresponding partial order.[^o7]

## Codominance {#Codominance}

Given a dominance drawing of a directed acyclic graph *D*<sub>1</sub> = (*V*, *E*<sub>1</sub>), inverting the interpretation of one axis results in a new relation one could call *coreachability*. Thus a point (*x<sub>a</sub>*, *y<sub>a</sub>*) could be considered coreachable from a point (*x<sub>b</sub>*, *y<sub>b</sub>*) whenever *x<sub>a</sub>* ≥ *x<sub>b</sub>* but *y<sub>a</sub>* ≤ *y<sub>b</sub>*. In this way, the dominance drawing may be seen to induce a second directed acyclic graph *D*<sub>2</sub> = (*V*, *E*<sub>2</sub>) on the same vertex set. The pairs {≤<sub>1</sub>, ≤<sub>2</sub>} of partial orders on a shared ground set that permit such simultaneous representation by a single drawing—interpreted in terms of reachability and coreachability—are called *codominant.*[^o9]

## Weak dominance drawing {#Weak_dominance_drawing}

For directed acyclic graphs whose reachability order has higher dimension, a **weak dominance drawing** is a drawing in which every edge is oriented upwards, rightwards, or both, but in which there exist pairs of vertices (*u*, *v*) for which *u* dominates *v* coordinatewise but *v* is not reachable from *u* in the graph. We said that a vertex *u* dominates another vertex *v* if the coordinates (u\_x, u\_y) of *u* are less or equal the coordinates (v\_x, v\_y) of *v*, i.e., u\_x \<= v\_x and u\_y \<= v\_y considering a XY-plane. The goal in this style of drawing is to minimize the number of such *falsely implied paths*.[^o12]
