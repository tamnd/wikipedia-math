# Dyson conjecture

*Image: Freeman Dyson.jpg.* Freeman Dyson in 2005

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dyson conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Dyson conjecture** () is a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) about the constant term of certain [Laurent polynomials](wikipedia://en/61351), [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) independently in 1962 by [Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_G._Wilson) and Gunson. [Andrews](wikipedia://en/3356273) generalized it to the **q-Dyson conjecture**, proved by [Zeilberger](wikipedia://en/1724825) and [Bressoud](wikipedia://en/21358538) and sometimes called the **Zeilberger–Bressoud theorem**. [Macdonald](wikipedia://en/3100378) generalized it further to more general [root systems](wikipedia://en/277087) with the **Macdonald constant term conjecture**, proved by [Cherednik](wikipedia://en/33333323).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Dyson conjecture"}

## Dyson conjecture {#Dyson_conjecture}

The Dyson conjecture states that the Laurent polynomial

$$\prod_{1\le i\ne j\le n}(1-t_i/t_j)^{a_i}$$

has constant term

$$\frac{(a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n)!}{a_1!a_2!\cdots a_n!}.$$

The conjecture was first proved independently by (Wilson 1962) and (Gunson 1962). (Good 1970) later found a short proof, by observing that the Laurent polynomials, and therefore their constant terms, satisfy the recursion relations

$$F(a_1,\dots,a_n) = \sum_{i=1}^nF(a_1,\dots,a_i-1,\dots,a_n).$$

The case *n* = 3 of Dyson's conjecture follows from the [Dixon identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixon_identity).

(Sills Zeilberger 2006) and (Sills 2006) used a computer to find expressions for non-constant coefficients of Dyson's Laurent polynomial.

:::

## Dyson integral {#Dyson_integral}

When all the values *a*<sub>*i*</sub> are equal to β/2, the constant term in Dyson's conjecture is the value of **Dyson's integral**

$$\frac{1}{(2\pi)^n}\int_0^{2\pi}\cdots\int_0^{2\pi}\prod_{1\le j<k\le n}|e^{i\theta_j}-e^{i\theta_k}|^\beta \, d\theta_1\cdots d\theta_n.$$

Dyson's integral is a special case of [Selberg's integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selberg's_integral) after a change of variable and has value

$$\frac{\Gamma(1+\beta n/2)}{\Gamma(1+\beta/2)^n}$$

which gives another proof of Dyson's conjecture in this special case.

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="q-Dyson conjecture"}

## *q*-Dyson conjecture {#q-Dyson_conjecture}

(Andrews 1975) found a [q-analog](wikipedia://en/1619396) of Dyson's conjecture, stating that the constant term of

$$\prod_{1\le i<j\le n}\left(\frac{x_i}{x_j};q\right)_{a_i}\left(\frac{qx_j}{x_i};q\right)_{a_j}$$

is

$$\frac{(q;q)_{a_1+\cdots+a_n}}{(q;q)_{a_1}\cdots(q;q)_{a_n}}.$$

Here (*a*;*q*)<sub>*n*</sub> is the [q-Pochhammer symbol](wikipedia://en/11149717). This conjecture reduces to Dyson's conjecture for *q* = 1, and was proved by (Zeilberger Bressoud 1985), using a [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) approach inspired by previous work of [Ira Gessel](wikipedia://en/53794712) and [Dominique Foata](wikipedia://en/26068475). A shorter proof, using [formal Laurent series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_Laurent_series), was given in 2004 by Ira Gessel and Guoce Xin, and an even shorter proof, using a quantitative form, due to Karasev and Petrov, and independently to Lason, of Noga Alon's Combinatorial Nullstellensatz, was given in 2012 by Gyula Karolyi and Zoltan Lorant Nagy. The latter method was extended, in 2013, by Shalosh B. Ekhad and Doron Zeilberger to derive explicit expressions of any specific coefficient, not just the constant term; see http://www.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/mamarim/mamarimhtml/qdyson.html, for detailed references.

:::

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Macdonald conjectures"}

## Macdonald conjectures {#Macdonald_conjectures}

(Macdonald 1982) extended the conjecture to arbitrary finite or affine [root systems](wikipedia://en/277087), with Dyson's original conjecture corresponding to the case of the *A*<sub>*n*−1</sub> root system and Andrews's conjecture corresponding to the affine *A*<sub>*n*−1</sub> root system. Macdonald reformulated these conjectures as conjectures about the norms of [Macdonald polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macdonald_polynomial). Macdonald's conjectures were proved by (Cherednik 1995) using doubly affine Hecke algebras.

[Macdonald](wikipedia://en/3100378)'s form of Dyson's conjecture for root systems of type BC is closely related to [Selberg's integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selberg's_integral).

:::
