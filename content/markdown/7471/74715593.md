# Gaussian probability space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gaussian probability space"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) particularly in the [Malliavin calculus](wikipedia://en/837875), a **Gaussian probability space** is a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) together with a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) of mean zero, real-valued [Gaussian random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_random_variable). Important examples include the [classical](wikipedia://en/6810363) or [abstract Wiener space](wikipedia://en/6592746) with some suitable collection of Gaussian random variables.[^o2][^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **Gaussian probability space** $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,\mathcal{H},\mathcal{F}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}})$ consists of

- a ([complete](wikipedia://en/43325#Complete_probability_space)) probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$,
- a closed [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) $\mathcal{H}\subset L^2(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$ called the *Gaussian space* such that all $X\in \mathcal{H}$ are mean zero Gaussian variables. Their [σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/29586) is denoted as $\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}$.
- a [σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/29586) $\mathcal{F}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}}$ called the *transverse σ-algebra* which is defined through

$\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}} \otimes \mathcal{F}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}}.$[^o6]

### Irreducibility {#Irreducibility}

A Gaussian probability space is called *irreducible* if $\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}}$. Such spaces are denoted as $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,\mathcal{H})$. Non-irreducible spaces are used to work on subspaces or to extend a given probability space.[^o6] Irreducible Gaussian probability spaces are classified by the dimension of the Gaussian space $\mathcal{H}$.[^o8]

### Subspaces {#Subspaces}

A *subspace* $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,\mathcal{H}_1,\mathcal{A}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}_1})$ of a Gaussian probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,\mathcal{H},\mathcal{F}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}})$ consists of

- a closed subspace $\mathcal{H}_1\subset \mathcal{H}$,
- a [sub σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/29586#Sub_σ-algebras) $\mathcal{A}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}_1}\subset \mathcal{F}$ of *transverse random variables* such that $\mathcal{A}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}_1}$ and $\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}_1}$ are independent, $\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}_1}\otimes \mathcal{A}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}_1}$ and $\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{F}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{A}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}_1}$.[^o6]

Example:

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,\mathcal{H},\mathcal{F}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}})$ be a Gaussian probability space with a closed subspace $\mathcal{H}_1\subset \mathcal{H}$. Let $V$ be the orthogonal complement of $\mathcal{H}_1$ in $\mathcal{H}$. Since orthogonality implies independence between $V$ and $\mathcal{H}_1$, we have that $\mathcal{A}_V$ is independent of $\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}_1}$. Define $\mathcal{A}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}_1}$ via $\mathcal{A}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}_1}:=\sigma(\mathcal{A}_V,\mathcal{F}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}})=\mathcal{A}_V \vee \mathcal{F}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}}$.

### Remark {#Remark}

For $G=L^2(\Omega,\mathcal{F}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}},P)$ we have $L^2(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)=L^2((\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}},P);G)$.

### Fundamental algebra {#Fundamental_algebra}

Given a Gaussian probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,\mathcal{H},\mathcal{F}^{\perp}_{\mathcal{H}})$ one defines the [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) of cylindrical random variables

$$\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{H}}=\{F=P(X_1,\dots,X_n):X_i\in \mathcal{H}\}$$

where $P$ is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) in $\mathbb{R}[X_n,\dots,X_n]$ and calls $\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{H}}$ the *fundamental algebra*. For any $p<\infty$ it is true that $\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{H}}\subset L^p(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$.

For an irreducible Gaussian probability $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,\mathcal{H})$ the fundamental algebra $\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{H}}$ is a [dense set](wikipedia://en/23523594) in $L^p(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)$ for all $p\in[1,\infty[$.[^o8]

### Numerical and Segal model {#Numerical_and_Segal_model}

An irreducible Gaussian probability $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,\mathcal{H})$ where a basis was chosen for $\mathcal{H}$ is called a *numerical model*. Two numerical models are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) if their Gaussian spaces have the same dimension.[^o8]

Given a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) Hilbert space $\mathcal{G}$, there exists always a canoncial irreducible Gaussian probability space $\operatorname{Seg}(\mathcal{G})$ called the *Segal model* (named after [Irving Segal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Segal)) with $\mathcal{G}$ as a Gaussian space. In this setting, one usually writes for an element $g\in \mathcal{G}$ the associated Gaussian random variable in the Segal model as $W(g)$. The notation is that of an [isornomal](wikipedia://en/254777) Gaussian process and typically the Gaussian space is defined through one. One can then easily choose an arbitrary Hilbert space $G$ and have the Gaussian space as $\mathcal{G}=\{W(g): g\in G\}$.[^o13]
