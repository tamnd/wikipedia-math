# Graph of a polytope

*Image: Cube graphs.svg.* The edge graphs of the [square](wikipedia://en/659939), [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) and [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph of a polytope"}

In [polytope theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytopes), the **edge graph** (also known as **vertex-edge graph** or just **graph**) of a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) is a combinatorial [graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_%28graph_theory%29) whose vertices and edges correspond directly to the [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) and [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) of the polytope. As a purely [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) object, the edge graph encodes incidence information, capturing which vertices are connected by edges, but it does not retain geometric data such as vertex positions or edge lengths. Further common names for the edge graph are **[skeleton](wikipedia://en/2977910)** and **1-skeleton**, though some authors reserve these terms for the geometric embedding formed by the vertices and edges in the polytope's [ambient space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambient_space). There is no universally agreed upon notation for the edge graph of a polytope $P$. Common notations include $G_P$, $G(P)$ or $\operatorname{skel}(P)$.

:::

Not all graphs are realizable as edge graphs of polytopes; those that are realizable in this manner are called **polytopal graphs**. Edge graphs of [3-dimensional polytopes](wikipedia://en/23470) are also called [polyhedral graphs](wikipedia://en/24656270). The problem of deciding whether a given graph is polytopal or not is known as the **realization problem** and is NP hard in general dimension. In dimension three the problem is also called the **Steinitz problem** in recognition of its resolution by [Ernst Steinitz](wikipedia://en/3966685).

Information about the polytope's faces of dimension two or higher is not immediately accessible from the edge graph, and often cannot be reconstruction from it at all. To capture the full combinatorial structure of a polytope, including the number of faces of each dimension and the incidence relations between them, one needs to work with the polytope's [face lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_lattice). In analogy to the term "1-skeleton", the part of the face lattice that contains the information about the combinatorics of faces up to dimension $k$ is called the *$k$-skeleton* of the polytope.

## General properties {#General_properties}

The edge graph of a [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885) is a finite [simple graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph). It is [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph), since a path between any two vertices can be obtained from the [simplex algorithm](wikipedia://en/349458). For low-dimensional polytopes the structure of the edge graph is essentially determined by the polytope's dimension:

- the only 0-dimensional polytope is the point; its edge graph is $K_1$.
- the only 1-dimensional polytope is the line segment; its edge graph is $K_2$.
- the 2-dimensional polytopes are [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621). The edge graph of an $n$-sided polygon is $C_n$, the [cycle](wikipedia://en/878962) with $n$ vertices.
- the edge graphs of 3-dimensional polytopes are rich in structure but well-understood: by [Steinitz's theorem](wikipedia://en/19762817) the edge graphs of 3-polytopes are precisely the [3-vertex-connected](wikipedia://en/7566175) [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), for this reason also known as [polyhedral graphs](wikipedia://en/24656270).

For $d$-polytopes with $d\ge 4$ no characterization of edge graphs is known. Some general statements can be made:

- the edge graph has [minimum degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_degree) at least $d$. If every vertex of a $d$-polytope has degree exactly $d$ (i.e. the edge graph is [$d$-regular](wikipedia://en/85821)), then the polytope is said to be [simple](wikipedia://en/8853286).
- the edge graph is [$d$-vertex connected](wikipedia://en/7566175). This is known as [Balinski's theorem](wikipedia://en/24732291).
- the edge graph contains a [subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_minor) of the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_{d+1}$.[^o21] In particular, for $d\ge 4$, the edge graph contains a $K_5$-minor and is not planar.

It is in general non-trivial to determine whether a given graph is the edge graph of a polytope, that is, whether it is a *polytopal graph*. For some graph classes, such as graphs of minimum degree $\delta\le 3$, the above properties can help to decide this question. For example, the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) is [3-regular](wikipedia://en/85821). Hence, if it were polytopal, it would be the edge graph of a 3-dimensional polytope. The Petersen graph is however not planar and thus cannot be the edge graph of a 3-polytope.

For graphs of minimum degree $\delta\ge 4$ such questions are generally much harder to answer. For example, as of July 2025 it is unknown whether the [Cartesian graph product](wikipedia://en/2856852) of two Petersen graphs is polytopal.[^o3] It is known that if it were polytopal, then the polytope must be of dimension four or five.[^o24]

## Examples {#Examples}

### Named families {#Named_families}

- The [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) $C_n$ is the edge graph of the $n$-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621).
- The [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_n$ is the edge graph of the $(n-1)$-dimensional [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) (including the [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654), [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) and [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821)).
- The [hypercube graph](wikipedia://en/6706815) $Q_n$ is th edge graph of the $n$-dimensional [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) (including [square](wikipedia://en/659939) and [cube](wikipedia://en/6285)). Its distance-two graph is known as the [halved cube graph](wikipedia://en/39329236) and is the edge graphs of the corresponding [demihypercube](wikipedia://en/6833695).
- The [Turán graph](wikipedia://en/360581) $T(2n,n)$ (also known as "cocktail party graph" [^o7]) is the edge graph of the $n$-dimensional [cross-polytope](wikipedia://en/716401) (including [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) and [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422)).
- The [Johnson graph](wikipedia://en/2948666) $J(n,k)$ is the edge graph of the [hypersimplex](wikipedia://en/44749312) $\Delta_{n,k}$.
- The [Hamming graph](wikipedia://en/8046549) $H(d,q)$ is the edge graph of the $d$-th [cartesian power](wikipedia://en/24104095) of the $(q-1)$-dimensional simplex. This includes the edge graphs of both simplices $H(1,q)$ and hypercubes $H(d,2)$, but also other polytopes such as the [(3,3)-duoprism](wikipedia://en/41745711) $H(2,3)$.
- The [Bruhat graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruhat_graph) is the edge graph of the [permutahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutahedron). More generally, the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of a finite [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) (with the natural generators) is the edge graph of the corresponding [omnitruncated](wikipedia://en/7083690) [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392), or more generally, the edge graph of a generic [orbit polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_polytope) of the associated [reflection group](wikipedia://en/2422023).

### Other named examples {#Other_named_examples}

- The [icosahedral graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_graph) is the edge graph of the [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968).
- The [dodecahedral graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodecahedral_graph) is the edge graph of the [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361).
- The [Schläfli graph](wikipedia://en/28200770) is the edge graph of the 6-dimensional [2<sub>21</sub> polytope](wikipedia://en/19232473).
- The [Gosset graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosset_graph) is the edge graph of the 7-dimensional [3<sub>21</sub> polytope](wikipedia://en/19236353).

## Operations {#Operations}

Some operations on polytopes translate to their edge graphs in a natural way.

- The edge graph of the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) $P\times Q$ of two polytopes is the [Cartesian graph product](wikipedia://en/2856852) of the edge graphs of $P$ and $Q$. For example, the product of a cycle graph and $K_1$ is the edge graph of a [prism](wikipedia://en/283007). There are non-polytopal graphs whose product is polytopal.[^o24]
- The edge graph of the [join](wikipedia://en/3127114) $P\star Q$ of two polytopes is the [graph join](wikipedia://en/9938244#Binary_operations) of the edge graphs of $P$ and $Q$. The graph join is constructed from the disjoint union of the edge graphs by adding all edges between them. The same edge graph is obtained for the [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_polytopes) $P\oplus Q$ (the dual of the Cartesian product) under the assumption that both $P$ and $Q$ are of dimension at least two.
- For 3-dimensional polytopes the edge graph of the [dual polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope) is the [dual graph](wikipedia://en/2536864) of its edge graph.

## Reconstruction from the edge graph {#Reconstruction_from_the_edge_graph}

Given the edge graph of a polytope of dimension three or lower it is possible to reconstruct the polytope's [full combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_lattice), that is, the complete list of faces and incidences between them. For example, the 2-dimensional faces of a 3-polytope correspond exactly to the [non-separating](wikipedia://en/1499165) [induced](wikipedia://en/1655188) [cycles](wikipedia://en/878962) in the edge graph.[^o11] Also, for polytopes of dimension up to three it is possible to tell the dimension of the polytope from the edge graph. In dimension $d\ge 4$ neither of this is the case. There exist combinatorially distinct polytopes with [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) edge graphs, and even polytopes of different dimensions with isomorphic edge graphs.

### Examples of non-reconstructability {#Examples_of_non-reconstructability}

The edge graph of a [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) is a [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816). However, in dimension $d\ge 4$ there are other polytopes whose edge graph is complete. These are called [1-neighborly polytopes](wikipedia://en/26212072). For example, both the $d$-dimension simplex and the 4-dimensional [cyclic polytope](wikipedia://en/26652374) $C_4(d+1)$ have edge graph $K_{d+1}$.

[Hypercubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercubes) of sufficiently large dimension share their edge graphs with *neighborly cubical polytopes*. For example, the edge graph of the 10-dimensional hypercube is also the edge graph of a 4-dimensional polytope.[^o13]

One general technique for obtaining combinatorially distinct polytopes with the same edge graph is by constructing both the [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_polytopes) and the [join](wikipedia://en/3127114) of two polytopes. While these operations never generate polytopes of the same dimension, the resulting polytopes always have the same edge graph (assuming that we start from polytopes of dimension at least two). For example, the join $\Delta\star \Delta$ of two triangles is a 5-dimensional simplex, whereas the direct sum $\Delta\oplus\Delta$ is the 4-dimensional cyclic polytope on six vertices $C_4(6)$. Both have $K_6$ as their edge graph.

### Reconstruction in special cases {#Reconstruction_in_special_cases}

Reconstruction of the polytope's [full combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_lattice) from the edge graph is possible in special cases or when additional data is available:

- The combinatorics of a [simple polytope](wikipedia://en/8853286) can be reconstructed from the edge graph. This was first proven by Blind & Mani.[^o20] Later, a short proof was given by [Gil Kalai](wikipedia://en/19709056) using [unique sink orientations](wikipedia://en/18476346).[^o19]
- The combinatorics of a [zonotope](wikipedia://en/669406) can be reconstructed from the edge graph.[^o15][^o16]
- For simplicial polytopes, given the polytope's edge graph and its [space of self-stresses](wikipedia://en/66489065) it is possible to reconstruct the polytope up to affine equivalence.[^o23]

## Other relations {#Other_relations}

- The [simplex algorithm](wikipedia://en/349458) traverses the edge graph of a polytope to find the optimal solution to a [linear program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_program).
- The [Hirsch conjecture](wikipedia://en/10412371) asserts that the edge graph of a $d$-polytope has [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) at most $d$. A counterexample was found by Santos in 2012,.[^o22] The conjecture has since been reformulated in different ways. As of June 2025, no polynomial bound on the diameter in $d$ is known.
- Historically, the terms "vertex" and "edge" for graphs originated in the study of [polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470) and were only subsequently adopted into [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401).
