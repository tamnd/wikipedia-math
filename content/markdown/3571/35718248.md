# Ioan Mire Melik

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ioan Mire Melik"}

**Ioan Mire Melik**, or **Melic** (born **Iacob Ioan Miren Melik**;[^o3] August 9, 1840 – January 29, 1889), was a [Wallachian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallachia), later [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania) mathematician, educator and political figure, one of the early members of *[Junimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junimea)* literary society. Known for his work in private education, and for his tenure at the [University of Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza_University), he was the author of several early introductions to science—dealing with [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), but also with [topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography) and [surveying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveying). He was perceived as a bland figure at *Junimea* meetings, and had little to do with its literary agenda, but took care of administrative chores and, for a while, of its publishing venture. In his other job as headmaster of *Institutele-Unite* high school, where he employed poet [Mihai Eminescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihai_Eminescu) as a German-language teacher, he was regarded as remarkably stern, and had a publicized showdown with the students.

:::

Taking his cue from the *Junimist* leadership, Melik followed the literary club as it transformed itself into a [liberal conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_conservatism) party. He represented [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iași) city in the [Assembly of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_of_Romania) in the [1884 legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1884_Romanian_general_election), and then, under a *Junimist* government, helped enforce [Titu Maiorescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titu_Maiorescu)'s policies in the educational field. Melik is also remembered as an engineer and entrepreneur, who contributed to the modern history of [Costeștii Botoșanilor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Răchiți) village, his personal estate. He died of an unspecified disease at age 48, and was survived by his son Eugen, who followed in his footsteps as both a politician and an academic. Melik Jr was a leader of the [Democratic Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Nationalist_Party_%28Romania%29) during and after World War I, but disappeared from public life after being exposed and sentenced as a confidence man.

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early years {#Early_years}

The future scientist was born in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest), the Wallachian capital, on August 9, 1840 (although traditionally believed to have been born on August 15[^o3] or August 21,[^o6] and wrongly credited in some documents as a "Iași native").[^o7] He was of [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Romania) origins: the Meliks traced their origin to Ohan, a [Gregorian Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) clergyman.[^o3] As reported by the mathematician's son Eugen, they were recent immigrants from the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire), who had been scattered across the continents by [mounting persecutions against their community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_the_Ottoman_Empire); [Boghos Yousefian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boghos_Yousefian), the [Foreign Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_%28Egypt%29) of [Ottoman Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Egypt), was a close relative of the clan.[^o8] Ioan's parents were Ohan's son, Arakel "Popovici" Melik, and his wife Maria Gedik; they had two other children: Gavril, a boy, and Anika, a girl.[^o3] Ohan's other son, [Iacob Melic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iacob_Melic), was a successful architect. The Ottoman statesman [Mustafa Reshid Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Reshid_Pasha) recommended his work to the Wallachian government, but Iacob left the country and settled in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).[^o9] From his new home, Iacob Melik corresponded with the exiled leaders of the [1848 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallachian_Revolution_of_1848), in particular the moderate and pro-Ottoman faction of [Ion Ghica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Ghica), [Alexandru G. Golescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_G._Golescu) and Nicolae Pleșoianu.[^o10]

To the end of his life, Ioan was listed as having "Armenian Gregorian nationality".[^o11] According to his own testimony, he first attended the Armenian School in Bucharest's [Yellow District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sectors_of_Bucharest), and then regular school in that same neighborhood.[^o3] From 1854 to 1856, he completed secondary school at the [Saint Sava College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sava_College) and was, in his own account, an award-winning student.[^o3] From 1856, Iacob Melik took over the boy's education, taking him to [Imperial France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire). Young Melik was tutored at a private boarding house (1857), before enlisting at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) Medical School, but changed his mind and applied for the *[École des Mines de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mines_ParisTech)* (1860).[^o3] He finished school in 1864, and worked for a short while at [salt mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_mine) in France and [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium).[^o3]

Melik's work took him to [Moldavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavia), which, in 1859, had federated with Wallachia into the [United Principalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Principalities). The regime of *[Domnitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domnitor)* [Alexandru Ioan Cuza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza) assigned him to inspect the Moldavian salt mines, at [Slănic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slănic-Moldova) and [Târgu Ocna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Târgu_Ocna).[^o3] Known to his Iași colleagues and friends as *Mirmilic*,[^o12] he began attending the *Junimea* club in 1865, being one of the outside sympathizers of the cause, and manifestly lacking literary ambitions. [Iacob Negruzzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iacob_Negruzzi), the *Junimea* co-founder and biographer of its members, asserted: "\[Melik was\] once a passionate *Junimist*, but always kept silent."[^o3][^o13] Negruzzi jokingly added that the standard estimate, according to which the maths teacher had uttered some 23 words at club meetings held between 1865 and 1880, was probably exaggerated in Melik's favor.[^o13] Literary historian [George Călinescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Călinescu) suggests that Melik, like astronomer [Niculae Culianu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niculae_Culianu) or schoolteacher [Pavel Paicu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Paicu), only came to *Junimea* "out of devotion or for the sake of entertainment".[^o14] The mathematician was notorious for not having published a single line in the *Junimist* tribune, *[Convorbiri Literare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convorbiri_Literare)*.[^o3][^o12][^o13][^o15]

### *Junimist* projects {#Junimist_projects}

With his *Junimist* credentials, Melik was made substitute Professor of Mathematics at Iași University (February 23, 1865).[^o3][^o12] In parallel, he taught at a high school for young cadets.[^o11] During October 1865, he joined a group of academics, also comprising Rector [Titu Maiorescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titu_Maiorescu), museum organizer [Gheorghe Panaiteanu Bardasare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Panaiteanu_Bardasare), Culianu, and Negruzzi, in expressing support for Cuza's autonomous interpretation of [Romanian vassalage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal_and_tributary_states_of_the_Ottoman_Empire)—namely, his attitude toward the Ottoman overlords. Their [open letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_letter) congratulated the *Domnitor* for not accepting to be scolded by [Grand Vizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ottoman_grand_viziers) [Fuad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_Fuad_Pasha).[^o17] Melik acquired university tenure in April 1866, shortly after Cuza's regime had been toppled by the "[monstrous coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monstrous_coalition)" and replaced by a [Princely Lieutenancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_Lieutenancy_of_Romania_%281866%29).[^o3] In May, he was caught up in the riots at Iași, whereby the [Free and Independent Faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_Independent_Faction) signaled its opposition to a proposed naturalization of [resident Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Romania). In a letter to *[Românul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Românul)*, Melik explained that he did not support the Factionalist platform, but had merely objected to the riots being quashed by the [Romanian Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Land_Forces), which reportedly ignored [academic freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_freedom). His protest against the arrest of a university colleague resulted in his temporary arrest, ending when the rectorate intervened in his favor.[^o18]

Soon after, Melik became a conservative ally of the *Junimist* Rector Maiorescu, helping him do battle with the University's well connected Factionalists and the other [left-liberal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_and_radicalism_in_Romania). As noted by Călinescu, the University's *Junimist* circle was formed around Professors Melik, Culianu, I. Negruzzi, [Ioan Caragiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioan_Caragiani) and [Nicolae Mandrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Mandrea), with *Junimea* itself being "a political get-together with some cultural aspects."[^o19] Although still recognized as a secondary and inactive participant, Melik was involved on several early *Junimist* projects, and referred to by Maiorescu as the group's "banker".[^o3][^o13] In addition to promoting *Junimist* goals at University, he participated in founding (and financing) the club's own private school, or "Academic Institute". He himself described the event as follows: "In summer 1866, I have founded the Academic Institute, in partnership with several colleagues, from both University and High School \[that is, the National High School Iași\]."[^o3] Melik took personal care about furnishing the Institute, purchasing its books from prestigious companies such as [Hachette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachette_%28publisher%29), and keeping score of the more reliable publishing houses in Moldavia and Wallachia.[^o3]

The institution itself left a mark on [Romanian educational history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Romania), being seen as a model school in the field of private enterprise.[^o3][^o20] It remains primarily noted for its contribution in [Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanities), which were assigned to Maiorescu. [Constantin Meissner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Meissner), the dedicated student and future *Junimist* pedagogue, recalled: "we \[students\] found ourselves speaking, gesticulating, moving our heads just like our precious model \[Maiorescu\]."[^o21] Another former student, and later teacher at the Institute, was scholar [A. D. Xenopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._D._Xenopol). He describes his alma mater as "an eminent private school", and Melik as one of the "leading professorial forces" in Moldavia; he also credits Melik's "complete and measured exactitude" as influential for his own work ethic.[^o15] According to Xenopol, Melik's refusal to tolerate staff truancy earned him a reputation as a strict colleague. Referencing an Armenian stereotype, the other *Junimists* began calling Melik a *mindirigiu* ("[quilter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quilter)") of their absences.[^o15]

Melik was, with [Vasile Pogor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasile_Pogor) and [Al. Farra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al._Farra), a manager of the literary club's own publishing company—a bankrupt project, since Pogor would only purchase books from his own area of interest.[^o22] Before going down, this commercial branch of *Junimea* put out a number of general education works, including Melik's textbook *Elemente de aritmetică* ("Elements of Arithmetic").[^o23] Melik was also a [cashier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cashier) during *Junimea* anniversaries.[^o3][^o13] Witnessing the decline of *Junimist* capital, he even proposed, unsuccessfully, that the club fortunes be invested in a salt mine.[^o3] Melik was not dissuaded by the opposition, and continued to draft plans about investing *Junimist* money, forming a [credit union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_union), and opening up the Humpel girls' institute.[^o3][^o13] His record of *Junimea* gains and expenses is seen by philologist Constantin Coroiu as evidence of Melik's "computer-like rigor".[^o13] According to Negruzzi, Melik was an outstandingly passionate defender of Maiorescu's reputation. When the *Junimist* leader was framed a [statutory rape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutory_rape) scandal and taken to court, Melik provoked the judge to a duel, over not being allowed to enter the courthouse.[^o3][^o13]

### *Institutele-Unite* and Assembly mandate {#Institutele-Unite_and_Assembly_mandate}

On February 18, 1867, the *Junimist* mathematician was also admitted into the conservative wing of [Romanian Freemasonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry_in_Romania), or "Star of Romania" [Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonic_Lodge). This branch of the [Oriental Rite of Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_and_Primitive_Rite) and the [Grand Orient de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Orient_de_France), wherein Pogor himself had reached the 90th Degree, had for its Iorgu Sutzu as its Venerable Master, and came to include most of the *Junimist* notabilities.[^o6][^o7] On October 21 of the same year, Melik was granted the Fellow Craft Rank in Masonic orders.[^o3][^o13] Noting the role of *Junimist* Masons in combating the liberals' stated [xenophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophobia), researcher [Mihai Dim. Sturdza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihai_Dim._Sturdza) suggests that the "Star of Romania" was in fact "a place of refuge, \[...\] where the foreign-born intellectuals \[...\] created a bloc with the conservative [boyars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyars_of_Wallachia_and_Moldavia) and the Jewish merchants".[^o6] Their main liberal detractors included senior academic [Simion Bărnuțiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simion_Bărnuțiu) and other outspoken [antisemites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism).[^o6][^o7]

Melik's 1867 arithmetic course was highly popular, going through ten reprints.[^o3] He followed up with other textbooks and introductory courses: *Despre moneta română* ("On the Romanian Currency", 1868); *Elemente de Geometrie* ("Elements of Geometry", 1869, ten reprints); *Elemente de Topografie* ("Elements of [Topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography)", 1879, four reprints).[^o3] From 1883 to 1888, Melik, Culianu and [Constantin Climescu](wikipedia://en/45493672) put out the magazine *Recreații Științifice* ("Scientific Recreations"), considered a direct precursor of the more prestigious *[Gazeta Matematică](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazeta_Matematică)*.[^o25] The author continued his work at the Institute before and after 1875, when it merged with the "New High School" into the private-run *Institutele-Unite* ("Unified Institutes");[^o3] according to the former student Eugeniu Vincler, the complete merger only took place in 1879.[^o26]

Also in 1875, Melik formalized his adoption by Iași city, by beginning construction work on a townhouse (completed 1882).[^o3] He married a local, Roxandra.[^o27] The new *Domnitor*, [Carol I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_I_of_Romania), recognized Melik's educational merits by granting him the Bene Merenti medal 1st class.[^o3][^o28] However, according to Vincler, he was a "stern" headmaster, and managed to alienate the students by bringing in [Mihai Eminescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihai_Eminescu) to serve as their tutor in German; they resented Eminescu because of his cantankerousness and his harsh grading of students.[^o29] A small-scale student revolt, which involved future socialist agitators [Constantin Mille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Mille) and [Vasile Morțun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasile_Morțun), was curbed by Melik.[^o30] According to one anecdotal account, he bent their will by ordering the cafeteria not to provide them with any food for the duration of their strike.[^o31] Reflecting back on his participation in the scandal, Vincler expressed shame, noting that Eminescu had since been discovered as a poetic genius, and elevated into the national pantheon.[^o32] Mille himself was spared expulsion after a personal interview with Melik, whom he knew, derisively, as *Mustață-Roșie* ("Red Mustache").[^o33]

Melik was a distant witness to the [Romanian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_War_of_Independence) (1877–1878), which saw the country cut off her remaining ties of vassalage with the Ottoman monarchy; he collected the letters sent home by his relative, Captain Grigore Bănulescu, who had fought [at Plevna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Plevna).[^o34] Shortly after the establishment of the [Romanian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) in 1881, Melik also made his way into the *Junimist* political party. Maiorescu, who led *Junimea* members in and out of the opposition [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_%28Romania,_1880–1918%29), assigned him a place on the independent list for Iași in the [election of 1884](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1884_Romanian_general_election). In practice, Melik, Pogor, Negruzzi and others were in a cartel with the ruling [National Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberal_Party_%28Romania,_1875%29), and were elected deputies on a united pro-government list.[^o35] A while after, with [Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Romania) [Ion Brătianu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Brătianu) ignoring repeated *Junimist* requests for cabinet positions, the entire faction moved back into the opposition.[^o36] Carol I, the newly crowned [King of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Romania), again rewarded Melik's services: in February 1884, *[Monitorul Oficial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitorul_Oficial)* published news that the mathematician had been made Officer of the [Order of the Star of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Star_of_Romania); when later [errata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errata) informed Melik that he was merely a Knight of that company, he refused to accept the award altogether.[^o3]

### Final appointments and death {#Final_appointments_and_death}

Melik had given up his position as headmaster in 1884, when Climescu took over.[^o38] He was still an active popularizer on scientific topics, publishing, in 1885, a [surveying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveying) and [ballistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistics) tract: *Equerulu grafometru sau quadrantulu de campania al D-lui Colonel de Artilerie A. Costiescu* ("The [Graphometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphometer) or Campaign Quadrant of the Artillery Colonel A. Costiescu").[^o3] In January 1886, *[România Liberă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/România_Liberă)* aired his polemic with the National Liberal [Education Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_%28Romania%29), [Dimitrie Sturdza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrie_Sturdza)—he objected in particular to Sturdza's new grading system, which made students with one failing grade repeat the entire year.[^o39] In 1888, Melik issued three new textbooks: Arithmetic for 3rd- and 4th-graders, Geometry for the primary course, and a *Curs practic de Geometrie elementară* ("Practical Course in Elementary Geometry").[^o3] By then, he had invested his money into a large-scale agricultural venture, on the Moldavian estate of [Costeștii Botoșanilor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Răchiți). The purchase took place in March 1885. As he himself noted, 507 *[fálce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_units_of_measurement)* of land (some 726 [hectares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hectare)) were exchanged for his 210,000 [lei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_leu), cash.[^o3] This was a third of the estate, as defined by tradition, and a half of the actual village.[^o40]

In March 1888, after prolonged political infighting, Carol I turned his back on both the Conservatives and the National Liberals. The *Junimists* experienced a moment of triumph, with [Theodor Rosetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Rosetti) as Premier and Maiorescu as Education Minister. For a while, Melik himself was Secretary General of the Education Ministry, his position at the University filled in by [Ion Ralet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Ralet).[^o3] The *Junimist* loyalist was assigned by Maiorescu to inspect the Moldavian schools. Their common goal was to purge from the schooling system a new political threat: socialist activists. Melik was ordered to work only with the known anti-socialists, and to sack all those suspected of having affiliated with the socialist clubs.[^o13][^o41] In one such letter, the Minister instructs his subordinate to inquire about whether the known [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism) [Ioan Nădejde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioan_Nădejde) still had relatives working on a state salary.[^o13]

In early 1889, still active as General Inspector of the Schools and Principal of *Institutele-Unite*,[^o42] Melik was struck down with a quickly progressing illness. He died in his home at Iași on January 29, and was buried with military honors at [Eternitatea cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternitatea_cemetery); despite his lifelong Gregorian faith, the funeral service was [Romanian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Orthodox_Church), delivered by the local *[Archiereus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archiereus)*, Irimia Galățanu, at the [Annunciation Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annunciation_Church,_Iași).[^o11] The ceremony was entirely sponsored by the education ministry, with a credit line of 1,500 lei.[^o43] His death was perceived as a tragedy by his fellow *Junimists*. Poet [Anton Naum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Naum) wrote, shortly after the events, "We have lost poor Melik in the space of 7 days, however good and healthy he had been. It left me with a hallowed heart, seeing that we were very close."[^o3] The same Naum took over Melik's job at the inspectorate, in March 1889.[^o44]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Melik's brief obituary in *[Familia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Familia_%28literary_magazine%29)* magazine informed readers that the inspector's schoolbooks had "genuine value";[^o42] a homage was rendered by *Institutele-Unite* in September 1891, upon its [silver jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_jubilee).[^o46] Later, the post-*Junimist* historiographer [I. E. Torouțiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._E._Torouțiu) presented Melik as a highly moral man of action: "He was one of the society's devoted members, without ever asking it for perks \[or\] advancements, even though he was in a position to demand them, under the governments of his political friends."[^o15] By 1978, when they were assigned entries in Ionel Maftei's dictionary of Iași-themed biographies, Melik and Naum were reportedly among the "picturesque and quite forgotten" subclass of *Junimists*.[^o47]

The mathematician was survived by his widow and their four children, three of whom were daughters[^o11] (four other children had died in infancy).[^o3] Roxandra remained active as a philanthropist, and by 1908 was serving on the board of Maria Hinna's *Pînea Săracilor* charity.[^o48] One of her and Ioan's daughters became the wife of Lieutenant Colonel Nicu Scorțescu. A former student of her father, he served with distinction in World War I and died in 1924, while leading the [Romanian Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Police) in [Hotin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotin_County).[^o49] Ioan's only surviving son, Eugen, took over his papers.[^o3][^o8] Himself a graduate of *Institutele-Unite*,[^o50] he was assigned as that school's headmaster in August 1898.[^o51] He then became a substitute Law Professor at the University of Iași, beginning in 1903. Unable to complete his Ph. D., and complaining about the criteria for academic advancement, he finally resigned in September 1920.[^o52] Melik Jr also built the towering landmark of Costeștii Botoșanilor, a castle-shaped vacation home;[^o40] one half of the village is commonly known as *Costești-Melic*.[^o40]

In parallel, Eugen became an establishment politician, serving as a Conservative deputy for [Botoșani County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botoșani_County) in 1911.[^o53] During the conflict opposing [Petre P. Carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petre_P._Carp) and [Virgil Arion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil_Arion) to Maiorescu, he sided with the former two and their "neo-*Junimist*" faction.[^o54] He finally defected to the National Liberal caucus in 1913.[^o55] During the second half of World War I, when [southern Romania under German occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Romanian_Debacle), Melik was in Iași, which functioned as a provisional capital of the rump Romanian state. He joined [Nicolae Iorga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Iorga) and [A. C. Cuza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._C._Cuza)'s [Democratic Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Nationalist_Party_%28Romania%29) (PND), and was designated a candidate for the Assembly in the scheduled elections of December 1918; his home also hosted the Committee for the Defense of Iași's Interests.[^o56]

Upon the creation of [Greater Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Romania), Melik Jr had become general secretary of the PND.[^o57] His political career was cut short when he acquired infamy as a confidence man: in June 1924, the [Ilfov County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilfov_County) tribunal found him guilty of several counts of fraud, after he had illegally obtained control of a Bucharest townhouse from his late aunt Anika. Litigation over this issue, which resulted in Melik being imprisoned for eight months, was initiated by the [Armenian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Church,_Bucharest), as one of the legal successors.[^o58] In April 1932, he was again charged with fraud, due to his land speculation with Anika's estate in [Tisău](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tisău), and was ordered to pay reparations to his victims.[^o59] He was living in [Botoșani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botoșani) in March 1940, witnessing there the death of his wife Elena; they had two daughters, Ioana and Nicolița.[^o60] His mother Roxandra died in August 1942, while in monastic seclusion at [Agapia Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agapia_Monastery), and was buried next to her husband, at Eternitatea.[^o27]
