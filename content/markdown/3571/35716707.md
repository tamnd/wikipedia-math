# William Galbraith (mathematician)

*Image: William Galbraith's grave, Grange Cemetery.JPG.* William Galbraith's grave, Grange Cemetery

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Galbraith"}

Rev **William Galbraith** (1786 – 27 October 1850) was a Scottish mathematician. He taught mathematics and nautical astronomy in Edinburgh, and took an interest in surveying work, becoming an advocate of the extension of the work of [triangulating Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_Triangulation_of_Great_Britain).[^o2]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born at [Greenlaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlaw), [Berwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwickshire).[^o4] Initially he was a schoolmaster. His pupil [William Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Rutherford_%28mathematician%29) walked long distances to attend his school at [Eccles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eccles,_Berwickshire). Subsequently, he moved to Edinburgh, and graduated A.M. at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) in 1821.[^o5]

## Surveyor {#Surveyor}

During the 1830s Galbraith became interested in the surveying problems of Scotland. In 1831 he pointed out that [Arthur's Seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur's_Seat) had a strongly magnetic peak.[^o7] In 1837 he pointed out the impact of anomalies in measurement, work that received recognition;[^o8] it was topical because of the 1836 geological map of Scotland by [John MacCulloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_MacCulloch), with which critics had found fault on topographical as well as geological grounds. A paper on the locations of places on the [River Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Clyde) was recognised in 1837 by a gold medal, from the Society for the Encouragement of the Useful Arts for Scotland.[^o10]

Galbraith followed with detailed *Remarks on the Geographical Position of some Points on the West Coast of Scotland* (1838).[^o11] Having made some accurate surveys of his own, he lobbied for further attention from the national survey.[^o2]

*Image: Pocket box circle Galbraith.png.* Galbraith's pocket-box circle, an invention for portable surveying.

## Later life {#Later_life}

About 1832 Galbraith was licensed a minister by the presbytery of [Dunse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunse). He married Eleanor Gale in 1833.[^o5]

Galbraith was buried with his wife in the north-east section of the [Grange Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grange,_Edinburgh) in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh).[^o13]

## Works {#Works}

Galbraith's major works combined textbook material with mathematical tables:

- *Mathematical and Astronomical Tables* (1827):[^o15] review.[^o16]
- *Trigonometrical Surveying, Levelling, and Railway Engineering* (1842)[^o17]

He edited [John Ainslie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ainslie)'s 1812 treatise on land surveying (1849),[^o18] and with William Rutherford revised [John Bonnycastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bonnycastle)'s *Algebra*.
