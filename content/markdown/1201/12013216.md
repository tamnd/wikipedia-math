# Daniel Kane (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Kane"}

**Daniel Mertz Kane** (born 1986) is an American mathematician. He is a full professor with a joint position in the Mathematics Department and the Computer Science and Engineering Department at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Kane was born in [Madison, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin), to [Janet E. Mertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_E._Mertz) and Jonathan M. Kane, professors of [oncology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncology) and of mathematics and computer science, respectively.[^o5][^o6]

He attended Wingra School, a small alternative K-8 school in Madison that focuses on [self-guided education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodidactism). By 3rd grade, he had mastered K through 9th-grade mathematics.[^o5] Starting at age 13, he took honors math courses at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) and did research under the mentorship of [Ken Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Ono) while dual enrolled at [Madison West High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_West_High_School).[^o7] He earned gold medals in the 2002 and 2003 [International Mathematical Olympiads](wikipedia://en/82208).[^o6] Prior to his 17th birthday, he resolved an open conjecture proposed years earlier by [Andrews](wikipedia://en/3356273) and Lewis; for this research, he was named Fellow Laureate of the [Davidson Institute for Talent Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidson_Institute_for_Talent_Development).[^o9]

He graduated [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 2007 with two bachelor's degrees, one in mathematics with computer science and the other in physics.[^o3] While at MIT, Kane was one of four people since 2003 (and one of nine in the history of the competition) to be named a four-time Putnam Fellow in the [William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition).[^o10] He also won the 2007 [Morgan Prize](wikipedia://en/4180157)[^o5] and competed as part of the MIT team in the [Mathematical Contest in Modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Contest_in_Modeling) four times, earning the highest score three times and winning the Ben Fusaro Award in 2004, INFORMS Award in 2006, and [SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376) Award in 2007.[^o11] He also won the [Machtey Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtey_Award) as an undergraduate in 2005, with Tim Abbott and Paul Valiant, for the best student-authored paper at the [Symposium on Foundations of Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symposium_on_Foundations_of_Computer_Science) that year, on the complexity of two-player win-loss games.[^o12]

Kane received his doctorate in mathematics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 2011; his dissertation, on [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), was supervised by [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679). In his [curriculum vitae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curriculum_vitae), Kane lists as mentors [Ken Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Ono) while in high school; [Erik Demaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Demaine), [Joseph Gallian](wikipedia://en/18351855), and Cesar Silva while an undergraduate student at MIT; and [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679), [Benedict Gross](wikipedia://en/4326863), and Henry Cohn while a graduate student at Harvard.[^o14]

## Research contributions {#Research_contributions}

In 2010, joint work with [Jelani Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelani_Nelson) and [David Woodruff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_P._Woodruff) won both the IBM Pat Goldberg Memorial[^o16] and [Symposium on Principles of Database Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symposium_on_Principles_of_Database_Systems) (PODS) best paper awards[^o17] for work on an optimal algorithm for the [count-distinct problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count-distinct_problem).

The bulk of his modern research consists of computational statistics, with a focus in [robust statistics](wikipedia://en/2885691).[^o18][^o19]
