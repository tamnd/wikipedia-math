# Eduard Čech

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eduard Čech"}

**Eduard Čech** (; 29 June 1893 – 15 March 1960) was a Czech [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). His research interests included [projective differential geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_differential_geometry) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). He is especially known for the technique known as [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897) (in topology) and the notion of [Čech cohomology](wikipedia://en/856614). He was the first to publish a proof of [Tychonoff's theorem](wikipedia://en/82739) in 1937.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in [Stračov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stračov), then in [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), now in the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic). His father was Čeněk Čech, a policeman, and his mother was Anna Kleplová.

After attending the gymnasium in [Hradec Králové](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hradec_Králové), Čech was admitted to the Philosophy Faculty of [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) of Prague in 1912. In 1915 he was drafted into the [Austro-Hungarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army) and participated in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), after which he completed his undergraduate degree in 1918.[^o4] He received his doctoral degree in 1920 at Charles University; his thesis, titled *On Curves and Plane Elements of the Third Order*, was written under the direction of [Karel Petr](wikipedia://en/4436275). In 1921–1922 he collaborated with [Guido Fubini](wikipedia://en/349051) in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), Italy. He taught at [Masaryk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaryk_University) in [Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno) and at Charles University. [Ivo Babuška](wikipedia://en/13503456), [Vlastimil Dlab](wikipedia://en/56596473), [Zdeněk Frolík](wikipedia://en/37391956), [Věra Trnková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Věra_Trnková), and [Petr Vopěnka](wikipedia://en/3105466) were among the notable mathematicians who studied as his doctoral students.[^o5]

He attended the [First International Topological Conference](wikipedia://en/60495654) held in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) 4–10 September 1935. He made two presentations there: "Accessibility and homology" and "[Betti groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betti_group) with different coefficient groups".[^o6]

He died in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague) in 1960.[^o7]
