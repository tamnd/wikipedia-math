# Hermitian wavelet

*Image: Hermitian wavelets.svg.* The Gaussian function (n=0) and its first derivatives.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hermitian wavelet"}

**Hermitian wavelets** are a family of [discrete](wikipedia://en/857564) and [continuous](wikipedia://en/679596) [wavelets](wikipedia://en/50903) used in the constant and discrete Hermite wavelet transforms. The $n^\textrm{th}$ Hermitian wavelet is defined as the normalized $n^\textrm{th}$ [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of a [Gaussian distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_distribution) for each positive $n$:[^o2]

$$\Psi_{n}(x)=(2n)^{-\frac{n}{2}}c_{n}\operatorname{He}_{n}\left(x\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}},$$

where $\operatorname{He}_{n}(x)$ denotes the $n^\textrm{th}$ [probabilist's Hermite polynomial](wikipedia://en/195984). Each normalization coefficient $c_{n}$ is given by

$$c_{n} = \left(n^{\frac{1}{2}-n}\Gamma\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}} = \left(n^{\frac{1}{2}-n}\sqrt{\pi}2^{-n}(2n-1)!!\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.$$

The function $\Psi\in L_{\rho, \mu}(-\infty, \infty)$ is said to be an admissible Hermite wavelet if it satisfies the admissibility condition:[^o3]

:::

$$C_\Psi = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{\|\hat\Psi (n)\|^2}{\|n\|}} < \infty$$

where $\hat \Psi (n)$ are the terms of the [Hermite transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermite_transform) of $\Psi$.

The $n^{th}$ Hermitian wavelet has $n$ [vanishing moments](wikipedia://en/78658977),

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^k \Psi_n(x)\,dx = 0, \qquad k=0,\ldots,n-1,$$

and is therefore insensitive to polynomial trends of degree less than $n$. [^o4]

In [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision) and [image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_processing), Gaussian derivative operators of different orders are frequently used as a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) for expressing various types of visual operations; see [scale space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_space) and [N-jet](wikipedia://en/6838895).[^o5]

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The first three derivatives of the [Gaussian function](wikipedia://en/245552) with $\mu=0,\;\sigma=1$:

$$f(t) = \pi^{-1/4}e^{(-t^2/2)},$$

are:

$$\begin{aligned} f'(t) & = -\pi^{-1/4}te^{(-t^2/2)}, \\ f''(t) & = \pi^{-1/4}(t^2 - 1)e^{(-t^2/2)},\\ f^{(3)}(t) & = \pi^{-1/4}(3t - t^3)e^{(-t^2/2)}, \end{aligned}$$

and their $L^2$ norms $\lVert f' \rVert=\sqrt{2}/2, \lVert f'' \rVert=\sqrt{3}/2, \lVert f^{(3)} \rVert= \sqrt{30}/4$.

Normalizing the derivatives yields three Hermitian wavelets:

$$\begin{aligned} \Psi_{1}(t) &= \sqrt{2}\pi^{-1/4}te^{(-t^2/2)},\\ \Psi_{2}(t) &=\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{3}\pi^{-1/4}(1-t^2)e^{(-t^2/2)},\\ \Psi_{3}(t) &= \frac{2}{15}\sqrt{30}\pi^{-1/4}(t^3 - 3t)e^{(-t^2/2)}. \end{aligned}$$

:::
