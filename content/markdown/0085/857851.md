# Ricker wavelet

*Image: MexicanHatMathematica.svg.* Mexican hat

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ricker wavelet"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), the **Ricker wavelet**,[^o2] **Mexican hat wavelet**, or **Marr wavelet** (for [David Marr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Marr_%28neuroscientist%29)) [^o3][^o4]

:::

$$\psi(t) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3\sigma}\pi^{1/4}} \left(1 - \left(\frac{t}{\sigma}\right)^2 \right) e^{-\frac{t^2}{2\sigma^2}}$$

is the negative [normalized](wikipedia://en/398189) $\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi^2=1\right)$ second [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of a [Gaussian function](wikipedia://en/245552), i.e., up to scale and normalization, the second [Hermite function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermite_function). It is a special case of the family of [continuous wavelets](wikipedia://en/862322) ([wavelets](wikipedia://en/50903) used in a [continuous wavelet transform](wikipedia://en/679596)) known as [Hermitian wavelets](wikipedia://en/857867). The Ricker wavelet is frequently employed to model seismic data and as a broad-spectrum source term in computational electrodynamics.

The Ricker wavelet has two [vanishing moments](wikipedia://en/78658977),

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \psi(t)\,dt = 0, \qquad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} t\,\psi(t)\,dt = 0,$$

and is therefore insensitive to any linear trends $y=ax + b$ in the analyzed signal.[^o5]

The 2-dimensional generalization

$$\psi(x,y) = \frac{1}{\pi\sigma^4}\left(1-\frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{x^2+y^2}{\sigma^2}\right)\right) e^{-\frac{x^2+y^2}{2\sigma^2}}$$

*Image: Marr-wavelet2.jpg.* 3D view of 2D Mexican hat wavelet

of this wavelet is called the [Laplacian of Gaussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian_of_Gaussian) function. In practice, this wavelet is sometimes approximated by the [difference of Gaussians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_of_Gaussians) (DoG) function, because the DoG is separable.[^o6] It can therefore save considerable computation time in two or more dimensions. The scale-normalized Laplacian (in $L_1$-norm) is frequently used as a [blob detector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blob_detection) and for automatic scale selection in [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision) applications; see [Laplacian of Gaussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian_of_Gaussian) and [scale space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_space). The relation between this Laplacian of the Gaussian operator and the [difference-of-Gaussians operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_of_Gaussians) is explained in appendix A in Lindeberg (2015).[^o7] [Derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921) of [cardinal B-splines](wikipedia://en/21834#Cardinal_B-spline) can also approximate the Mexican hat wavelet.[^o8]

## Frequency domain {#Frequency_domain}

Since Ricker wavelet is, up to a scaling factor, the second derivative of a Gaussian function, according to the properties of the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247#Differentiation), its spectrum is equal to the spectrum of a Gaussian function multiplied by the square of the frequency. The spectrum of a Gaussian function with a given parameter σ is a Gaussian function with parameter $\omega=1/\sigma$. Thus, the wavelet spectrum has the shape [^o10]

$$\hat{\psi}(\omega) \propto \omega^2 \exp\left(-\frac{\sigma^2\omega^2}{2}\right).$$

The $w^2$ term is responsible for suppressing low frequencies, while the Gaussian term is responsible for suppressing high frequencies. Thus, the Ricker wavelet acts as a [band-pass filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band-pass_filter) with a peak transmission frequency $\omega_p=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sigma}$. The frequency band for half amplitude transmission level is $\omega_{min}\approx0.48\omega_p$, $\omega_{max}\approx1.64\omega_p$, its width $\Delta\omega_{\frac12 A} \approx1.16\omega_p$.
