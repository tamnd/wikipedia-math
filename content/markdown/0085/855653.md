# Benedetto Castelli

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Benedetto Castelli"}

**Benedetto Castelli** (1578 – 9 April 1643), born **Antonio Castelli**, was an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians) mathematician. Benedetto was his religious name that he adopted when he entered the [Benedictine Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine_Order) in 1595.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia), Castelli studied at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua) and later became an abbot at the [Benedictine monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine_monastery) in [Monte Cassino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Cassino).

He was a long-time friend and supporter of his teacher, [Galileo Galilei](wikipedia://en/29688374), and in turn, he was a teacher to [Galileo's son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Gamba). He assisted Galileo's study of [sunspots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunspot) and participated in the examination of the theories of [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus). Castelli was interested in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [hydraulics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulics). He was appointed as a mathematician to the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa), replacing Galileo, and later at the [University of Rome La Sapienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_La_Sapienza). Castelli introduced [Bonaventura Cavalieri](wikipedia://en/412561) to Galileo, leading to extensive correspondence between the latter; Galileo was instrumental in procuring a position for Cavalieri at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) in 1629.

Castelli was involved in the discovery of the [phases of Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phases_of_Venus):

In December 1610, Galileo received a letter from Castelli, asking if the phases of Venus were observable through Galileo's new telescope.[^o4] Days later, Galileo wrote in a letter to [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) saying that he'd observed Venus going through phases, but took complete credit for himself. It is unclear, lacking copies of any earlier correspondence, whether Castelli was telling Galileo of it for the first time, or responding to Galileo having previously informed him of it.[^o5]

Castelli died in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome). His students included [Giovanni Alfonso Borelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Alfonso_Borelli), [Raffaello Magiotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaello_Magiotti), [Antonio Nardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Nardi), and [Evangelista Torricelli](wikipedia://en/210413), the inventor of the [barometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barometer) and an early proponent of the [air pump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pump). He recommended [Gasparo Berti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gasparo_Berti) for a chair of mathematics at Sapienza. Berti was to be his successor at the university, but he died before he could take the post.

## Works {#Works}

*Image: OPUSCOLI.FILO.jpg.* *Opuscoli filosofici*, Brescia, 1669

He published *Mensuration of Running Water*, an important work on fluids in motion, and then his *Geometrical Demonstrations of the Measure of Running Waters* in which the publishing notes described him as Abbot of [San Benedetto Aloysio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Benedetto_Aloysio) and Mathematician to [Pope Urban VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII), once a supporter of his mentor, Galileo.

He dedicated both publications to "the most Illustrious, and most Excellent Prince" [Taddeo Barberini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taddeo_Barberini), a nephew of Pope Urban VIII.[^o7]
