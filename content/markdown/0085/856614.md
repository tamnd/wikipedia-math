# Čech cohomology

*Image: Penrose-dreieck.svg.* A [Penrose triangle](wikipedia://en/56108) depicts a nontrivial element of the first cohomology of an [annulus](wikipedia://en/356158) with values in the group of distances from the observer.[^o1]

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Čech cohomology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), **Čech cohomology** is a [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) theory based on the intersection properties of [open](wikipedia://en/39358) [covers](wikipedia://en/317552) of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). It is named for the mathematician [Eduard Čech](wikipedia://en/859830).

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

Let *X* be a topological space, and let $\mathcal{U}$ be an open cover of *X*. Let $N(\mathcal{U})$ denote the [nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_of_a_covering) of the covering. The idea of Čech cohomology is that, for an open cover $\mathcal{U}$ consisting of sufficiently small open sets, the resulting [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) $N(\mathcal{U})$ should be a good combinatorial model for the space *X*. For such a cover, the Čech cohomology of *X* is defined to be the [simplicial](wikipedia://en/1060236) [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of the nerve. This idea can be formalized by the notion of a [good cover](wikipedia://en/32160209). However, a more general approach is to take the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of the cohomology groups of the nerve over the system of all possible open covers of *X*, ordered by [refinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover#Refinement). This is the approach adopted below.

## Construction {#Construction}

Let *X* be a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), and let $\mathcal{F}$ be a [presheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presheaf) of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) on *X*. Let $\mathcal{U}$ be an [open cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover) of *X*.

### Simplex {#Simplex}

A *q*-**simplex** σ of $\mathcal{U}$ is an ordered collection of *q*+1 sets chosen from $\mathcal{U}$, such that the intersection of all these sets is non-empty. This intersection is called the *support* of σ and is denoted |σ|.

Now let $\sigma = (U_i)_{i \in \{ 0 , \ldots , q \}}$ be such a *q*-simplex. The *j-th partial boundary* of σ is defined to be the (*q*−1)-simplex obtained by removing the *j*-th set from σ, that is:

$$\partial_j \sigma := (U_i)_{i \in \{ 0 , \ldots , q \} \setminus \{j\}}.$$

The *boundary* of σ is defined as the alternating sum of the partial boundaries:

$$\partial \sigma := \sum_{j=0}^q (-1)^{j+1} \partial_j \sigma$$

viewed as an element of the [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) spanned by the simplices of $\mathcal{U}$.

### Cochain {#Cochain}

A *q*-**cochain** of $\mathcal{U}$ with coefficients in $\mathcal{F}$ is a map which associates with each *q*-simplex σ an element of $\mathcal{F}(|\sigma|)$, and we denote the set of all *q*-cochains of $\mathcal{U}$ with coefficients in $\mathcal{F}$ by $C^q(\mathcal U, \mathcal F)$. $C^q(\mathcal U, \mathcal F)$ is an abelian group by pointwise addition.

### Differential {#Differential}

The cochain groups can be made into a [cochain complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochain_complex) $(C^{\bullet}(\mathcal U, \mathcal F), \delta)$ by defining the **coboundary operator** $\delta_q : C^q(\mathcal U, \mathcal F) \to C^{q+1}(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})$ by:

$$\quad (\delta_q f)(\sigma) := \sum_{j=0}^{q+1} (-1)^j \mathrm{res}^{|\partial_j \sigma|}_{|\sigma|} f (\partial_j \sigma),$$

where $\mathrm{res}^{|\partial_j \sigma|}_{|\sigma|}$ is the [restriction morphism](wikipedia://en/245466) from $\mathcal F(|\partial_j \sigma|)$ to $\mathcal F(|\sigma|).$ (Notice that ∂<sub>j</sub>σ ⊆ σ, but $|$σ$|$ ⊆ $|$∂<sub>j</sub>σ$|$.)

A calculation shows that $\delta_{q+1} \circ \delta_q = 0.$

The coboundary operator is analogous to the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) of [De Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530), so it sometimes called the differential of the cochain complex.

#### Cocycle {#Cocycle}

A *q*-cochain is called a *q*-cocycle if it is in the kernel of $\delta$, hence $Z^q(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}) := \ker ( \delta_q) \subseteq C^q(\mathcal U, \mathcal F)$ is the set of all *q*-cocycles.

Thus a (*q*−1)-cochain $f$ is a cocycle if for all *q*-simplices $\sigma$ the cocycle condition

$$\sum_{j=0}^{q} (-1)^j \mathrm{res}^{|\partial_j \sigma|}_{|\sigma|} f (\partial_j \sigma) = 0$$

holds.

A 0-cocycle $f$ is a collection of local sections of $\mathcal{F}$ satisfying a compatibility relation on every intersecting $A,B\in \mathcal{U}$

$$f(A)|_{A \cap B} = f(B)|_{A \cap B}$$

A 1-cocycle $f$ satisfies for every non-empty $U = A\cap B \cap C$ with $A,B,C \in \mathcal{U}$

$$f(B \cap C)|_U - f(A \cap C)|_U + f(A \cap B)|_U = 0$$

#### Coboundary {#Coboundary}

A *q*-cochain is called a *q*-coboundary if it is in the image of $\delta$ and $B^q(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}) := \mathrm{Im} ( \delta_{q-1}) \subseteq C^{q}(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})$ is the set of all *q*-coboundaries.

For example, a 1-cochain $f$ is a 1-coboundary if there exists a 0-cochain $h$ such that for every intersecting $A,B\in \mathcal{U}$

$$f(A \cap B) = h(A)|_{A \cap B} - h(B)|_{A \cap B}$$

### Cohomology {#Cohomology}

The **Čech cohomology** of $\mathcal{U}$ with values in $\mathcal{F}$ is defined to be the cohomology of the cochain complex $(C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}), \delta)$. Thus the *q*th Čech cohomology is given by

$$\check{H}^q(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}) := H^q((C^{\bullet}(\mathcal U, \mathcal F), \delta)) = Z^q(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}) / B^q(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}).$$

The Čech cohomology of *X* is defined by considering [refinements](wikipedia://en/317552#Refinement) of open covers. If $\mathcal{V}$ is a refinement of $\mathcal{U}$ then there is a map in cohomology $\check{H}^*(\mathcal U,\mathcal F) \to \check{H}^*(\mathcal V,\mathcal F).$ The open covers of *X* form a [directed set](wikipedia://en/9165) under refinement, so the above map leads to a [direct system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_system_%28mathematics%29) of abelian groups. The **Čech cohomology** of *X* with values in *$\mathcal{F}$* is defined as the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) $\check{H}(X,\mathcal F) := \varinjlim_{\mathcal U} \check{H}(\mathcal U,\mathcal F)$ of this system.

The Čech cohomology of *X* with coefficients in a fixed abelian group *A*, denoted $\check{H}(X;A)$, is defined as $\check{H}(X,\mathcal{F}_A)$ where $\mathcal{F}_A$ is the [constant sheaf](wikipedia://en/2783581) on *X* determined by *A*.

A variant of Čech cohomology, called **numerable Čech cohomology**, is defined as above, except that all open covers considered are required to be *numerable*: that is, there is a [partition of unity](wikipedia://en/48636) {ρ<sub>*i*</sub>} such that each support $\{x\mid\rho_i(x)>0\}$ is contained in some element of the cover. If *X* is [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact) and [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637), then numerable Čech cohomology agrees with the usual Čech cohomology.

## Relation to other cohomology theories {#Relation_to_other_cohomology_theories}

If *X* is [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) to a [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022), then the Čech cohomology $\check{H}^{*}(X;A)$ is [naturally isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturally_isomorphic) to the [singular cohomology](wikipedia://en/431041) $H^*(X;A) \,$. If *X* is a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219), then $\check{H}^*(X;\mathbb{R})$ is also naturally isomorphic to the [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530); the article on de Rham cohomology provides a brief review of this isomorphism. For less well-behaved spaces, Čech cohomology differs from singular cohomology. For example if *X* is the [closed topologist's sine curve](wikipedia://en/292224), then $\check{H}^1(X;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z},$ whereas $H^1(X;\mathbb{Z})=0.$

If *X* is a differentiable manifold and the cover $\mathcal{U}$ of *X* is a "good cover" (*i.e.* all the sets *U*<sub>α</sub> are [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414) to a point, and all finite intersections of sets in $\mathcal{U}$ are either empty or contractible to a point), then $\check{H}^{*}(\mathcal U;\mathbb{R})$ is isomorphic to the de Rham cohomology.

If *X* is compact Hausdorff, then Čech cohomology (with coefficients in a [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536)) is isomorphic to [Alexander-Spanier cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander-Spanier_cohomology).

For a presheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on *X*, let $\mathcal{F}^+$ denote its [sheafification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheafification). Then we have a natural comparison map

$$\chi: \check{H}^*(X,\mathcal{F}) \to H^*(X,\mathcal{F}^+)$$

from Čech cohomology to [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357). If *X* is paracompact Hausdorff, then $\chi$ is an isomorphism. More generally, $\chi$ is an isomorphism whenever the Čech cohomology of all presheaves on *X* with zero sheafification vanishes.[^o13]

## In algebraic geometry {#In_algebraic_geometry}

Čech cohomology can be defined more generally for objects in a [site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site_%28mathematics%29) **C** endowed with a topology. This applies, for example, to the Zariski site or the etale site of a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X*. The Čech cohomology with values in some [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) $\mathcal{F}$ is defined as

$$\check H^n (X, \mathcal{F}) := \varinjlim_{\mathcal U} \check H^n(\mathcal U, \mathcal{F}).$$

where the [colimit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit) runs over all coverings (with respect to the chosen topology) of *X*. Here $\check H^n(\mathcal U, \mathcal F)$ is defined as above, except that the *r*-fold intersections of open subsets inside the ambient topological space are replaced by the *r*-fold [fiber product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_product)

$$\mathcal U^{\times^r_X} := \mathcal U \times_X \dots \times_X \mathcal U.$$

As in the classical situation of topological spaces, there is always a map

$$\check H^n(X, \mathcal F) \rightarrow H^n(X, \mathcal F)$$

from Čech cohomology to sheaf cohomology. It is always an isomorphism in degrees *n* = 0 and 1, but may fail to be so in general. For the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) on a [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/2936080) [separated scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separated_scheme), Čech and sheaf cohomology agree for any [quasi-coherent sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-coherent_sheaf). For the [étale topology](wikipedia://en/5707590), the two cohomologies agree for any étale sheaf on *X*, provided that any [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742) of points of *X* are contained in some open affine subscheme. This is satisfied, for example, if *X* is [quasi-projective](wikipedia://en/1321785) over an [affine scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme).[^o15]

The possible difference between Čech cohomology and sheaf cohomology is a motivation for the use of [hypercoverings](wikipedia://en/54077383): these are more general objects than the Čech [nerve](wikipedia://en/5127933)

$$N_X \mathcal U : \dots \to \mathcal U \times_X \mathcal U \times_X \mathcal U \to \mathcal U \times_X \mathcal U \to \mathcal U.$$

A hypercovering *K*<sub>∗</sub> of *X* is a certain [simplicial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_object) in **C**, i.e., a collection of objects *K*<sub>*n*</sub> together with boundary and degeneracy maps. Applying a sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ to *K*<sub>∗</sub> yields a [simplicial abelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_abelian_group) $\mathcal{F}(K_\ast)$ whose *n*-th cohomology group is denoted $H^n(\mathcal F (K_\ast))$. (This group is the same as $\check H^n(\mathcal U, \mathcal F)$ in case *K*<sub>∗</sub> equals $N_X \mathcal U$.) Then, it can be shown that there is a canonical isomorphism

$$H^n (X, \mathcal F) \cong \varinjlim_{K_*} H^n(\mathcal F(K_*)),$$

where the colimit now runs over all hypercoverings.[^o16]

### Examples {#Examples}

The most basic example of Čech cohomology is given by the case where the presheaf $\mathcal{F}$ is a [constant sheaf](wikipedia://en/2783581), e.g. $\mathcal{F}=\mathbb{R}$. In such cases, each $q$-cochain $f$ is simply a function which maps every $q$-simplex to $\mathbb{R}$. For example, we calculate the first Čech cohomology with values in $\mathbb{R}$ of the unit circle $X=S^1$. Dividing $X$ into three arcs and choosing sufficiently small open neighborhoods, we obtain an open cover $\mathcal{U}=\{U_0,U_1,U_2\}$ where $U_i \cap U_j \ne \emptyset$ but $U_0 \cap U_1 \cap U_2 = \emptyset$.

Given any 1-cocycle $f$, $\delta f$ is a 2-cochain which takes inputs of the form $(U_i,U_i,U_i),(U_i,U_i,U_j),(U_j,U_i,U_i),(U_i,U_j,U_i)$ where $i \ne j$ (since $U_0 \cap U_1 \cap U_2 = \emptyset$ and hence $(U_i,U_j,U_k)$ is not a 2-simplex for any permutation $\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}$). The first three inputs give $f(U_i,U_i)=0$; the fourth gives

$$\delta f(U_i,U_j,U_i)=f(U_j,U_i)-f(U_i,U_i)+f(U_i,U_j)=0 \implies f(U_j,U_i)=-f(U_i,U_j).$$

Such a function is fully determined by the values of $f(U_0,U_1),f(U_0,U_2),f(U_1,U_2)$. Thus,

$$Z^1(\mathcal{U},\mathbb{R})=\{f \in C^1(\mathcal{U},\mathbb{R}) : f(U_i,U_i)=0, f(U_j,U_i)=-f(U_i,U_j)\} \cong \mathbb{R}^3.$$

On the other hand, given any 1-coboundary $f = \delta g$, we have

$$\begin{cases} f(U_i,U_i)=g(U_i)-g(U_i)=0 & (i=0,1,2); \\ f(U_i,U_j)=g(U_j)-g(U_i)=-f(U_j,U_i) & (i \ne j) \end{cases}$$

However, upon closer inspection we see that $f(U_0,U_1)+f(U_1,U_2)=f(U_0,U_2)$ and hence each 1-coboundary $f$ is uniquely determined by $f(U_0,U_1)$ and $f(U_1,U_2)$. This gives the set of 1-coboundaries:

$$\begin{aligned} B^1(\mathcal{U},\mathbb{R})=\{f \in C^1(\mathcal{U},\mathbb{R}) : \ & f(U_i,U_i)=0, f(U_j,U_i)=-f(U_i,U_j), \\ &f(U_0,U_2)=f(U_0,U_1)+f(U_1,U_2)\} \cong \mathbb{R}^2. \end{aligned}$$

Therefore, $\check{H}^1(\mathcal{U},\mathbb{R})=Z^1(\mathcal{U},\mathbb{R})/B^1(\mathcal{U},\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{R}$. Since $\mathcal{U}$ is a [good cover](wikipedia://en/32160209) of $X$, we have $\check{H}^1(X,\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{R}$ by [Leray's theorem](wikipedia://en/3017426).

We may also compute the [coherent sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/23535337) of $\Omega^1$ on the projective line $\mathbb{P}^1_\mathbb{C}$ using the Čech complex. Using the cover

$$\mathcal{U} = \{ U_1 = \text{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[y]), U_2 = \text{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[y^{-1}]) \}$$

we have the following modules from the cotangent sheaf

$$\begin{aligned} &\Omega^1(U_1) = \mathbb{C}[y]dy \\ &\Omega^1(U_2) = \mathbb{C} \left [y^{-1} \right ]dy^{-1} \end{aligned}$$

If we take the conventions that $dy^{-1} = -(1/y^2)dy$ then we get the Čech complex

$$0 \to \mathbb{C}[y]dy \oplus \mathbb{C} \left [y^{-1} \right ]dy^{-1} \xrightarrow{d^0} \mathbb{C} \left [y,y^{-1} \right ]dy \to 0$$

Since $d^0$ is injective and the only element not in the image of $d^0$ is $y^{-1}dy$ we get that

$$\begin{aligned} &H^1(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^1,\Omega^1) \cong \mathbb{C} \\ &H^k(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^1,\Omega^1) \cong 0 \text{ for } k \neq 1 \end{aligned}$$

:::
