# Brian Hartley (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brian Hartley"}

**Brian Hartley** (15 May 1939 – 8 October 1994) was a British mathematician specialising in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890).[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Hartley's PhD thesis was completed in 1964 at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) under [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033)'s supervision.[^o6]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Hartley spent a year at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), and another at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) before being appointed as a lecturer at the newly established [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) in 1966, and was promoted to reader in 1973. He moved to a chair at the [School of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Manchester) at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester) in 1977 where he served as head of the Mathematics department between 1982 and 1984.

He published more than 100 papers, mostly on group theory, and collaborated widely with other mathematicians. His main interest was [locally finite groups](wikipedia://en/4939270) where he used his wide knowledge of finite groups to prove properties of [infinite groups](wikipedia://en/4587030) which shared some of the features of finite groups. One recurrent theme appearing in his work was the relationship between the structure of groups and their subgroups consisting of elements fixed by particular [automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphisms).

Hartley is perhaps best known by undergraduates for his book *Rings, Modules and Linear Algebra*, with Trevor Hawkes.[^o9]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Hartley was a keen hill walker, and it was while descending [Helvellyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvellyn) in the English Lake District that he collapsed with a heart attack and died.[^o5]
