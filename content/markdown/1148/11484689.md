# Radical of a Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Radical of a Lie algebra"}

In the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) field of [Lie theory](wikipedia://en/1595681), the **radical** of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak{g}$ is the largest [solvable](wikipedia://en/2966308) [ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_%28Lie_algebra%29) of $\mathfrak{g}.$[^o2]

:::

The radical, denoted by ${\rm rad}(\mathfrak{g})$, fits into the exact sequence

$$0 \to {\rm rad}(\mathfrak{g}) \to \mathfrak g \to \mathfrak{g}/{\rm rad}(\mathfrak{g}) \to 0.$$

where $\mathfrak{g}/{\rm rad}(\mathfrak{g})$ is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1337587). When the ground field has characteristic zero and $\mathfrak g$ has finite dimension, [Levi's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi's_theorem) states that this exact sequence splits; i.e., there exists a (necessarily semisimple) subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$ that is isomorphic to the semisimple quotient $\mathfrak{g}/{\rm rad}(\mathfrak{g})$ via the restriction of the quotient map $\mathfrak g \to \mathfrak{g}/{\rm rad}(\mathfrak{g}).$

A similar notion is a [Borel subalgebra](wikipedia://en/62074925), which is a (not necessarily unique) maximal solvable subalgebra.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $k$ be a field and let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a finite-dimensional [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) over $k$. There exists a unique maximal solvable ideal, called the *radical,* for the following reason.

Firstly let $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{b}$ be two solvable ideals of $\mathfrak{g}$. Then $\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}$ is again an ideal of $\mathfrak{g}$, and it is solvable because it is an [extension](wikipedia://en/345807) of $(\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b})/\mathfrak{a}\simeq\mathfrak{b}/(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b})$ by $\mathfrak{a}$. Now consider the sum of all the solvable ideals of $\mathfrak{g}$. It is nonempty since $\{0\}$ is a solvable ideal, and it is a solvable ideal by the sum property just derived. Clearly it is the unique maximal solvable ideal.

:::

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

- A Lie algebra is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1337587) if and only if its radical is $0$.
- A Lie algebra is [reductive](wikipedia://en/863720) if and only if its radical equals its center.
