# Jacques Vanneste

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacques Vanneste"}

**Jacques Vanneste** is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), whose main research area is [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034).[^o2]

:::

His particular research interest is in analytic methods for handling systems with dynamics on two distinct time or length scales.[^o3] This is relevant, for example, for the interaction between weather and ocean circulation, where fast [inertial waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_waves) can be generated by slow underlying flows; see for example his work[^o4] on the [tropopause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropopause), and his most-cited paper.[^o5] He is also interested in the dynamics of stirring.[^o6]

## Awards {#Awards}

He is the recipient of the 2010 [Adams Prize](wikipedia://en/1098110).[^o8] In 2014, Vanneste was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh).[^o9]
