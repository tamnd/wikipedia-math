# Thomas Goodwillie (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Goodwillie"}

**Thomas G. Goodwillie** (born 1954) is an American mathematician and professor at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) who has made fundamental contributions to algebraic and geometric [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). He is especially famous for developing the concept of the [calculus of functors](wikipedia://en/23694557), often also named **Goodwillie calculus**.

:::

## Life {#Life}

While studying at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), Goodwillie became a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) in 1974 and 1975.[^o4] He then studied at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he completed his PhD in 1982, under the supervision of [Wu-Chung Hsiang](wikipedia://en/50980728). He returned to Harvard as a Junior Fellow in 1979, and was an associate professor (without tenure) at Harvard from 1982 to 1987. In 1987 he was hired with tenure by [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), where he was promoted to full professor in 1991.[^o6]

He developed the [calculus of functors](wikipedia://en/23694557) in a series of three papers in the 1990s and 2000s,[^o7][^o8][^o9] which have since been expanded and applied in a number of areas, including the theory of [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifolds), [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500), and [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831).[^o10]

He has advised 13 PhD students.[^o5]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Goodwillie received a [Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) and the Harriet S. Sheridan Award. He is a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o12]

A conference with leading topologists as speakers was organized on the occasion of his 60th birthday.[^o13]
