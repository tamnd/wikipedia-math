# Morava E-theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Morava E-theory"}

In the theory of [spectra](wikipedia://en/1033697) in mathematics, a **Morava E-theory** (also called **Lubin–Tate theory** or **Johnson–Wilson theory**) is a particular spectrum or equivalently [generalized (co)homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_cohomology_theory). For a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$ (obmitted in notation), these form a sequence $E(n)$ of spectra, hence one for each [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $n\in\mathbb{N}$. Morava E-theories form the foundation of [chromatic homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/42245935), in which a spectrum is separated into so-called (mono)chromatic layers by using its various Bousfield localizations with respect to the different Morava E-theories, so-called chromatic localizations $L_{E(n)}$. Morava E-theory is named after [Jack Morava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Morava), who introduced it in unpublished preprints in the 1970s.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088) $\mathbb{K}$, hence in which every [irreducible polynomial](wikipedia://en/188725) is [separable](wikipedia://en/362400), of characteristic $p$, let $W(\mathbb{K})$ be its ring of [Witt vectors](wikipedia://en/5573710).[^o4] Let $R=W(\mathbb{K})[v_1,\ldots,v_{n-1}]$ be the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) in $n-1$ variables, called Lubin–Tate ring. According to [Landweber's exact functor theorem](wikipedia://en/28686098), a [formal group law](wikipedia://en/480335) over the Lubin–Tate ring defines a [generalized cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_cohomology_theory), which according to [Brown's representability theorem](wikipedia://en/1073746) is represented by a spectrum, which is Morava E-theory $E(n)$. It fulfills:

$$\pi_0E(n) =R[\beta^{\pm 1}]$$

for its ring of coefficients with $\beta\in R$ in second degree.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The chromatic localization $L_{E(n)}$ preserves [smash products](wikipedia://en/698010).[^o7]
- $E(n)$ is Bousfield equivalent to $E(n-1)\times K(n)$.[^o8] ($K(n)$ is [Morava K-theory](wikipedia://en/1033664). Two spectra are Bousfield equivalent, when their [generalized homology theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_homology_theory) vanish on the exact same [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450). Hence for every topological space $X$, one has $E(n)_*(X)=0$ if and only if $E(n-1)_*(X)=0$ and $K(n)_*(X)=0$.)
- $E(n)$ is $K(n)$-local.[^o9] ($K(n)$ is [Morava K-theory](wikipedia://en/1033664). $K(n)$-local means that every spectrum morphism $X\rightarrow E(n)$ from a $K(n)$-acyclic spectrum $X$ is [nullhomotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullhomotopic). $K(n)$-acyclic means that $K(n)\wedge X\simeq 0$.)
- A connected [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) [spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_manifold) 10-manifold is generalized oriented in second Morava E-theory $E(2)$ (for $p=2$) if and only if its seventh integral [Stiefel–Whitney class](wikipedia://en/877761) $W_7$ vanishes.

:::

## E(n)-local spheres {#E(n)-local_spheres}

For the [sphere spectrum](wikipedia://en/21118593) $\mathbb{S}$ (with $\mathbb{S}_n=S^n$), its chromatic localizations $L_{E(n)}\mathbb{S}$ are also called $E(n)$-local spheres.
