# Heap (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Heap"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **semiheap** is an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) consisting of a [non-empty](wikipedia://en/9566) [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $H$ with a [ternary operation](wikipedia://en/459413) denoted $[x,y,z] \in H$ that satisfies a modified associativity property:[^o2]

$$\forall a,b,c,d,e \in H \quad [[a,b,c],d,e] = [a,[d,c,b],e] = [a,b,[c,d,e]].$$

:::

A **biunitary element** $h$ of a semiheap satisfies $[h,h,k] = k = [k,h,h]$ for every $k$ in $H$.[^o2]

A **heap** is a semiheap in which every element is biunitary.[^o2] It can be thought of as a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) with the identity element "forgotten".

The term *heap* is derived from груда, Russian for heap or pile. [Anton Sushkevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Sushkevich) used the term in his *Theory of Generalized Groups* (1937) which influenced [Viktor Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Wagner), promulgator of semiheaps, heaps, and generalized heaps.[^o2] Груда contrasts with группа ([group](wikipedia://en/19447)) which was taken into Russian by transliteration. Indeed, a heap has been called a **groud** in English text.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Two element heap {#Two_element_heap}

Turn $H=\{a,b\}$ into the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) $\mathrm{C}_2$, by defining $a$ the identity element, and $bb = a$. Then it produces the following heap:

$$[a,a,a]=a,\, [a,a,b]=b,\, [b,a,a]=b,\, [b,a,b]=a,$$

$$[a,b,a]=b,\, [a,b,b]=a,\, [b,b,a]=a,\, [b,b,b]=b.$$

Defining $b$ as the identity element and $aa = b$ would have given the same heap.

### Heap of integers {#Heap_of_integers}

If $x,y,z$ are integers, we can set $[x,y,z] = x-y+z$ to produce a heap. We can then choose any [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $k$ to be the identity of a new group on the set of integers, with the operation $*$

$$x*y = x+y-k$$

and inverse

$$x^{-1} = 2k-x.$$

### Heap of a group {#Heap_of_a_group}

The previous two examples may be generalized to any group $G$ by defining the ternary operation as $[x,y,z] = xy^{-1}z,$ using the multiplication and inverse of $G$.

### Heap of a groupoid with two objects {#Heap_of_a_groupoid_with_two_objects}

The heap of a group may be generalized again to the case of a [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) which has two [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) $A$ and $B$ when viewed as a [category](wikipedia://en/53759). The elements of the heap may be identified with the [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) from A to B, such that three morphisms $x$, $y$, $z$ define a heap operation according to $[x,y,z] = xy^{-1}z.$

This reduces to the heap of a group if a particular morphism between the two objects is chosen as the identity. This intuitively relates the description of isomorphisms between two objects as a heap and the description of isomorphisms between multiple objects as a groupoid.

### Heterogeneous relations {#Heterogeneous_relations}

Let $A$ and $B$ be different sets and $\mathcal{B}(A,B)$ the collection of [heterogeneous relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterogeneous_relation) between them. For $p, q, r \in \mathcal{B}(A,B)$ define the ternary operator $[p, q, r] = p q^\mathrm{T} r$ where $q^{T}$ is the [converse relation](wikipedia://en/3941020) of $q$. The result of this composition is also in $\mathcal{B}(A,B)$ so a mathematical structure has been formed by the ternary operation.[^o11] [Viktor Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Wagner) was motivated to form this heap by his study of transition maps in an [atlas](wikipedia://en/47436) which are [partial functions](wikipedia://en/23577).[^o12] Thus a heap is more than a tweak of a group: it is a general concept including a group as a trivial case.

## Theorems {#Theorems}

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

A semiheap with a biunitary element $e$ may be considered an [involuted semigroup](wikipedia://en/22679231) with operation given by $ab = [a, e, b]$ and involution by $a^{-1} = [e, a, e]$.[^o2]

:::

When the above construction is applied to a heap, the result is in fact a group.[^o2] Note that the identity $e$ of the group can be chosen to be any element of the heap.

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

Every semiheap may be embedded in an involuted semigroup.[^o2]

:::

As in the study of [semigroups](wikipedia://en/27799), the structure of semiheaps is described in terms of [ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_ideal) with an "i-simple semiheap" being one with no proper ideals. Mustafaeva translated the [Green's relations](wikipedia://en/1192012) of semigroup theory to semiheaps and defined a ρ class to be those elements generating the same principle two-sided ideal. He then proved that no i-simple semiheap can have more than two ρ classes.[^o17]

He also described regularity classes of a semiheap $S$:

$$D(m,n) = \{a \mid \exists x \in S : a = a^n x a^m \}$$

where $n$ and $m$ have the same [parity](wikipedia://en/143135) and the ternary operation of the semiheap applies at the left of a string from $S$. He proves that $S$ can have at most 5 regularity classes. Mustafaev calls an ideal $B$ "isolated" when $a^n \in B \implies a \in B .$ He then proves that when $S = D(2,2)$, then every ideal is isolated and conversely.[^o18]

Studying the semiheap $Z(A, B)$ of [heterogeneous relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterogeneous_relation) between sets $A$ and $B$, in 1974 K. A. Zareckii followed Mustafaev's lead to describe ideal equivalence, regularity classes, and ideal factors of a semiheap.[^o19]

## Generalizations and related concepts {#Generalizations_and_related_concepts}

- A **pseudoheap** (or **pseudogroud**) satisfies the partial para-associative condition[^o22] $$[[a,b,c],d,e] = [a,b,[c,d,e]] .$$
- A **Malcev operation** satisfies the identity law but not necessarily the para-associative law,[^o23] that is, a [ternary operation](wikipedia://en/459413) $f$ on a set $X$ satisfying the identity $f(x, x, y) = f(y, x, x) = y$. (See [Congruence-permutable algebra](wikipedia://en/45325150).)
- A **semiheap** (or **semigroud**) is required to satisfy only the para-associative law but need not obey the identity law.[^o24]An example of a semiheap that is not in general a heap is given by $M$ a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) of fixed size with $$[x,y,z] = x \cdot y^\mathrm{T} \cdot z$$ where • denotes [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280) and T denotes [matrix transpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_transpose).[^o24]
- An **idempotent semiheap** is a semiheap where $[a,a,a] = a$ for all $a$.
- A **generalised heap** (or **generalised groud**) is an idempotent semiheap where $$[a,a,[b,b,x]] = [b,b,[a,a,x]]$$ and $$[[x,a,a],b,b] = [[x,b,b],a,a]$$ for all $a$ and $b$.In a semiheap, the relation → defined by $$a \rightarrow b \Leftrightarrow [a,b,a] = a$$ is [transitive](wikipedia://en/200463). A semiheap is a generalized heap if and only if → is an [order relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_relation), that is, is also [reflexive](wikipedia://en/200458) (the semiheap is idempotent) and [antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/1176).[^o25]
