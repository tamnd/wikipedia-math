# Consani–Scholten quintic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Consani–Scholten quintic"}

*Image: Consani-Scholten.png.* A slice of the Consani–Scholten quintic

In the mathematical fields of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), the **Consani–Scholten quintic** is an [algebraic](wikipedia://en/248808) [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) (the set of solutions to a single [polynomial equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_equation) in multiple variables) studied in 2001 by [Caterina Consani](wikipedia://en/58845863) and Jasper Scholten. It has been used as a test case for the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Consani and Scholten define their hypersurface from the ([projectivized](wikipedia://en/21861125)) set of solutions to the equation

$$P(x,y)=P(z,w)$$

in four complex variables, where

$$P(x,y)=x^5+y^5-(5xy-5)(x^2+y^2-x-y).$$

In this form the resulting hypersurface is [singular](wikipedia://en/54240): it has 120 [double points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_point). Its [Hodge diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_diamond) is 1

The Consani–Scholton quintic itself is the non-singular hypersurface obtained by [blowing up](wikipedia://en/1817790) these singularities. As a non-singular [quintic threefold](wikipedia://en/23562429), it is a [Calabi–Yau manifold](wikipedia://en/188932).

:::

## Modularity {#Modularity}

According to the Langlands program, for any Calabi–Yau threefold $X$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, the [Galois representations](wikipedia://en/486929) giving the action of the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) on the [$\ell$-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) $H^3(X_{\bar\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})$ (for [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) $\ell$ of [good reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_reduction), which for this curve means any prime other than 2, 3, or 5) should have the same [L-series](wikipedia://en/245283) as an [automorphic form](wikipedia://en/782146). This was known for "rigid" Calabi–Yau threefolds, for which the family of Galois representations has dimension two, by the proof of [Serre's modularity conjecture](wikipedia://en/2908546). The Consani–Scholton quintic provides a non-rigid example, where the dimension is four. Consani and Scholten constructed a [Hilbert modular form](wikipedia://en/2333880) and conjectured that its L-series agreed with the Galois representations for their curve; this was proven by (Dieulefait Pacetti Schütt 2012).
