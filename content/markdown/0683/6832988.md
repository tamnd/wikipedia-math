# Wiener–Ikehara theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wiener–Ikehara theorem"}

The **Wiener–Ikehara theorem** is a [Tauberian theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauberian_theorem), originally published by [Shikao Ikehara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikao_Ikehara), a student of [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185)'s, in 1931. It is a special case of [Wiener's Tauberian theorems](wikipedia://en/4106793), which were published by Wiener one year later. It can be used to prove the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) (Chandrasekharan, 1969), under the assumption that the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) has no zeros on the line of real part one.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *A*(*x*) be a non-negative, [monotonic](wikipedia://en/48260) nondecreasing function of *x*, defined for 0 ≤ *x* \< ∞. Suppose that

$$f(s)=\int_0^\infty A(x) e^{-xs}\,dx$$

converges for ℜ(*s*) > 1 to the function *ƒ*(*s*) and that, for some non-negative number *c*,

$$f(s) - \frac{c}{s-1}$$

has an extension as a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) for ℜ(*s*) ≥ 1. Then the [limit](wikipedia://en/285759) as *x* goes to infinity of *e*<sup>−*x*</sup> *A*(*x*) is equal to c.

:::

## One Particular Application {#One_Particular_Application}

An important number-theoretic application of the theorem is to [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) of the form

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty a(n) n^{-s}$$

where *a*(*n*) is non-negative. If the series converges to an analytic function in

$$\Re(s) \ge b$$

with a simple pole of residue *c* at *s* = *b*, then

$$\sum_{n\le X}a(n) \sim \frac{c}{b} X^b.$$

Applying this to the logarithmic derivative of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809), where the coefficients in the Dirichlet series are values of the [von Mangoldt function](wikipedia://en/1928862), it is possible to deduce the [Prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) from the fact that the zeta function has no zeroes on the line

$$\Re(s)=1.$$
