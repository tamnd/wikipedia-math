# Demihypercube

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Demihypercube"}

*Image: CubeAndStel.svg.* [Alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) of the *n*-cube yields one of two *n*-demicubes, as in this 3-dimensional illustration of the two [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) that arise as the 3-demicubes of the 3-cube.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **demihypercubes** (also called *n-demicubes*, *n-hemicubes*, and *half measure polytopes*) are a class of *n*-[polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytopes) constructed from [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) of an *n*-[hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783), labeled as *hγ<sub>n</sub>* for being *half* of the hypercube family, *γ<sub>n</sub>*. Half of the vertices are deleted and new facets are formed. The 2*n* facets become 2*n* **(*n* − 1)-demicubes**, and 2<sup>*n - 1*</sup> **(*n* − 1)-simplex** facets are formed in place of the deleted vertices.[^o2]

:::

They have been named with a *demi-* prefix to each [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) name: demi[cube](wikipedia://en/6285), demi[tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112), etc. The demicube is identical to the [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521), and the demitesseract is identical to the regular [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422). The [demipenteract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demipenteract) is considered *semiregular* for having only regular facets. Higher forms do not have all regular facets but are all [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392).

The [vertex-edge graph](wikipedia://en/74718534) of the demihypercube is the [halved cube graph](wikipedia://en/39329236).

An *n*-demicube has [inversion symmetry](wikipedia://en/12487536) if *n* is [even](wikipedia://en/143135).

## Discovery {#Discovery}

[Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) described the demipenteract in his 1900 publication listing all of the regular and semiregular figures in *n*-dimensions above three. He called it a *5-ic semi-regular*. It also exists within the [semiregular *k*<sub>21</sub> polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiregular_k_21_polytope) family.

The demihypercubes can be represented by extended [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394) of the form h{4,3,...,3} as half the vertices of {4,3,...,3}. The [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719) of demihypercubes are [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) *n*-[simplexes](wikipedia://en/39781).

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Constructions"}

## Constructions {#Constructions}

They are represented by [Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) of three constructive forms:

1. ... (As an [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) [orthotope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthotope)) s{2<sup>1,1,...,1</sup>}
1. ... (As an alternated [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783)) h{4,3<sup>*n*−1</sup>}
1. .... (As a demihypercube) {3<sup>1,*n*−3,1</sup>}

[H.S.M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) also labeled the third bifurcating diagrams as **1<sub>*k*1</sub>** representing the lengths of the three branches and led by the ringed branch.

An *n-demicube*, *n* greater than 2, has *n*(*n* − 1)/2 edges meeting at each vertex. The graphs below show less edges at each vertex due to overlapping edges in the symmetry projection.

In general, a demicube's elements can be determined from the original *n*-cube: (with C<sub>*n*,*m*</sub> = *m<sup>th</sup>*-face count in [*n*-cube](wikipedia://en/39783) = 2<sup>*n*−*m*</sup> *n*!/(*m*!(*n* − *m*)!))

- **Vertices:** D<sub>*n*,0</sub> = 1/2 C<sub>*n*,0</sub> = 2<sup>*n*−1</sup> (Half the *n*-cube vertices remain)
- **Edges:** D<sub>*n*,1</sub> = C<sub>*n*,2</sub> = 1/2 *n*(*n* – 1) 2<sup>*n*−2</sup> (All original edges lost, each square faces create a new edge)
- **Faces:** D<sub>*n*,2</sub> = 4 \* C<sub>*n*,3</sub> = 2/3 *n*(*n* − 1)(*n* − 2) 2<sup>*n*−3</sup> (All original faces lost, each cube creates 4 new triangular faces)
- **Cells:** D<sub>*n*,3</sub> = C<sub>*n*,3</sub> + 2<sup>3</sup> C<sub>*n*,4</sub> (tetrahedra from original cells plus new ones)
- **Hypercells:** D<sub>*n*,4</sub> = C<sub>*n*,4</sub> + 2<sup>4</sup> C<sub>*n*,5</sub> (16-cells and 5-cells respectively)
- ...
- \[For *m* = 3, ... , *n* − 1\]: D<sub>*n*,*m*</sub> = C<sub>*n*,*m*</sub> + 2<sup>*m*</sup> C<sub>*n*,*m*+1</sub> (*m*-demicubes and *m*-simplexes respectively)
- ...
- **Facets:** D<sub>*n*,*n*−1</sub> = 2*n* + 2<sup>*n*−1</sup> ((*n* − 1)-demicubes and (*n* − 1)-simplices respectively)

:::

## Symmetry group {#Symmetry_group}

The stabilizer of the demihypercube in the [hyperoctahedral group](wikipedia://en/24268961) (the [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) $BC_n$ \[4,3<sup>*n*−1</sup>\]) has index 2. It is the Coxeter group $D_n,$ \[3<sup>*n*−3,1,1</sup>\] of order $2^{n-1}n!$, and is generated by permutations of the coordinate axes and reflections along *pairs* of coordinate axes.[^o18]

## Orthotopic constructions {#Orthotopic_constructions}

*Image: Rhombic disphenoid.png.* The rhombic disphenoid inside of a [cuboid](wikipedia://en/156802)

Constructions as alternated [orthotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthotope) have the same topology, but can be stretched with different lengths in *n*-axes of symmetry.

The [rhombic disphenoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombic_disphenoid) is the three-dimensional example as alternated cuboid. It has three sets of edge lengths, and [scalene triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalene_triangle) faces.
