# Tribonacci ratio

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tribonacci ratio"}

In mathematics, the **tribonacci ratio** is a geometrical [proportion](wikipedia://en/1021), given by the unique real [solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_root) of the equation $x3 = x2 + x + 1$. Its decimal expansion begins with $1.839286755214161$ .

:::

The moniker *tribonacci* was introduced by high-school student Mark Feinberg in an article published in the *[Fibonacci Quarterly](wikipedia://en/12227896)* of october 1963.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Tribonacci cuboid.svg.* $\tau = \tfrac{a+b+c}{a} = \tfrac{a}{b} = \tfrac{b}{c}$. With $b = 1$ the boxes have volumes $\tau3 = \tau2 (\operatorname{red}) + \tau (\operatorname{green}) + 1 (\operatorname{blue}).$

Three quantities $a > b > c > 0$ are in the tribonacci ratio if

$$\frac{a+b+c}{a} =\frac{a}{b} =\frac{b}{c} =\tau$$

This ratio is commonly denoted $\tau.$

Substituting $b =\tau c$ and $a =\tau b =\tau^2 c$ in the first fraction gives

$$\tau =\frac{c(\tau^2 +\tau +1)}{\tau^2 c}.$$

It follows that the tribonacci ratio is the unique real solution of the [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787) $\tau^3 =\tau^2 +\tau +1$.

Closed-form expressions for $\tau$ are found by solving the [depressed cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depressed_cubic) $y^3 -\tfrac43 y -\tfrac{38}{27}$, which has real zero $\tau -\tfrac13$.

$$\begin{aligned} w_{1,2} &=19 \pm 3\sqrt{33} \\ \tau &=\left(1 +\sqrt[3]{w_1} +\sqrt[3]{w_2} \right) /3 \\ &= \frac13 +\frac43 \cosh \left( \frac13 \operatorname{arcosh} \left( \frac{19}{8} \right) \right). \end{aligned}$$

Dividing the defining polynomial $z^3 -z^2 -z -1$ by $z -\tau$ one obtains $z^2 +(\tau -1)z +1 /\tau$, and the [conjugate elements](wikipedia://en/458467) of $\tau$ are

$$z_{1,2} = \left( 1 -\tau \pm i \sqrt{(\tau +1)(3\tau -5)} \right) /2 ,$$

with $z_1 +z_2 =1 -\tau$ and $z_1 z_2 =1 /\tau$.

*Image: Tribonacci\_definition.svg.* Unit circle and hyperbola $(x + 1) y = 1$.

The point of intersection $P =(x,y)$ with $x \ne 0$ of [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) $x^2 +y^2 =1$ and [rectangular hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052#Rectangular_hyperbola) $y =\frac{1}{x+1}$ is $(\tau -1,\tau^{-1}).$ Substituting for $y$, the equation of the [abscissa](wikipedia://en/60487) is $\, x^3 +2x^2 -2 =0 \,$ or $\, x^2 (x +2) =2.$

Dividing both sides by $\, x+2$ results in the [fixed point](wikipedia://en/7344320) iteration $\, x_{n+1} \gets \sqrt{ \frac{2}{2 +x_n} } \,,$ and the continued reciprocal square root

$$\tau -1 =\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{2+\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{2+\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{2 +\ddots}}}}}}$$

An image of the Julia set of the backward iteration $\, z_{n-1} \gets \frac{2}{z^2_n} -2 \,$ in the complex plane is found below.

Alternative expressions for the tribonacci ratio are derived from the [reciprocal equation](wikipedia://en/1106042), that is, the depressed cubic $\, 2x^3 -2x -1 \,$ with real zero $\frac{1}{\tau-1}$ and [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) $-44$.[^o8]

$$\begin{aligned} w_{1,2} &=\left( 1 \pm \frac13 \sqrt{ \frac{11}{3}} \right) /4 \\ \tau &=1 +(\sqrt[3]{w_1} +\sqrt[3]{w_2})^{-1} \\ &= 1 +\frac{\sqrt3}{2} \operatorname{sech}\left( \frac13 \operatorname{arcosh} \left( \frac{3\sqrt3}{4} \right) \right). \end{aligned}$$

Additionally, $\, 2x^3 =2x +1 \,$ leads to the [infinite radical](wikipedia://en/2503693#Infinitely_nested_radicals)

$$\frac{1}{\tau-1} =\sqrt[3]{\tfrac12 +\sqrt[3]{\tfrac12 +\sqrt[3]{\tfrac12 +\cdots}}}$$

$\frac{1}{\tau-1}$ is the [superstable](wikipedia://en/5493795#Stability_of_fixed_points) fixed point of the [Newton iteration](wikipedia://en/22145) $x \gets (2x^3 +\tfrac12) /(3x^2 -1)$.

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: TribonacciSquare\_6.svg.* Rectangles with aspect ratios $\tfrac{1}{\tau-1}, \tau, \tfrac{\tau}{\tau-1}$ tile the square.

The tribonacci ratio can be written in terms of itself as fractions

$$\begin{aligned} \tau &=\frac{\tau^2 +1}{\tau^2 -1} \\ \tau^2 &=\frac{\tau +1}{\tau -1} \\ \tau^3 &=\frac{\tau^4 +1}{2} \\ \tau^4 &=\frac{\,(\tau +1)^3}{2}.\end{aligned}$$

Similarly as the infinite [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630)

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\tau^2 +1}{2} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \tau^{-n} \\ \frac{\tau +1}{2} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \tau^{-2n} \\ \frac{1}{\tau -1} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \tau^{-3n} \\ \frac{\tau}{2(\tau -1)} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \tau^{-4n}.\end{aligned}$$

For every integer $n$ one has

$$\begin{aligned} \tau^n &=\tau^{n-1} +\tau^{n-2} +\tau^{n-3} \\ &=2\tau^{n-2} +2\tau^{n-3} +\tau^{n-4} \\ &=3\tau^{n-2} +\tau^{n-4} +\tau^{n-6} \end{aligned}$$

from this an infinite number of further relations can be found. A notable example is $\tau +\tau^{-3} =2$.

[Continued fraction](wikipedia://en/46802) pattern of a few low powers [^o11]

$$\begin{aligned} \tau^{-1} &=[0;1,1,5,4,2,305,1,8,2,...] \approx 0.5437 \;(\tfrac{31}{57}) \\ \tau^0 &=[1] \\ \tau^1 &=[1;1,5,4,2,305,1,8,2,1,...] \approx 1.8393 \;(\tfrac{103}{56}) \\ \tau^2 &=[3;2,1,1,1,1,2,1,152,2,...] \approx 3.3830 \;(\tfrac{159}{47}) \\ \tau^3 &=[6;4,2,305,1,8,2,1,4,6,...] \approx 6.2223 \;(\tfrac{56}{9}) \\ \tau^4 &=[11;2,4,152,1,17,1,2,2,...] \approx 11.4445 \;(\tfrac{103}{9}) \end{aligned}$$

The tribonacci ratio is the fourth smallest cubic [Pisot number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot_number).[^o12] By definition of these numbers, the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991#Complex_numbers) $1 /\sqrt{\tau}$ of the algebraic conjugates is smaller than 1, thus powers of $\tau$ generate [almost integers](wikipedia://en/14041933). For example: $\tau^{18} =58034.99919...$ $\approx 58035 -1/1234$. After 18 rotation steps the [phases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_%28waves%29) of the inward spiraling conjugate pair – initially close to $\pm 9\pi /13$ – nearly align with the imaginary axis.

The first implied mention of the tribonacci constant was in the eleventh century, when the Persian poet and [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath) [Omar Khayyam](wikipedia://en/92550) found the solution $10\tfrac{\tau+1}{\tau}$ of the cubic $x^3 +200x =20x^2 +2000$ by considering the intersection of a circle and a rectangular hyperbola.[^o13]

$W_{11}(x) = x^3 -2x^2 +2x -2,$ with real zero $\omega =\tfrac{\tau+1}{\tau} =\tau(\tau -1),$ is the [Weber class polynomial](wikipedia://en/33559472) associated with discriminant $\Delta=-11$. Properties of the related [Klein j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988#The_q-expansion_and_moonshine) result in near-identity $\omega \approx (e^{\pi \sqrt{-\Delta}} +24)^{1/24}.$

[Benne de Weger's example](https://math.deweger.net/reports/%5b1999%5dBroberg.txt) for the algebraic [abc conjecture](wikipedia://en/174108) derives from the [Binet-type expression](wikipedia://en/10918#Binet's_formula) for the zero element at index $52$ of the Berstel sequence. The [companion polynomial](wikipedia://en/50700194#Definitions) to the recurrence relation is $x^3 -2x^2 +4x -4$, with real zero $\alpha =2\tfrac{\tau}{\tau +1}$.

*Image: TRIBONACCI.jpg.* Construction of the tribonacci ratio with compass and marked ruler. $\operatorname{BC} = \tau - 1$ and $\operatorname{BD} = \tfrac{1}{\tau}$.

Argument $\theta =\arccos(\tfrac12 \tau) \,$ satisfies $\, 4\sin(3\theta) -\tan(\theta) =\sqrt{11}$, a result which is related through distance parameter $\, z =\tau(1 -\tau) \cdot 2\cos(\tfrac{2\pi}{11})$ to the 'miraculous' [neusis construction](wikipedia://en/3752339) of the [hendecagon](wikipedia://en/614840), found by Benjamin and Snyder.[^o15][^o16]

The reciprocal $\tfrac{1}{\tau}$ of the tribonacci ratio solves the equation $\, 2\arctan(x) =\arccos(x)$.[^o17] The angle is close to $1$ [radian](wikipedia://en/26003). Its [complement](wikipedia://en/1196#Complementary_angle) $\, \arccos(\tau-1) =\arcsin(\tfrac{1}{\tau}) \,$ figures in the geometric construction of the tribonacci constant found by biologist Xerardo Neira.[^o18]

The tribonacci ratio is particularly important in the study of the [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249): all its metrics can be expressed in terms of $\tau$.

## Tribonacci sequence {#Tribonacci_sequence}

The first muddled mention of the tribonacci sequence is in [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin)'s *[On the Origin of Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Origin_of_Species)* (1859), illustrating the population growth of elephants on the supposition that during their lifetime each pair of parents produces three pair of young.[^o21]

The number of [compositions](wikipedia://en/550741) of $n - 2$ into parts 1, 2 and 3 is counted by the *n*th tribonacci number ($n > 1$).

The tribonacci sequence is defined by the third-order [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)

$$T_n =T_{n-1} +T_{n-2} +T_{n-3} \text{ for } n >2,$$

with initial values

$$T_0 =T_1 =0, T_2 =1.$$

The first few terms are 0, 0, 1, 1, 2, 4, 7, 13, 24, 44, 81, 149, 274, 504, 927,... . The limit ratio between consecutive terms is the tribonacci constant:$\lim_{n\rightarrow \infty} T_{n+1}/T_n =\tau.$ An alternative relation for $n >3$ is given by $T_n =2T_{n-1} -T_{n-4}.$

The sequence is extended to negative indices using

$$T_n =T_{n+3} -T_{n+2} -T_{n+1},$$

obtaining (0), 1,−1, 0, 2,−3, 1, 4,−8, 5, 7,−20, 18, 9,−47,... .

The relationship between the negative and positive indexed segments of the sequence is given by

$$T_{1-n} =T_n^2 - T_{n+1} T_{n-1}.$$

This [reflection formula](wikipedia://en/4025531) holds for all integers $n$ and can be derived from Agronomof's identity [^o22][^o23]

$$T_{n+k} = T_{k+1} T_{n+1} + \left(T_k +T_{k-1} \right) T_n + T_k T_{n-1}.$$

The sequence is related to sums of [binomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/4668) by

$$T_{n+2} =\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor} \sum_{j=0}^{i} {i \choose j}{n-i-j \choose i}$$

*Image: Tribonacci\_Bells.jpg.* Julia set (in black) of the map $z \leftarrow \tfrac{2}{z^{2}} - 2$ with a single attracting, real five-point limit cycle. Viewport width $[-3,3]$, pole $z_{p} = 0$. The tiny white circle is centred at repelling fixed point $z_{f} = \tau - 1$.

Powers of the tribonacci ratio can be written with tribonacci numbers as quadratic coefficients

$$\tau^n =\tau^2 T_{n} +\tau (T_{n-1} +T_{n-2}) +T_{n-1},$$

which is proved by [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881) on $n.$ This relation also holds for $n < 0.$ The order of the coefficients corresponds to the bottom row of matrix $Q$ below.

The [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) of the tribonacci sequence for non-negative $n$ is given by

$$\frac{x^2}{1 -x -x^2 -x^3} =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} T_n x^n \text{ for } x <\tfrac{1}{\tau}$$

Let $\tau$ and [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/458467) pair $\beta$ and $\gamma$ be the zeros of polynomial $f:x^3 -x^2 -x -1$ with [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) $-44$, the tribonacci numbers are then given by the [Binet formula](wikipedia://en/10918#Binet's_formula)

$$T_{n+1} =a \tau^n +b \beta^n +c \gamma^n ,$$

with real $a$ and conjugates $b$ and $c$ the roots of $44y^3 -2y -1 =0.$

Since $\left\vert b \beta^n +c \gamma^n \right\vert < \tfrac25$, the number $T_n$ is the [nearest integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nearest_integer_function) to $\,a\,\tau^{n-1}$, with $n > 0$ and coefficient $a =\tau^2 /(\tau^3 +\tau +2) =$ 0.33622

The tribonacci numbers are obtained as integral powers $n \ge 2$ of [companion matrix](wikipedia://en/532542) $Q$ to [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) $f$ with dominant [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429#Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors_of_matrices) $\tau$

$$Q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

By the [Cayley–Hamilton theorem](wikipedia://en/173547),

$$Q^n = \begin{pmatrix} T_{n+2} & T_{n+1} +T_{n} & T_{n+1} \\ T_{n+1} & T_{n} +T_{n-1} & T_{n} \\ T_{n} & T_{n-1} +T_{n-2} & T_{n-1} \end{pmatrix}$$

The [trace](wikipedia://en/43270#Relationship_to_eigenvalues) of $Q^n$ gives the tribonacci-Lucas numbers 3, 1, 3, 7, 11, 21, 39, 71, 131, 241, 443, 815, 1499, 2757,... satisfying the same recurrence relation. Variously, $L_n =\lfloor \tau^n \rceil \text{ for } n >3.$

These Lucas numbers have the [Fermat property](wikipedia://en/53453): if p is prime, $L_{p} \equiv L_1 \bmod p .$ The converse does not hold, but the small number of tribonacci [pseudoprimes](wikipedia://en/28787420) $\,n \mid (L_n -1)$ makes the sequence special. The only composite numbers below $10^{7}$ to pass the test are n = 182, 25201, 233${}^{2}$, 63618, 194390, 750890, 804055, 1889041, 2487941, 3542533, 3761251, 6829689.

The tribonacci word sequence on the alphabet $\{a,b,c\}$ is defined by the [substitution rule](wikipedia://en/2452154)

$$\begin{cases} a \;\mapsto \;ab \\ b \;\mapsto \;ac \\ c \;\mapsto \;a \end{cases}$$

With initiator $w_0=c$, the first seven words are

$$c,a,ab,ab.ac,abac.aba,abacaba.abacab,abacabaabacab.abacabaabac$$

For $n \ge 3$, the series of words $w_n$ produced by [iterating](wikipedia://en/68833) the substitution are similarly obtained by [concatenating](wikipedia://en/64474) the previous three: $w_n =w_{n-1} +w_{n-2} +w_{n-3}.$ The number of $c, b\text{ and } a's$ in each word is equal to successive tribonacci numbers and the word length $l(w_n) =T_{n+2}$. The tribonacci word sequence is the basis of the construction of the classic [Rauzy fractal](wikipedia://en/29344658).[^o26]

The [four-number game](wikipedia://en/10129659) played with sets $S_0$ of successive tribonacci numbers $\{ T_{n-3},T_{n-2},T_{n-1},T_n \}$, and [circular shifts](wikipedia://en/449166) or reflections thereof, results in subsequent maximum run lengths $l(S_0) =3\lfloor \tfrac{n-1}{2} \rfloor,$ $n \ge 3$. A game of infinite length is obtained by taking real elements $S_0 =\{ 1,\tau,\tau^2,\tau^3 \},$ given to unlimited precision.[^o27]

## Tribonacci spiral {#Tribonacci_spiral}

*Image: Tribonacci\_spiral.svg.* Tribonacci spirals with different initial radii on a $\tau$− rectangle.

A tribonacci spiral is a [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373) that gets wider by a factor of $\tau$ for every quarter turn. It is described by the [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation) $r( \theta) =a \exp(k \theta),$ with initial radius $a$ and parameter $k =\frac{2}{\pi} \ln( \tau).$

If drawn on a tribonacci rectangle, the spiral has its pole at the foot of altitude of a triangle on the diagonal and passes through vertices of rectangles with aspect ratio $\tau(\tau -1)$ which are perpendicularly aligned and successively scaled by a factor $\tau^{-1}$. The pole of the spiral divides the main diagonal in ratio $\tau^2$ and [bisects](wikipedia://en/152547) the diagonal of the lower right rectangle.
