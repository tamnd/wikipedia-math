# René Thom

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="René Thom"}

**René Frédéric Thom** (; 2 September 1923 – 25 October 2002) was a French mathematician, who received the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1958.

:::

He made his reputation as a [topologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologist), moving on to aspects of what would be called [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763); he became world-famous among the wider academic community and the educated general public for one aspect of this latter interest, his work as the founder of [catastrophe theory](wikipedia://en/434288) (later developed by [Christopher Zeeman](wikipedia://en/871415)).[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

René Thom grew up in a modest family in [Montbéliard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montbéliard), Doubs and obtained a [Baccalauréat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baccalauréat) in 1940. After the [German invasion of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_France_%281940%29), his family took refuge in Switzerland and then in [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon). In 1941 he moved to Paris to attend [Lycée Saint-Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Saint-Louis) and in 1943 he began studying mathematics at [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure), becoming [agrégé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégé) in 1946.[^o9]

He received his PhD in 1951 from the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). His thesis, titled *Espaces fibrés en sphères et carrés de Steenrod* (*Sphere bundles and Steenrod squares*), was written under the direction of [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536).[^o10]

After a fellowship at [Princeton University Graduate College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University_Graduate_College) (1951–1952), he became [Maître de conférences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maître_de_conférences) at the Universities of [Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Joseph_Fourier) (1953–1954) and [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) (1954–1963), where he was appointed Professor in 1957. In 1964 he moved to the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques), in [Bures-sur-Yvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bures-sur-Yvette), where he worked until 1990.[^o11]

In 1958, Thom received the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Edinburgh for the foundations of [cobordism](wikipedia://en/391832) theory, which were already present in his thesis.[^o12] He was [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) two more times: in 1970 in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice)[^o13] and 1983 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) (which he did not attend).[^o14]

He was awarded the [Brouwer Medal](wikipedia://en/42578624) in 1970,[^o15] the Grand Prix Scientifique de la Ville de Paris in 1974, and the [John von Neumann Lecture Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann_Lecture_Prize) in 1976.[^o16] He became the first president, together with [Louis Néel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Néel), of the newly established [Fondation Louis-de-Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fondation_Louis-de-Broglie) In 1973[^o17] and was elected Member of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences) of Paris in 1976.[^o18]

[Salvador Dalí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dalí) paid homage to René Thom with the paintings *[The Swallow's Tail](wikipedia://en/1827768)* and *Topological Abduction of Europe*.[^o19]

## Research {#Research}

While René Thom is most known to the public for his development of [catastrophe theory](wikipedia://en/434288) between 1968 and 1972,[^o21] his academic achievements concern mostly his mathematical work on topology.[^o22][^o23]

In the early 1950s, it concerned what are now called [Thom spaces](wikipedia://en/1243451), [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530), [cobordism theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobordism_theory), and the [Thom transversality theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_transversality_theorem). Another example of this line of work is the [Thom conjecture](wikipedia://en/3846986), versions of which have been investigated using [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155). From the mid 1950s he moved into [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763), of which catastrophe theory is just one aspect, and in a series of deep (and at the time obscure) papers between 1960 and 1969 developed the theory of [stratified sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically_stratified_space) and stratified maps, proving a basic stratified isotopy theorem describing the local conical structure of [Whitney stratified sets](wikipedia://en/9322242), now known as the [Thom–Mather isotopy theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom–Mather_isotopy_theorem). Much of his work on stratified sets was developed so as to understand the notion of topologically [stable maps](wikipedia://en/2300947), and to eventually prove the result that the set of topologically stable mappings between two smooth manifolds is a [dense set](wikipedia://en/23523594).

Thom's lectures on the stability of differentiable mappings, given at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) in 1960, were written up by [Harold Levine](wikipedia://en/30102215) and published in the proceedings of a year long symposium on singularities at [Liverpool University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_University) during 1969–70, edited by [C. T. C. Wall](wikipedia://en/2613478). The proof of the density of topologically stable mappings was completed by [John Mather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mather_%28mathematician%29) in 1970, based on the ideas developed by Thom in the previous ten years. A coherent detailed account was published in 1976 by Christopher Gibson, Klaus Wirthmüller, Andrew du Plessis, and [Eduard Looijenga](wikipedia://en/45224498).[^o24]

During the last twenty years of his life Thom's published work was mainly in philosophy and epistemology, and he undertook a reevaluation of [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308)'s writings on science. In 1992, he was one of eighteen academics who sent a letter to [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) protesting against plans to award [Jacques Derrida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Derrida) an honorary doctorate.[^o25]

Beyond Thom's contributions to algebraic topology, he studied differentiable mappings, through the study of [generic properties](wikipedia://en/12886758). In his final years, he turned his attention to an effort to apply his ideas about structural topography to the questions of thought, language, and meaning in the form of a "semiophysics".
