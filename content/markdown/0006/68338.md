# Peter Shor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Shor"}

**Peter Williston Shor** (born August 14, 1959) is an American [theoretical computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_computer_scientist) known for his work on [quantum computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computation), in particular for devising [Shor's algorithm](wikipedia://en/42674), a quantum algorithm for [factoring](wikipedia://en/15491) exponentially faster than the best currently-known algorithm running on a classical computer. He has been a professor of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT) since 2003.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Shor was born on August 14, 1959, in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City), to Joan Bopp Shor and S. W. Williston Shor.[^o13][^o14] He grew up in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) and [Mill Valley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_Valley,_California).[^o13] While attending [Tamalpais High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamalpais_High_School), he placed third in the 1977 [USA Mathematical Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Mathematical_Olympiad).[^o15] After graduation that year, he won a silver medal at the [International Math Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Math_Olympiad) in [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) (the U.S. team achieved the most points per country that year).[^o16][^o17]

Shor graduated from the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) (Caltech) in 1981 with a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in mathematics.[^o18] He was a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) in 1978. He then did doctoral study in applied mathematics at MIT, receiving a Ph.D. in 1985.[^o19] His doctoral advisor was [F. Thomson Leighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Thomson_Leighton), and his thesis was on probabilistic analysis of [bin-packing](wikipedia://en/287015) algorithms.

## Career {#Career}

After being awarded his PhD by MIT, he spent one year as a [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), and then accepted a position at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Laboratories) in [New Providence, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Providence,_New_Jersey). It was there that he developed [Shor's algorithm](wikipedia://en/42674). This development was inspired by [Simon's problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon's_problem). Shor first found an efficient quantum algorithm for the [discrete log problem](wikipedia://en/181334) (which relates point-finding on a hypercube to a torus) and,

> "Later that week, I was able to solve the factoring problem as well. There’s a strange relation between discrete log and factoring."[^o21]

Both of these problems are examples of the [HSP](wikipedia://en/3728000). For his work discovering the efficient quantum algorithms for factoring and discrete logarithm he was awarded the [Nevanlinna Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevanlinna_Prize) at the 23rd [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1998[^o22][^o23] and the [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize) in 1999.[^o24] In 1999, he was awarded a [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellowship).[^o25] In 2017, he received the [Dirac Medal of the ICTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_Medal_%28ICTP%29) and for 2019 the [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Award) in Basic Sciences.[^o26]

Shor began his [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) position in 2003. Currently, he is the Henry Adams Morss and Henry Adams Morss, Jr. Professor of Applied Mathematics in the Department of Mathematics at MIT.[^o27] He also is affiliated with [CSAIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSAIL).[^o28]

He received a Distinguished Alumni Award from Caltech in 2007.[^o18]

On October 1, 2011, he was inducted into the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o29][^o30] He was elected as an [ACM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Fellow) in 2019 "for contributions to quantum-computing, information theory, and randomized algorithms".[^o31] He was elected as a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2002.[^o32] In 2020, he was elected a member of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) for pioneering contributions to quantum computation.[^o33]

In an interview published in *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29)* on October 30, 2020, Shor said that he considers [post-quantum cryptography](wikipedia://en/26605226) to be a solution to the quantum threat, although a lot of engineering effort is required to switch from vulnerable algorithms.[^o34]

Along with three others, Shor was awarded the 2023 [Breakthrough Prize in Fundamental Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize_in_Fundamental_Physics) for "foundational work in the field of quantum information."[^o11]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Through his father, S. W. Williston Shor, he is a descendant of [Samuel Wendell Williston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Wendell_Williston).[^o36][^o13]
