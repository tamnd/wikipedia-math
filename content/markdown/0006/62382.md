# Catalan's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Catalan's conjecture"}

**Catalan's conjecture** (or **Mihăilescu's theorem**) is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) that was [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) by the mathematician [Eugène Charles Catalan](wikipedia://en/639553) in 1842 and [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) in 2002 by [Preda Mihăilescu](wikipedia://en/639556) at [Paderborn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paderborn_University).[^o2][^o3] The [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) 2<sup>3</sup> and 3<sup>2</sup> are two [perfect powers](wikipedia://en/4023591) (that is, powers of exponent higher than one) of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) whose values (8 and 9, respectively) are consecutive. The theorem states that this is the *only* case of two consecutive perfect powers. That is to say, that

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=math-theorem name="Catalan's conjecture"}

**Catalan's conjecture.**

the only [solution in the natural numbers](wikipedia://en/9109) of

$x^a - y^b = 1$ for $a, b > 1$, $x, y > 0$ is $x = 3$, $a = 2$, $y = 2$, $b = 3$.

:::

:::

## History {#History}

The history of the problem dates back at least to [Gersonides](wikipedia://en/61626), who proved a special case of the conjecture in 1343 where (*x*, *y*) was restricted to be (2, 3) or (3, 2). The first significant progress after Catalan made his conjecture came in 1850 when [Victor-Amédée Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/43386105) dealt with the case *b* = 2.[^o6]

In 1976, [Robert Tijdeman](wikipedia://en/9006087) applied [Baker's method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker's_method) in [transcendence theory](wikipedia://en/1157698) to establish a [bound](wikipedia://en/42693) on *a*,*b* and used existing results bounding *x*,*y* in terms of *a*, *b* to give an effective upper bound for *x*,*y*,*a*,*b*. Michel Langevin computed a value of $\exp \exp \exp \exp 730 \approx 10^{10^{10^{10^{317}}}}$ for the bound,[^o7] resolving Catalan's conjecture for all but a finite number of cases.

Catalan's conjecture was proven by [Preda Mihăilescu](wikipedia://en/639556) in April 2002. The proof was published in the *[Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_für_die_reine_und_angewandte_Mathematik)*, 2004. It makes extensive use of the theory of [cyclotomic fields](wikipedia://en/30872462) and [Galois modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module). An exposition of the proof was given by [Yuri Bilu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Bilu) in the [Séminaire Bourbaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Séminaire_Bourbaki).[^o8] In 2005, Mihăilescu published a simplified proof.[^o9]

## Pillai's conjecture {#Pillai's_conjecture}

::: {#o12 .problem cue=unsolved}

Does each positive integer occur only finitely many times as a difference of perfect powers?

:::

**Pillai's conjecture** concerns a general difference of perfect powers : it is an [open problem](wikipedia://en/1333335) initially proposed by [S. S. Pillai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._S._Pillai), who conjectured that the gaps in the sequence of perfect powers tend to infinity. This is equivalent to saying that each positive integer occurs only finitely many times as a difference of perfect powers: more generally, in 1931 Pillai conjectured that for fixed positive integers *A*, *B*, *C* the equation $Ax^n - By^m = C$ has only finitely many solutions (*x*, *y*, *m*, *n*) with (*m*, *n*) ≠ (2, 2). Pillai proved that for fixed *A*, *B*, *x*, *y*, and for any λ less than 1, we have $|Ax^n - By^m| \gg x^{\lambda n}$ uniformly in *m* and *n*.[^o13]

The general conjecture would follow from the [ABC conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_conjecture).[^o13][^o14]

Pillai's conjecture means that for every natural number *n*, there are only finitely many pairs of perfect powers with difference *n*.
