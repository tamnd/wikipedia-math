# Complete category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complete category"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **complete category** is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) in which all small [limits](wikipedia://en/39248) exist. That is, a category *C* is complete if every [diagram](wikipedia://en/7651625) *F* : *J* → *C* (where *J* is [small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_category)) has a limit in *C*. [Dually](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duality_%28category_theory%29), a **cocomplete category** is one in which all small [colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit) exist. A **bicomplete category** is a category which is both complete and cocomplete.

:::

The existence of *all* limits (even when *J* is a [proper class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_class)) is too strong to be practically relevant. Any category with this property is necessarily a [thin category](wikipedia://en/31353487): for any two objects there can be at most one morphism from one object to the other.

A weaker form of completeness is that of finite completeness. A category is **finitely complete** if all finite limits exists (i.e. limits of diagrams indexed by a finite category *J*). Dually, a category is **finitely cocomplete** if all finite colimits exist.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Theorems"}

## Theorems {#Theorems}

It follows from the [existence theorem for limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existence_theorem_for_limits) that a category is complete [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it has [equalizers](wikipedia://en/310767) (of all pairs of morphisms) and all (small) [products](wikipedia://en/237868). Since equalizers may be constructed from [pullbacks](wikipedia://en/841689) and binary products (consider the pullback of (*f*, *g*) along the diagonal Δ), a category is complete if and only if it has pullbacks and products.

Dually, a category is cocomplete if and only if it has [coequalizers](wikipedia://en/431940) and all (small) [coproducts](wikipedia://en/310889), or, equivalently, [pushouts](wikipedia://en/582024) and coproducts.

Finite completeness can be characterized in several ways. For a category *C*, the following are all equivalent:

- *C* is finitely complete,
- *C* has equalizers and all finite products,
- *C* has equalizers, binary products, and a [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object),
- *C* has [pullbacks](wikipedia://en/841689) and a terminal object.

The dual statements are also equivalent.

A [small category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_category) *C* is complete if and only if it is cocomplete.[^o4] A small complete category is necessarily thin.

A [posetal category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posetal_category) vacuously has all equalizers and coequalizers, whence it is (finitely) complete if and only if it has all (finite) products, and dually for cocompleteness. Without the finiteness restriction a posetal category with all products is automatically cocomplete, and dually, by a theorem about complete lattices.

:::

## Examples and nonexamples {#Examples_and_nonexamples}

- The following categories are bicomplete:
  - **Set**, the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829)[^o6]
  - **Top**, the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051)
  - **Grp**, the [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092)
  - **Ab**, the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748)
  - **Ring**, the [category of rings](wikipedia://en/15300181)
  - ***K*-Vect**, the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K*
  - ***R*-Mod**, the [category of modules](wikipedia://en/11231812) over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R*
  - **CmptH**, the category of all [compact Hausdorff spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Hausdorff_space)
  - **Cat**, the [category of all small categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_all_small_categories)
  - **Whl**, the category of [wheels](wikipedia://en/634240)
  - **sSet**, the category of [simplicial sets](wikipedia://en/1027784)[^o7]
- The following categories are finitely complete and finitely cocomplete but neither complete nor cocomplete:
  - The category of [finite sets](wikipedia://en/11742)
  - The category of [finite abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_abelian_group)
  - The category of [finite-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-dimensional) vector spaces
- Any ([pre](wikipedia://en/62775))[abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) is finitely complete and finitely cocomplete.
- The category of [complete lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_lattices) is complete but not cocomplete.
- The [category of metric spaces](wikipedia://en/535617), **Met**, is finitely complete but has neither binary coproducts nor infinite products.
- The [category of fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_fields), **Field**, is neither finitely complete nor finitely cocomplete.
- A [poset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poset), considered as a small category, is complete (and cocomplete) if and only if it is a [complete lattice](wikipedia://en/44777).
- The [partially ordered class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partially_ordered_class) of all [ordinal numbers](wikipedia://en/26547932) is cocomplete but not complete (since it has no terminal object).
- A group, considered as a category with a single object, is complete if and only if it is [trivial](wikipedia://en/193059). A nontrivial group has pullbacks and pushouts, but not products, coproducts, equalizers, coequalizers, terminal objects, or initial objects.
