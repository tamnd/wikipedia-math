# Demographics of Albania

::: {#o6 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Albania"}

[Demographic](wikipedia://en/45642) features of the [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_%28human_biology%29) of [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania) include [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density), [ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group), education level, health of the populace, economic status, religious affiliations and other aspects. The demography of Albania is monitored by the [Institute of Statistics](wikipedia://en/31272108) of Albania. The institute has performed demographic [censuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_in_Albania) since the 1920s.[^o7] The latest census in Albania was performed in September 2023.[^o8]

:::

Albania is a fairly ethnically and linguistically homogeneous country, with ethnic [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians) forming 91% of the total population in the country. The total population residing in Albania in September 2023 was 2,402,113 according to the most recent [INSTAT](wikipedia://en/31272108) census.

The first official population statistic for Albania was the 1923 census, when the country had a total of 823,000 inhabitants. Previous censuses were carried out by the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire), but have not been made public. A shift in administrative borders in 1913 make comparison of various periods more complicated. Maddison from 2001, estimates that in Albania about 200,000 people lived up to the year 1600, and that the population grew to 300,000 by 1700, implying an annual average growth rate of 0.4% in that period. However, population growth accelerated from the [declaration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Declaration_of_Independence) in 1912 to 1944 to 0.7% per year. After the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), population growth policies pursued by the communist government and a large [life expectancy](wikipedia://en/18669) fueled a 2.5% annual increase for the following 45 years. Albania consistently had the highest [birth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_rate) and one of the lowest [death rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Rate) in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) at the time.[^o9] This prolonged growth strained economic resources in a [Malthusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malthusianism) fashion, contributing to the collapse of the communist regime and the [emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emigration) of about 20 to 25 percent of the population in the following two decades. Albania experienced a demographic transition starting from 1960s, when crude birth rates began a slow decline, despite a government policy that called for a population increase. After the 1990s, the population showed an average decline of about 0.3% per year, caused by emigration. In the 2001 Census, the population declined to 3,023,000 from almost 3,300,000 million in 1990.

The permanent population of Albania at the 2023 census had decreased by 420,000 people compared to the 2011 census. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) in 2023 was 84 inhabitants per square kilometre. The [overall life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_life_expectancy) in Albania at birth was 80.6 years in 2024.[^o10][^o11] In 2011, for the first time in the history of population censuses in Albania, the population in urban areas (53.7%) was larger than the population of rural areas (46.3%).[^o12]

The [Albanian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language) is the official language, but minority languages are officially used in some local government units. Albanian is declared as the native language by 98.76% of the population. Albanians are amongst the most [monolingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monolingualism) people in Europe, with 59.9% of inhabitants only speaking one language.[^o13] Commonly spoken second languages include [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) (concentrated in the south) and English, the latter of which is increasing, particularly amongst the youth.

According to the 2023 census, the main religions of Albania are [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Albania) (50.67%), [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Albania) (16%); of which [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Albania) (8.38%), [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy_in_Albania) (7.22%) and [Evangelicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants_in_Albania) (0.4%), Believers without denomination (13.83%), [Atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism_in_Albania) (3.55%) and other religions (0.15%) . Additionally, 10.17% of respondents preferred not to answer.

The main ethnic groups in Albania are [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians) (91%), [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_in_Albania) (1%), [Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkali) (0.5%), [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Albania) (0.4%), [Bulgarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarians_in_Albania) (0.3%), as well as [Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks_in_Albania), [Aromanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanians_in_Albania), [Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonians_in_Albania), [Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs) and [Montenegrins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrins).

Albania has a high [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) of 0.810, ranking [71st](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_Human_Development_Index) in the world in 2023[^o14]. In 2016, Albania had a total population of 2,786,026, 1,361,326 being males and 1,424,700 females. 42,922 inhabitants have left Albania and in the same year the number of [immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrants) in the country was 25,846.[^o15]

## Population {#Population}

*Image: Albania single age population pyramid 2020.png.* 2020 Albanian [population pyramid](wikipedia://en/989011)

With an estimated population of 2.36 million in 2025,[^o17] Albania ranks [142nd in the world by population](wikipedia://en/69058). The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_territories_by_population_density) is 101 inhabitants per square kilometre.[^o18] The [life expectancy at birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_life_expectancy) in 2024 was 80 years.[^o19] The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) is 1.21 children per mother, the third lowest in Europe after [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta) and [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain).[^o20] There were 23,617 births and 21,286 deaths in Albania in 2023, a positive natural population increase as the number of births exceeded the number of deaths by 2,331.[^o21] In 2025, the total [dependency ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependency_ratio) of population in Albania is 57.1% and the median age is 44.3 years old.[^o17]

In 2001, the number of households amounted to 726,895.[^o22] The [age structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_structure) was under 6 years by 10.8% and 65 years and older by 7.5%. However, the sex ratio amounted to 49.9% males and 50.1% females of the total population. In 2011, the total population was 2,831,741.[^o23] The comparison of the figures shows that the population has decreased by 7.7% in about ten years. Large scale emigration and fertility decline are supposed to be the main causes of the observed population decrease. A preliminary estimate of the number of persons that refused to participate in the census is 29,355 (1.04%). This figure is based on the number of dwellings for which a refusal was recorded and is included in the total population. The total population is composed of 1,421,810 males (50.2%) and 1,409,931 females (49.8%). For the first time in the history of population censuses in the nation, the population in urban areas is larger than the population of rural areas. According to 2011 census preliminary results, 53.7 percent of the population lives in urban areas and 46.3 percent in rural areas.

The 2011 census is regarded as unreliable and inaccurate by the Council of Europe, showing incompatibility with the protection of national minorities.[^o24] Also, the World Council of Churches sent letters to the United Nations Human Rights Council regarding the matter, having conducted their own questionnaire which showed major irregularities.[^o25] It was the first census to include [ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity), was struck by controversy since according to article 20 of the Census law, there is a \$1,000 fine for anyone who declares anything other than what was written down on the individual's birth certificate.[^o26] Some of the minorities, mainly the Greeks, boycotted the census.[^o27] In 2024, the religious affiliation in Albania was 36.5% [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam), 33.8% Irreligious believers, 8% [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Orthodox_Church) Christians, 7.9% [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church), 7.2% [Atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism) and [Agnostics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnosticism), 5.2% [Bektashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bektashism) and 1.2% [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism).[^o28]

*Image: Dita e Flamurit.JPG.* [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians)

[Tirana County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana_County) is the area with the highest population of 811,649 in the country. [Fier County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fier_County) remains the population with the second highest population with a total of 312,488. The Counties with the lowest result are [Gjirokastër](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gjirokastër_County), [Kukës](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kukës_County) and [Dibër](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibër_County) respectively with 70,331, 84,035 and 134,153 inhabitants.[^o29] About 53.4% of Albania's population live in cities. The three largest counties account for half of the population.

*Image: City of Durrës.jpg.* View of [Durrës](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrës)

Almost 53.4% of the population of Albania living in cities. According to the Institute of Statistics (INSTAT), the three largest counties account for half of the population. Over 1 million people live in [Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana) and [Durrës](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrës), making it the largest urban area in Albania.[^o30] The area of the capital [Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana), is one of largest cities in the [Balkan Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Peninsula) and ranks 7th with a population about 800,000.[^o31] The second largest is the port city of Durrës, with a population of 201.110, followed by [Vlorë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlorë), the largest city in southern Albania, with 141.513 inhabitants. The Institute of Statistics forecast that the population may even increase by less than a fifth from 763.560 by 2011 to 909.252 by 2031, depending on the actual birth rate and the level of net migration.[^o32]

## Ethnic groups {#Ethnic_groups}

Albania is inhabited mostly by Albanians. In the 2011 official census, 97.8% of those who disclosed their identity were Albanians (82.58% overall), while 2.3% stated other ethnicities (1.9% overall).[^o88] Albania recognizes [nine national minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minorities_of_Albania). These include [Aromanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanians), [Balkan Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Egyptians), [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks), [Bulgarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarians), [Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks), [Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonians_%28ethnic_group%29), [Montenegrins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrins_%28ethnic_group%29), [Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs), and [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people).[^o89] As conducting a satisfactory census of ethnic minorities is one of the country's commitments to the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union), the [Government of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Albania) conducted an official census to clarify the ethnic composition of the population in 2011. However this census was deemed unreliable by internal and external organisations, including the Council of Europe and many of Albania's ethnic minority groups.[^o90] The last census to include data on ethnic minorities was conducted in 1989.[^o91] The census conducted in 2001 did not collect information about ethnic groups and nationalities in the population.

Since the 21st century, Albania has also witnessed a significant settlement of foreign expats into the country, such as [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people), [Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos), [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people), [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis), [Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people)[^o92] and other foreign workers. In 2021 there were as many as 15,000 foreigners living in Albania (0.5% of the population).[^o93]

Albania also houses 3,200 [Afghan refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_refugees) and over 3,800 [Ukrainian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_refugee_crisis_%282022–present%29).[^o94][^o95][^o96]

According to the 2023 census, there were 1,101,718 (45.86%) [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam) (mostly [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) and [Non-denominational Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational_Muslim)) 201,530 (8.38%) [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism), 173,645 (7.22%) [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy), 115,644 (4.81%) [Bektashi Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bektashi_Order), 9,658 (0.4%) [Evangelicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism), 3 670 (0.15%) of other religions, 332,155 (13.82%) believers without denomination, 85,311 (3.55%) Atheists and 378,782 (15.76%) did not provide an answer.[^o8]

The [Constitution of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Albania) extends freedom of religion to all citizens and the government generally respects this right in practice. It declares no official religion and provides for equality of all religions. However, the predominant religious communities enjoy a greater degree of official recognition and social status based on their historical presence in the country. All registered religious groups have the right to hold bank accounts and to own property and buildings. Religious freedoms have in large part been secured by the generally amicable relationship among religions. The [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_%28Albania%29) has the right to approve the curricula of religious schools to ensure their compliance with national education standards while the State Committee on Cults oversees implementation. There are also 68 vocational training centers administered by religious communities.[^o128]

Government policy and practice contributed to the generally free exercise of religion. The government is secular and the Ministry of Education asserts that public schools in the country are secular and that the law prohibits ideological and religious indoctrination. Religion is not taught in public schools.[^o128]

## Language {#Language}

*Image: Albanian dialects.svg.* Map illustrating the various dialects of the Albanian language in Southern Europe

The [Albanian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language) is the official language of Albania. It has two distinct dialects, Tosk, spoken in the south, and Gheg, spoken in the north. The [Shkumbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shkumbin) river is the rough dividing line between the two dialects. The language is spoken primarily in [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy), [Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo), [North Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Macedonia) and [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegro). Centuries-old communities speaking Albanian-based dialects can be found scattered in Greece ([Arvanites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvanites)), Southern Italy, Sicily and Calabria ([Arbëreshë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbëreshë_people)) and in [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_in_Ukraine). However, due to the large [Albanian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_diaspora), the total number of speakers is much higher than the native speakers in [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe). The four dialects include [Tosk Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tosk_Albanian), [Gheg Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheg_Albanian), [Arbëresh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbëresh_language) and [Arvanitika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvanitika).

Foreign languages spoken generally include one – or more – amongst the following: [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language). Language knowledge is increasing due to returning migrants, as well as new Greek and Italian communities in the country. In particular, Italian is widely spoken throughout Albania. [La Francophonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Francophonie) states that 30,000 French speakers can be found in Albania.[^o130] Greek, the language of the Greek minority of the south, is also very widespread in that region. Nowadays, knowledge of English is growing very rapidly, especially among the youth.

The ethnic minorities languages include [Aromanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanian_language), [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language), [Macedonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_language), [Bosnian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_language), [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language), [Gorani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gora_dialect), and [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_language). Greek is the largest minority language of the country as well as the first largest foreign language. Approximately 5,000 Macedonian language-speakers can be found in Albania. Most of these living in the southeastern part of the country.

## Education {#Education}

*Image: Tirana, palazzo dell%27università 02.JPG.* The [University of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tirana) is the largest and highest ranking university in Albania.

Literacy in the country is 98.7 percent.[^o132] The [school life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_life_expectancy) (primary to tertiary education) of Albania is 16 years. The nation ranks 25th out of 167 countries in the world.[^o133] In 2015, the overall [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_literacy_rate) in Albania was 98.7%; the male [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_rate) was 99.2% and female literacy rate was 98.3%.

[Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Albania) for primary *(arsimi fillor)*, secondary *(arsimi i mesëm)*, and tertiary *(arsimi universitar)* levels are mostly supported by the state. Classes start in September and end in June.[^o134] Albanian is the primary language of instruction in all public schools.[^o135] Education takes place in three stages: primary, secondary, and pre-university. Primary education is obligatory from grade 1 to 9. Students must pass the graduation exams at the end of the 9th grade in order to continue their education. After primary school, the general education is provided at the secondary schools. Students get prepared for the [Matura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matura) examination, allowing them to obtain their matura diploma, which grants admission to higher education. The country follows the [Bologna model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_model) in accordance with the 2007 Law on 'Higher Education'.[^o136] These institutions can be public or private, and may offer one, two or three levels of higher education depending on the institution.

## Health {#Health}

*Image: Albania life expectancy 2022 map.jpg.* Life expectancy in Albania

[Life expectancy at birth](wikipedia://en/18669) in [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania) was 80.6 years in 2024,[^o138] outperforming a number of [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union) countries, such as [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) and the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic).[^o139] [Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirana), Albania's capital and most populous city, had a life expectancy at birth of 82.8 years in 2022,[^o140] remarkably high for the region, outperforming significantly wealthier capital cities in [Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe), such as [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866)[^o141] and [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin).[^o142]

Albania has a [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care) system. In 2000, Albania had the world's 55th [best healthcare performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization_ranking_of_health_systems_in_2000).

According to the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization) (WHO), Albania ranks around the 40th in the world in terms of [life expectancy](wikipedia://en/18669).[^o143][^o144] Compared to other Western countries, Albania has a low rate of [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity).[^o145]

## Diaspora {#Diaspora}

*Image: Albanians in Europe.jpg.* Distribution of [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_people) in Europe

Albanians have established communities in many regions throughout southern Europe. The modern Albanian diaspora was formed largely in the 15th century, when many Albanians emigrated to southern [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbereshe_people), especially in [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily) and [Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabria) also to [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_in_greece), to escape either various socio-political difficulties and the Ottoman conquest. Other destinations include the [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada), [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina), and [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey).

Over the last twenty years, Albania has experienced major demographic changes, having the [highest population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) in Europe before the [collapse of communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_communism_in_Albania) in the country.[^o150] Albania has also shifted to population decline since the changes. However, during the final days of the transition from communism to capitalism in 1990, over a million Albanians moved to foreign countries. These include [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Australians), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Canadians), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania-France_relations), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_in_Germany), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_in_Italy), [the Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_in_the_Nordic_countries), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_in_Switzerland) and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_in_the_United_Kingdom). About 440,000 of them settled in [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece), where Albanians make up 60% of immigrants.[^o151] 350,000 Albanians have migrated to Italy over the 1990s to 2000s, however the number has increased substantially.[^o152] The situation in Kosovo is similar. More than a million Albanians have left Kosovo permanently since the late 1980s, not counting those fleeing the [Kosovo War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_War) who have subsequently returned.[^o153] Important destinations for Albanians emigrating from Kosovo have been Switzerland and Germany.[^o154]
