# Demographics of South Africa

*Image: South Africa 2011 population density map (hex cells).svg.* Population density in South Africa (darker color = higher density)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of South Africa"}

According to the 2022 census, the population of [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa) is about 62 million people of diverse origins, cultures, [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_South_Africa), and [religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_South_Africa), with a majority being Black Africans.[^o4] The [South African National Census of 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Census_of_2022) was the most recent census held; the next will be in 2032.[^o5]

:::

In 2011, [Statistics South Africa](wikipedia://en/1125602) counted 2.1 million foreign nationals.[^o6] Reports suggest that is an underestimation. Some estimates suggest the number could be higher, though figures vary widely and are not officially confirmed,[^o7] including some three million [Zimbabweans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Zimbabwe).[^o8]

## History {#History}

*Image: Historical population of South Africa.svg.* Historical population of South Africa

The earliest creatures that can be identified as human ancestors in South Africa are [australopithecines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australopithecine). The first evidence of this was a [child's skull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taung_Child) found in the [Taung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taung) quarry site. This was in the modern day [North-West province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_%28South_African_province%29). More [fossils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil) australopithecines were found in limestone caves [Sterkfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterkfontein), [Swartkrans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swartkrans), and [Kromdaai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kromdraai_fossil_site).[^o10]

#### UN age and population estimates: 1950 to 2030 {#UN_age_and_population_estimates:_1950_to_2030}

According to the 2019 revision of the *[United Nations World Population Prospects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_World_Population_Prospects)*, South Africa's total population was 55,386,000 in 2015, compared to only 13,628,000 in 1950. In 2015, 29.3% of the people were children under the age of 15, 65.7% were between 15 and 64 years of age, and 5.0% were 65 or older.[^o73] All population estimates are rounded to the nearest thousand.

## Ethnic groups {#Ethnic_groups}

*Image: South Africa 2011 dominant population group map (hex cells).svg.* Dominant population groups in South Africa. \#fb8072 \#8dd3c7 \#80b1d3 \#ffffb3 \#bebada \#ffffff

[Statistics South Africa](wikipedia://en/1125602) asks people to describe themselves in the census in terms of five [racial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_%28classification_of_human_beings%29) population groups.[^o119] The [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Census_of_2011) figures for these groups were [Black African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples_of_South_Africa) at 80.2%, [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_South_Africans) at 8.4%, [Coloured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Coloureds) at 8.8%, [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_South_Africans)/[South Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_South_Africans) at 2.5%, and Other/Unspecified at 0.5%.[^o120]

The white percentage of the population has sharply declined. The first census in South Africa in 1911 showed that whites made up 22% of the population. This declined to 16% in 1980,[^o121] 8.9% in 2011 and 7.65% in 2022.[^o122] Cape Coloureds replaced Whites as the largest minority group around 2010.

## Languages {#Languages}

South Africa has twelve [official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language):[^o58] [isiZulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IsiZulu)[^o120] 24.4%, [isiXhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IsiXhosa)[^o120] 16.3%, [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans)[^o120] 10.6%, [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)[^o120] 8.7%, [Sepedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepedi_language)[^o120] 10.0%, [Setswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setswana)[^o120] 8.3%, [Sesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesotho)[^o120] 7.8%, [Xitsonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xitsonga)[^o120] 4.7%, [siSwati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SiSwati)[^o120] 2.8%, [Tshivenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tshivenda)[^o120] 2.5%, [isiNdebele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ndebele_language)[^o120] 1.7% and [South African Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Sign_Language).[^o124]

In this regard, it is third only to [Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivia) and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_languages_of_India) in number. While all the languages are formally equal, some languages are spoken more than others. According to the [2022 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_South_African_census), the three most spoken languages in the household are Zulu (24.4%), Xhosa (16.3%), and Afrikaans (10.6%).[^o4]

Between 1996 and 2022, the proportion of Afrikaans speakers declined from 14.5% to 10.6% and English held steady at 8.7%, while isiZulu grew from 22.8% to 24.4%, and Xhosa declined from 17.9% to 16.3%[^o58]

The country also recognises several unofficial languages, including [Sekholokoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makholokoe_Tribes), [Fanagalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanagalo_language), [Khwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwe_language), [Lobedu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobedu_language), [Nama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nama_language), [Northern Ndebele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ndebele_language), [Phuthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuthi_language) and [San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_language).[^o125] These unofficial languages may be used in certain official uses in limited areas where it has been determined that these languages are prevalent. Nevertheless, their populations are not such that they require nationwide recognition.

Many of the "unofficial languages" of the [San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_people) and [Khoikhoi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoikhoi_people) contain [regional dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_dialect) stretching northwards into [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia) and [Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana), and elsewhere. These people, who are a physically distinct population from other Africans, have their own cultural identity based on their hunter-gatherer societies. They have been marginalised to a great extent, and many of their languages are in danger of becoming [extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinct_language).

Many white South Africans also speak other [European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_languages), such as [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language) (also spoken by black [Angolans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola) and [Mozambicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique)), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language) and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), while some Indians and other Asians in South Africa speak [South Asian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_languages), such as [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language), [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language), [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), [Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_language), [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_language) and [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language). Although many South Africans are of [Huguenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenots) (French) origin, South African French is spoken by fewer than 10,000 individuals country-wide. Congolese French is also spoken in South Africa by migrants.

The primary sign language of [deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deafness) South Africans is [South African Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Sign_Language). Other [sign languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_language) among indigenous peoples are also used.

### By ethnicity {#By_ethnicity}

In 2011, the first language was [Zulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_language) for 28.1% of Black African South African residents, [Xhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_language) for 19.8%, [Sepedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Sotho_language) for 11.2%, [Tswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tswana_language) for 9.7%, [Sesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesotho_language) for 9.3%, [Tsonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsonga_language) for 5.5%, [siSwati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siswati) for 3.1%, [Venda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venda_language) for 2.9%, English for 2.8%, [Southern Ndebele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ndebele_language) for 2.6%, [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans) for 1.5%, while 3.4% had another first language.[^o127]

Among whites, Afrikaans was the first language for 59.1% of the population, compared to 35.0% for English. Other languages accounted for the remaining 5.9%.[^o127]

## Religion {#Religion}

According to the 2022 national census, [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_South_Africa) accounted for 85.3% of the population.[^o4] This includes [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_South_Africa) (36.6%), [Zionist Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist_Christian) (11.1%), [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism)/[Charismatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charismatic_movement) (8.2%), [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_South_Africa) (7.1%), [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Church_of_Southern_Africa) (6.8%), [Dutch Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederduits_Gereformeerde_Kerk) (6.7%), [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Church_of_Southern_Africa) (3.8%); members of other Christian churches accounted for another 36% of the population. [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_South_Africa) accounted for 1.5% of the population, Other 2.3%, and 1.4% were Unspecified and None 15.1%.

[Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_South_Africa) are largely found among the [Coloured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coloured) and [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_South_African) ethnic groups. They have been joined by Black African or white South African converts as well as immigrants from other parts of Africa.[^o129]

The [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) population has its roots in the [British colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonial_period), but later waves of immigration from India have also contributed to it. Most Hindus are of South Asian origin, but there are many who come from mixed racial stock. Some are converts due to the efforts of [ISKCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Society_for_Krishna_Consciousness).

Other minority religions in South Africa are [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism_in_South_Africa), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism), [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baháʼí_Faith_in_South_Africa) and [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism).[^o130]

### By ethnicity {#By_ethnicity}

\86.0% of Black African residents are Christian, 3.0% have [no religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion), 0.3% are Muslim, 0.0% are [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews), 0.0% are [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), 9.5% [Traditional African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions) and 1.2% have other or undetermined beliefs (2022 Census).

\90.1% of white residents are Christian, 4.9% have no religion, 0.3% are Muslim, 0.7% are Jewish, and 0.1% are Hindu. 3.9% have other or undetermined beliefs (2022 Census).

## Immigration {#Immigration}

*Image: COB data South Africa.PNG.*

South Africa hosts a sizeable [refugee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee) and [asylum seeker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asylum_seeker) population. According to the *World Refugee Survey 2008*, published by the [U.S. Committee for Refugees and Immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Committee_for_Refugees_and_Immigrants), this population numbered approximately 144,700 in 2007.[^o154] Groups of refugees and asylum seekers numbering over 10,000 included people from [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe) (48,400), the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) (24,800), and [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia) (12,900).[^o154] These populations mainly lived in [Johannesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg), [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria), [Durban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durban), [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town), and [Port Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Elizabeth).[^o154] Many refugees have now also started to work and live in rural areas in provinces such as [Mpumalanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpumalanga) and [KwaZulu-Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KwaZulu-Natal).

[Statistics SA](wikipedia://en/1125602) assumes in some of their calculations that there are fewer than 2 million immigrants in South Africa.[^o155] Other institutions, like the police and [Médecins Sans Frontières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Médecins_Sans_Frontières) place estimate the figure at 4 million.[^o156][^o157][^o158][^o159]
