# Supersonic speed

*Image: F-18-diamondback blast.jpg.* A [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy) [F/A-18F Super Hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18F_Super_Hornet) in [transonic](wikipedia://en/247737) [flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight)

*Image: FA-18 Hornet breaking sound barrier (7 July 1999) - filtered.jpg.* U.S. Navy [F/A-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18_Hornet) approaching the speed of sound. The white cloud forms as a result of the [supersonic expansion fans](wikipedia://en/6936536) dropping the air temperature below the [dew point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dew_point).[^o1][^o2]

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Supersonic speed"}

**Supersonic speed** is the speed of an object that exceeds the [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853) ([Mach](wikipedia://en/20051) 1). For objects traveling in dry air of a temperature of 20 °C (68 °F) at [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level), this speed is approximately \343.2 m/s. Speeds greater than five times the speed of sound (Mach 5) are often referred to as [hypersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic). Flights during which only some parts of the air surrounding an object, such as the ends of rotor blades, reach supersonic speeds are called [transonic](wikipedia://en/247737). This occurs typically somewhere between Mach 0.8 and Mach 1.2.

:::

Sounds are traveling vibrations in the form of pressure waves in an elastic medium. Objects move at supersonic speed when the objects move faster than the speed at which sound propagates through the medium. In gases, sound travels longitudinally at different speeds, mostly depending on the [molecular mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_mass) and [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050) of the gas, and [pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure) has little effect. Since air temperature and composition varies significantly with altitude, the speed of sound, and [Mach numbers](wikipedia://en/20051) for a steadily moving object may change. In water at [room temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_temperature), supersonic speed means any speed greater than 1,440 m/s (4,724 ft/s). In solids, sound waves can be polarized longitudinally or transversely and have higher velocities.

[Supersonic fracture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic_fracture) is crack formation faster than the speed of sound in a [brittle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittle) material.

## Early meaning {#Early_meaning}

The word supersonic comes from two [Latin derived words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_influence_in_English); 1) *super*: above and 2) *sonus*: sound, which together mean above sound, or faster than sound.

At the beginning of the 20th century, the term "supersonic" was used as an adjective to describe sound whose frequency is above the range of normal human hearing. The modern term for this meaning is "[ultrasonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasonic)".

## Supersonic objects {#Supersonic_objects}

*Image: British Concorde.jpg.* [British Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Airways) [Concorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concorde) in early BA livery at [London-Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London-Heathrow_Airport), in the early 1980s

The tip of a [bullwhip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullwhip) is generally seen as the first object designed to reach the speed of sound. This action results in its telltale "crack", which is actually just a [sonic boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_boom). The first human-made supersonic boom was likely caused by a piece of common cloth, leading to the whip's eventual development.[^o6] It is the [wave motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_motion) travelling through the bullwhip that makes it capable of achieving supersonic speeds.[^o7][^o8]

Most modern [firearm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firearm) [bullets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullets) are supersonic, with rifle [projectiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projectiles) often travelling at speeds approaching and in some cases[^o9] well exceeding [Mach 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mach_%28speed%29).

Most [spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacecraft) are supersonic at least during portions of their reentry, though the effects on the spacecraft are reduced by low air densities. During ascent, launch vehicles generally avoid going supersonic below 30 km (~98,400 feet) to reduce air drag.

The [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853#Speed_in_ideal_gases_and_in_air) decreases somewhat with altitude, due to lower temperatures found there (typically up to 25 km). At even higher altitudes the temperature starts increasing, with the corresponding increase in the speed of sound.

When an inflated [balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balloon) is burst, the torn pieces of [latex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latex) contract at supersonic speed, which contributes to the sharp and loud popping noise.

### Supersonic land vehicles {#Supersonic_land_vehicles}

To date, only one land vehicle has officially travelled at supersonic speed, the [ThrustSSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ThrustSSC). The vehicle, driven by [Andy Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Green_%28RAF_officer%29), holds the world land speed record, having achieved an average speed on its bi-directional run of 763 mph in the [Black Rock Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Rock_Desert) on 15 October 1997.

The [Bloodhound LSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodhound_LSR) project planned an attempt on the record in 2020 at [Hakskeenpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakskeenpan) in South Africa with a combination jet and hybrid rocket propelled car. The aim was to break the existing record, then make further attempts during which (the members of) the team hoped to reach speeds of up to 1000 mph. The effort was originally run by [Richard Noble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Noble) who was the leader of the ThrustSSC project, however following funding issues in 2018, the team was bought by [Ian Warhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Warhurst) and renamed Bloodhound LSR. Later the project was indefinitely delayed due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic) and the vehicle was put up for sale.

### Supersonic flight {#Supersonic_flight}

Most modern [fighter aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter_aircraft) are supersonic aircraft. No modern-day passenger aircraft are capable of supersonic speed, but there have been [supersonic passenger aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic_transport), namely [Concorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concorde) and the [Tupolev Tu-144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-144). Both of these passenger [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft) and some modern fighters are also capable of [supercruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercruise), a condition of sustained supersonic flight without the use of an [afterburner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterburner). Due to its ability to supercruise for several hours and the relatively high frequency of flight over several decades, Concorde spent more time flying supersonically than all other aircraft combined by a considerable margin. Since Concorde's final retirement flight on November 26, 2003, there are no supersonic passenger aircraft left in service, though the [Boom Overture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boom_Overture) may reintroduce supersonic flight in the future once completed. Some large [bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombers), such as the [Tupolev Tu-160](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-160) and [Rockwell B-1 Lancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwell_B-1_Lancer) are also supersonic-capable.

*Image: Dopplereffectsourcemovingrightatmach1.4.gif.* The sound source is traveling at 1.4 times the speed of sound, c (Mach 1.4). Because the source is moving faster than the sound waves it creates, it actually leads the advancing wavefront. The sound source will pass by a stationary observer before the observer actually hears the sound it creates.

The [aerodynamics](wikipedia://en/2819) of [supersonic aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic_aircraft) is simpler than subsonic aerodynamics because the airsheets at different points along the plane often cannot affect each other. [Supersonic jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic_jet) and rocket vehicles require several times greater thrust to push through the extra [aerodynamic drag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodynamic_drag) experienced within the [transonic](wikipedia://en/247737) region (around Mach 0.85–1.2). At these speeds [aerospace engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineer) can gently guide air around the [fuselage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuselage) of the aircraft without producing new [shock waves](wikipedia://en/146253), but any change in cross area farther down the vehicle leads to shock waves along the body. Designers use the [Supersonic area rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic_area_rule) and the [Whitcomb area rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitcomb_area_rule) to minimize sudden changes in size; however, in practical applications, a supersonic aircraft must operate stably in both subsonic and supersonic profiles, hence aerodynamic design is more complex.

The main key to having low supersonic drag is to properly shape the overall aircraft to be long and thin, and close to a "perfect" shape, the [von Karman ogive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nose_cone_design#Haack_series) or [Sears-Haack body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sears-Haack_body). This has led to almost every supersonic cruising aircraft looking very similar to every other, with a very long and slender fuselage and large delta wings, cf. [SR-71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SR-71_Blackbird), [Concorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concorde), etc. Although not ideal for passenger aircraft, this shaping is quite adaptable for bomber use.
