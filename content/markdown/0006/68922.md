# Demographics of Bangladesh

::: {#o11 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Bangladesh"}

[Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh) is the [eighth-most populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population) country in the world, accounting for approximately 2.2% of the [global population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population). According to the final results of the [2022 Census of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Census_of_Bangladesh), the country's population was 169,828,911.[^o12] Bangladesh has one of the highest [population densities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population_density) in the world and is the only country ranked among the top ten globally in both population density and total population.

:::

Bangladesh is largely [ethnically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) homogeneous, with the [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_people) ethnolinguistic group comprising around 99% of the population. The [Chittagong Hill Tracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts), [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_Division), [Mymensingh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mymensingh_Division), [Barisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisal_Division), and parts of [North Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajshahi_Division) are home to diverse indigenous communities. Numerous dialects of Bengali are spoken across the country, with the dialects of [Chittagong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong) and [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet) being particularly distinctive.

According to the 2022 census, approximately 91.04% of Bangladeshis are [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims), followed by [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) (7.95%), [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhists) (0.61%), Christians (0.30%), and others (0.12%).

Bangladesh has experienced a significant decline in [fertility rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rates) since its independence in 1971. The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) (TFR) decreased from over 6 births per woman in the 1970s to approximately 2.1 births per woman in 2024, nearing [replacement-level fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replacement-level_fertility).[^o13]

## Population {#Population}

*Image: Bangladesh Population 1947to2025.png.* Demographic evolution of the population of [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh) (1947–2025)

The population of Bangladesh has grown rapidly since 1947, when the region was part of [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan). According to United Nations estimates, the population increased from approximately 44 million in 1950 to around 175.7 million in 2025. [Population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) accelerated during the second half of the 20th century before gradually slowing due to declining fertility rates.

As of mid-2025, the population of Bangladesh was estimated at 175,686,899 people, according to the United Nations [World Population Prospects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Population_Prospects) 2024 revision.[^o15]

### Population growth rate {#Population_growth_rate}

*Image: Dhaka 14th March (32624769393).jpg.* A view of [Paltan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paltan) and [Segunbagicha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segunbagicha) areas in [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka) in 2017

[Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh) had high rates of population growth in the 1960s and 1970s. Since then however it has seen significant reduction in its total [fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate). Over a period of three decades it dropped from almost 7 to 2 in 2005–2018.[^o22]

### Urban and rural {#Urban_and_rural}

The sprawling mega-city of Dhaka has a huge population, but the majority of the people nonetheless still live in villages in rural areas.

Urban population: 37.4% of total population (2019 est.)

Rate of urbanisation: 3.13% annual rate of change (2019 est.)

Bangladesh is considered an urban country based on their population density

## Ethnic group {#Ethnic_group}

The vast majority (about 99%) of Bangladeshis are of the [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_people) ethno-linguistic group. This group also spans the neighbouring Indian province of [West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal). [Minority ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_Bangladesh) include [Meitei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_people), [Tripuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripuri_people), [Marma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marma_people), [Tanchangya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanchangya_people), [Barua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barua_%28Bangladesh%29), [Khasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khasi_people), [Santhals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santhal_people), [Chakma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_people), [Rakhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people), [Garo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garo_%28tribe%29), [Biharis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biharis), [Oraons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurukh_people), and [Mundas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munda_people).

*Image: Map of the largest ethnic group per Union council in Bangladesh.png.* Map of the largest ethnic group per Union council in Bangladesh

[Biharis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biharis) are [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu)-speaking, non-Bengalis who emigrated from the state of [Bihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar) and other parts of northern India during the [1947 partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India). They are concentrated in the [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka) and [Rangpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpur,_Bangladesh) areas and number some 300,000.[^o58][^o59] In the 1971 independence war many of them sided with Pakistan, as they stood to lose their positions in the upper levels of society.[^o60] Hundreds of thousands went to Pakistan and those that remained were interned in [refugee camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranded_Pakistanis). Their population declined from about 1 million in 1971 to 600,000 in the late 1980s.[^o60] [Refugees International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_International) has called them a "neglected and stateless" people as they are denied citizenship by the governments of Bangladesh and Pakistan.[^o61] As nearly 40 years has passed, two generations of Biharis have been born in these camps. Biharis were granted Bangladeshi citizenship and voting rights in 2008.[^o62]

Bangladesh's tribal population was enumerated at 897,828 in the 1981 census.[^o60] These tribes are concentrated in the [Chittagong Hill Tracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts) and around [Mymensingh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mymensingh), [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet), and [Rajshahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajshahi). They are of mixed Indo-Aryan and Sino-Tibetan descent and differ markedly in their social customs, religion, language and level of development. They speak Indo-Aryan and Tibeto-Burman languages and most are Buddhist or Hindu.[^o60] The four largest tribes are [Chakmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_people), Marmas, Tipperas, [Tanchangya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanchangya_people), and Mros. Smaller groups include the Santals in Rajshahi and Dinajpur, and Khasis, Garos, and Khajons in Mymensingh and Sylhet regions.[^o60]

There are small communities of [Meitei people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_people) (alias [Manipuri people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipuri_people)) in the Sylhet district, which is close to the Meitei homeland across the border in [Manipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipur), India.[^o63][^o64]

There is a large population of [Rohingya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya) refugees from [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar) near the border in the southeast. There are 28,000 living in two UN refugee camps in [Cox's Bazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar) as well as some 200,000 "unregistered people of concern" living outside of the camps.[^o65] The refugee crisis originated in the early 1990s when the first wave numbering some 250,000 of the predominantly Muslim ethnic group fled persecution from their home in Rakhaine—Myanmar's westernmost state. Bangladesh seeks to repatriate the refugees back to Myanmar.[^o66] Since [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_genocide), there are approximately 1.1 million Rohingya refugees living in Bangladesh.

## Languages {#Languages}

- Official language: [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language) (also known as Bangla)
- Other varieties that are variously considered as the dialects of Bengali: [Chittagonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagonian_language), [Noakhailla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noakhali_language), [Sylheti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylheti_language), and [Rajbongshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangpuri_language).[^o69]
- Bengali–Meitei [creole language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_language): [Bishnupriya Manipuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishnupriya_Manipuri_language)[^o70][^o71]
- Other [Indic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_languages): [Assamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_language), [Rohingya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_language), [Chakma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakma_language), [Tanchangya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanchangya_language) and various [Bihari languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihari_languages)
- [Tibeto-Burman languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman_languages): [A'Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A'Tong_language), [Chak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chak_language), [Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_language), [Garo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garo_language), [Megam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megam_language), [Tripuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokborok_language), [Meitei language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitei_language) ([Manipuri language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manipuri_language)[^o72][^o73]), [Mizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizo_language), [Mru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mru_language), [Pangkhua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangkhua_language), [Rakhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_language)/[Marma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marma_Language), various [Chin languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chin_languages)[^o69]
- [Austroasiatic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austroasiatic_languages): [Khasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khasi_language), [Koda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koda_language), [Mundari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundari_language), [Pnar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pnar_language), [Santali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santali_language), [War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_language)[^o69]
- [Dravidian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages): [Kurukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurukh_language)[^o69]
- Other languages: English *(spoken and known widely in upper-class & politics)*, Arabic *(sometimes spoken and known by many Muslims, due to Islam being the primary religion)*, [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi)/[Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) *(understood by some, and spoken by [Biharis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biharis))*

Bangladesh has 44 indigenous languages according to Professor Shameem Reza.[^o74]

According to the [Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnologue), there are 36 indigenous living languages, which include 17 [Sino-Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Tibetan), 10 [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European), 7 [Austro-Asiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Asiatic) and 2 [Dravidian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages).[^o69]

## Migration {#Migration}

### Immigration {#Immigration}

According to the [United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Department_of_Economic_and_Social_Affairs) (UNDESA), Bangladesh hosted approximately 2.9 million international migrants in 2024, representing about 1.7% of the country's population.[^o85] The majority are [Rohingya refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_people) who fled persecution and violence in neighboring Myanmar following the [Rohingya genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_genocide) and subsequent conflict in [Rakhine State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_State).[^o86]

As of 2025, Bangladesh was hosting more than 1 million Rohingya refugees, primarily in camps in [Cox's Bazar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_District), making it one of the world's largest refugee-hosting countries.[^o87]

Other migrant communities in Bangladesh include expatriate workers, diplomats, students, and business professionals from South Asia, East Asia, and the Middle East.[^o88]

### Emigration {#Emigration}

Bangladesh is one of the world's largest sources of migrant labour. According to UNDESA estimates, the Bangladeshi diaspora reached approximately 8.7 million people in 2024.[^o92] The country has a strongly negative net migration rate of approximately −4.3 migrants per 1,000 population.[^o93]

Labour migration plays a major role in the Bangladeshi economy, with remittances constituting one of the country's largest sources of foreign exchange earnings.[^o94]

The principal destinations for Bangladeshi migrants are countries in the Persian Gulf, Southeast Asia, Europe, and North America.[^o95]
