# Prime element

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prime element"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **prime element** of a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) is an object satisfying certain properties similar to the [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) in the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) and to [irreducible polynomials](wikipedia://en/188725). Care should be taken to distinguish prime elements from [irreducible elements](wikipedia://en/60693), a concept that is the same in [unique factorization domains](wikipedia://en/60546) but not the same in general.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

An element $p$ of a commutative ring $R$ is said to be **prime** if it is not the [zero element](wikipedia://en/3476379) or a [unit](wikipedia://en/289666), and for all $a, b$ in $R$, whenever $p$ [divides](wikipedia://en/32981716) $ab$, $p$ divides $a$ or $p$ divides $b$ (that is, $p\mid ab \implies p\mid a\ \lor\ p\mid b$).[^o4] With this definition, [Euclid's lemma](wikipedia://en/826617) is the assertion that [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) are prime elements in the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613). Equivalently, an element $p$ is prime if, and only if, the [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126) $(p)$ generated by $p$ is a nonzero [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928).[^o5] (In an integral domain, the ideal $(0)$ is a [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928), but $0$ is not considered to be a prime element.) *Note:* References defining primality for an element $p\in R$ often restrict $R$ to be an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) or a [Euclidean domain](wikipedia://en/10376), or may add the additional requirement that $p$ is not a zero-divisor.[^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9]

Interest in prime elements comes from the [fundamental theorem of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/11556), which asserts that each nonzero integer can be written in essentially only one way as 1 or −1 multiplied by a product of positive prime numbers. This led to the study of [unique factorization domains](wikipedia://en/60546), which generalize what was just illustrated in the integers.

Being prime is relative to which ring an element is considered to be in; for example, 2 is a prime element in $\mathbf{Z}$ but it is not in $\mathbf{Z}[i]$, the ring of [Gaussian integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_integers), since $2 = (1 + i)(1 - i)$ and 2 does not divide any factor on the right.

:::

## Connection with prime ideals {#Connection_with_prime_ideals}

An ideal $I$ in the ring $R$ (with unity) is [prime](wikipedia://en/24928) if the factor ring $R/I$ is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462). Equivalently, $I$ is prime if whenever $ab \in I$ then either $a \in I$ or $b \in I$.

A nonzero [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126) is [prime](wikipedia://en/24928) if and only if it is generated by a prime element.

## Irreducible elements {#Irreducible_elements}

Prime elements should not be confused with [irreducible elements](wikipedia://en/60693). Recall that an element $a$ of an integral domain $R$ is *irreducible* if it is not a unit and whenever $a = bc$, either $b$ or $c$ is a unit, while several non-equivalent definitions of irreducibility of varying strength exist for elements of general commutative rings (*see the main article*). In an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462), every prime is irreducible[^o12] but the converse is not true in general. However, in unique factorization domains,[^o13] or more generally in [GCD domains](wikipedia://en/2932361), primes and irreducibles are the same.

::: {#o15 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The following are examples of prime elements in rings:

- The integers $\pm2$, $\pm3$, $\pm5$, $\pm7$, $\pm11$, ... in the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) $\mathbf{Z}$
- the complex numbers $(1 + i)$, $19$, and $(2 + 3i)$ in the ring of [Gaussian integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_integers) $\mathbf{Z}[i]$
- the polynomials $x^{2} - 2$ and $x^{2} + 1$ in $\mathbf{Z}[x]$, the [ring of polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_polynomials) over $\mathbf{Z}$.
- 2 in the [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) $\mathbf{Z}/6\mathbf{Z}$
- $x^{2} + (x^{2} + x)$ is prime but not irreducible in the ring $\mathbf{Q}[x]/(x^{2} + x)$
- In the ring $\mathbf{Z}^{2}$ of pairs of integers, $(1, 0)$ is prime but not irreducible (one has $(1, 0)^{2} = (1, 0)$).
- In the [ring of algebraic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_algebraic_integers) $\mathbf Z[\sqrt{-5}],$ the element $3$ is irreducible but not prime (as 3 divides $9=(2+\sqrt{-5})(2-\sqrt{-5})$ and 3 does not divide any factor on the right).

:::
