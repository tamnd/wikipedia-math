# Wilhelm Ackermann

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wilhelm Ackermann"}

**Wilhelm Friedrich Ackermann** (; ; 29 March 1896 – 24 December 1962) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) best known for his work in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636)[^o3] and the [Ackermann function](wikipedia://en/2925), an important example in the [theory of computation](wikipedia://en/30402).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Ackermann was born in [Herscheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herscheid), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), and was awarded a Ph.D. by the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in 1925 for his thesis *Begründung des "tertium non datur" mittels der Hilbertschen Theorie der Widerspruchsfreiheit*, which was a consistency proof of arithmetic apparently without [Peano induction](wikipedia://en/25005) (although it did use e.g. induction over the length of proofs). This was one of two major works in [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478) in the 1920s and the only one following [Hilbert's](wikipedia://en/8302) school of thought.[^o3] From 1929 until 1948, he taught at the Arnoldinum Gymnasium in [Burgsteinfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinfurt), and then at [Lüdenscheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lüdenscheid) until 1961. He was also a corresponding member of the Akademie der Wissenschaften (*Academy of Sciences*) in Göttingen, and was an honorary professor at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster).

In 1928, Ackermann helped [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) turn his 1917 – 22 lectures on introductory [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) into a text, *[Principles of Mathematical Logic](wikipedia://en/2134047)*. This text contained the first exposition ever of [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983), and posed the problem of its [completeness](wikipedia://en/12450) and [decidability](wikipedia://en/913118) ([Entscheidungsproblem](wikipedia://en/9672)). Ackermann went on to construct [consistency proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistency_proof) for [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) (1937), [full arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) (1940), [type-free logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) (1952), and a new [axiomatization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory) of [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) (1956).

Later in life, Ackermann continued working as a high school teacher. He kept engaged in the field of research and published many contributions to the foundations of mathematics until the end of his life. He died in [Lüdenscheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lüdenscheid), [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) in December 1962.
