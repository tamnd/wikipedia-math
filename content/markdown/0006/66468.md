# Demographics of Afghanistan

*Image: Afghanistan Population 1950-2021 Forecast 2022-2032 UN World Population Prospects 2022.svg.* Population, [fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate) and [net reproduction rate](wikipedia://en/12983357), United Nations estimates

::: {#o9 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Afghanistan"}

As of 2025, [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan) has an estimated [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_%28human_biology%29) of around 49.5 million people.[^o10] The country is characterized by a highly diverse, [multi-ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_state), and [multilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingualism) society, reflecting its historical position at the crossroads of [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asia), [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia), and [Western Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia), along ancient trade and invasion routes. The four major ethnic categories in the country are [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns), [Tajik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajiks)/[Farsiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farsiwan), [Hazara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazaras), and [Uzbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbeks). In addition, Afghanistan has a plethora of minor ethnic categories, including [Pashayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashayi_people), [Nuristani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuristanis), [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism_in_Afghanistan), [Hindkowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindkowans), [Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmens), [Kyrgyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyz_in_Afghanistan), [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people_in_Afghanistan), [Aimaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aimaq_people), [Qizilbash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qizilbash), [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Arabs_in_Afghanistan) and many others.[^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15][^o30][^o16]

:::

Approximately 43% of the population was under the age of 15, and about 74% of Afghans lived in rural areas as of 2016.[^o17] The country has one of the highest [fertility rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) in the world outside of [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa), with the average woman giving birth to five children over her lifetime. Around 6.8% of infants die during childbirth or early infancy.[^o17] According to the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization) (WHO), the nation's average [life expectancy](wikipedia://en/18669) was estimated at about 63 years as of 2019.[^o18][^o19] The prevalence of [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV) remains very low, affecting only about 0.04% of the population.[^o30]

[Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto) (پښتو) and [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari) (دری) are the [official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_languages) of Afghanistan.[^o119] Dari serves as the inter-ethnic [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) across most of the country, particularly in northern and eastern provinces as well as in most major cities. Pashto is predominantly spoken in regions south of the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) mountains and extends eastward toward the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River) in neighbouring [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan). Smaller languages such as [Uzbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_language) and [Turkmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_language) are spoken in parts of northern Afghanistan.[^o30] Multilingualism is widespread, particularly in the major urban centres.

Up to 89.7% of the population adheres to [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) and follows the [Hanafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi) school of [Islamic jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiqh), while between 10% and 15% are followers of [Shia Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam_in_Afghanistan), with the majority belonging to the [Twelver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelver) branch, and smaller communities adhering to [Ismaili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismailism) traditions.[^o30][^o132] The remaining 0.3% of the population practices other religions, including Sikhism and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Afghanistan). Outside the principal urban centres, much of the population is organized into tribal or other kinship-based groups that maintain their own traditional customs.

## Population size and distribution {#Population_size_and_distribution}

Anatol Lieven of [Georgetown University in Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University_in_Qatar) wrote in 2021 that "it may be noted that in the whole of modern Afghan history there has never been a census that could be regarded as remotely reliable."[^o21]

### Historical {#Historical}

*Image: Afghans at Ghazi Stadium in 2011.jpg.* [Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Afghanistan) fans inside the [Ghazi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_Stadium) in the capital of [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul), which is [multi-ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiethnic_society) and the largest city of Afghanistan.

The first and only nationwide census of Afghanistan was carried out in 1979. It revealed a population of 13,051,358 (rural 11,037,231, urban 2,014,127).[^o23] Previously there had been scattered attempts to conduct censuses in individual cities.[^o24] According to the 1876 census, [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul) had a population of 140,700 people.[^o25] In [Kandahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandahar) in 1891 a population census was carried out, according to which 31,514 people lived in the city, of which 16,064 were men and 15,450 were women.[^o26]

From 1979 until the end of 1983, some 5 million people left the country to take shelter in neighbouring northwestern Pakistan and eastern Iran. This exodus was largely unchecked by any government. The Afghan government in 1983 reported a population of 15.96 million, which presumably included the exodus.[^o27]

It is assumed that roughly 600,000 to as high as 2 million Afghans may have been killed during the various 1979–2001 wars.[^o28] These figures are questionable and no attempt has ever been made to verify if they were actually killed or had moved to neighbouring countries as refugees.[^o27]

As no census has been performed after 1979 and millions of people may have left the country, the current population of Afghanistan can only be guessed.

### Current and latest {#Current_and_latest}

As of 2021, the total population of Afghanistan is around 37.5 million,[^o30][^o31] which includes the 3 million Afghan nationals living in both [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghans_in_Pakistan) and [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghans_in_Iran).[^o32] About 26% of the population is [urbanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) and the remaining 74% lives in rural areas.[^o30]

Afghanistan's [Central Statistics Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Statistics_Organization) (CSO) stated in 2011 that the total number of Afghans living inside Afghanistan was about 26 million[^o32] and by 2017 it reached 29.2 million. Of this, 15 million are males and 14.2 million are females.[^o33] The country's population is expected to reach 82 million by 2050.[^o34]

Urban areas have experienced rapid population growth in the last decade, which is due to the return of over 5 million [expats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate). The only city in Afghanistan with over a million residents is its capital, [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul).

## Religion {#Religion}

Almost the entire Afghan population is Muslim, with less than 1% being non-Muslim. Despite attempts to secularise Afghan society, Islamic practices pervade all aspects of life. Likewise, Islamic religious tradition and codes, together with traditional practices, provide the principal means of controlling personal conduct and settling legal disputes. Islam was used as the main basis for expressing opposition to the progressive reforms of Afghanistan by [King Amanullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanullah_Khan) in the 1920s.

The members of Sikh and Hindu communities are mostly concentrated in urban areas. They numbered hundreds of thousands in the 1970s but over 90% have since fled due to the Afghan wars and persecution.[^o131]

- **[Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Afghanistan)**: 99.7% of the total population
  - [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim): 84.7–89.7%[^o30][^o132]
  - [Shia Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam_in_Afghanistan): 7[^o132]-15%[^o30][^o132]
- **[others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Afghanistan#Minority_religious_groups)**: 0.3%

  - [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism): Unknown/unreported
