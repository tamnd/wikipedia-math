# I-adic topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="I-adic topology"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), the mathematical study of [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346), **adic topologies** are a family of [topologies](wikipedia://en/30450) on the underlying set of a [module](wikipedia://en/276410), generalizing the [$p$-adic topologies](wikipedia://en/51423) on the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $M$ an $R$-module. Then each [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) $\text{𝔞}$ of $R$ determines a topology on $M$ called the $\text{𝔞}$-adic topology, characterized by the [pseudometric](wikipedia://en/68503)

$$d(x,y) = 2^{-\sup{\{n \mid x-y\in\mathfrak{a}^nM\}}}.$$

The family

$$\{x+\mathfrak{a}^nM:x\in M,n\in\mathbb{Z}^+\}$$

is a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29) for this topology.[^n1]

An $\text{𝔞}$-adic topology is a [linear topology](wikipedia://en/35938255) (a topology generated by some submodules).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

With respect to the topology, the module operations of addition and scalar multiplication are [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_%28topology%29), so that $M$ becomes a [topological module](wikipedia://en/1616141). However, $M$ need not be [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637); it is Hausdorff [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922)

$$\bigcap_{n > 0}{\mathfrak{a}^nM} = 0\text{,}$$

so that $d$ becomes a genuine [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29). Related to the usual terminology in topology, where a Hausdorff space is also called separated, in that case, the $\text{𝔞}$-adic topology is called *separated*.[^n1]

By [Krull's intersection theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krull's_intersection_theorem), if $R$ is a [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) which is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) or a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391), it holds that $\bigcap_{n > 0}{\mathfrak{a}^n} = 0$ for any proper ideal $\text{𝔞}$ of $R$. Thus under these conditions, for any proper ideal $\text{𝔞}$ of $R$ and any $R$-module $M$, the $\text{𝔞}$-adic topology on $M$ is separated.

For a submodule $N$ of $M$, the [canonical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_isomorphism_theorem) [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) to $M/N$ induces a [quotient topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_topology) which coincides with the $\text{𝔞}$-adic topology. The analogous result is not necessarily true for the submodule $N$ itself: the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) need not be the $\text{𝔞}$-adic topology. However, the two topologies coincide when $R$ is Noetherian and $M$ [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430). This follows from the [Artin–Rees lemma](wikipedia://en/9553854).[^n2]

:::

## Completion {#Completion}

When $M$ is Hausdorff, $M$ can be [completed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_of_a_metric_space) as a metric space; the resulting space is denoted by $\widehat M$ and has the module structure obtained by extending the module operations by continuity. It is also the same as (or [canonically isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) to):

$$\widehat{M} = \varprojlim M/\mathfrak{a}^n M$$

where the right-hand side is an [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of [quotient modules](wikipedia://en/364820) under natural projection.[^n3]

For example, let $R = k[x_1, \ldots, x_n]$ be a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $k$ and $\text{𝔞} = (x_{1}, ..., x_{n})$ the (unique) homogeneous [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164). Then $\hat{R} = k[[x_1, \ldots, x_n]]$, the [formal power series ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_power_series_ring#Power_series_in_several_variables) over $k$ in $n$ variables.[^o11]

## Closed submodules {#Closed_submodules}

The $\text{𝔞}$-adic closure of a submodule $N \subseteq M$ is $\overline{N} = \bigcap_{n > 0}{(N + \mathfrak{a}^n M)}\text{.}$[^o13] This closure coincides with $N$ whenever $R$ is $\text{𝔞}$-adically complete and $M$ is finitely generated.[^o14]

$R$ is called [Zariski](wikipedia://en/27901866) with respect to $\text{𝔞}$ if every ideal in $R$ is $\text{𝔞}$-adically closed. There is a characterization:

$R$ is Zariski with respect to $\text{𝔞}$ if and only if $\text{𝔞}$ is contained in the [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) of $R$.

In particular a Noetherian local ring is Zariski with respect to the maximal ideal.[^o15]
