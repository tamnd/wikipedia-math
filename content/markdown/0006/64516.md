# 2

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="2"}

**2** (**two**) is a [number](wikipedia://en/21690), [numeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numeral_%28linguistics%29) and [digit](wikipedia://en/280582). It is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [1](wikipedia://en/77591639) and preceding [3](wikipedia://en/208157). It is the smallest and the only even [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666).

:::

Because it forms the basis of a [duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualistic_cosmology), it has [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion) and [spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirituality) significance in many [cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture).

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

The number 2 is the second natural number, after [1](wikipedia://en/77591639). Each natural number, including 2, is constructed by succession, that is, by adding 1 to the previous natural number.[^o4] 2 is the smallest and the only even [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666), and the first [Ramanujan prime](wikipedia://en/7011111).[^o5] It is also the first [superior highly composite number](wikipedia://en/1759451),[^o6] and the first [colossally abundant number](wikipedia://en/1759217).[^o7]

An [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) is determined to be [even](wikipedia://en/143135) if it is [divisible](wikipedia://en/53696) by two. When written in base 10, all [multiples](wikipedia://en/3912709) of 2 will end in [0](wikipedia://en/34513), 2, 4, 6, or [8](wikipedia://en/208174); more generally, in any even base, even numbers will end with an even digit.

[Binary](wikipedia://en/238686) is a number system with a [base](wikipedia://en/701207) of two, where each "[bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit)" (binary digit) is either 0 (off) or 1 (on). It is used extensively in [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer), since simple on-off logic is relatively simple to keep track of with [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics).[^o9]

A [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728) is a polygon with two sides (or [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034)) and two [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417).[^o10] In [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), digons are [degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952), collapsing to a line segment between the two vertices.[^o11] In [spherical geometry](wikipedia://en/174026), however, non-degenerate digons can exist.[^o12]

Two distinct [points](wikipedia://en/593693) in a [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) are always [sufficient](wikipedia://en/169319) to define a unique [line](wikipedia://en/946975) in a nontrivial Euclidean space.[^o13]

The integers modulo 2 form the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) $\mathbb F_2$, the smallest finite field. It has two elements, usually denoted 0 and 1, and addition in $\mathbb F_2$ corresponds to parity. Thus reduction modulo 2 records the [parity](wikipedia://en/143135) of an integer: even integers are congruent to 0 modulo 2, and odd integers are congruent to 1 modulo 2. In algebra, structures of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) 2 have special behavior because $1+1=0$; in particular, every element satisfies $x=-x$. For this reason, many algebraic constructions have separate forms in characteristic 2.[^o14]

A symmetry of order two is called an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450).

## As a word {#As_a_word}

*Two* is most commonly a [determiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_determiners) used with [plural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_number) countable nouns, as in *two days* or *I'll take these two*.[^o22] *Two* is a [noun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_nouns) when it refers to the number two as in *two plus two is four.*

The word *two* is derived from the [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) words twā ([feminine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_gender)), tū (neuter), and twēġen (masculine, which survives today in the form twain).

## Evolution of the Arabic digit {#Evolution_of_the_Arabic_digit}

*Image: Evolution2glyph.png.*

The digit used in the modern [Western world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world) to represent the number 2 traces its roots back to the Indic [Brahmic script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmic_script), where "2" was written as two horizontal lines. The modern [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_written_language) and [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_writing_system) languages (and Korean [Hanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanja)) still use this method. The [Gupta script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_script) rotated the two lines 45 degrees, making them diagonal. The top line was sometimes also shortened and had its bottom end curve towards the center of the bottom line. In the [Nagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devanagari) script, the top line was written more like a curve connecting to the bottom line. In the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) [Ghubar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghubār_numerals) writing, the bottom line was completely vertical, and the digit looked like a dotless closing question mark. Restoring the bottom line to its original horizontal position, but keeping the top line as a curve that connects to the bottom line leads to our modern digit.[^o25]

## In science {#In_science}

- The first [magic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_number_%28physics%29) - number of electrons in the innermost electron shell of an atom.[^o27]
- The chemical element with atomic number 2 is [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium).
