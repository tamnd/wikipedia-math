# Verbal subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Verbal subgroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the area of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **verbal subgroup** is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) that is [generated](wikipedia://en/99945) by all elements that can be formed by substituting group elements for variables in a given set of [words](wikipedia://en/12792326). For example, given the word $x^2$, the corresponding verbal subgroup is the group generated by all squares.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For a given group $G$ the following are examples of verbal subgroups:

- The entire group $G$, given by the word $x$.
- The [trivial subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_subgroup), given by the empty word.
- The [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847), given by the word $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$.
  - Furthermore every subgroup in the [derived series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_series) is a verbal subgroup. The $n$th term of the derived series $G^{(n)}$ is given by the set of words $\{[w,v]\mid w,v\in F^{(n-1)}\}$ where $F$ is a free group with at least as many generators as $G$.
  - Every subgroup in the [lower central series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_central_series) is a verbal subgroup. The $n$th term of the lower central series $G_{n}$ is given by the set of words $\{[w,x]\mid w\in F_{n-1}\}$ where $F$ is a free group with at least as many generators as $G$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Verbal subgroups are [fully characteristic subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fully_characteristic_subgroup).[^o6][^o7] That is they are closed under endomorphisms of the ambient group. Since all fully characteristic subgroups are [normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/21918), verbal subgroups are normal.
- In free groups, the verbal subgroups are exactly the fully characteristic subgroups.[^o6] Therefore verbal subgroups represent the generic example of fully characteristic subgroups.[^o7]
- The [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) of verbal subgroups of the countably generated free group under inclusion is [anti-isomomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-isomorphism) to the lattice of [group varieties](wikipedia://en/1021753) under inclusion.[^o6]

:::
