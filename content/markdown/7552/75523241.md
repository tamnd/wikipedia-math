# Ida May Schottenfels

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ida May Schottenfels"}

**Ida May Schottenfels** (December 21, 1869 – March 11, 1942) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and university professor.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

She was a student at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), earning a master's degree in mathematics in 1896.[^o4] After working as a schoolteacher, she joined the [New York Normal College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College) as an instructor in 1901, and by 1913, she headed the mathematics department at [Adrian College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_College) in Michigan.[^o5] She was cited as one of the most "active" women mathematicians of the time.[^o6] From 1891 to 1906 she gave 17 lectures at meetings of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and published three papers. She presented her paper "On a set of generators for certain substitution and Galois field groups" at the 1904 AMS meeting.[^o4]

## Research {#Research}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), Schottenfels was the first mathematician to prove that there exist two non-isomorphic [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600) of the same order, by demonstrating that there are two non-isomorphic simple groups of order 20,160.[^o8]
