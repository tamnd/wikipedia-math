# Plant arithmetic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Plant arithmetic"}

**Plant arithmetic** is a form of [plant intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_intelligence) whereby plants appear to perform [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) operations – a form of [number sense](wikipedia://en/4642495) in plants. Some such plants include the [Venus flytrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_flytrap) and *[Arabidopsis thaliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabidopsis_thaliana).*

:::

## Arithmetic by species {#Arithmetic_by_species}

### Venus flytrap {#Venus_flytrap}

*Image: Dionaea muscipula closing trap animation.gif.* A closing trap

The [Venus flytrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_flytrap) can count to two and five in order to trap and then digest its prey.[^o4][^o5]

The Venus flytrap is a [carnivorous plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnivorous_plant) that catches its prey with a trapping structure formed by the terminal portion of each of the plant's leaves, which is triggered by tiny hairs on their inner surfaces. A Venus flytrap's reactions can occur due to electric and mechanical, or movement-related, changes.[^o6][^o7][^o8] When an insect or spider crawling along the leaves contacts a hair, the trap prepares to close, snapping shut only if a second contact occurs within approximately twenty seconds of the first strike. The requirement of redundant triggering in this mechanism serves as a safeguard against wasting energy by trapping objects with no nutritional value, and the plant will only begin digestion after five more stimuli to ensure it has caught a live bug worthy of consumption.

There are two steps, which are a closed and locked state, that a Venus flytrap undergoes after its open state and before [digestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestion), which differ due to the formation of the trap.[^o6][^o7][^o8] A closed trap occurs when the two lobes close or catch prey.[^o6][^o7][^o8] A locked trap occurs when the cilia further trap the prey.[^o6][^o7] The trap can possess a strength of four [newtons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_%28unit%29).[^o7] In addition, the [cilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilium) can further hinder a creature's ability to escape.[^o6][^o7]

The mechanism is so highly specialized that it can distinguish between living prey and non-prey stimuli, such as falling raindrops;[^o9] two trigger hairs must be touched in succession within 20 seconds of each other or one hair touched twice in rapid succession,[^o9] whereupon the lobes of the trap will snap shut, typically in about one-tenth of a second.[^o10]

The number of days that the trap remains closed will depend on whether or not the plant has caught prey.[^o6] Furthermore, the size of the prey can affect the number of days needed for digestion.[^o6] If a creature is too small, then the Venus flytrap has the ability to release it, which means that it can start the stage of becoming semi-open.[^o6][^o7] The transition from closed to open will take two days and can result after the plant has finished digesting or determining it has not caught anything worthwhile.[^o6][^o7] One day will be needed to become semi-open, which creates a concave look, and the other day will allow the Venus flytrap to become fully open, which creates a convex look.[^o6][^o7] The angle of a Venus flytrap's lobes when they are open can be impacted by the water within it.[^o8]

### *Arabidopsis thaliana* {#Arabidopsis_thaliana}

*Image: Arabidopsis thaliana.jpg.* *[Arabidopsis thaliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabidopsis_thaliana)*

*[Arabidopsis thaliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabidopsis_thaliana)* in effect performs division to control [starch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starch) use at night.[^o12]

Most plants accumulate starch by day, then metabolize it at a fixed rate during night time. However, if the onset of darkness is unusually early, *Arabidopsis thaliana* reduces its use of starch by an amount that effectively requires division.[^o13] However, there are alternative explanations,[^o14] such as feedback control by sensing the amount of soluble sugars left.[^o15] As of 2015, open questions remain.[^o16]
