# Steffen's polyhedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Steffen's polyhedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **Steffen's polyhedron** is a [flexible polyhedron](wikipedia://en/9070022) discovered (in 1978[^o3]) by and named after Klaus Steffen. It is based on the [Bricard octahedron](wikipedia://en/18605515), but unlike the Bricard octahedron its surface does not cross itself.[^o4] It has nine vertices, 21 edges, and 14 triangular faces.[^o5] Its faces can be decomposed into three subsets: two six-triangle-patches from a Bricard octahedron, and two more triangles (the central two triangles of the net shown in the illustration) that link these patches together.[^o6]

:::

It obeys the [strong bellows conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_bellows_conjecture), meaning that (like the Bricard octahedron on which it is based) its [Dehn invariant](wikipedia://en/338051) stays constant as it flexes.[^o7]

Although it has been claimed to be the simplest possible flexible polyhedron without self-crossings,[^o5] a 2024 preprint by Gallet et al. claims to construct a simpler non-self-crossing flexible polyhedron with only eight vertices.[^o8]
