# Median follow-up

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Median follow-up"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **median follow-up** is the [median](wikipedia://en/18837) time between a specified event and the time when data on outcomes are gathered. The concept is used in [cancer survival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_survival_rates) analyses.

:::

Many [cancer studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_research) aim to assess the time between two events of interest, such as from [treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatment_of_cancer) to [remission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remission_%28medicine%29), treatment to [relapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relapse), or [diagnosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagnosis_%28medical%29) to death. This duration is generically called [survival time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_time), even if the end point is not death.[^o2]

[Time-to-event studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_to_event_analysis) must have sufficiently long follow-up durations to capture enough events to reveal meaningful patterns in the data. A short follow-up duration is appropriate for studying very severe cancers with poor [prognoses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prognosis), whereas a long follow-up duration is better suited to studying less-severe disease, or participants with good prognoses.[^o2]

Median follow-up time is included in about half the survival analyses published in cancer journals, but of those, only 31% specify the method used to compute it.[^o3]
