# Leo Sario

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Leo Sario"}

**Leo Reino Sario** (18 May 1916 – 15 August 2009) was a Finnish-born mathematician who worked on [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and [Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surfaces).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

After service as a Finnish artillery officer in the [Winter War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_War) and World War II, he received his PhD in 1948 under [Rolf Nevanlinna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Nevanlinna) at the [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki).

## Career {#Career}

Nevanlinna and Sario were founding members of the [Academy of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Finland), and there is a statue on the academy grounds named after Sario.[^o7][^o2] Sario moved to the United States in 1950 and obtained temporary positions at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179),[^o8] [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). In 1954 he became a professor at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), remaining there until his retirement in 1986.

He was the author or co-author of five major books on complex analysis and over 130 papers. He supervised 36 doctoral students, including Kōtarō Oikawa and [Burton Rodin](wikipedia://en/19417305).[^o2]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1957 he was awarded the Cross of the Commander of Finland's Order of Knighthood.[^o8] He was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) for the academic year 1957–1958.[^o10]
