# Doob–Meyer decomposition theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Doob–Meyer decomposition theorem"}

The **Doob–Meyer decomposition theorem** is a theorem in [stochastic calculus](wikipedia://en/228001) stating the conditions under which a [submartingale](wikipedia://en/274536#Submartingales_and_supermartingales) may be decomposed in a unique way as the sum of a [martingale](wikipedia://en/274536) and an [increasing](wikipedia://en/3759404) [predictable process](wikipedia://en/33414015). It is named for [Joseph L. Doob](wikipedia://en/1547599) and [Paul-André Meyer](wikipedia://en/3653083).

:::

## History {#History}

In 1953, Doob published the [Doob decomposition theorem](wikipedia://en/23360896) which gives a unique decomposition for certain discrete time martingales.[^o3] He conjectured a continuous time version of the theorem and in two publications in 1962 and 1963 [Paul-André Meyer](wikipedia://en/3653083) proved such a theorem, which became known as the Doob-Meyer decomposition.[^o4][^o5] In honor of Doob, Meyer used the term "class D" to refer to the class of supermartingales for which his unique decomposition theorem applied.[^o6]

## Class D supermartingales {#Class_D_supermartingales}

A [càdlàg](wikipedia://en/30862839) [supermartingale](wikipedia://en/274536#Submartingales_and_supermartingales) $Z$ is of Class D if $Z_0=0$ and the collection

$$\{Z_T \mid T \text{ a finite-valued stopping time} \}$$

is [uniformly integrable](wikipedia://en/9610107).[^o8]

::: {#o10 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \ge 0}, \mathbb{P})$ be a filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions (i.e. the filtration is right-continuous and complete; see [Filtration (probability theory)](wikipedia://en/57854817)). If $X = (X_t)_{t\ge0}$ is a right-continuous submartingale of class D, then there exist unique adapted processes $M$ and $A$ such that

$$X_t = M_t + A_t, \qquad t \ge 0,$$

where

- $M$ is a uniformly integrable martingale,
- $A$ is a [predictable](wikipedia://en/33414015), right-continuous, increasing process with $A_0 = 0$.

The decomposition $(M, A)$ is unique up to indistinguishability.

*Remark*. For a class D supermartingale, the process A is integrable and of finite variation on bounded intervals.[^o11]

:::
