# Minimal volume

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minimal volume"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in particular in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **minimal volume** is a number that describes one aspect of a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold)'s [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). This diffeomorphism invariant was introduced by [Mikhael Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29).

:::

Given a smooth [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $(M, g)$, one may consider its volume $\operatorname{vol}(M, g)$ and [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) $K_{g}$. The minimal volume of a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) $M$ is defined to be:

$$\operatorname{MinVol}(M):=\inf\{\operatorname{vol}(M,g) :g\text{ a complete Riemannian metric with }|K_{g}|\leq 1\}.$$

Any [closed manifold](wikipedia://en/669475) can be given an arbitrarily small volume by scaling any choice of a Riemannian metric. The minimal volume removes the possibility of such scaling by the constraint on sectional curvatures. So, if the minimal volume of $M$ is zero, then a certain kind of nontrivial [collapsing phenomena](wikipedia://en/16838918) can be exhibited by Riemannian metrics on $M$. A trivial example, the only in which the possibility of scaling is present, is a [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) [flat manifold](wikipedia://en/12756616). The [Berger spheres](wikipedia://en/11205365) show that the minimal volume of the three-dimensional [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) is also zero. Gromov has conjectured that every closed [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) odd-dimensional manifold has zero minimal volume.

By contrast, a positive lower bound for the minimal volume of $M$ amounts to some (usually nontrivial) geometric inequality for the volume of an arbitrary complete Riemannian metric on $M$ in terms of the size of its curvature. According to the [Gauss-Bonnet theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss-Bonnet_theorem), if $M$ is a [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) and connected two-dimensional manifold, then $\operatorname{MinVol}(M) = 2\pi|\chi(M)|$. The infimum in the definition of minimal volume is realized by the metrics appearing from the [uniformization theorem](wikipedia://en/288270). More generally, according to the [Chern-Gauss-Bonnet formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern-Gauss-Bonnet_formula), if $M$ is a closed and connected manifold then:

$$\operatorname{MinVol}(M)\geq c(n)\big|\chi(M)\big|.$$

Gromov, in 1982, showed that the volume of a complete Riemannian metric on a smooth manifold can always be estimated by the size of its curvature and by the [simplicial volume](wikipedia://en/11197892) of the manifold, via the inequality:

$$\operatorname{MinVol}(M)\geq\frac{\|M\|}{(n-1)^nn!}.$$
