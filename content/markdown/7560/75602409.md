# Second continuum hypothesis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Second continuum hypothesis"}

The **second continuum hypothesis**, also called **Luzin's hypothesis** or **Luzin's second continuum hypothesis**, is the hypothesis that $2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_1}$. It is the negation of [a weakened form](wikipedia://en/75603109), $2^{\aleph_0}<2^{\aleph_1}$, of the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705) (CH). It was discussed by [Nikolai Luzin](wikipedia://en/576228) in 1935, although he did not claim to be the first to postulate it.p. 157, 171p. 130-131 The statement $2^{\aleph_0}<2^{\aleph_1}$ may also be called Luzin's hypothesis.

:::

The second continuum hypothesis is independent of [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214) with the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) (ZFC): its truth is consistent with ZFC since it is true in [Cohen](wikipedia://en/22994)'s model of ZFC with the negation of the continuum hypothesis;p. 109-110 its falsity is also consistent since it is contradicted by the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705), which follows from [V=L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V=L). It is implied by [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783) together with the negation of the CH.
