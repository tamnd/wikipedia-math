# Jerzy Weyman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jerzy Weyman"}

**Jerzy Maria Weyman** (Polish pronunciation: \[ˈjɛʐɨ ˈveyman\]; born April 29, 1955)[^o3] is a [Polish-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) whose field is [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923). With over four decades of professional expertise, he has authored more than 100 publications in peer-reviewed journals and has written two books.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Weyman was born on April 29, 1955, in [Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toruń), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland), into a family with a rich history, including notable figures from [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) history and literature. His maternal great-great-grandfather, Count [Aleksander Fredro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksander_Fredro), was a poet and playwright from the era of [Polish Romanticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Romanticism), while his great-grandfather, [Piotr Szembek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Szembek), served as a military general in the 19th century.[^o5] Weyman's father's family immigrated to [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) from [Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace) in the 19th century, where the surname Weyman is common.[^o6] Both of Weyman's parents were doctors.

His interest in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) began in the 4th grade at IV LO \[High School in [Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toruń), where a teacher inspired him. Enrolling in the [Faculty of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_of_Mathematics) at [Nicolaus Copernicus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus_University) of Toruń in 1973, Weyman developed a passion for [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), particularly influenced by his mentor Tadeusz Józefiak. The same year, Weyman won a bronze medal at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) in Moscow.[^o7]

In 1977, Weyman defended his master's thesis on ideals generated by monomials. He became an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) at the [Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Institute) of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences). He later pursued graduate studies at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) in the [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US), completing his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1980 on free resolutions of determinantal ideals. Weyman returned to [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) in the early 1980s, continuing his role at the Mathematical Institute and collaborating on [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) problems. In 1982, he received the Award of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791) for Young Mathematicians, and in 1984, he became a laureate of the [Kazimierz Kuratowski Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_Kuratowski_Prize) along with Piotr Pragacz.[^o8]

In 1985, Weyman permanently moved to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), becoming an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) at [Northeastern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_University) of [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston). He rose to the rank of [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_professor) in 1995. His academic journey involved collaborations with renowned mathematicians, such as [David Buchsbaum](wikipedia://en/30781131), [David Eisenbud](wikipedia://en/3649845), [Harm Derksen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harm_Derksen), [Kiyoshi Igusa](wikipedia://en/62079633), [Marc Levine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Levine_%28mathematician%29), [Joseph Landsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Landsberg), [Gordana Todorov](wikipedia://en/67313547) and [Andrei Zelevinsky](wikipedia://en/32415867). In 2003, he published his first book, "Cohomology of Vector Bundles and Syzygies." He received the [Humboldt Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Research_Award) in 2012.[^o9]

In 2013, Weyman joined the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut) as the Stuart and Joan Sidney [Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_of_Mathematics).[^o10] He contributed to the [Commutative Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_Algebra) Special Year at [MSRI](wikipedia://en/401367) and received the title of [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland).[^o11] In 2015, he was awarded the [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122) Medal and Lecture.[^o12][^o13] In 2017, Weyman co-authored the book "Introduction to Quiver Representations." After 34 years in the U.S., he returned to [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) in 2019, joining [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University). In 2021, he received the [Stefan Banach Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Banach_Prize) from the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791) for outstanding achievements in [mathematical sciences](wikipedia://en/25750436).[^o14] Weyman continues his work at [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University), leading various research projects, most notably implementing a research grant focused on Applications of Lie algebras to Commutative Algebra, such as Research in commutative algebra and representation theory[^o15] and Structure of equivariant D-modules.[^o16]
