# Otto calculus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Otto calculus"}

The **Otto calculus** (also known as **Otto's calculus**) is a mathematical system for studying [diffusion equations](wikipedia://en/645335) that views the space of [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) as an infinite dimensional [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) by interpreting the [Wasserstein distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasserstein_distance) as if it were a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric).[^o2][^o3]

:::

It is named after [Felix Otto](wikipedia://en/4248548),[^o2] who developed it in the late 1990s and published it in a 2001 paper on the geometry of dissipative evolution equations.[^o4][^o5] Otto acknowledges inspiration from earlier work by [David Kinderlehrer](wikipedia://en/46814818) and conversations with [Robert McCann](wikipedia://en/60947361) and [Cédric Villani](wikipedia://en/27172341).[^o5]
