# Demographics of Tees Valley

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Tees Valley"}

The [Tees Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley) is an economic region and [strategic authority area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_authority_area) in the [North East of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_of_England). It had a population of approximately 678,000 as of official 2024 estimates.[^o3]

:::

Demographic data for the region is fragmented due to the history of its statistical geography. The [Teesside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesside) urban area centred on [Middlesbrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough) and [Stockton-on-Tees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton-on-Tees) has undergone several changes to local government boundaries. These changes have subsequently altered the definition of the economic region for statistical purposes. The Teesside area was in a single non-metropolitan county between 1974 and 1998, but this excluded [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlington), which is now in the Tees Valley.

## Data fragmentation {#Data_fragmentation}

Demographic data for the [Tees Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley) or [Teesside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesside) metropolitan area of [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) is recorded with differing definitions. The area's fragmented data into different area definitions every other census after 1971 has meant a lack of clear lineal correlation analysis and anachronistic data.

The first recording of the multiple towns in the area with combined statistics was the 1971 census, during the 1968–1974 [borough of Teesside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesside_%28borough%29)'s existence. By the 1981 and 1991 censuses the borough had been split into three ([Middlesbrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Middlesbrough), [Stockton-On-Tees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Stockton-on-Tees) and what is now known as [Redcar and Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcar_and_Cleveland)) with the additional [Borough of Hartlepool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Hartlepool) to form the [County of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Cleveland) for the combined statistical data of the area. For the 2001 and 2011 Censuses, the county had been abolished with an area recorded (between the size of the Teesside borough and the three replacement boroughs) as an [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) and [built-up area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Built-up_area) respectively. The former county's four boroughs and the [Borough of Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Darlington) are now recorded as the [Tees Valley Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley_Combined_Authority) mayoral area which was created in 2016 with its first census in 2021.

### Historical {#Historical}

The following information cannot be easily compared to the modern data, due to the fact that the administrative areas of the modern districts do not fully map to that of historical land divisions, meaning the population estimates for before 1981 must be taken with this context.

The [University of Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Portsmouth) mapped out historic data from 1801 to 1911, using modern [Unitary Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority) land divisions to try and create estimates for what the population for each borough was like. ## Religion, Language and Identity {#Religion,_Language_and_Identity}

### Religion {#Religion}

The religious statistics for 2021 for the [Tees Valley Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley_Combined_Authority), were released in the December 2022 statistics. For the area, the largest recorded group were [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians) (50.7%), followed by [None Religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-religious) (39.0%), the third largest was No Answer (5.2%), those identifying as [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam) came fourth (3.8%). The rest of the categories were all less than 1%, and so approximations of their total size may be inaccurate due to accuracy loss. Those identifying as [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) were the fifth largest (0.5%), followed by equal percentages for [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism), [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism) and 'Other Religion'. Those identifying as [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) recorded less than 0.1%, so the recorded number is unknown.
