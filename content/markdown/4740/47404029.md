# Trachette Jackson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trachette Jackson"}

**Trachette Levon Jackson** (born July 24, 1972) is an American mathematician who is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) and is known for work in mathematical [oncology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncology).[^o3] She uses many different approaches, including continuous and discrete mathematical models, numerical simulations, and experiments to study tumor growth and treatment. Specifically, her lab is interested in "[molecular pathways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolic_pathway) associated with intratumoral [angiogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiogenesis)," "cell-tissue interactions associated with tumor-induced angiogenesis," and "tumor [heterogeneity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterogeneity) and cancer stem cells."[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Jackson's parents were in the military and traveled frequently through her childhood; as a teenager, she lived in [Mesa, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesa,_Arizona). There, in a summer calculus course, her talent for mathematics brought her to the attention of [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University) mathematics professor [Joaquín Bustoz, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquín_Bustoz,_Jr.) She went on to undergraduate studies at ASU, originally intending to study engineering, but she was steered to mathematics by Bustoz.[^o6] From there, her interest in pure math developed into an interest in [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology) when she attended a talk by her future PhD advisor, [James D. Murray](wikipedia://en/30862391), on the mathematics of pattern formation and "how the leopard got its spots."[^o7] She graduated in 1994, and she earned her MS and PhD at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in 1996 and 1998.[^o8] After postdoctoral research at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), [Environmental Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Environmental_Protection_Agency), and [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University), she joined the University of Michigan faculty in 2000, and she was promoted to full professor in 2008.[^o10]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

She was awarded a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) in 2003,[^o12] becoming the second African-American woman after [Kathleen Adebola Okikiolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Adebola_Okikiolu) to become a Sloan Fellow in mathematics. She won a James S. McDonnell 21st Century Scientist Grant in 2005,[^o13] the [Etta Z. Falconer award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecture) in 2006[^o14], and the [Blackwell-Tapia Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwell-Tapia_Prize) in 2010.[^o15] In 2006, [^o16] In 2017, she was selected as a fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in the inaugural class.[^o17] Jackson's work also earned her recognition by [Mathematically Gifted & Black](wikipedia://en/64552006) as a Black History Month 2017 Honoree.[^o18] She was named a [SIAM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM_Fellow) in the 2021 class of fellows, "for innovative contributions to mathematical modeling in cancer biology and for the advancement of underrepresented minorities in science".[^o19] In 2021, she was awarded the University Diversity and Social Transformation Professorship at the University of Michigan,[^o20] in recognition of her "extraordinary commitment to increasing opportunities for girls, women, and underrepresented minority students in [STEM](wikipedia://en/3437663), through her teaching and leadership."[^o21]
