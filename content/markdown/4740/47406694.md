# Ester Samuel-Cahn

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ester Samuel-Cahn"}

**Ester Samuel-Cahn** (אסתר שמואל-כחן; May 16, 1933 – November 20, 2015) was an [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelis) [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and educator. She was a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Samuel-Cahn was born in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo), Norway. During the [Nazi occupation of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Norway), in 1942, her father, a [rabbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbi), was warned that he would be arrested by the [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany).[^o5] He refused to leave in order to try to support his community.[^o6] In September, her father was ordered to report to the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo) office, where he was questioned and later sent to [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp).[^o6] Later that year, the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) were going to arrest the other [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Norway) in Oslo, however, Samuel-Cahn's family were moved by members of [the underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_resistance_movement), Ingebjørg Sletten-Fosstvedt and [Sigrid Helliesen Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigrid_Helliesen_Lund), to safety and later to a refugee camp in [neutral Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_during_World_War_II).[^o5] In order to cross [the border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway–Sweden_border), Samuel-Cahn and the rest of her family had to hide in trucks used to transport potatoes.[^o7] In [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm), Samuel-Cahn's family found out that her father had been killed in Auschwitz.[^o5] In 1946, Samuel-Cahn, her mother, and brothers moved to [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) (part of which later became Israel).[^o8]

Samuel-Cahn received her Ph.D. from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1961.[^o3] From 1993 to 1995, she was the president of the Israel Statistical Association.[^o9] In 1989, she was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o10] She was also a fellow of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters) from 1989,[^o11] and of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).[^o12]

In 2004, Samuel-Cahn won the [Israel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Israel_Prize_recipients) for her work in statistics.[^o13] In 2012, she spoke at a memorial ceremony in the [Martyrs' Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_of_the_Martyrs) in Jerusalem Hills, commemorating Norwegian Jews [murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_Norway) in [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust) and remembering those who helped hide and [protect Jews in Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Norwegian_Righteous_Among_the_Nations).[^o14]

She died in November 2015.[^o15]
