# Alexander Gluhovsky

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Gluhovsky"}

**Alexander B. Gluhovsky** is an American physicist and statistician known for his contributions to [geophysical fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/28996847), particularly in the development of low-order models for atmospheric and climate phenomena, and for his work in [computational statistics](wikipedia://en/15832717) applied to [time series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series_analysis) in the [geosciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoscience). He is a professor emeritus at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) (in West Lafayette, Indiana) in the Department of Earth, Atmospheric, and Planetary Sciences, and has a joint appointment in the Department of Statistics as well.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Gluhovksy earned his Master of Science (M.S.) in mathematics and statistics from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) and received his Ph.D. in applied mathematics from the [USSR Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1973.[^o3][^o4] His doctoral advisor was the prominent Soviet physicist and applied mathematician [Alexander Obukhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Obukhov).[^o6]

Gluhovsky's association with Purdue University began in 1992 when he joined the Department of Earth and Atmospheric Sciences. In 1996 he joined the Department of Statistics as a visiting professor.[^o7] In 2007, his position was formalized with joint appointments as an associate professor in both departments.[^o7] He subsequently achieved the rank of professor and is currently a professor emeritus at the university.[^o3]

## Research contributions {#Research_contributions}

Gluhovsky's research is interdisciplinary, lying at the intersection of [nonlinear dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_dynamics), [atmospheric science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_science), and [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577).

### Low-order models (LOMs) {#Low-order_models_(LOMs)}

A major theme of his work is the formulation and analysis of [low-order models](wikipedia://en/42908722) (LOMs) for complex geophysical flows, such as those related to the atmosphere and climate.[^o10]

He proposed and analyzed LOMs, often in the form of coupled 3-mode nonlinear systems known in mechanics as [Volterra gyrostats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volterra_gyrostats) (extensions of the [Lorenz system](wikipedia://en/5642583)). The equations of the gyrostat, which describe the [motion of a rigid body](wikipedia://en/481856) containing a spinning rotor, are adapted by Gluhovsky as a basis to construct [geophysical fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/28996847) models while ensuring the [conservation of energy](wikipedia://en/67088) and other physical properties.[^o10] This approach has been applied to model phenomena such as [Rayleigh-Bénard convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayleigh-Bénard_convection) (which is related to mesoscale atomospheric dynamics) and the [El Niño-Southern Oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Niño-Southern_Oscillation) (ENSO).[^o11]

His work connects the dynamic properties of LOMs, such as the existence of [coherent structures](wikipedia://en/25077398), to observable features in turbulent flows and atmospheric datasets.[^o12]

### Subsampling methodology {#Subsampling_methodology}

Gluhovsky is a notable contributor to the field of computational statistics for time series, particularly through the use of [resampling](wikipedia://en/3763850) methods in weather and climate analysis.

He has championed the use of subsampling (a non-parametric resampling technique) to derive reliable confidence intervals and estimates for statistical parameters of highly complex and non-linear atmospheric and climate time series, where traditional parametric methods often fail due to unmet assumptions.[^o14][^o15]

His work has extended these statistical techniques to estimate higher-order moments (such as [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) and [kurtosis](wikipedia://en/16848)) for nonlinear time series, which are essential for characterizing [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather) events.[^o16]

Gluhovsky has also published research on the effects of land-use/land-cover change on surface temperature trends, employing statistical methods to separate anthropogenic local effects from larger-scale [climate forcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_forcing).
