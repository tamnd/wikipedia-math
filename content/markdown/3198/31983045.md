# Ronald Gould (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ronald Gould"}

**Ronald James Gould** (born April 15, 1950) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specializing in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). He is a Goodrich C. White professor emeritus in the [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University) Department of Mathematics.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

After attending [SUNY Fredonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUNY_Fredonia) for his undergraduate degree, Gould received his Ph.D. in 1979 from [Western Michigan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Michigan_University). His thesis was titled *Traceability in Graphs*, and was completed under the supervision of [Gary Chartrand](wikipedia://en/22782409).[^o5] He spent a short period as a lecturer at [San Jose State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_State_University) in 1978 and 1979, then moved to [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University) in 1979. He was named to the Goodrich C. White professorship in 2001, and retired in 2016.[^o6]

Gould is most noted for his work in the area of [Hamiltonian graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph) theory.[^o7] He is the author of the book *Mathematics in Games, Sports, and Gambling: The Games People Play* (2010).[^o8]
