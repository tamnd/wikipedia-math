# Square trisection

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Square trisection"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **square trisection** is a type of [dissection problem](wikipedia://en/2915839) which consists of [cutting](wikipedia://en/48191322) a [square](wikipedia://en/659939) into pieces that can be rearranged to form three identical squares.

:::

*Image: Trisection Blanvillain.png.* Square trisection using 6 pieces of same area (2010).

## History {#History}

The [dissection](wikipedia://en/2915839) of a square in three [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [partitions](wikipedia://en/340240) is a geometrical problem that dates back to the [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age). Craftsman who mastered the art of [zellige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zellige) needed innovative techniques to achieve their fabulous mosaics with complex geometric figures. The first solution to this problem was proposed in the 10th century AD by the Persian mathematician [Abu'l-Wafa'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul_Wafa) (940-998) in his treatise *"On the geometric constructions necessary for the artisan"*.[^o3] [Abu'l-Wafa'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul_Wafa) also used his dissection to demonstrate the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034).[^o4] This geometrical proof of Pythagoras' theorem would be rediscovered in the years 1835 - 1840 [^o5] by [Henry Perigal](wikipedia://en/33379864) and published in 1875.[^o6]

## Search of optimality {#Search_of_optimality}

The beauty of a dissection depends on several parameters. However, it is usual to search for solutions with the minimum number of parts. Far from being minimal, the square trisection proposed by [Abu'l-Wafa'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul_Wafa) uses 9 pieces. In the 14th century [Abu Bakr al-Khalil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_al-Khalil) gave two solutions, one of which uses 8 pieces.[^o8] In the late 17th century [Jacques Ozanam](wikipedia://en/1120947) came back to this issue [^o9] and in the 19th century, solutions using 8 and 7 pieces were found, including one given by the mathematician [Édouard Lucas](wikipedia://en/150102).[^o10] In 1891 [Henry Perigal](wikipedia://en/33379864) published the first known solution with only 6 pieces [^o11] (see illustration below). Nowadays, new dissections are still found [^o12] (see illustration above) and the conjecture that 6 is the minimal number of necessary pieces remains unproved.
