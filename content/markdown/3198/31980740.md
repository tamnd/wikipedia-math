# Stanley symmetric function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stanley symmetric function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and especially in [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582), the **Stanley symmetric functions** are a family of [symmetric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_polynomials) introduced by (txt) in his study of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) of [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027).

:::

Formally, the Stanley symmetric function *F*<sub>*w*</sub>(*x*<sub>1</sub>, *x*<sub>2</sub>, ...) indexed by a permutation *w* is defined as a sum of certain [fundamental quasisymmetric functions](wikipedia://en/29661763#Important_bases). Each summand corresponds to a reduced decomposition of *w*, that is, to a way of writing *w* as a product of a minimal possible number of [adjacent transpositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transposition_%28mathematics%29). They were introduced in the course of Stanley's enumeration of the reduced decompositions of permutations, and in particular his proof that the permutation *w*<sub>0</sub> = *n*(*n* − 1)...21 (written here in [one-line notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-line_notation)) has exactly

$$\frac{\binom{n}{2}! }{1^{n - 1} \cdot 3^{n - 2} \cdot 5^{n - 3} \cdots (2n - 3)^1}$$

reduced decompositions. (Here $\binom{n}{2}$ denotes the [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668) *n*(*n* − 1)/2 and ! denotes the [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606).)

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The Stanley symmetric function *F*<sub>*w*</sub> is [homogeneous](wikipedia://en/1698977) with [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) equal to the number of [inversions](wikipedia://en/21473801) of *w*. Unlike other nice families of symmetric functions, the Stanley symmetric functions have many linear dependencies and so do not form a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) of the [ring of symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/364460). When a Stanley symmetric function is expanded in the basis of [Schur functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur_polynomials), the coefficients are all [non-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative) [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).

The Stanley symmetric functions have the property that they are the stable limit of [Schubert polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schubert_polynomials)

$$F_w(x) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \mathfrak{S}_{1^n \times w}(x)$$

where we treat both sides as formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958), and take the limit coefficientwise.
