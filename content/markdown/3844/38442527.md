# Rudolf Ernest Langer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rudolf Ernest Langer"}

**Rudolf Ernest Langer** or **Rudolph Ernest Langer**[^o3] (8 March 1894 – 11 March 1968) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).[^o4] He is known for the [Langer correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langer_correction) in [semiclassical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiclassical_physics).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Langer, the elder brother of [William L. Langer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_L._Langer) and [Walter Charles Langer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Charles_Langer), earned his PhD in 1922 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) under [G. D. Birkhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._D._Birkhoff). He taught mathematics at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College) from 1922 to 1925. From 1927 to 1964 he was a mathematics professor at the [University of Wisconsin-Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin-Madison) and, from 1942 to 1952, the chair of the mathematics department.[^o4] From 1956 to 1963 he was the director of the [Army Mathematics Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_Hall_bombing#Army_Mathematics_Research_Center); he was succeeded as director by [J. Barkley Rosser](wikipedia://en/679224).[^o6] Langer's doctoral students include [Nicholas D. Kazarinoff](wikipedia://en/76230827), [Homer Newell, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer_E._Newell,_Jr.), and [Henry Scheffé](wikipedia://en/25306204).

Langer was a colleague of American physicist [Carl David Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_David_Anderson), discoverer of the [positron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron), and was one of the few people to have read [Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac)’s work on the anti-electron and made a connection. He sent a short paper to *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_%28journal%29)* making connections between the new observations and Dirac’s theories, putting forth imaginative claims such as that the proton is made of a neutron and a positron. His paper was not taken seriously.[^o7]
