# Subordinator (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Subordinator"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **subordinator** is a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) that is [non-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative) and whose increments are [stationary](wikipedia://en/22497977) and [independent](wikipedia://en/877315).[^o12] Subordinators are a special class of [Lévy process](wikipedia://en/1025748) that play an important role in the theory of [local time](wikipedia://en/5678101).[^o13] In this context, subordinators describe the evolution of time within another stochastic process, the subordinated stochastic process. In other words, a subordinator will determine the [random](wikipedia://en/25685) number of "time steps" that occur within the subordinated process for a given unit of chronological time.

:::

In order to be a subordinator a process must be a [Lévy process](wikipedia://en/1025748)[^o2]. It also must be increasing, [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908),[^o2] or an [additive process](wikipedia://en/64384314).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A subordinator is a [real-valued stochastic process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-valued_stochastic_process) $X=(X_t)_{t \geq 0}$ that is a [non-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative) and a [Lévy process](wikipedia://en/1025748).[^o12] Subordinators are the stochastic processes $X=(X_t)_{t \geq 0}$ that have all of the following properties:

- $X_0=0$ [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908)
- $X$ is non-negative, meaning $X_t \geq 0$ for all $t$
- $X$ has [stationary increments](wikipedia://en/22497977), meaning that for $t \geq 0$ and $h > 0$, the distribution of the random variable $Y_{t,h}:=X_{t+h} - X_t$ depends only on $h$ and not on $t$
- $X$ has [independent increments](wikipedia://en/877315), meaning that for all $n$ and all $t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_n$ , the random variables $(Y_i)_{i=0, \dots, n-1}$ defined by $Y_i=X_{t_{i+1}}-X_{t_{i}}$ are [independent](wikipedia://en/27593) of each other
- The paths of $X$ are [càdlàg](wikipedia://en/30862839), meaning they are continuous from the right everywhere and the limits from the left exist everywhere

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [variance gamma process](wikipedia://en/20809590) can be described as a [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) subject to a [gamma subordinator](wikipedia://en/4183150).[^o2] If a [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/149984), $W(t)$, with drift $\theta t$ is subjected to a random time change which follows a [gamma process](wikipedia://en/4183150), $\Gamma(t; 1, \nu)$, the variance gamma process will follow:

$$X^{VG}(t; \sigma, \nu, \theta) \;:=\; \theta \,\Gamma(t; 1, \nu) + \sigma\,W(\Gamma(t; 1, \nu)).$$

The [Cauchy process](wikipedia://en/38454328) can be described as a [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) subject to a [Lévy subordinator](wikipedia://en/1346871).[^o2]

:::

## Representation {#Representation}

Every subordinator $X=(X_t)_{t \geq 0}$ can be written as

$$X_t = at + \int_0^t \int_0^\infty x \; \Theta( \mathrm ds \; \mathrm dx )$$

where

- $a \geq 0$ is a scalar and
- $\Theta$ is a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) on $(0, \infty) \times (0, \infty)$ with [intensity measure](wikipedia://en/56214154) $\operatorname E \Theta = \lambda \otimes \mu$. Here $\mu$ is a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) on $(0, \infty )$ with $\int_0^\infty \max(x,1) \; \mu (\mathrm dx) < \infty$, and $\lambda$ is the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198).

The measure $\mu$ is called the [Lévy measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lévy_measure) of the subordinator, and the pair $(a, \mu)$ is called the characteristics of the subordinator.

Conversely, any scalar $a \geq 0$ and measure $\mu$ on $(0, \infty)$ with $\int \max(x,1) \; \mu (\mathrm dx) < \infty$ define a subordinator with characteristics $(a, \mu)$ by the above relation.[^o11][^o12]
