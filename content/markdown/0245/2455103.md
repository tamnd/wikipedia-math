# 3-torus

*Image: 3-Manifold 3-Torus.png.* A view from inside a 3-torus. All of the cubes in the image are the same cube, since light in the manifold wraps around into closed loops.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="3-torus"}

The **three-dimensional torus**, or **3-torus**, is defined as any topological space that is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of three circles, $\mathbb{T}^3 = S^1 \times S^1 \times S^1.$ In contrast, the usual [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) is the Cartesian product of only two circles.

:::

## Description {#Description}

The 3-torus is a three-dimensional [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) with no [boundary](wikipedia://en/2073470#Manifold_with_boundary). It can be obtained by "gluing" the three pairs of opposite faces of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), where being "glued" can be intuitively understood to mean that when a particle moving in the interior of the cube reaches a point on a face, it goes through it and appears to come forth from the corresponding point on the opposite face, producing [periodic boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/9667552). Gluing only one pair of opposite faces produces a [solid torus](wikipedia://en/1251473) while gluing two of these pairs produces the solid space between two nested tori.

## Usage {#Usage}

In 1984, [Alexei Starobinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Starobinsky) and [Yakov Zeldovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakov_Zeldovich) at the [Landau Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau_Institute_for_Theoretical_Physics) in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) proposed a [cosmological model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_model) where the [shape of the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_of_the_universe) is a 3-torus.[^o4]
