# Gino Fano

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Gino Fano"}

**Gino Fano** (5 January 1871 – 8 November 1952) was an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), best known as the founder of [finite geometry](wikipedia://en/267061). He was born to a wealthy [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish)[^o4] family in [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantua), in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) and died in [Verona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verona), also in Italy.

:::

Fano made various contributions to [projective](wikipedia://en/243849) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). His work in the [foundations of geometry](wikipedia://en/21317755) predates the similar, but more popular, work of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) by about a decade.

He was the father of [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) [Ugo Fano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugo_Fano) and electrical engineer [Robert Fano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fano) and uncle to physicist and mathematician [Giulio Racah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Racah).

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Fano was an early writer in the area of finite projective spaces. In his article[^o6] on proving the independence of his set of axioms for [projective *n*-space](wikipedia://en/242135),[^o7] among other things, he considered the consequences of having a [fourth harmonic point](wikipedia://en/16094600) be equal to its conjugate. This leads to a configuration of seven points and seven lines contained in a [finite three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/59836267) with 15 points, 35 lines and 15 planes, in which each line contained only three points.[^o6]

All the planes in this space consist of seven points and seven lines and are now known as [Fano planes](wikipedia://en/390404):

*Image: Fano plane.svg.* Fano Plane (7 points and 7 lines)

Fano went on to describe finite projective spaces of arbitrary dimension and prime orders.

In 1907, Gino Fano contributed two articles to Part III of [Klein's encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein's_encyclopedia). The first (SS. 221–88) was a comparison of [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202) and [synthetic geometry](wikipedia://en/267484) through their historic development in the 19th century. The second (SS. 282–388) was on [continuous groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_group) in geometry and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) as a unifying principle in geometry.[^o8]

## World War II {#World_War_II}

Fano lost his university position in Italy in November 1938 due to the [Racial Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_racial_laws) promulgated by [Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini)'s government. He moved to [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne) in [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) and remained there until 1945; though he gave some lectures during this period, he did not hold a formal university position in Switzerland.[^o10]
