# Cesare Burali-Forti

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Cesare Burali-Forti"}

**Cesare Burali-Forti** (13 August 1861 – 21 January 1931) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), after whom the [Burali-Forti paradox](wikipedia://en/51653) is named.[^o3] He was a prolific writer, with 200 publications.[^o4]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Burali-Forti was born in [Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arezzo), and he obtained his degree from the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa) in 1884.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

In 1886, after two years of middle-school service in Sicily, Burali-Forti won a competition to become professor of analytic and projective geometry at the military academy in Turin.[^o8] He was an assistant of [Giuseppe Peano](wikipedia://en/13043) in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin) from 1894 to 1896, during which time he discovered a theorem which [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163) later realised contradicted a previously proved result by [Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216). The contradiction came to be known as the [Burali-Forti paradox](wikipedia://en/51653) of Cantorian [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553).

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

He married Gemma Viviani on 29 October 1887 and they had a son named Umberto.[^o3]

He died in Turin on 21 January 1931.
