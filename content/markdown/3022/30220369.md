# Amin Shokrollahi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Amin Shokrollahi"}

**Amin Shokrollahi** (born 1964) is a German-Iranian mathematician who has worked on a variety of topics including [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869) and algebraic [complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543). He is best known for his work on iterative decoding of graph based [codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_error_correction_code) for which he received the IEEE Information Theory Paper Award of 2002 (together with [Michael Luby](wikipedia://en/9552275), [Michael Mitzenmacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Mitzenmacher), and [Daniel Spielman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Spielman), as well as Tom Richardson and Ruediger Urbanke).[^o3] He is one of the inventors of a modern class of practical [erasure codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasure_code) known as [tornado codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_code),[^o4] and the principal developer of [raptor codes](wikipedia://en/3340191),[^o5] which belong to a class of rateless erasure codes known as [Fountain codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain_code). In connection with the work on these codes, he received the [IEEE Eric E. Sumner Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Eric_E._Sumner_Award) in 2007 together with Michael Luby "for bridging mathematics, Internet design and mobile broadcasting as well as successful standardization"[^o6] and the [IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Richard_W._Hamming_Medal) in 2012 together with Michael Luby "for the conception, development, and analysis of practical rateless codes".[^o7] He also received the 2007 joint Communication Society and Information Theory Society best paper award [^o8] as well as the 2017 [Mustafa Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Prize)[^o9] for his work on raptor codes.

:::

He is the principal inventor of Chordal Codes, a new class of codes specifically designed for communication on electrical wires between chips. In 2011 he founded the company [Kandou Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandou_Bus) dedicated to commercialization of the concept of Chordal Codes. The first implementation, transmitting data on 8 correlated wires and implemented in a 40 nm process, received the Jan Van Vessem Award for best European Paper at the [International Solid-State Circuits Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Solid-State_Circuits_Conference) (ISSCC) 2014.
