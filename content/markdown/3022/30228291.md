# Sanford L. Segal

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Sanford L. Segal"}

**Sanford Leonard Segal** (1937-10-11 – 2010) was a mathematician and historian of science and mathematics at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester). Mathematically he specialized in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759). He wrote the textbook *Nine Introductions in Complex Analysis* (1981), and the tome *Mathematicians Under the Nazis* (2003), a historical recount from that period. He also taught courses in [women's studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_studies), and [nuclear arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_arms). He was on the Committee of Actuarial Studies at the University of Rochester.[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

In 1937 he was born into a conservative Jewish family.[^o5]

In 1958, he received his B.A. degree from [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University) with Honors in Mathematics and High Honors in Classical Civilization studies.[^o6]

In 1959 he spent a year as a [Fulbright student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) in [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz), Germany.

In 1963, he earned his Ph.D. in Mathematics at [University of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado) under the supervision of [Sarvadaman D. S. Chowla](wikipedia://en/292142) with the dissertation entitled *The Error Term in the Formula for the Average Value of the Euler Phi Function*.[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

After his Ph.D., he worked at the University of Rochester for 44 years until his retirement in 2008. In 1965 he received a grant from the [Fulbright Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) as a research fellow in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), Austria. And in 1977, he received a grant from the [National Institute for Pure and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2334708) to teach in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro).[^o6][^o10]

In 1981 he published *Nine Introductions in Complex Analysis* with North Holland Press (a revised edition was published in 2011 by Elsevier, which had taken over North Holland Press). He later received a grant from [The Alexander von Humboldt Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation) to research history of science in [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany). [Princeton University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University_Press) later published the book *Mathematicians Under the Nazis* in 2003,[^o6] which addresses the experience of mathematics academics in Nazi Germany. The book involved a lot of direct research and interviews with survivors and translations from German.

He translated from French the book *History of Mathematics: Highways and Byways* in 2009. In addition, Segal published more than 45 papers on mathematics, [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471), and the [history of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science).

He was a member of the [Religious Society of Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Society_of_Friends). He was also a member of [Sigma Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Xi) and of [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa).

He married Rima Maxwell and had three children, Adam, Joshua, and Zoë.

He died on May 7, 2010.[^o11]
