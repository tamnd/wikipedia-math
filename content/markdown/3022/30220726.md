# Quasisymmetric map

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasisymmetric map"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **quasisymmetric** [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) between metric spaces is a map that generalizes [bi-Lipschitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_function) maps. While bi-Lipschitz maps shrink or expand the diameter of a set by no more than a multiplicative factor, quasisymmetric maps satisfy the weaker geometric property that they preserve the relative sizes of sets: if two sets *A* and *B* have diameters *t* and are no more than distance *t* apart, then the ratio of their sizes changes by no more than a multiplicative constant. These maps are also related to [quasiconformal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiconformal) maps, since in many circumstances they are in fact equivalent.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $(X,d_X)$ and $(Y,d_Y)$ be two [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018). A [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) $f\colon X\to Y$ is said to be **η-quasisymmetric** for some increasing function $\eta:[0,+\infty) \to [0,+\infty)$ if for any triple $x,y,z$ of distinct points in $X$, we have:

$$\frac{d_Y(f(x),f(y))}{d_{Y}(f(x),f(z))} \leq \eta\left(\frac{d_X(x,y)}{d_X(x,z)}\right).$$

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Basic properties"}

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

**Inverses are quasisymmetric**

If $f\colon X\to Y$ is an invertible *η*-quasisymmetric map as above, then its inverse map is $\eta'$-quasisymmetric, where $\eta'(t) = 1/\eta^{-1}(1/t).$

**Quasisymmetric maps preserve relative sizes of sets**

If $A$ and $B$ are subsets of $X$ and $A$ is a subset of $B$, then

$$\frac{1}{2}\eta^{-1}\left(\frac{\operatorname{diam} B}{\operatorname{diam} A}\right)\leq \frac{\operatorname{diam}f(B)}{\operatorname{diam}f(A)}\leq 2\eta\left(\frac{\operatorname{diam} B}{\operatorname{diam}A}\right).$$

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Weakly quasisymmetric maps {#Weakly_quasisymmetric_maps}

A map $f\colon X\to Y$ is said to be **H-weakly-quasisymmetric** for some $H>0$ if for all triples of distinct points $x,y,z$ in $X$, then

$$|f(x)-f(y)|\leq H|f(x)-f(z)|\;\;\;\text{ whenever }\;\;\; |x-y|\leq |x-z|$$

Not all weakly quasisymmetric maps are quasisymmetric. However, if $X$ is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) and $X$ and $Y$ are [doubling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubling_measures_and_metric_spaces), then all weakly quasisymmetric maps are quasisymmetric. The appeal of this result is that proving weak-quasisymmetry is much easier than proving quasisymmetry directly, and in many natural settings the two notions are equivalent.

### δ-monotone maps {#δ-monotone_maps}

A [monotone map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotone_map#Monotonicity_in_functional_analysis) $f\colon H\to H$ on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $H$ is **δ-monotone** if for all $x,y$ in $H$,

$$\langle f(x)-f(y),x-y\rangle\geq \delta |f(x)-f(y)|\cdot|x-y|.$$

To grasp what this condition means geometrically, suppose $f(0)=0$ and consider the above estimate when $y=0$. Then it implies that the angle between the vector $x$ and its image $f(x)$ stays between 0 and $\arccos(\delta)<\pi/2$.

These maps are quasisymmetric, although they are a much narrower subclass of quasisymmetric maps. For example, while a general quasisymmetric map in the complex plane could map the real line to a set of [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) strictly greater than one, a *δ*-monotone will always map the real line to a rotated [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of a Lipschitz function $L:\mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$.[^o11]

:::

## Doubling measures {#Doubling_measures}

### The real line {#The_real_line}

Quasisymmetric homeomorphisms of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) to itself can be characterized in terms of their derivatives.[^o14] An increasing homeomorphism $f:\mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is quasisymmetric if and only if there is a constant $C>0$ and a [doubling measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubling_measures_and_metric_spaces) *μ* on the real line such that

$$f(x)=C+\int_0^x \, d\mu(t).$$

### Euclidean space {#Euclidean_space}

An analogous result holds in Euclidean space. Suppose $C=0$ and we rewrite the above equation for $f$ as

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{x-t}{|x-t|}+\frac{t}{|t|}\right)d\mu(t).$$

Writing it this way, we can attempt to define a map using this same integral, but instead integrate (what is now a vector valued integrand) over $\mathbb{R}^n$: if *μ* is a doubling measure on $\mathbb{R}^n$ and

$$\int_{|x|>1}\frac{1}{|x|}\,d\mu(x)<\infty$$

then the map

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}+\frac{y}{|y|}\right)\,d\mu(y)$$

is quasisymmetric (in fact, it is *δ*-monotone for some *δ* depending on the measure *μ*).[^o16]

## Quasisymmetry and quasiconformality in Euclidean space {#Quasisymmetry_and_quasiconformality_in_Euclidean_space}

Let $\Omega$ and $\Omega'$ be open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$. If $f\colon \Omega\to\Omega'$ is *η*-quasisymmetric, then it is also *K*-[quasiconformal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiconformal), where $K>0$ is a constant depending on $\eta$.

Conversely, if $f\colon \Omega\to\Omega'$ is *K*-quasiconformal and $B(x,2r)$ is contained in $\Omega$, then $f$ is *η*-quasisymmetric on $B(x,2r)$, where $\eta$ depends only on $K$.

## Quasi-Möbius maps {#Quasi-Möbius_maps}

A related but weaker condition is the notion of quasi-Möbius maps where instead of the ratio only the cross-ratio is considered:[^o19]

::: {#o21 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

Let $(X,d_X)$ and $(Y,d_Y)$ be two [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018) and let $\eta:[0,+\infty) \to [0,+\infty)$ be an increasing function. An ***η*-quasi-Möbius** [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) $f\colon X\to Y$ is a homeomorphism such that for every quadruple $x,y,z,t$ of distinct points in $X$, we have

$$\frac{d_Y(f(x),f(z))d_Y(f(y),f(t))}{d_Y(f(x),f(y))d_Y(f(z),f(t))} \leq \eta\left(\frac{d_X(x,z)d_X(y,t)}{d_X(x,y)d_X(z,t)}\right).$$

:::
