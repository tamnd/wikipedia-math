# Ring of mixed characteristic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ring of mixed characteristic"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), a **ring of mixed characteristic** is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $R$ having [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero and having an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) $I$ such that $R/I$ has positive characteristic.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $\mathbb{Z}$ have characteristic zero, but for any [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$, $\mathbb{F}_p=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) with $p$ elements and hence has characteristic $p$.
- The [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of any [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) is of mixed characteristic
- Fix a prime *p* and [localize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) the integers at the [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) (*p*). The resulting ring **Z**<sub>(*p*)</sub> has characteristic zero. It has a unique [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) *p***Z**<sub>(*p*)</sub>, and the [quotient](wikipedia://en/184635) **Z**<sub>(*p*)</sub>/*p***Z**<sub>(*p*)</sub> is a finite field with *p* elements. In contrast to the previous example, the only possible characteristics for rings of the form **Z**<sub>(*p*)</sub> /*I* are zero (when *I* is the [zero ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_ideal)) and powers of *p* (when *I* is any other non-unit ideal); it is not possible to have a quotient of any other characteristic.
- If $P$ is a non-zero prime ideal of the ring $\mathcal{O}_K$ of integers of a number field $K$, then the [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) of $\mathcal{O}_K$ at $P$ is likewise of mixed characteristic.
- The [*p*-adic integers](wikipedia://en/51423) **Z**<sub>*p*</sub> for any prime *p* are a ring of characteristic zero. However, they have an ideal generated by the [image](wikipedia://en/579311#Image_of_an_element) of the prime number *p* under the canonical map **Z** → **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>. The quotient **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>/*p***Z**<sub>*p*</sub> is again the finite field of *p* elements. **Z**<sub>*p*</sub> is an example of a [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28ring_theory%29) [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633) of mixed characteristic.

:::
