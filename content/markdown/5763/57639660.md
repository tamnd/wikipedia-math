# Jennifer McLoud-Mann

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jennifer McLoud-Mann"}

**Jennifer McLoud-Mann** is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) mathematician known for her 2015 discovery, with [Casey Mann](wikipedia://en/62481104) and undergraduate student David Von Derau, of the 15th and last class of [convex](wikipedia://en/660019) [pentagons](wikipedia://en/20741014) to [tile the plane](wikipedia://en/4850275). She is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Washington Bothell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington_Bothell), where she is currently the Vice Dean of Curriculum & Instruction of the School of STEM. Beyond tiling, her research interests include [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), [tiling theory](wikipedia://en/321671), and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

McLoud-Mann grew up in [Weleetka, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weleetka,_Oklahoma). As a member of the [Cherokee Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee_Nation),[^o5] she participated in the Oklahoma Louis Stokes Alliance for Minority Participation and McNair Scholars at [East Central University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Central_University).[^o6] She graduated from East Central University in 1997 with a B.S. degree in Mathematics. She is the first person in her family to earn a bachelor's degree.[^o6] She completed a M.S. in Mathematics at the [University of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas) in 1998.

McLoud-Mann completed her Ph.D. in 2002 from the [University of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas). Her dissertation in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), supervised by Mark Ray Johnson, was titled *On a Certain Family of Determinantal-Like Ideals*.

## Career {#Career}

Upon completing her doctorate, McLoud-Mann joined the [University of Texas at Tyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Tyler) faculty. In addition, she was associate dean of arts and sciences from 2009 to 2013.

In 2013, she moved to the [University of Washington Bothell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington_Bothell) where she chaired the School of STEM's Engineering & Mathematics division for three years. In September 2020, she became the Associate Dean of Curriculum & Instruction for the School of STEM.

After two years of research, McLoud-Mann and research co-director [Casey Mann](wikipedia://en/62481104) found the 15th kind of pentagon that can tile a plane.[^o8][^o9] This discovery was facilitated by undergraduate researcher David Von Derau, who automated an algorithm developed by McLoud-Mann and Mann.[^o8] It was the first tile discovery in 30 years.[^o10]

## Awards {#Awards}

McLoud-Mann won the Henry L. Alder Award for Distinguished Teaching by a Beginning College or University Mathematics Faculty Member of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) in 2008. In 2016, she was the recipient of the Distinguished Research, Scholarship, and Creative Activity award, which recognizes scholarly or creative achievement exemplifying the research-intensive education environment of the [University of Washington Bothell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington_Bothell).[^o12]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

She was the first in her family to obtain a college degree. She is married to [Casey Mann](wikipedia://en/62481104). [^o9]
