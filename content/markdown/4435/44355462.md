# Chordal bipartite graph

*Image: Chordal bipartite revised 2.svg.* Every [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) of length at least 6 has a chord connecting two vertices that are a distance > 1 apart from each other in the cycle.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chordal bipartite graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) area of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **chordal bipartite graph** is a [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) *B* = (*X*,*Y*,*E*) in which every [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) of length at least 6 in *B* has a *chord*, i.e., an edge that connects two vertices that are a distance > 1 apart from each other in the cycle. [^o2] A better name would be weakly chordal and bipartite since chordal bipartite graphs are in general not [chordal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chordal) as the induced cycle of length 4 shows.

:::

## Characterizations {#Characterizations}

Chordal bipartite graphs have various characterizations in terms of [perfect elimination orderings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_elimination_ordering), [hypergraphs](wikipedia://en/232535) and [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859). They are closely related to [strongly chordal graphs](wikipedia://en/24809733). By definition, chordal bipartite graphs have a [forbidden subgraph characterization](wikipedia://en/13547826) as the graphs that do not contain any [induced cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_cycle) of length 3 or of length at least 5 (so-called holes) as an [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188). Thus, a graph *G* is chordal bipartite if and only if *G* is [triangle-free](wikipedia://en/7667996) and hole-free. In (Golumbic 1980), two other characterizations are mentioned: *B* is chordal bipartite if and only if every minimal edge separator induces a complete bipartite subgraph in *B* if and only if every induced subgraph is perfect elimination bipartite.

Martin Farber has shown: A graph is strongly chordal if and only if the bipartite incidence graph of its clique hypergraph is chordal bipartite. [^o6]

A similar characterization holds for the closed neighborhood hypergraph: A graph is strongly chordal if and only if the bipartite incidence graph of its closed neighborhood hypergraph is chordal bipartite.[^o7]

Another result found by Elias Dahlhaus is: A bipartite graph *B* = (*X*,*Y*,*E*) is chordal bipartite if and only if the [split graph](wikipedia://en/9928681) resulting from making *X* a clique is strongly chordal.[^o8]

A bipartite graph *B* = (*X*,*Y*,*E*) is chordal bipartite if and only if every induced subgraph of *B* has a maximum *X*-neighborhood ordering and a maximum Y-neighborhood ordering.[^o9]

Various results describe the relationship between chordal bipartite graphs and totally balanced neighborhood hypergraphs of bipartite graphs. [^o10]

A characterization of chordal bipartite graphs in terms of intersection graphs related to hypergraphs is given in.[^o11]

A bipartite graph is chordal bipartite if and only if its [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) is [totally balanced](wikipedia://en/27803326) if and only if the adjacency matrix is [Gamma-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma-free_Matrix). [^o12]

:::

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Chordal bipartite graphs can be recognized in time O(min(*n*<sup>2</sup>, (*n* + *m*) log *n*)) for a graph with *n* vertices and *m* edges.[^o14]

## Complexity of problems {#Complexity_of_problems}

Various problems such as Hamiltonian cycle,[^o16] Steiner tree [^o17] and Efficient Domination [^o18] remain NP-complete on chordal bipartite graphs.

Various other problems which can be solved efficiently for bipartite graphs can be solved more efficiently for chordal bipartite graphs as discussed in [^o19]

## Related graph classes {#Related_graph_classes}

Every chordal bipartite graph is a [modular graph](wikipedia://en/51807881). The chordal bipartite graphs include the [complete bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/525320) and the bipartite [distance-hereditary graphs](wikipedia://en/17315337).[^o21]
