# Phantom map

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Phantom map"}

In [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), **phantom maps** are continuous maps $f: X \to Y$ of [CW-complexes](wikipedia://en/353022) for which the restriction of $f$ to any finite subcomplex $Z \subset X$ is inessential (i.e., [nullhomotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullhomotopic)). (txt) produced the first known nontrivial example of such a map with $X$ finite-dimensional (answering a question of [Paul Olum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Olum)). Shortly thereafter, the terminology of "phantom map" was coined by (txt), who constructed a stably essential phantom map from infinite-dimensional [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977) to $S^3$.[^o4] The subject was analysed in the thesis of Gray, much of which was elaborated and later published in (). Similar constructions are defined for maps of [spectra](wikipedia://en/1033697).[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\alpha$ be a [regular cardinal](wikipedia://en/374128). A morphism $f: x \to y$ in the [homotopy category](wikipedia://en/2040756) of [spectra](wikipedia://en/1033697) is called an $\alpha$-phantom map if, for any spectrum s with fewer than $\alpha$ cells, any composite $s \to x \xrightarrow{f} y$ vanishes.[^o9]

:::
