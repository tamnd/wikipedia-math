# James Stark (statistician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Stark"}

**James Stark of Huntfield** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) [FRCPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRCPE) [FSSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Society_of_Arts) (9 November 1811–2 July 1890) was a 19th-century Scottish physician who became the first Superintendent of Statistics in [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland). He created the concept of [vital statistics](wikipedia://en/999909) in 1854.

:::

## Life {#Life}

*Image: The grave of James Stark, Logie Kirk churchyard.jpg.* The grave of James Stark, Logie Kirk churchyard

*Image: The grave of John Stark FRSE, St Cuthbert's Churchyard, Edinburgh.jpg.* Stark memorial, St Cuthbert's Churchyard, Edinburgh

He was born on 9 November 1811 at 2 Bristo Street[^o3] in Edinburgh's South Side, the son of Emma Brown (d.1815) and her husband, [John Stark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stark_%28printer%29).

He studied medicine at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), and undertook postgraduate studies in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) and [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn) gaining his doctorate (MD) from Edinburgh in 1833. He then set up as a GP in the city at 21 Rutland Street in the West End of the city.[^o4] In 1839 he became a Fellow of the [Royal College of Physicians of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians_of_Edinburgh) and became Curator of their museum.

In 1842 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh), the proposer being his father.[^o5]

In 1854, following the passing of the Scottish Registration Act, [William Pitt Dundas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Pitt_Dundas) in his role as [Registrar General for Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Registrar_General_for_Scotland), requested that the government fund a Superintendent of Statistics; preferably of medical background. The [Royal College of Physicians of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians_of_Edinburgh) put forward Dr James Stark.[^o6] He thereafter worked for the [General Register Office of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Register_Office_of_Scotland). He held the role until retiral in 1873, still living at 21 Rutland Street.[^o7]

He died at Underwood House in [Bridge of Allan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_of_Allan) on 2 July 1890. He is buried nearby in [Logie Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logie_Kirk). The grave lies to the north-east of the church. He is also memorialised on his parents' grave in [St Cuthbert's Churchyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Cuthbert's_Church,_Edinburgh) in central Edinburgh, at the west end of [Princes Street Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princes_Street_Gardens). The memorial lies against the outer north wall of the church.

## Family {#Family}

He was married to Isabella Black (1815-1874). Following her death he married Agnes Durie (1818-1907).[^o9]
