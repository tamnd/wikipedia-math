# Heinz Otto Cordes

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Heinz Otto Cordes"}

**Heinz Otto Cordes** (March 18, 1925 – October 30, 2018) was a German-American mathematician, specializing in [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) (PDEs).[^o3] He is known for the Aronszajn–Cordes uniqueness theorem for solutions of [elliptic PDEs](wikipedia://en/662787) (due independently to [Nachman Aronszajn](wikipedia://en/13496987)).[^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

At the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), Cordes received in 1952 his doctorate. His doctoral dissertation, supervised by [Franz Rellich](wikipedia://en/4172897), is entitled *Separation von Variablen in Hilbertschen Raumen* (Separation of variables in [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932)). Cordes held a junior academic appointment at Göttingen from 1952 to 1956, when he was appointed to an assistant professorship at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California). At the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) (UC Berkeley), he was an assistant professor to 1958 to 1959, an associate professor from 1959 to 1963, and a full professor from 1963 to 1991, when he retired as professor emeritus. In retirement he remained active in research.[^o4]

Cordes made a number of significant contributions to the theory of PDEs. He also introduced [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) techniques to define the symbol of elements of algebras of [singular integral operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_integral_operator) (as well as algebras of [pseudodifferential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudodifferential_operators)). Thereby he extended the operator symbol calculus from compact manifolds to various classes of non-compact manifolds. This research led him to study [Dirac operators](wikipedia://en/1079448) with their connections with relativistic quantum mechanics. He was the author of 4 books and the author or co-author of more than 60 articles.[^o4] From 1963 to 1999, he sometimes collaborated with [Tosio Kato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tosio_Kato).[^o9]

Cordes received an Alfred P. Sloan fellowship in 1959. He declined an invitation to address the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) held in Moscow in 1966. For the academic year 1971–1972, Cordes was a visiting professor at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University), where he gave a course on pseudodifferential operators via a C\*-algebra approach. His 19 doctoral students at UC Berkeley include [Michael G. Crandall](wikipedia://en/35265777) and [Michael E. Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_E._Taylor).[^o4][^o8]

Upon his death in 2018, Heinz Cordes had been married to his wife Hillgia for 63 years. They were the parents of a son and two daughters.[^o4]
