# Mal'cev's criterion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mal'cev's criterion"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), **Mal'cev's criterion**, proved by [Anatoly Mal'cev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Maltsev), states that a [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/3358867) [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) admits a lattice, i.e., a [discrete](wikipedia://en/56061) [co-compact](wikipedia://en/12746615) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011), if and only if the [associated](wikipedia://en/43302095) [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) admits a basis such that the [structure constants](wikipedia://en/20285142) are rational.[^n1]

:::

The relevance of Mal'cev's criterion comes from the fact that it gives us a one-to-one correspondence between isomorphism classes of Lie algebras with rational structure constants and [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [nilmanifolds](wikipedia://en/3668693). Indeed, Mal'cev showed that compact nilmanifolds are precisely quotients of simply connected nilpotent Lie groups by a lattice.[^n2]

## Relevance in Kähler geometry {#Relevance_in_Kähler_geometry}

Mal'cev's criterion is relevant in [Kähler geometry](wikipedia://en/390538) because compact nilmanifolds with a Kähler structure must be [diffeomorphic](wikipedia://en/8564) to a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800).[^n3][^n4] Therefore, when looking for manifolds that do not admit a Kähler structure, one may use Mal'cev's criterion to generate a compact nilmanifold from any rational Lie algebra.[^n4] By constructing other structures on the manifold, such as a [complex](wikipedia://en/509742) or [symplectic](wikipedia://en/28356) structure, one finds a non-Kähler manifold of said structure. It therefore helps finding solutions to the [Thurston–Weinstein problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurston–Weinstein_problem), which concerns itself with the existence of non-Kähler symplectic manifolds.[^n3]
