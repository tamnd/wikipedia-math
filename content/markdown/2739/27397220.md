# Sakabe Kōhan

Sakabe

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sakabe Kōhan"}

**Sakabe Kōhan** was a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) in the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period).[^o2]

:::

Sakabe served for a time in the Fire Department of the shogunate, but he resigned that position to become a *[rōnin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rōnin)* or masterless samurai. He spent the rest of this life in study, in teaching, and in promoting mathematics education in Japan.[^o3]

Sakabe was a student of [Ajima Naonobu](wikipedia://en/841517).[^o4][^o5]

Sakabe investigated some European and Chinese works which had appeared in Japan, but his general method was later construed to be innovative, clarified and thus improved.[^o6] Foreign influence shows itself indirectly some of his published work.[^o7]

Sakabe's *Sampo Tenzan Shinan-roku* (*Treatise on Tenzan Algebra*) in 1810 was the first published work in Japan proposing the use of [logarithmic tables](wikipedia://en/174482). He explained that "these tables save much labor, \[but\] they are but little known for the reason that they have never been printed in our country."[^o8] Sakabe's proposal would not be realized until twenty years after his death when the first extensive logarithmic table was published in 1844 by [Koide Shuke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koide_Shuke).[^o9]

In Sakabe's *Treatise on Tenzan Algebra*, mathematical problems are arranged in order from easy problems to difficult ones. The text presents a method for finding the length of a circumference and the length an arc of an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277). This was the first appearance of the problems pertaining to ellipses in printed books in Japan.[^o10]

## Selected works {#Selected_works}

In a statistical overview derived from writings by and about Harry Smith Parkes, [OCLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OCLC)/[WorldCat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WorldCat) encompasses roughly 10+ works in 10+ publications in 1 language and 10+ library holdings.[^o12]

- 1795 — *Shinsen Tetsujutsu*[^o3]
- 1802 — *Kaiujutsu-keima* (*Considerations on the theory of the polygon*)[^o3]
- 1803 — *Rippō-eijiku*, method for finding cube root[^o5]
- 1810 — *Tenzan Shinan-roku* [OCLC 22057236896](http://www.worldcat.org/search?q=no:%22057236896%22), *Treatise on Tenzan Algebra*[^o8]
- 1812 — *Kwanki-kodo-shōhō*, measurement of spherical arcs and trigonometrical tables[^o13]
- 1816 — *Kairo Anshin-roku* [OCLC 122810576](http://www.worldcat.org/search?q=no:%22122810576%22), theory of navigation applying the spherical astronomy of the West[^o7]
