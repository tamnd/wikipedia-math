# Evelyn Nelson (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Evelyn Nelson"}

**Evelyn Merle Nelson** (November 25, 1943 – August 1, 1987), born **Evelyn Merle Roden**, was a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Nelson made contributions to the area of [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) with applications to [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392). She, along with [Cecilia Krieger](wikipedia://en/11581652), is the namesake of the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829), awarded by the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) for outstanding research by a female mathematician.[^o3]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Nelson was born on November 25, 1943, in [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), Canada. Her parents were immigrants from [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) in the 1920s.[^o5] Nelson went to high school at [Westdale Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westdale_Secondary_School) in Hamilton.[^o5]

## Education {#Education}

After spending two years at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto), Nelson returned to [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario) to study at [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University). She received her [B.Sc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from McMaster in 1965, followed by an [M.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in mathematics from McMaster in 1967. She succeeded in having her thesis work published in the *[Canadian Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Journal_of_Mathematics)*, also in 1967; the article was entitled "Finiteness of semigroups of operators in universal algebra".[^o2]

Nelson completed her [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1970. Her thesis was entitled "The lattice of equational classes of commutative semigroups", and the ideas also formed a journal paper published in the [Canadian Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Journal_of_Mathematics).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Following completion of her Ph.D., Nelson continued at McMaster. She first worked as a post-doctoral researcher, later as a "research associate", and in 1978 was appointed [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor).[^o5] Serving as chair of the Unit of Computer Science at McMaster from 1982 until 1984, Nelson became a full [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in 1983.[^o9]

Nelson's teaching record was, according to one colleague, "invariably of the highest order". However, before earning a faculty position at McMaster, prejudice against her led to doubts about her teaching ability.[^o5]

Nelson published over 40 papers during her 20-year career. She died from [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer) in 1987.[^o5]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Nelson is the namesake, along with [Cecilia Krieger](wikipedia://en/11581652), of the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829), which is awarded to a female mathematician in recognition of outstanding achievement.[^o3] The Department of Mathematics at [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University) has a lecture series, "The Evelyn Nelson Lectures", held since 1991.[^o11]
