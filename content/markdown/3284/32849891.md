# News media phone hacking scandal reference lists

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="News media phone hacking scandal reference lists"}

The [news media phone hacking scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media_phone_hacking_scandal) is a controversy over illegal acquisition of confidential information by news media organizations that reportedly occurred in the United Kingdom, the United States and Australia between 1995 and 2011. This article includes reference lists for various topics relating to that scandal.

:::

## Overview and topics {#Overview_and_topics}

These lists are organized according to the topics shown below in blue type.

By 2002, the practice of [publications using private investigators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Publications_mentioned_in_the_ICO_report) to acquire confidential information was widespread,[^o62] with some individuals using illegal methods.[^o3][^o4] [Victims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Victims) of these illegal methods included celebrities,[^o5] politicians,[^o6] law enforcement officials,[^o6] solicitors,[^o6] and ordinary citizens.[^o7] As this illegal activity became apparent, [arrests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Arrests) were made[^o70][^o8] and some [convictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Convictions) achieved.[^o70][^o9] Upon learning their privacy had been violated, some victims retained [solicitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Solicitors) and [filed suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Civil_suits) against news media companies and their agents,[^o10] in some cases receiving [financial payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#For_violation_of_privacy) for violation of privacy.[^o11] Successful suits and publicity from [investigative news articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Key_newspaper_articles)[^o12] led to further disclosures, including the names of more victims,[^o7] more [documentary evidence of wrongdoing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Key_evidentiary_documents),[^o13] [admissions of wrongdoing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Individuals_acknowledging_that_confidential_information_was_acquired_illegally),[^o73] and potentially [related payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#For_other_matters).[^o14] Allegations were made of poor judgement[^o15] and cover up by news media executives[^o16][^o17] and law enforcement officials.[^o18] As a result, new [investigations were initiated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Investigations)[^o19] and several senior executives and police officials resigned.[^o20][^o21] There were also significant [commercial consequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Commercial_consequences) of the scandal.[^o22][^o23]

Individuals included on these lists are reportedly involved as victims, perpetrators, investigators, solicitors, or responsible oversight officials in the phone hacking scandal. Illegal acquisition of information was allegedly accomplished by accessing private voicemail accounts, hacking into computers, making false statements to officials to obtain confidential information, entrapment,[^o24][^o25] blackmail,[^o26] burglaries,[^o27] theft of mobile phones[^o28] and making payments to officials in exchange for confidential information. The kind of information acquired illegally may have include private communication, physical location of individuals, bank account records, medical records, phone bills, tax files, and organizational strategies.[^o29]

There is evidence that illegal acquisition of confidential information continued at least into 2010.[^o30][^o31] Solicitors representing victims were targeted for surveillance by news organizations being sued as recently as 2011.[^o40]

## Surveillance targets {#Surveillance_targets}

These are lists of individuals who were targeted for surveillance beyond simple phone hacking by news media organizations. Some were targeted simply to acquire information for developing a story but others were vocal critics of news media companies and may have been targeted to obtain information with which to influence criminal investigations, civil suits, and Parliamentary hearings.

### Critics of news media organizations {#Critics_of_news_media_organizations}

This list includes a serving police officer, two solicitors with clients suing [News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International), and a Member of Parliament. Dates in parentheses indicate the approximate date that surveillance was initiated.

1. **Cook, David**; (June 2002) While investigating [Jonathan Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Rees) and Sid Fillery in connection with the 1987 murder of [Daniel Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Morgan_%28private_investigator%29), Metropolitan Police detective chief superintendent Cook reportedly became a surveillance target of [Glenn Mulcaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Mulcaire), who was working for *News of the World.*[^o6][^o35][^o36] Fillery reportedly used his relationship with Alex Marunchak at *News of the World* to arrange for Mulcaire to obtain Cook's home address, his internal payroll number at the Metropolitan police, his date of birth and figures for the amount that he and his wife were paying for their mortgage. [Surveillance of Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Police_role_in_the_News_International_phone_hacking_scandal#Operation_Nigeria_%281999%29) is also reported to have involved physically following him and his young children, attempts to access his voicemail and that of his wife, and possibly attempts to send a "Trojan horse" email in an attempt to steal information from his computer. Documents reportedly in the possession of the Scotland Yard shows that "Mulcaire did this on the instructions of [Greg Miskiw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Miskiw), the paper's assistant editor and a close friend of Marunchak." Attempts also appear to have been made to open letters which had been left in Cook's external postbox, indicating a "possible attempt to pervert the course of justice." [Rebekah Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebekah_Brooks), then editor for *News of the World*, was aware of the surveillance.[^o37]
1. **[Watson, Tom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Watson,_Baron_Watson_of_Wyre_Forest)**; (September 2009) Watson had been at odds with News International since 2006, when he quit as Government Minister and signed a letter demanding that [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) resign as Prime Minister. [Rebekah Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebekah_Brooks), then editor of News International's *[The Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_%28United_Kingdom%29)*, reportedly stated she would not forgive him for this and that she would pursue Watson for the rest of his life.[^o38] Watson found himself targeted, with strangers going through his rubbish and harassing his family.[^o38] As a member of the [Culture, Media and Sport Select Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture,_Media_and_Sport_Select_Committee), he confronted News International executives with critical observations and probing questions. While the Committee was holding hearings leading to a formal report on phone hacking, three as yet unnamed News International executives arranged for Watson to be put under surveillance by private investigator and former policeman, [Derek Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Webb).[^o39]
1. **Lewis, Mark**; (May 2010) While Lewis was representing claimants against *[News of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_of_the_World)* for phone hacking, its parent company, [News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International), hired Derek Webb to put Lewis and his family under surveillance. This was reportedly part of an attempt to gather evidence for false smears about his private life and thereby interfere with his ability to represent clients.[^o40] The attempt to discredit the solicitors may also have been intended to protect the reputation of [Andy Coulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Coulson), formerly editor of *[News of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_of_the_World)* who subsequently became head of communications for the Conservative Party and senior media adviser to Prime Minister [David Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Cameron).[^o41] A dossier including video taken of his ex-wife and daughter was given to [Tom Crone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Crone_%28barrister%29), the senior legal manager at News International. [James Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Murdoch) was executive chairman of the company at the time.[^o40][^o42][^o54][^o43] Lewis told the Leveson Inquiry that "News International sought to destroy my life, and very nearly succeeded."[^o43]
1. **Harris, Charlotte**; (January 2011) Like Mark Lewis, Harris also came under surveillance by an agent of [News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International) while representing clients suing the company in relation to phone hacking by *[News of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_of_the_World)*." Her pursuit of evidence for her clients led to disclosure of information that reportedly resulted in the firing of [Ian Edmondson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Edmondson) from *News of the World* and the resignation of the prime minister's media adviser, [Andy Coulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Coulson). Also like Lewis, both she and her family were followed and video-taped, with a dossier given to News International's [Tom Crone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Crone_%28barrister%29). The private investigator, Derek Webb, was asked to establish that Harris was having an affair with someone it turned out that she had never met. Over the years, News International had engaged Webb to target more than 90 people.[^o40][^o39][^o41][^o42]

1. **Winskell, Robin**; sports attorney who has represented prominent footballers in disciplinary trials, FIFA arbritrations, and libel cases.[^o49]

### Otherwise involved in phone hacking scandal {#Otherwise_involved_in_phone_hacking_scandal}

1. **Lawrence Abramson**; As solicitor at [Harbottle & Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbottle_&_Lewis), Abramson sent a letter 29 May 2007 stating H&L's review of emails provided by [News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International) did not indicate involvement in phone hacking by other than Clive Goodman.[^o57] This letter was subsequently used by News International executives to maintain that Goodman was a single "rogue reporter."[^o58] When **[Lord Kenneth Macdonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Macdonald)** reviewed the emails again in July 2011, he reported "evidence of serious criminal offences" including payments to police.[^o59]
1. **Pike, Julian**; Partner at Farrer & Co; represented the [News Group Newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Group_Newspapers) subsidiary of [News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International) during the 2008 settlement negotiations leading to payment of £425,000 plus costs to **[Gordon Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Taylor_%28footballer%29)**. Pike told the Culture, Media and Sport Committee on 19 October 2011 that he had informed News International of evidence suggesting "a powerful case" could be made that three *[News of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_of_the_World)* journalists had illegally accessed confidential information. News International maintained through 2009 that only **[Clive Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Goodman)** had been involved. Pike also told the Committee that **Colin Myler** met with James Murdoch on 27 May 2008, after Pike had received a copy of the new evidence which had been emailed by **Tom Crone** to Myler. This meeting was in addition to Myler's meeting with Murdoch and Crone 10 June 2008. Pike had not reported earlier that senior News International personnel had misled Parliament because of professional obligations to his client. Farrer & Co. also represents Queen Elizabeth II.[^o60] In 2011, Pike advised News International that [Mark Lewis and Charlotte Harris should be put under surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International_phone_hacking_scandal_reference_lists#Surveillance_targets) while they were representing clients suing the company.[^o42] As a result, both of them and their families were followed and video-taped. Pike continued to characterize this apparent attempt to find information with which to discredit opposing solicitors as "unusual" but "justified" and "would do it again tomorrow," even after News International acknowledged that it was "deeply inappropriate."[^o54] Farrer & Co. also wrote to Lewis threatening to enjoin him from accepting more clients that wanted to sue *News of the World* over phone hacking.[^o41] Pike also discussed the possibility with News International's legal affairs manager, [Tom Crone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Crone_%28barrister%29), of making charges of professional misconduct against Lewis and Harris.[^o39] Lewis told the [Leveson Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leveson_Inquiry) that "News International sought to destroy my life, and very nearly succeeded."[^o43]

## Individuals who worked for both News International and the police {#Individuals_who_worked_for_both_News_International_and_the_police}

*Image: News Internaltional Building in London.JPG.* By mid-2012 formal charges had been filed against many News International journalists and executives, including former chief executive [Rebekah Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebekah_Brooks).

This is an alphabetical list of individuals who at various times worked for both [News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International) and the [Metropolitan Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Police_Service).

1. [Andy Hayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Hayman) was with the [Essex Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex_Police) from 1978 until 1998, when he transferred to the [Metropolitan Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Police_Service). He became [Chief Constable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Constable) for the [Norfolk Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Constabulary) in 2002 but returned to the Met in February 2005.[^o84] Hayman was in charge of the Royal Household/Goodman Inquiry in 2006. He resigned from the Met in December 2007 during an investigation of reportedly lavish entertainment expenditures for "long lunches and dinners with *[News of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_of_the_World)* journalists at a time when the newspaper was under investigation for phone hacking."[^o85] Two months later he was hired by [News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International), where he wrote an article for *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* defending the police investigation he led, maintaining that there were "perhaps a handful" of hacking victims.[^o79][^o85]
1. [Alex Marunchak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Marunchak) worked as a freelance Ukrainian language interpreter for the [Metropolitan Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Police_Service) between 1980 and 2000 while he was also an executive for *[News of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_of_the_World)*, where he worked from 1981 to 2006. He provide "interpretation and translation services for victims, witnesses and suspects of crime who do not speak English."[^o86] Marunchak was a regular customer of private investigator [Jonathan Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Rees), who was reportedly purchasing information from improper sources. No evidence became public that Marunchak or other journalists had committed criminal offences or that they were aware of how Rees acquired the information.[^o4] Marunchak reportedly arranged for [Glenn Mulcaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Mulcaire), then doing work for *News of the World*, to [conduct surveillance on the detective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Police_role_in_the_News_International_phone_hacking_scandal#Operation_Nigeria_%281999%29) investigating Rees and his partner, Sid Fillery, for murder.[^o37]
1. [John Stevens, Baron Stevens of Kirkwhelpington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stevens,_Baron_Stevens_of_Kirkwhelpington) was Met commissioner from 2000 to 2005. He has been credited with reopening communication between the news media and the police after his predecessor, [Sir Paul Condon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Paul_Condon) had all but closed it down in the mid-1990s with his anti-corruption drive at Scotland Yard.[^o87] After retiring, he wrote a column for the *[News of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_of_the_World)*. In his autobiography, he stated that he had "worked hard to foster good relations with newspapers and had made himself 'available' to editors including [Rebekah Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebekah_Brooks) (then Wade) at *[The Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_%28United_Kingdom%29)* and [Andy Coulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Coulson), then at the *[News of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_of_the_World)*." His diary, which may record meetings between him and [News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International) executives between February 2000 to January 2005, went missing but was found in October 2011 at New Scotland Yard.[^o88] In March 2011, he was chairman of security firm Axiom International.
1. [Neil Wallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Wallis) worked for [News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International) from 1987 to 1998 and again from 2003 to 2009. He then started his own public relations firm, Chamy Media, which provided "strategic communication advice and support" to the Met on a part-time basis from October 2009 to September 2010.[^o89] Wallis's firm was paid £24,000 by the Met at the same time Wallis received more than £25,000 from [News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International) for providing "crime exclusives" using details of Met investigations.[^o90][^o91] Wallis was arrested in July 2011 on suspicion of conspiring to intercept communications[^o92] Also in July, the [Independent Police Complaints Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Police_Complaints_Commission) began an Inquiry into "whether [John Yates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Yates_%28police_officer%29) used his position to help get Wallis's daughter a job at Scotland Yard.[^o93][^o94]

## Attempted suicides {#Attempted_suicides}

On 6 March 2012, Reuters reported that two senior journalists working for *[The Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_%28United_Kingdom%29)*, a newspaper owned by [News International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_International), appeared to have attempted suicide in the face of ongoing investigations relating to the phone hacking scandal. At that time, eleven current and former staff members of *The Sun* had been arrested on suspicion of bribing police or civil servants for information.[^o97]

*Image: Final NOTW cover.jpeg.* Public outrage over hacking [Milly Dowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milly_Dowler)'s voice mail resulted in loss of advertising to *[News of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_of_the_World)*, leading to its being shut down after 168 years.[^o98][^o99]
