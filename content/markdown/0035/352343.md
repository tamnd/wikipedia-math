# Outerplanar graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Outerplanar graph"}

*Image: Triangulation 3-coloring.svg.* A maximal outerplanar graph and its 3-coloring

*Image: Finite-3-regular-graph-4-vertices.png.* The [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) K<sub>4</sub> is the smallest planar graph that is not outerplanar.

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **outerplanar graph** is a graph that has a [planar drawing](wikipedia://en/24314) for which all vertices belong to the outer face of the drawing.

:::

Outerplanar graphs may be characterized (analogously to [Wagner's theorem](wikipedia://en/3126130) for planar graphs) by the two [forbidden minors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbidden_minor) $K_{4}$ and $K_{2,3}$, or by their [Colin de Verdière graph invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_de_Verdière_graph_invariant). They have Hamiltonian cycles if and only if they are biconnected, in which case the outer face forms the unique Hamiltonian cycle. Every outerplanar graph is 3-colorable, and has [degeneracy](wikipedia://en/27545816) and [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) at most 2.

The outerplanar graphs are a subset of the [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), the subgraphs of [series–parallel graphs](wikipedia://en/9938559), and the [circle graphs](wikipedia://en/1399873). The **maximal outerplanar graphs**, those to which no more edges can be added while preserving outerplanarity, are also [chordal graphs](wikipedia://en/744165) and [visibility graphs](wikipedia://en/1448291).

## History {#History}

Outerplanar graphs were first studied and named by (Chartrand Harary 1967), in connection with the problem of determining the planarity of graphs formed by using a [perfect matching](wikipedia://en/44031) to connect two copies of a base graph (for instance, many of the [generalized Petersen graphs](wikipedia://en/23716451) are formed in this way from two copies of a [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962)). As they showed, when the base graph is [biconnected](wikipedia://en/4989397), a graph constructed in this way is planar if and only if its base graph is outerplanar and the matching forms a [dihedral](wikipedia://en/100267) permutation of its outer cycle. Chartrand and Harary also proved an analogue of [Kuratowski's theorem](wikipedia://en/54493) for outerplanar graphs, that a graph is outerplanar if and only if it does not contain a [subdivision](wikipedia://en/353021) of one of the two graphs *K*<sub>4</sub> or *K*<sub>2,3</sub>.

## Definition and characterizations {#Definition_and_characterizations}

An outerplanar graph is an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) that can be [drawn](wikipedia://en/8149170) in the [plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) without [crossings](wikipedia://en/18298594) in such a way that all of the vertices belong to the unbounded face of the drawing. That is, no vertex is totally surrounded by edges. Alternatively, a graph *G* is outerplanar if the graph formed from *G* by adding a new vertex, with edges connecting it to all the other vertices, is a [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314).[^o5][^o6]

A **maximal outerplanar graph** is an outerplanar graph that cannot have any additional edges added to it while preserving outerplanarity. Every maximal outerplanar graph with *n* vertices has exactly 2*n* − 3 edges, and every bounded face of a maximal outerplanar graph is a triangle.

### Forbidden graphs {#Forbidden_graphs}

Outerplanar graphs have a [forbidden graph characterization](wikipedia://en/13547826) analogous to [Kuratowski's theorem](wikipedia://en/54493) and [Wagner's theorem](wikipedia://en/3126130) for planar graphs: a graph is outerplanar if and only if it does not contain a [subdivision](wikipedia://en/353021) of the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K*<sub>4</sub> or the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) *K*<sub>2,3</sub>.[^o8] Alternatively, a graph is outerplanar if and only if it does not contain *K*<sub>4</sub> or *K*<sub>2,3</sub> as a [minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_%28graph_theory%29), a graph obtained from it by deleting and contracting edges.[^o9]

A [triangle-free graph](wikipedia://en/7667996) is outerplanar if and only if it does not contain a subdivision of *K*<sub>2,3</sub>.[^o16]

### Colin de Verdière invariant {#Colin_de_Verdière_invariant}

A graph is outerplanar if and only if its [Colin de Verdière graph invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_de_Verdière_graph_invariant) is at most two. The graphs characterized in a similar way by having Colin de Verdière invariant at most one, three, or four are respectively the linear forests, [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), and [linklessly embeddable graphs](wikipedia://en/772031).

## Properties {#Properties}

### Biconnectivity and Hamiltonicity {#Biconnectivity_and_Hamiltonicity}

An outerplanar graph is [biconnected](wikipedia://en/4989397) if and only if the outer face of the graph forms a [simple cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) without repeated vertices. An outerplanar graph is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle) if and only if it is biconnected; in this case, the outer face forms the unique Hamiltonian cycle.[^o14] More generally, the size of the longest cycle in an outerplanar graph is the same as the number of vertices in its largest [biconnected component](wikipedia://en/4842394). For this reason finding Hamiltonian cycles and longest cycles in outerplanar graphs may be solved in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time), in contrast to the [NP-completeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) of these problems for arbitrary graphs.

Every maximal outerplanar graph satisfies a stronger condition than Hamiltonicity: it is [node pancyclic](wikipedia://en/28691490), meaning that for every vertex *v* and every *k* in the range from three to the number of vertices in the graph, there is a length-*k* cycle containing *v*. A cycle of this length may be found by repeatedly removing a triangle that is connected to the rest of the graph by a single edge, such that the removed vertex is not *v*, until the outer face of the remaining graph has length *k*.[^o15]

A planar graph is outerplanar if and only if each of its biconnected components is outerplanar.[^o16]

### Coloring {#Coloring}

All loopless outerplanar graphs can be [colored](wikipedia://en/426743) using only three colors;[^o18] this fact features prominently in the simplified proof of [Chvátal's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Václav_Chvátal) [art gallery theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_gallery_theorem) by (Fisk 1978). A 3-coloring may be found in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time) by a [greedy coloring](wikipedia://en/21051195) algorithm that removes any vertex of [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) at most two, colors the remaining graph recursively, and then adds back the removed vertex with a color different from the colors of its two neighbors.

According to [Vizing's theorem](wikipedia://en/5449464), the [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) of any graph (the minimum number of colors needed to color its edges so that no two adjacent edges have the same color) is either the maximum [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) of any vertex of the graph or one plus the maximum degree. However, in a connected outerplanar graph, the chromatic index is equal to the maximum degree except when the graph forms a [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) of odd length.[^o20] An edge coloring with an optimal number of colors can be found in linear time based on a [breadth-first traversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadth_first_search) of the weak dual tree.[^o18]

### Other properties {#Other_properties}

Outerplanar graphs have [degeneracy](wikipedia://en/27545816) at most two: every subgraph of an outerplanar graph contains a vertex with degree at most two.[^o22]

Outerplanar graphs have [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) at most two, which implies that many graph optimization problems that are [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) for arbitrary graphs may be solved in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) by [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297) when the input is outerplanar. More generally, *k*-outerplanar graphs have treewidth O(*k*).[^o23]

Every outerplanar graph can be represented as an [intersection graph](wikipedia://en/7726759) of axis-aligned rectangles in the plane, so outerplanar graphs have [boxicity](wikipedia://en/11167471) at most two.[^o24]

## Related families of graphs {#Related_families_of_graphs}

*Image: Cactus graph.svg.* A [cactus graph](wikipedia://en/9944209). The cacti form a subclass of the outerplanar graphs.

Every outerplanar graph is a [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314). Every outerplanar graph is also a subgraph of a [series–parallel graph](wikipedia://en/9938559).[^o26] However, not all planar series–parallel graphs are outerplanar. The [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) *K*<sub>2,3</sub> is planar and series–parallel but not outerplanar. On the other hand, the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K*<sub>4</sub> is planar but neither series–parallel nor outerplanar. Every [forest](wikipedia://en/48560) and every [cactus graph](wikipedia://en/9944209) are outerplanar.[^o27]

The [weak planar dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_dual) graph of an embedded outerplanar graph (the graph that has a vertex for every bounded face of the embedding, and an edge for every pair of adjacent bounded faces) is a forest, and the weak planar dual of a [Halin graph](wikipedia://en/19658548) is an outerplanar graph. A planar graph is outerplanar if and only if its weak dual is a forest, and it is Halin if and only if its weak dual is biconnected and outerplanar.[^o28]

There is a notion of degree of outerplanarity. A 1-outerplanar embedding of a graph is the same as an outerplanar embedding. For *k* > 1 a planar embedding is said to be [*k*-outerplanar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-outerplanar_graph) if removing the vertices on the outer face results in a (*k* − 1)-outerplanar embedding. A graph is *k*-outerplanar if it has a *k*-outerplanar embedding.[^o29]

An [outer-1-planar graph](wikipedia://en/38249632#Generalizations_and_related_concepts), analogously to [1-planar graphs](wikipedia://en/38249632) can be drawn in a disk, with the vertices on the boundary of the disk, and with at most one crossing per edge.

Every maximal outerplanar graph is a [chordal graph](wikipedia://en/744165). Every maximal outerplanar graph is the [visibility graph](wikipedia://en/1448291) of a [simple polygon](wikipedia://en/1059530).[^o30] Maximal outerplanar graphs are also formed as the graphs of [polygon triangulations](wikipedia://en/679384). They are examples of [2-trees](wikipedia://en/31104438), of [series–parallel graphs](wikipedia://en/9938559), and of [chordal graphs](wikipedia://en/744165).

Every outerplanar graph is a [circle graph](wikipedia://en/1399873), the [intersection graph](wikipedia://en/7726759) of a set of chords of a circle.[^o31]
