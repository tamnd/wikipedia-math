# Induced representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Induced representation"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **induced representation** is a [representation of a group](wikipedia://en/12695), $G$, which is constructed using a known representation of a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) $H$. Given a representation of $H$*,* the induced representation is, in a sense, the "most general" representation of $G$ that extends the given one. Since it is often easier to find representations of the smaller group $H$ than of $G$*,* the operation of forming induced representations is an important tool to construct new representations*.*

:::

Induced representations were initially defined by [Frobenius](wikipedia://en/341048), for [linear representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707). The idea is by no means limited to the case of finite groups, but the theory in that case is particularly well-behaved.

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Constructions"}

## Constructions {#Constructions}

### Algebraic {#Algebraic}

Let $G$ be a finite group and $H$ any subgroup of $G$. Furthermore let $(\pi, V)$ be a representation of $H$. Let $n = [G : H]$ be the [index](wikipedia://en/230581) of $H$ in $G$ and let $g_{1}, ..., g_{n}$ be a full set of representatives in $G$ of the [left cosets](wikipedia://en/98759) in $G/H$. The induced representation $\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H} \pi$ can be thought of as acting on the following space:

$$W=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n g_i V.$$

Here each $g_{i}\,V$ is an [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) copy of the vector space *V* whose elements are written as $g_{i}\,v$ with $v \in V$. For each *g* in $G$ there is an *h<sub>i</sub>* in $H$ for each *i* and a permutation *j* of {1, ..., *n*} such that $g g_{i} = g_{j(i)} h_{i}$, where *h<sub>i</sub>* as well as *j* depend on *g*. This is just another way of saying that $g_{1}, ..., g_{n}$ is a full set of representatives. Via the induced representation $G$ acts on $W$ as follows:

$$g\cdot\sum_{i=1}^n g_i v_i=\sum_{i=1}^n g_{j(i)} \pi(h_i) v_i$$

where $v_i \in V$ for each *i*.

Alternatively, one can construct induced representations by [extension of scalars](wikipedia://en/41410139#Extension_of_scalars): any *K-*linear representation $\pi$ of the group *H* can be viewed as a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) *V* over the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) *K*\[*H*\]. We can then define

$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\pi= K[G]\otimes_{K[H]} V.$$

This latter formula can also be used to define $\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H} \pi$ for any group $G$ and subgroup $H$, without requiring any finiteness.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

#### Examples {#Examples}

For any group, the induced representation of the [trivial representation](wikipedia://en/450573) of the [trivial subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_subgroup) is the right [regular representation](wikipedia://en/360011). More generally the induced representation of the [trivial representation](wikipedia://en/450573) of any subgroup is the [permutation representation](wikipedia://en/59487764) on the cosets of that subgroup.

An induced representation of a one-dimensional representation is called a **monomial representation**, because it can be represented as [monomial matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monomial_matrix). Some groups have the property that all of their irreducible representations are monomial, the so-called [monomial groups](wikipedia://en/16388251).

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

#### Properties {#Properties}

If $H$ is a subgroup of the group $G$, then every $K$-linear representation $\rho$ of $G$ can be viewed as a $K$-linear representation of $H$; this is known as the [restriction](wikipedia://en/880754) of $\rho$ to $H$ and denoted by $\operatorname{Res}(\rho)$. In the case of finite groups and finite-dimensional representations, the **[Frobenius reciprocity theorem](wikipedia://en/55690071)** states that, given representations $\sigma$ of $H$ and $\rho$ of $G$, the space of $H$-[equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) linear maps from $\sigma$ to $\operatorname{Res}(\rho)$ has the same dimension over *K* as that of $G$-equivariant linear maps from $\operatorname{Ind}(\sigma)$ to $\rho$.[^o10]

The [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) of the induced representation, which is also valid for infinite groups, is equivalent to the adjunction asserted in the reciprocity theorem. If $(\sigma,V)$ is a representation of *H* and $(\operatorname{Ind}(\sigma),\hat{V})$ is the representation of *G* induced by $\sigma$, then there exists a $H$-equivariant linear map $j:V\to\hat{V}$ with the following property: given any representation $(\rho,W)$ of $G$ and $H$-equivariant linear map $f:V\to W$, there is a unique $G$-equivariant linear map $\hat{f}: \hat{V}\to W$ with $\hat{f}j=f$. In other words, $\hat{f}$ is the unique map making the following [diagram commute](wikipedia://en/138484):[^o11]

*Image: Universal property of the induced representation 2.svg.*

The **Frobenius formula** states that if $\chi$ is the [character](wikipedia://en/691741) of the representation $\sigma$, given by $\chi(h) = \operatorname{Tr} \sigma(h)$, then the character $\psi$ of the induced representation is given by

$$\psi(g) = \sum_{x\in G / H} \widehat{\chi}\left(x^{-1}gx \right),$$

where the sum is taken over a system of representatives of the left cosets of $H$ in $G$ and

$$\widehat{\chi} (k) = \begin{cases} \chi(k) & \text{if } k \in H \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$

### Analytic {#Analytic}

If $G$ is a [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) (possibly infinite) and $H$ is a [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) then there is a common analytic construction of the induced representation. Let $(\pi, V)$ be a [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) unitary representation of $H$ into a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) *V*. We can then let:

$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\pi= \left\{\phi\colon G \to V \ : \ \phi(gh^{-1})=\pi(h)\phi(g)\text{ for all }h\in H,\; g\in G \text{ and } \ \phi \in L^2(G/H)\right\}.$$

Here $\varphi\in L^{2}(G/H)$ means: the space *G*/*H* carries a suitable invariant measure, and since the norm of $\varphi(g)$ is constant on each left coset of *H*, we can integrate the square of these norms over *G*/*H* and obtain a finite result. The group $G$ acts on the induced representation space by translation, that is, $(g.\varphi)(x)=\varphi(g^{-1}x)$ for *g,x*∈*G* and $\varphi\in\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H} \pi$.

This construction is often modified in various ways to fit the applications needed. A common version is called **normalized induction** and usually uses the same notation. The definition of the representation space is as follows:

$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\pi= \left \{\phi \colon G \to V \ : \ \phi(gh^{-1})=\Delta_G^{-\frac{1}{2}}(h)\Delta_H^{\frac{1}{2}}(h)\pi(h)\phi(g) \text{ and } \phi\in L^2(G/H) \right \}.$$

Here $\Delta_{G}, \Delta_{H}$ are the [modular functions](wikipedia://en/40645#The_modular_function) of $G$ and $H$ respectively. With the addition of the *normalizing* factors this induction [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) takes [unitary representations](wikipedia://en/292788) to unitary representations.

One other variation on induction is called **compact induction**. This is just standard induction restricted to functions with [compact support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support). Formally it is denoted by ind and defined as:

$$\operatorname{ind}_H^G\pi= \left\{\phi\colon G \to V \ : \ \phi(gh^{-1})=\pi(h)\phi(g) \text{ and } \phi \text{ has compact support mod } H \right\}.$$

Note that if $G/H$ is compact then Ind and ind are the same functor.

### Geometric {#Geometric}

Suppose $G$ is a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) and $H$ is a [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of $G$. Also, suppose $\pi$ is a representation of $H$ over the vector space $V$. Then $G$ [acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on the product $G \times V$ as follows:

$$g.(g',x)=(gg',x)$$

where $g$ and $g'$ are elements of $G$ and $x$ is an element of $V$.

Define on $G \times V$ the [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259)

$$(g,x) \sim (gh,\pi(h^{-1})(x)) \text{ for all }h\in H.$$

Denote the equivalence class of $(g,x)$ by $[g,x]$. Note that this equivalence relation is invariant under the action of $G$; consequently, $G$ acts on $(G \times V)/\sim$ . The latter is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) over the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) $G/H$ with $H$ as the [structure group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_group) and $V$ as the fiber. Let $W$ be the space of sections $\phi : G/H \to (G \times V)/ \! \sim$ of this vector bundle. This is the vector space underlying the induced representation $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\pi : W \to \mathcal L_W$. The group $G$ acts on a section $\phi : G/H \to (G \times V)/ \! \sim$ given by $gH \mapsto [g,\phi_g]$ as follows:

$$(g\cdot \phi)(g'H)=[g',\phi_{g^{-1}g'}] \ \text{ for } g,g'\in G.$$

### Systems of imprimitivity {#Systems_of_imprimitivity}

In the case of [unitary representations](wikipedia://en/292788) of locally compact groups, the induction construction can be formulated in terms of [systems of imprimitivity](wikipedia://en/1388070).

:::

## Lie theory {#Lie_theory}

In [Lie theory](wikipedia://en/1595681), an extremely important example is [parabolic induction](wikipedia://en/11118768): inducing representations of a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) from representations of its [parabolic subgroups](wikipedia://en/588356). This leads, via the [philosophy of cusp forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_cusp_forms), to the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905).
