# Affine representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Affine representation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **affine representation** of a [topological](wikipedia://en/42315) [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) *G* on an [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) *A* is a [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_%28topology%29) ([smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function)) [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) from *G* to the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of *A*, the [affine group](wikipedia://en/298828) Aff(*A*). Similarly, an affine representation of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) **g** on *A* is a [Lie algebra homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebra_homomorphism) from **g** to the Lie algebra **aff**(*A*) of the affine group of *A*.

:::

An example is the action of the [Euclidean group](wikipedia://en/652164) $E(n)$ on the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $E^{n}$.

Since the affine group in dimension $n$ is a matrix group in dimension $n + 1$, an affine representation may be thought of as a particular kind of [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation). We may ask whether a given affine representation has a [fixed point](wikipedia://en/449738) in the given affine space *A*. If it does, we may take that as origin and regard *A* as a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370); in that case, we actually have a linear representation in dimension $n$. This reduction depends on a [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) question, in general.
