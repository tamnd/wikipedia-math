# Quaternary numeral system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quaternary numeral system"}

**Quaternary** is a [numeral system](wikipedia://en/21170) with [four](wikipedia://en/208161) as its [base](wikipedia://en/701207). It uses the [digits](wikipedia://en/280582) 0, 1, 2, and 3 to represent any [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438). Conversion from [binary](wikipedia://en/238686) is straightforward.

:::

Four is the largest number within the [subitizing](wikipedia://en/734854) range and one of two numbers that is both a square and a [highly composite number](wikipedia://en/208732) (the other being thirty-six), making quaternary a convenient choice for a base at this scale. Despite being twice as large, its [radix economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radix_economy) is equal to that of binary. However, it fares no better in the localization of prime numbers (the smallest better base being the [primorial](wikipedia://en/422786) base six, [senary](wikipedia://en/29652)).

Quaternary shares with all fixed-[radix](wikipedia://en/701207) numeral systems many properties, such as the ability to represent any real number with a canonical representation (almost unique) and the characteristics of the representations of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) and [irrational numbers](wikipedia://en/20647689). See [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) and [binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system) for a discussion of these properties.

As with the [octal](wikipedia://en/22330) and [hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263) numeral systems, quaternary has a special relation to the [binary numeral system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system). Each [radix](wikipedia://en/701207) four, eight, and sixteen is a [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948), so the conversion to and from binary is implemented by matching each digit with two, three, or four binary digits, or [bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit). For example, in quaternary,

230210<sub>4</sub> = 10 11 00 10 01 00<sub>2</sub>.

Since sixteen is a power of four, conversion between these bases can be implemented by matching each hexadecimal digit with two quaternary digits. In the above example,

23 02 10<sub>4</sub> = B24<sub>16</sub>

Although octal and hexadecimal are widely used in [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing) and [computer programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programming) in the discussion and analysis of binary arithmetic and logic, quaternary does not enjoy the same status.

Although quaternary has limited practical use, it can be helpful if it is ever necessary to perform hexadecimal arithmetic without a calculator. Each hexadecimal digit can be turned into a pair of quaternary digits. Then, arithmetic can be performed relatively easily before converting the end result back to hexadecimal. Quaternary is convenient for this purpose, since numbers have only half the digit length compared to binary, while still having very simple multiplication and addition tables with only three unique non-trivial elements.

By analogy with *byte* and *nybble*, a quaternary digit is sometimes called a *crumb*.

## Occurrence in human languages {#Occurrence_in_human_languages}

Many or all of the [Chumashan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumashan_languages) (spoken by the Native American [Chumash peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumash_people)) originally used a quaternary numeral system, in which the names for numbers were structured according to multiples of four and sixteen, instead of ten. There is a surviving list of [Ventureño language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventureño_language) number words up to thirty-two written down by a Spanish priest ca. 1819.[^o20]

The [Kharosthi numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharosthi#Numerals) (from the languages of the tribes of Pakistan and Afghanistan) have a partial quaternary numeral system from one to ten.

## Hilbert curves {#Hilbert_curves}

Quaternary numbers are used in the representation of 2D [Hilbert curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_curve). Here, a real number between 0 and 1 is converted into the quaternary system. Every single digit now indicates in which of the respective four sub-quadrants the number will be projected.

## Genetics {#Genetics}

Parallels can be drawn between quaternary numerals and the way [genetic code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_code) is represented by [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA). The four DNA [nucleotides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleotide) in [alphabetical order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabetical_order), abbreviated [A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenine), [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytosine), [G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanine), and [T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymine), can be taken to represent the quaternary digits in [numerical order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collation) 0, 1, 2, and 3. With this encoding, the complementary digit pairs 0↔3, and 1↔2 (binary 00↔11 and 01↔10) match the complementation of the [base pairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_pair): A↔T and C↔G and can be stored as data in DNA sequence.[^o21] For example, the nucleotide sequence GATTACA can be represented by the quaternary number 2033010 (= [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) 9156 or [binary](wikipedia://en/238686) 10 00 11 11 00 01 00). The [human genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_genome) is 3.2 billion base pairs in length.[^o14]

## Data transmission {#Data_transmission}

Quaternary [line codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_code) have been used for transmission, from the [invention of the telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_telegraph#Gauss-Weber_telegraph_and_Carl_Steinheil) to the [2B1Q](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2B1Q) code used in modern [ISDN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISDN) circuits.

The GDDR6X standard, developed by [Nvidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nvidia) and [Micron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micron_Technology), uses quaternary bits to transmit data.[^o16]

## Computing {#Computing}

Some computers have used [quaternary floating point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary_floating_point) arithmetic including the [Illinois ILLIAC&nbsp;II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_ILLIAC&nbsp;II) (1962)[^o22] and the Digital Field System DFS IV and DFS V high-resolution site survey systems.[^o23]
