# Girard Desargues

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Girard Desargues"}

**Girard Desargues** (; 21 February 1591 – September 1661) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and engineer, who is considered one of the founders of [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849).[^o3] [Desargues's theorem](wikipedia://en/358488), the [Desargues graph](wikipedia://en/1578059), and the crater [Desargues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desargues_%28crater%29) on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) are named in his honour.

*Image: Bosse - Pratique du trait a preuves, 1643 - 1219339.jpg.* *Pratique du trait a preuves* (1643)

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon), Desargues came from a family devoted to service to the French crown. His father was a royal [notary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_notary), an investigating commissioner of the [Seneschal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seneschal)'s court in Lyon (1574), the collector of the [tithes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tithe) on ecclesiastical revenues for the city of Lyon (1583) and for the [diocese of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Lyon).

Girard Desargues worked as an [architect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture) from 1645. Prior to that, he had worked as a tutor and may have served as an engineer and technical consultant in the entourage of [Richelieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Richelieu). Yet his involvement in the Siege of La Rochelle, though alleged by Ch. Weiss in *Biographie Universelle*[^o5] (1842), has never been testified.

As an architect, Desargues planned several private and public buildings in Paris and Lyon. As an engineer, he designed a system for raising water that he installed near Paris. It was based on the use of the [epicycloidal](wikipedia://en/713354) wheel, the principle of which was unrecognized at the time.

His research on perspective and geometrical projections can be seen as a culmination of centuries of scientific inquiry across the classical epoch in optics that stretched from al-Hasan [Ibn al-Haytham](wikipedia://en/1645) ([Alhazen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhazen)) to Johannes [Kepler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler), and going beyond a mere synthesis of these traditions with Renaissance perspective theories and practices.[^o6]

His work was rediscovered and republished in 1864. A collection of his works was published in 1951,[^o7] and the 1864 compilation remains in print.[^o8] One notable work, often cited by others in mathematics, is "Rough draft for an essay on the results of taking plane sections of a cone" (1639).

Late in his life, Desargues published a paper with the cryptic title of *DALG*. The most common theory about what this stands for is *Des Argues, Lyonnais, Géometre* (proposed by [Henri Brocard](wikipedia://en/14897496)).

He died in Lyon. The [Desargues crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desargues_%28crater%29) on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) is named after him.[^o9]
