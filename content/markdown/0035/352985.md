# Chen Jingrun

[Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_%28surname%29)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Chen Jingrun"}

**Chen Jingrun** (; 22 May 1933 – 19 March 1996), also known as **Jing-Run Chen**, was a Chinese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made significant contributions to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), including [Chen's theorem](wikipedia://en/1400840) and the [Chen prime](wikipedia://en/2331143).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Chen was the third son in a large family from [Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou), [Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian), China. His father was a postal worker. Chen Jingrun graduated from the Mathematics Department of [Xiamen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiamen_University) in 1953. His advisor at the [Chinese Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Academy_of_Sciences) was [Hua Luogeng](wikipedia://en/2337582).

His work on the [twin prime conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_prime_conjecture), [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569), [Goldbach's conjecture](wikipedia://en/44758) and [Legendre's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2620450) led to progress in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513). In a 1966 paper he [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) what is now called [Chen's theorem](wikipedia://en/1400840): every [sufficiently large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficiently_large) even number can be written as the sum of a prime and a number which is either a prime or a [semiprime](wikipedia://en/339542) (the product of two primes).[^o5] Despite being persecuted during the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution), he expanded his proof in the 1970s.[^o6]

After the end of the Cultural Revolution, [Xu Chi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Chi) wrote a biography of Chen, *Goldbach's Conjecture* (哥德巴赫猜想). First published in *[People's Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Literature)* in January 1978, it was reprinted in the *[People's Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Daily)* a month later and became a national sensation. Chen became a household name in China and received a sackful of love letters from all over the country within two months.[^o7]

Chen died of complications of [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia) on 19 March 1996, at age 62.[^o8]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

*Image: A statue of Chen Jingrun.JPG.* Chen's statue at [Xiamen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiamen_University), China.

The [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) [7681 Chenjingrun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7681_Chenjingrun), discovered in 1996, was named after him.[^o5]

In 1999, China issued an 80-cent postage stamp, titled *The Best Result of Goldbach Conjecture,* with a silhouette of Chen and the inequality:[^o5]

$$P_x(1, 2) \ge \frac{0.67xC_x}{(\log x)^2}.$$

Several statues in China have been built in memory of Chen. At Xiamen University, the names of Chen and four other mathematicians—[Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002), [Matti Jutila](wikipedia://en/6716887), [Yuri Linnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Linnik), and [Pan Chengdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Chengdong)—are inscribed on the marble slab behind Chen's statue.
