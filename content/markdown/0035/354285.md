# List of rules of inference

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of rules of inference"}

This is a list of [rules of inference](wikipedia://en/252311), logical laws that relate to mathematical formulae.

:::

## Introduction {#Introduction}

**Rules of inference** are syntactical **transform** rules which one can use to infer a conclusion from a premise to create an argument. A set of rules can be used to infer any valid conclusion if it is complete, while never inferring an invalid conclusion, if it is sound. A sound and complete set of rules need not include every rule in the following list, as many of the rules are redundant, and can be proven with the other rules.

*Discharge rules* permit inference from a [subderivation](wikipedia://en/82285) based on a temporary assumption. Below, the notation

$$\varphi \vdash \psi$$

indicates such a subderivation from the temporary assumption $\varphi$ to $\psi$.

## Rules for [propositional calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus) {#Rules_for_propositional_calculus}

### Rules for negations {#Rules_for_negations}

**[Reductio ad absurdum](wikipedia://en/22456146) (or *[Negation Introduction](wikipedia://en/42211842)*)**

$$\varphi \vdash \psi$$

$$\underline{\varphi \vdash \lnot \psi}$$

$$\lnot \varphi$$

**[Reductio ad absurdum](wikipedia://en/22456146) (related to the [law of excluded middle](wikipedia://en/17637))**

$$\lnot \varphi \vdash \psi$$

$$\underline{\lnot \varphi \vdash \lnot \psi}$$

$$\varphi$$

**[Ex contradictione quodlibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_contradictione_quodlibet)**

$$\varphi$$

$$\underline{\lnot \varphi}$$

$$\psi$$

### Rules for conditionals {#Rules_for_conditionals}

**[Deduction theorem](wikipedia://en/480010) (or *[Conditional Introduction](wikipedia://en/6562)*)**

$$\underline{\varphi \vdash \psi}$$

$$\varphi \rightarrow \psi$$

**[Modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900) (a type of *[Conditional Elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_Elimination)*)**

$$\varphi \rightarrow \psi$$

$$\underline{\varphi \quad \quad \quad}$$

$$\psi$$

**[Modus tollens](wikipedia://en/18901) (a type of *[Conditional Elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_Elimination)*)**

$$\varphi \rightarrow \psi$$

$$\underline{\lnot \psi \quad \quad \quad}$$

$$\lnot \varphi$$

### Rules for conjunctions {#Rules_for_conjunctions}

**[Adjunction](wikipedia://en/6563) (or *[Conjunction Introduction](wikipedia://en/6563)*)**

$$\varphi$$

$$\underline{\psi \quad \quad \ \ }$$

$$\varphi \land \psi$$

**[Simplification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplification_%28logic%29) (or *[Conjunction Elimination](wikipedia://en/788704)*)**

$$\underline{\varphi \land \psi}$$

$$\varphi$$

$$\underline{\varphi \land \psi}$$

$$\psi$$

### Rules for disjunctions {#Rules_for_disjunctions}

**[Addition](wikipedia://en/8528) (or *[Disjunction Introduction](wikipedia://en/8528)*)**

$$\underline{\varphi \quad \quad \ \ }$$

$$\varphi \lor \psi$$

$$\underline{\psi \quad \quad \ \ }$$

$$\varphi \lor \psi$$

**[Case analysis](wikipedia://en/8529) (or *[Proof by Cases](wikipedia://en/359970)* or *[Argument by Cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_by_Cases)* or *[Disjunction elimination](wikipedia://en/8529)*)**

$$\varphi \rightarrow \chi$$

$$\psi \rightarrow \chi$$

$$\underline{\varphi \lor \psi}$$

$$\chi$$

**[Disjunctive syllogism](wikipedia://en/7963)**

$$\varphi \lor \psi$$

$$\underline{\lnot \varphi \quad \quad}$$

$$\psi$$

$$\varphi \lor \psi$$

$$\underline{\lnot \psi \quad \quad}$$

$$\varphi$$

**[Constructive dilemma](wikipedia://en/1316648)**

$$\varphi \rightarrow \chi$$

$$\psi \rightarrow \xi$$

$$\underline{\varphi \lor \psi}$$

$$\chi \lor \xi$$

### Rules for biconditionals {#Rules_for_biconditionals}

**[Biconditional introduction](wikipedia://en/4286)**

$$\varphi \rightarrow \psi$$

$$\underline{\psi \rightarrow \varphi}$$

$$\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi$$

**[Biconditional elimination](wikipedia://en/4287)**

$$\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi$$

$$\underline{\varphi \quad \quad}$$

$$\psi$$

$$\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi$$

$$\underline{\psi \quad \quad}$$

$$\varphi$$

$$\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi$$

$$\underline{\lnot \varphi \quad \quad}$$

$$\lnot \psi$$

$$\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi$$

$$\underline{\lnot \psi \quad \quad}$$

$$\lnot \varphi$$

$$\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi$$

$$\underline{\psi \lor \varphi}$$

$$\psi \land \varphi$$

$$\varphi \leftrightarrow \psi$$

$$\underline{\lnot \psi \lor \lnot \varphi}$$

$$\lnot \psi \land \lnot \varphi$$

## Rules of classical [predicate calculus](wikipedia://en/10983) {#Rules_of_classical_predicate_calculus}

In the following rules, $\varphi(\beta / \alpha)$ is exactly like $\varphi$ except for having the term $\beta$ wherever $\varphi$ has the free variable $\alpha$.

**[Universal Generalization](wikipedia://en/939333) (or [Universal Introduction](wikipedia://en/939333))**

$$\underline{\varphi{(\beta / \alpha)}}$$

$$\forall \alpha\, \varphi$$

Restriction 1: $\beta$ is a variable which does not occur in $\varphi$.

Restriction 2: $\beta$ is not mentioned in any hypothesis or undischarged assumptions.

**[Universal Instantiation](wikipedia://en/1564194) (or [Universal Elimination](wikipedia://en/1564194))**

$$\forall \alpha\, \varphi$$

$$\overline{\varphi{(\beta / \alpha)}}$$

Restriction: No free occurrence of $\alpha$ in $\varphi$ falls within the scope of a quantifier quantifying a variable occurring in $\beta$.

**[Existential Generalization](wikipedia://en/33907942) (or [Existential Introduction](wikipedia://en/33907942))**

$$\underline{\varphi(\beta / \alpha)}$$

$$\exists \alpha\, \varphi$$

Restriction: No free occurrence of $\alpha$ in $\varphi$ falls within the scope of a quantifier quantifying a variable occurring in $\beta$.

**[Existential Instantiation](wikipedia://en/34632680) (or [Existential Elimination](wikipedia://en/34632680))**

$$\exists \alpha\, \varphi$$

$$\underline{\varphi(\beta / \alpha) \vdash \psi}$$

$$\psi$$

Restriction 1: $\beta$ is a variable which does not occur in $\varphi$.

Restriction 2: There is no occurrence, free or bound, of $\beta$ in $\psi$.

Restriction 3: $\beta$ is not mentioned in any hypothesis or undischarged assumptions.

## Rules of [substructural logic](wikipedia://en/412909) {#Rules_of_substructural_logic}

The following are special cases of universal generalization and existential elimination; these occur in substructural logics, such as [linear logic](wikipedia://en/579675).

**Rule of weakening (or [monotonicity of entailment](wikipedia://en/693185)) (aka [no-cloning theorem](wikipedia://en/22035))**

$$\alpha\vdash\beta$$

$$\overline{\alpha,\alpha\vdash\beta}$$

**Rule of contraction (or [idempotency of entailment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotency_of_entailment)) (aka [no-deleting theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-deleting_theorem))**

$$\underline{\alpha,\alpha,\gamma\vdash\beta}$$

$$\alpha,\gamma\vdash\beta$$

The rules above can be summed up in the following table.[^o12] The "[Tautology](wikipedia://en/4495335)" column shows how to interpret the notation of a given rule.

|---|---|---|
| $\begin{aligned} p\\ p \rightarrow q\\ \therefore \overline{q \quad \quad \quad} \\ \end{aligned}$ | $(p \wedge (p \rightarrow q)) \rightarrow q$ | [Modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900) |
| $\begin{aligned} \neg q\\ p \rightarrow q\\ \therefore \overline{\neg p \quad \quad \quad} \\ \end{aligned}$ | $(\neg q \wedge (p \rightarrow q)) \rightarrow \neg p$ | [Modus tollens](wikipedia://en/18901) |
| $\begin{aligned} p \rightarrow q\\ q \rightarrow r\\ \therefore \overline{p \rightarrow r} \\ \end{aligned}$ | $((p \rightarrow q) \wedge (q \rightarrow r)) \rightarrow (p \rightarrow r)$ | [Hypothetical syllogism](wikipedia://en/177633) |
| $\begin{aligned} p \rightarrow q\\ \therefore \overline{p \rightarrow (p \wedge q)} \\ \end{aligned}$ | $(p \rightarrow q) \rightarrow (p \rightarrow (p \wedge q))$ | [Absorption](wikipedia://en/34620850) |
| $\begin{aligned} p\\ q\\ \therefore \overline{p \wedge q} \\ \end{aligned}$ | $((p) \wedge (q)) \rightarrow (p \wedge q)$ | [Conjunction introduction](wikipedia://en/6563) |
| $\begin{aligned} p \wedge q \\ \therefore \overline{p \quad \quad \quad} \\ \end{aligned}$ | $(p \wedge q) \rightarrow p$ | [Conjunction elimination](wikipedia://en/788704) |
| $\begin{aligned} p \\ \therefore \overline{p \vee q} \\ \end{aligned}$ | $p \rightarrow (p \vee q)$ | [Disjunction introduction](wikipedia://en/8528) |
| $\begin{aligned} p \rightarrow q\\ r \rightarrow q\\ p \vee r\\ \therefore \overline{q \quad \quad \quad} \\ \end{aligned}$ | $((p \rightarrow q) \wedge (r \rightarrow q) \wedge (p \vee r)) \rightarrow q$ | [Disjunction elimination](wikipedia://en/8529) |
| $\begin{aligned} p \vee q \\ \neg p \\ \therefore \overline{q \quad \quad \quad} \\ \end{aligned}$ | $((p \vee q) \wedge \neg p) \rightarrow q$ | [Disjunctive syllogism](wikipedia://en/7963) |

| $\begin{aligned} p \vee q \\ \neg p \vee r \\ \therefore \overline{q \vee r} \\ \end{aligned}$ | $((p \vee q) \wedge (\neg p \vee r)) \rightarrow (q \vee r)$ | [Resolution](wikipedia://en/2724082) |

Each logic operator can be used in an assertion about variables and operations, showing a basic rule of inference. Examples:

- The column-14 operator (OR), shows *Addition rule*: when *p*=T (the hypothesis selects the first two lines of the table), we see (at column-14) that *p*∨*q*=T.

We can see also that, with the same premise, another conclusions are valid: columns 12, 14 and 15 are T.

- The column-8 operator (AND), shows *Simplification rule*: when *p*∧*q*=T (first line of the table), we see that *p*=T.

With this premise, we also conclude that *q*=T, *p*∨*q*=T, etc. as shown by columns 9–15.

- The column-11 operator (IF/THEN), shows *Modus ponens rule*: when *p*→*q*=T and *p*=T only one line of the truth table (the first) satisfies these two conditions. On this line, *q* is also true. Therefore, whenever p → q is true and p is true, q must also be true.

Machines and well-trained people use this [look at table approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lookup_table) to do basic inferences, and to check if other inferences (for the same premises) can be obtained.

### Example 1 {#Example_1}

Consider the following assumptions: "If it rains today, then we will not go on a canoe today. If we do not go on a canoe trip today, then we will go on a canoe trip tomorrow. Therefore (Mathematical symbol for "therefore" is $\therefore$), if it rains today, we will go on a canoe trip tomorrow". To make use of the rules of inference in the above table we let $p$ be the proposition "If it rains today", $q$ be "We will not go on a canoe today" and let $r$ be "We will go on a canoe trip tomorrow". Then this argument is of the form:

$$\begin{aligned} p \rightarrow q\\ q \rightarrow r\\ \therefore \overline{p \rightarrow r} \\ \end{aligned}$$

### Example 2 {#Example_2}

Consider a more complex set of assumptions: "It is not sunny today and it is colder than yesterday". "We will go swimming only if it is sunny", "If we do not go swimming, then we will have a barbecue", and "If we will have a barbecue, then we will be home by sunset" lead to the conclusion "We will be home by sunset." Proof by rules of inference: Let $p$ be the proposition "It is sunny today", $q$ the proposition "It is colder than yesterday", $r$ the proposition "We will go swimming", $s$ the proposition "We will have a barbecue", and $t$ the proposition "We will be home by sunset". Then the hypotheses become $\neg p \wedge q, r \rightarrow p, \neg r \rightarrow s$ and $s \rightarrow t$. Using our intuition we conjecture that the conclusion might be $t$. Using the Rules of Inference table we can prove the conjecture easily:

::: {#o33 .table cue=table}

| Step | Reason |
|---|---|
| \1.$\neg p \wedge q$ | [Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis) |
| \2. $\neg p$ | [Simplification](wikipedia://en/788704) using Step 1 |
| \3. $r \rightarrow p$ | [Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis) |
| \4. $\neg r$ | [Modus tollens](wikipedia://en/18901) using Step 2 and 3 |
| \5. $\neg r \rightarrow s$ | [Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis) |
| \6. $s$ | [Modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900) using Step 4 and 5 |
| \7. $s \rightarrow t$ | [Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis) |
| \8. $t$ | [Modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900) using Step 6 and 7 |

:::
