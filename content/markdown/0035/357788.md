# Per capita

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Per capita"}

*Image: Agricultural-area-per-capita.svg.* Agricultural land use per capita, 1961-2018

***Per capita*** is a [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) phrase literally meaning "by heads" or "for each head", and idiomatically used to mean "per person".

:::

## Social statistics {#Social_statistics}

The term is used in a wide variety of [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science) and statistical research contexts, including government statistics, [economic indicators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_indicator), and [built environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Built_environment) studies.

It is commonly used in the [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_discipline) of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) in place of saying "per person"[^o3] (although *per caput* is the Latin for "per head"[^o4]).

It is also used in [wills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_and_testament) to indicate that each of the named [beneficiaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beneficiary) should receive, by [bequest or devise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bequest_and_devise), equal shares of the estate.[^o4] This is in contrast to a *[per stirpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_stirpes)* division, in which each branch (Latin: *[stirps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirps)*, : *stirpes*) of the [inheriting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inheritance) family inherits an equal share of the [estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_%28law%29). This is often used with the '2-0 rule', a statistical principle that determines which group is larger *per capita*. Under the 2-0 rule, a group is the largest *per capita* if it has both the biggest total size and size of the group of the objects in question, therefore resulting in a 2-0 score.
