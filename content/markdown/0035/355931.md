# Camille Jordan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Camille Jordan"}

**Marie Ennemond Camille Jordan** (; 5 January 1838 – 22 January 1922) was a French mathematician, known both for his foundational work in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and for his [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) *[Cours d'analyse de l'École polytechnique](wikipedia://en/80991658)*.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Jordan was born in [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon) and educated at the [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique). He was an engineer by profession; later in life he taught at the École polytechnique and the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France), where he had a reputation for eccentric choices of notation.

He is remembered now by name in a number of results:

- The [Jordan curve theorem](wikipedia://en/267444), a topological result required in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759)
- The [Jordan normal form](wikipedia://en/312877) and the [Jordan matrix](wikipedia://en/4495764) in [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422)
- In [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [Jordan measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_measure) (or *Jordan content*) is an area measure that predates [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory)
- In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the [Jordan–Hölder theorem on composition series](wikipedia://en/297493) is a basic result.
- [Jordan's theorem on finite linear groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan's_theorem_on_finite_linear_groups)

Jordan's work did much to bring [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) into the mainstream. He also investigated the [Mathieu groups](wikipedia://en/380207), the first examples of [sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806). His *Traité des substitutions*, on [permutation groups](wikipedia://en/24634), was published in 1870; this treatise won for Jordan the 1870 [*prix Poncelet*](wikipedia://en/20842828).[^o4] He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1920 in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg).[^o5]

The [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) [25593 Camillejordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25593_Camillejordan) and the Institut Camille Jordan are named in his honour.

Camille Jordan is not to be confused with the [geodesist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesist) [Wilhelm Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Jordan_%28geodesist%29) ([Gauss–Jordan elimination](wikipedia://en/13035)) or the physicist [Pascual Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascual_Jordan) ([Jordan algebras](wikipedia://en/936371)).
