# Solomon Feferman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Solomon Feferman"}

**Solomon Feferman** (; December 13, 1928 – July 26, 2016)[^o4] was an American [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). In addition to his prolific technical work in [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), [computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414), and [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), he was known for his contributions to the [history of logic](wikipedia://en/59945) (for instance, via biographical writings on figures such as [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736), [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), and [Jean van Heijenoort](wikipedia://en/2137251)) and as a vocal proponent of the philosophy of mathematics known as [predicativism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicativism), notably from an anti-[platonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonism_%28mathematics%29) stance.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Solomon Feferman was born in [The Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) to working-class parents who had immigrated to the United States after [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) and had met and married in New York. Neither parent had any advanced education. The family moved to Los Angeles, where Feferman graduated from high school at age 16.

He received his [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S.) from the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1948, and in 1957 his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), under [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), after having been drafted and having served in the U.S. Army from 1953 to 1955. In 1956 he was appointed to the Departments of Mathematics and Philosophy at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), where he later became the [Patrick Suppes](wikipedia://en/4824664) Professor of Humanities and Sciences. While the majority of his career was spent at Stanford, he also spent time as a post-doctoral fellow at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton), a visiting professor at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), and a visiting fellow at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) ([Wolfson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfson_College,_Oxford) and [All Souls College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Souls_College,_Oxford)).[^o7]

Feferman died on 26 July 2016 at his home in [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford,_California), following an illness that lasted three months and a [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke).[^o4][^o8][^o9] At his death, he had been a member of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for 37 years.[^o10]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Feferman was editor-in-chief of the five-volume *Collected Works* of [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736), published by [Oxford University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press) between 2001 and 2013.

In 2004, together with his wife [Anita Burdman Feferman](wikipedia://en/61980750), he published a biography of [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), *Alfred Tarski: Life and Logic*.[^o24]

Influenced by the writings of [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544), he worked on [predicative mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicative_mathematics). In particular, he introduced the [Feferman–Schütte ordinal](wikipedia://en/16084455) as a measure of the strength of certain predicative systems.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Feferman was awarded [Guggenheim Fellowships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) in 1972 and 1986[^o13] and the [Rolf Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Schock_Prizes) in logic and philosophy in 2003.[^o14] He was invited to give the [Gödel Lecture](wikipedia://en/69216431) in 1997[^o15] and the [Tarski Lectures](wikipedia://en/54104355) in 2006.[^o16] In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o17]
