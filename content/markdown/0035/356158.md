# Annulus (mathematics)

*Image: Annulus area.svg.* An annulus

*Image: Mamikon\_annulus\_area\_visualisation.svg.* Illustration of Mamikon's [visual calculus](wikipedia://en/10458716) method showing that the areas of two annuli with the same chord length are the same regardless of inner and outer radii.[^o1]

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Annulus"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **annulus** (: **annuli** or **annuluses**) is the region between two concentric circles. Informally, it is shaped like a ring or a [hardware washer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washer_%28hardware%29). The word "annulus" is borrowed from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) word *anulus* or *annulus* meaning 'little ring'. The adjectival form is ***annular*** (as in [annular eclipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annular_eclipse)).

:::

The open annulus is [topologically equivalent](wikipedia://en/13660) to both the open [cylinder](wikipedia://en/859283) $S^{1} \times (0,1)$ and the [punctured plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punctured_plane).

## Area {#Area}

The area of an annulus is the difference in the areas of the larger [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) of radius $R$ and the smaller one of radius $r$:

$$A = \pi R^2 - \pi r^2 = \pi\left(R^2 - r^2\right) = \pi (R+r)(R-r) .$$

*Image: annuli\_with\_same\_area\_around\_unit\_regular\_polygons.svg.* As a corollary of the chord formula, the area bounded by the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) and [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle) of every unit convex regular polygon is $\pi$/4

The area of an annulus is determined by the length of the longest [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) within the annulus, which is the [chord](wikipedia://en/297947) tangent to the inner circle, $2d$ in the accompanying diagram. That can be shown using the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034) since this line is [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to the smaller circle and perpendicular to its radius at that point, so $d$ and $r$ are sides of a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse $R$, and the area of the annulus is given by

$$A = \pi\left(R^2 - r^2\right) = \pi d^2.$$

The area can also be obtained via [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) by dividing the annulus up into an infinite number of annuli of [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) width $d\rho$ and area $2\pi\rho d\rho$ and then [integrating](wikipedia://en/15532) from $\rho = r$ to $\rho = R$:

$$A = \int_r^R\!\! 2\pi\rho\, d\rho = \pi\left(R^2 - r^2\right).$$

The area of an *annulus sector* (the region between two [circular sectors](wikipedia://en/1967249) with overlapping radii) of angle $\theta$, with $\theta$ measured in radians, is given by

$$A = \frac{\theta}{2} \left(R^2 - r^2\right).$$

## Complex structure {#Complex_structure}

In [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) an **annulus** $\operatorname{ann}(a; r, R)$ in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) is an [open region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_region) defined as

$$r < |z - a| < R.$$

If $r = 0$, the region is known as the **punctured disk** (a [disk](wikipedia://en/329542) with a [point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_%28mathematics%29) hole in the center) of radius $R$ around the point $a$.

As a subset of the complex [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852), an annulus can be considered as a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181). The complex structure of an annulus depends only on the ratio $\tfrac{r}{R}$. Each annulus $\operatorname{ann}(a; r, R)$ can be [holomorphically](wikipedia://en/14110) mapped to a standard one centered at the origin and with outer radius 1 by the map

$$z \mapsto \frac{z - a}{R}.$$

The inner radius is then $\tfrac{r}{R} < 1$.

The [Hadamard three-circle theorem](wikipedia://en/1499388) is a statement about the maximum value a holomorphic function may take inside an annulus.

The [Joukowsky transform](wikipedia://en/3392547) [conformally maps](wikipedia://en/50627) an annulus onto an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) with a slit cut between foci.
