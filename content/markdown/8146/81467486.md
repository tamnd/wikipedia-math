# Rahmat Ali Rahmat

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rahmat Ali Rahmat"}

**Rahmat Ali Rahmat** (1891 – 7 April 1963) was an Indian educator, poet, and author associated with Urdu and Odia literature. He was a prominent mathematics teacher in Odisha and also wrote Urdu poetry characterized by elements of mysticism, humour, and social satire.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Rahmat Ali Rahmat was born in 1891 in Rasulpur, [Cuttack district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuttack_district), [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency) (now in [Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odisha)).[^o4] He received his early education in Urdu, Persian, and mathematics from his elder brother, Irfan Ali Fidai. After completing schooling at [Ravenshaw Collegiate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenshaw_Collegiate_School), Cuttack (1908–1912), he passed the [matriculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculation) under [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta) in 1912, the [F.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate_of_Arts) in 1914, and graduated in 1916. He completed his [Licentiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licentiate_%28degree%29) in Training (L.T.) in 1918. During his school years, he was a contemporary and friend of [Subhas Chandra Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhas_Chandra_Bose).[^o5][^n1]

## Career {#Career}

After completing his L.T. in 1918, Rahmat began teaching at Muslim Seminary School (now [Sayeed Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayeed_Seminary)) in 1919. In 1920, he entered government service and taught in various district schools, including [Sambalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalpur) and [Puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puri_%28city%29). By 1935, he was appointed Headmaster of the Practising Middle School, Cuttack. Later, he was promoted as Special Inspecting Officer for Mohammedan Education (1943) and served as District Inspector of Schools (D.I.) in [Balasore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balasore) and [Ganjam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganjam) Plains until his retirement in May 1950.[^n2][^n3]

His notable students include [Sachidananda Routray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachidananda_Routray), Hrudananda Ray, Rajkishore Ray, Gobinda Chandra Udgata, [Nandini Satpathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandini_Satpathy), [Surendranath Dwivedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surendranath_Dwivedy), Sheikh Habibullah, and Sheikh Inayatullah.[^n4]

## Literary works {#Literary_works}

Rahmat wrote several mathematics textbooks in [Odia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language) and English, which remained in the Odisha school curriculum for about four decades (1936–1976). These included *Ganit Sopan*, *Probeshika Ganit Sopan*, *Bijoganit Sopan*, *Jyamiti Sopan*, *Man Sankha Mala*, and *Beauties of Algebra* (1952).[^n4]

He also authored *Jawahar al-Qawaid*, an Urdu grammar book, and translated [Deputy Nazir Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Nazir_Ahmad)’s *Taubat-un-Nasuh* into Odia, which remains unpublished.[^o13]

Rahmat's poetry reflects elements of Sufism, humour, and philosophical critique. Although influenced by [Akbar Allahabadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar_Allahabadi) and [Deputy Nazir Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Nazir_Ahmad), his works exhibit originality in tone and diction.[^n5]

The book *Cuttack, One Thousand Years* mentions him among notable 20th-century Urdu writers and poets from Odisha.[^o15]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Rahmat married twice. His first wife, Hajra Begum of Dariapur, Sungra, bore him one son and two daughters, of whom only Umm-e-Salam survived childhood. His second wife, Waziran Begum, was the daughter of Muhammad Ismail Khan of Talpatk. They had three sons and five daughters, including [Karamat Ali Karamat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karamat_Ali_Karamat), who became a noted Urdu poet and writer.[^o17]

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

Rahmat Ali Rahmat died on 7 April 1963 and was buried near Azan Gah in the precincts of [Qadam Rasool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadam_e_Rasool,_Cuttack), Cuttack.[^n2][^n3]

Rahmat’s contribution to education and Urdu literature in Odisha is recognized in several Urdu literary anthologies. [Malik Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Ram) noted that [Amjad Najmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amjad_Najmi) had benefited from Rahmat’s scholarship.[^o21] In his autobiographical account, Najmi himself wrote that Rahmat was known as a distinguished teacher of both mathematics and Urdu literature. He mentioned that Rahmat authored an Urdu grammar book titled *Jawahar al-Qawaid*, written in a conversational style, which became widely popular soon after publication. Najmi described him as simple, modest, and deeply fond of the prose of [Deputy Nazir Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Nazir_Ahmad), the language of [Daagh Dehlvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daagh_Dehlvi), and the humour of [Akbar Allahabadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar_Allahabadi).[^o22] He is also mentioned in *Odisha Mein Urdu Shayari* by Saeed Rahmani for his mastery of mathematics and humour in Urdu poetry.[^o23]
