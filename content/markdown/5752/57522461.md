# Eric-Jan Wagenmakers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eric-Jan Wagenmakers"}

**Eric-Jan Wagenmakers** (born May 21, 1972) is a Dutch mathematical psychologist. He is a professor at the Methodology Unit in the Department of Psychology at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) (UvA).[^o3] Since 2012, he has also been Professor of Neurocognitive Modeling: Interdisciplinary Integration at UvA's Faculty of Social and Behavioral Sciences.[^o4] A noted expert on [research methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_methods) in psychology, he has been highly critical of some dubious practices by his fellow psychologists, including [Daryl Bem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daryl_Bem)'s research purporting to find support for the [parapsychological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parapsychological) concept of [extrasensory perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrasensory_perception),[^o5][^o6] and the tendency for psychologists in general to favor the publication of studies with surprising, eye-catching results.[^o7] He has also been actively addressing the [replication crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replication_crisis) in psychology by helping to conduct a series of studies aimed at reproducing a 1988 study on the supposed effects of smiling on the perceived funniness of [cartoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon).[^o8][^o9] František Bartoš, Dr Wagenmakers, [Alexandra Sarafoglou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Sarafoglou), [Henrik Godmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Godmann), and many colleagues were awarded the 2024 [Ig Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ig_Nobel_Prize) Probability Prize for "showing, both in theory and by 350,757 experiments, that when you [flip a coin](wikipedia://en/494410), it tends to land on the same side as it started."[^o10][^o11]
