# This Book Needs No Title

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="This Book Needs No Title"}

***This Book Needs No Title: A Budget of Living Paradoxes*** is a 1980 collection of essays about [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), [paradoxes](wikipedia://en/24390), and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), by [Raymond Smullyan](wikipedia://en/56517). It was first published by [Prentice-Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prentice-Hall).

:::

In 2023, it was reissued by What Is the Name of This Press, with a new foreword by [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095).[^o3]

## Reception {#Reception}

*[Kirkus Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkus_Reviews)* called it "funny" and "provocative", commending Smullyan's descriptions of [Zen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen) and noting that the book could appeal to both children and adults, but conceded that Smullyan's work may be an "[acquired taste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquired_taste)".[^o5]

*[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post)* has described "This Book Needs No Title" as "tantalizing",[^o6] while [Michael Dirda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Dirda) has declared that (along with Smullyan's earlier "What is the Name of this Book") it has "the cleverest of all titles", positing that Smullyan may have been inspired by [Denis Diderot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Diderot)'s "[Ceci n'est pas un conte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceci_n'est_pas_un_conte)".[^o7]
