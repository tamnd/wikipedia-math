# Gerhard Larcher

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gerhard Larcher"}

**Gerhard Larcher** is an Austrian mathematician and professor of [financial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics) at the [Johannes Kepler University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kepler_University) (JKU) in [Linz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linz), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria).[^o2] He is the head of the Institute of Financial Mathematics.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Gerhard Larcher studied mathematics at the [University of Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Salzburg) from 1978 to 1982 and received his doctorate with the highest distinction [sub auspiciis Praesidentis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_auspiciis_Praesidentis) under [Harald Niederreiter](wikipedia://en/47275252) in 1985; he completed his postdoc in mathematics four years later.

From 1983 to 2000, Larcher worked at the University of Salzburg as an assistant professor and lecturer and from 1996 as an associate professor of mathematics. During this time he headed the Institute of Mathematics for two years. Since 1999 he was speaker and leader of the [FWF-Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Science_Fund) (FSP) *[Number-Theoretic](wikipedia://en/21527) [Algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) and their Applications*.

In 2000, Larcher was appointed as a full [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of financial mathematics at the Johannes Kepler University (JKU) in Linz, Austria. Three years later, he founded the [asset management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_management) company Art In Finance in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), which developed and implemented [alternative investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_investment) strategies. After years of strong profits, the [option](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_%28finance%29) strategies of Art In Finance sustained massive losses during the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis).[^o4] The strategies were then adapted accordingly and successfully reintroduced. At the start of 2017, he withdrew from the company and the asset management business. From 2003 to 2005, he was the department head of financial mathematics at the Johann Radon Institute for Computational and Applied Mathematics at the [Austrian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o5]

Since 2014 he has been the speaker of the Austrian special research area *Quasi-Monte Carlo Methods: Theory and Applications*[^o6] at the [Austrian Science Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Science_Fund).[^o7] In addition to his work at the Johannes Kepler University, Larcher runs seminars and workshops in the field of quantitative finance.

He is the author of the monograph *Quantitative Finance: Strategien, Investments, Analysen*[^o8] published by [Springer-Gabler Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Verlag), of the trilogy *The Art of Quantitative Finance. Strategies, Investmens, Research* by Springer and *Die [Black-Scholes-Theorie](wikipedia://en/113515), In 100 Schritten vom Münzwurf zum Wirtschaftsnobelpreis*[^o9] also published by Springer.

In 2019, he founded the *Linz School of Quantitative Finance (LSQF)*, a work group at the JKU Linz that deals with finance consulting and the creation of highly specialized finance [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software).[^o10] Among other things, the LSQF team developed the Fynup Ratio,[^o11] a new method for measuring the quality of [investment funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_fund) based on [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) techniques.

His work and research focuses on the development and analysis of trading strategies, the [valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_%28finance%29) of [derivative finance products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_%28finance%29), [Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/56098) and [quasi-Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/487599) methods and number theory.[^o12]
