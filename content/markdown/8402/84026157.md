# Suzanne Sindi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Suzanne Sindi"}

**Suzanne Soraya Fernandes-Sindi** is an American applied mathematician whose research uses mathematics and [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) to model biological phenomena including [prion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prion) aggregation, [blood coagulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coagulation), [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466), and [structural variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_variation). She is a professor of applied mathematics in the School of Natural Sciences at the [University of California, Merced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Merced), and the former chair of the Department of Applied Mathematics at UC Merced. She chairs the Equity, Diversity, and Inclusion Activity Group of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Sindi is originally from [Placentia, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placentia,_California); her parents were immigrants from Saudi Arabia and from the [Azores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores) of Portugal. She describes her interest in mathematical biology as inspired by reading the 1990 novel *[Jurassic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic_Park_%28novel%29)* as a middle school student. As an undergraduate at [California State University, Fullerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_Fullerton), she received a National Science Foundation Graduate Fellowship, funding her graduate study at the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park). There, she received a master's degree in 2004 and completed her Ph.D. in 2006. Her doctoral dissertation, *Describing and Modeling Repetitive Sequences in DNA*, was supervised by [James A. Yorke](wikipedia://en/641019).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Sindi was named as a Fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698), in the 2027 class of fellows, "for her sustained national leadership in advancing women and underrepresented groups in the mathematical sciences through founding Cal-Bridge Mathematics, her stewardship of the Workshops for Women in Mathematical Biology, and her inclusive departmental leadership as Chair of UC Merced Applied Mathematics".
