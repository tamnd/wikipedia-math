# Anne's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anne's theorem"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), **Anne's theorem** describes an equality of certain areas within a [convex quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278). This theorem is named after the French mathematician [Pierre-Léon Anne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Léon_Anne) (1806–1850).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

*Image: Anne\_theorem.svg.* A point $L$ lies on the [Newton line](wikipedia://en/35411198) if the two sums of the areas of opposing triangles are equal, according to Anne's theorem.

The theorem is stated as follows: Let $ABCD$ be a convex quadrilateral with [diagonals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonals) $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BD}$, that is not a [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496). Furthermore, let $E$ and $F$ be the [midpoints](wikipedia://en/437979) of the diagonals, and let $L$ be an arbitrary point in the interior of $ABCD$, resulting in that $L$ forms four [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) with the edges of $ABCD$. If the two sums of areas of opposite triangles are equal:

$$\left|\triangle BCL \right| + \left|\triangle DAL \right| = \left|\triangle LAB \right| + \left|\triangle DLC \right|,$$

then the point $L$ is located on the [Newton line](wikipedia://en/35411198), that is the line which connects $E$ and $F$.

For a parallelogram, the Newton line does not exist since both midpoints of the diagonals coincide with point of intersection of the diagonals. Moreover, the area identity of the theorem holds in this case for any inner point of the quadrilateral.

The converse of Anne's theorem is true as well, that is for any point on the Newton line which is an inner point of the quadrilateral, the area identity holds.
