# The First Moderns

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="The First Moderns"}

***The First Moderns: Profiles in the Origins of Twentieth-Century Thought*** is a book on [Modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism) by the historian [William Everdell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Everdell), published in 1997 by the [University of Chicago Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago_Press). A *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)* Notable Book of 1997, and included by the [New York Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Public_Library) on its list of "25 Books to Remember from 1997", *The First Moderns* suggests that "the heart of Modernism is the postulate of ontological discontinuity".[^o3]

:::

## Background and overview {#Background_and_overview}

Everdell, dean of humanities at [Saint Ann's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Ann's_School_%28Brooklyn%29) in Brooklyn Heights,[^o5] posits that Modernism first emerged in the field of mathematics rather than the arts, specifically in the work of the German mathematician [Richard Dedekind](wikipedia://en/23475106), who, in 1872, demonstrated that mathematicians operate without a continuum. This represents the formalization of Everdell's axiom of "ontological discontinuity", which he goes on to examine in a multiplicity of contexts. He examines this emerging framework of discreteness in science ([Ludwig Boltzmann](wikipedia://en/544255)'s mechanics, [Cajal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Ramón_y_Cajal)'s neuroscience, [Hugo de Vries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_de_Vries)' conception of the gene and [Max Planck](wikipedia://en/19848)'s quantum work, [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s physics), mathematics, logic and philosophy ([Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216), [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416), [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163) and the [linguistic turn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_turn), [Husserl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husserl) and the beginnings of [phenomenology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomenology_%28philosophy%29)), in addition to the arts ([James Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Joyce)'s novels, [Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picasso)'s *[Les Demoiselles d'Avignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Demoiselles_d'Avignon)*, [Schoenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schoenberg)'s 12-tone music).

## Reviews {#Reviews}

Critics largely reviewed *The First Moderns* favorably, appreciating Everdell's interdisciplinary approach, in publications including the *[New York Review of Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Review_of_Books)*, the *New York Times*, the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* and the *[Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Post)*.[^o7] The Pulitzer Prize-winning book critic [Michael Dirda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Dirda) considers it among his "favorites".[^o8]
