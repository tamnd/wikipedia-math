# Charles Jasper Joly

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Jasper Joly"}

**Charles Jasper Joly** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) [FRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society#Fellows) [MRIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy#Membership) (27 June 1864 – 4 January 1906) was an Irish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) who was [Andrews Professor of Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrews_Professor_of_Astronomy) from 1897 until his death in 1906.[^o3][^o4] He was an important figure in the study of [quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternions).[^o5]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Joly was born at St Catherine's Rectory, Hop Hill, [Tullamore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullamore), [County Offaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Offaly), the eldest of six children of Rev. John Swift Joly (1818-1887) and Elizabeth Slator (1835-1904). He was a second cousin to [John Joly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Joly). He was educated at Galway Grammar School. In 1882, he was enrolled at [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin) on a mathematical scholarship and graduated with first place in mathematics in 1886. Winning a studentship and following his great interest in experimental physics, he moved to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) to work in [Helmholtz](wikipedia://en/53311)’s laboratory.[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

In 1897, Joly was appointed [Andrews Professor of Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrews_Professor_of_Astronomy) at [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin). Although there was some dispute as to his suitability, his mathematical skill was recognised, particularly his work on the mathematics of [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817).

Over the next five years, he wrote numerous mathematical papers, particularly on the applicability of the theory of quaternions to several areas of mathematics: theory of strain, [spherical harmonics](wikipedia://en/203056), [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamics) and [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism).

In 1900, he went on the RIA/RDS eclipse expedition to Spain and later took part in the British Association visit to South Africa in 1905.

In 1902, he became Secretary of the [Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

On 20 March 1897, Joly married Jessie Sophie Meade. They had three daughters.

A keen member of the [Alpine Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_Club), he spent his holidays in the [Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alps), scaling the most difficult peaks. He was especially fond of rock climbing.

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

- 1895: Elected a [member of the Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy#Membership).
- 1898: Elected a [fellow of the Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society#Fellows).
- 1904: Elected a [fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society).

## Death {#Death}

Shortly after his return from South Africa, he and his daughter contracted [typhoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoid). He died on 4 January 1906. His daughter survived.[^o7]

He was buried at [Mount Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Jerome) cemetery, Dublin.
