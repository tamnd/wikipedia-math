# Kullback's inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kullback's inequality"}

In [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **Kullback's inequality** is a lower bound on the [Kullback–Leibler divergence](wikipedia://en/1115052) expressed in terms of the [large deviations](wikipedia://en/3772909) [rate function](wikipedia://en/3772816).[^o2] If *P* and *Q* are [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) on the [real line](wikipedia://en/287188), such that *P* is [absolutely continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_continuous) with respect to *Q*, i.e. *P* \<\< *Q*, and whose first moments exist, then

$$D_{KL}(P\parallel Q) \ge \Psi_Q^*(\mu'_1(P)),$$

where $\Psi_Q^*$ is the rate function, i.e. the [convex conjugate](wikipedia://en/1001490) of the [cumulant](wikipedia://en/359684)-generating function, of $Q$, and $\mu'_1(P)$ is the first [moment](wikipedia://en/368684) of $P.$

:::

The [Cramér–Rao bound](wikipedia://en/581124) is a corollary of this result.

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Let *P* and *Q* be [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) (measures) on the real line, whose first moments exist, and such that [*P* \<\< *Q*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_continuous#Absolute_continuity_of_measures). Consider the **[natural exponential family](wikipedia://en/9413032)** of *Q* given by

$$Q_\theta(A) = \frac{\int_A e^{\theta x}Q(dx)}{\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{\theta x}Q(dx)} = \frac{1}{M_Q(\theta)} \int_A e^{\theta x}Q(dx)$$

for every measurable set *A*, where $M_Q$ is the **[moment-generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment-generating_function)** of *Q*. (Note that *Q*<sub>0</sub> = *Q*.) Then

$$D_{KL}(P\parallel Q) = D_{KL}(P\parallel Q_\theta) + \int_{\operatorname{supp}P}\left(\log\frac{\mathrm dQ_\theta}{\mathrm dQ}\right)\mathrm dP.$$

By [Gibbs' inequality](wikipedia://en/2035678) we have $D_{KL}(P\parallel Q_\theta) \ge 0$ so that

$$D_{KL}(P\parallel Q) \ge \int_{\operatorname{supp}P}\left(\log\frac{\mathrm dQ_\theta}{\mathrm dQ}\right)\mathrm dP = \int_{\operatorname{supp}P}\left(\log\frac{e^{\theta x}}{M_Q(\theta)}\right) P(dx)$$

Simplifying the right side, we have, for every real *θ* where $M_Q(\theta) < \infty:$

$$D_{KL}(P\parallel Q) \ge \mu'_1(P) \theta - \Psi_Q(\theta),$$

where $\mu'_1(P)$ is the first moment, or mean, of *P*, and $\Psi_Q = \log M_Q$ is called the **[cumulant-generating function](wikipedia://en/359684)**. Taking the [supremum](wikipedia://en/39382) completes the process of [convex conjugation](wikipedia://en/1001490) and yields the [rate function](wikipedia://en/3772816):

$$D_{KL}(P\parallel Q) \ge \sup_\theta \left\{ \mu'_1(P) \theta - \Psi_Q(\theta) \right\} = \Psi_Q^*(\mu'_1(P)).$$

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=named-heading name="Corollary: the Cramér–Rao bound"}

## Corollary: the Cramér–Rao bound {#Corollary:_the_Cramér–Rao_bound}

::: {#o8 .statement cue=named-heading name="Start with Kullback's inequality"}

### Start with Kullback's inequality {#Start_with_Kullback's_inequality}

Let *X*<sub>θ</sub> be a family of probability distributions on the real line indexed by the real parameter θ, and satisfying certain [regularity conditions](wikipedia://en/581124#Regularity_conditions). Then

$$\lim_{h\to 0} \frac {D_{KL}(X_{\theta+h} \parallel X_\theta)} {h^2} \ge \lim_{h\to 0} \frac {\Psi^*_\theta (\mu_{\theta+h})}{h^2},$$

where $\Psi^*_\theta$ is the [convex conjugate](wikipedia://en/1001490) of the [cumulant-generating function](wikipedia://en/359684) of $X_\theta$ and $\mu_{\theta+h}$ is the first moment of $X_{\theta+h}.$

:::

### Left side {#Left_side}

The left side of this inequality can be simplified as follows:

$$\begin{aligned} \lim_{h\to 0} \frac {D_{KL}(X_{\theta+h}\parallel X_\theta)} {h^2} &=\lim_{h\to 0} \frac 1 {h^2} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \log \left( \frac{\mathrm dX_{\theta+h}}{\mathrm dX_\theta} \right) \mathrm dX_{\theta+h} \\ &=-\lim_{h\to 0} \frac 1 {h^2} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \log \left( \frac{\mathrm dX_{\theta}}{\mathrm dX_{\theta+h}} \right) \mathrm dX_{\theta+h} \\ &=-\lim_{h\to 0} \frac 1 {h^2} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \log\left( 1- \left (1-\frac{\mathrm dX_{\theta}}{\mathrm dX_{\theta+h}} \right ) \right) \mathrm dX_{\theta+h} \\ &= \lim_{h\to 0} \frac 1 {h^2} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left[ \left( 1 - \frac{\mathrm dX_\theta}{\mathrm dX_{\theta+h}} \right) +\frac 1 2 \left( 1 - \frac{\mathrm dX_\theta}{\mathrm dX_{\theta+h}} \right) ^ 2 + o \left( \left( 1 - \frac{\mathrm dX_\theta}{\mathrm dX_{\theta+h}} \right) ^ 2 \right) \right]\mathrm dX_{\theta+h} && \text{Taylor series for } \log(1-t) \\ &= \lim_{h\to 0} \frac 1 {h^2} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left[ \frac 1 2 \left( 1 - \frac{\mathrm dX_\theta}{\mathrm dX_{\theta+h}} \right)^2 \right]\mathrm dX_{\theta+h} \\ &= \lim_{h\to 0} \frac 1 {h^2} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left[ \frac 1 2 \left( \frac{\mathrm dX_{\theta+h} - \mathrm dX_\theta}{\mathrm dX_{\theta+h}} \right)^2 \right]\mathrm dX_{\theta+h} \\ &= \frac 1 2 \mathcal I_X(\theta) \end{aligned}$$

which is half the [Fisher information](wikipedia://en/598971) of the parameter *θ*.

### Right side {#Right_side}

The right side of the inequality can be developed as follows:

$$\lim_{h\to 0} \frac {\Psi^*_\theta (\mu_{\theta+h})}{h^2} = \lim_{h\to 0} \frac 1 {h^2} {\sup_t \{\mu_{\theta+h}t - \Psi_\theta(t)\} }.$$

This supremum is attained at a value of *t*=τ where the first derivative of the cumulant-generating function is $\Psi'_\theta(\tau) = \mu_{\theta+h},$ but we have $\Psi'_\theta(0) = \mu_\theta,$ so that

$$\Psi''_\theta(0) = \frac{d\mu_\theta}{d\theta} \lim_{h \to 0} \frac h \tau.$$

Moreover,

$$\lim_{h\to 0} \frac {\Psi^*_\theta (\mu_{\theta+h})}{h^2} = \frac 1 {2\Psi''_\theta(0)}\left(\frac {d\mu_\theta}{d\theta}\right)^2 = \frac 1 {2\operatorname{Var}(X_\theta)}\left(\frac {d\mu_\theta}{d\theta}\right)^2.$$

### Putting both sides back together {#Putting_both_sides_back_together}

We have:

$$\frac 1 2 \mathcal I_X(\theta) \ge \frac 1 {2\operatorname{Var}(X_\theta)}\left(\frac {d\mu_\theta}{d\theta}\right)^2,$$

which can be rearranged as:

$$\operatorname{Var}(X_\theta) \ge \frac{(d\mu_\theta / d\theta)^2} {\mathcal I_X(\theta)}.$$

:::
