# Genaille–Lucas rulers

*Image: Genaille-Lucas rulers full.svg.* A complete set of Genaille–Lucas rulers, including an additional index rod.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Genaille–Lucas rulers"}

**Genaille–Lucas rulers** (also known as **Genaille's rods**) are an [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) tool invented by [Henri Genaille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Genaille), a French railway engineer, in 1891. The device is a variant of [Napier's bones](wikipedia://en/252176). By representing the [carry](wikipedia://en/1629687) graphically, the user can read off the results of simple [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) problems directly, with no intermediate [mental calculations](wikipedia://en/567292).

:::

## History {#History}

In 1885, French mathematician [Édouard Lucas](wikipedia://en/150102) posed an arithmetic problem during a session of the *[Académie française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_française)*. Genaille, already known for having invented a number of arithmetic tools, created his rulers in the course of solving the problem. He presented his invention to the *Académie française* in 1891. The popularity of Genaille's rods was widespread but short-lived, as [mechanical calculators](wikipedia://en/432897) soon began to displace manual arithmetic methods.[^o3]

## Design {#Design}

A full set of Genaille–Lucas rulers consists of eleven strips. On each strip is printed a column of triangles and a column of numbers.

## Multiplication {#Multiplication}

By arranging the rulers in the proper order, the user can find unit multiples of short natural numbers by sight.

## Division {#Division}

*Image: Genaille division rods.svg.* Genaille–Lucas rulers for division indicate their paths with lines, not arrows.

Soon after their development by Genaille, the rulers were adapted to a set of rods that can perform division. The division rods are aligned similarly to the multiplication rods, with the index rod on the left denoting the [divisor](wikipedia://en/49492), and the following rods spelling out the digits of the dividend. After these, a special "remainder" rod is placed on the right. The [quotient](wikipedia://en/332640) is read from left to right, following the lines from one rod to the next. The path of digits ends with a number on the remainder rod, which is the [remainder](wikipedia://en/502897) given by the division.
