# Brane

brane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brane"}

In [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) and related theories (such as [supergravity](wikipedia://en/432632)), a **brane** is a physical object that generalizes the notion of a zero-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [point particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_particle), a one-dimensional [string](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_%28physics%29), or a two-dimensional membrane to higher-dimensional objects. Branes are [dynamical](wikipedia://en/9087) objects which can propagate through [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) according to the rules of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202). They have [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass) and can have other attributes such as [charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_%28physics%29).

:::

Mathematically, branes can be represented within [categories](wikipedia://en/53759), and are studied in [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) for insight into [homological mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/2164886) and [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917).

The word "brane" originated in 1987 as a contraction of "[membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane)".[^o2]

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="P-brane"}

## *p*-branes {#p-branes}

A point particle is a 0-brane, of dimension zero; a string, named after vibrating [musical strings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_%28music%29), is a 1-brane; a membrane, named after [vibrating membranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_membrane) such as [drumheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drumhead), is a 2-brane.[^o5] The corresponding object of arbitrary dimension *p* is called a *p*-brane, a term coined by [M. J. Duff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Duff_%28physicist%29) *et al.* in 1988.[^o6]

A *p*-brane sweeps out a (*p*+1)-dimensional volume in spacetime called its **worldvolume**. Physicists often study [fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29) analogous to the [electromagnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_field), which live on the worldvolume of a brane.[^o7]

:::

## D-branes {#D-branes}

*Image: D3-brane et D2-brane.PNG.* Open strings attached to a pair of [D-branes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-brane)

In [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305), a [string](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_%28physics%29) may be open (forming a segment with two endpoints) or closed (forming a closed loop). [D-branes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-brane) are an important class of branes that arise when one considers open strings. As an open string propagates through spacetime, its endpoints are required to lie on a D-brane. The letter "D" in D-brane refers to the [Dirichlet boundary condition](wikipedia://en/423504), which the D-brane satisfies.[^o9]

One crucial point about D-branes is that the dynamics on the D-brane worldvolume is described by a [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155), a kind of highly symmetric physical theory which is also used to describe the behavior of elementary particles in the [Standard Model of particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Model_of_particle_physics). This connection has led to important insights into gauge theory and [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267). For example, it led to the discovery of the [AdS/CFT correspondence](wikipedia://en/644443), a theoretical tool that physicists use to translate difficult problems in gauge theory into more mathematically tractable problems in string theory.[^o10]

## Categorical description {#Categorical_description}

Mathematically, branes can be described using the notion of a [category](wikipedia://en/53759).[^o12] This is a mathematical structure consisting of *objects*, and for any pair of objects, a set of *[morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphisms)* between them. In most examples, the objects are mathematical structures (such as [sets](wikipedia://en/26691), [vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_spaces), or [topological spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_spaces)) and the morphisms are [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) between these structures.[^o13] One can likewise consider categories where the objects are D-branes and the morphisms between two branes $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavefunction) of open strings stretched between $\alpha$ and $\beta$.[^o14]

*Image: Calabi yau.jpg.* A cross section of a [Calabi–Yau manifold](wikipedia://en/188932)

In one version of string theory known as the [topological B-model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_string_theory), the D-branes are [complex submanifolds](wikipedia://en/509742) of certain six-dimensional shapes called [Calabi–Yau manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabi–Yau_manifolds), together with additional data that arise physically from having [charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_%28physics%29) at the endpoints of strings.[^o15] Intuitively, one can think of a submanifold as a surface embedded inside of a Calabi–Yau manifold, although submanifolds can also exist in dimensions different from two.[^o16] In mathematical language, the category having these branes as its objects is known as the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) of [coherent sheaves](wikipedia://en/603780) on the Calabi–Yau.[^o17] In another version of string theory called the [topological A-model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_string_theory), the D-branes can again be viewed as submanifolds of a Calabi–Yau manifold. Roughly speaking, they are what mathematicians call [special Lagrangian submanifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Lagrangian_submanifold).[^o18] This means, among other things, that they have half the dimension of the space in which they sit, and they are length-, area-, or volume-minimizing.[^o19] The category having these branes as its objects is called the [Fukaya category](wikipedia://en/31598730).[^o20]

The derived category of coherent sheaves is constructed using tools from [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), a branch of mathematics that describes geometric shapes in [algebraic terms](wikipedia://en/18716923) and solves geometric problems using [algebraic equations](wikipedia://en/404001).[^o21] On the other hand, the Fukaya category is constructed using [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), a branch of mathematics that arose from studies of [classical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_physics). Symplectic geometry studies spaces equipped with a [symplectic form](wikipedia://en/28356), a mathematical tool that can be used to compute [area](wikipedia://en/1209) in two-dimensional examples.[^o22]

The [homological mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/2164886) conjecture of [Maxim Kontsevich](wikipedia://en/104509) states that the derived category of coherent sheaves on one Calabi–Yau manifold is equivalent in a certain sense to the Fukaya category of a completely different Calabi–Yau manifold.[^o23] This equivalence provides an unexpected bridge between two branches of geometry, namely complex and symplectic geometry.[^o24]
