# Tolerance relation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tolerance relation"}

In [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) and [lattice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_theory), a **tolerance relation** on an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) is a [reflexive](wikipedia://en/200458) [symmetric relation](wikipedia://en/200459) that is compatible with all operations of the structure. Thus a tolerance is like a [congruence](wikipedia://en/48167), except that the assumption of [transitivity](wikipedia://en/200463) is dropped.[^o2] On a [set](wikipedia://en/26691), an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) with empty family of operations, tolerance relations are simply reflexive symmetric relations. A set that possesses a tolerance relation can be described as a **tolerance space**.[^o3] Tolerance relations provide a convenient general tool for studying [indiscernibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiscernibility)/indistinguishability phenomena. The importance of those for mathematics had been first recognized by [Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740).[^o4]

:::

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A **tolerance relation** on an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) $(A,F)$ is usually defined to be a [reflexive](wikipedia://en/200458) [symmetric relation](wikipedia://en/200459) on $A$ that is compatible with every operation in $F$. A tolerance relation can also be seen as a [cover](wikipedia://en/317552) of $A$ that satisfies certain conditions. The two definitions are equivalent, since for a fixed [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364), the tolerance relations in the two definitions are in [one-to-one correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-to-one_correspondence). The tolerance relations on an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) $(A,F)$ form an [algebraic lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_lattice) $\operatorname{Tolr}(A)$ under inclusion. Since every [congruence relation](wikipedia://en/48167) is a tolerance relation, the congruence lattice $\operatorname{Cong}(A)$ is a subset of the tolerance lattice $\operatorname{Tolr}(A)$, but $\operatorname{Cong}(A)$ is not necessarily a sublattice of $\operatorname{Tolr}(A)$.[^o7]

### As binary relations {#As_binary_relations}

A **tolerance relation** on an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) $(A,F)$ is a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) $\sim$ on $A$ that satisfies the following conditions.

- ([Reflexivity](wikipedia://en/200458)) $a\sim a$ for all $a\in A$
- ([Symmetry](wikipedia://en/200459)) if $a\sim b$ then $b\sim a$ for all $a,b\in A$
- ([Compatibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compatible_relation)) for each $n$-ary operation $f\in F$ and $a_1,\dots,a_n,b_1,\dots,b_n\in A$, if $a_i\sim b_i$ for each $i=1,\dots,n$ then $f(a_1,\dots,a_n)\sim f(b_1,\dots,b_n)$. That is, the set $\{(a,b)\colon a\sim b\}$ is a subalgebra of the [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887) $A^2$ of two $A$.

A **[congruence relation](wikipedia://en/48167)** is a tolerance relation that is also [transitive](wikipedia://en/200463).

### As covers {#As_covers}

A **tolerance relation** on an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) $(A,F)$ is a [cover](wikipedia://en/317552) $\mathcal C$ of $A$ that satisfies the following three conditions.[^o10]

- For every $C\in\mathcal C$ and $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal C$, if $\textstyle C\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal S$, then $\textstyle\bigcap\mathcal S\subseteq C$.
  - In particular, no two distinct elements of $\mathcal C$ are comparable. (To see this, take $\mathcal S=\{D\}$.)
- For every $S\subseteq A$, if $S$ is not contained in any set in $\mathcal C$, then there is a two-element subset $\{s,t\}\subseteq S$ such that $\{s,t\}$ is not contained in any set in $\mathcal C$.
- For every $n$-ary $f\in F$ and $C_1,\dots,C_n\in\mathcal C$, there is a $(f/{\sim})(C_1,\dots,C_n)\in\mathcal C$ such that $\{f(c_1,\dots,c_n)\colon c_i\in C_i\}\subseteq(f/{\sim})(C_1,\dots,C_n)$. (Such a $(f/{\sim})(C_1,\dots,C_n)$ need not be unique.)

Every [partition](wikipedia://en/340240) of $A$ satisfies the first two conditions, but not conversely. A **[congruence relation](wikipedia://en/48167)** is a tolerance relation that also forms a set partition.

### Equivalence of the two definitions {#Equivalence_of_the_two_definitions}

Let $\sim$ be a tolerance [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) on an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) $(A,F)$. Let $A/{\sim}$ be the family of [maximal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_element) subsets $C\subseteq A$ such that $c\sim d$ for every $c,d\in C$. Using graph theoretical terms, $A/{\sim}$ is the set of all [maximal cliques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_clique) of the [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) $(A,\sim)$. If $\sim$ is a [congruence relation](wikipedia://en/48167), $A/{\sim}$ is just the [quotient set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_set) of [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260). Then $A/{\sim}$ is a [cover](wikipedia://en/317552) of $A$ and satisfies all the three conditions in the cover definition. (The last condition is shown using [Zorn's lemma](wikipedia://en/51442).) Conversely, let $\mathcal C$ be a [cover](wikipedia://en/317552) of $A$ and suppose that $\mathcal C$ forms a tolerance on $A$. Consider a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) $\sim_{\mathcal C}$ on $A$ for which $a\sim_{\mathcal C}b$ if and only if $a,b\in C$ for some $C\in\mathcal C$. Then $\sim_{\mathcal C}$ is a tolerance on $A$ as a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931). The map ${\sim}\mapsto A/{\sim}$ is a [one-to-one correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-to-one_correspondence) between the tolerances as [binary relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_relations) and as [covers](wikipedia://en/317552) whose inverse is $\mathcal C\mapsto{\sim_{\mathcal C}}$. Therefore, the two definitions are equivalent. A tolerance is [transitive](wikipedia://en/200463) as a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) if and only if it is a [partition](wikipedia://en/340240) as a [cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cover_%28mathematics%29). Thus the two characterizations of [congruence relations](wikipedia://en/48167) also agree.

### Quotient algebras over tolerance relations {#Quotient_algebras_over_tolerance_relations}

Let $(A,F)$ be an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) and let $\sim$ be a tolerance relation on $A$. Suppose that, for each $n$-ary operation $f\in F$ and $C_1,\dots,C_n\in A/{\sim}$, there is a unique $(f/{\sim})(C_1,\dots,C_n)\in A/{\sim}$ such that

$$\{f(c_1,\dots,c_n)\colon c_i\in C_i\}\subseteq(f/{\sim})(C_1,\dots,C_n)$$

Then this provides a natural definition of the **quotient algebra**

$$(A/{\sim},F/{\sim})$$

of $(A,F)$ over $\sim$. In the case of [congruence relations](wikipedia://en/48167), the uniqueness condition always holds true and the quotient algebra defined here coincides with the usual one.

A main difference from [congruence relations](wikipedia://en/48167) is that for a tolerance relation the uniqueness condition may fail, and even if it does not, the quotient algebra may not inherit the identities defining the [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753) that $(A,F)$ belongs to, so that the quotient algebra may fail to be a member of the variety again. Therefore, for a [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753) $\mathcal V$ of [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364), we may consider the following two conditions.[^o7]

- (Tolerance factorability) for any $(A,F)\in\mathcal V$ and any tolerance relation $\sim$ on $(A,F)$, the uniqueness condition is true, so that the quotient algebra $(A/{\sim},F/{\sim})$ is defined.
- (Strong tolerance factorability) for any $(A,F)\in\mathcal V$ and any tolerance relation $\sim$ on $(A,F)$, the uniqueness condition is true, and $(A/{\sim},F/{\sim})\in\mathcal V$.

Every strongly tolerance factorable variety is tolerance factorable, but not vice versa.

## Examples {#Examples}

### Sets {#Sets}

A [set](wikipedia://en/26691) is an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) with no operations at all. In this case, tolerance relations are simply [reflexive](wikipedia://en/200458) [symmetric relations](wikipedia://en/200459) and it is trivial that the variety of sets is strongly tolerance factorable.

### Groups {#Groups}

On a [group](wikipedia://en/19447), every tolerance relation is a [congruence relation](wikipedia://en/48167). In particular, this is true for all [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364) that are groups when some of their operations are forgot, e.g. [rings](wikipedia://en/48404), [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), [modules](wikipedia://en/276410), [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/54476844), etc.[^o17] Therefore, the varieties of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), [rings](wikipedia://en/48404), [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) and [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/54476844) are also strongly tolerance factorable trivially.

### Lattices {#Lattices}

For a tolerance relation $\sim$ on a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) $L$, every set in $L/{\sim}$ is a [convex sublattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_sublattice) of $L$. Thus, for all $A\in L/{\sim}$, we have

$$A=\mathop\uparrow A\cap\mathop\downarrow A$$

In particular, the following results hold.

- $a\sim b$ if and only if $a\vee b\sim a\wedge b$.
- If $a\sim b$ and $a\le c,d\le b$, then $c\sim d$.

The variety of [lattices](wikipedia://en/469304) is strongly tolerance factorable. That is, given any [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) $(L,\vee_L,\wedge_L)$ and any tolerance relation $\sim$ on $L$, for each $A,B\in L/{\sim}$ there exist unique $A\vee_{L/{\sim}}B,A\wedge_{L/{\sim}}B\in L/{\sim}$ such that

$$\{a\vee_Lb\colon a\in A,\;b\in B\}\subseteq A\vee_{L/{\sim}}B$$

$$\{a\wedge_Lb\colon a\in A,\;b\in B\}\subseteq A\wedge_{L/{\sim}}B$$

and the quotient algebra

$$(L/{\sim},\vee_{L/{\sim}},\wedge_{L/{\sim}})$$

is a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) again.[^o19][^o20][^o21]

In particular, we can form quotient lattices of [distributive lattices](wikipedia://en/310883) and [modular lattices](wikipedia://en/1089311) over tolerance relations. However, unlike in the case of [congruence relations](wikipedia://en/48167), the quotient lattices need not be distributive or modular again. In other words, the varieties of [distributive lattices](wikipedia://en/310883) and [modular lattices](wikipedia://en/1089311) are tolerance factorable, but not strongly tolerance factorable.[^o19][^o7] Actually, every subvariety of the variety of lattices is tolerance factorable, and the only strongly tolerance factorable subvariety other than itself is the trivial subvariety (consisting of one-element lattices).[^o19] This is because every [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a sublattice of the quotient lattice over a tolerance relation of a sublattice of a [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887) of two-element lattices.[^o19]
