# Carleman's condition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carleman's condition"}

In mathematics, particularly, in [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), **Carleman's condition** gives a sufficient condition for the determinacy of the [moment problem](wikipedia://en/1244992). That is, if a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) $\mu$ satisfies Carleman's condition, there is no other measure $\nu$ having the same [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) as $\mu.$ The condition was discovered by [Torsten Carleman](wikipedia://en/6063370) in 1922.[^o2]

:::

## Hamburger moment problem {#Hamburger_moment_problem}

For the [Hamburger moment problem](wikipedia://en/3216491) (the moment problem on the whole real line), the theorem states the following:

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Carleman's condition"}

**Carleman's condition.**

Let $\mu$ be a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) on $\mathbb{R}$ such that all the moments

$$m_n = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} x^n \, d\mu(x)~, \quad n = 0,1,2,\dots$$

are finite. If

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty m_{2n}^{-\frac{1}{2n}} = + \infty,$$

then the moment problem for $(m_n)$ is *determinate*; that is, $\mu$ is the only measure on $\mathbb{R}$ with $(m_n)$ as its sequence of moments.

:::

## Stieltjes moment problem {#Stieltjes_moment_problem}

For the [Stieltjes moment problem](wikipedia://en/3242790), the sufficient condition for determinacy is

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty m_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2n}} = + \infty.$$

## Generalized Carleman's condition {#Generalized_Carleman's_condition}

In[^o7] Nasiraee et al. showed that, despite previous assumptions,[^o8] when the integrand is an arbitrary function, Carleman's condition is not sufficient, as demonstrated by a counter-example. In fact, the example violates the [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942), i.e. determinacy, property in the probability sum theorem. When the integrand is an arbitrary function, they further establish a sufficient condition for the determinacy of the moment problem, referred to as the *generalized Carleman's condition*.
