# Siegel–Walfisz theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Siegel–Walfisz theorem"}

*Image: Walfisz,Arnold\_1920\_Göttingen.jpg.* Mathematician Arnold Walfisz, 1920 at Göttingen.

In [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), the **Siegel–Walfisz theorem** was obtained by [Arnold Walfisz](wikipedia://en/4956669)[^o2] as an application of a [theorem](wikipedia://en/1967197) by [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602)[^o3] to [primes in arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/11740178). It is a refinement both of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) and of [Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet's_theorem_on_primes_in_arithmetic_progressions).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Define

$$\psi(x;q,a) = \sum_{n\,\leq\,x \atop n\,\equiv\,a\!\pmod{\!q}}\Lambda(n),$$

where $\Lambda$ denotes the [von Mangoldt function](wikipedia://en/1928862), and let *$\varphi$* denote [Euler's totient function](wikipedia://en/53452).

Then the theorem states that given any [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) *N* there exists a positive constant *C*<sub>*N*</sub> depending only on *N* such that

$$\psi(x;q,a)=\frac{x}{\varphi(q)}+O\left(x\exp\left(-C_N(\log x)^\frac{1}{2}\right)\right),$$

whenever (*a*, *q*) = 1 and

$$q\le(\log x)^N.$$

:::

## Remarks {#Remarks}

The constant *C*<sub>*N*</sub> is not [effectively computable](wikipedia://en/250237) because Siegel's theorem is ineffective.

From the theorem we can deduce the following bound regarding the [prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_number_theorem_for_arithmetic_progressions): If, for (*a*, *q*) = 1, by $\pi(x;q,a)$ we denote the number of primes less than or equal to *x* which are [congruent](wikipedia://en/20087) to *a* mod *q*, then

$$\pi(x;q,a) = \frac{{\rm Li}(x)}{\varphi(q)}+O\left(x\exp\left(-\frac{C_N}{2}(\log x)^\frac{1}{2}\right)\right),$$

where *N*, *a*, *q*, *C*<sub>*N*</sub> and φ are as in the theorem, and Li denotes the [logarithmic integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_integral).
