# Door space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Door space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in the field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is said to be a **door space** if every subset is [open](wikipedia://en/39358) or [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) (or [both](wikipedia://en/233398)).[^n1] The term comes from the introductory topology mnemonic that "a subset is not like a door: it can be open, closed, both, or neither".

:::

## Properties and examples {#Properties_and_examples}

Every door space is [T<sub>0</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T0-space) (because if $x$ and $y$ are two [topologically indistinguishable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically_indistinguishable) points, the singleton $\{x\}$ is neither open nor closed).

Every [subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_%28topology%29) of a door space is a door space.[^o4] So is every [quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_%28topology%29) of a door space.[^n2]

Every topology [finer than](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finer_topology) a door topology on a set $X$ is also a door topology.

Every [discrete space](wikipedia://en/56061) is a door space. These are the spaces without [accumulation point](wikipedia://en/226505), that is, whose every point is an [isolated point](wikipedia://en/390557).

Every space $X$ with exactly one accumulation point (and all the other point isolated) is a door space (since subsets consisting only of isolated points are open, and subsets containing the accumulation point are closed). Some examples are: (1) the [one-point compactification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-point_compactification) of a discrete space (also called [Fort space](wikipedia://en/10105571)), where the point at infinity is the accumulation point; (2) a space with the [excluded point topology](wikipedia://en/5539197), where the "excluded point" is the accumulation point.

Every [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) door space is either discrete or has exactly one accumulation point. (To see this, if $X$ is a space with distinct accumulation points $x$ and $y$ having respective disjoint [neighbourhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood) $U$ and $V,$ the set $(U\setminus\{x\})\cup\{y\}$ is neither closed nor open in $X.$)[^o6]

An example of door space with more than one accumulation point is given by the [particular point topology](wikipedia://en/5336200) on a set $X$ with at least three points. The open sets are the subsets containing a particular point $p\in X,$ together with the empty set. The point $p$ is an isolated point and all the other points are accumulation points. (This is a door space since every set containing $p$ is open and every set not containing $p$ is closed.) Another example would be the [topological sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_sum) of a space with the particular point topology and a discrete space.

Door spaces $(X,\tau)$ with no isolated point are exactly those with a topology of the form $\tau=\mathcal U \cup \{\emptyset\}$ for some [free ultrafilter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_ultrafilter) $\mathcal U$ on $X.$[^o7] Such spaces are necessarily infinite.

There are exactly three types of connected door spaces $(X,\tau)$:[^n3][^o9]

- a space with the [excluded point topology](wikipedia://en/5539197);
- a space with the [included point topology](wikipedia://en/5336200);
- a space with topology $\tau$ such that $\tau\setminus\{\emptyset\}$ is a [free ultrafilter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_ultrafilter) on $X.$
