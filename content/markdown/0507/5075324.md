# Collectionwise Hausdorff space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Collectionwise Hausdorff space"}

In mathematics, in the field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is said to be **collectionwise Hausdorff** if given any [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [discrete](wikipedia://en/390557) subset of $X$, there is a pairwise disjoint family of open sets with each point of the discrete subset contained in exactly one of the open sets.[^o2]

:::

Here a subset $S\subseteq X$ being *discrete* has the usual meaning of being a discrete space with the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) (i.e., all points of $S$ are isolated in $S$).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Every [T<sub>1</sub> space](wikipedia://en/148721) that is collectionwise Hausdorff is also [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637).
- Every [collectionwise normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectionwise_normal) space is collectionwise Hausdorff. (This follows from the fact that given a closed discrete subset $S$ of $X$, every singleton $\{s\}$ $(s\in S)$ is closed in $X$ and the family of such singletons is a discrete family in $X$.)
- [Metrizable spaces](wikipedia://en/19738) are collectionwise normal and hence collectionwise Hausdorff.

:::

## References {#References}

[^o2]: Tall, Franklin. (1976). "The density topology". *Pacific Journal of Mathematics*. 62 (1). p. 275–284. doi:10.2140/pjm.1976.62.275Definition 3.4
[^o3]: If $X$ is [T<sub>1</sub> space](wikipedia://en/148721), $S\subseteq X$ being closed and discrete is equivalent to the family of singletons $\{\{s\}:s\in S\}$ being a *discrete family* of subsets of $X$ (in the sense that every point of $X$ has a neighborhood that meets at most one set in the family). If $X$ is not T<sub>1</sub>, the family of singletons being a discrete family is a weaker condition. For example, if $X=\{a,b\}$ with the indiscrete topology, $S=\{a\}$ is discrete but not closed, even though the corresponding family of singletons is a discrete family in $X$.
