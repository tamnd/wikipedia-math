# Locally Hausdorff space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Locally Hausdorff space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is said to be **locally Hausdorff** if every point has a [neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood_%28topology%29) that is a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) under the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926).[^o2]

:::

## Examples and sufficient conditions {#Examples_and_sufficient_conditions}

- Every [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) is locally Hausdorff.
- There are locally Hausdorff spaces where a sequence has more than one limit. This can never happen for a Hausdorff space.
- The [line with two origins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_with_two_origins) is locally Hausdorff (it is in fact [locally metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_metrizable_space)) but not Hausdorff.
- The [etale space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etale_space) for the [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of differentiable functions on a [differential manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_manifold) is not Hausdorff, but it is locally Hausdorff.
- Let $X$ be a set given the [particular point topology](wikipedia://en/5336200) with particular point $p.$ The space $X$ is locally Hausdorff at $p,$ since $p$ is an [isolated point](wikipedia://en/390557) in $X$ and the singleton $\{p\}$ is a Hausdorff neighbourhood of $p.$ For any other point $x,$ any neighbourhood of it contains $p$ and therefore the space is not locally Hausdorff at $x.$

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A space is locally Hausdorff exactly if it can be written as a union of Hausdorff open subspaces.[^o6] And in a locally Hausdorff space each point belongs to some Hausdorff dense open subspace.[^o7]

Every locally Hausdorff space is [T<sub>1</sub>](wikipedia://en/148721).[^n1] The converse is not true in general. For example, an infinite set with the [cofinite topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofinite_topology) is a [T<sub>1</sub> space](wikipedia://en/148721) that is not locally Hausdorff.

Every locally Hausdorff space is [sober](wikipedia://en/602490).[^n2]

If $G$ is a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) that is locally Hausdorff at some point $x \in G,$ then $G$ is Hausdorff. This follows from the fact that if $y \in G,$ there exists a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) from $G$ to itself carrying $x$ to $y,$ so $G$ is locally Hausdorff at every point, and is therefore [T<sub>1</sub>](wikipedia://en/148721) (and T<sub>1</sub> topological groups are Hausdorff).

:::
