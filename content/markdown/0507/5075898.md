# Shrinking space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shrinking space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is said to have the **shrinking property**[^o2] or to be a **shrinking space** if every [open cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover) admits a shrinking. A *shrinking* of an open cover is another open cover indexed by the same indexing set, with the property that the closure of each open set in the shrinking lies inside the corresponding original open set.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The following facts are known about shrinking spaces:

- Every shrinking space is [normal](wikipedia://en/48629).[^o2]
- Every shrinking space is [countably paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_paracompact).[^o2]
- In a [normal space](wikipedia://en/48629), every locally finite, and in fact, every point-finite open cover admits a shrinking.[^o2]
- Thus, every normal [metacompact space](wikipedia://en/5074872) is a shrinking space. In particular, every Hausdorff [paracompact space](wikipedia://en/48631) is a shrinking space.[^o2]

These facts are particularly important because shrinking of open covers is a common technique in the theory of [differential manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_manifold) and while constructing functions using a [partition of unity](wikipedia://en/48636).

:::
