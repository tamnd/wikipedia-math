# Supercompact space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Supercompact space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is called **supercompact** if there is a [subbasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subbasis) such that every [open cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover) of the topological space from elements of the subbasis has a subcover with at most two subbasis elements. Supercompactness and the related notion of [superextension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superextension) was introduced by [J. de Groot](wikipedia://en/6022862) in 1967.[^n1]

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

By the [Alexander subbase theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_subbase_theorem), every supercompact space is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042). Conversely, many (but not all) compact spaces are supercompact. The following are examples of supercompact spaces:

- Compact [linearly ordered spaces](wikipedia://en/30330) with the [order topology](wikipedia://en/213650) and all continuous images of such spaces[^n2]
- Compact [metrizable spaces](wikipedia://en/19738) (due originally to (Strok Szymański 1975), see also (Mills 1979))
- A product of supercompact spaces is supercompact (like a similar statement about compactness, [Tychonoff's theorem](wikipedia://en/82739), it is equivalent to the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840).)[^n3]

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Some compact [Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/13637) are not supercompact; such an example is given by the [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897) of the natural numbers (with the discrete topology).[^n4]

A continuous image of a supercompact space need not be supercompact.

In a supercompact space (or any continuous image of one), the cluster point of any countable subset is the limit of a nontrivial convergent sequence.[^n5]

:::
