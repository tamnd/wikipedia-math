# Thompson transitivity theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thompson transitivity theorem"}

In mathematical [finite group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_group_theory), the **Thompson transitivity theorem** gives conditions under which the [centralizer](wikipedia://en/145661) of an [abelian subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_subgroup) *A* acts [transitively](wikipedia://en/12781#Transitivity_properties) on certain subgroups normalized by *A*. It originated in the proof of the [odd order theorem](wikipedia://en/1656473) by (txt), where it was used to prove the [Thompson uniqueness theorem](wikipedia://en/29764807).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose that *G* is a finite group and *p* a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) such that all [*p*-local subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-local_subgroup) are [*p*-constrained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-constrained). If *A* is a self-centralizing normal abelian subgroup of a *p*-Sylow subgroup such that *A* has rank at least 3, then the centralizer C<sub>*G*</sub>(*A*) act transitively on the maximal *A*-invariant *q* subgroups of *G* for any prime *q* ≠ *p*.

:::
