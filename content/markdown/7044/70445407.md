# Log-t distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Log-t distribution"}

In probability theory, a **log-t distribution** or **log-Student t distribution** is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) whose [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) is distributed in accordance with a [Student's t-distribution](wikipedia://en/105375). If *X* is a random variable with a Student's t-distribution, then *Y* = [exp(*X*)](wikipedia://en/9678) has a log-t distribution; likewise, if *Y* has a log-t distribution, then *X* = log(*Y*) has a Student's t-distribution.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

The log-t distribution has the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487):

$$p(x\mid \nu,\hat{\mu},\hat{\sigma}) = \frac{\Gamma(\frac{\nu + 1}{2})}{x\Gamma(\frac{\nu}{2})\sqrt{\pi\nu}\hat\sigma\,} \left(1+\frac{1}{\nu}\left( \frac{ \ln x-\hat{\mu} } {\hat{\sigma} } \right)^2\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}},$$

where $\hat{\mu}$ is the [location parameter](wikipedia://en/17516) of the underlying (non-standardized) Student's t-distribution, $\hat{\sigma}$ is the [scale parameter](wikipedia://en/769503) of the underlying (non-standardized) Student's t-distribution, and $\nu$ is the number of [degrees of freedom](wikipedia://en/176622) of the underlying Student's t-distribution.[^o2] If $\hat{\mu}=0$ and $\hat{\sigma}=1$ then the underlying distribution is the standardized Student's t-distribution.

If $\nu=1$ then the distribution is a [log-Cauchy distribution](wikipedia://en/33450252).[^o2] As $\nu$ approaches [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824), the distribution approaches a [log-normal distribution](wikipedia://en/102476).[^o2][^o5] Although the log-normal distribution has finite [moments](wikipedia://en/368684), for any finite degrees of freedom, the [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) and [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) and all higher [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of the log-t distribution are infinite or do not exist.[^o2]

The log-t distribution is a special case of the [generalized beta distribution](wikipedia://en/36221287) of the [second kind](wikipedia://en/36221287#Generalized_beta_of_the_second_kind_%28GB2%29).[^o2][^o6][^o7] The log-t distribution is an example of a [compound probability distribution](wikipedia://en/28897820) between the lognormal distribution and [inverse gamma distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_gamma_distribution) whereby the variance parameter of the lognormal distribution is a random variable distributed according to an inverse gamma distribution.[^o6][^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The log-t distribution has applications in finance.[^o6] For example, the distribution of stock market returns often shows fatter tails than a [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462), and thus tends to fit a Student's t-distribution better than a normal distribution. While the [Black-Scholes model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-Scholes_model) based on the log-normal distribution is often used to price [stock options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_options), option pricing formulas based on the log-t distribution can be a preferable alternative if the returns have fat tails.[^o11] The fact that the log-t distribution has infinite mean is a problem when using it to value options, but there are techniques to overcome that limitation, such as by truncating the probability density function at some arbitrary large value.[^o11][^o12][^o13]

The log-t distribution also has applications in [hydrology](wikipedia://en/13435) and in analyzing data on [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer) remission.[^o2][^o14]

:::

## Multivariate log-t distribution {#Multivariate_log-t_distribution}

Analogous to the log-normal distribution, [multivariate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_distribution) forms of the log-t distribution exist. In this case, the location parameter is replaced by a vector ***μ***, the scale parameter is replaced by a matrix **Σ**.[^o2]
