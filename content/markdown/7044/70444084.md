# Joseph L. Doob Prize

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph L. Doob Prize"}

The **Joseph L. Doob Prize** of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) awards \$5,000 (U.S.) every three years for "a single, relatively recent, outstanding research book that makes a seminal contribution to the research literature, reflects the highest standards of research exposition, and promises to have a deep and long-term impact in its area."[^o2] The prize, endowed in 2005 by [Paul](wikipedia://en/318598) and [Virginia Halmos](wikipedia://en/78544295), is named in honor of AMS President [Joseph L. Doob](wikipedia://en/1547599),[^o2] who was Paul Halmos's doctoral advisor in the department of mathematics at the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois). According to Paul Halmos, "Doob was the first well-informed modern mathematician in the department".[^o3] In order for a mathematical research book to be eligible for the prize, it must have been published within the past 6 calendar years of the year of its nomination.[^o2] The prize was originally named the AMS Book Prize,[^o4] but after the first award was renamed the Doob Prize.[^o5]

:::

## Recipients {#Recipients}

- 2005 —[William P. Thurston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Thurston) for *Three-dimensional Geometry and Topology* (Princeton University Press 1997)
- 2008 — [Enrico Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475) and [Walter Gubler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Gubler) for *Heights in Diophantine Geometry* (Cambridge University Press 2006)
- 2011 — [Peter Kronheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kronheimer) and [Tomasz Mrowka](wikipedia://en/20652006) for *Monopoles and Three Manifolds* (Cambridge University Press 2007)
- 2014 — [Cédric Villani](wikipedia://en/27172341) for *Optimal Transport: Old and New* (Springer Verlag 2009)
- 2017 — [John Friedlander](wikipedia://en/9704785) and [Henryk Iwaniec](wikipedia://en/9338319) for *Opera de Cribro* (AMS, 2010)
- 2020 — [René Carmona](wikipedia://en/81038023) and François Delarue for *Probabilistic Theory of Mean Field Games with Applications* (Springer, 2018)
- 2023 — [Bjorn Poonen](wikipedia://en/16283918) for "Rational Points on Varieties" (American Mathematical Society, 2017)
- 2026 — [Semyon Dyatlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semyon_Dyatlov) and [Maciej Zworski](wikipedia://en/34110829) for *Mathematical Theory of Scattering Resonances* (AMS 2019)[^o7]
