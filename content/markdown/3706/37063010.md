# Michael Rosen (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Rosen"}

**Michael Ira Rosen** (born March 7, 1938) is an American mathematician who works on [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), arithmetic theory of function fields, and [arithmetic algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Rosen earned a bachelor's degree from [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) in 1959 and a PhD from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1963 under [John Coleman Moore](wikipedia://en/13433862) with thesis *Representations of twisted group rings*. He is a mathematics professor at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University).

Rosen is known for his textbooks, especially for the book with co-author Kenneth Ireland on number theory, which was inspired by ideas of [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019);[^o4] this book, *A Classical Introduction to Modern Number Theory*, gives an introduction to [zeta functions](wikipedia://en/34532) of [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260), the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705), and the arithmetic of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225).

For his essay *[Niels Hendrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573) and equations of the fifth degree*[^o5] Rosen received the 1999 [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize).
