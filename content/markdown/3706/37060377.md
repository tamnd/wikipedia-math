# Oscar Goldman (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Oscar Goldman"}

**Oscar Goldman** (1925 – 17 December 1986, [Bryn Mawr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr,_Pennsylvania)) was an American mathematician, who worked on algebra and its applications to number theory.[^o2]

:::

Oscar Goldman received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1948 under [Claude Chevalley](wikipedia://en/366040) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). He was chair of the Mathematics Department at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) from 1952 to 1960. As chair of the department his immediate successor was [Maurice Auslander](wikipedia://en/30337642).

In 1962, Goldman left Brandeis to become a professor and chair of the mathematics department at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania). [Murray Gerstenhaber](wikipedia://en/30237327) and [Chung Tao Yang](wikipedia://en/15315411) had persuaded [Provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provost_%28education%29) [David R. Goddard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_R._Goddard) to hire Goldman to help improve the quality of U. Penn's mathematics department to the level of the mathematics departments of the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). From 1963 to 1967, Goldman served as the chair of the mathematics department of U. Penn., hired several outstanding mathematicians including [Richard Kadison](wikipedia://en/25798650) and [Eugenio Calabi](wikipedia://en/621000), and regularly consulted [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574) and [Donald C. Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_C._Spencer) in making his decisions on hiring and curriculum improvements.[^o3]

Goldman was shot and wounded on February 11, 1970, at the University of Pennsylvania campus by disgruntled student Robert Cantor, who also shot and killed Professor Walter Koppelman before killing himself. Goldman ultimately recovered from his wounds.
