# Keith Moffatt

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Keith Moffatt"}

**Henry Keith Moffatt**, [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) (born 12 April 1935) is a British mathematician with research interests in the field of [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), particularly [magnetohydrodynamics](wikipedia://en/40310) and the theory of [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664). He was Professor of Mathematical Physics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) from 1980 to 2002.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Moffatt was born on 12 April 1935 to Emmeline Marchant and Frederick Henry Moffatt*.*[^o4] He was schooled at [George Watson's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Watson's_College), Edinburgh, going on to study Mathematical Sciences at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), graduating in 1957. He then went to [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where he studied mathematics and, 1959, he was a [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29). In 1960, he was awarded a [Smith's Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize) while preparing his PhD degree.[^o5] He received his doctorate in 1962; his dissertation, *Magnetohydrodynamic Turbulence*, was supervised by [George Batchelor](wikipedia://en/1763397).

## Career {#Career}

After completing his doctorate, Moffatt joined the staff of the Mathematics Faculty at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) as an Assistant Lecturer[^o4] and became a Fellow of [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). He was appointed a lecturer in 1964, and held the office of Tutor, then Senior Tutor, at Trinity between 1970 and 1976.[^o8]

In 1977, he was appointed to the Chair of Applied Mathematics at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol).[^o4] He held this position until 1980 when he returned to Cambridge to take up the Chair in Mathematical Physics, renewing his Fellowship of Trinity College. In 2002, he was made an Emeritus Professor of the University (he remains a Fellow of Trinity).[^o5]

He held the *Chaire Internationale de Recherche Blaise Pascal* at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure), Paris, from 2001 to 2003, and was [Leverhulme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverhulme_Trust) Professor, from 2003 to 2005. He was president of the [International Union of Theoretical and Applied Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_of_Theoretical_and_Applied_Mechanics) from 2000 to 2004.[^o5]

## Publications {#Publications}

Moffatt has published more than 200 papers. He is the author or co-author of books including:

- Keith Moffatt. (1978). "Magnetic Field Generation in Electrically Conducting Fluids". Cambridge University Press. isbn:9780521216401 2nd ed., 1983.[^o11]
- Emmanuel Dormy. (2019). "Self-Exciting Fluid Dynamos". Cambridge University Press. isbn:9781108717052[^o13]

He also co-edited

- (2000). "Perspectives in Fluid Dynamics: A Collective Introduction to Current Research". Cambridge University Press[^o15]
- (2011). "Environmental Hazards: The Fluid Dynamics and Geophysics of Extreme Events". World Scientific[^o17]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

Moffatt was elected [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1986;[^o19] [Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1988;[^o5] Foreign Member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1991;[^o20] [Member of Academia Europæa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Academia_Europæa) in 1994; Foreign Member of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences), Paris, in 1998; Foreign Member of the [Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_dei_Lincei), Rome, in 2001; Fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society) in 2003; and a foreign associate of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2008.[^o5]

He became an *officier* in the *[Ordre des Palmes académiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_des_Palmes_académiques)* in 1998. He received the Panetti-Ferrari International Prize and Gold Medal of the [Academy of Sciences of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_Turin) in 2001, the Euromech Prize for Fluid Mechanics in 2003,[^o5] the Caribbean Award for Fluid Dynamics in 2004,[^o19] the [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 2005,[^o5] and the [Hughes Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hughes_Medal) of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 2005.[^o19]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1960, he married Katharine (Linty), and together they had four children, two daughters and two sons, one of whom is deceased.[^o4]
