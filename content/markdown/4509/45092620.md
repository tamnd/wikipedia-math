# Paul Kelly (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Kelly"}

**Paul Joseph Kelly** (June 26, 1915 – July 15, 1995) was an American mathematician who worked in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401).[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Kelly was born in [Riverside, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside,_California). He earned bachelor's and master's degrees from the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) before moving to the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) for doctoral studies; he earned his Ph.D. in 1942 with a dissertation concerning [geometric transformations](wikipedia://en/43194879) under the supervision of [Stanislaw Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Ulam).[^o2][^o3]

He spent the rest of the war years serving in the [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force) as a First Lieutenant, before returning to academia with a teaching appointment at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) in 1946. He moved to the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara) in 1949, and was chair there from 1957 to 1962.[^o2][^o3] At UCSB, his students included [Brian Alspach](wikipedia://en/42451632) (through whom he has nearly 30 [academic descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_genealogy)) and [Phyllis Chinn](wikipedia://en/45092410).[^o5] He retired in 1982.[^o2][^o3]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Kelly is known for posing the [reconstruction conjecture](wikipedia://en/255594) with his advisor Ulam, which states that every graph is uniquely determined by the ensemble of subgraphs formed by deleting one vertex in each possible way.[^o7] He also proved a special case of this conjecture, for [trees](wikipedia://en/48560).[^o8]

He is the coauthor of three textbooks: *Projective geometry and projective metrics* (1953, with [Herbert Busemann](wikipedia://en/25491802)),[^o9] *Geometry and convexity: A study in mathematical methods* (1979, with Max L. Weiss),[^o10] and *The non-Euclidean, hyperbolic plane: Its structure and consistency* (1981, with Gordon Matthews).[^o11]
