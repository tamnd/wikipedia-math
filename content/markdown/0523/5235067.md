# Coherent risk measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Coherent risk measure"}

In the fields of [actuarial science](wikipedia://en/189373) and [financial economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_economics) there are a number of ways that risk can be defined; to clarify the concept theoreticians have described a number of properties that a [risk measure](wikipedia://en/12498127) might or might not have. A **coherent risk measure** is a function that satisfies properties of [monotonicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonicity), [sub-additivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-additive), [homogeneity](wikipedia://en/622844), and [translational invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translational_invariance).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Consider a random outcome $X$ viewed as an element of a linear space $\mathcal{L}$ of measurable functions, defined on an appropriate probability space. A [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948) $\varrho : \mathcal{L}$ → $\mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$ is said to be coherent risk measure for $\mathcal{L}$ if it satisfies the following properties:[^o4]

### Normalized {#Normalized}

$$\varrho(0) = 0$$

That is, the risk when holding no assets is zero.

### Monotonicity {#Monotonicity}

$$\mathrm{If}\; Z_1,Z_2 \in \mathcal{L} \;\mathrm{and}\; Z_1 \leq Z_2 \; \mathrm{a.s.} ,\; \mathrm{then} \; \varrho(Z_1) \geq \varrho(Z_2)$$

That is, if portfolio $Z_2$ always has better values than portfolio $Z_1$ under [almost all](wikipedia://en/351908) scenarios then the risk of $Z_2$ should be less than the risk of $Z_1$.[^o7] E.g. If $Z_1$ is an in the money [call option](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_option) (or otherwise) on a stock, and $Z_2$ is also an in the money call option with a lower [strike price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_price). In financial risk management, monotonicity implies a portfolio with greater future returns has less risk.

### Sub-additivity {#Sub-additivity}

$$\mathrm{If}\; Z_1,Z_2 \in \mathcal{L} ,\; \mathrm{then}\; \varrho(Z_1 + Z_2) \leq \varrho(Z_1) + \varrho(Z_2)$$

Indeed, the risk of two portfolios together cannot get any worse than adding the two risks separately: this is the [diversification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversification_%28finance%29) principle. In financial risk management, sub-additivity implies diversification is beneficial. The sub-additivity principle is sometimes also seen as problematic.[^o9][^o10]

### Positive homogeneity {#Positive_homogeneity}

$$\mathrm{If}\; \alpha \ge 0 \; \mathrm{and} \; Z \in \mathcal{L} ,\; \mathrm{then} \; \varrho(\alpha Z) = \alpha \varrho(Z)$$

Loosely speaking, if you double your portfolio then you double your risk. In financial risk management, positive homogeneity implies the risk of a position is proportional to its size.

### Translation invariance {#Translation_invariance}

If $A$ is a deterministic portfolio with guaranteed return $a$ and $Z \in \mathcal{L}$ then

$$\varrho(Z + A) = \varrho(Z) - a$$

The portfolio $A$ is just adding cash $a$ to your portfolio $Z$. In particular, if $a=\varrho(Z)$ then $\varrho(Z+A)=0$. In [financial risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_risk_management), translation invariance implies that the addition of a sure amount of [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_%28economics%29) reduces the risk by the same amount.

### Convex risk measures {#Convex_risk_measures}

The notion of coherence has been subsequently relaxed. Indeed, the notions of Sub-additivity and Positive Homogeneity can be replaced by the notion of [convexity](wikipedia://en/245568):[^o14]

**Convexity**

$$\text{If }Z_1,Z_2 \in \mathcal{L}\text{ and }\lambda \in [0,1] \text{ then }\varrho(\lambda Z_1 + (1-\lambda) Z_2) \leq \lambda \varrho(Z_1) + (1-\lambda) \varrho(Z_2)$$

:::

## Examples of risk measure {#Examples_of_risk_measure}

### Value at risk {#Value_at_risk}

It is well known that [value at risk](wikipedia://en/148633) **is not** a coherent risk measure as it does not respect the sub-additivity property. An immediate consequence is that [value at risk](wikipedia://en/148633) might discourage diversification.[^o4] [Value at risk](wikipedia://en/148633) is, however, coherent, under the assumption of [elliptically distributed](wikipedia://en/27014075) losses (e.g. [normally distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normally_distributed)) when the portfolio value is a linear function of the asset prices. However, in this case the value at risk becomes equivalent to a mean-variance approach where the risk of a portfolio is measured by the variance of the portfolio's return.

The Wang transform function (distortion function) for the Value at Risk is $g(x)=\mathbf{1}_{x\geq 1-\alpha}$. The non-concavity of $g$ proves the non coherence of this risk measure.

**Illustration**

As a simple example to demonstrate the non-coherence of value-at-risk consider looking at the VaR of a portfolio at 95% confidence over the next year of two default-able zero coupon bonds that mature in 1 years time denominated in our numeraire currency.

Assume the following:

- The current yield on the two bonds is 0%
- The two bonds are from different issuers
- Each bond has a 4% [probability of defaulting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_of_default) over the next year
- The event of default in either bond is independent of the other
- Upon default the bonds have a recovery rate of 30%

Under these conditions the 95% VaR for holding either of the bonds is 0 since the probability of default is less than 5%. However if we held a portfolio that consisted of 50% of each bond by value then the 95% VaR is 35% (= 0.5\*0.7 + 0.5\*0) since the probability of at least one of the bonds defaulting is 7.84% (= 1 - 0.96\*0.96) which exceeds 5%. This violates the sub-additivity property showing that VaR is not a coherent risk measure.

### Average value at risk {#Average_value_at_risk}

The average value at risk (sometimes called [expected shortfall](wikipedia://en/8307819) or conditional value-at-risk or $AVaR$) is a coherent risk measure, even though it is derived from Value at Risk which is not. The domain can be extended for more general Orlitz Hearts from the more typical [Lp spaces](wikipedia://en/45194).[^o18]

### Entropic value at risk {#Entropic_value_at_risk}

The [entropic value at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropic_value_at_risk) is a coherent risk measure.[^o20]

### Tail value at risk {#Tail_value_at_risk}

The [tail value at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tail_value_at_risk) (or tail [conditional expectation](wikipedia://en/435099)) is a coherent risk measure only when the underlying distribution is [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_distribution).

The Wang transform function (distortion function) for the [tail value at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tail_value_at_risk) is $g(x)=\min(\frac{x}{\alpha},1)$. The concavity of $g$ proves the coherence of this risk measure in the case of continuous distribution.

### Proportional Hazard (PH) risk measure {#Proportional_Hazard_(PH)_risk_measure}

The PH risk measure (or Proportional Hazard Risk measure) transforms the hazard rates $\scriptstyle \left( \lambda(t) = \frac{f(t)}{\bar{F}(t)}\right)$ using a coefficient $\xi$.

The Wang transform function (distortion function) for the PH risk measure is $g_{\alpha}(x) = x^{\xi}$. The concavity of $g$ if $\scriptstyle \xi<\frac{1}{2}$ proves the coherence of this risk measure.

*Image: Sample\_of\_Wang\_transform\_function\_or\_distortion\_function.png.* Sample of Wang transform function or distortion function

### g-Entropic risk measures {#g-Entropic_risk_measures}

[g-entropic risk measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-entropic_risk_measure) are a class of information-theoretic coherent risk measures that involve some important cases such as CVaR and EVaR.[^o20]

### The Wang risk measure {#The_Wang_risk_measure}

The Wang risk measure is defined by the following Wang transform function (distortion function) $g_{\alpha}(x)=\Phi\left[ \Phi^{-1}(x)-\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)\right]$. The coherence of this risk measure is a consequence of the concavity of $g$.

### Entropic risk measure {#Entropic_risk_measure}

The [entropic risk measure](wikipedia://en/28107702) is a convex risk measure which is not coherent. It is related to the [exponential utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_utility).

### Superhedging price {#Superhedging_price}

The [superhedging price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superhedging_price) is a coherent risk measure.

## Set-valued {#Set-valued}

In a situation with $\mathbb{R}^d$-valued portfolios such that risk can be measured in $n \leq d$ of the assets, then a set of portfolios is the proper way to depict risk. Set-valued risk measures are useful for markets with [transaction costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transaction_cost).[^o28]

::: {#o30 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

A set-valued coherent risk measure is a function $R: L_d^p \rightarrow \mathbb{F}_M$, where $\mathbb{F}_M = \{D \subseteq M: D = cl (D + K_M)\}$ and $K_M = K \cap M$ where $K$ is a constant [solvency cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvency_cone) and $M$ is the set of portfolios of the $m$ reference assets. $R$ must have the following properties:[^o31]

**Normalized**

$$K_M \subseteq R(0) \; \mathrm{and} \; R(0) \cap -\mathrm{int}K_M = \emptyset$$

**Translative in M**

$$\forall X \in L_d^p, \forall u \in M: R(X + u1) = R(X) - u$$

**Monotone**

$$\forall X_2 - X_1 \in L_d^p(K) \Rightarrow R(X_2) \supseteq R(X_1)$$

**Sublinear**

## General framework of Wang transform {#General_framework_of_Wang_transform}

**Wang transform of the cumulative distribution function**

A Wang transform of the cumulative distribution function is an increasing function $g \colon [0,1] \rightarrow [0,1]$ where $g(0)=0$ and $g(1)=1$.[^o33] This function is called *distortion function* or Wang transform function.

The *dual distortion function* is $\tilde{g}(x) = 1 - g(1-x)$.[^o34][^o35] Given a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$, then for any [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) $X$ and any distortion function $g$ we can define a new [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) $\mathbb{Q}$ such that for any $A \in \mathcal{F}$ it follows that $\mathbb{Q}(A) = g(\mathbb{P}(X \in A)).$[^o34]

**Actuarial premium principle**

For any increasing concave Wang transform function, we could define a corresponding premium principle :[^o33] $\varrho(X)=\int_0^{+\infty}g\left(\bar{F}_X(x)\right) dx$

**Coherent risk measure**

A coherent risk measure could be defined by a Wang transform of the cumulative distribution function $g$ if and only if $g$ is concave.[^o33]

### Set-valued convex risk measure {#Set-valued_convex_risk_measure}

If instead of the sublinear property,*R* is convex, then *R* is a set-valued convex risk measure.

## Dual representation {#Dual_representation}

A [lower semi-continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_semi-continuous) convex risk measure $\varrho$ can be represented as

$$\varrho(X) = \sup_{Q \in \mathcal{M}(P)} \{E^Q[-X] - \alpha(Q)\}$$

such that $\alpha$ is a [penalty function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_function_%28risk%29) and $\mathcal{M}(P)$ is the set of probability measures [absolutely continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_continuous) with respect to *P* (the "real world" [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437)), i.e. $\mathcal{M}(P) = \{Q \ll P\}$. The dual characterization is tied to $L^p$ [spaces](wikipedia://en/45194), Orlitz hearts, and their dual spaces.[^o18]

A [lower semi-continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_semi-continuous) risk measure is coherent if and only if it can be represented as

$$\varrho(X) = \sup_{Q \in \mathcal{Q}} E^Q[-X]$$

such that $\mathcal{Q} \subseteq \mathcal{M}(P)$.[^o38]
