# Andrzej Trybulec

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Andrzej Trybulec"}

**Andrzej Wojciech Trybulec** (29 January 1941 in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) – 11 September 2013 in [Białystok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Białystok), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland)) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) noted for work on the [Mizar system](wikipedia://en/209074).[^o3]

:::

## Early years {#Early_years}

His parents Jan W. Trybulec and Barbara H. Kurlus both were professional pharmacists who owned a chemists shop in a small town [Szczucin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szczucin) near the city of [Tarnów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnów) in the [south-eastern Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Poland) where they dispensed medicines. He went to high school in [Ruda Śląska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruda_Śląska) and then, on his own initiative, he transferred to a prestigious high school in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków), where he matriculated. He studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw), from 1964 to 1966 he lectured at the Chair of Geometry, in 1966 he graduated to the [magister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) degree. Until 1967 he lectured at the Institute of Mathematics at the University of Warsaw, from 1967 to 1971 he was an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) at the [Warsaw University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University_of_Technology), since 1971 he worked at the Institute of Library and Information Science of the University of Warsaw. In September and October 1973, Trybulec was a [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_scholar) to the [All-Russian Scientific and Technical Information Institute (VINITI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VINITI) in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), then the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR), where he invented the idea of [machine-readability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine-readable_data) of a mathematical text. He earned the doctoral degree in 1974 from the Institute of Mathematics of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) under [Karol Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk).

## Research work {#Research_work}

Trybulec's first [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) papers were in the various [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) and [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) topics pioneered by [Karol Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk). In parallel to his generic [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) research, he also worked in [computational linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_linguistics) and [semantics](wikipedia://en/29107) of [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015). Applying the framework of [Tarski–Grothendieck set theory](wikipedia://en/6105873) axioms, essentially the [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214) supplemented by the [Tarski axiom](wikipedia://en/2181360) with all the objects being sets and eliminated notion of class, together with the first-order logic of the [Gentzen](wikipedia://en/46027)-[Jaśkowski](wikipedia://en/4304907) [natural deduction](wikipedia://en/51072), in 1973 he designed the formalization system [Mizar](wikipedia://en/209074) consisting of a formal language for writing mathematical definitions and proofs, a [proof assistant](wikipedia://en/1258607), able to mechanically check proofs written in this language. Although the first presentation of the Mizar system on 14 November 1973 at a seminar in the Institute of Library Science and Scientific Information was an ideology understood as a visionary speculation rather than research project, his idea was later developed by himself and his collaborators to the Mizar Mathematical Library (MML), a library of formalized mathematics which can be used in the proof of new theorems and the world’s largest repository of formalized and computer-checked mathematics. Since 1978 until his death, he had lectured as a professor at the Institute of Computer Science at the [University of Białystok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Białystok), while in 1984-1985 hold visiting professorship at the Department of Computer Science and Engineering of the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut). He published a number of articles, mostly with the journal *Formalized Mathematics* dedicated to MML contributions.
