# Cora Sadosky

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Cora Sadosky"}

**Cora Susana Sadosky de Goldstein** (May 23, 1940 – December 3, 2010) was an Argentine mathematician and Professor of Mathematics at [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Sadosky was born in Buenos Aires, Argentina, the daughter of mathematicians [Manuel Sadosky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Sadosky) and [Corina Eloísa "Cora" Ratto de Sadosky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cora_Ratto_de_Sadosky). At the age of 6, she moved with her parents to France and Italy.[^o5]

Sadosky began college at age 15 in the School of Science of the [University of Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Buenos_Aires), obtaining her degree of Licenciatura (comparable to a Bachelor's degree in the US nomenclature) in 1960. She earned her doctorate at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1965.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

After receiving her doctorate, she returned to Argentina. She became an assistant professor of Mathematics at the [University of Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Buenos_Aires). She resigned her position in 1966, along with 400 other faculty members, in protest over a police assault on the School of Science. She taught for one semester at Uruguay National University and then became an assistant professor at [Johns Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). She returned to Argentina in 1968 but was unable to obtain an academic position there, instead working as a technical translator and editor.[^o5]

In 1974, due to political persecution, Sadosky left Argentina, relocating to Caracas to join the faculty of the Central University of Venezuela. At this time she wrote a graduate text on mathematics, *Interpolation of Operators and Singular Integrals: An Introduction to Harmonic Analysis*, which was published in the United States in 1979. She spent the academic year of 1978–1979 at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton, New Jersey. In 1980 she became an associate professor at Howard University. After spending a year as a visiting professor at the University of Buenos Aires, she returned to Howard University as a full professor in 1985.[^o5] From 1995 to 1997, she served as an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) Council member at large.[^o7]

## Awards {#Awards}

She was appointed a visiting professorship for women (VPW) in science and technology from the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) (NSF) for 1983–1984 and spent it at the Institute for Advanced Study. She received a second VPW in 1995 which she spent as visiting professor at the University of California at Berkeley. She received a Career Advancement Award from the NSF in 1987–1988 which allowed her to spend the year as a member of the classical analysis program at [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) (MSRI), where she later returned as a research professor.

She was elected president of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) (AWM) for 1993–1995. The [Sadosky Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadosky_Prize) of the AWM is named in her honor.[^o9]

## Research {#Research}

Sadosky's research was in the field of [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_%28mathematics%29), particularly [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659) and [Operator Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_Theory). Sadosky's doctoral thesis was on parabolic singular integrals, written under [Alberto Pedro Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Pedro_Calderón) and [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477).[^o11]

Together with [Mischa Cotlar](wikipedia://en/35234149), Sadosky wrote more than 30 articles as part of a collaborative research program. Their research included work on moments theory and [lifting theorems for measures](wikipedia://en/25956787), Toeplitz forms, [Hankel operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hankel_operator), and scattering systems, as well as their applications using weighted norm inequalities and functions of bounded mean oscillation.[^o12]

In addition to the above topics, Sadosky wrote extensively on [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), particularly harmonic analysis on [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), the Hilbert transform, and other topics related to scattering and lifting techniques.[^o13]
