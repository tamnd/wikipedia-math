# The Lady's and Gentleman's Diary

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="The Lady's and Gentleman's Diary"}

***The Lady's and Gentleman's Diary*** was a [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446) magazine formed as a successor of *[The Ladies' Diary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ladies'_Diary)* and *[Gentleman's Diary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentleman's_Diary)* in 1841. It was published annually between 1841 and 1871[^o2] by the Company of Stationers; its editor from 1844 to 1865 was [Wesley S. B. Woolhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_S._B._Woolhouse). It consisted mostly of problems posed by its readers, with their solutions given in later volumes, though it also contained word puzzles and poetry. The magazine was based in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London). It ceased publication in 1871.[^o2] This should not be confused with *Ladies and Gentlemens Diary, or Royal Almanack* (1775 to 1786) which was printed by Thomas Carnan and edited by [Reuben Burrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuben_Burrow) and was a short lived competitor to *The Ladies' Diary*.

:::

[Kirkman's schoolgirl problem](wikipedia://en/8876082) originated in a question in the magazine.
