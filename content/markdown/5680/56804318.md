# Erdős–Tenenbaum–Ford constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erdős–Tenenbaum–Ford constant"}

The **Erdős–Tenenbaum–Ford constant** is a [mathematical constant](wikipedia://en/48779551) that appears in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o2] Named after mathematicians [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), [Gérald Tenenbaum](wikipedia://en/36258646), and [Kevin Ford](wikipedia://en/55700462), it is defined as

:::

$$\delta := 1 - \frac{1 + \log \log 2}{\log 2} = 0.0860713320\dots$$

where $\log$ is the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476).

Following up on earlier work by Tenenbaum, Ford used this constant in analyzing the number $H(x,y,z)$ of integers that are at most $x$ and that have a divisor in the range $[y,z]$.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

## Multiplication table problem {#Multiplication_table_problem}

For each positive integer $N$, let $M(N)$ be the number of distinct integers in an $N \times N$ [multiplication table](wikipedia://en/57122). In 1960,[^o7] Erdős studied the [asymptotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic) behavior of $M(N)$ and proved that

$$M(N) = \frac{N^2}{(\log N)^{\delta + o(1)}},$$

as $N \to +\infty$.

Tenenbaum improved the Erdős multiplication table estimate, and Ford established an asymptotic best bound.
