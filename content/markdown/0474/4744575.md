# Minakshisundaram–Pleijel zeta function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minakshisundaram–Pleijel zeta function"}

The **Minakshisundaram–Pleijel zeta function** is a [zeta function](wikipedia://en/26527265) encoding the eigenvalues of the [Laplacian](wikipedia://en/174706) of a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652). It was introduced by (txt). The case of a compact region of the plane was treated earlier by (txt).

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For a compact Riemannian manifold *M* of dimension *N* with eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots$ of the [Laplace–Beltrami operator](wikipedia://en/1763356) $\Delta$, the zeta function is given for $\operatorname{Re}(s)$ sufficiently large by

$$Z(s) = \mbox{Tr}(\Delta^{-s}) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \vert \lambda_{n} \vert^{-s}.$$

(where if an eigenvalue is zero it is omitted in the sum). The manifold may have a boundary, in which case one has to prescribe suitable boundary conditions, such as [Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/423504) or [Neumann boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/735443).

More generally one can define

$$Z(P, Q, s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{f_n(P)f_n(Q)}{ \lambda_{n}^s}$$

for *P* and *Q* on the manifold, where the $f_n$ are normalized eigenfunctions. This can be analytically continued to a [meromorphic function](wikipedia://en/80857) of *s* for all complex *s*, and is holomorphic for $P\ne Q$.

The only possible poles are simple poles at the points $s = N/2, N/2-1, N/2-2,\dots, 1/2,-1/2, -3/2,\dots$ for *N* odd, and at the points $s = N/2, N/2-1, N/2-2, \dots,2, 1$ for *N* even. If *N* is odd then $Z(P,P,s)$ vanishes at $s= 0, -1, -2,\dots$. If *N* is even, the residues at the poles can be explicitly found in terms of the metric, and by the [Wiener–Ikehara theorem](wikipedia://en/6832988) we find as a corollary the relation

$$\sum_{\lambda_n < T} f_n(P)^2 \sim\frac{T^{N/2}}{(2\sqrt{\pi})^N\Gamma(N/2+1)},$$

where the symbol $\sim$ indicates that the quotient of both the sides tend to 1 when T tends to $+\infty$.[^o6]

The function $Z(s)$ can be recovered from $Z(P,P,s)$ by integrating over the whole manifold *M*:

$$\displaystyle Z(s) = \int_M Z(P,P,s)dP.$$

:::

## Heat kernel {#Heat_kernel}

The [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) of the zeta function can be found by expressing it in terms of the [heat kernel](wikipedia://en/3904336)

$$K(P,Q,t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f_n(P)f_n(Q) e^{- \lambda_{n}t}$$

as the [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297)

$$Z(P,Q,s) = \frac{1}{\Gamma(s)} \int_0^\infty K(P,Q,t) t^{s-1} dt$$

In particular, we have

$$Z(s) = \frac{1}{\Gamma(s)} \int_0^\infty K(t) t^{s-1} dt$$

where

$$K(t)=\int_M K(P,P,t)dP=\sum^\infty_{i=1}e^{-\lambda_i t}$$

is the trace of the heat kernel.

The poles of the zeta function can be found from the asymptotic behavior of the heat kernel as *t*→0.

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

If the manifold is a circle of dimension *N*=1, then the eigenvalues of the Laplacian are *n*<sup>2</sup> for integers *n*. The zeta function

$$Z(s) = \sum_{n\ne 0}\frac{1}{(n^2)^s} = 2\zeta(2s)$$

where ζ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).

:::
