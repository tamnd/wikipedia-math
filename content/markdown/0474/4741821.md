# Bol loop

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bol loop"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **Bol loop** is an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) generalizing the notion of [group](wikipedia://en/19447). Bol loops are named for the Dutch mathematician [Gerrit Bol](wikipedia://en/40927837) who introduced them in (Bol 1937).

:::

A [loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loop_%28algebra%29), *L*, is said to be a **left Bol loop** if it satisfies the [identity](wikipedia://en/298428)

$a(b(ac))=(a(ba))c$, for every *a*,*b*,*c* in *L*,

while *L* is said to be a **right Bol loop** if it satisfies

$((ca)b)a=c((ab)a)$, for every *a*,*b*,*c* in *L*.

These identities can be seen as weakened forms of [associativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity), or a strengthened form of (left or right) [alternativity](wikipedia://en/186050).

A loop is both left Bol and right Bol [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is a [Moufang loop](wikipedia://en/755329). Alternatively, a right or left Bol loop is Moufang if and only if it satisfies the flexible identity *a(ba) = (ab)a* . Different authors use the term "Bol loop" to refer to either a left Bol or a right Bol loop.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The left (right) Bol identity directly implies the left (right) [alternative property](wikipedia://en/186050), as can be shown by setting b to the identity.

It also implies the [left (right) inverse property](wikipedia://en/25223#Inverse_properties), as can be seen by setting b to the left (right) inverse of a, and using loop division to cancel the superfluous factor of a. As a result, Bol loops have two-sided inverses.

Bol loops are also [power-associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-associative).

:::

## Bruck loops {#Bruck_loops}

A Bol loop where the aforementioned two-sided inverse satisfies the *automorphic inverse property,* (*ab*)<sup>−1</sup> = *a*<sup>−1</sup> *b*<sup>−1</sup> for all *a,b* in *L*, is known as a (left or right) **Bruck loop** or **K-loop** (named for the American mathematician [Richard Bruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bruck)). The example in the following section is a Bruck loop.

Bruck loops have applications in [special relativity](wikipedia://en/26962); see Ungar (2002). Left Bruck loops are equivalent to Ungar's (2002) **gyrocommutative [gyrogroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrogroup)**, even though the two structures are defined differently.

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Let *L* denote the set of *n x n* [positive definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_matrix), [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/189682) [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) over the complex numbers. It is generally not true that the [matrix product](wikipedia://en/125280) *AB* of matrices *A*, *B* in *L* is Hermitian, let alone positive definite. However, there exists a unique *P* in *L* and a unique [unitary matrix](wikipedia://en/129222) *U* such that *AB = PU*; this is the [polar decomposition](wikipedia://en/1369241) of *AB*. Define a [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) \* on *L* by *A* \* *B* = *P*. Then (*L*, \*) is a left Bruck loop. An explicit formula for \* is given by *A* \* *B* = (*A B*<sup>2</sup> *A*)<sup>1/2</sup>, where the superscript 1/2 indicates the unique positive definite Hermitian [square root](wikipedia://en/2929701).

:::

## Bol algebra {#Bol_algebra}

A (left) Bol algebra is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) equipped with a binary operation $[a,b]+[b,a]=0$ and a ternary operation $\{a,b,c\}$ that satisfies the following identities:[^o9]

$$\{a, b, c\} + \{b, a, c\} = 0$$

and

$$\{a, b, c\} + \{b, c, a\} + \{c, a, b\}= 0$$

and

$$[\{a, b, c\}, d] - [\{a, b, d\}, c] + \{c, d, [a, b]\} - \{a, b, [c, d]\}+ [[a, b],[c, d]] = 0$$

and

$$\{a, b, \{c, d, e\}\} - \{\{a, b, c\}, d, e\} - \{c, \{a, b, d\}, e\} - \{c, d, \{a, b, e\}\} = 0.$$

Note that {.,.,.} acts as a [Lie triple system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_triple_system). If $A$ is a left or right [alternative algebra](wikipedia://en/3160) then it has an associated Bol algebra $A^{b}$, where $[a,b]=ab-ba$ is the [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193) and $\{a,b,c\}=\langle b,c,a\rangle$ is the [Jordan associator](wikipedia://en/19287276).
