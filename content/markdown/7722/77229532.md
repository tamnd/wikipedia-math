# Doug Butterworth

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Doug Butterworth"}

**Douglas Stuart Butterworth** is a retired South African [fisheries scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries_scientist) and [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician). He is professor emeritus of applied mathematics at the [University of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town), where he is the director of the Marine Resource Assessment and Management (MARAM) research group.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Butterworth attended the Western Province Preparatory School in [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town),[^o4] and he matriculated from nearby [Bishops Diocesan College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishops_Diocesan_College) in 1963.[^o5] Trained as a [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), he holds an MSc from the [University of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town) and a PhD in [fundamental particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle_physics) from [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London).[^o6]

After his doctoral degree, he spent four months as an adjunct lecturer at the [University of Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Natal). He returned to Cape Town in 1977 to work in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) for the Sea Fisheries Branch, unable to find a job in physics.[^o7] He became involved in fisheries research in 1979, when he advised a co-worker – marine biologist [Peter Best](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_B._Best) – about techniques for survey-based marine mammal [abundance estimation](wikipedia://en/42122213). With Best's support, he became increasingly engaged in the research that Best was engaged in for the [International Whaling Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Whaling_Commission).[^o7]

## University of Cape Town {#University_of_Cape_Town}

After two years at the Sea Fisheries Branch, Butterworth joined the Department of Mathematics and Applied Mathematics at the University of Cape Town.[^o7] His focus was [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) and he primarily taught [biomathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomathematics) and [environmental modelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_modelling). His most important research concerned [fisheries assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_assessment), [fisheries management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries_management), and related modelling.[^o6]

In particular, Butterworth is known for developing the so-called management procedure approach to fisheries regulation in the late 1980s and early 1990s.[^o9] The procedure originated in an informal competition undertaken by Butterworth and [Andre Punt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Punt), a PhD student, against foreign research groups; in the course of the competition, Butterworth and Punt devised the procedure by using [feedback control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feedback_control) to refine computer simulations for [whaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whaling) quotas.[^o7] The management procedure approach is extremely compatible with the [precautionary principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precautionary_principle) advocated by the [Earth Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_Summit).[^o6] The approach was subsequently applied to calculate annual catch targets for [South African hake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_hake), sardine, anchovy, and [rock lobster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_lobster),[^o10] and it spread beyond South Africa: Butterworth has advised at least 12 other countries, as well as [fishing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry) associations and international bodies (among them the scientific committees of the [United Nations Food and Agriculture Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) and the [Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CITES)).[^o6][^o7] In total, he has written over 1,500 technical reports, in addition to some 250 academic publications.[^o7]

After he retired from teaching, Butterworth remained the director of the university's Marine Resource Assessment and Management (MARAM) research group.[^o7]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

Butterworth is a fellow of the [Royal Society of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_South_Africa).[^o7] In October 2008, President [Kgalema Motlanthe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kgalema_Motlanthe) admitted him to the [Order of Mapungubwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Mapungubwe),**[^o12]** granting him the award in silver for, "His excellent contribution to the betterment of the environment and [sustainability of fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability_of_fisheries)."[^o6]

In September 2019, the [Emperor of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_Japan) admitted Butterworth to the [Order of the Rising Sun, Third Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Rising_Sun).[^o13][^o14] Having served on the Japanese delegation to the scientific committee of the [Commission for the Conservation of Southern Bluefin Tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_for_the_Conservation_of_Southern_Bluefin_Tuna), he received the award for his contribution to the sustainable management of [Japan's marine resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_in_Japan), particularly [southern bluefin tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_bluefin_tuna).[^o15][^o16]
