# Link 16

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Link 16"}

**Link 16** is a [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) [tactical data link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_data_link) network used by NATO members and other nations, as allowed by the [MIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multifunctional_Information_Distribution_System) International Program Office (IPO). Its [specification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_standard) is part of the family of Tactical Data Links.

:::

Link 16 enables [military aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aircraft), [ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ships), and ground forces to exchange their tactical picture in near-real time; it also supports the exchange of text messages, imagery, and voice (the latter on two digital channels: 2.4 [kbit/s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kbit/s) or 16 kbit/s in any combination). It is one of the digital services of the [JTIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JTIDS) / [MIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multifunctional_Information_Distribution_System) in NATO's *[Standardization Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standardization_Agreement)* STANAG 5516. MIL-STD-6016 is the related [United States Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense) Link 16 [MIL-STD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIL-STD).

## Technical characteristics {#Technical_characteristics}

Link 16 is a [TDMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-division_multiple_access)-based [secure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security), jam-resistant, high-speed [digital data](wikipedia://en/8276) link that operates in the radio frequency band 960–1,215 [MHz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz), allocated in line with the [International Telecommunication Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Telecommunication_Union) (ITU) [Radio regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_regulation) to the *aeronautical radionavigation* service and to the *radionavigation satellite* service. This frequency range limits the exchange of information to users within line-of-sight of one another, although with satellite capabilities and ad hoc protocols, it is nowadays possible to pass Link 16 data over long-haul protocols such as [TCP/IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_protocol_suite) using [MIL-STD 3011 (JREAP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JREAP) or [STANAG 5602 (SIMPLE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIMPLE_%28military_communications_protocol%29). It uses the transmission characteristics and [protocols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_%28computing%29), conventions, and [fixed-length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TADIL-J) or [variable length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_Message_Format) message formats defined by MIL-STD 6016 and STANAG 5516 (formerly the [JTIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Tactical_Information_Distribution_System) technical interface design plan). Information is typically passed at one of three [data rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_rates): 31.6, 57.6, or 115.2 kilobits per second (kbit/s), although the radios and [frequency-hopping spread spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency-hopping_spread_spectrum) (FHSS) waveform itself can support [throughput](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throughput) values well over 1 Mbit/s.[^o3]

Link 16 information is primarily coded in [J-series messages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TADIL-J) which are [binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system) data words with well-defined meanings. These data words are grouped in *functional areas*, and allocated to *network participation groups* (NPG) (virtual networks), most importantly:

- *PPLI*, or Precise Participant Location and Identification (network participation groups 5 and 6),
- *Surveillance* (network participation group 7),
- *Command (Mission Management/Weapons Coordination)* (network participation group 8),
- *(Aircraft) Control* (network participation group 9),
- *Electronic Warfare & Coordination* (network participation group 10).

## Development {#Development}

Link 16 is intended to advance [Tactical Data Links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_data_link) (TDLs) as the NATO standard for [data link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_link) information exchange. Link 16 equipment is located in ground, airborne, and sea-based [air defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aircraft_warfare) platforms and selected [fighter aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter_aircraft). The U.S. industry is now developing a new Link 16 SCA compliant radio [MIDS-JTRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Tactical_Radio_System) which currently is projected to implement nine various tactical waveforms, including Link 16.

The [MIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multifunctional_Information_Distribution_System) program, which manage the development of the American aspect of communication component for Link 16, is managed by the International Program Office located in [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego,_California), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California). In the United States, the lead Air Force command for the [MIL-STD-6016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIL-STD-6016) standard, plans, and requirements is the [Air Force Global Cyberspace Integration Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Global_Cyberspace_Integration_Center) at [Langley AFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langley_AFB), with JTIDS program execution managed by the [653d Electronic Systems Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/653d_Electronic_Systems_Wing) at [Hanscom Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanscom_Air_Force_Base) near [Boston, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston,_Massachusetts). The MIL-STD-6016 Standard [configuration management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Configuration_management) custodian is the [Defense Information Systems Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Information_Systems_Agency).

- [Joint Data Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Data_Network)

The U.S. Army is integrating Link 16 into select [command and control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_control) elements of its [UH-60 Black Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-60_Black_Hawk) fleet, and intends to pursue fielding to AH-64 Apache and other aviation assets.

The [USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force) will add Link 16 to its [Rockwell B-1 Lancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwell_B-1_Lancer) and [Boeing B-52 Stratofortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-52_Stratofortress) bombers with the Common Link Integration Processing system.[^o12] Early versions of the [Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-22_Raptor) could only receive but not transmit Link 16 data,[^o13] on the basis that transmitting data would reveal its location.[^o14] Upgrades to the F-22 have since given it the ability to transmit Link 16 as well.[^o15]
