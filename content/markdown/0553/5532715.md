# Ideal triangle

*Image: Ideal circles.svg.* Three ideal triangles in the [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836) creating an ideal pentagon

*Image: IdealTriangle HalfPlane.svg.* Two ideal triangles in the [Poincaré half-plane model](wikipedia://en/461942)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ideal triangle"}

In [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291) an **ideal triangle** is a [hyperbolic triangle](wikipedia://en/1283865) whose three vertices all are [ideal points](wikipedia://en/4995922). Ideal triangles are also sometimes called *triply asymptotic triangles* or *trebly asymptotic triangles*. The vertices are sometimes called **ideal vertices**. All ideal triangles are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Ideal triangles have the following properties:

- All ideal triangles are congruent to each other.
- The interior angles of an ideal triangle are all zero.
- An ideal triangle has infinite perimeter.
- An ideal triangle is the largest possible triangle in hyperbolic geometry.

In the standard hyperbolic plane (a surface where the constant [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) is −1) we also have the following properties:

- Any ideal triangle has area π.[^o4]

### Distances in an ideal triangle {#Distances_in_an_ideal_triangle}

*Image: Hyperbolic ideal triangle and its incircle.svg.* Dimensions related to an ideal triangle and its incircle, depicted in the [Beltrami–Klein model](wikipedia://en/4792791) (left) and the [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836) (right)

- The [inscribed circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscribed_circle) to an ideal triangle has radius

$r=\ln\sqrt{3} = \frac{1}{2} \ln 3 = \operatorname{artanh}\frac{1}{2} = 2 \operatorname{artanh}(2- \sqrt{3}) =$ $= \operatorname{arsinh}\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{3} = \operatorname{arcosh}\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{3} \approx 0.549$ .[^o6]

The distance from any point in the triangle to the closest side of the triangle is less than or equal to the radius *r* above, with equality only for the center of the inscribed circle.

- The inscribed circle meets the triangle in three points of tangency, forming an equilateral [contact triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscribed_circle#Gergonne_triangle_and_point) with side length $d = \ln\left(\frac{\sqrt 5 + 1}{\sqrt 5 - 1}\right)= 2\ln\varphi\approx 0.962$[^o6] where $\varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt 5}{2}$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).

A circle with radius *d* around a point inside the triangle will meet or intersect at least two sides of the triangle.

- The distance from any point on a side of the triangle to another side of the triangle is equal or less than $a = \ln\left(1+ \sqrt 2\right) \approx 0.881$, with equality only for the points of tangency described above.

*a* is also the [altitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude) of the [Schweikart triangle](wikipedia://en/1283865#Schweikart_triangle).

## Thin triangle condition {#Thin_triangle_condition}

*Image: Delta thin triangle condition.svg.* The δ-thin triangle condition used in [δ-hyperbolic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Δ-hyperbolic_space)

Because the ideal triangle is the largest possible triangle in hyperbolic geometry, the measures above are maxima possible for any [hyperbolic triangle](wikipedia://en/1283865). This fact is important in the study of [δ-hyperbolic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Δ-hyperbolic_space).

## Models {#Models}

In the [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836) of the hyperbolic plane, an ideal triangle is bounded by three circles which intersect the boundary circle at right angles.

In the [Poincaré half-plane model](wikipedia://en/461942), an ideal triangle is modeled by an [arbelos](wikipedia://en/887197), the figure between three mutually tangent [semicircles](wikipedia://en/617831).

In the [Beltrami–Klein model](wikipedia://en/4792791) of the hyperbolic plane, an ideal triangle is modeled by a Euclidean triangle that is [circumscribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumscribed) by the boundary circle. Note that in the Beltrami-Klein model, the angles at the vertices of an ideal triangle are not zero, because the Beltrami-Klein model, unlike the Poincaré disk and half-plane models, is not [conformal](wikipedia://en/50627) i.e. it does not preserve angles.

The real ideal [triangle group](wikipedia://en/2069342) is the [reflection group](wikipedia://en/2422023) generated by reflections of the hyperbolic plane through the sides of an ideal triangle. Algebraically, it is isomorphic to the [free product](wikipedia://en/360036) of three order-two groups (Schwartz 2001).
