# Excluded point topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Excluded point topology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **excluded point topology** is a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) where exclusion of a particular point defines [openness](wikipedia://en/39358). Formally, let *X* be any non-empty set and *p* ∈ *X*. The collection

:::

$$T = \{S \subseteq X : p \notin S\} \cup \{X\}$$

of [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of *X* is then the excluded point topology on *X*. There are a variety of cases which are individually named:

- If *X* has two points, it is called the [Sierpiński space](wikipedia://en/387651). This case is somewhat special and is handled separately.
- If *X* is [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) (with at least 3 points), the topology on *X* is called the **finite excluded point topology**
- If *X* is [countably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite), the topology on *X* is called the **countable excluded point topology**
- If *X* is [uncountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable), the topology on *X* is called the **uncountable excluded point topology**

A generalization is the [open extension topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_extension_topology); if $X\setminus \{p\}$ has the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology), then the open extension topology on $(X \setminus \{p\}) \cup \{p\}$ is the excluded point topology.

This topology is used to provide interesting examples and counterexamples.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Let $X$ be a space with the excluded point topology with special point $p.$

The space is [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_%28topology%29), as the only neighborhood of $p$ is the whole space.

The topology is an [Alexandrov topology](wikipedia://en/542198). The smallest neighborhood of $p$ is the whole space $X;$ the smallest neighborhood of a point $x\ne p$ is the singleton $\{x\}.$ These smallest neighborhoods are compact. Their closures are respectively $X$ and $\{x,p\},$ which are also compact. So the space is [locally relatively compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_relatively_compact) (each point admits a [local base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_base) of relatively compact neighborhoods) and [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) in the sense that each point has a local base of compact neighborhoods. But points $x\ne p$ do not admit a local base of closed compact neighborhoods.

The space is [ultraconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraconnected), as any nonempty closed set contains the point $p.$ Therefore the space is also [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_%28topology%29) and [path-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path-connected).

:::
