# Ken Kamrin

T

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ken Kamrin"}

**Kenneth Norman Kamrin** is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [Applied Mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician) and [Mechanical Engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering). He serves as the Chair of Engineering Science and Professor of Mechanical Engineering at [University of California Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).

:::

Kamrin’s research focuses on [constitutive modeling](wikipedia://en/1034699) and [computational mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_mechanics) for large deformation processes spanning elastic and plastic solid [modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model), [granular mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granular_material), [amorphous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amorphous_solid) [solid mechanics](wikipedia://en/458866), and [fluid-structure interaction](wikipedia://en/6061729).

## Education {#Education}

Kamrin received a BS in [engineering physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_physics) with a minor in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from the [University of California Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 2003[^o4]. Kamrin received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT) in 2008 for his work on stochastic and deterministic models for dense granular flows with [Martin Bazant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Z._Bazant)[^o5].

## Academic Career {#Academic_Career}

Kamrin began his academic career as an [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) lecturer and [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Engineering and Applied Sciences at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 2008. Kamrin joined the faculty of the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in the [Mechanical Engineering department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) in 2011, where he later served as the Class of 1956 Career Development Chair and also received a second faculty appointment in [Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969). After serving as a professor at MIT for 13 years, Kamrin joined the [University of California Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) as a Mechanical Engineering professor in 2024[^o7] where he was appointed the Chair of [Engineering Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_Science) in 2025.

A performer by upbringing in a musical theater family, Kamrin is a sought-after teacher and speaker on the subject of mechanics. He has provided commentary for the [National Geographic channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_%28American_TV_channel%29)[^o8][,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_%28American_TV_channel%29) was the subject of a New-England-Emmy nominated film on granular media[^o9][^o10], and gave the 10-university [Midwest Mechanics Seminar](https://sites.google.com/iit.edu/mms/home) tour in 2025[^o11]. Kamrin received MIT's highest honor in undergraduate education, the [MacVicar Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_MacVicar), in 2022.

## Research {#Research}

Kamrin's research focuses on [continuum mechanics](wikipedia://en/5918) and the modeling of [granular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granular_material), [deformable](wikipedia://en/5918), and [multiphase](wikipedia://en/5587769) materials. His work combines [constitutive](wikipedia://en/1034699) modeling, [theoretical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_mechanics), and [computational methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_engineering) to relate microscopic material behavior to macroscopic deformation and flow.[^o13]

A major area of his research is [granular rheology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granular_material). Kamrin has developed non-local continuum models for dense granular flow that incorporate finite spatial interactions into constitutive descriptions[^o19]. These models have been used to describe many different flow regimes, including localized deformation and transitions between flowing and arrested states. His research has also examined the effects of particle size and characteristic length scales on granular rheology and the scaling of granular behavior across different flow geometries[^o19][^o20][^o22]. Kamrin has investigated continuum models for systems containing interacting granular and fluid phases, including [fluid–sediment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slurry) mixtures and non-Newtonian fine-particle suspensions. These [multi-phase models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-phase_flow) treat the constituent phases as coupled continua and seek to describe multiple regimes of [particulate transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment_transport) within a common framework[^o23][^o24][^o25]. Another area of his research concerns the mechanics of solid objects moving through granular materials[^o23][^o26][^o14]. His work on granular intrusion has examined the forces acting on bodies moving through granular media and developed reduced descriptions of these forces. Related studies have considered [locomotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locomotion) in granular materials, [terramechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terramechanics), and the interaction between moving bodies and granular terrains on earth and beyond[^o23][^o26].

Kamrin has also developed [computational methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_methods_in_engineering) for materials undergoing large deformation, including the Reference Map Technique[^o15] for [finite-deformation mechanics](wikipedia://en/18233581) and methods coupling [discrete](wikipedia://en/63473) and [continuum](wikipedia://en/5918) descriptions of particulate materials[^o24]. A recurring theme of this work is the development of continuum and reduced-order models that capture [macroscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroscopic_scale) material behavior without requiring all [microscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscopic_scale) degrees of freedom to be explicitly resolved.
