# Penman equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Penman equation"}

The **Penman equation** describes [evaporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporation) (*E*) from an open water surface, and was developed by [Howard Penman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Penman) in 1948. Penman's equation requires daily mean [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050), [wind speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_speed), [air pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pressure), and [solar radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_radiation) to predict *E*. Simpler [Hydrometeorological equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evapotranspiration#Hydrometeorological_equations) continue to be used where obtaining such data is impractical, to give comparable results within specific contexts, e.g. humid vs arid climates.

:::

## Details {#Details}

Numerous variations of the Penman equation are used to estimate [evaporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaporation) from water, and land. Specifically the [Penman–Monteith equation](wikipedia://en/13793909) refines weather based [potential evapotranspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evapotranspiration#Potential_evapotranspiration) (PET) estimates of vegetated land areas.[^o3] It is widely regarded as one of the most accurate models, in terms of estimates.

The original equation was developed by Howard Penman at the [Rothamsted Experimental Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Experimental_Station), Harpenden, UK.

The equation for evaporation given by Penman is:

$$E_{\mathrm{mass}}=\frac{m R_n + \rho_a c_p \left(\delta e \right) g_a }{\lambda_v \left(m + \gamma \right) }$$

where:

*m* = Slope of the saturation [vapor pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vapor_pressure) curve (Pa K<sup>−1</sup>)

*R*<sub>n</sub> = Net [irradiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irradiance) (W m<sup>−2</sup>)

*ρ*<sub>a</sub> = [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) of air (kg m<sup>−3</sup>)

*c*<sub>p</sub> = [heat capacity](wikipedia://en/194227) of air (J kg<sup>−1</sup> K<sup>−1</sup>)

δ*e* = [vapor pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vapor_pressure) deficit (Pa)

*g*<sub>a</sub> = momentum surface aerodynamic conductance (m s<sup>−1</sup>)

*λ*<sub>v</sub> = [latent heat of vaporization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_heat_of_vaporization) (J kg<sup>−1</sup>)

*γ* = [psychrometric constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychrometric_constant) (Pa K<sup>−1</sup>)

which (if the SI units in parentheses are used) will give the evaporation *E*<sub>mass</sub> in units of kg/(m<sup>2</sup>·s), kilograms of water evaporated every second for each square meter of area.

Remove λ to obviate that this is fundamentally an energy balance. Replace *λ*<sub>v</sub> with L to get familiar precipitation units *ET*<sub>vol</sub>, where *L*<sub>v</sub>=*λ*<sub>v</sub>*ρ*<sub>water</sub>. This has units of m/s, or more commonly mm/day, because it is flux m<sup>3</sup>/s per m<sup>2</sup>=m/s.

This equation assumes a daily time step so that net heat exchange with the ground is insignificant, and a unit area surrounded by similar open water or vegetation so that net heat & vapor exchange with the surrounding area cancels out. Some times people replace *R*<sub>n</sub> with and *A* for total net available energy when a situation warrants account of additional heat fluxes.

[Temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050), [wind speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_speed), [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity) impact the values of *m*, *g*, *c*<sub>p</sub>, *ρ*, and δ*e*.

## Shuttleworth (1993) {#Shuttleworth_(1993)}

In 1993, W.Jim Shuttleworth modified and adapted the Penman equation to use [SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI), which made calculating evaporation simpler.[^o5] The resultant equation is:

$$E_{\mathrm{mass}}=\frac{m R_n + \gamma * 6.43\left(1+0.536 * U_2 \right)\delta e}{\lambda_v \left(m + \gamma \right) }$$

where:

*E*<sub>mass</sub> = Evaporation rate (mm day<sup>−1</sup>)

*m* = Slope of the saturation [vapor pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vapor_pressure) curve (kPa K<sup>−1</sup>)

*R*<sub>n</sub> = Net [irradiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irradiance) (MJ m<sup>−2</sup> day<sup>−1</sup>)

*γ* = [psychrometric constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychrometric_constant) = $\frac{0.0016286 * P_{kPa}} {\lambda_v}$ (kPa K<sup>−1</sup>)

*U*<sub>2</sub> = wind speed (m s<sup>−1</sup>)

δ*e* = [vapor pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vapor_pressure) deficit (kPa)

*λ*<sub>v</sub> = [latent heat of vaporization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_heat_of_vaporization) (MJ kg<sup>−1</sup>)

## Some useful relationships {#Some_useful_relationships}

δ*e* = (e<sub>s</sub> - e<sub>a</sub>) = (1 – [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity)) e<sub>s</sub>

*e*<sub>s</sub> = saturated vapor pressure of air, as is found inside plant stoma.

*e*<sub>a</sub> = vapor pressure of free flowing air.

*e*<sub>s</sub>, mmHg = exp(21.07-5336/*T*<sub>a</sub>), approximation by Merva, 1975[^o7]

Therefore $m= \Delta =\frac{d e_s}{d T_a} = \frac{5336}{T_a^2} e^{\left(21.07 - \frac{5336}{T_a}\right)}$, mmHg/K

*T*<sub>a</sub> = air temperature in kelvins
