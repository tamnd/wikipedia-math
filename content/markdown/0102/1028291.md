# Krystyna Kuperberg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Krystyna Kuperberg"}

**Krystyna Maria Kuperberg** (born *Krystyna M. Trybulec*; 17 July 1944) is a Polish-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who currently works as a professor of mathematics at [Auburn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn_University). She is a foreign member of the [Polish Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and a fellow of the American Mathematical Society.

:::

## Early life and family {#Early_life_and_family}

Her parents, Jan W. and Barbara H. Trybulec, were [pharmacists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacist) and owned a pharmacy in [Tarnów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnów). Her older brother is [Andrzej Trybulec](wikipedia://en/5235558). Her husband [Włodzimierz Kuperberg](wikipedia://en/12918859) and her son [Greg Kuperberg](wikipedia://en/16189453) are also mathematicians,[^o5] while her daughter Anna Kuperberg is a photographer.[^o5][^o6]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

After attending high school in [Gdańsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gdańsk), she entered the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) in 1962, where she studied mathematics. Her first mathematics course was taught by [Andrzej Mostowski](wikipedia://en/1029314); later she attended [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) lectures of [Karol Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk) and became fascinated by topology.[^o4][^o5]

After obtaining her undergraduate degree, Kuperberg began graduate studies at Warsaw under Borsuk, but stopped after earning a master's degree.[^o4][^o5] She left Poland in 1969 with her young family to live in [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden), then moved to the United States in 1972.[^o8][^o4][^o5] She finished her Ph.D. in 1974, from [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University), under the supervision of [William Jaco](wikipedia://en/8017927).[^o4][^o5] In the same year, both she and her husband were appointed to the faculty of Auburn University.[^o4][^o5] From 1996 to 1998, Kuperberg served as an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) Council member at large.[^o10] In 1998, she was elected to the AMS Editorial Boards Committee.[^o11]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In 1987 she solved a problem of [Bronisław Knaster](wikipedia://en/1003125) concerning bi-homogeneity of [continua](wikipedia://en/24758520).[^o4][^o5] In the 1980s she became interested in fixed points and topological aspects of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087). In 1989 Kuperberg and [Coke Reed](wikipedia://en/48528791) solved a problem posed by [Stanislaw Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Ulam) in the [Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115).[^o13] The solution to that problem led to her 1993 work in which she constructed a smooth counterexample to the [Seifert conjecture](wikipedia://en/1275978).[^o8][^o4][^o5] She has since continued to work in dynamical systems.[^o5]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

At Auburn University, Kuperberg was awarded an Alumni Professorship for five years.[^o4][^o5][^o8] In 1995 Kuperberg received the [Alfred Jurzykowski Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Jurzykowski_Prize) from the [Kościuszko Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kościuszko_Foundation).[^o4][^o5] Her major lectures include an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) Plenary Lecture in March 1995, a [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) Plenary Lecture in January 1996, and an [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) invited talk in 1998.[^o8][^o15] In 2012, she became a fellow of the American Mathematical Society.[^o16] In 2025, she was elected as a foreign member of the [Polish Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o17]
