# Kazimierz Twardowski

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kazimierz Twardowski"}

**Kazimierz Jerzy Skrzypna-Twardowski** (; 20 October 1866 – 11 February 1938) was a Polish [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), [psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychologist), [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), and [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) of the [Lwów University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów_University).

:::

He was initially affiliated with [Alexius Meinong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexius_Meinong)'s [Graz School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz_School) of [object theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_object_theory).[^o3] Twardowski emphasized "small philosophy" or the detailed, systematic analysis of specific problems.[^o4]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Twardowski's family belonged to the [Ogończyk coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogończyk_coat_of_arms).

Twardowski studied [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) with [Franz Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Brentano) and [Robert von Zimmermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_von_Zimmermann). In 1891 he received his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) with his dissertation, *Idee und Perzeption* (*Idea and Perception*).[^n1]

In 1894 Twardowski published a book, entitled *Zur Lehre vom Inhalt und Gegenstand der Vorstellungen, Eine psychologische Untersuchung* (*[On the Doctrine of the Content and Object of Presentations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Doctrine_of_the_Content_and_Object_of_Presentations)*). The book was written between 1891 and 1893, although excerpts of the book were republished under the title *On the Content and Object of Representations*, which is an early text on [semiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiotics).[^o7]

Twardowski originated many novel ideas related to [metaphilosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphilosophy).[^o3]

He lectured at the University of Vienna in the years 1894 and 1895.

In 1895 was appointed professor at Lwów ([Lemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemberg) in [Austrian Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Galicia), now [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv) in [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)). An outstanding [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer), he was also a rector of the Lwów University during World War I.[^n1] There Twardowski soon established the [Lwów–Warsaw school of logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów–Warsaw_school_of_logic) and became the "father of Polish logic".[^n2][^o10] Among his students were the logicians [Stanisław Leśniewski](wikipedia://en/422271), [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064) and [Tadeusz Czeżowski](wikipedia://en/1983388), the psychologist [Władysław Witwicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Władysław_Witwicki), the historian of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) [Władysław Tatarkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Władysław_Tatarkiewicz), the [phenomenologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomenology_%28philosophy%29) and [aesthetician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetics) [Roman Ingarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Ingarden), as well as philosophers close to the [Vienna Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Circle) such as [Tadeusz Kotarbiński](wikipedia://en/1022909) and [Kazimierz Ajdukiewicz](wikipedia://en/900467).

Twardowski also established the [Polish Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Philosophical_Society) in 1904, the first laboratory of experimental psychology in Poland in 1907, and the journal *Ruch Filozoficzny* in 1911.[^o3]

He officially retired in 1930.[^o3]

## Work {#Work}

In his 1894 book *[On the Content and Object of Presentations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Content_and_Object_of_Presentations)* (also known as *On the Doctrine of the Content and Object of Presentations*), Twardowski argues for a distinction between [content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_analysis) and [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28philosophy%29) in the frame of the [theory of intentionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_intentionality) of his teacher [Franz Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Brentano). According to him the [mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind) is divided in two main areas: *acts* or *mental phenomena*, and a *physical phenomenon*. For example, an act of [mental representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_representation) is aimed at a presentation. This is what he called ‘intentionality’, aboutness. Every act is about something, but also every presentation goes together with an act of presentation.

*Image: 20070206 uw buw hall glowny biblioteki.jpg.* [Warsaw University Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University_Library) – at the entrance are pillared statues of [Lviv-Warsaw School of Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv-Warsaw_School_of_Logic) philosophers (*right to left*) Kazimierz Twardowski, [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064), [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), and [Stanisław Leśniewski](wikipedia://en/422271)

This theory suffers from the problem that it is not clear what the presentation exactly is. Is the presentation something only in the mind, or is it also in the world as object? Twardowski says that sometimes presentation is used for the object in the world and sometimes for the immanent content of a mental phenomenon.[^o12]

Twardowski offers a solution for this problem and proposes to make a distinction between the content of a presentation and the object of a presentation.

In his book Twardowski offers an analogy to clarify this distinction. He uses the example of a [painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painting).[^o13] People say of a [landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape) that it is painted, but also of a painting that it is painted. In the first case the word ‘painting’ is used in a modifying way (a painted landscape is not a landscape at all), while in the latter case the word painting is used in a qualitative or attributive way. Twardowski argues that presentations are similar. The content is the painted painting and the object is the painted landscape. The content resembles the present ‘picture’ in one's mind, and the object the landscape.
