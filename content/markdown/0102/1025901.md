# Reynolds decomposition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reynolds decomposition"}

In [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034) and [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664) theory, a **Reynolds decomposition** is a mathematical technique used to separate a field into its [mean](wikipedia://en/9653) and [fluctuating](wikipedia://en/28084980) components.[^o2]

:::

## Decomposition {#Decomposition}

A Reynolds decomposition of a field $\mathbf{u}$ (*e.g.,* a velocity field) is given by

$$\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t) = \overline{\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t)} + \mathbf{u}'(\mathbf{x},t),$$

where $\overline{\mathbf{u}}$ denotes the mean of $\mathbf{u}$ (which can be a time, space, or [ensemble average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_average)), and $\mathbf{u}'$ denotes the fluctuations from that mean.[^o4] The fluctuating field is defined as

$$\mathbf{u}'(\mathbf{x},t) \equiv \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t) - \overline{\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t)}$$

and satisfies[^o2][^o5]

$$\overline{\mathbf{u}'(\mathbf{x},t)} =0.$$

Note that the mean field $\overline{\mathbf{u}}$ is also frequently denoted as $\langle \mathbf{u}\rangle$.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

[Direct numerical simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_numerical_simulation), or resolution of the [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395) (nearly) completely in both space and time, is only possible on extremely fine computational grids using small time steps even for low [Reynolds numbers](wikipedia://en/23868856). Running direct numerical simulations often becomes prohibitively computationally expensive at high Reynolds' numbers. Due to computational constraints, simplifications of the Navier-Stokes equations are useful to parameterize turbulence that are smaller than the computational grid, allowing larger computational domains.[^o9]

Reynolds decomposition allows the simplification of the Navier–Stokes equations by substituting in the sum of the steady component and perturbations to the velocity profile and taking the [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) value, to obtain the [Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/624033). The resulting equation contains a nonlinear term known as the [Reynolds stresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reynolds_stresses), representing effects of turbulence.
