# Wigner semicircle distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wigner semicircle distribution"}

The **Wigner semicircle distribution**, named after the physicist [Eugene Wigner](wikipedia://en/10101), is the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) defined on the domain \[−*R*, *R*\] whose [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) *f* is a scaled [semicircle](wikipedia://en/617831), i.e. a semi-ellipse, centered at (0, 0):

:::

$$f(x)={2 \over \pi R^2}\sqrt{R^2-x^2\,}\,$$

for −*R* ≤ *x* ≤ *R*, and *f*(*x*) = 0 if *\|x|* > *R*. The parameter R is commonly referred to as the "radius" parameter of the distribution.

The distribution arises as the limiting distribution of the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues) of many [random symmetric matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrices), that is, as the dimensions of the random matrix approach infinity. The distribution of the spacing or gaps between eigenvalues is addressed by the similarly named [Wigner surmise](wikipedia://en/58756838).

## General properties {#General_properties}

Because of symmetry, all of the odd-order [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of the Wigner distribution are zero. For positive integers $n$, the $2n$-th moment of this distribution is

$$\frac{1}{n+1}\left({R \over 2}\right)^{2n} {2n\choose n}\,$$

In the typical special case that $R = 2$, this sequence coincides with the [Catalan numbers](wikipedia://en/216488) 1, 2, 5, 14, etc. In particular, the second moment is $\tfrac{R^{2}}{4}$ and the fourth moment is $\tfrac{R^{4}}{8}$, which shows that the [excess kurtosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excess_kurtosis) is $-1$. As can be calculated using the [residue theorem](wikipedia://en/73390), the [Stieltjes transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stieltjes_transform) of the Wigner distribution is given by

$$s(z)=-\frac{2}{R^2}(z-\sqrt{z^2-R^2})$$

for complex numbers $z$ with positive imaginary part, where the [complex square root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_square_root) is taken to have positive imaginary part.

The Wigner distribution coincides with a scaled and shifted [beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074): if $Y$ is a beta-distributed random variable with parameters $\alpha = \beta = \tfrac{3}{2}$, then the random variable $2RY - R$ exhibits a Wigner semicircle distribution with radius $R$. By this transformation it is straightforward to directly compute some statistical quantities for the Wigner distribution in terms of those for the beta distributions, which are better known.

The [Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chebyshev_polynomial) are [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) with respect to the Wigner semicircle distribution of radius $1$.[^o3]

## Characteristic function and moment generating function {#Characteristic_function_and_moment_generating_function}

The [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401) of the Wigner distribution can be determined from that of the beta-variate $Y$:

$$\varphi(t)=e^{-iRt}\varphi_Y(2Rt)=e^{-iRt}{}_1F_1\left(\frac{3}{2}; 3; 2iRt\right)=\frac{2J_1(Rt)}{Rt},$$

where ${}_{1}F_{1}$ is the [confluent hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/1516095) and $J_{1}$ is the [Bessel function of the first kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel_function_of_the_first_kind).

Likewise the [moment generating function](wikipedia://en/194634) can be calculated as

$$M(t)=e^{-Rt}M_Y(2Rt)=e^{-Rt}{}_1F_1\left(\frac{3}{2}; 3; 2Rt\right)=\frac{2I_1(Rt)}{Rt}$$

where $I_{1}$ is the [modified Bessel function of the first kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modified_Bessel_function_of_the_first_kind). The final equalities in both of the above lines are well-known identities relating the confluent hypergeometric function with the Bessel functions.[^o5]

## Relation to free probability {#Relation_to_free_probability}

In [free probability](wikipedia://en/679696) theory, the role of Wigner's semicircle distribution is analogous to that of the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) in classical probability theory. Namely, in free probability theory, the role of [cumulants](wikipedia://en/359684) is occupied by "free cumulants", whose relation to ordinary cumulants is simply that the role of the set of all [partitions of a finite set](wikipedia://en/340240) in the theory of ordinary cumulants is replaced by the set of all [noncrossing partitions](wikipedia://en/695026) of a finite set. Just as the cumulants of degree more than 2 of a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) are all zero [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the distribution is normal, so also, the *free* cumulants of degree more than 2 of a probability distribution are all zero if and only if the distribution is Wigner's semicircle distribution.
