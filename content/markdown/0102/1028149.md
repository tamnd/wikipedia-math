# Edward Marczewski

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edward Marczewski"}

**Edward Marczewski** (15 November 1907 – 17 October 1976[^o5]) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was born **Szpilrajn** but changed his name while hiding from Nazi persecution.[^o6]

:::

Marczewski was a member of the [Warsaw School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1028194). His life and work after the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) were connected with [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław), where he was among the creators of the Polish scientific centre. He worked at the [State Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/67042302), which was incorporated into the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1952.[^o7]

Marczewski's main fields of interest were [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), [descriptive set theory](wikipedia://en/396619), [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200). He also published papers on real and complex analysis, applied mathematics and mathematical logic.

Marczewski proved that the [topological dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_dimension), for arbitrary [metrisable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrisable_space) [separable space](wikipedia://en/27855) *X*, coincides with the [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) under one of the metrics in *X* which induce the given topology of *X* (while otherwise the Hausdorff dimension is always greater or equal to the topological dimension).[^o8] This is a fundamental theorem of [fractal theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_theory). (Certain contributions to this development were also made by [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501), see: [Witold Hurewicz](wikipedia://en/362385) and [Henry Wallman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Wallman), *Dimension Theory*, 1941, Chapter VII.)
