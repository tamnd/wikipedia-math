# Leon Chwistek

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leon Chwistek"}

**Leon Chwistek** (Polish: '; 13 June 1884, [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) – 20 August 1944, [Barvikha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barvikha) near [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_SFSR)) was a Polish [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde) [painter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painting), theoretician of modern art and [literary critic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_critic).

:::

He mainly worked in the field of the [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science) and [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). As an artist, he was a representative of Polish Formism, drawing on [Cubist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubism) and [Futurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurism) ideas.[^o7] He taught at the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University) and the [University of Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv) and was one of the founders of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791).

## Career and philosophy {#Career_and_philosophy}

He was born on 13 June 1884 in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków) to Bronisław Chwistek and Emilia (Majewska).[^o4] His father was a physician who opened one of the first [hydrotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrotherapy) establishments in [Zakopane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakopane). His mother was a pianist and a painter whose teachers included [Jan Matejko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Matejko). Growing up in Zakopane, Chwistek became close friends with [Stanisław Ignacy Witkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanisław_Ignacy_Witkiewicz) and [Bronisław Malinowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronisław_Malinowski). He was also influenced by the young [Felix Dzerzhinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Dzerzhinsky) who introduced him to the ideas of [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism).[^o5] In 1902, he graduated from the John Sobieski Gymnasium No. 3 in Kraków passing his [Matura exam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matura).[^o6]

In 1906, he earned his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) at the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University). In 1908-1909, he studied philosophy in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen).[^o7] During this period, he attended lectures by [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) and [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740). He also met [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735) and his sister, Olga, whom he later married.[^o5] In 1914-1916, he served in the [Polish Legions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Legions_in_World_War_I). In 1919, Chwistek was one of the founders of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791). From 1922, he lectured in mathematics at the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University), where he obtained his habilitation in 1928 in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636)[^o8], based on his work entitled "The Theory of Constructive Types" published in *Annales de la Société Polonaise de Mathématique*.[^o6]

In 1930, Chwistek was appointed Professor of Logic in the Department of Mathematical nad Natural Sciences at the [University of Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lwów)[^o7] in a position for which [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) had also applied but on [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163)'s recommendation it was offered to Chwistek.[^o9] He visited the famous [Scottish Café](wikipedia://en/362651) where he debated with such prominent mathematicians as [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646), [Stanisław Ulam](wikipedia://en/41531) and [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162). [^o5] His interests in the 1930s were in a general system of [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science), which was published in a book in 1935[^o6] and translated into English in 1948 as *The Limits of Science*.[^o10]

In the 1920s–30s, many [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) philosophers attempted to reform traditional philosophy by means of mathematical logic. Leon Chwistek did not believe that such reform could succeed. He thought that [reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality) could not be described in one homogeneous system, based on the principles of [formal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic), because there was not one reality but many.

After the outbreak of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) and the occupation of [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv) (renamed to [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv)) by the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), he remained at the university. He also started cooperation with *[Czerwony Sztandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czerwony_Sztandar_%28Lviv_newspaper%29)*.[^o11] In September 1940, he joined the Union of [Soviet Writers of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers'_Union_of_Ukraine). In June 1941, just before the entry of the German troops, he evacuated from [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv) together with the Soviet troops deep into Russia. From 1941 to 1943, he lived in [Tbilisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tbilisi), where he taught mathematical analysis, and from 1943 in Moscow. He was active in the [Union of Polish Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Polish_Patriots) in the USSR.[^o12]

Chwistek argued against the [axiomatic method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_method) by demonstrating that the extant axiomatic systems are inconsistent.[^o13] He was also, together with [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064), among the early pioneers of the use of the so‑called [Polish notation](wikipedia://en/25056) in logic. In his correspondence with philosopher [Wincenty Lutosławski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wincenty_Lutosławski), he criticized the Warsaw-Lwów School as "immature" and ascribed his choice of philosophical studies to Lutosławski's influence.[^o14]

He died in 1944 in [Barvikha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barvikha) near [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) after a long illness. His ashes rest in a mass grave of the victims of Stalinist repressions at the [Donskoye Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donskoye_Cemetery) in Moscow. Chwistek also has a symbolic grave at the [Rakowicki Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakowicki_Cemetery) in Kraków.[^o15]

## Artist {#Artist}

*Image: Witkacy-Udzielny BYK na urlopie, portret Leona Chwistka.jpg.* Portrait of Leon Chwistek by [Witkacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanisław_Ignacy_Witkiewicz), 1913

Between 1903 and 1904, Chwistek spent six months studying at the [Kraków Academy of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków_Academy_of_Fine_Arts) under [Józef Mehoffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Mehoffer). A visit to [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866) in 1910 sparked his interest in [Venetian painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_painting). During his time in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) in 1913–1914, he took drawing classes and first encountered [Cubism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubism). While in Paris, Chwistek took part in a duel at [Place de la Concorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_de_la_Concorde) against painter Władysław Dunin-Borkowski who insulted his fiancée. The duel was won by Chwistek who wounded his opponent.[^o5] In 1917, Chwistek helped establish the Kraków group known as the Polish Expressionists, later renamed the Formists in 1919, and soon became its principal theorist.[^o7]

In 1935, the [Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łódź) journal *Forma* (Form) printed a debate between Chwistek and [Władysław Strzemiński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Władysław_Strzemiński), effectively setting Zonism against Unism. The discussion contrasted Chwistek’s vision of dynamic, life‑affirming art rooted in ideas of biological vitality with Strzemiński’s [Constructivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_%28art%29) approach, which emphasized intellectually driven and socially purposeful artistic practice.[^o7]

Chwistek developed his theory of the multiplicity of realities first with regard to the [arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art). He distinguished four basic types of realities, then matched them with four basic types of painting.[^o17]

The four types of realities were: popular reality (common-sense realism); physical reality (constructed by physics); phenomenal reality (sensory impressions); and visionary/intuitive reality (dreams, hallucinations, subconscious states). The types of painting corresponding to the aforemenitoned were: [Primitivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitivism); [Realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_%28arts%29); [Impressionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionism); [Futurism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurism_%28art%29).

Chwistek never intended his views to constitute a new [metaphysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) theory. He was a defender of "common sense" against metaphysics and irrational feeling. His theory of plural reality was merely an attempt to specify the various ways in which the term, “real,” is used.

Chwistek's fellow artist and closest friend, [Stanislaw Ignacy Witkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Ignacy_Witkiewicz), harshly criticized his philosophical views. Witkiewicz's own philosophy was based on a [monadic](wikipedia://en/9273782) character to the individual's existence, embracing a multiplicity of existences, with the world being made up of a multiplicity of Particular Existences. In his 1919 painting titled *Fencing* inspirations from avant-garde trends prior to [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) such as [cubism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubism), Italian [futurism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurism), and [Robert Delaunay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Delaunay)’s simultanism can be observed.[^o18]
