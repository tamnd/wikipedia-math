# Martin J. Taylor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Martin J. Taylor"}

**Martin John Taylor** (born 18 February 1952)[^o15] is a British mathematician and academic. He was Professor of [Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_Mathematics) at the [School of Mathematics, University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Manchester) and, prior to its formation and merger, [UMIST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester_Institute_of_Science_and_Technology) where he was appointed to a chair after moving from [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in 1986. He was elected Warden of [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford) on 5 November 2009, took office on 2 October 2010 and retired in September 2018.[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Taylor was born in [Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester) in 1952 and educated at [Wyggeston Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyggeston_Grammar_School_for_Boys). He gained a first class degree from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) where he was a student of [Pembroke College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembroke_College,_Oxford) in 1973, and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) with a thesis on [Galois representations](wikipedia://en/486929) in 1976 supervised by [Albrecht Fröhlich](wikipedia://en/1096590).[^o3][^o6]

## Career and Research {#Career_and_Research}

Taylor's early research investigated various properties and structures of [algebraic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_numbers). In 1981 he proved the Fröhlich conjecture relating the symmetries of [algebraic integers](wikipedia://en/98981) to the behaviour of certain [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) called [Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283). More recently his research has led him to study various aspects of [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177): in particular, he and his collaborators have demonstrated how geometric properties of zeros of integral [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) in many variables can be determined by the behaviour of associated L-functions.

### Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Taylor was awarded the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) by the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 1982[^o9] and shared the [Adams Prize](wikipedia://en/1098110) in 1983.[^o10] He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) in 1996.[^o10] He was President of the London Mathematical Society from 1998 to 2000 and in 2004 was appointed Physical Secretary and Vice-President of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society).[^o10] Taylor was appointed [Knight Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor) in the [2009 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_New_Year_Honours).[^o12] Taylor received an honorary Doctorate of Science from the University of East Anglia in July 2012.[^o13]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Taylor's hobbies include [fly fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly_fishing) and [hill walking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillwalking), and he is a supporter of [Manchester United F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.). He married Sharon Lynn Marlow in 1973 and has four children.[^o15]
