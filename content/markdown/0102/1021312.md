# Gerhard Frey (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gerhard Frey"}

**Gerhard Frey** (; born 1 June 1944) is a German mathematician, known for his work in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). Following an original idea of Yves Hellegouarch,[^o3] he developed the notion of [Frey–Hellegouarch curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frey–Hellegouarch_curve), a construction of an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) from a purported solution to the [Fermat equation](wikipedia://en/19021953), that is central to [Wiles's proof of Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/21950759).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

He studied mathematics and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen), graduating in 1967. He continued his postgraduate studies at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University), where he received his PhD in 1970, and his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1973. He was assistant professor at Heidelberg University from 1969–1973, professor at the [University of Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen-Nuremberg) (1973–1975) and at [Saarland University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland_University) (1975–1990). Until 2009, he held a chair for [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) at the Institute for Experimental Mathematics at the [University of Duisburg-Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Duisburg-Essen), campus [Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essen).

Frey was a visiting scientist at several universities and research institutions, including the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University), [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) (MSRI), the Institute for Advanced Studies at [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), and the [Instituto Nacional de Matemática Pura e Aplicada](wikipedia://en/2334708) (IMPA) in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro).

Frey was also the co-editor of the journal *Manuscripta Mathematica*.

## Research contributions {#Research_contributions}

*Image: Gerhard Frey, Boston 1995.JPG.* Frey at a convention in Boston, 1995

His research areas are [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205), as well as applications to [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869) and [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432). In 1985, Frey pointed out a connection between [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) and the [Taniyama-Shimura conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama-Shimura_conjecture), and this connection was made precise shortly thereafter by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), who formulated the [epsilon conjecture](wikipedia://en/700981), and showed that the Taniyama-Shimura and epsilon conjectures together imply Fermat. Soon after, [Ken Ribet](wikipedia://en/30876850) proved enough of the epsilon conjecture to deduce that Taniyama-Shimura implied Fermat's Last Theorem.[^o9] This approach provided a framework for the subsequent successful attack on Fermat's Last Theorem by [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027) in the 1990s.[^o10]

In 1998, Frey proposed the idea of [Weil descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_descent) attack for elliptic curves over finite fields with composite degree. As a result of this attack, [cryptographers](wikipedia://en/18934432) lost their interest in these curves.[^o11]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Frey was awarded the Gauss medal of the *Braunschweigische Wissenschaftliche Gesellschaft* in 1996 for his work on [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953).[^o13] Since 1998, he has been a member of the [Göttingen Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o14]

In 2006, he received the *Certicom ECC Visionary Award* for his contributions to [elliptic-curve cryptography](wikipedia://en/9966).[^o15]
