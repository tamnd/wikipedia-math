# Stanisław Mazur

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Stanisław Mazur"}

**Stanisław Mieczysław Mazur** (; 1 January 1905 – 5 November 1981) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), a member of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) and one of the leading representatives of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328)

:::

Mazur made important contributions to geometrical methods in linear and [nonlinear functional analysis](wikipedia://en/8546912) and to the study of [Banach algebras](wikipedia://en/4665). He was also interested in [summability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summability_theory), [infinite games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_game) and [computable functions](wikipedia://en/1139338).

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

*Image: MazurGes.jpg.* Mazur awards a live goose to [Per Enflo](wikipedia://en/7180897), who solved Mazur's 1936 problem in the [Scottish book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_cafe). The photograph comes from the book *Pół wieku matematyki polskiej 1920-1970* ("Half a Century of Polish Mathematics 1920-1970").

Mazur was born on 1 January 1905 in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów), then part of the [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria) within [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), to Tomasz Mazur and Aniela (Zawrotniak).[^o4] In 1923, he graduated from a [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów). Between 1923–1926, Mazur studied at the [Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kazimierz_University) (now the [University of Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv)).[^o5] In 1925, he completed an internship at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).[^o4]

Mazur was a student of [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) at [Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kazimierz_University). His doctorate entitled *O szeregach warunkowo sumowalnych* (On Conditionally Summable Series), under Banach's supervision, was awarded in 1932.[^o4] Mazur, with [Juliusz Schauder](wikipedia://en/685314), was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1936 in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo).[^o7] In 1936, he earned his habilitation and in 1947, he became a professor at the [University of Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Łódź) and the following year at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw).[^o4]

Mazur was a close collaborator with Banach at Lwów and was a member of the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328), where he participated in the mathematical activities at the [Scottish Café](wikipedia://en/362651). He was the author or co-author of 47 problems included in the [Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115).[^o4] On 6 November 1936, he posed the "[basis problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_problem)" of determining whether every [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) has a [Schauder basis](wikipedia://en/3460662), with Mazur promising a "live goose" as a reward: 37 years later and in a ceremony that was broadcast throughout Poland, Mazur awarded a live goose to [Per Enflo](wikipedia://en/7180897) for constructing a counter-example.[^o8]

In 1939, he became a deputy to the [People’s Assembly of Western Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_to_the_People's_Assemblies_of_Western_Ukraine_and_Western_Belorussia#People's_Assemblies_of_Western_Ukraine_and_Western_Belorussia); from 1940 he served as a councillor on the Lviv City Council, and after the [Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) returned in 1944 he joined the Main Board of the [Union of Polish Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Polish_Patriots).[^o9]

In 1946, Mazur was awarded the Officer's Cross of the [Order of Polonia Restituta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta).[^o10] From 1948 Mazur worked at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw). He also worked at the [State Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/67042302), which was incorporated into the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1952.[^o11] He was a foreign member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o4] In 1949, he received the [Stefan Banach Prize](wikipedia://en/2121791#Stefan_Banach_Prize) conferred by the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791).[^o12]

In 1978, he was awarded an honorary life membership in the Polish Mathematical Society and in 1980, an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate) at the University of Warsaw.[^o5] His notable doctoral students included [Aleksander Pełczyński](wikipedia://en/56285756) and [Wiesław Żelazko](wikipedia://en/81590569).[^o13]
