# Midpoint polygon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Midpoint polygon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **midpoint polygon** of a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) $P$ is the polygon whose [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) are the [midpoints](wikipedia://en/437979) of the [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) of $P$.[^n1][^n2] It is sometimes called the **Kasner polygon** after [Edward Kasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Kasner), who termed it the *inscribed polygon* "for brevity".[^n3][^n4]

## Examples {#Examples}

### Triangle {#Triangle}

The midpoint polygon of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is called the [medial triangle](wikipedia://en/3339743). It shares the same [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) and [medians](wikipedia://en/1072006) with the original triangle. The [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of the medial triangle equals the [semiperimeter](wikipedia://en/886617) of the original triangle, and the area is one quarter of the area of the original triangle. This can be proven by the midpoint theorem of triangles and [Heron's formula](wikipedia://en/194123). The [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) of the medial triangle coincides with the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) of the original triangle.

### Quadrilateral {#Quadrilateral}

The midpoint polygon of a [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) is a parallelogram called its [Varignon parallelogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varignon_parallelogram). If the quadrilateral is [simple](wikipedia://en/1059530), the area of the parallelogram is one half the area of the original quadrilateral. The [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of the parallelogram equals the sum of the diagonals of the original quadrilateral.
