# Robert Penner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Penner"}

**Robert Clark Penner** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) whose work in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) has found applications in [high-energy physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-energy_physics) and more recently in [theoretical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_biology). He is the son of [Sol Penner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_S._Penner), an aerospace engineer.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Robert Clark Penner received his [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1977 and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1981, the latter under the direction of [James Munkres](wikipedia://en/7615776) and [David Gabai](wikipedia://en/6129609). In his doctoral studies, he solved a 50 year old problem posed by [Max Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602) on the action of the [mapping class group](wikipedia://en/793295) on curves and arcs in surfaces, developed combinatorial aspects of Thurston's theory of [train tracks](wikipedia://en/1277396) and generalized Thurston's construction of [pseudo-Anosov maps](wikipedia://en/2327355).[^o4]

After postdoctoral positions at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and at the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452), Penner spent most of the period of 1985–2003 at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California). From 2004 until 2012, he worked at [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University), where he co-founded with [Jørgen Ellegaard Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jørgen_Ellegaard_Andersen) the [Center for the Quantum Geometry of Moduli Spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_the_Quantum_Geometry_of_Moduli_Spaces).[^o5] Since 2013 Penner has held the position of the [René Thom](wikipedia://en/61633) Chair in Mathematical Biology at the [Institut des Hautes Etudes Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Etudes_Scientifiques).[^o6]

Throughout his career Penner held various visiting positions around the world including [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), [Max-Planck-Institut für Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck-Institut_für_Mathematik) at [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn), [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo), [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452), [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech), [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA), [Fields Institute](wikipedia://en/2495768), [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich), [University of Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bern), [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki), [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg), [University of Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Grenoble), [Nonlinear Institute of Nice-Sophia Antipolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_Institute_of_Nice-Sophia_Antipolis).

## Contributions to mathematics, physics, and biology {#Contributions_to_mathematics,_physics,_and_biology}

Penner's research began in the theory of [train tracks](wikipedia://en/1277396) including a generalization of [Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190)'s original construction of [pseudo-Anosov maps](wikipedia://en/2327355) to the so-called Penner-Thurston construction, which he used to give estimates on least dilatations. He then co-discovered the so-called Epstein-Penner decomposition of non-compact complete hyperbolic manifolds with [David Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_B._A._Epstein), in dimension 3 a central tool in knot theory. Over several years he developed the decorated [Teichmüller theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teichmüller_theory) of punctured surfaces including the so-called Penner matrix model, the basic partition function for Riemann's moduli space. Extending the foregoing to orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of the circle, Penner developed his model of universal [Teichmüller theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teichmüller_theory) together with its Lie algebra. He discovered combinatorial cocycles with [Shigeyuki Morita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeyuki_Morita) for the first and with [Nariya Kawazumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nariya_Kawazumi) for the higher Johnson homomorphisms. Penner has also contributed to theoretical biology in joint work with [Jørgen E. Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jørgen_E._Andersen) et al. discovering a priori geometric constraints on protein geometry, and with [Michael S. Waterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_S._Waterman), [Piotr Sulkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotr_Sulkowski), [Christian Reidys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Reidys) et al. introducing and solving the matrix model for RNA topology.
