# Cumulative hierarchy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cumulative hierarchy"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), a **cumulative hierarchy** is a family of [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) $W_\alpha$ indexed by [ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932) $\alpha$ such that

:::

- $$W_\alpha \subseteq W_{\alpha + 1}$$
- If $\lambda$ is a [limit ordinal](wikipedia://en/373137), then $W_\lambda = \bigcup_{\alpha < \lambda} W_{\alpha}$

Some authors additionally require that $W_{\alpha + 1} \subseteq \mathcal P(W_\alpha)$.

The [union](wikipedia://en/47949) $W = \bigcup_{\alpha \in \mathrm{On}} W_\alpha$ of the sets of a cumulative hierarchy is often used as a model of set theory.

The phrase "the cumulative hierarchy" usually refers to the [von Neumann hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_hierarchy), which has $W_{\alpha + 1} = \mathcal P(W_\alpha)$.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Reflection principle"}

## Reflection principle {#Reflection_principle}

A cumulative hierarchy satisfies a form of the [reflection principle](wikipedia://en/3225574): any [formula](wikipedia://en/404582) in the language of set theory that holds in the union $W$ of the hierarchy also holds in some stages $W_\alpha$.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The von Neumann universe is built from a cumulative hierarchy $\mathrm{V}_\alpha$.
- The sets $\mathrm{L}_\alpha$ of the [constructible universe](wikipedia://en/343335) form a cumulative hierarchy.
- The [Boolean-valued models](wikipedia://en/2732301) constructed by [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) are built using a cumulative hierarchy.
- The [well-founded sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-founded_set) in a model of set theory (possibly not satisfying the [axiom of foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_of_foundation)) form a cumulative hierarchy whose union satisfies the axiom of foundation.

:::
