# Chudnovsky brothers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chudnovsky brothers"}

**David Volfovich Chudnovsky** (born January 22, 1947) and **Gregory Volfovich Chudnovsky** (born April 17, 1952) are American [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) and engineers known for their world-record mathematical calculations and developing the [Chudnovsky algorithm](wikipedia://en/15893520) used to calculate the digits of [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601) with extreme precision. Both were born in [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev), [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (now [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)).

:::

## Careers in mathematics {#Careers_in_mathematics}

As a child, Gregory Chudnovsky was given a copy of *[What Is Mathematics?](wikipedia://en/8997784)* by his father (Volf Grigorovich Chudnovski, a Soviet professor of technical sciences) and decided that he wanted to be a mathematician. As a high schooler, he solved [Hilbert's tenth problem](wikipedia://en/101851), shortly after [Yuri Matiyasevich](wikipedia://en/2627712) had solved it.[^o3] He received a mathematics degree from [Kyiv State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko_National_University_of_Kyiv) in 1974 and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) the following year from the [Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/36753361), [National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences_of_Ukraine).[^o5]

In part to avoid [religious persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Ukraine) and in part to seek better medical care for Gregory, who had been diagnosed with [myasthenia gravis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myasthenia_gravis), a [neuromuscular disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromuscular_disease), the Chudnovsky family applied in 1976 for permission to emigrate from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).[^o4][^o5] Although the family was harassed by the [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB) for attempting to leave the country, the brothers were eventually able to secure their emigration with the help of [United States Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senator) [Henry M. Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_M._Jackson) and mathematician [Edwin Hewitt](wikipedia://en/7482590).[^o6]

A 1992 article in *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker)* quoted the opinion of several mathematicians that Gregory Chudnovsky was one of the world's best living mathematicians. David Chudnovsky works closely with and assists his brother Gregory.[^o7]

Despite their accomplishments and the attention brought to them by their profile in *The New Yorker*, the Chudnovsky brothers largely worked alone for decades. A 1997 [Karen Arenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Arenson) article in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* theorized that this was due to some combination of the brothers' lack of a specialization (they worked on topics including [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [applied physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_physics) and [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science)), Gregory's medical condition, their refusal to leave [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) and their insistence on being hired together. In the summer of 1997, they were hired as professors at [Polytechnic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_Tandon_School_of_Engineering) in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn) after [borough president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_president) [Howard Golden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Golden) helped find funding for their salaries.[^o8]

The Chudnovsky brothers have held records, at different times, for computing [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601) to the largest number of places, including two billion digits in the early 1990s on a supercomputer they built (dubbed "m-zero") in their apartment in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan). In 1987, the Chudnovsky brothers developed the algorithm (now called the [Chudnovsky algorithm](wikipedia://en/15893520)) that they used to break several $\pi$ computation records. Today, this algorithm is used by [Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica) to calculate $\pi$, and has continued to be used by others who have achieved [world records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_record) [in pi calculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology_of_computation_of_π).

The brothers also assisted the [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art) around 2003 in the merging of a series of digital photographs taken of *[The Hunt of the Unicorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunt_of_the_Unicorn)* tapestries during their cleaning.[^o9] [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Broadcasting_Service) aired a program on its science show *[Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_%28American_TV_series%29)*, hosted by [Robert Krulwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Krulwich), that described the difficulties in photographing the tapestries and the math used to fix them.[^o10]

The brothers later became Distinguished Industry Professors at the [New York University Tandon School of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_Tandon_School_of_Engineering), where they work on subjects such as [graph isomorphism](wikipedia://en/247577).[^o11] Gregory was awarded the [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellowship) (also known as the "Genius Grant") in 1981.
