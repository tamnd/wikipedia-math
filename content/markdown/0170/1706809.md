# David Slepian

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Slepian"}

**David S. Slepian** (June 30, 1923 – November 29, 2007) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is best known for his work with [algebraic coding theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_coding_theory), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), and [distributed source coding](wikipedia://en/23699292). He was colleagues with [Claude Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693) and [Richard Hamming](wikipedia://en/99326) at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Born in [Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh,_Pennsylvania), he gained a [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.Sc.) at [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) before joining the US Army in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), as a [sonic deception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_deception) officer in the 3133rd Signal Service Company of the [Ghost Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_Army).[^o4]

He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1949, writing his dissertation in physics. After post-doctoral work at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) and [University of Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sorbonne), he worked at the Mathematics Research Center at [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs), where he pioneered work in [algebraic coding theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_coding_theory) on [group codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_code), first published in the paper *A Class of Binary Signaling Alphabets*. Here, he also worked along with other information theory giants such as [Claude Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693) and [Richard Hamming](wikipedia://en/99326). He also proved the possibility of [singular detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_detection), a perhaps unintuitive result. He is also known for [Slepian's lemma](wikipedia://en/12085484) in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) (1962), and for discovering a fundamental result in [distributed source coding](wikipedia://en/23699292) called [Slepian–Wolf coding](wikipedia://en/30474423) with [Jack Keil Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Keil_Wolf) (1973).

He later joined the [University of Hawaiʻi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawaiʻi). His father was [Joseph Slepian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Slepian), also a scientist.[^o5] His wife is the noted children's author [Jan Slepian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Slepian).

## Slepian Functions {#Slepian_Functions}

*Image: DPSS figure.svg.* Three examples of [Slepian functions](wikipedia://en/76986425), named after David Slepian.

Slepian's joint work with H.J. Landau and H.O. Pollak[^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11] on discrete prolate spheroidal wave functions and sequences (DPSWF, DPSS) eventually led to the naming of the sequences as [Slepian functions](wikipedia://en/76986425) or "Slepians". The naming suggestion was provided by [Bob Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Ladislav_Parker) of Scripp's Institute of Oceanography, who suggested that "discrete prolate spheroidal sequences" was a "mouthful". The term "prolates" is equally in current use.

This work was fundamental to the development of the method of [multitaper](wikipedia://en/9166436) spectral analysis, where the discrete Slepian functions are used as an essential component to provide [bias](wikipedia://en/40786) control while reducing estimation [variance](wikipedia://en/32344).
