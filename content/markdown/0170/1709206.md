# J. W. S. Cassels

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="J. W. S. Cassels"}

John Cassels

**John William Scott** "**Ian**" **Cassels**, [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o3] (11 July 1922 – 27 July 2015[^o4]) was a British mathematician.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Cassels was educated at Neville's Cross Council School in [Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_England) and [George Heriot's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Heriot's_School) in Edinburgh. He went on to study at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) and graduated with an undergraduate [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Scotland%29) (MA) degree in 1943.

His academic career was interrupted in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) when he was involved in [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) at [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037). After the war he became a research student of [Louis Mordell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Mordell) at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge); he received his PhD in 1949 and was elected a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow#Oxford,_Cambridge_and_Dublin) of Trinity in the same year.

Cassels then spent a year lecturing in mathematics at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) before returning to Cambridge as a lecturer in 1950. He was appointed [Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_%28academic_rank%29) in Arithmetic in 1963, the same year he was elected as a fellow of the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London). In 1967 he was appointed as [Sadleirian Professor of Pure Mathematics](wikipedia://en/291556) at Cambridge. In 1969 he became Head of the [Department of Pure Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics](wikipedia://en/3254271). He retired in 1984.

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

He initially worked on [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225). After a period when he worked on [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153) and [diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558), he returned in the later 1950s to the arithmetic of elliptic curves, writing a series of papers connecting the [Selmer group](wikipedia://en/1970862) with [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) and laying some of the foundations of the modern theory of [infinite descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_descent).[^o3] His best-known single result may be the proof that the [Tate-Shafarevich group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate-Shafarevich_group) of an elliptic curve, if it is finite, must have order that is a square; the proof being by construction of an [alternating form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_form).

Cassels often studied individual [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109) by [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) and [p-adic methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_methods).

His publications include 200 papers. His advanced textbooks have influenced generations of mathematicians; some of Cassels's books have remained in print for decades.
