# Yinyu Ye

[Ye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ye_%28surname%29)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yinyu Ye"}

**Yinyu Ye** (; born 1948) is a [Chinese American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_American) [theoretical computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_computer_scientist) working on [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033). He is a specialist in [interior point methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_point_method), especially in [convex minimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_minimization) and [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730). He is a professor of Management Science and Engineering and [Kwoh-Ting Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwoh-Ting_Li) Chair Professor of Engineering at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). He also holds a courtesy appointment in the Department of [Electrical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_Engineering). Ye also is a co-founder of minMax Optimization Inc.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Yinyu Ye was born in 1948 in [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan), [Hubei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubei), China. He attended [Huazhong University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huazhong_University_of_Science_and_Technology) and graduated with a B.S. in Systems and Control in 1982. He received a Ph.D in Engineering Economic Systems from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1988, under the supervision of [George B. Dantzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_B._Dantzig).

## Research publications {#Research_publications}

Ye wrote *Interior-Point Algorithms: Theory and Analysis*. He joined [David Luenberger](wikipedia://en/9706330) for the third edition of Luenberger's *Linear and Nonlinear Programming*.[^o4]

In recent years, Ye has developed computational methods and theory using [semidefinite programming](wikipedia://en/4993539) for practical problems like the localization of network sensors. In [computational economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_economics), Ye has also established new complexity results for problems concerning the computation of an [economic equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_equilibrium).[^o5]

## Awards {#Awards}

Ye was a 2009 co-recipient of the [John von Neumann Theory Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann_Theory_Prize).[^o5] He was elected to the 2006 class of [Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Institute for Operations Research and the Management Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences).[^o7]

## Positions {#Positions}

Before joining [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), Ye was a Henry B. Tippie Research Professor at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa). Ye is a co-founder of minMax Optimization, a technology company based in Palo Alto and Shanghai focused on creating optimization tools for geospatial and financial problems.
