# Hervé Jacquet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hervé Jacquet"}

**Hervé Jacquet** is a [French American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_American) mathematician, working in [automorphic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_forms). He is considered one of the founders of the theory of [automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_forms#Automorphic_representations) and their associated [L-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-functions), and his results play a central role in modern [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Jacquet entered the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in 1959 and obtained his doctorat d'état under the direction of [Roger Godement](wikipedia://en/971810) in 1967. He held academic positions at the [Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_National_de_la_Recherche_Scientifique) (1963–1969), the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) (1967–1969), the [University of Maryland at College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland_at_College_Park) (1969–1970), the [Graduate Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUNY_Graduate_Center) of the [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York) (1970–1974), and became a professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1974, becoming Professor Emeritus in 2007.

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

The book by Jacquet and [Robert Langlands](wikipedia://en/25986) on $\operatorname{GL}(2)$[^o5] was an eclipsing event in the history of number theory. It presented a [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of automorphic forms and their associated L−functions for the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) $\operatorname{GL}(2)$, establishing among other things the [Jacquet–Langlands correspondence](wikipedia://en/20606232) which explains very precisely how automorphic forms for $\operatorname{GL}(2)$ relate to those for [quaternion algebras](wikipedia://en/2776890).

Equally important was the book by Godement and Jacquet,[^o6] which defined, for the first time, the standard L-functions attached to automorphic representations of $\operatorname{GL}(n)$, now called Godement–Jacquet L-functions, and proved their basic, oft-used analytic properties.

His papers with [Joseph Shalika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Shalika)[^o7][^o8] and the papers with [Ilya Piatetski-Shapiro](wikipedia://en/802738) and Shalika[^o9][^o10][^o11] pertain to L-functions of pairs, called the Rankin-Selberg L-functions, attached to representations of $\operatorname{GL}(n)$ and $\operatorname{GL}(m)$, and the so-called converse theorem, which are crucial to our understanding of automorphic forms. A basic ingredient of this effort was an elaboration of properties of [Whittaker models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whittaker_models) and [functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whittaker_functions), which Jacquet had made contributions to since his thesis. The papers with Shalika also established the uniqueness of isobaric decompositions of automorphic forms on $\operatorname{GL}(n)$, thus providing evidence for certain conjectures of Langlands.

In the mid-1980s, Jacquet forayed into a new territory in the field and created[^o12][^o13][^o14] the [relative trace formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_trace_formula) in representation theory, an important tool in modern number theory, which vastly generalizes the [Kuznetsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuznetsov_trace_formula) and [Petersson formulae](wikipedia://en/20484815) from the classical setup. While the usual [Selberg trace formula](wikipedia://en/981694), as well as its generalizations due to [James Arthur](wikipedia://en/9525221), consists in developing an expression for the integral of the kernel over the diagonal, the relative version integrates the kernel over other appropriate subgroups.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

He was elected corresponding member of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences) in 1980. In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o16] He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2013.[^o17]
