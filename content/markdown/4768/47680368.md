# Faultless disagreement

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Faultless disagreement"}

A **faultless disagreement** is a disagreement when Party A states that *P* is true, while Party B states that *non-P* is true, and neither party is at fault. Disagreements of this kind may arise in areas of evaluative discourse, such as [aesthetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetics), justification of [beliefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief) or [moral values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_value), etc. A representative example is that John says Paris is more interesting than Rome, while Bob claims Rome is more interesting than Paris. In the case of a faultless disagreement, it is possible that if any party gives up their claim, there will be no improvement in the position of any of them.[^o3]

:::

Within the framework of [formal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic) it is impossible that both *P* and *not-P* are true, and it was attempted to justify faultless disagreements within the framework of [relativism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativism) of the [Truth](wikipedia://en/29920) (propositional truth being [relative to perspectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspectivism)),[^o2] Max Kölbel and Sven Rosenkranz argued that genuine faultless disagreements are impossible.[^o3][^o2] However, defenses of faultless disagreement, and of alethic relativism more generally, continue to be made by critics of formal logic as it is currently constructed.[^o4]
