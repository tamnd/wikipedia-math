::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Kaleidocycle"}

*Image: Chain-kaleidocycle6-rotate.gif.* A kaleidocycle before it is wrapped into a ring makes a chain of 6 [disphenoids](wikipedia://en/8476166) connected edge to edge.

A **kaleidocycle** or **flextangle** is a [flexible polyhedron](wikipedia://en/9070022) connecting six [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) (or [disphenoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetragonal_disphenoid)) on opposite edges into a cycle. If the faces of the disphenoids are equilateral triangles, it can be constructed from a stretched [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858) net with four triangles in one direction and an even number in the other direction.

:::

The kaleidocycle has degenerate pairs of coinciding edges in transition, which function as hinges. The kaleidocycle has an additional property that it can be continuously twisted around a ring axis, showing 4 sets of 6 triangular faces. The kaleidocycle is invariant under twists about its ring axis by $k\pi/2$, where $k$ is an integer, and can therefore be continuously twisted.

Kaleidocycles can be constructed from a single piece of paper (with dimensions $l \times 2l$) without tearing or using adhesive. Because of this and their continuous twisting property, they are often given as examples of simple [origami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origami) toys. The kaleidocycle is sometimes called a **flexahedron** in analogy to the planar [flexagon](wikipedia://en/98316), which has similar symmetry under flexing transformations.

## Variations {#Variations}

Beyond 6 sides, higher even number of tetrahedra, 8, 10, 12, etc., can be chained together. These models will leave a central gap, depending on the proportions of the triangle faces.[^o8]

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Kaleidozyklen Escher.jpg.* Crafted kaleidocycles from the book *M. C. Escher: Kaleidocycles*, based on templates from [Doris Schattschneider](wikipedia://en/39957951).

In 1958, during a project focused on developing structural configurations for paper, [Wallace Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Walker) discovered a geometric [net](wikipedia://en/1148129) consisting of sixty [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428) that, when scored and folded, form a movable three-dimensional ring capable of continuous inversion. This was patented as **[IsoAxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IsoAxis)** () and served as the inspiration for [Doris Schattschneider](wikipedia://en/39957951), from [Moravian College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_College), to build a similar ring using tetrahedrons instead. [^o11]

Wallace Walker coined the word *kaleidocycle* in the 1950s from the Greek *kalos* (beautiful), *eidos* (form), and *kyklos* (ring).

In 1977 [Doris Schattschneider](wikipedia://en/39957951) and Wallace Walker published a book about them using [M.C. Escher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.C._Escher) patterns.[^o12][^o13]

## Cultural uses {#Cultural_uses}

The shape was called a *flextangle* by characters in the 2018 science fantasy adventure film *[A Wrinkle in Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Wrinkle_in_Time_%282018_film%29)*, which depicted a paper model with its inner faces colored with hearts and patterns which become hidden when those faces are folded together. The paper toy suggested how space and time could be folded to explain the magical travels of the story. The toy is given to the daughter by her father at the start of the movie and its hearts show how love can be *enfolded* and still be there, even after the father mysteriously disappears.[^o15]
