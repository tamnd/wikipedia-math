# Gillies' conjecture

Mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gillies' conjecture"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Gillies' conjecture** is a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) about the distribution of [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) [factors](wikipedia://en/49492) of [Mersenne numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_number). It was made by [Donald B. Gillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_B._Gillies) in a 1964 paper[^o2] in which he also announced the discovery of three new [Mersenne primes](wikipedia://en/18908). The conjecture is a specialization of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) and is a refinement of conjectures due to [I. J. Good](wikipedia://en/404404)[^o3] and [Daniel Shanks](wikipedia://en/18171362).[^o4]

:::

Several papers have given empirical support to the conjecture, but it disagrees with the widely accepted (but also open) [Lenstra–Pomerance–Wagstaff conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenstra–Pomerance–Wagstaff_conjecture).

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=heading name="The conjecture"}

## The conjecture {#The_conjecture}

Let $p$ be a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) and $M_p=2^p-1$ the corresponding [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908). If $A < B < \sqrt{M_p}$, as $B/A$ and $M_p \rightarrow \infty$, the number of prime divisors of $M$ in the interval $[A, B]$ is [Poisson distributed](wikipedia://en/23009144) with [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653)

$$\begin{cases} \log(\log B /\log A) & \text{ if }A \geq 2p, \\[1pt] \log(\log B/\log 2p) & \text{ if } A < 2p. \end{cases}$$

The conjecture implies that

1. The number of Mersenne primes less than $x$ is asymptotically $\textstyle\frac{2\log\log x}{\log 2}$.
1. The expected number of Mersenne primes $M_p$ with $x \le p \le 2x$ is $2$.
1. The probability that $M_p$ is prime is $\textstyle\frac{2 \log 2p }{p\log 2}$.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Incompatibility with Lenstra–Pomerance–Wagstaff conjecture"}

## Incompatibility with Lenstra–Pomerance–Wagstaff conjecture {#Incompatibility_with_Lenstra–Pomerance–Wagstaff_conjecture}

The [Lenstra–Pomerance–Wagstaff conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenstra–Pomerance–Wagstaff_conjecture) gives different values:[^o9][^o10]

1. The number of Mersenne primes less than $x$ is asymptotically $\textstyle\frac{e^\gamma}{\log 2} \log\log x$.
1. The expected number of Mersenne primes $M_p$ with $x \le p \le 2x$ is $e^\gamma$.
1. The probability that $M_p$ is prime is $\textstyle\frac{e^\gamma\log ap}{p\log 2}$, where $a=2$ if $p=3 \!\!\pmod{4}$ and $a=6$ otherwise.

Asymptotically, these values are about 11% smaller.

:::
