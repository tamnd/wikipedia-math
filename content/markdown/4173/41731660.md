# Pollock's conjectures

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pollock's conjectures"}

**Pollock's conjectures** are a set of closely related [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) in [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333).[^o2] They were first stated in 1850 by [Frederick Pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Frederick_Pollock,_1st_Baronet),[^o2][^o3] a lawyer and politician who contributed many papers on mathematics to the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society). These conjectures are a partial extension of the [Fermat polygonal number theorem](wikipedia://en/847886) to three-dimensional [figurate numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figurate_numbers), also called polyhedral numbers.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Statement of the conjectures"}

## Statement of the conjectures {#Statement_of_the_conjectures}

- **Pollock tetrahedral numbers conjecture**: Every [positive integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer) is the sum of at most 5 [tetrahedral numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_number).

The numbers that are not the sum of at most 4 tetrahedral numbers are given by the sequence 17, 27, 33, 52, 73, ..., of 241 terms, with 343,867 conjectured to be the last such number.

- **Pollock octahedral numbers conjecture**: Every positive integer is the sum of at most 7 [octahedral numbers](wikipedia://en/584310).

This conjecture has been proven for all sufficiently large numbers. Namely, every number greater than $e^{10^7}\approx6.59\cdot10^{4342944}$ is sufficiently large.[^o7]

- **Pollock cube numbers conjecture**: Every positive integer is the sum of at most 9 [cube numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_numbers).

The cube numbers case was established from 1909 to 1912 by [Wieferich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Wieferich)[^o8] and [A. J. Kempner](wikipedia://en/36825600).[^o9]

- **Pollock icosahedral and dodecahedral numbers conjectures**: Every positive integer is the sum of at most 13 [icosahedral numbers](wikipedia://en/39530422). Every positive integer is the sum of at most 21 [dodecahedral numbers](wikipedia://en/39530296).

These two conjectures are corrected and confirmed true in 2025.[^o10]

- **Pollock centered nonagonal numbers conjecture**: Every positive integer is the sum of at most 11 [centered nonagonal numbers](wikipedia://en/1216013).

This conjecture was confirmed as true in 2023.[^o11]

:::
