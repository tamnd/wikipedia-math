# Math Prize for Girls

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Math Prize for Girls"}

The **Advantage Testing Foundation** **Math Prize for Girls,** often referred to as **The Math Prize for Girls,** is an annual [mathematics competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_competition) open to female high school students from the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) and [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada). The competition offers the world’s largest single monetary math prize in a math contest for young women. In 2017, the First-Place prize was \$46,000 (split equally amongst the three-way tie for first) with another \$9,000 divided among the remaining finalists.[^o2] Girls may win a maximum of \$100,000[^o3] by participating in the competition over multiple years.[^o4] Organized each year by the [Advantage Testing Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advantage_Testing_Foundation), the competition is considered to be the preeminent female math competition for young women in North America.[^o5][^o6]

:::

The single-day annual contest is open to female high-school students in 12th grade or below, from the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) and [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada) who have attained a qualifying score on the [American Mathematics Competitions](wikipedia://en/665435) Exams, specifically the [AMC 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_10) or [AMC 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMC_12) given in February each year.[^o7] Up to 300 participants are then selected each year for the competition. Participants must complete 20 short-answer problems in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261), and other math topics in 150 minutes. The exams are then reviewed by a panel of judges, who award cash prizes to the top-scoring participants.[^o8]

## History {#History}

The competition was founded in 2009 by [Arun Alagappan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arun_Alagappan) and Dr. Ravi Boppana in an effort to inspire the next generation of [female mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_mathematicians) and create a community of young women who share a passion for math.[^o10] Boppana, the competition’s cofounder and Director, said in a statement that "the Math Prize was created to debunk gender stereotypes, and to support young women who see higher-level mathematics as a pursuit that is challenging, fun, and incredibly rewarding.”[^o11] The first two years of the competition were held at [NYU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYU), and since 2011, the competition has been held annually at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts).[^o12]

## Advisory board {#Advisory_board}

As of 2017, the competition's Board of Advisors has the following members:[^o18]

- Ravi Boppana, Director of Mathematics at *Advantage Testing, Inc.*
- [Ioana Dumitriu](wikipedia://en/44832995), Professor of Mathematics at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego)
- [Maria Klawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Klawe), President of [Harvey Mudd College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Mudd_College)
- [Richard Rusczyk](wikipedia://en/2233847), Founder of *Art of Problem Solving, Inc*. and Director of the [USA Mathematical Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAMTS)
- [Michael Sipser](wikipedia://en/3644015), Professor of [Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Mathematics) and Dean of Science at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT)
- [Gigliola Staffilani](wikipedia://en/44916025), the *Abby Rockefeller Mauze* Professor of Mathematics at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT)
- [Lauren Williams](wikipedia://en/43551379), Professor of Mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University)
- Joseph Woo
