# Sujit Sahu

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sujit Sahu"}

**Sujit K Sahu** is a [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who is a professor of statistics, mathematics and S3RI at the [University of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southampton). He has contributed to practical [Bayesian modelling](wikipedia://en/49571).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Sahu received a BSc in statistics from Presidency College, [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta) (1984-1989) and a Master of Statistics from the [Indian Statistical Institute](wikipedia://en/2238418), Calcutta (1987-1989).[^o3]

He has a PhD in statistics from the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut) and previously worked at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) (1994-1996) and [Cardiff University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_University) (1997-1999) before joining the [University of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southampton) in 1999.[^o4]

## Work {#Work}

Sahu worked on an [EPSRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_and_Physical_Sciences_Research_Council) funded research project to develop methodology in both air pollution and health outcome data modelling and their integration.[^o4] In addition, Sahu worked as a co-investigator on a NERC funded research grant to develop statistical models to study nutrient dynamics in annual and seasonal periods.[^o4]

Professor Sujit Sahu has collaborated on methodological spatial statistics projects with [Alan Gelfand](wikipedia://en/53438362) and David Holland.[^o4]

He has also worked on an EPSRC funded (2010-2012), cross-disciplinary research project titled "MetSim: A Hospital Simulation Support Tool" with Paul Harper, [Cardiff University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_University), the [Met Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Met_Office), and the Southampton University Hospital Trust. His statistical work as part of this project included a Bayesian model for forecasting the number of hospital admissions and was piloted by the Met Office for use by several UK hospitals.[^o4]
