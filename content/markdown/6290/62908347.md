# Giovanni Prodi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Giovanni Prodi"}

**Giovanni Prodi** (28 July 1925 – 29 January 2010) was an Italian mathematician, also known for many activities concerning the teaching of mathematics.

:::

There is a professorship of mathematics at the [University of Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Würzburg) named in his honour,[^o3] created in 2006.

## Early life {#Early_life}

His father, Mario Prodi, was an engineer and his mother, Enrica, a primary school teacher. He was the eldest among 9 siblings, which included former Italian Prime Minister [Romano Prodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romano_Prodi), member of the European Parliament [Vittorio Prodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_Prodi), and the medical scientist [Giorgio Prodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Prodi). Prodi studied at the Liceo Ariosto in Reggio nell'Emilia, which was the main city of the region. Following that, he entered the [University of Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Parma) to study mathematics in 1943 amid World War II hostilities.[^o6]

## Military service {#Military_service}

He was drafted into the [National Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Republican_Army), reluctantly because of the threat of harm to his family. He was sent to Germany as part of an Italian camp and trained there as a telephonist. In 1944, he deserted the Army along with several comrades and returned to Parma, where he was taken prisoner by the advancing Allies. He was detained at [Coltano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coltano), near Pisa, where he remained for 5 months before being released.

## Academia {#Academia}

After his military service ended, Prodi returned to the University of Parma to continue his university studies. Upon graduation, he joined the [University of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Milan) as an assistant professor, where he worked with [Giovanni Ricci](wikipedia://en/62908346). He held the chair of mathematical analysis at the [University of Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Trieste) from 1956 to 1963, and then at the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa).[^o6]

He was also interested in improving mathematics education, proposing radical new ideas on mathematical teaching, emphasising on [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [constructive mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_mathematics) and promoting algorithmic thinking and problem solving.[^o6][^o8]

## Death {#Death}

After several years of deteriorating health due to [Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson's_disease), he died as a result of a cardiac arrest in 2010.[^o6]
