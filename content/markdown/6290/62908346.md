# Giovanni Ricci (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Giovanni Ricci"}

**Giovanni Ricci** (17 August 1904 – 9 September 1973) was an Italian mathematician.

:::

He was born and brought up in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), where he did his school education. He then moved to [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) to study mathematics at the [Scuola Normale Superiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore) (associated with the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa)). He was an assistant professor at the [University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome) for two years until 1928 when he moved to his alma mater Scuola Normale Superiore, where he was a professor for 8 years and produced research works in the fields of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), and theory of series, with highly significant results being obtained on the [Goldbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldbach_conjecture) and [Hilbert's seventh problem](wikipedia://en/392579).[^o4]

Ricci moved to the [University of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Milan) towards the end of 1936, where he remained as a professor for 36 years until his death on 9 September 1973. While in Milan, Ricci was largely committed to teaching and administrative work and his research output declined.[^o3][^o4]

Ricci served as the president of [Italian Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/11969092) from 1964 to 1967. He was a member of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) since 1957. He was also a member of [Istituto Lombardo Accademia di Scienze e Lettere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Lombardo_Accademia_di_Scienze_e_Lettere).[^o4]

Ricci is noted to have had a significant influence on [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859)-winning mathematician [Enrico Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475).[^o5]
