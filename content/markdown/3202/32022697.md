# Stone algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stone algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Stone algebra** or **Stone lattice** is a [pseudocomplemented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudocomplemented) [distributive lattice](wikipedia://en/310883) *L* in which any of the following equivalent statements hold for all $x, y \in L:$[^o4]

:::

- $$(x\wedge y)^* = x^*\vee y^*;$$
- $$(x\vee y)^{**} = x^{**}\vee y^{**};$$
- $$x^* \vee x^{**} = 1.$$

They were introduced by (Grätzer Schmidt 1957),[^o3] and named after [Marshall Harvey Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Harvey_Stone).

The set $S(L) \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=} \{ x^* \mid x\in L \}$ is called the **skeleton** of *L*. Then *L* is a Stone algebra if and only if its skeleton *S*(*L*) is a sublattice of *L*.[^o4]

[Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959) are Stone algebras, and Stone algebras are [Ockham algebras](wikipedia://en/32002449).

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The open-set lattice of an [extremally disconnected space](wikipedia://en/5783949) is a Stone algebra.
- The lattice of positive [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) of a given positive integer is a Stone lattice.

:::
