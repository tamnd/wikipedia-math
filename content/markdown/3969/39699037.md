# Robert Wallace (minister)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Wallace"}

*Image: Wallace - Dissertation on the numbers of mankind in antient and modern times, 1753 - 5808147.tif.* *Dissertation on the numbers of mankind in antient and modern times*, 1753

**Robert Wallace** (7 January 1697 – 29 July 1771) was a minister of the [Church of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scotland) and writer on population.

:::

## Life {#Life}

*Image: DSCN6050 St. Giles' Cathedral during Covid-19 lockdown.jpg.* The New North was one of four parishes contained within St. Giles' Cathedral

He was the only son of Margaret Stewart, wife of Rev Matthew Wallace, the parish minister of [Kincardine-in-Menteith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kincardine-in-Menteith)[^o3] (west of [Stirling](wikipedia://en/61311)), where he was born on 7 January 1697. Educated at [Stirling](wikipedia://en/61311) grammar school, he then attended the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) in 1711, and acted for a time (1720) as assistant to James Gregory, the University professor of mathematics. He was one of the founders of the [Rankenian Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rankenian_Club) in 1717.[^o4]

On 31 July 1722, Wallace was licensed as a preacher by the presbytery of [Dunblane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunblane), Perthshire, and he was presented by the [Marquis of Annandale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Johnstone,_2nd_Marquess_of_Annandale) to the parish of [Moffat, Dumfriesshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moffat,_Dumfriesshire), in August 1723. In 1733, he became minister of [New Greyfriars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Greyfriars), [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh). He offended the government of 1736 by declining to read from his pulpit the proclamation against the [Porteous rioters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porteous_riot). On 30 August 1738, he was translated to the [New North (St Giles)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_St_Giles'_Parish_Church). In 1742, on a change of ministry at Westminster, he regained influence, and was entrusted for five years with the management of church business and the distribution of ecclesiastical patronage. From a suggestion of John Mathison of the [High Kirk, St Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Giles_Cathedral), Wallace, togerther with [Alexander Webster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Webster) of the Tolbooth St Giles, developed the Ministers' Widows' Fund.[^o4]

On 12 May 1743, Wallace was elected [Moderator of the General Assembly of the Church of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderator_of_the_General_Assembly_of_the_Church_of_Scotland). The Assembly approved the Widows' Fund. scheme, and at the end of the year he submitted it in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) to [Robert Craigie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Craigie,_Lord_Glendoick), the [Lord Advocate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Advocate), who saw it into legislation as the [Ministers' Widows Fund (Scotland) Act 1743](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministers'_Widows_Fund_%28Scotland%29_Act_1743) ([17 Geo. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17_Geo._2). c. 11).[^o4]

In June 1744, Wallace was appointed a [Chaplain in Ordinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_Household) to King George II in Scotland and [Dean of the Chapel Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_of_the_Chapel_Royal). He received the honorary degree of D.D. from the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) on 13 March 1759, and died on 29 July 1771.[^o4] His position at New Church, St Giles was filled by [Rev William Gloag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gloag_%28minister%29).

## Works {#Works}

Wallace published in 1753 a *Dissertation on the Numbers of Mankind in Ancient and Modern Times*. It contained criticism of the chapter on the *Populousness of Ancient Nations* in [David Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hume)'s *Political Discourses*. The work was translated into French under the supervision of [Montesquieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montesquieu), and it was republished in an English edition with a memoir in 1809. In 1758 appeared Wallace's *Characteristics of the Present State of Great Britain*. In *Various Prospects of Mankind, Nature, and Providence* (1761), he recurred to his population theories, and was believed (by [William Hazlitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hazlitt) and [Thomas Noon Talfourd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Noon_Talfourd)) to have influenced [Thomas Malthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Robert_Malthus).[^o4]

## Family {#Family}

In October 1726, Wallace married Helen Turnbull, daughter of Rev George Turnbull, minister of [Tyninghame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyninghame) in [East Lothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Lothian). She died on 9 February 1776, leaving two sons. All three of their children died unmarried:

- Rev Dr Matthew Wallace DD, vicar of [Tenterden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenterden) in [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent)
