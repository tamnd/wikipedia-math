# Fabry gap theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fabry gap theorem"}

In [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), the **Fabry gap theorem** is a result about the [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) of [lacunary](wikipedia://en/8969021) [power series](wikipedia://en/59958). Such a power series is "badly behaved" in the sense that it cannot be extended to be an [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) anywhere on its [circle of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476). It was proved by [Eugène Fabry](wikipedia://en/74324419) in 1896.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $0<n_0<n_1<\dots$ be a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) such that the sequence $n_k/k$ diverges to $\infty$, or equivalently

$$D := \limsup_{k\to\infty} \frac{k}{n_k} = 0.$$

Let $(a_k)_{k\ge0}$ be a sequence of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) such that the power series

$$f(z) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k z^{n_k}$$

has [radius of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476) $1$. Then the unit circle is a [natural boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_boundary) for the series *$f$*.[^o5][^o6]

The theorem may be deduced from the first main theorem of [Turán's method](wikipedia://en/39697339).

### Converse statement {#Converse_statement}

An exact [converse](wikipedia://en/155941) to the theorem was established by [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) in 1942.[^o8] If $D > 0$, then there exists a power series with exponent sequence $(n_k)_{k\ge0}$ of radius of convergence equal to $1$, but for which the unit circle is not a natural boundary.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Pólya's gap theorem"}

## Pólya's gap theorem {#Pólya's_gap_theorem}

**Pólya's gap theorem**,[^o11] also known as the **Fabry–Pólya theorem**,[^o12] is a refinement of the Fabry gap theorem. The theorem was proven by Fabry in 1899[^o13] and put in its modern form by [Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) in 1929,[^o14] who also supplied a simpler proof. It states that every arc of the circle of convergence of length greater than $2\pi D$ contains a [singularity](wikipedia://en/54240) of *$f$*. When *$D = 0$*, it follows that the singularities are [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) on the circle of convergence, which is the conclusion of the Fabry gap theorem. This extension also bears a converse.[^o11][^o12]
