# Michio Jimbo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michio Jimbo"}

**Michio Jimbo** is a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). He is known for his introduction of [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678) (independently of [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747)), his contributions to the theory of [integrable systems](wikipedia://en/2573213) and their [correlation functions](wikipedia://en/1651916), and his work with [Mikio Sato](wikipedia://en/373336) and [Tetsuji Miwa](wikipedia://en/62132537) on [holonomic quantum fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holonomic_quantum_field) and [isomonodromic deformation](wikipedia://en/16987249) theory.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Jimbo was born in [Chiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiba_%28city%29), Japan. He received his bachelor's degree from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in 1974 and entered graduate study at the [Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/1978962) (RIMS) at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University) under the supervision of [Mikio Sato](wikipedia://en/373336). He received his master's degree from Kyoto University in 1976 and his doctorate in 1986.[^o3] Between 1976 and 1988, Jimbo worked as a research associate at RIMS. In 1988 he became an associate professor in the Department of Mathematics at Kyoto University, where he was later promoted to full professor. In 2000 he moved to the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo). He is currently a specially appointed professor at [Rikkyo University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikkyo_University) in Tokyo.[^o3][^o6]

## Research {#Research}

### Holonomic quantum fields and isomonodromic deformation {#Holonomic_quantum_fields_and_isomonodromic_deformation}

In the late 1970s, Jimbo, together with Sato and Miwa, developed the theory of [holonomic quantum fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holonomic_quantum_field), establishing an unexpected connection between [isomonodromic deformation](wikipedia://en/16987249) theory for linear differential equations and [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267).[^o9] This work provided a field-theoretic framework for the [correlation functions](wikipedia://en/1651916) of the two-dimensional [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744) and led to connections with [Painlevé transcendents](wikipedia://en/2033003).[^o10]

### Quantum groups {#Quantum_groups}

In 1985, Jimbo introduced a *q*-deformation of the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) of a [simple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/59500649), independently of Drinfeld's contemporaneous work on [Hopf algebras](wikipedia://en/325714) and the quantum [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810).[^o12] These structures, now known as [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678) or Drinfeld–Jimbo algebras, have had far-reaching applications in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), and [exactly solvable models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exactly_solvable_model) in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481).[^o4]

### Integrable hierarchies {#Integrable_hierarchies}

Working with Etsurō Date, [Masaki Kashiwara](wikipedia://en/4376209), and Miwa, Jimbo developed the theory of τ-functions for the [KP (Kadomtsev–Petviashvili)](wikipedia://en/4211219) [integrable hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_hierarchy) and related hierarchies, connecting them to the representation theory of infinite-dimensional [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944).[^o14][^o15]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Jimbo has received several major awards for his contributions to mathematical physics, often jointly with his long-time collaborator Tetsuji Miwa:[^o3]

- 1987 – Autumn Prize of the [Mathematical Society of Japan](wikipedia://en/18376786) (with [Tetsuji Miwa](wikipedia://en/62132537))
- 1993 – [Japan Academy Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Academy_Prize_%28academics%29)[^o17]
- 1999 – [Asahi Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahi_Prize) (with Tetsuji Miwa)[^o3]
- 2010 – [Wigner Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigner_Medal), "for his introduction of quantum groups and his study of affine Lie algebras, in connection with classical and quantum integrable systems"[^o4][^o18]
- 2013 – [Dannie Heineman Prize for Mathematical Physics](wikipedia://en/4854281) (with Tetsuji Miwa), "for profound developments in integrable systems and their correlation functions in statistical mechanics and quantum field theory, making use of quantum groups, algebraic analysis, and deformation theory"[^o3][^o19]
