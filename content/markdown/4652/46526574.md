# Philip Drazin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Philip Drazin"}

**Philip Gerald Drazin** (25 May 1934 – 10 January 2002) was a British mathematician and a leading international expert in [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Drazin was born in East London to Isaac Drazin, who was of Russian-Jewish origin and ran a shop in [Hampstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead), and Leah Wexler. Drazin went to boarding school at [St Christopher School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Christopher_School), [Letchworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letchworth) during the Blitz. His older brother [Michael](wikipedia://en/13119440) is also a mathematician.[^o3]

Drazin completed his PhD at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) under [G. I. Taylor](wikipedia://en/610133) in 1958. He was awarded the [Smith's Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize) in 1957. After leaving Cambridge, he spent two years at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) before moving to the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol), where he stayed and became a Professor until retiring in 1999. After retiring, he lectured at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) and the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath) until his death in 2002.

Drazin worked on [hydrodynamic stability](wikipedia://en/21757046) and the transition to [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664). His 1974 paper *On a model of instability of a slowly-varying flow* introduced the concept of a [global mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_mode) solution to a system of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) such as the [Navier-Stokes equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier-Stokes_equations). He also worked on [solitons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitons).

In 1998 he was awarded the [Symons Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symons_Gold_Medal) of the [Royal Meteorological Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Meteorological_Society).[^o4]

Drazin died at his home in [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol) in 2002.[^o3]
