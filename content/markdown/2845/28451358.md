# Michael Herman (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Herman"}

**Michael Robert Herman** (6 November 1942 – 2 November 2000) was a [French American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_American) mathematician. He was one of the leading experts on the theory of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087).

:::

Born in New York City, he was educated in France. He was a student at [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique) before being one of the first members of the Centre de Mathématiques created there by [Laurent Schwartz](wikipedia://en/317485). In 1976 he earned his PhD at the [Paris-Sud 11 University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_11_University), under supervision of [Harold Rosenberg](wikipedia://en/46753021). He introduced [Herman rings](wikipedia://en/29176060) in 1979.

Herman received the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize) in 1976. He was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in 1978 in Helsinki[^o3] and the ICM in 1998 in Berlin.[^o4] Among his students was [Jean-Christophe Yoccoz](wikipedia://en/589307), 1994 [Fields Medalist](wikipedia://en/10859).
