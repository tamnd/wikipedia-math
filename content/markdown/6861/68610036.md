# Shmuel Onn

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Shmuel Onn"}

**Shmuel Onn** (שמואל און; born 1960) is a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [Operations Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_Research) and Dresner Chair at the [Technion - Israel Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_-_Israel_Institute_of_Technology).[^o3] He is known for his contributions to [integer programming](wikipedia://en/411215) and nonlinear [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555).[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Shmuel Onn did his high school education in [Kadoorie Agricultural High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadoorie_Agricultural_High_School).[^o6] He received his B.Sc. (Cum Laude) in [Electrical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_Engineering) from Technion in 1980, and following his obligatory service in the [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Navy), received his M.Sc. from Technion in 1987.[^o6] Onn obtained his Ph.D. in operations research from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), with minors in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), in 1992. His thesis, "Discrete Geometry, Group Representations and Combinatorial Optimization: an Interplay", was advised by [Louis J. Billera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_J._Billera), [Bernd Sturmfels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernd_Sturmfels), and Leslie E. Trotter Jr.[^o7]

During 1992–1993 he was a postdoctoral fellow at [DIMACS](wikipedia://en/3684858),[^o8] and during 1993-1994 he was an [Alexander von Humboldt postdoctoral fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation) at the [University of Passau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Passau), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany).[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

In 1994 Onn joined the [Faculty of Data and Decision Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_William_Davidson_Faculty_of_Industrial_Engineering_&_Management) of Technion, where he is currently professor and Dresner Chair. He was also a Visiting Professor and Nachdiplom Lecturer at the [Institute for Mathematical Research](wikipedia://en/33084376), [ETH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH) [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) in 2009,[^o10] and visiting professor at the Mathematics Department in the [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis) (2001-2002).[^o11] Professor Onn has been also a long-term visitor at various mathematical research institutes including [Mittag-Leffler](wikipedia://en/10096452) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm), [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California),[^o12] and [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach) in Germany.[^o13] He also served as associate editor for *[Mathematics of Operations Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_Operations_Research)* in 2010–2016[^o14] and associate editor for *Discrete Optimization* in 2004–2010.[^o6]

Onn advised several students and postdoctoral researchers who proceeded to pursue academic careers, including Antoine Deza, Sharon Aviran, Tal Raviv, Nir Halman, and Martin Koutecký.[^o15]

## Research {#Research}

Shmuel Onn is known for his contributions to [integer programming](wikipedia://en/411215) and nonlinear [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555). In particular, he developed an algorithmic theory of linear and nonlinear integer programming in variable dimension using [Graver bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graver_bases).[^o4] This work introduced the theory of block-structured and n-fold integer programming,[^o17][^o18] and the broader theory of sparse and bounded tree-depth integer programming, shown to be fixed-parameter tractable.[^o19][^o20][^o21] These theories were followed up by other authors,[^o22][^o23][^o24][^o25][^o26][^o27] and have applications in a variety of areas.[^o28] [^o29][^o30][^o31][^o32][^o33]

Some other contributions of Onn include a framework that uses edge-directions for solving convex multi-criteria combinatorial optimization problems and its applications,[^o34][^o35][^o36] a universality theorem showing that every integer program is one over slim three-dimensional tables,[^o37][^o38] the settling of the complexity of hypergraph degree sequences,[^o39] and the introduction of colorful linear programming.[^o40]
