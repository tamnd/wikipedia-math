# Kevin McCurley (cryptographer)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kevin McCurley"}

**Kevin Snow McCurley**[^o7] is a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), and [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer), and a former[^o3] [research scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_scientist) at [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google). He has written publications about [information retrieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_retrieval), [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775), [parallel computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing), [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

When he was a child, McCurley had built model planes and cars, and he enjoyed making things with his hands.[^o4]

McCurley attended a high school in [San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California). There, one of his teachers, Judy Jones, showed him that "mathematics really could be fun and interesting" and encouraged him to attend mathematical contests.[^o4]

In his first year at [Santa Clara University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara_University), McCurley had Jerry Anderson, a former president of the MAA, as his professor in calculus; Anderson told "interesting stories" and was able to "relate the mathematics to history and to activities that were meaningful".[^o4] He started out as a mathematician, but he later retrained himself as a computer scientist.[^o4][^o6]

In 1981, McCurley received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign). His dissertation in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) was titled *Explicit Estimates for Functions of Primes in Arithmetic Progressions*, and his advisor was Paul Trevier Bateman.[^o4][^o7] He also received a master's in statistics there.[^o4]

In the fall of 1995, McCurley taught an undergraduate course on cryptology at the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico).[^o8][^o9]

After he was a post-doc at [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University), McCurley took a job at [USC (Los Angeles)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_%28Los_Angeles%29), where he published some papers with [Leonard Adleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Adleman) about algorithms and complexity.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Before 2005, McCurley worked at IBM Almaden Research Center, Sandia National Laboratories, and at the University of Southern California*.*[^o4] McCurley worked in a cryptography group at [Sandia National Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandia_National_Laboratories), where he worked on applying number theory to cryptography and parallel computing.[^o4] He then worked at IBM Research in California on [digital rights management](wikipedia://en/18938226), where he wrote a few patents;[^o4] he was there in January 1999.[^o11]

A former [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_%28corporate_title%29) of the [International Association for Cryptologic Research](wikipedia://en/87043), McCurley was selected as an IACR Fellow in 2005 for his "exemplary service as IACR President and essential leadership in IACR information systems".[^o12]

From 2005 to at least 2009 McCurley was a research scientist at Google Research.[^o4] There, he worked on search, advertisements, and [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_%28operating_system%29).[^o4]

## Miscellany {#Miscellany}

In 2000, McCurley suggested, while speaking at Financial Cryptography '00 conference, that, as a countermeasure against [email spam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email_spam), recipients of email from unknown senders should request that the message include a first name, a few dollars, or a donation to a specific charity as compensation. He also suggested that this be formalized in an [open standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_standard).[^o14]

### Bets about P vs NP {#Bets_about_P_vs_NP}

McCurley has made three bets with Ron Fagin about the outcome of the [P versus NP problem](wikipedia://en/6115). In each bet, the outcome P = NP would require Fagin to pay McCurley \$50, whereas P != NP would require McCurley to pay Fagin \$10. The first bet had a deadline of 31 December 2010, the second a deadline of 31 December 2020, and the third (made in 2021) a deadline of 31 December 2030.[^o16]2030
