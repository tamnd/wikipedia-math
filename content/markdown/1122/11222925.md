# Albert Nijenhuis

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Albert Nijenhuis"}

**Albert Nijenhuis** (November 21, 1926 – February 13, 2015)[^o3] was a Dutch-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specialized in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and the theory of [deformations in algebra and geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformations_in_algebra_and_geometry), and later worked in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).[^o4]

:::

His high school studies at the gymnasium in Arnhem were interrupted by the evacuation of Arnhem by the Nazis after the failure of [Operation Market Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Market_Garden) by the Allies. He continued his high school mathematical studies by himself on his grandparents’ farm, and then took state exams in 1945.

His university studies were carried out at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), where he received the degree of Candidaat (equivalent to a Bachelor of Science) in 1947, and a Doctorandus (equivalent to a Masters in Science) in 1950, cum laude. He was a Medewerker (associate) at the Mathematisch Centrum (now the [Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica](wikipedia://en/506063)) in Amsterdam 1951–1952. He obtained a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in mathematics in 1952, cum laude (Theory of the geometric object). His thesis advisor was [Jan Arnoldus Schouten](wikipedia://en/2862062).

He came to the United States in 1952 as a [Fulbright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright) fellow (1952–1953) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). He then studied at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton, New Jersey 1953–1955,[^o5] after which he spent a year as an Instructor in mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). He then moved to the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in Seattle, first as an assistant professor and then a professor of mathematics, departing in 1963 for the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), where he was a professor of mathematics until his retirement in 1987. He was a Fulbright Professor at the University of Amsterdam in 1963–1964, and a visiting professor at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva) in 1967–1968, and at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College) in 1977–1978. Following his retirement, he was a professor emeritus of the University of Pennsylvania and an Affiliate Professor at the University of Washington.

In 1958 he was an invited speaker at the [International Mathematical Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Congress) in Edinburgh. He was a J.S. [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow) in 1961–1962,[^o6] again studying at the Institute for Advanced Study. In 1966 he became a correspondent member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), and in 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

His early work was in the area of differential geometry. He developed the [Nijenhuis tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijenhuis_tensor) in 1951, during his PhD studies at the University of Amsterdam. It was also during this time that he explored the properties of the [Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schouten-Nijenhuis_bracket), although his work was not published until 1955. In a lecture at the American Mathematical Society Summer Institute in Differential Geometry (1956) in Seattle he was the first to mention [deformations](wikipedia://en/679351) of [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) structures and their exact relationship to [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060).[^o10]

With Alfred Frölicher, he developed the [Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frölicher-Nijenhuis_bracket) (1955). Further work in this area with Roger Richardson yielded the [Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket](wikipedia://en/3291647) (1964).

Soon thereafter his interests shifted to combinatorics. Much of his work was done with [Herbert S. Wilf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_S._Wilf), with whom he published a book in 1975.

After retiring, his interest in differential geometry was rekindled. His last conference presentation and paper were presented when he was nearly 70.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Albert Nijenhuis became a U.S. citizen in 1959. He was married since 1955 and had four children. He died at the age of 88 after several months of failing health.
