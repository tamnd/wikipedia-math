# Vincenzo Mollame

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vincenzo Mollame"}

**Vincenzo Mollame** ([Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples), [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies) 4 July 1848 – [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania), 23 June 1912) was an Italian mathematician.

:::

Mollame was privately tutored by Achille Sanni and then studied Mathematics at the [University of Naples Federico II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II). After obtaining his degree, he became a high-school teacher, first at [Benevento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benevento) and after that at Naples, starting in 1878. He became a professor at the [University of Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Catania) in 1880 and remained there for the rest of his career, having retired in 1911, a few months before his death.[^o2]

His research area was the theory of equations, and he proved in 1890 that when a cubic polynomial with rational coefficients has three real roots but it is irreducible in $\mathbf{Q}[x]$ (the so-called *[casus irreducibilis](wikipedia://en/11153189)*), then the roots cannot be expressed from the coefficients using real radicals alone, that is, complex non-real numbers must be involved if one expresses the roots from the coefficients using radicals,[^o3] probably unaware of the fact that [Pierre Wantzel](wikipedia://en/2610953) had already proved it in 1843. Molleme's research activity stopped in 1896, due to health problems.

Mollame was the author of a textbook on [determinants](wikipedia://en/8468).[^o4]
