# Friedrichs' inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Friedrichs' inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Friedrichs' inequality** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), due to [Kurt Friedrichs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_O._Friedrichs). It places a bound on the [*L<sup>p</sup>* norm](wikipedia://en/45194) of a function using *L<sup>p</sup>* bounds on the [weak derivatives](wikipedia://en/1411087) of the function and the [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of the [domain](wikipedia://en/50263), and can be used to show that certain [norms](wikipedia://en/990534) on [Sobolev spaces](wikipedia://en/611964) are equivalent. Friedrichs's inequality generalizes the [Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré–Wirtinger_inequality), which deals with the case *k* = 1.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Statement of the inequality"}

## Statement of the inequality {#Statement_of_the_inequality}

Let $\Omega$ be a [bounded subset](wikipedia://en/48258) of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb R^n$ with [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) $d$. Suppose that $u:\Omega\to\mathbb R$ lies in the Sobolev space $W_0^{k, p} (\Omega)$, i.e., $u\in W^{k,p}(\Omega)$ and the [trace](wikipedia://en/611964#Traces) of $u$ on the boundary $\partial\Omega$ is zero. Then

$$\| u \|_{L^p (\Omega)} \leq d^k \left( \sum_{| \alpha | = k} \| \mathrm{D}^{\alpha} u \|_{L^p (\Omega)}^p \right)^{1/p}.$$

In the above

- $\| \cdot \|_{L^p (\Omega)}$ denotes the [*L<sup>p</sup>* norm](wikipedia://en/45194);
- *α* = (*α*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *α*<sub>*n*</sub>) is a [multi-index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-index) with norm |*α*| = *α*<sub>1</sub> + ... + *α*<sub>*n*</sub>;
- D<sup>α</sup>*u* is the mixed [partial derivative](wikipedia://en/52565) $$\mathrm{D}^\alpha u = \frac{\partial^{| \alpha |} u}{\partial_{x_1}^{\alpha_1} \cdots \partial_{x_n}^{\alpha_n} }.$$
