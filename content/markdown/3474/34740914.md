# Delaporte distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Delaporte distribution"}

The **Delaporte distribution** is a [discrete probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_probability_distribution) that has received attention in [actuarial science](wikipedia://en/189373).[^o3][^o4] It can be defined using the [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) of a [negative binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/45177) with a [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144).[^o4] Just as the [negative binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/45177) can be viewed as a Poisson distribution where the mean parameter is itself a random variable with a [gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079), the Delaporte distribution can be viewed as a [compound distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_distribution) based on a Poisson distribution, where there are two components to the mean parameter: a fixed component, which has the $\lambda$ parameter, and a gamma-distributed variable component, which has the $\alpha$ and $\beta$ parameters.[^o5] The distribution is named for Pierre Delaporte, who analyzed it in relation to automobile accident claim counts in 1959,[^o6] although it appeared in a different form as early as 1934 in a paper by Rolf von Lüders,[^o7] where it was called the Formel II distribution.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) of the Delaporte distribution is:

$$\frac{\lambda + \alpha\beta(1+3\beta+2\beta^2)}{\left(\lambda + \alpha\beta(1+\beta)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$$

The [excess kurtosis](wikipedia://en/16848) of the distribution is:

$$\frac{\lambda+3\lambda^2+\alpha\beta(1+6\lambda+6\lambda\beta+7\beta+12\beta^2+6\beta^3+3\alpha\beta+6\alpha\beta^2+3\alpha\beta^3)}{\left(\lambda + \alpha\beta(1+\beta)\right)^2}$$
