# Mathematicall Magick

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematicall Magick"}

***Mathematicall Magick. Or, The Wonders that may be Performed by Mechanicall Geometry***[^o3] is a [treatise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatise) by the [English clergyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_ministry), [natural philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy), [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath) and author [John Wilkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wilkins) (1614–1672). It was first published in 1648 in London;[^o4] another edition was printed in 1680[^o5] and further editions were published in 1691 and 1707. The work is dedicated to [Charles I Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_Louis), the [Elector Palatine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elector_Palatine).

:::

The first book describes traditional [mechanical devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_device), [speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed), [siege engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_engines) and the modern [guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guns) of Wilkins' era. The second book covers Wilkins' theories and observations on [land yachts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_sailing), [submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarines), [flying machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_machine), and [perpetual motion](wikipedia://en/54077). Wilkins thought that human [aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation) is feasible, if only sufficient exercise, [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) is directed towards it. He envisioned flying machines that would be large enough to carry several people.

The book repeats tales about early attempts at human flight, including [Busbequius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busbequius)' 16th-century reports about Turkish flight experiments in [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople). Wilkins also mentions [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon) flight experiments during the reign of [Edward the Confessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_the_Confessor). The researcher called "Elmerus" in the text is probably [Eilmer of Malmesbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilmer_of_Malmesbury), who experimented with [gliding flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gliding_flight) in the 11th century. [^o6]

## Abstract {#Abstract}

Wilkins dedicated his work to *His Highness the Prince Elector Palatine* ([Charles I Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_Louis,_Elector_Palatine)) who was in London at the time. It is divided into two books, one headed *Archimedes*, *because he was the chiefest in discovering of Mechanical powers*, the other was called *Daedalus* because he *was one of the first and most famous amongst the Ancients for his skill in making Automata.*[^o8] Wilkins sets out and explains the principles of mechanics in the first book and gives an outlook in the second book on future technical developments like flying which he anticipates as certain if only sufficient exercise, research and development would be directed to these topics. The treatise is an example of his general intention to disseminate scientific knowledge and method and of his attempts to persuade his readers to pursue further scientific studies.[^o9]

## First book {#First_book}

In the 20 chapters of the first book, traditional mechanical devices are discussed such as the [balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighing_scale), the [lever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lever), the [wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheel) or [pulley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulley) and the [block and tackle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_and_tackle), the [wedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedge_%28mechanical_device%29), and the [screw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screw_%28simple_machine%29). The powers acting on them are compared to those acting in the human body. The book deals with the phrase attributed to [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) saying *that if he did but know where to stand and fasten his instrument, he could move the world* and shows the effect of a series of gear transmissions one linked to the other. It shows the importance of various speeds and the theoretical possibility to increase speed beyond the speed of the Earth at the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator). Finally, [siege engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_engines) like [catapults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catapult) are compared with the cost and effect of then-modern guns.

## Second book {#Second_book}

### Various devices {#Various_devices}

In the 15 chapters of the second book, various devices are examined which move independently of human interference like clocks and watches, [water mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_mills) and [wind mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_mills). Wilkins explains devices being driven by the motion of air in a [chimney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimney) or by pressurized air. A [land yacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_sailing) is proposed driven by two sails on two masts, and a wagon powered by a [vertical axis wind turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_axis_wind_turbine). A number of independently moving small artificial figures representing men and animals are described. The possibilities are considered to improve the type of [submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine) designed and built by [Cornelis Drebbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelis_Drebbel). The tales about various flying devices are related and doubts as to their truth are dissipated. Wilkins explains that it should be possible for a man, too, to fly by himself[^o13] if a frame were built where the person could sit and if this frame was sufficiently pushed in the air.

### Art of flying {#Art_of_flying}

In chapter VII, Wilkins discusses various methods how a man could fly, namely by the help of spirits and good or evil angels (as related on various occasions in the Bible), by the help of fowls, by wings fastened immediately to the body or by a flying chariot. The whole of this chapter (and of the following one) concern the possibilities of flying. In a single preliminary phrase, he refers to previous reports of flight attempts:

Tis related of a certain English Monk called Elmerus \[probably [Eilmer of Malmesbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilmer_of_Malmesbury)\], about the [Confessors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_the_Confessor) time, that he did by such wings fly from a Tower above a furlong; and so another \[probably [Fausto Veranzio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fausto_Veranzio)\] from Saint Marks steeple in Venice; another at [Norinberge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurenberg); and [Busbequius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busbequius) speaks of a Turk in [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), who attempted something this way. Mt. Burton mentioning this quotation, doth believe that some new-fangled wit ('tis his Cynical phrase) will some time or other find out this art. Though the truth is, most of these Artists did unfortunately miscarry by falling down and breaking their arms or legs, yet that may be imputed to their want of experience ...

He writes that sufficient practise should enable a man to fly, most probably by "a flying chariot, which may be so contrived as to carry a man within it"[^o15] and equipped with a sort of engine, or else big enough to carry several people, each successively working to fly it. He used the next chapter to dissipate any doubts there may be as to the possibility of such a flying chariot, should a number of particular items be developed and tested.

### Perpetual motion and perpetual lamps {#Perpetual_motion_and_perpetual_lamps}

In Chapters IX to XV, extensive discussions and deliberations are set out why a [perpetual motion](wikipedia://en/54077) should be feasible, why the stories about lamps burning for hundreds of years were true and how such lamps could be made and perpetual motions created.
