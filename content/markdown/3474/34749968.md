# Charles Wells (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Wells"}

**Charles Frederick Wells** (4 May 1937 in [Atlanta, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta,_Georgia) – 17 June 2017)[^o2][^o3] was an American mathematician known for his fundamental contributions to [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869). He was Professor Emeritus of Mathematics at [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University).[^o4]

:::

Wells taught there for about 35 years, with sabbatical interruptions at [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) (in mathematics) and [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Computing_Laboratory) (in computing science). He had a research career in mathematics in [finite fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_fields), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869). In the last twenty years of this life he had also been interested in the [language of mathematics](https://abstractmath.org/MM/MMLangMath.htm) and related issues concerning teaching and communicating abstract ideas.

## Publications {#Publications}

In addition to his scholarly publications, Wells produced *A Handbook of Mathematical Discourse,*[^o6][^o7] which is a dictionary of words and concepts used by mathematicians that are easily misunderstood, explained in a way that laypersons can also appreciate.

As a life-long [shape note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_note) singer, in 2002 Wells jointly compiled a tunebook called *Oberlin Harmony,*[^o8] which included some of his own compositions.
