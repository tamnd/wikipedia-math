# Chuck Waseleski

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chuck Waseleski"}

**Charles** "**Chuck**" **Waseleski** (November 30, 1954 – April 7, 2016)[^o3] was an American pioneering [sabermetrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabermetrician) from Massachusetts.

:::

Waseleski was called "the czar of hardball software" by *[Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Globe)* sportswriter [Dan Shaughnessy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shaughnessy),[^o4] but was most famously known in Boston as "The Maniacal One", a sobriquet often seen on the sports pages of the *Globe*. The nickname, coined by [Steve Fainaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Fainaru) and continued in use by [Gordon Edes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Edes), [Peter Gammons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gammons), and [Nick Cafardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Cafardo), honored Waseleski's extreme attention to detail.[^o6][^o4]

Waseleski, more a statistics compiler than an analyst, kept track for many years of every pitch and every ball in play of every [Boston Red Sox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox) game,[^o4] during the early days of sabermetrics when this data was not routinely compiled.[^o6]

Waseleski did not practice sabermetrics as a full-time profession (he worked for an engineering consulting firm), although he did publish monthly and seasonal reports for a while and was employed by [sports agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_agent),[^o4][^o6] and excerpts of his work appeared in works by [Bill James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_James)[^o5] and in *Globe* [newspaper columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper_column)[^o6] beginning in the 1980s.[^o7]
