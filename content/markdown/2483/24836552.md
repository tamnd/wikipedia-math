# Mean dependence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mean dependence"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) $Y$ is said to be **mean independent** of random variable $X$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its [conditional mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_mean) $E(Y \mid X = x)$ equals its (unconditional) [mean](wikipedia://en/9653) $E(Y)$ for all $x$ such that the probability density/mass of $X$ at $x$, $f_X(x)$, is not zero. Otherwise, $Y$ is said to be **mean dependent** on $X$.

:::

[Stochastic independence](wikipedia://en/27593) implies mean independence, but the converse is not true.;[^o2][^o3] moreover, mean independence implies uncorrelatedness while the converse is not true. Unlike stochastic independence and uncorrelatedness, mean independence is not symmetric: it is possible for $Y$ to be mean-independent of $X$ even though $X$ is mean-dependent on $Y$.

The concept of mean independence is often used in [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390)[^o3] to have a middle ground between the strong assumption of independent random variables ($X_1 \perp X_2$) and the weak assumption of uncorrelated random variables $(\operatorname{Cov}(X_1, X_2) = 0).$
