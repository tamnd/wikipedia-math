# Conway knot

*Image: Gateknot.jpg.* Conway knot emblem on a closed gate at [Isaac Newton Institute](wikipedia://en/736837)

*Image: Conway-knot.png.* Conway knot

*Image: Starred Conway Knot.png.* A five star version of the Conway knot

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Conway knot"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **Conway knot** (or **Conway's knot**) is a particular [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) with 11 crossings, named after [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807).[^o5]

:::

It is related by [mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation_%28knot_theory%29) to the [Kinoshita–Terasaka knot](wikipedia://en/42563626),[^o6] with which it shares the same [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642).[^o7][^o8] Both knots also have the property of having the same [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) and [Conway polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647#Alexander–Conway_polynomial) as the [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075).[^o9]

The issue of the [sliceness](wikipedia://en/4158126) of the Conway knot was resolved in 2020 by [Lisa Piccirillo](wikipedia://en/64011543), 50 years after Conway first proposed the knot.[^o9][^o10][^o11] Her proof made use of [Rasmussen's s-invariant](wikipedia://en/2639906#Applications), and showed that the knot is not a smoothly slice knot, though it is topologically slice (the Kinoshita–Terasaka knot is both).[^o12]
