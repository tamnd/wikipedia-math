# A. O. L. Atkin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="A. O. L. Atkin"}

**Arthur Oliver Lonsdale Atkin** (31 July 1925 – 28 December 2008), who published under the name **A. O. L. Atkin**, was a British mathematician.

:::

After matriculating from Cambridge University at the age of 16, Atkin worked at Bletchley Park [cracking](wikipedia://en/18934432) [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) codes during World War 2.[^o3] He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1952 from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), where he was one of [John Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371)'s research students. In 1952 he moved to [Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University) as a lecturer in mathematics.[^o5] During 1964–1970, he worked at the [Atlas Computer Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Computer_Laboratory) at Chilton, computing modular functions. Toward the end of his life, he was [Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus) of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago).

Atkin, along with [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999), extended [Schoof's algorithm](wikipedia://en/3596006) to create the [Schoof–Elkies–Atkin algorithm](wikipedia://en/3717018). Together with [Daniel J. Bernstein](wikipedia://en/198983), he developed the [sieve of Atkin](wikipedia://en/2150920).

Atkin is also known for his work on properties of the [integer partition function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_partition_function) and the [monster module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_module). He was a vocal fan of [using computers in mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833), so long as the end goal was theoretical advance: "Each new generation of machines makes feasible a whole new range of computations; provided mathematicians pursue these rather than merely break old records for old sports, computation will have a significant part to play in the development of mathematics."[^o6]

Atkin died of [nosocomial pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital-acquired_pneumonia) on 28 December 2008, in [Maywood, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maywood,_Illinois).[^o7]
