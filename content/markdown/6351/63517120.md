# Pietro Cossali

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Pietro Cossali"}

**Pietro Cossali** (29 June 1748 — 20 December 1815) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer).

:::

*Image: Cossali - Controversia analitica tra il signor cavalier Lorgna ed il signor giornalista di Pisa decisa col fatto aggiunto l'esame logico d'un logico principio , 1786 - 1261322.jpg.* *Controversia analitica*, 1786

From 1787 to 1805, he taught physics at the [University of Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Parma). In 1805, [Napoleon](wikipedia://en/69880) named Cossali a professor of higher calculus at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua).[^o3]

From 1797 to 1799, he wrote *Origin, Transmission to Italy, and Early Progress of Algebra There* (Origine, transporto in Italia, primi progressi in essa dell'algebra), in which he describes mathematical achievements from the emergence of algebra due to [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949) to the new research on *[casus irreducibilis](wikipedia://en/11153189)* in the 18th century. This work can be considered the first professional text on the history of Italian mathematics. In this work, Cossali corrects some factual mistakes made earlier by [Jean Paul de Gua de Malves](wikipedia://en/2680338), [John Wallis](wikipedia://en/239290) and [Jean-Étienne Montucla](wikipedia://en/2157781), although he makes another important error in attributing everything after Fibonacci and before [Luca Pacioli](wikipedia://en/18473) to the latter.[^o3]

Besides his works on mathematics and its history, Cossali also wrote on astronomy. His articles were published in *Ephémérides astronomiques.*[^o4]
