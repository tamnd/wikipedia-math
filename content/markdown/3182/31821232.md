# E9 honeycomb

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="E9 honeycomb"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **E<sub>9</sub> honeycomb** is a tessellation of uniform polytopes in hyperbolic 9-dimensional space. ${\bar{T}}_9$, also (E<sub>10</sub>) is a paracompact hyperbolic group, so either [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet) or [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719) will not be bounded.

:::

[E<sub>10</sub>](wikipedia://en/5351858) is last of the series of [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004) with a bifurcated [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) of lengths 6,2,1. There are 1023 unique E<sub>10</sub> honeycombs by all combinations of its [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram). There are no regular honeycombs in the family since its Coxeter diagram is a nonlinear graph, but there are three simplest ones, with a single ring at the end of its 3 branches: 6<sub>21</sub>, 2<sub>61</sub>, 1<sub>62</sub>.

The **6<sub>21</sub> honeycomb** is constructed from alternating [9-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-simplex) and [9-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-orthoplex) facets within the symmetry of the E<sub>10</sub> Coxeter group.

This honeycomb is highly regular in the sense that its symmetry group (the affine E<sub>9</sub> Weyl group) acts transitively on the [*k*-faces](wikipedia://en/149926) for *k* ≤ 7. All of the *k*-faces for *k* ≤ 8 are simplices.

This honeycomb is last in the series of [*k*<sub>21</sub> polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiregular_k_21_polytope), enumerated by [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) in 1900, listing polytopes and honeycombs constructed entirely of regular facets, although his list ended with the 8-dimensional the Euclidean honeycomb, 5<sub>21</sub>.[^o9]

::: {#o11 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

It is created by a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) upon a set of 10 [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) mirrors in 9-dimensional hyperbolic space.

The facet information can be extracted from its [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram).

Removing the node on the end of the 2-length branch leaves the [9-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-orthoplex), 6<sub>11</sub>.

Removing the node on the end of the 1-length branch leaves the [9-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-simplex).

The [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is determined by removing the ringed node and ringing the neighboring node. This makes the [5<sub>21</sub> honeycomb](wikipedia://en/26842937).

The [edge figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_figure) is determined from the vertex figure by removing the ringed node and ringing the neighboring node. This makes the [4<sub>21</sub> polytope](wikipedia://en/19218705).

The *face figure* is determined from the edge figure by removing the ringed node and ringing the neighboring node. This makes the [3<sub>21</sub> polytope](wikipedia://en/19236353).

The *cell figure* is determined from the face figure by removing the ringed node and ringing the neighboring node. This makes the [2<sub>21</sub> polytope](wikipedia://en/19239704).

:::

### Related polytopes and honeycombs {#Related_polytopes_and_honeycombs}

The 6<sub>21</sub> is last in a dimensional series of [semiregular polytopes](wikipedia://en/11684875) and honeycombs, identified in 1900 by [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset). Each [member of the sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_k21_polytope) has the previous member as its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719). All facets of these polytopes are [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693), namely [simplexes](wikipedia://en/39781) and [orthoplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex).

The **2<sub>61</sub>** honeycomb is composed of [2<sub>51</sub> 9-honeycomb](wikipedia://en/19550444) and [9-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-simplex) [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647). It is the final figure in the [2<sub>*k*1</sub> family](wikipedia://en/18621102).

::: {#o21 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

It is created by a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) upon a set of 10 [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) mirrors in 9-dimensional hyperbolic space.

The facet information can be extracted from its [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram).

Removing the node on the short branch leaves the [9-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-simplex).

Removing the node on the end of the 6-length branch leaves the [2<sub>51</sub> honeycomb](wikipedia://en/19550444). This is an infinite facet because E10 is a paracompact hyperbolic group.

The [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is determined by removing the ringed node and ringing the neighboring node. This makes the [9-demicube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-demicube), 1<sub>61</sub>.

The *edge figure* is the vertex figure of the edge figure. This makes the [rectified 8-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectified_8-simplex), 0<sub>51</sub>.

The *face figure* is determined from the edge figure by removing the ringed node and ringing the neighboring node. This makes the [5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex) prism.

:::

### Construction {#Construction}

It is created by a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) upon a set of 10 [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) mirrors in 9-dimensional space.
