# Chiral algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chiral algebra"}

In mathematics, a **chiral algebra** is an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) introduced by Beilinson and Drinfeld (2004)[^o2] as a rigorous version of the rather vague concept of a chiral algebra in physics. In Chiral Algebras, [Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613) and [Drinfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinfeld) introduced the notion of chiral algebra, which based on the pseudo-tensor category of [D-modules](wikipedia://en/3440755). They give a 'coordinate independent' notion of [vertex algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_algebra), which are based on [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012). Chiral algebras on curves are essentially [conformal vertex algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_algebra#vertex_operator_algebra).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **chiral algebra**[^o5] on a [smooth](wikipedia://en/1408000) [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) $X$ is a [right](wikipedia://en/12781#Right_group_action) D-module $\mathcal{A}$, equipped with a D-module [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564)

$$\mu : \mathcal{A} \boxtimes \mathcal{A}(\infty \Delta) \rightarrow \Delta_! \mathcal{A}$$

on $X^2$ and with an [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) $\Omega \hookrightarrow \mathcal{A}$, satisfying the following conditions

- $\mu = -\sigma_{12} \circ \mu \circ \sigma_{12}$ ([Skew-symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skew-symmetry))
- $\mu_{1\{23\}} = \mu_{\{12\}3} + \mu_{2\{13\}}$ ([Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370))
- The unit map is compatible with the homomorphism $\mu_\Omega: \Omega \boxtimes \Omega (\infty \Delta) \rightarrow \Delta_!\Omega$; that is, the following diagram commutes

$$\begin{array}{lcl} & \Omega \boxtimes \mathcal{A}(\infty\Delta) & \rightarrow & \mathcal{A} \boxtimes \mathcal{A}(\infty \Delta) & \\ & \downarrow && \downarrow \\ & \Delta_!\mathcal A & \rightarrow & \Delta_! \mathcal A & \\ \end{array}$$

Where, for [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) $\mathcal{M}, \mathcal{N}$ on $X$, the sheaf $\mathcal{M}\boxtimes\mathcal{N}(\infty \Delta)$ is the sheaf on $X^2$ whose sections are sections of the external tensor product $\mathcal{M}\boxtimes\mathcal{N}$ with arbitrary poles on the diagonal:

$$\mathcal M \boxtimes \mathcal N (\infty \Delta) = \varinjlim \mathcal{M} \boxtimes \mathcal{N} (n \Delta),$$

$\Omega$ is the [canonical bundle](wikipedia://en/734893), and the 'diagonal extension by delta-functions' $\Delta_!$ is

$$\Delta_!\mathcal{M} = \frac{\Omega \boxtimes \mathcal{M}(\infty \Delta)}{\Omega \boxtimes \mathcal{M}}.$$

:::

## Relation to other algebras {#Relation_to_other_algebras}

### Vertex algebra {#Vertex_algebra}

The [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of vertex algebras as defined by [Borcherds](wikipedia://en/292169) or [Kac](wikipedia://en/4697697) is equivalent to the category of chiral algebras on $X = \mathbb{A}^1$ equivariant with respect to the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $T$ of [translations](wikipedia://en/204682).

### Factorization algebra {#Factorization_algebra}

Chiral algebras can also be reformulated as [factorization algebras](wikipedia://en/73098277).
