# Euclidean relation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Euclidean relation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Euclidean relations** are a class of [binary relations](wikipedia://en/3931) that formalize "Axiom 1" in [Euclid's *Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107): "Magnitudes which are equal to the same are equal to each other."

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Euclidean.PNG.* Right Euclidean property: solid and dashed arrows indicate antecedents and consequents, respectively.

A [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) *R* on a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) *X* is **Euclidean** (sometimes called **right Euclidean**) if it satisfies the following: for every *a*, *b*, *c* in *X*, if *a* is related to *b* and *c*, then *b* is related to *c*.[^o4] To write this in [predicate logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_logic):

$$\forall a, b, c\in X\,(a\,R\, b \land a \,R\, c \to b \,R\, c).$$

Dually, a relation *R* on *X* is **left Euclidean** if for every *a*, *b*, *c* in *X*, if *b* is related to *a* and *c* is related to *a*, then *b* is related to *c*:

$$\forall a, b, c\in X\,(b\,R\, a \land c \,R\, a \to b \,R\, c).$$

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Right Euclidean relation scheme svg.svg.* Schematized right Euclidean relation according to property 10. Deeply-colored squares indicate the equivalence classes of *R*. Pale-colored rectangles indicate possible relationships of elements in *X*\\ran(*R*). In these rectangles, relationships may, or may not, hold.

1. Due to the commutativity of ∧ in the definition's antecedent, *aRb* ∧ *aRc* even implies *bRc* ∧ *cRb* when *R* is right Euclidean. Similarly, *bRa* ∧ *cRa* implies *bRc* ∧ *cRb* when *R* is left Euclidean.
1. The property of being Euclidean is different from [transitivity](wikipedia://en/200463). For example, ≤ is transitive, but not right Euclidean,[^o7] while *xRy* defined by 0 ≤ *x* ≤ *y* + 1 ≤ 2 is not transitive,[^o8] but right Euclidean on [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474).
1. For [symmetric relations](wikipedia://en/200459), transitivity, right Euclideanness, and left Euclideanness all coincide. However, a non-symmetric relation can also be both transitive and right Euclidean, for example, *xRy* defined by *y*=0.
1. A relation that is both right Euclidean and [reflexive](wikipedia://en/200458) is also symmetric and therefore an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259).[^o4][^o9] Similarly, each left Euclidean and reflexive relation is an equivalence.
1. The [range](wikipedia://en/579311#Generalization_to_binary_relations) of a right Euclidean relation is always a subset[^o10] of its [domain](wikipedia://en/579311#Generalization_to_binary_relations). The [restriction](wikipedia://en/3931#Restriction) of a right Euclidean relation to its range is always reflexive,[^o11] and therefore an equivalence. Similarly, the domain of a left Euclidean relation is a subset of its range, and the restriction of a left Euclidean relation to its domain is an equivalence. Therefore, a right Euclidean relation on *X* that is also [right total](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_total) (respectively a left Euclidean relation on *X* that is also [left total](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_total)) is an equivalence, since its range (respectively its domain) is *X*.[^o12]
1. A relation *R* is both left and right Euclidean, if, and only if, the domain and the range set of *R* agree, and *R* is an equivalence relation on that set.[^o13]
1. A right Euclidean relation is always [quasitransitive](wikipedia://en/6770393),[^o14] as is a left Euclidean relation.[^o15]
1. A [connected](wikipedia://en/57495962) right Euclidean relation is always transitive;[^o16] and so is a connected left Euclidean relation.[^o17]
1. If *X* has at least 3 elements, a connected right Euclidean relation *R* on *X* cannot be [antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/1176),[^o18] and neither can a connected left Euclidean relation on *X*.[^o19] On the 2-element set *X* = { 0, 1 }, e.g. the relation *xRy* defined by *y*=1 is connected, right Euclidean, and antisymmetric, and *xRy* defined by *x*=1 is connected, left Euclidean, and antisymmetric.
1. A relation *R* on a set *X* is right Euclidean if, and only if, the restriction *R* := *R*|<sub>ran(*R*)</sub> is an equivalence and for each *x* in *X*\\ran(*R*), all elements to which *x* is related under *R* are equivalent under *R*.[^o20] Similarly, *R* on *X* is left Euclidean if, and only if, *R* := *R*|<sub>dom(*R*)</sub> is an equivalence and for each *x* in *X*\\dom(*R*), all elements that are related to *x* under *R* are equivalent under *R*.
1. A left Euclidean relation is [left-unique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-unique_relation) if, and only if, it is [antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/1176). Similarly, a right Euclidean relation is right unique if, and only if, it is anti-symmetric.
1. A left Euclidean and left unique relation is vacuously transitive, and so is a right Euclidean and right unique relation.
1. A left Euclidean relation is left [quasi-reflexive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-reflexive_relation). For left-unique relations, the converse also holds. Dually, each right Euclidean relation is right quasi-reflexive, and each right unique and right quasi-reflexive relation is right Euclidean.[^o21]
1. A binary relation is left Euclidean if and only if its converse is right Euclidean.
