# Binyamin Amirà

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Binyamin Amirà"}

**Binyamin A. Amirà** (בנימין אמירה; 3 June 1896 – 20 January 1968) was an Israeli [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in 1896 in [Mohilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohyliv-Podilskyi), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), Binyamin Amirà immigrated with his family to [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv) in [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_%28region%29) in 1910, where he attended the [Herzliya Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzliya_Hebrew_Gymnasium). Amirà went on to study mathematics at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva), after which he moved to the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in 1921 to undertake research for his doctorate under the supervision of [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230).

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

After completing his [D.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) in 1924, Amirà spent a brief period at the University of Geneva as [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent), after which he followed Landau in 1925 to help him in establishing the [Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/59217494) of the newly-founded [Hebrew University in Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_in_Jerusalem).[^o7] There, he became the institute's first tenured staff member.[^o8]

Amirà founded the [Journal d'Analyse Mathématique](wikipedia://en/71721837) in 1951, which he edited alongside [Ze'ev Nehari](wikipedia://en/25242678) and [Menahem Schiffer](wikipedia://en/48545257).[^o9][^o10] He retired in 1960.[^o11]
