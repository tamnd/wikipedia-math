# Frank E. Grubbs

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frank E. Grubbs"}

**Frank Ephraim Grubbs** (September 2, 1913 – January 19, 2000) was an American statistician. [Grubbs's test for outliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grubbs's_test_for_outliers), and the Mann-Grubbs method for calculating a binomial series lower confidence bound, are named after him.

:::

He worked at the [Ballistic Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_Research_Laboratory) while he was a Captain in the [U.S. Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army).

He retired in 1975 and died on January 19, 2000. He is buried at the Five Points Belcher Cemetery in Alabama.

## Education {#Education}

He received his bachelor's degree from the [Alabama Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama_Polytechnic_Institute). He earned his Ph.D. in Statistics from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1949.[^o4] He studied under Cecil C. Craig and his dissertation research was on the detection of [outliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outliers).[^o5]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

For his contributions to statistics, he was awarded the [Wilks Memorial Award](wikipedia://en/15966180) by the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 1964. In 1969, he was the recipient of the first Jack Youden Prize and [Frank Wilcoxon Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wilcoxon_Prize) for the best expository paper in [Technometrics](wikipedia://en/5854084). In 1971, he was awarded the [Shewhart Medal](wikipedia://en/831964) by the [American Society for Quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Quality).
