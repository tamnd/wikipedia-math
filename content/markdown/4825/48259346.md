# Emanuele Foà

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Emanuele Foà"}

**Emanuele Foà** (16 August 1892[^o2] – 9 October 1949) was an Italian [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer) and [engineering physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_physics), known for his contribution to [mathematical fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034). In particular he proved the first known uniqueness theorem for the solutions to the [three-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28mathematics%29) [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395) for incompressible fluids in [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258) [domains](wikipedia://en/16796643).[^o5]

:::

## Life and academic career {#Life_and_academic_career}

He was born in Savigliano, in a Jewish family of distinguished professionals and officials:[^o7] his father, Teodoro Foà, was a [military physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_physician) serving as a [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_%28rank%29) the [Royal Italian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Italian_Army),[^o8] who died at the age of 42 due to the [viral fevers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_fever) he contracted during the [Eritrea war campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Eritrea).[^o7] Despite having lost his father at a young age and having a disabled sister,[^o9] he succeeded in studying engineering at the [Polytechnic University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_University_of_Turin) thanks to a scholarship.[^o10] The outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) in Italy in 1915 forced him to interrupt his engineering studies: he joined the army and served as an [artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery) [officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_%28armed_forces%29) for the years 1916 and 1917. On 28 October 1917, during the [battle of Caporetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Caporetto), he was taken prisoner and spent a year in a [prisoner camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner-of-war_camp) located in Germany.[^o8][^o11]

At the end of the war, notwithstanding his health problems, he successfully completed his university studies: he got his [Laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea) degree in [industrial engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_engineering) at the Polytechnic University of Turin in August 1919.[^o12] From 1 December 1919 he started to work at his [alma mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_mater),[^o13] as [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) to the chair of thermal engineering,[^o14] which at the time was held by Benedetto Luigi Montel.[^o3] In 1927 he participated and won a competitive examination for a professorship in engineering physics at the then called "Royal School of Engineering of Bologna":[^o15] in 1928 he left Turin for Bologna,[^o16] succeeding, after a brief time period, to [Luigi Donati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Donati) who had held the chair for several decades.[^o17] The very same year he met Dario Graffi, who had earlier become assistant professor to the chair of engineering physics:[^o18] their cordial relations became over time a deep and tenacious friendship, lasted until Foà's death.[^o19]

In Bologna, he passionately devoted himself to teaching as his course handouts, published in several editions, testify:[^o20] the same time was fruitful for his researches activity,[^o21] and in 1930 he was appointed [ordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_professor).[^o9]

::: {#o23 .statement cue=named-heading name="The years from 1938 to 1945: the \"Italian Racial Laws\" and World War II"}

### The years from 1938 to 1945: the "Italian Racial Laws" and World War II {#The_years_from_1938_to_1945:_the_"Italian_Racial_Laws"_and_World_War_II}

His teaching at the university was interrupted in 1938, the year the Italian Government approved the "[Racial Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Racial_Laws)", "unreasonable, before being unjust".[^o24] Forced by the law to an early [retirement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retirement), the [Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council) of the faculty of engineering substituted him with Graffi: he was very happy with the council choice,[^o9] due to their friendship and mutual esteem.[^o25] For his part, Graffi, who could not adopt Foà's handouts due to prohibition imposed by the laws on publications by Jewish authors, published them under his name:[^o9] cautiously, he kept sending to Foà's house students for private lessons, in order to help him supplement his small retirement pension.[^o26]

During the [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) period, Foà and his wife managed to stay in Bologna but had to change their accommodation frequently, being hosted by friendly families.[^o27] In October 1943, being warned by Dino Zanobetti about a raid of the police, he and his wife left their house and went to an apartment made available by Dante Piccioli, a wealthy engineer and friend of theirs.[^o28] More than a month later, on 7 December, Bologna was bombed and the apartment where Foà and his wife resided was destroyed:[^o29] being at home,[^o30] Foà was severely wounded in the right leg and was brought to the [Sant'Orsola Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Policlinico_Sant'Orsola-Malpighi).[^o31]

:::

### Honours {#Honours}

In 1933, he was elected [corresponding member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corresponding_member) of the [Accademia delle Scienze dell'Istituto di Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_delle_Scienze_dell'Istituto_di_Bologna)[^o33] and, after being reintegrated in his role of professor at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) in 1945,[^o34] he became ordinary member in 1947.[^o35] Also in 1947, jointly with some fellow engineers, he founded the Bologna Section of the Associazione Termotecnica Italiana,[^o34][^o36] he was elected as his first president.[^o34]
