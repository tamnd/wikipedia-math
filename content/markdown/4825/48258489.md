# Frank Merle (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frank Merle"}

**Frank Merle** (born 22 November 1962) is a French mathematician, specializing in [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) and mathematical physics.

:::

## Early life, education and career {#Early_life,_education_and_career}

Frank Merle was born on 22 November 1962 in [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille). After graduation from the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure) (ENS), Merle received in 1987 his Ph.D. from the [University of Paris VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_VI) under [Henri Berestycki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Berestycki) with thesis *Contributions a l'etude de certaines equations aux derivees partielles non lineaires de la physique mathematique*.[^o4] He became a researcher for [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS) at ENS. In 1989/90 he was an assistant professor at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) at NYU. Since 1991 Merle has been a professor at the [University of Cergy-Pontoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cergy-Pontoise). From 1998 to 2003 he was a member of the [Institut Universitaire de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Universitaire_de_France). In the fall of 1996, the fall of 2001, and the academic year 2003–2004 he was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o6] He was a visiting professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) (MSRI) at Berkeley, [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University), and the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo). Frank Merle holds the Analysis Chair at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) in Bures-sur-Yvette [^o7].

## Mathematical research {#Mathematical_research}

Merle does research on [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) (PDEs) and mathematical physics, notably dispersive nonlinear PDEs such as the [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748) and the [Korteweg–De Vries equation](wikipedia://en/344116), and the study of such PDE solutions which over time break down or diverge (blow up). Such research earned him in 2005 the [Bôcher Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bôcher_Prize) which he earned again in 2023.

Merle received the 2026 [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933)[^o9] for significantly advancing the modern understanding of nonlinear evolution equations.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Frank Merle is married to the artist [Rebecca Dolinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Dolinsky) (born 26 May 1962) and has two sons, computer scientist Jascha Theophile Dolinsky Merle (born 23 May 2002) and Maxim Salomon Dolinsky Merle (born 13 June 2007).

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

- 1997: Prix de l'Institut Poincaré en Physique théorique
- 1998: [Invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in Berlin; lecture entitled *Blow-up phenomena for critical nonlinear Schrödinger and Zakharov equations*[^o16]
- 2000: Prix Charles-Louis de Saulse de Freycinet de l'Académie des Sciences de Paris
- 2005: Médaille d'argent du CNRS
- 2005: [Bôcher Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/1099872)[^o17]
- 2014: [Plenary speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in Seoul; lecture entitled *Asymptotics for critical nonlinear dispersive equations*
- 2018: [Prix Ampère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Ampère) awarded by [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences)
- 2023: [Bôcher Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/1099872),[^o18] jointly to [Merle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Merle), Raphaël, [Rodnianski](wikipedia://en/33944172), and Szeftel
- 2023: [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167)[^o19]
