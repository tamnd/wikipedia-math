# François Noël (missionary)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="François Noël"}

**François Noël**, [SJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) (18 August 1651 – 17 September 1729) was a [Flemish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_people) [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits), [poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet), [dramatist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dramatist), and [missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit_China_missions) to the [Qing Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_Empire). Nöel unsuccessfully testified in support of Chinese converts to [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) retaining [ancestral veneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_veneration) during the [Chinese Rites controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Rites_controversy) but also opposed incorporating other elements of [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism) into Catholic practice. He also achieved notability for translating several Chinese texts for European audiences.

:::

## Name {#Name}

Noël wrote his translations in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), in which his name [appears as](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinization_of_names) **Franciscus Noel**. He is also known by its [anglicization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicization) as **Francis Noel**. He was known to the Chinese as **Wei Fangji**.[^n1]

## Life {#Life}

### Early life {#Early_life}

François Noël was a [Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_people)[^o78] born on 18 August 1651 in [Hestrud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hestrud), [Hainault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Hainault), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France).[^n1] He joined the [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) at the age of nineteen on 30 September 1670 in [Tournai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournai),[^n1] which had just been returned to France from the [Spanish Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Netherlands) two years before under the terms of the [War of Devolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Devolution)'s [Treaty of Aachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Aachen_%281668%29). He was a teacher of [grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_grammar) and [rhetoric](wikipedia://en/25447) for several years.[^o8] He studied [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_theology), [mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220#Mathematics_during_the_Scientific_Revolution), and [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_astronomy#Renaissance_Period) at the [University of Douai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Douai).[^n1]

### In China {#In_China}

*Image: The tomb of Xuguangqi.jpg.* The [tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_tomb) of [Xu Guangqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Guangqi) (d. 1633) in [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai)'s [Xujiahui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xujiahui) neighborhood

*Image: Paradigma XV Provinciarum &c.png.* China as known to the Jesuits 1687.

*Image: Carte la plus generale et qui comprend la Chine, la Tartarie Chinoise, et le Thibet (1734).jpg.* China as known to the Jesuits 1735.

He wanted to join the [Japan mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit_Japan_mission) but by this point [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Japan) (and European visitors generally) had been banned within the [Tokugawa shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate) for many years.[^n1] He nonetheless traveled to [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon), [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal), and left for [East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia) in January 1684. His journey was funded by [Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_de_Guadalupe_of_Lencastre,_6th_Duchess_of_Aveiro), the [duchess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Aveiro) of [Aveiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aveiro,_Portugal).[^n1] He hoped to get passage to Japan on a [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) [mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_missions_to_Edo), but in [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Malacca) he was assured by "Belgian Catholics from our cities" that this was impossible.[^o8]

He debarked at [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Macau) on 9 August 1685, where the return of some shipwrecked Japanese sailors caused him to hope that trade would be resumed; this was fruitless.[^o13] He made his [vows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Solemn_Vows) of [poverty, chastity, obedience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty,_chastity,_obedience), and the [fourth vow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_vow) of "special obedience" to the [pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope) on 2 February the next year[^n1] and, after a final failed attempt to reach Japan,[^o19] finally formally joined the [China mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit_China_missions)[^n1] by September 1687.[^o19] He is sometimes numbered among the [Figurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figurists),[^n2] the Jesuit missionaries who came to think that Christianity had been the ancient religion of China, brought there by [Noah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah)'s son [Shem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shem).

Noël learned rudimentary [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) on Macau and traveled to the mainland in 1687. He traveled to [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai),[^n1] then part of [Jiangsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangsu) and—at nearby [Xujiahui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xujiahui)—the home of the family of the influential convert [Xu Guangqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Guangqi). After further training, he began his mission on nearby [Chongming Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongming_Island) in early October 1688 and reported great success by August 1689:[^n1] 120 [baptized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism) converts in Shanghai, 300 on Chongming, and 800 in regions dependent on Chongming.[^o19]

From there, he travelled to [Huai'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huai'an) and [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) in [Jiangsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangsu); [Wuhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhe_County) in [Anhui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhui); [Nanchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang), [Ganzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzhou), [Jianchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou,_Jiangxi) (now Fuzhou), and [Nanfeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanfeng) in [Jiangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangxi); and [Nan'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nan'an,_Fujian) in [Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian).[^n1]

A 1703 report to the Jesuit Provincial shows that Noël's work was primarily among the lower and working classes, especially to women and [abandoned children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orphan), which left open the problem of how to pay for church construction and mission work without resorting to begging for alms in the manner of the [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_China) [monks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonzes).[^o21]

### First Roman embassy {#First_Roman_embassy}

*Image: Mandatum seu Edictum, Charles Maigrot.jpg.* [Charles Maigrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Maigrot)'s 1693 *Mandate*, which reopened the [Chinese Rites controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Rites_controversy)

*Image: P. Caspar Castner S.J.jpg.* [Caspar Castner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspar_Castner), Noël's companion on his 1st embassy to Rome

On 9 November 1701,[^o23] he was selected—probably through the influence of his compatriot, the Vice Provincial Superior [Antoine Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Thomas)[^n1]—to act as the [procurator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procurator_%28Catholic_Church%29) for the China mission in an embassy concerning the [Chinese Rites Controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Rites_Controversy).[^n1] He was to argue on behalf of the Jesuits and four Chinese bishops that the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) should continue to permit the Chinese practice with [Confucian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius) and [ancestral veneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_veneration) after their [conversion to Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_conversion).[^n1]

Thomas's letter reached him in Nanchang on the 25th; he left on 6 December and reached [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou) on 1 January.[^o23] He was originally to travel with José Ramón Arxó and [Claude de Visdelou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_de_Visdelou), but Arxó suffered accidental delays. Visdelou, meanwhile, was delayed first owing to reticence by the French mission to allow him to leave and then under various pretexts because the [visitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_visitation) Carlo Turcotti (correctly) suspected his position on the question of the rites.[^o23] Since there was already an [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) ship ready to sail, Turcotti replaced the pair[^o27] with the [Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria) mathematician[^n3] [Caspar Castner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspar_Castner), who was already working nearby.[^o29]

The English ship departed on 14 January 1702 for Macau, which it reached on the 21st and left on the 24th.[^o29] It traveled to [Batavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Dutch_East_Indies) in the [Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies) (now [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta), [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia)), where unbalanced cargo and heavy weather required a 17-day delay.[^o29] On the journey around the [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope), it was again held up for almost a month because of unfavorable winds.[^o29] After a 43 day wait on [Saint Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Helena) owing to fear of a new European war, the ship passed through two storms to the [Azores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores) and through a collision off [Calais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calais) to [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London), which it reached on 4 October.[^o29] The Jesuits met and presumably lobbied various ambassadors while in London, as well as the directors of the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company).[^o29] They crossed into France on the 31st, had an audience with [Philip V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_V_of_Spain) of [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Spain) at [Aix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-en-Provence), and passed from [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille) to [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa) on 15 December; they finally reached Rome on the 29th or 30th.[^o29]

In Rome, the pair scheduled audiences, lobbied [cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_cardinal), prepared their documents, and attended sessions at the [Holy Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Office).[^o30] They met [Cardinal Fabroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Agostino_Fabroni), the secretary of the [Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Propagation_of_the_Faith) on 10 January 1703 and [Pope Clement XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI) two days later.[^o30] On the 14th, they gave Fabroni the first round of documents: an overview, a dossier of verified testimony, books by [De Rocha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/João_de_Rocha) and [Alenio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Alenio), and a 1664 anti-Christian pamphlet by [Yang Guangxian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Guangxian) whose complaints proved that the Jesuits were mentioning Jesus's [crucifixion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Jesus) to the Chinese.[^o30] Some of these were rejected on various grounds, and they were forced to hire a lawyer surnamed Ursaia to present them in the proper format around March.[^o30] The [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor) [Giovanni Francesco Nicolai da Leonessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Francesco_Nicolai_da_Leonessa) had been opposing them before their arrival; on 10 March he was joined by the [MEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society) missionary [Artus de Lionne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artus_de_Lionne).[^o30]

Despite the Noël and Castner's efforts negotiating the Roman bureaucracy over the next two years, the voluminous Chinese testimony—including an official pronouncement by the [Kangxi Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor)—on the respectful but not worshipful nature of [Chinese veneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_veneration), and the pains the Jesuits undertook transmitting it around the French mission in [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) (which supposedly intercepted it under orders from [Bishop Maigrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Maigrot)),[^o31] there is no evidence that the Roman court ever weighed any of the Jesuit evidence.[^o31] Instead, despite the pope's kind words,[^o36] the decision had already been reached well ahead of its formal proclamation: [Charles-Thomas Maillard de Tournon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles-Thomas_Maillard_de_Tournon) had been named [legate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_legate) for China and the East Indies on 5 December 1701 with the specific instructions to disallow further practice of Chinese rituals by Christians there;[^n4][^o33] he was given [papal-like supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_legate#Legatus_a_latere) over the clerics there and, on the 27th, consecrated as the [titular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see) [patriarch of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Antioch),[^n4] making him the notional head of the churches across most of Asia. He left for the Qing Empire aboard the French ship *Maurepas* on 9 February 1703, only weeks after Noël's arrival, and disallowed the Jesuit leniency towards to the [Malabar rites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_rites) while he waited out the [monsoon season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon_season) in [Pondicherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_India).[^n4] By 1704, Noël and Castner were forbidden from publishing their arguments, although their opponents were printing their treatises in great volume, and correspondence and treatises shipped from China were confiscated at [Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livorno).[^o36] Noël seems to have accepted that there was little to be done or been otherwise occupied for the rest of the year.[^o36] Castner continued his lobbying, assisted after 26 February 1704 by [Jean-François de Pélisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-François_de_Pélisson), who arrived with further documentation from the Jesuits in China.[^o36]

*Image: Portrait of the Kangxi Emperor in Court Dress.jpg.* Aixinjueluo Xuanye, the [Kangxi Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor) of the [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) (18th c.)

On 20 November [Pope Clement XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI)'s [decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_decree) [Cum Deus Optimus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cum_Deus_Optimus)... ruled almost completely against the Jesuits,[^o37] formalizing a ban on both the rites and further discussion of the topic. Christians could not refer to [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_God) as 天 (*tiān*, "[Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tian)" or "the sky") and their churches could not display the imperial plaque ordering parishioners to "Revere Heaven" (敬, *Jìng Tiān*).[^o37] [Tournon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles-Thomas_Maillard_de_Tournon) was to prepare more detailed regulations to avoid "every hint of pagan superstition",[^o37] and the decree was worded legalistically and carefully—"hall or temple", "sacrifice or offering"—to limit any chance the Jesuits might evade or limit its application.[^o38]

Noël returned east in 1706, traveling—at Castner's insistence—not via [Goa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Goa) and through the [Straits of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Malacca) but around [Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor); this route proved faster and subsequently became standard for journeys between Europe and [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Macau).[^n3] They arrived on 22 July 1707,[^n3] finding the [legate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_legate) [Tournon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles-Thomas_Maillard_de_Tournon) under arrest in Macau[^n4] and the entire mission in chaos. The sickly Tournon had arrived at Macau in April 1705[^n5] and Beijing on 4 December,[^o43] insisting on the incompatibility of [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism) and [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism).[^n6] His first imperial audience the same month had been diplomatic and held out hope for [permanent relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China–Holy_See_relations) between China and the [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States);[^o43] his second, on 29 June 1706, had found the emperor displeased that any controversy had arisen over the Jesuits' accommodation of rites he had personally verified as secular[^o51] and, in any case, necessary for Chinese society.[^o45] Tournon—still generally uninformed on the details of the situation[^o43] — had deferred to the "great expert" Maigrot, whose analysis had prompted Rome's reversal, and the emperor agreed to receive him at the new [summer palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengde_Mountain_Resort) at Rehe (now [Chengde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengde)).[^o46] Maigrot had already been summoned from Fujian[^o38] and was interviewed on 2 August 1706.[^n7] Despite having lived in China since 1684,[^n6] he proved so grossly ignorant — he knew only [Fuzhounese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhounese)[^o49] and required [Dominique Parrenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Parrenin) to translate the emperor's questions.[^o46] He claimed to have read the [Four Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Books) but was unable to remember two characters from them by heart;[^o46] he had not even read [Matteo Ricci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Ricci)'s [Chinese catechism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianzhu_Shiyi);[^n8][^o38] and he could read only one of the four [Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_character) on the plaque behind the emperor's head[^o49] but presumed to lecture the patron of the [Kangxi Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Dictionary) on the permissible meanings of the character 天;[^o51]—and stubborn[^o52] that he had finally been expelled from the country on 17 December [^n7] and Christian missionaries required to receive an imperial permit (票, *piào*) attesting to their support of "the method of [Matteo Ricci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Ricci)" and their willingness to remain in China for the rest of their lives.[^o54][^n7] Finally receiving notice of Cum Deus Optimus...,[^o54]

Tournon had ordered a [summary and automatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latae_sententiae) [excommunication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excommunication) of any Christian permitting Confucian rituals from [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) on 25 January 1707;[^n4] on 7 February, he had further issued instructions concerning the *piao* examination—again on pain of excommunication[^o54] — that precluded its ever being approved.[^o38] Enraged, the emperor finally had him arrested and deported on 13 June,[^o57] with the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) then holding him under [house arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_arrest) for their own reasons.[^o58]

### Second Roman Embassy {#Second_Roman_Embassy}

*Image: Open letter from Kangxi to Pope Clement XI.png.* The [Kangxi Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor)'s 1716 [open letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_letter) to [Clement XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI), inquiring about the fate of his 1706 and 1708 embassies

About half of the missionaries then in China joined [Maigrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Maigrot) and [Tournon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles-Thomas_Maillard_de_Tournon) in exile.[^o60] At the emperor's insistence, a second embassy was dispatched to Rome to overturn Cum Deus Optimus... and Maigrot and Tournon's various rulings in 1706; this was apparently lost at sea.[^o58] Unable to secure a residence permit without fear of [excommunication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excommunication), Noël joined a third embassy. (He has sometimes been said to have been specifically requested by the [Kangxi Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor), although this seems unlikely.)[^o37]

Noël departed for Europe from Macau on 14 January 1708 on the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal) ship *Bom Jesus de Mazagão das Brotas* with the Jesuits José Ramón Arxó and António Francesco Giuseppe Provana and the Chinese convert [Louis Fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Fan_%28convert%29).[^n3] Traveling via [Batavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Dutch_East_Indies) and [Bahia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahia), [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Brazil), they arrived in [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon) in September and in Rome by February the next year.[^n3] En route, he sent a letter ahead to the pope imploring:[^o63]

It is all up with this once flourishing mission now collapsing, and rushing to certain ruin, unless [Your Holiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI) should please the [emperor of the Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor) by a swift response, and graciously agree to his requests regarding the Chinese rites so long in dispute.

[Clement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI) supported Tournon completely.[^o64] A [decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_decree) from the [Holy Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Office) was issued on 25 September 1710 upholding all of his rules and condemnations.[^o64] The embassy may have been enjoined from sending the Kangxi Emperor any notice of that fact, since he never learned the fate of either of his embassies; in 1716, he resorted to providing open letters (the "Red Manifesto")[^o37] to passing European merchants to try to ascertain their fate.[^n9] (Noël, however, was not one of those individually listed for the merchants to search for.)[^o37]

### In Europe {#In_Europe}

*Image: Clementinum\_baroque\_library\_2.jpg.* The [Czech National Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_National_Library), formerly the [Clementinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clementinum) Library of [Charles-Ferdinand University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles-Ferdinand_University), completed in the 1720s

Noël then appears to have moved to [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague), in the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire)'s [Kingdom of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia) (now the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic)). There, he published mathematical and astronomical observations from [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_India) and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_Empire)[^n10] and [lectured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) on mathematics at the Jesuit-staffed [Charles-Ferdinand University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles-Ferdinand_University).[^n3] He also published [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Latin) translations of [classic Chinese texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucian_classics)[^n11] and works on [Sinology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinology) which he had worked over for decades.[^n3] Noël's translations were banned in the [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States) and [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), but were praised by [Du Halde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Du_Halde) in his *[Description of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Description_of_China)*.[^n3][^o72] They were difficult to acquire in France and western Europe but were among the most influential Jesuit works in Germany and eastern Europe,[^o73] where they inspired works by [Johann Benedikt Carpzov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Benedikt_Carpzov),[^o99] [Wilhelm von Leibnitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_von_Leibnitz), and [Christian Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Wolff_%28philosopher%29),[^n3] the latter of whom lost his position at the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle) because of his immoderate praise of [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius) and admission that the Chinese had been able to [distinguish between right and wrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics) without exposure to Christianity. Noël's *Historical Notices*—which aimed to reopen the Chinese rites issue—does not seem to have been formally banned but was almost immediately suppressed.[^n12] It seems likely that its claimed papal [imprimatur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imprimatur) was that which [Clement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI) had granted to publish findings *before* his 1704 decision;[^n12] on 19 March 1715, he issued the [bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_bull) [Ex Illa Die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_Illa_Die)... repeating in stronger terms his condemnations and the incompatibility of Chinese ritual with Catholicism.

On 10 June that year, Noël sought approval to return to China[^n12] although he was 64 at the time. He was denied permission.[^o78] He died on 17 September 1729 in [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France).[^n12]

## Works {#Works}

*Image: LifeAndWorksOfConfucius1687.jpg.* The engraving of [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius) in the 1687 *[Confucius, Philosopher of the Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius_Sinarum_Philosophus)*. Noël's own translations had no illustrations, apart from their floral tailpieces.

Noël published his *Mathematical and Physical Observations Made in India and China* (Observationes Mathematicae et Physicae in India et China Factae) at the [Charles-Ferdinand University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles-Ferdinand_University)'s press in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague), [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia) (now the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic)), in 1710.[^n10]

Noël's effort to translate the Chinese classics was a generally scholarly one, aiming to present it more correctly on its own terms than previous Jesuit editions like the *[Confucius Sinarum Philosophus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius_Sinarum_Philosophus)* had,[^n13][^o99][^n14] but it was still an exercise undertaken in order to further [missionizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mission) among the Chinese.[^n15] The preface to his *Six Chinese Classics* ends with the admonition that the reader should bear the [Christian life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_culture) in mind while reading the volume's [Chinese teachings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism) and the hope that the work would assist in making [Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ) the cornerstone to every life.[^n16] The Jesuits initially focused on translating [Confucian classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucian_classics), rather than [Buddhist scriptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_scripture) or the [Taoist canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoist_canon), because of its greater importance in [Chinese officialdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholar-bureaucrat) under the [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) and [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty).[^n15] The [works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengzi_%28book%29) of [Mencius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mencius) were not originally translated because [Matteo Ricci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Ricci) disliked Mencian interpretations of the other classic texts, particularly his strong condemnation of [celibacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clerical_celibacy) as [unfilial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filial_piety).[^n17]

Noël published his *Six Classic Books of the Chinese Empire* (Sinensis Imperii Libri Classici Sex) at the same press the next year,[^n11] although his manuscripts show he had been working on them since at least 1700.[^n3] The six classics were the *[Great Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Learning)* (Adultorum Schola or Doctrina),[^n18] the *[Doctrine of the Mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine_of_the_Mean)* (Immutabile Medium),[^n19] the *[Analects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analects)* (Liber Sententiarum),[^n20] the [collected works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengzi_%28book%29) of [Mencius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mencius) (Memcius),[^n21] the *[Classic of Filial Piety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_of_Filial_Piety)* (Filialis Observantia),[^n22] and the *[Lesser Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Learning)* (Parvulorum Schola).[^n23][^n14] Each of the first four are completely new translations prefaced by [Zhu Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Xi)'s [commentaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sishu_Jizhu) ([t](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_characters) 四書, [s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_characters) 四书, *Sìshū Jízhù*).[^n13][^o99] Each of the last three were the first European translations of the works.[^n13] All were rather fairly freely translated from the editions established by Zhu Xi; his preface states the works are "not, so to speak, what the Chinese wrote but, I hope, what they really meant".[^n13] For example, the first lines of the *Doctrine of the Mean* were rendered "The [Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_of_Heaven) of [Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tian) is nature itself; the tendency of this nature is the [way of acting correctly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao); the direction of this life is a right discipline of life, or the right precepts for living."[^n13]

At the same time, he published his *Three Treatises on Chinese Philosophy* (Philosophia Sinica Tribus Tractatibus).[^n24] Its three sections deal with "On Knowledge of the First Being or God among the Chinese" (De Cognitione Primi Entis seu Dei apud Sinas), "On the Ceremonies of the Chinese for the Dead" (De Ceremoniis Sinarum erga Denunctos), and "On Chinese Ethics" (De Ethica Sinensi).[^n24] Unlike earlier Jesuit works, it does not claim that the [Neo-Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Confucianism) of [Zhu Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Xi) and others was a [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_China) corruption of [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism); it treats it as an organic development although still cautioning that its vague terms should not be used in reference to the [Christian God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_God).[^n3]

Finally, in the same year, he also published *Historical Notices of Chinese Rituals and Ceremonies in the Veneration of Deceased Parents and Benefactors*.[^n25] It expands on the topic of the second his *Three Treatises*, with many more citations from Chinese works.[^n12] Combatively [polemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polemic) in describing an understanding of [Chinese ancestral veneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_ancestral_veneration) that is compatible with [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism), it claimed a papal [imprimatur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imprimatur) for its publishing but was almost immediately suppressed.[^n12]

He published his *Little Poetic Works* (Opuscula Poetica) at [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt-on-Main) in 1717.[^n26] Its four parts comprise a *Life of Jesus Christ under the Name of Divine Love* (Vita Jesu Christi sub Nomine Divini Amoris);[^n27] *Marian Letters* (Epistolae Marianae);[^n28] a *Life of St Ignatius of Loyola, Founder of the Jesuits* (Vita Sancti Ignatii de Loyola Societatis Jesu Fundatoris);[^n29] and several tragedies (Tragoediae), including *Philotas*,[^n30] *Herod* (Herodes),[^n31] *Love* (Amor),[^n32] *Lucifer*,[^n33] *Accianus*,[^n34] and *Henry* (Henricus).[^n35] An appendix includes the comedy *Blind Sight* (Caecus Videns).[^n36]

He also published a popular theology textbook.[^o78]
