# John Dougall (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Dougall"}

[Dr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_%28title%29) **John Dougall** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (June 1867 – 24 February 1960) was "one of Scotland's leading [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902)".[^o2] Two formulas are named [Dougall's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dougall's_formula_%28disambiguation%29) after him: one for the sum of a [<sub>7</sub>*F*<sub>6</sub> hypergeometric series](wikipedia://en/361184), and another for the sum of a [bilateral hypergeometric series](wikipedia://en/19635243).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Dougall was born in June 1867 in [Kippen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kippen), a small village near [Stirling](wikipedia://en/61311), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland); his father, a watchmaker and postmaster, had nine children, among whom John was the eldest.[^o5]

He was educated locally at Kippen School. He left school at age 13 to become a post office worker, but a year later entered [Glasgow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow), from which he earned an M.A. in 1886.[^o4][^o5] (He was later given a doctorate by the same university.)[^o5] After graduating, he taught mathematics at the [Glasgow and West of Scotland Technical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Science_and_Technology) before becoming an editor and translator of mathematical publications for [Blackie and Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackie_and_Son), a Glasgow publisher.[^o4][^o5] He died on 24 February 1960 in Glasgow.[^o6]

Dougall became a member of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940) in 1885, and was president of the society for 1925–1926.[^o4] He won the Makdougall-Brisbane Prize of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) for 1902–1904, and was elected a Fellow of the Society in 1921.[^o4] His proposers were [George Alexander Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Alexander_Gibson), Sir [Edmund Taylor Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Taylor_Whittaker), [Cargill Gilston Knott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargill_Gilston_Knott), and [James Gordon Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gordon_Gray).[^o7]

He frequently published mathematical works in the proceedings and transactions of these two societies.[^o4] He was also an honorary president of the Glasgow Mathematical Association, and in 1936 he followed [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) as Gibson Lecturer at Glasgow University.[^o2][^o5]

At Blackie and Son, Dougall oversaw the publication of many advanced mathematics books, not only from English authors but also translations from writings in German and Italian by [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238), [Konrad Knopp](wikipedia://en/5630788), [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180), [Vito Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Volterra), and others.[^o5] Dougall's own contributions to mathematics include works on [Bessel functions](wikipedia://en/4700), [Mathieu functions](wikipedia://en/1724802), [hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_series), and the [Schläfli double six](wikipedia://en/35301595).[^o4][^o5] He also made contributions to the theory of [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29), for which he won the Makdougall-Brisbane Prize.[^o5]

He died at home, 47 Airthrey Avenue in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow) on 25 February 1960.[^o8]

## Publications {#Publications}

Dougall translated [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born)'s critical book *Atomic Physics*, and [Émile Borel](wikipedia://en/356161)'s *Space and Time* into English
