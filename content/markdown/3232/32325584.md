# King effect

*Image: Rank order countries.png.* Rank-ordering of the population of countries follows a [stretched exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/6262575)[^o1] except in the cases of the two "kings": China and India.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="King effect"}

The **king effect** is the name given by [Jean Laherrère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Laherrère) and [Didier Sornette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Sornette)[^o3] to the phenomenon in natural distributions where the top one or two members of a ranked set are clear [outliers](wikipedia://en/160951). These top one or two members are unexpectedly large and do not conform to the statistical [distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) or [rank-distribution](wikipedia://en/10063629) which the remainder of the set follows.[^o4]

:::

Distributions typically followed include the [power-law distribution](wikipedia://en/24522#Power-law_probability_distributions),[^o5] that is a basis for the [stretched exponential function](wikipedia://en/6262575),[^o1][^o6] and [parabolic fractal distribution](wikipedia://en/1096151). Laherrere and Sornette noted the King effect in the distributions of:

- French city sizes (where the point representing Paris is the "king", failing to conform to the stretched exponential[^o1]), and similarly for other countries with a [primate city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate_city), such as the United Kingdom (London), and the extreme case of [Bangkok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok) (see [list of cities in Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Thailand)).
- [Country populations](wikipedia://en/69058) (where only the points representing China and India fail to fit a stretched exponential[^o1]).

Note, however, that the king effect is not limited to outliers with a positive evaluation attached to their rank: for rankings on an undesirable attribute, there may exist a **pauper effect**, with a similar detachment of extremely ranked data points from the reasonably distributed portion of the data set.
