# Double centralizer theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Double centralizer theorem"}

In the branch of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) called [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), the **double centralizer theorem** can refer to any one of several similar results. These results concern the [centralizer of a subring](wikipedia://en/145661) *S* of a ring *R*, denoted **C**<sub>*R*</sub>(*S*) in this article. It is always the case that **C**<sub>*R*</sub>(**C**<sub>*R*</sub>(*S*)) contains *S*, and a double centralizer theorem gives conditions on *R* and *S* that guarantee that **C**<sub>*R*</sub>(**C**<sub>*R*</sub>(*S*)) is *equal* to *S*.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statements of the theorem"}

## Statements of the theorem {#Statements_of_the_theorem}

### Motivation {#Motivation}

The centralizer of a subring *S* of *R* is given by

$$\mathrm{C}_R(S)=\{r\in R \mid rs=sr \text{ for all } s\in S\}.\,$$

Clearly **C**<sub>*R*</sub>(**C**<sub>*R*</sub>(*S*)) ⊇ *S*, but it is not always the case that one can say the two sets are equal. The double centralizer theorems give conditions under which one can conclude that equality occurs.

There is another special case of interest. Let *M* be a right *R* module and give *M* the natural left *E*-module structure, where *E* is End(*M*), the ring of endomorphisms of the abelian group *M*. Every map *m*<sub>*r*</sub> given by *m*<sub>*r*</sub>(*x*) = *xr* creates an additive endomorphism of *M*, that is, an element of *E*. The map *r* → *m*<sub>*r*</sub> is a ring homomorphism of *R* into the ring *E*, and we denote the image of *R* inside of *E* by *R*<sub>*M*</sub>. It can be checked that the [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) of this canonical map is the [annihilator](wikipedia://en/328684) Ann(*M*<sub>*R*</sub>). Therefore, by an [isomorphism theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_theorem#First_isomorphism_theorem_2) for rings, *R*<sub>*M*</sub> is isomorphic to the quotient ring *R*/Ann(*M*<sub>*R*</sub>). Clearly when *M* is a [faithful module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_module), *R* and *R*<sub>*M*</sub> are isomorphic rings.

So now *E* is a ring with *R*<sub>*M*</sub> as a subring, and **C**<sub>*E*</sub>(*R*<sub>*M*</sub>) may be formed. By definition one can check that **C**<sub>*E*</sub>(*R*<sub>*M*</sub>) = End(*M*<sub>*R*</sub>), the ring of *R* module endomorphisms of *M*. Thus if it occurs that **C**<sub>*E*</sub>(**C**<sub>*E*</sub>(*R*<sub>*M*</sub>)) = *R*<sub>*M*</sub>, this is the same thing as saying **C**<sub>*E*</sub>(End(*M*<sub>*R*</sub>)) = *R*<sub>*M*</sub>.

### Central simple algebras {#Central_simple_algebras}

Perhaps the most common version is the version for [central simple algebras](wikipedia://en/404082), as it appears in (Knapp 2007, p.115):

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**: If *A* is a finite-dimensional central simple algebra over a field *F* and *B* is a simple subalgebra of *A*, then **C**<sub>*A*</sub>(**C**<sub>*A*</sub>(*B*)) = *B*, and moreover the dimensions satisfy

$$\mathrm{dim}_F(B)\cdot\mathrm{dim}_F(\mathrm{C}_A(B))=\mathrm{dim}_F(A).\,$$

:::

### Artinian rings {#Artinian_rings}

The following generalized version for [Artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273) (which include finite-dimensional algebras) appears in (Isaacs 2009, p.187). Given a [simple](wikipedia://en/29430) *R* module *U*<sub>*R*</sub>, we will borrow notation from the above motivation section including *R*<sub>*U*</sub> and *E*=End(*U*). Additionally, we will write *D*=End(*U*<sub>*R*</sub>) for the subring of *E* consisting of *R*-homomorphisms. By [Schur's lemma](wikipedia://en/521696), *D* is a [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067).

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**: Let *R* be a right Artinian ring with a simple right module *U*<sub>*R*</sub>, and let *R*<sub>*U*</sub>, *D* and *E* be given as in the previous paragraph. Then

$$R_U=\mathrm{C}_E(\mathrm{C}_E(R_U))\,.$$

**Remarks**

- In this version, the rings are chosen with the intent of proving the [Jacobson density theorem](wikipedia://en/488391). Notice that it only concludes that a particular subring has the centralizer property, in contrast to the central simple algebra version.
- Since algebras are normally defined over commutative rings, and all the involved rings above may be noncommutative, it's clear that algebras are not necessarily involved.
- If *U* is additionally a [faithful module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_module), so that *R* is a right [primitive ring](wikipedia://en/485862), then *R*<sub>*U*</sub> is ring isomorphic to *R*.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement cue=named-heading name="Polynomial identity rings"}

### Polynomial identity rings {#Polynomial_identity_rings}

In (Rowen 1980, p.154), a version is given for [polynomial identity rings](wikipedia://en/25386269). The notation Z(*R*) will be used to denote the [center of a ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_a_ring) *R*.

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**: If *R* is a [simple](wikipedia://en/443387) polynomial identity ring, and *A* is a simple Z(*R*) subalgebra of *R*, then **C**<sub>*R*</sub>(**C**<sub>*R*</sub>(*A*)) = *A*.

**Remarks**

- This version can be considered to be "between" the central simple algebra version and the Artinian ring version. This is because simple polynomial identity rings are Artinian,[^o15] but unlike the Artinian version, the conclusion still refers to all central simple subrings of *R*.

:::

:::

### von Neumann Algebras {#von_Neumann_Algebras}

The [Von Neumann bicommutant theorem](wikipedia://en/455962) states that a \*-subalgebra *A* of the algebra of [bounded operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_operators) *B*(*H*) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) *H* is a [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) (i.e. is [weakly closed](wikipedia://en/458698)) if and only if *A* = C<sub>*B*(*H*)</sub>C<sub>*B*(*H*)</sub>(A).

:::

## Double centralizer property {#Double_centralizer_property}

A module *M* is said to have the *[double centralizer property](wikipedia://en/34302118)* or to be a *[balanced module](wikipedia://en/34302118)* if **C**<sub>*E*</sub>(**C**<sub>*E*</sub>(*R*<sub>*M*</sub>)) = *R*<sub>*M*</sub>, where *E* = End(*M*) and *R*<sub>*M*</sub> are as given in the motivation section. In this terminology, the Artinian ring version of the double centralizer theorem states that simple right modules for right Artinian rings are balanced modules.
