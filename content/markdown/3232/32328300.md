# Automorphic L-function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Automorphic L-function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **automorphic *L*-function** is a function *L*(*s*,π,*r*) of a complex variable *s*, associated to an [automorphic representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation) π of a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) *G* over a [global field](wikipedia://en/288358) and a finite-dimensional complex representation *r* of the [Langlands dual group](wikipedia://en/6789133) <sup>*L*</sup>*G* of *G*, generalizing the [Dirichlet L-series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_L-series) of a [Dirichlet character](wikipedia://en/174515) and the [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297) of a [modular form](wikipedia://en/286000). They were introduced by (txt).

:::

(Borel 1979) and (Arthur Gelbart 1991) gave surveys of automorphic L-functions.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Automorphic $L$-functions should have the following properties (which have been proved in some cases but are still conjectural in other cases).

The L-function $L(s, \pi, r)$ should be a product over the places $v$ of $F$ of local $L$ functions.

$$L(s, \pi, r) = \prod_v L(s, \pi_v, r_v)$$

Here the [automorphic representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation) $\pi = \otimes\pi_v$ is a tensor product of the representations $\pi_v$ of local groups.

The L-function is expected to have an analytic continuation as a meromorphic function of all complex $s$, and satisfy a functional equation

$$L(s, \pi, r) = \epsilon(s, \pi, r) L(1 - s, \pi, r^\lor)$$

where the factor $\epsilon(s, \pi, r)$ is a product of "local constants"

$$\epsilon(s, \pi, r) = \prod_v \epsilon(s, \pi_v, r_v, \psi_v)$$

almost all of which are 1.

:::

## General linear groups {#General_linear_groups}

(Godement Jacquet 1972) constructed the automorphic L-functions for general linear groups with *r* the standard representation (so-called [standard L-functions](wikipedia://en/33791083)) and verified analytic continuation and the functional equation, by using a generalization of the method in [Tate's thesis](wikipedia://en/3149490). Ubiquitous in the Langlands Program are [Rankin-Selberg](wikipedia://en/27989406) products of representations of GL(m) and GL(n). The resulting Rankin-Selberg L-functions satisfy a number of analytic properties, their functional equation being first proved via the [Langlands–Shahidi method](wikipedia://en/31789393).

In general, the [Langlands functoriality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_functoriality) conjectures imply that automorphic L-functions of a connected [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) are equal to products of automorphic L-functions of general linear groups. A proof of Langlands functoriality would also lead towards a thorough understanding of the analytic properties of automorphic L-functions.
