# Donald Burkholder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donald Burkholder"}

*Image: Burkholder.jpg.* Donald Burkholder

**Donald Lyman Burkholder** (January 19, 1927 – April 14, 2013[^o2]) was an American mathematician known for his contributions to [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), particularly the theory of [martingales](wikipedia://en/274536). The [Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality](wikipedia://en/2143889#Martingales) is co-named after him. Burkholder spent most of his professional career as a professor in the Department of Mathematics of the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign). After his retirement in 1998, Donald Burkholder remained a professor emeritus in the Department of Mathematics of the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign) and a CAS Professor Emeritus of Mathematics at the Center for Advanced Study, [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign). He was a member of the [U.S. National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._National_Academy_of_Sciences) and a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o3] His son, [J. Peter Burkholder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Peter_Burkholder), is Distinguished Professor Emeritus of Musicology at [Indiana University Bloomington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington).[^o4]

:::

## Biographical data {#Biographical_data}

Burkholder received a PhD in statistics in 1955 from the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill), under the direction of [Wassily Hoeffding](wikipedia://en/2547060).[^o6] He was appointed an assistant professor in the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign) in 1955[^o7] where he remained until his retirement in 1998.[^o8] He was promoted to associate professor in 1960,[^o9] became a professor in the department in 1964 and was appointed as professor at the Center for Advanced Study at UIUC in 1978.[^o8]

Burkholder delivered an [invited lecture at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 1970, a Wald Lecture at the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1971, a Mordell Lecture at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) in 1986, and a Zygmund Lecture at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1988.[^o18]

Donald Burkholder was elected a member of the [U.S. National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1992.[^o10][^o11] The same year he was elected a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o12] On December 20, 2010, Burkholder was elected a Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) for "distinguished contributions to probability theory, particularly the theory of martingales, and his work in stochastic processes, [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), and [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659)."[^o13][^o14]

In 2009 Burkholder was named a SIAM Fellow by the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) *"for advances in martingale transforms and applications of probabilistic methods in analysis"*.[^o15][^o16]

Burkholder was an editor (1964–1967) of the journal *[Annals of Mathematical Statistics](wikipedia://en/23772805)*.[^o17] He served as the president of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1975–76.[^o18] He is a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).[^o19]
