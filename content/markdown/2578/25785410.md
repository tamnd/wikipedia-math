# Taha Abdurrahman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Taha Abdurrahman"}

**Taha Abderrahmane** (طه عبد الرحمن; born 28 May 1944)[^o3][^o4] is a Moroccan [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and one of the leading philosophers and thinkers in the Arab and Islamic worlds. His work centers on [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language), [moral philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_morality), and [contractarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_contract) ethics. He believes in multiple modernities and seeks to establish an ethical and humanitarian modernity based on the values and principles of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam) and the [Arab tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_culture).[^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Abderrahmane was born on 28 May 1944 and raised in [El Jadida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Jadida) (province of [El Jadida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Jadida_%28province%29)), Morocco. There, he attended primary school, after which he moved to [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), where he continued his high school education. He later joined [Mohammad V University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_V_University) (in [Rabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabat), Morocco), where he obtained his [licentiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licentiate_%28degree%29) in philosophy. Abderrahmane completed his studies at the [University of Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sorbonne), where he received his second licentiate and obtained his third-level [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral) in 1972 on the subject: "Language and philosophy: a study of the linguistic structures of ontology", and in 1985 he earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in philosophy on the subject "study of argumentation and its methods" (in French: *Essai sur les logiques des raisonnements argumentatifs et naturels*, or in English as "Essay on the logic of argumentative and natural reasonings").[^o7]

In addition to speaking Arabic, French, and English, he also reads German, Latin and Ancient Greek[^o8] in order to read philosophers in their original languages.

Abderrahmane is known in all his books as "Taha Abderrahmane", although his given name is Abderrahmane and his family name is Taha. Scholars in the [Anglophone world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-speaking_world) often repeat "Abderrahmane" as if it were his family name, following the way it appears in his books, while in the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), his philosophy is known under the name "Taha'iyya", i.e., "Taha'ian". The official spelling of his name in official documentation is Abderrahmane (not Abdurrahman or [Abdul Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Rahman)).

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Abderrahmane served as a professor of philosophy of language and logic at [Mohammad V University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_V_University) from 1970 until his retirement in 2005. He is a member of the International Society for the Study of Argumentation, which he represents in Morocco; a representative of the [Society of Intercultural Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Intercultural_Philosophy) (German: *Gesellschaft für Interkulturelle Philosophie*); and director of the Wisdom Circle for Thinkers and Researchers.[^o10]

Abderrahmane was awarded the Prize of Morocco twice. In 2006, he received the [ISESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISESCO) Prize in [Islamic thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_thought) and philosophy.

## Characteristics of his method {#Characteristics_of_his_method}

His philosophical practice is characterized by a combination of "logical analysis" and "linguistic derivation" proceeding from a mystical experience, within a framework that provides the concepts related to the Islamic heritage and is based on the most important achievements of modern Western thought at the level of "theories of speech", "argumentative logic", and "philosophy of ethics", which makes his philosophizing predominantly appear in a "moral" and "deliberative" style.
