# Amelia Reid

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Amelia Reid"}

**Amelia Reid** (November 13, 1924 – March 3, 2001) was a mathematician, pilot, [flight instructor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_instructor), businesswoman, [airshow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airshow) performer, and airport advocate. She was the founder of Amelia Reid Aviation (now AeroDynamic Aviation) and is known as California's First Lady of Aviation.[^o8]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Reid got her first taste of flying in 1940 when pilot [Evelyn Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evelyn_Sharp_%28aviator%29) took her on a ride in a Piper Cub J-2.[^o4] In 1941 Reid had her first flying lessons,[^o8] and in May 1946 she earned her pilot's license.[^o4]

*Image: Amelia Reid plaque.jpg.* A plaque honoring Reid at Reid Hillview Airport in [San Jose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California).

Reid studied mathematics at [Kearney State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska_at_Kearney) in Nebraska, earning her bachelor's degree in June 1945.[^o8][^o4][^o5] Reid then attended [San Jose State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_State_University) where she earned her master's degree in mathematics.[^o8] She worked at [NACA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NACA) (now known as [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA)) as a [human computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_%28occupation%29) and [programing mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmer) from 1945 to 1958.[^o8][^o5] Because the NACA [Ames Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ames_Research_Center), where she worked, did not allow flexibility in her work schedule, she left after the birth of her son.[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

From there Reid returned to flying and became a certified commercial pilot with instructor ratings in 1960.[^o8] That same year, Reid founded Amelia Reid Aviation, a flight school at [Reid-Hillview Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reid-Hillview_Airport) in [San Jose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California), California.[^o8] The company bought their first aircraft, a Taylorcraft L-2, for just \$350. Reid initially ran the business out of her 1959 Ford and, later, out of two trailers.[^o4] She mortgaged her home and constructed a permanent [hangar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangar) and office building in 1967, from which she operated her flight school for the rest of her life.[^o7][^o4]

Reid logged over 55,000 flight hours and trained more than 4,000 pilots.[^o8] She flew in countless [airshows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airshows), her last at age 75 in a [Cessna Aerobat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_150). She won the 1996 [Aircraft Owners and Pilots Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_Owners_and_Pilots_Association) Lawrence P. Sharples Award and was inducted into the [National Association of Flight Instructors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Flight_Instructors) Hall of Fame.[^o7][^o8][^o9]

Reid's students include noted airshow performer [Sean D. Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_D._Tucker),[^o8] aviation author and speaker [Rod Machado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Machado), aerospace engineer and author [H. Paul Shuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Paul_Shuch) (whose recurring fictional character Avalon Eden[^o10] is based in part on Reid), and Jason Dahl, the captain on [United flight 93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines_Flight_93) on [September 11, 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11,_2001).

## Activism {#Activism}

In the 1960s, [Reid-Hillview Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reid–Hillview_Airport), founded in 1939 by five local airmen including Reid's father-in-law, was sold to [Santa Clara County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara_County,_California).[^o8] The growth of [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley) in the San Jose area instigated campaigns to close the airport.[^o8] Reid was a strong advocate of the preservation of the airport and a founder of the Reid-Hillview Airport Association.

## Later life {#Later_life}

On January 16, 2001, Reid had a stroke, and she died on March 3, 2001, at age 76.[^o8]
