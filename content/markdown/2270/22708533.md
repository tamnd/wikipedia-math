# MRB constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="MRB constant"}

*Image: MRB-Gif.gif.* First 100 partial sums of $(-1)^k (k^{1/k} - 1)$

The **MRB constant** is a [mathematical constant](wikipedia://en/48779551), with decimal expansion 0.187859… . The constant is named after its discoverer, Marvin Ray Burns, who published his discovery of the constant in 1999.[^o2] Burns had initially called the constant "rc" for root constant[^o3] but, at [Simon Plouffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Plouffe)'s suggestion, the constant was renamed the 'Marvin Ray Burns's Constant', or "MRB constant".[^o4]

:::

The MRB constant is defined as the [upper limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_limit) of the [partial sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_sums)[^o9][^o5][^o6][^o8]

$$s_n = \sum_{k=1}^n (-1)^k k^{1/k}$$

As $n$ grows to infinity, the sums have [upper and lower limit points](wikipedia://en/44837) of −0.812140... and 0.187859..., separated by an [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) of length 1. The constant can also be explicitly defined by the following infinite sums:

$$0.187859\ldots = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} (-1)^k (k^{1/k} - 1) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left((2k)^{1/(2k)} - (2k-1)^{1/(2k-1)}\right).$$

The constant relates to the [divergent series](wikipedia://en/876428):

$$\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} (-1)^k k^{1/k}.$$

There is no known [closed-form expression](wikipedia://en/585143) of the MRB constant,[^o10] nor is it known whether the MRB constant is [algebraic](wikipedia://en/1158), [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325) or even [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689).
