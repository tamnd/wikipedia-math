# Maps of manifolds

*Image: MorinSurfaceAsSphere'sInsideVersusOutside.PNG.* A [Morin surface](wikipedia://en/2070457), an [immersion](wikipedia://en/6993953) used in [sphere eversion](wikipedia://en/478672).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maps of manifolds"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), various types of [maps](wikipedia://en/516931) or [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) between [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) are studied, both as objects in their own right and for the light they shed.

:::

## Types of maps {#Types_of_maps}

Just as there are various types of manifolds, there are various types of maps of manifolds.

In [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), the basic types of maps correspond to various [categories](wikipedia://en/53759) of manifolds: DIFF for [smooth functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) between [differentiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219), PL for [piecewise linear functions](wikipedia://en/444224) between [piecewise linear manifolds](wikipedia://en/2174184), and TOP for [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) between [topological manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119193). These are progressively weaker structures, though they are not contained within each other as sets: smooth functions are not piecewise linear, nor are piecewise linear functions smooth, except for globally linear functions. The set of [piecewise-smooth manifolds](wikipedia://en/10131478) includes both smooth manifolds and piecewise linear manifolds, but piecewise differentiable maps are not closed under composition, so they do not form a category. However, every piecewise smooth manifold admits a piecewise linear structure.

In addition to these general categories of maps, there are maps with special properties; these may or may not form categories, and may or may not be generally discussed categorically.

*Image: TrefoilKnot 01.svg.* The right-handed [trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773).

In [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732) a basic type are [embeddings](wikipedia://en/52119), of which [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008) is a central example, and generalizations such as [immersions](wikipedia://en/6993953), [submersions](wikipedia://en/356413), [covering spaces](wikipedia://en/250323), and [ramified covering spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramified_covering_space). Basic results include the [Whitney embedding theorem](wikipedia://en/477578) and [Whitney immersion theorem](wikipedia://en/1146707).

*Image: Riemann sqrt.svg.* [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) for the function *f*(*z*) = $\sqrt{z}$, shown as a [ramified covering space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramified_covering_space) of the complex plane.

In complex geometry, ramified covering spaces are used to model [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181), and to analyze maps between surfaces, such as by the [Riemann–Hurwitz formula](wikipedia://en/497413).

In Riemannian geometry, one may ask for maps to preserve the Riemannian metric, leading to notions of [isometric embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometric_embedding), [isometric immersions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometric_immersion), and [Riemannian submersions](wikipedia://en/2850614); a basic result is the [Nash embedding theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_embedding_theorem).

## Scalar-valued functions {#Scalar-valued_functions}

*Image: Spherical harmonics.png.* 3D color plot of the [spherical harmonics](wikipedia://en/203056) of degree $n=5$

A basic example of maps between manifolds are scalar-valued functions on a manifold, $\scriptstyle f\colon M \to \mathbb{R}$ or $\scriptstyle f\colon M \to \mathbb{C},$ sometimes called [regular functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_function) or [functionals](wikipedia://en/1374948), by analogy with algebraic geometry or linear algebra. These are of interest both in their own right, and to study the underlying manifold.

In geometric topology, most commonly studied are [Morse functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function), which yield [handlebody](wikipedia://en/864472) decompositions, which generalize to [Morse–Bott functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse–Bott_function) and can be used for instance to understand classical groups, such as in [Bott periodicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_periodicity).

In [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), one often studies solution to [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), an important example of which is [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), where one studies [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762): the kernel of the [Laplace operator](wikipedia://en/174706). This leads to such functions as the [spherical harmonics](wikipedia://en/203056), and to [heat kernel](wikipedia://en/3904336) methods of studying manifolds, such as [hearing the shape of a drum](wikipedia://en/1072325) and some proofs of the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752).

The [monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250) around a [singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity) or [branch point](wikipedia://en/483173) is an important part of analyzing such functions.

## Curves and paths {#Curves_and_paths}

*Image: Football3c.jpg.* A [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) on an [American football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_%28ball%29) illustrating the proof of Gromov's [filling area conjecture](wikipedia://en/12083818) in [systolic geometry](wikipedia://en/2545455), in the hyperelliptic case (see [explanation](wikipedia://en/2545455#Filling_area_conjecture)).

Dual to scalar-valued functions – maps $\scriptstyle M \to \mathbb{R}$ – are maps $\scriptstyle \mathbb{R} \to M,$ which correspond to curves or paths in a manifold. One can also define these where the domain is an interval $[a,b],$ especially the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) $[0,1],$ or where the domain is a circle (equivalently, a periodic path) *S*<sup>1</sup>, which yields a loop. These are used to define the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004), [chains](wikipedia://en/224765) in [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory), [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) curves, and [systolic geometry](wikipedia://en/2545455).

Embedded paths and loops lead to [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), and related structures such as [links](wikipedia://en/1161784), [braids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braid_theory), and [tangles](wikipedia://en/30858815).

## Metric spaces {#Metric_spaces}

[Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) are special cases of [metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_spaces), and thus one has a notion of [Lipschitz continuity](wikipedia://en/51731), [Hölder condition](wikipedia://en/4005892), together with a [coarse structure](wikipedia://en/4960605), which leads to notions such as coarse maps and connections with [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164).
