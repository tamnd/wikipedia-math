# Orthogonal circles

*Image: Orthogonal circles.svg.* Three mutually orthogonal circles

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthogonal circles"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), two [circles](wikipedia://en/6220) are said to be **orthogonal** if their respective [tangent lines](wikipedia://en/19491492) at the points of [intersection](wikipedia://en/41604289) are [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) (meet at a [right angle](wikipedia://en/76956)).

:::

A straight line through a circle's center is orthogonal to it. Straight lines may also considered as a kind of [generalized circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_circle), for instance in [inversive geometry](wikipedia://en/295844). An orthogonal pair of lines or line and circle are orthogonal generalized circles. Straight lines are the only generalized circles that pass through the center of a circle to which they are orthogonal.

In the [conformal disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836) of the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane), every [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) is an arc of a generalized circle orthogonal to the circle of [ideal points](wikipedia://en/4995922) bounding the disk.
