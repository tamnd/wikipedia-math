# Linkage disequilibrium score regression

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Linkage disequilibrium score regression"}

In [statistical genetics](wikipedia://en/1590736), **linkage disequilibrium score regression** (**LDSR**[^o5] or **LDSC**[^o2]) is a technique that aims to quantify the separate contributions of [polygenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygenic) effects and various [confounding](wikipedia://en/3105999) factors, such as [population stratification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_stratification), based on [summary statistics](wikipedia://en/27587) from [genome-wide association studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome-wide_association_studies) (GWASs). The approach involves using [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997) to examine the relationship between [linkage disequilibrium](wikipedia://en/681230) scores and the [test statistics](wikipedia://en/1416975) of the [single-nucleotide polymorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-nucleotide_polymorphism) (SNPs) from the GWAS. Here, the "linkage disequilibrium score" for a SNP "is the sum of LD [*r<sup>2</sup>*](wikipedia://en/1500869) measured with all other SNPs".[^o3]

:::

LDSC can be used to produce SNP-based [heritability](wikipedia://en/155624) estimates, to partition this heritability into separate categories, and to calculate [genetic correlations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_correlation) between separate [phenotypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenotype). Because the LDSC approach relies only on summary statistics from an entire GWAS, it can be used efficiently even with very large sample sizes.[^o4] In LDSC, genetic correlations are calculated based on the deviation between [chi-square statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi-square_statistic) and what would be expected assuming the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis).[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extensions"}

## Extensions {#Extensions}

LDSC can also be applied across traits to estimate genetic correlations. This extension of LDSC, known as **cross-trait LD score regression**, has the advantage of not being biased if used on overlapping samples.[^o8][^o9] Another extension of LDSC, known as **stratified LD score regression** (abbreviated **SLDSR**),[^o10] aims to partition heritability by functional annotation by taking into account [genetic linkage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_linkage) between [markers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_marker).[^o11][^o12]

:::
