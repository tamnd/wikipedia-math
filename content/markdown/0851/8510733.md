# Muhyi al-Din al-Maghribi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Muhyi al-Din al-Maghribi"}

**Muḥyī al‐Milla wa al‐Dīn Yaḥyā Abū ʿAbdallāh ibn Muḥammad ibn Abī al‐Shukr al‐Maghribī al‐Andalusī** (محيي الدين المغربي; c. 1220 – June 1283), referred to in sources as **Muhyi l'din**, was an [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), [astrologer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrologer) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) of the [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age). He belonged to the group of astronomers associated with the [Maragheh observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maragheh_observatory) in the [Ilkhanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate), most notably [Nasir al-Din al-Tusi](wikipedia://en/33642424). In astronomy, [Muhyi l'din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohyeddin) carried out a large‐scale project of systematic planetary observations, which led to the development of several new astronomical parameters.[^n1]

:::

Muhyi l'din died in [Maragheh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maragheh) in modern-day Iran in June 1283.[^n1]

## Early career {#Early_career}

Muhyi al-Dīn al-Maghribī was born in c. 1220 in [al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus).[^n2] He worked for the [Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty) sultan [An-Nasir Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-Nasir_Yusuf) in [Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus).[^n3] This relationship was ultimately cut short when the sultan was killed by the Mongols in the [Siege of Aleppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Aleppo_%281260%29) in 1257. He was then sent to the [observatory at Maragheh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maragheh_observatory).[^n3]

## Work at the Maragheh observatory {#Work_at_the_Maragheh_observatory}

*Image: Maragha Observatory panorama.jpg.* The site of the [Maragheh observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maragheh_observatory); a dome now protects the most important remains

The Maragheh [observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatory) was founded in the [Ilkhanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate), a part of the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire),[^n4] [Muhyi l'din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohyeddin) went to Maragheh in 1258 as a guest of the [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) ruler [Hulagu Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulagu_Khan), where from 1259 he was involved, along with [Nasir al-Din al-Tusi](wikipedia://en/33642424), in its construction. The observatory was completed in 1262.[^n2]

At Maragheh, [Muhyi l'din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohyeddin) observed up to a total of eight of the brightest stars, of which he used the latitudes collected to compare with the values within ancient computations.[^n5] He concluded that the difference between his latitudes and ancients were not substantial, and any inconsistences were in fact due to the observations and not the subject itself. In his *Talkhīṣ al‐Majisṭī*, he commentated on Ptolemy's Almagest, presenting his own observations and hypothesizes in addition with it. For instance, Muhyi l'din supposed that the [precession](wikipedia://en/24714) would only occur in a motion that was uniform and continuous at a rate that was 1° for ever 66 years from his systematic stellar observations.[^n5]

An extant [manuscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuscript) by Muhyi l'din details of observations made from 1262 to 1274.[^n2] He continued to work on his observations at Maragheh until his death in 1283.[^n3]

## Other work {#Other_work}

[Muhyi l'din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohyeddin) considered the problem of [doubling the cube](wikipedia://en/91110), which he approached means of a method devised by the Greek mathematician [Hippocrates of Chios](wikipedia://en/3632522).[^n2]

## Commentaries and treatises {#Commentaries_and_treatises}

### Astronomy {#Astronomy}

Muhyi l'din's known works on astronomy include:[^n1]

- ar, a description of the construction and use of the [astrolabe](wikipedia://en/73664);
- ar, a description of the [geometrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrical) methods used to determine the [meridian line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_%28astronomy%29), the rising amplitude, and the revolution of the sphere;
- ar, a chronological work on the [Chinese calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_calendar), which was later translated from [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_%28language%29) into [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_%28language%29).
- Three ar:
  - ar (*The Crown of Astronomical Handbooks*), also known as ar;
  - ar, which contains astronomical observations carried out by Muhyi l'dinin Marāgha;
  - ar;
- Three commentaries on [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979)'s *[Almagest](wikipedia://en/148060)*:
  - ar (*Compendium of the Almagest*), based on his observations carried out between 1264 and 1275;
  - ar (*Summary of the Almagest*);
  - ar (*Prolegomena on the Motion of the Stars*), which contains five geometric premises on the planetary motions in the *Almagest*.

### Astrology {#Astrology}

[Muhyi l'din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohyeddin)'s astrological works were mainly devoted to [horoscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horoscope) and planetary [conjunctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjunction_%28astronomy%29), used to tell the future.[^n1]

### Mathematics {#Mathematics}

[Muhyi l'din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohyeddin)'s notable works in [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261) were *The Book on the Theorem of Menelaus* and *Treatise on the Calculation of Sines*.[^n2] He is known for his commentaries on [ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) mathematical works, in particular, his commentary on Book XV of *[Euclid's Elements](wikipedia://en/244107)*, which discussed measurements of the [regular polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedra).[^n2][^n6] Muhyi l'din's writings on trigonometry contain some elements that are original.[^n1]

In his treatise on the calculation of [sines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine), Muhyi l'din [interpolated](wikipedia://en/14569) a value for the sine of one degree; a more accurate value was not obtained until the 15th century, when the mathematicians [Qāḍī Zāda al-Rūmī](wikipedia://en/11468771) and [Jamshid al-Kashi](wikipedia://en/10553199) tackled the problem. Whilst working on the sines, Muhyi l'din used the methods devised by [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) to find an approximate value for [pi](wikipedia://en/23601).[^n2]
