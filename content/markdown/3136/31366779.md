# Opposite group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Opposite group"}

*Image: Opposite\_group\_nature.svg.* This is a [natural transformation](wikipedia://en/45022) of binary operation from a group to its opposite. $\langle g_{1}, g_{2}\rangle$ denotes the [ordered pair](wikipedia://en/22362) of the two group elements. \*' can be viewed as the naturally induced addition of +.

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **opposite group** is a way to construct a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) from another group that allows one to define [right action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) as a special case of [left action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29).

:::

[Monoids](wikipedia://en/19652), groups, [rings](wikipedia://en/48404), and [algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_over_a_ring) can be viewed as [categories](wikipedia://en/53759) with a single object. The construction of the [opposite category](wikipedia://en/372399) generalizes the opposite group, [opposite ring](wikipedia://en/3129323), etc.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $G$ be a group under the operation $*$. The opposite group of $G$, denoted $G^{\mathrm{op}}$, has the same underlying set as $G$, and its group operation $\mathbin{\ast'}$ is defined by $g_1 \mathbin{\ast'} g_2 = g_2 * g_1$.[^o4]

If $G$ is [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974), then it is equal to its opposite group. Also, every group $G$ (not necessarily abelian) is [naturally isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturally_isomorphic) to its opposite group: An isomorphism $\varphi: G \to G^{\mathrm{op}}$ is given by $\varphi(x) = x^{-1}$. More generally, any [antiautomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiautomorphism) $\psi: G \to G$ gives rise to a corresponding isomorphism $\psi': G \to G^{\mathrm{op}}$ via $\psi'(g)=\psi(g)$, since

$$\psi'(g * h) = \psi(g * h) = \psi(h) * \psi(g) = \psi(g) \mathbin{\ast'} \psi(h)=\psi'(g) \mathbin{\ast'} \psi'(h).$$

:::

## Group action {#Group_action}

Let $X$ be an object in some category, and $\rho: G \to \mathrm{Aut}(X)$ be a [right action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29). Then $\rho^{\mathrm{op}}: G^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathrm{Aut}(X)$ is a left action defined by $\rho^{\mathrm{op}}(g)x = x\rho(g)$, or $g^{\mathrm{op}}x = xg$.
