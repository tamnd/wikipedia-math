# Peter Orno

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Orno"}

Beginning in 1974, the fictitious **Peter Orno** (alternatively, **Peter Ørno**, **P. Ørno**, and **P. Orno**) appeared as the author of research papers in mathematics. According to [Robert Phelps](wikipedia://en/31368502),[^o3] the name "P. Orno" is a [pseudonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudonym) that was inspired by "porno", an abbreviation for "[pornography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pornography)".[^o30][^o4] Orno's short papers have been called "elegant" contributions to [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). Orno's theorem on [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) is important in the theory of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989). Research mathematicians have written acknowledgments that have thanked Orno for stimulating discussions and for Orno's generosity in allowing others to publish his results. The [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772)'s journals have also published more than a dozen [problems](wikipedia://en/256700) whose solutions were submitted in the name of Orno.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: OSU Numbers Garden.JPG.* Peter Orno's publications list his affiliation as [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University), site of the *Garden of Constants*.[^o6]

Peter Orno appears as the author of short papers written by an anonymous mathematician; thus "Peter Orno" is a [pseudonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudonym). According to [Robert R. Phelps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_R._Phelps),[^o3] the name "P. Orno" was inspired by "porno", a shortening of "pornography".[^o30][^o4]

Orno's papers list his affiliation as the Department of Mathematics at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University). This affiliation is confirmed in the description of Orno as a "special creation" at Ohio State in Pietsch's *History of Banach spaces and linear operators*.[^o7] The publications list of Ohio State mathematician [Gerald Edgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Edgar) includes two items that were published under the name of Orno. Edgar indicates that he published them "as Peter Ørno".[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

His papers feature "surprisingly simple" proofs and solutions to open problems in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713), according to reviewers from *[Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646)*: In one case, Orno's "elegant" approach was contrasted with the previously known "elementary, but masochistic" approach. Peter Orno's "permanent interest and sharp criticism stimulated" the "work" on *Lectures on Banach spaces of analytic functions* by Aleksander Pełczyński, which includes several of Orno's unpublished results.[^o10] Tomczak-Jaegermann thanked Peter Orno for his stimulating discussions.[^o11]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

Peter Orno has published in research journals and in collections; his papers have always been short, having lengths between one and three pages. Orno has also established himself as a formidable solver of mathematical problems in peer-reviewed journals published by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).

### Research papers {#Research_papers}

- Ørno, P. (1974). "On Banach lattices of operators". *Israel Journal of Mathematics*. 19 (3). p. 264–265. doi:10.1007/BF02757723. mr:374859 According to *[Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646)* (), this paper proves the following theorem, which has come to be known as "*Orno's theorem*": Suppose that *E* and *F* are [Banach lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_lattice), where *F* is an [infinite-dimensional vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite-dimensional_vector_space) that contains no [Riesz subspace](wikipedia://en/8099018) that is uniformly [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/1802169) to the [sequence space](wikipedia://en/1701075) equipped with the [supremum norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum_norm). If each linear operator in the uniform closure of the [finite-rank operators](wikipedia://en/8405295) from E to F has a Riesz decomposition as the difference of two [positive operators](wikipedia://en/454936), then E can be renormed so that it is an [L-space](wikipedia://en/54391334) (in the sense of Kakutani and Birkhoff).[^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18][^o19][^o20][^o21]
- Ørno, P. (1976). "A note on unconditionally converging series in Lp". *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society*. 59 (2). p. 252–254. doi:10.1090/S0002-9939-1976-0458156-7. mr:458156. jstor:2041478 According to *[Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646)* (), Orno proved the following theorem: The series Σ*f*<sub>*k*</sub> [unconditionally converges](wikipedia://en/2620508) in the [Lebesgue space](wikipedia://en/45194) of [absolutely integrable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_integrable_function) *L*<sub>1</sub>\[0,1\] if and only if, for each *k* and every *t*, we have *f*<sub>*k*</sub>(*t*)=*a*<sub>*k*</sub>*g*(*t*)*w*<sub>*k*</sub>(*t*), for some sequence (*a*<sub>*k*</sub>)∈*l*<sub>2</sub>, some function *g*∈*L*<sub>2</sub>\[0,1\], and for some [orthonormal sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthonormal_sequence) (*w*<sub>*k*</sub>) in *L*<sub>2</sub>\[0,2\] . Another result is what [Joseph Diestel](wikipedia://en/57504979) described as the "elegant proof" by Orno of a theorem of Bennet, Maurey and Nahoum.[^o23]
- Ørno, P. (1977). "Banach Spaces of Analytic Functions: Proceedings of the Pelczynski Conference Held at Kent State University, Kent, Ohio, July 12–17, 1976". 604. Springer. p. 73–75. doi:10.1007/BFb0069208. isbn:978-3-540-08356-6. mr:454485 In this paper, Orno solves an eight-year-old problem posed by [Ivan Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Singer), according to *Mathematical Reviews* ().
- Ørno, P. (1991). "On J. Borwein's concept of sequentially reflexive Banach spaces" Here, Orno solved a problem posed by [Jonathan M. Borwein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_M._Borwein). Orno characterized [sequentially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_sequence) [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091) [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) in terms of their lacking bad subspaces: Orno's theorem states that a Banach space *X* is sequentially reflexive if and only if the [space](wikipedia://en/1701075#.E2.84.93p_spaces) of [absolutely summable sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_summability) [ℓ1](wikipedia://en/1701075#.E2.84.93p_spaces) is not isomorphic to a subspace of *X*.

### Problem-solving {#Problem-solving}

Between 1976 and 1982, Peter Orno contributed problems or solutions that appeared in eighteen issues of *[Mathematics Magazine](wikipedia://en/4230329)*, which is published by the Mathematical Association of America (MAA).[^o27] In 2006, Orno solved a problem in the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)*, another peer-reviewed journal of the MAA:

- Quet, L.; Ørno, P. (2006). "A continued fraction related to π (Problem 11102, 2004, p. 626)". *American Mathematical Monthly*. 113 (6). p. 572–573. doi:10.2307/27641994. jstor:27641994

## Context {#Context}

Peter Orno is one of several pseudonymous contributors in the field of mathematics. Other pseudonymous mathematicians active in the 20th century include [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394), [John Rainwater](wikipedia://en/31333155), [M. G. Stanley](wikipedia://en/31277386#Stanley), and [H. C. Enos](wikipedia://en/31277386#Stanley).[^o30]

## See also {#See_also}

Besides connoting "pornography", the name "Ørno" features a non-standard symbol:

- [∅](wikipedia://en/9566), which symbolizes the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) in mathematics.
- [Ø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Œ), an (archaic) English vowel, also denoted "OE", "Ö", and "Œ".
