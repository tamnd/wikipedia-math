# Manfred W. Padberg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Manfred W. Padberg"}

**Manfred Wilhelm Padberg** (10 October 1941 in [Bottrop, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottrop,_Germany)[^o3] – 12 May 2014) was a German mathematician who worked with [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_optimization) and [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555). He and [Ellis L. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellis_L._Johnson) won the [John von Neumann Theory Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann_Theory_Prize) in 2000.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Padberg grew up in Zagreb, Croatia, and Westphalia. From 1961 he studied mathematics at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster), where he graduated in 1967. In 1967, he was a research assistant at the [University of Mannheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mannheim). Starting in September 1968, he studied at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), where he obtained a master's degree and his Ph.D. (1971) in Industrial Administration. His advisor was [Egon Balas](wikipedia://en/35872667) and his dissertation was titled *Essays in Integer Programming*.

Afterwards he worked from 1971 to 1974 at the Berlin Science Center. From 1974, he was Associate Professor and, from 1978, Professor of Operations Research at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University). He became a professor emeritus in 2002. He moved to Paris in 2002.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

He, [Martin Shubik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Shubik), [Ellis L. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellis_L._Johnson), and Harlan Crowder won the [Frederick W. Lanchester Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_W._Lanchester_Prize) in 1983. He was elected to the 2002 class of [Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Institute for Operations Research and the Management Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences).[^o6]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He died in 2014 from cancer.[^o8]
