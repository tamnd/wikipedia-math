# Wilfrid Dixon

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wilfrid Dixon"}

**Wilfrid Joseph Dixon** (December 13, 1915 – September 20, 2008) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661). He made notable contributions to [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369), [statistical education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_education) and [experimental design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_design).

:::

A native of [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon), Dixon received a bachelor's degree in mathematics from [Oregon State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University) in 1938. He continued his graduate studies at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison), where he earned a master's degree in 1939. Under supervision of [Samuel S. Wilks](wikipedia://en/8136395), he then earned a Ph.D. in mathematical statistics from Princeton in 1944.[^o3] During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he was an operations analyst on [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam).

Dixon was on the faculties at [Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oklahoma) (1942–1943), [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon) (1946–1955), and [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) (1955–1986, then emeritus). While at Oregon, Dixon (together with [Alexander M. Mood](wikipedia://en/80708103)) described and provided theory and estimation methods for the adaptive [Up-and-Down experimental design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up-and-Down_Designs), which was new and poorly documented at the time.[^o4] This article became the cornerstone publication for up-and-down, a family of designs used in many scientific, engineering and medical fields, and to which Dixon continued to contribute in later years. In 1951 Dixon, together with [Frank Massey](wikipedia://en/80980182), published a statistics textbook - the first such textbook intended to a non-mathematical audience.[^o5] In 1955 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o6]

In the 1960s at UCLA, Dixon developed [BMDP](wikipedia://en/10030230), a statistical software package for biomedical analyses.[^o7]

His daughter, [Janet D. Elashoff](wikipedia://en/55618216), is also a statistician who became a UCLA faculty member, and was named an ASA fellow in 1978.[^o8] In December 2008 she funded the American Statistical Association's [W. J. Dixon Award for Excellence in Statistical Consulting](https://www.amstat.org/your-career/awards/w-j-dixon-award-for-excellence-in-statistical-consulting) in his honor.[^o9]
