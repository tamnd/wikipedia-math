# Restricted power series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Restricted power series"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **ring of restricted power series** is the [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of a [formal power series ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_power_series_ring) that consists of power series whose coefficients approach zero as degree goes to infinity.[^o2] Over a non-archimedean [complete field](wikipedia://en/21156575), the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) is also called a **Tate algebra**. [Quotient rings](wikipedia://en/184635) of the ring are used in the study of a [formal algebraic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_algebraic_space) as well as [rigid analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_analysis), the latter over non-archimedean complete fields.

:::

Over a [discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology) [topological ring](wikipedia://en/60097), the ring of restricted power series coincides with a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065); thus, in this sense, the notion of "restricted power series" is a generalization of a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *A* be a [linearly topologized ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_topologized_ring), separated and complete and $\{ I_{\lambda} \}$ the fundamental system of open ideals. Then the ring of restricted power series is defined as the [projective limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_limit) of the polynomial rings over $A/I_{\lambda}$:

$A \langle x_1, \dots, x_n \rangle = \varprojlim_{\lambda} A/I_{\lambda}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$.[^o5][^o6]

In other words, it is the [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28ring_theory%29) of the polynomial ring $A[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ with respect to the filtration $\{ I_{\lambda}[x_1, \dots, x_n] \}$. Sometimes this ring of restricted power series is also denoted by $A \{ x_1, \dots, x_n \}$.

Clearly, the ring $A \langle x_1, \dots, x_n \rangle$ can be identified with the subring of the formal power series ring $A[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$ that consists of series $\sum c_{\alpha} x^{\alpha}$ with coefficients $c_{\alpha} \to 0$; i.e., each $I_\lambda$ contains all but finitely many coefficients $c_{\alpha}$. Also, the ring satisfies (and in fact is characterized by) the [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245):[^o7] for (1) each [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) $A \to B$ to a linearly topologized ring $B$, separated and complete and (2) each elements $b_1, \dots, b_n$ in $B$, there exists a unique continuous ring homomorphism

$$A \langle x_1, \dots, x_n \rangle \to B, \, x_i \mapsto b_i$$

extending $A \to B$.

:::

## Tate algebra {#Tate_algebra}

In [rigid analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_analysis), when the base ring *A* is the [valuation ring](wikipedia://en/966856) of a complete non-archimedean field $(K, | \cdot |)$, the ring of restricted power series tensored with $K$,

$$T_n = K \langle \xi_1, \dots \xi_n \rangle = A \langle \xi_1, \dots, \xi_n \rangle \otimes_A K$$

is called a Tate algebra, named for [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933).[^o10] It is equivalently the subring of formal power series $k[[\xi_1, \dots, \xi_n]]$ which consists of series convergent on $\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{k}}^n$, where $\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{k}} := \{x \in \overline{k} : |x| \leq 1\}$ is the valuation ring in the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) $\overline{k}$.

The [maximal spectrum](wikipedia://en/38256214#S) of $T_n$ is then a [rigid-analytic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid-analytic_space) that models an affine space in [rigid geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_geometry).

Define the **Gauss norm** of $f = \sum a_{\alpha} \xi^{\alpha}$ in $T_n$ by

$$\|f\| = \max_{\alpha} |a_\alpha|.$$

This makes $T_n$ a [Banach algebra](wikipedia://en/4665) over *k*; i.e., a [normed algebra](wikipedia://en/1991870) that is [complete](wikipedia://en/7832) as a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018). With this [norm](wikipedia://en/990534), any [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) $I$ of $T_n$ is closed[^o11] and thus, if *I* is radical, the quotient $T_n/I$ is also a (reduced) Banach algebra called an [affinoid algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affinoid_algebra).

Some key results are:

- (Weierstrass division) Let $g \in T_n$ be a $\xi_n$-distinguished series of order *s*; i.e., $g = \sum_{\nu = 0}^{\infty} g_{\nu} \xi_n^{\nu}$ where $g_{\nu} \in T_{n-1}$, $g_s$ is a unit element and $| g_s | = \|g\| > |g_v |$ for $\nu > s$.[^o12] Then for each $f \in T_n$, there exist a unique $q \in T_n$ and a unique polynomial $r \in T_{n-1}[\xi_n]$ of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) $< s$ such that

$f = qg + r.$[^o13]

- ([Weierstrass preparation](wikipedia://en/372198)) As above, let $g$ be a $\xi_n$-distinguished series of order *s*. Then there exist a unique [monic polynomial](wikipedia://en/314730) $f \in T_{n-1}[\xi_n]$ of degree $s$ and a unit element $u \in T_n$ such that $g = f u$.[^o14]
- (Noether normalization) If $\mathfrak{a} \subset T_n$ is an ideal, then there is a finite homomorphism $T_d \hookrightarrow T_n/\mathfrak{a}$.[^o15]

As consequence of the division, preparation theorems and Noether normalization, $T_n$ is a [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) [unique factorization domain](wikipedia://en/60546) of [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) *n*.[^o16] An analog of [Hilbert's Nullstellensatz](wikipedia://en/149215) is valid: the radical of an ideal is the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of all [maximal ideals](wikipedia://en/48164) containing the ideal (we say the ring is Jacobson).[^o17]

:::

## Results {#Results}

Results for polynomial rings such as [Hensel's lemma](wikipedia://en/1633368), division algorithms (or the theory of [Gröbner bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gröbner_bases)) are also true for the ring of restricted power series. Throughout the section, let *A* denote a linearly topologized ring, separated and complete.

- (Hensel) Let $\mathfrak m \subset A$ be a maximal ideal and $\varphi : A \to k := A/\mathfrak{m}$ the quotient map. Given an $F$ in $A\langle \xi \rangle$, if $\varphi(F) = gh$ for some monic polynomial $g \in k[\xi]$ and a restricted power series $h \in k\langle \xi \rangle$ such that $g, h$ generate the [unit ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ideal) of $k \langle \xi \rangle$, then there exist $G$ in $A[\xi]$ and $H$ in $A\langle \xi \rangle$ such that

$F = G H, \, \varphi(G) = g, \varphi(H) = h$.[^o20]

:::
