# Kaniadakis logistic distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kaniadakis logistic distribution"}

The **Kaniadakis Logistic distribution** (also known as ***κ-*Logisticdistribution)** is a generalized version of the [Logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/542666) associated with the [Kaniadakis statistics](wikipedia://en/70596336). It is one example of a [Kaniadakis distribution](wikipedia://en/71322459). The κ-Logistic probability distribution describes the population kinetics behavior of bosonic ($0 < \lambda < 1$) or fermionic ($\lambda > 1$) character.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

### Probability density function {#Probability_density_function}

The Kaniadakis *κ*-Logistic distribution is a four-parameter family of [continuous statistical distributions](wikipedia://en/23543), which is part of a class of [statistical distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_distributions) emerging from the [Kaniadakis κ-statistics](wikipedia://en/70596336). This distribution has the following [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487):[^o5]

$$f_{_{\kappa}}(x) = \frac{\lambda \alpha \beta x^{\alpha-1}}{\sqrt{1+\kappa^2 \beta^2 x^{2\alpha} }} \frac{ \exp_\kappa(-\beta x^\alpha) }{ [ 1 + (\lambda - 1) \exp_\kappa(-\beta x^\alpha)]^2 }$$

valid for $x \geq 0$, where $0 \leq |\kappa| < 1$ is the entropic index associated with the [Kaniadakis entropy](wikipedia://en/70596336), $\beta > 0$ is the [rate parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_parameter), $\lambda > 0$, and $\alpha > 0$ is the shape parameter.

The [Logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/542666) is recovered as $\kappa \rightarrow 0.$

### Cumulative distribution function {#Cumulative_distribution_function}

The [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) of *κ*-Logistic is given by

$$F_\kappa(x) = \frac{ 1 - \exp_\kappa(-\beta x^\alpha) }{ 1 + (\lambda - 1) \exp_\kappa(-\beta x^\alpha) }$$

valid for $x \geq 0$. The cumulative Logistic distribution is recovered in the classical limit $\kappa \rightarrow 0$.

### Survival and hazard functions {#Survival_and_hazard_functions}

The survival distribution function of *κ*-Logistic distribution is given by

$$S_\kappa(x) = \frac{\lambda}{\exp_\kappa(\beta x^\alpha) + \lambda - 1}$$

valid for $x \geq 0$. The survival [Logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/542666) is recovered in the classical limit $\kappa \rightarrow 0$.

The hazard function associated with the *κ*-Logistic distribution is obtained by the solution of the following evolution equation:

> $$\frac{ S_\kappa(x) }{ dx } = -h_\kappa S_\kappa(x) \left( 1 - \frac{ \lambda -1 }{ \lambda } S_\kappa(x) \right)$$

with $S_\kappa(0) = 1$, where $h_\kappa$ is the hazard function:

$$h_\kappa = \frac{\alpha \beta x^{\alpha-1}}{\sqrt{1+\kappa^2 \beta^2 x^{2\alpha} }}$$

The cumulative Kaniadakis *κ*-Logistic distribution is related to the hazard function by the following expression:

$$S_\kappa = e^{-H_\kappa(x)}$$

where $H_\kappa (x) = \int_0^x h_\kappa(z) dz$ is the cumulative hazard function. The cumulative hazard function of the [Logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/542666) is recovered in the classical limit $\kappa \rightarrow 0$.

:::

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

- The survival function of the *κ*-Logistic distribution represents the *κ*-deformation of the Fermi-Dirac function, and becomes a [Fermi-Dirac distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi-Dirac_distribution) in the classical limit $\kappa \rightarrow 0$.[^o5]
- The *κ*-Logistic distribution is a generalization of the [*κ*-Weibull distribution](wikipedia://en/71231546) when $\lambda = 1$.
- A *κ*-Logistic distribution corresponds to a [Half-Logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/7035148) when $\lambda = 2$, $\alpha = 1$ and $\kappa = 0$.
- The ordinary Logistic distribution is a particular case of a *κ*-Logistic distribution, when $\kappa = 0$.

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The *κ*-Logistic distribution has been applied in several areas, such as:

- In [quantum statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_statistics), the survival function of the *κ*-Logistic distribution represents the most general expression of the Fermi-Dirac function, reducing to the [Fermi-Dirac distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi-Dirac_distribution) in the limit $\kappa \rightarrow 0$.[^o11][^o12][^o13]
