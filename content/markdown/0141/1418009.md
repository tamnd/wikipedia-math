# C. R. Rao

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="C. R. Rao"}

Calyampudi

**Prof. Calyampudi Radhakrishna Rao** FRS (10 September 1920 – 22 August 2023) was an Indian-American mathematician and statistician.[^o5] He was [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) and research professor at the [University at Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Buffalo). Rao was honoured by numerous colloquia, honorary degrees, and [festschrifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) and was awarded the US [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science) in 2002.[^o6] The [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) has described him as "a [living legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_legend_%28person%29)" whose work has influenced not just statistics, but has had far reaching implications for fields as varied as economics, genetics, anthropology, geology, national planning, demography, biometry, and medicine."[^o6] *[The Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_India)* listed Rao as one of the top 10 Indian scientists of all time.[^o7]

:::

In 2023, Rao was awarded the [International Prize in Statistics](wikipedia://en/52048719),[^o8] an award often touted as the "statistics' equivalent of the Nobel Prize".[^o9] Rao was also a Senior Policy and Statistics advisor for the [Indian Heart Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Heart_Association) non-profit focused on raising South Asian cardiovascular disease awareness.[^o10]

## Early life {#Early_life}

C. R. Rao was the eighth of the ten children born to a [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_people) family[^o12][^o37] in [Hoovina Hadagali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoovina_Hadagali), [Bellary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballari_district), [Madras Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras_Presidency), [Britain ruled India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) (now in [Vijayanagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagara_district), [Karnataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka), India). His schooling was completed in [Gudur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudur,_Nellore_district), [Nuzvid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuzvid), [Nandigama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandigama), and [Visakhapatnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visakhapatnam), all in the present state of [Andhra Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andhra_Pradesh).[^o3] He received an [MSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from [Andhra University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andhra_University) and an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in statistics from [Calcutta University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_University) in 1943.[^o6] He obtained a PhD degree at [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), under [R. A. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Fisher) in 1948, to which he added a [DSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science#The_Commonwealth_of_Nations_and_Ireland) degree, also from Cambridge, in 1965.[^o13]

## Career {#Career}

Rao first worked at the [Indian Statistical Institute](wikipedia://en/2238418) and the [Anthropological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Archaeology_and_Anthropology,_University_of_Cambridge) in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). Later he held several important positions, as the Director of the Indian Statistical Institute, Jawaharlal Nehru Professor and National Professor in India, University Professor at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) and Eberly Professor and Chair of Statistics and Director of the Center for Multivariate Analysis at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University).[^o15]

As Head and later Director of the Research and Training School at the [Indian Statistical Institute](wikipedia://en/2238418), India for a period of over 40 years, Rao developed research and training programs and produced several leaders in the field of Mathematics. On the basis of Rao's recommendation, the Asian Statistical Institute (ASI), now known as the Statistical Institute for Asia and Pacific, was established in Tokyo to provide training to statisticians working in government and industrial organisations.[^o16]

Among his best-known discoveries are the [Multivariate Analysis of Variance (MANOVA)](wikipedia://en/19384), Rao Distance,[^o17] [Rao's Score test](wikipedia://en/1599160),[^o18] *Rao's Quadratic Entropy*, [Cramér–Rao bound](wikipedia://en/581124) and the [Rao–Blackwell theorem](wikipedia://en/400010) these are related to the quality of [estimators](wikipedia://en/10043).[^o19] Majority of the publications were during his time period in [Indian Statistical Institute](wikipedia://en/2238418) in 60 years of his career. Other areas he worked in include [multivariate analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_analysis), [estimation theory](wikipedia://en/1565926), and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). His other contributions include the Fisher–Rao theorem, Rao distance, and orthogonal arrays. He was the author of 15 books[^o15] and authored over 400 journal publications.

Rao received 38 honorary doctoral degrees from universities in 19 countries around the world and numerous awards and medals for his contributions to statistics and science. He was a member of eight National Academies in India, the United Kingdom, the United States, and Italy. Rao was awarded [Santhi Swarup Bhatnagar Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanti_Swarup_Bhatnagar_Prize_for_Science_and_Technology) in 1963 and followed by [Padma Bhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bhushan) in 1968. Rao was awarded the United States [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science), that nation's highest award for lifetime achievement in fields of scientific research, in June 2002. He was given the [India Science Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Science_Award) in 2010, the highest honour conferred by the government of India in a scientific domain. He was most recently honoured with his 38th honorary doctorate by the Indian Institute of Technology, Kharagpur, on 26 July 2014 for "his contributions to the foundations of modern statistics through the introduction of concepts such as Cramér–Rao inequality, Rao–Blackwellization, Rao distance, Rao measure, and for introducing the idea of orthogonal arrays for the industry to design high-quality products."

Rao was the President of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute), Institute of Mathematical Statistics (USA), and the [International Biometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Biometric_Society). He was inducted into the Hall of Fame of India's National Institution for Quality and Reliability (Chennai Branch) for his contribution to industrial statistics and the promotion of quality control programs in industries.

The *Journal of Quantitative Economics* published a special issue in Rao's honour in 1991: "Dr. Rao is a very distinguished scientist and a highly eminent statistician of our time. His contributions to statistical theory and applications are well known, and many of his results, which bear his name, are included in the curriculum of courses in statistics at bachelor's and master's level all over the world. He was an inspiring teacher and has guided the research work of numerous students in all areas of statistics. His early work had greatly influenced the course of statistical research during the last four decades. One of the purposes of this special issue is to recognise Dr. Rao's own contributions to econometrics and acknowledge his major role in the development of econometric research in India."

## Death {#Death}

Rao died in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York) on 22 August 2023, aged 102.[^o22]
