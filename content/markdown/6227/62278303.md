# Gregory Beylkin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gregory Beylkin"}

**Gregory Beylkin** (born 16 March 1953) is a Russian–American mathematician.[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

He studied from 1970 to 1975 at the [University of Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leningrad), with Diploma in Mathematics in November 1975. From 1976 to 1979 he was a research scientist at the Research Institute of Ore Geophysics, Leningrad. From 1980 to 1982 he was a graduate student at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University),[^o4] where he received his PhD under the supervision of [Peter Lax](wikipedia://en/486365). From 1982 to 1983 Beylkin was an associate research scientist at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences). From 1983 to 1991 he was a member of the professional staff of [Schlumberger-Doll Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlumberger#History) in [Ridgefield, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridgefield,_Connecticut). Since 1991 he has been a professor in the Department of Applied Mathematics at the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder). He was a visiting professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), and the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452) and participated in 2012 and 2015 in the summer seminar on "Applied Harmonic Analysis and Sparse Approximation" at Oberwolfach. He is the author or co-author of over 100 articles in refereed journal and has served on several editorial boards.[^o4]

Gregory Beylkin's research is focused on analysis and development of fast algorithms for solving integral and differential equations. Applications include [quantum chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chemistry), [gravity field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_field) evaluation and estimation, [wave propagation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_propagation) and [inverse problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_problems). A number of algorithms developed by Gregory Beylkin and his group have been implemented and are used in practical applications.[^o2]
