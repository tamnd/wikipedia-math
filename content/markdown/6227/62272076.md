# Mikhail Kapranov

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mikhail Kapranov"}

**Mikhail Kapranov**, (Михаил Михайлович Капранов, born 1962) is a Russian mathematician, specializing in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), and [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869). He is currently a professor of the [Kavli Institute for the Physics and Mathematics of the Universe](wikipedia://en/34880544) at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo).

:::

Kapranov graduated from [Lomonosov University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov_University) in 1982 and received his doctorate in 1988 under the supervision of [Yuri Manin](wikipedia://en/1692086) at the [Steklov Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Institute) in Moscow. Afterwards he worked at the Steklov Institute and from 1990 to 1991 at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). At [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) he was from 1991 to 1993 an assistant professor, from 1993 to 1995 an associate professor, and from 1995 to 1999 a full professor. He was from 1999 to 2003 a professor at [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) and from 2003 to 2014 a professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). In 1993 he was a [Sloan Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellow). From fall 2018 to spring 2019 he was a visiting professor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o3]

From 1989 to 1990 he collaborated with [Vladimir Voevodsky](wikipedia://en/104482) on $\infty$-groupoids, following the proposal made by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) in *[Esquisse d'un Programme](wikipedia://en/19575137)*. In 1990 Voevodsky and Kapranov published “$\infty$-Groupoids as a Model for a Homotopy Category”,[^o4] in which they claimed to provide a rigorous mathematical formulation and a logically valid proof of Grothendieck's idea connecting two classes of mathematical objects: $\infty$-groupoids and homotopy types. In October 1998, [Carlos Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Simpson) published on [arXiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv) the article “Homotopy Types of Strict 3-groupoids”,[^o5] which argued that the main result of the “$\infty$-groupoids” paper, published by Kapranov and Voevodsky in 1990, is false. It was not until 2013 Voevodsky convinced himself that Carlos Simpson's article is correct.[^o6] Kapranov was also involved in the beginning of Voevodsky's program for the development of motivic cohomology.

With [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643) and [Andrei Zelevinsky](wikipedia://en/32415867), Kapranov investigated generalized Euler integrals, $A$-hypergeometric functions, $A$-discriminants, and hyperdeterminants, and authored *Discriminants, Resultants, and Multidimensional Determinants* in 1994.[^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10]

According to Gelfand, Kapranov, and Zelevinsky: ... in an 1848 note on the resultant, [Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) ... laid out the foundations of homological algebra. The place of discriminants in the general theory of hypergeometric functions is similar to the place of quasi-classical approximation in quantum mechanics. ... The relation between differential operators and their highest symbols is the mathematical counterpart of the relation between quantum and classical mechanics; so we can say that hypergeometric functions provide a "quantization" of discriminants.[^o11]

In 1995 Kapranov provided a framework for a [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905) for higher-dimensional schemes,[^o12] and with, [Victor Ginzburg](wikipedia://en/14995490) and Éric Vasserot, extended the "Geometric Langlands Conjecture" from algebraic curves to algebraic surfaces.

In 1998 Kapranov was an Invited Speaker with talk *Operads and Algebraic Geometry* at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o13]
