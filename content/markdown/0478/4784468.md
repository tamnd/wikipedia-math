# Werner Fenchel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Werner Fenchel"}

**Moritz Werner Fenchel** (; 3 May 1905 – 24 January 1988) was a German-Danish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his contributions to [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and to [optimization theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29). Fenchel established the basic results of [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201) and nonlinear optimization theory which would, in time, serve as the foundation for [nonlinear programming](wikipedia://en/679709). A German-born Jew and early refugee from Nazi suppression of intellectuals, Fenchel lived most of his life in Denmark. Fenchel's monographs and lecture notes are considered influential.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Fenchel was born on 3 May 1905 in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), Germany,[^o5] his younger brother was the Israeli film director and architect [Heinz Fenchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Fenchel).

Fenchel studied mathematics and physics at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) between 1923 and 1928.[^o5] He wrote his doctorate thesis in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) (*Über Krümmung und Windung geschlossener Raumkurven*)[^o6] under [Ludwig Bieberbach](wikipedia://en/616186).[^o5]

### Professorship in Germany {#Professorship_in_Germany}

From 1928 to 1933, Fenchel was Professor [E. Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._Landau)'s Assistant at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). During a one-year leave (on [Rockefeller Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Fellowship)) between 1930 and 1931, Fenchel spent time in Rome with [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180), as well as in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen) with [Harald Bohr](wikipedia://en/638305) and [Tommy Bonnesen](wikipedia://en/50669027). He visited Denmark again in 1932.[^o5]

### Professorship in exile {#Professorship_in_exile}

Fenchel taught at Göttingen until 1933, when the [Nazi discrimination laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_for_the_Restoration_of_the_Professional_Civil_Service) led to [mass-firings of Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_policy_of_Nazi_Germany).[^o9]

Fenchel emigrated to Denmark somewhere between April and September 1933, ultimately obtaining a position at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen). In December 1933, Fenchel married fellow German refugee mathematician [Käte Sperling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Käte_Sperling).[^o5]

When [Germany occupied Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Denmark), Fenchel and roughly eight-thousand other Danish Jews [received refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_of_the_Danish_Jews) in Sweden, where he taught (between 1943 and 1945) at the Danish School in [Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund).[^o5] After the Allied powers' [liberation of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Denmark#Hardship_and_the_end_of_the_war), Fenchel returned to Copenhagen.

### Professorship postwar {#Professorship_postwar}

In 1946, Fenchel was elected a member of the [Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters).[^o5]

On leave between 1949 and 1951, Fenchel taught in the U.S. at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o5]

From 1952 to 1956 Fenchel was the professor in mechanics at the Polytechnic in Copenhagen.[^o5]

From 1956 to 1974 he was the professor in mathematics at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen).[^o5]

### Last years, death, legacy {#Last_years,_death,_legacy}

Professor Fenchel died on 24 January 1988.[^o5]

## Geometric contributions {#Geometric_contributions}

### Convex geometry {#Convex_geometry}

### Optimization theory {#Optimization_theory}

Fenchel lectured on "Convex Sets, Cones, and Functions" at Princeton University in the early 1950s. His lecture notes shaped the field of [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201), according to the monograph *Convex Analysis* of [R. T. Rockafellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._T._Rockafellar).
