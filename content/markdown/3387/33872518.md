# De Donder–Weyl theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="De Donder–Weyl theory"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), the **De Donder–Weyl theory** is a generalization of the [Hamiltonian formalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_formalism) in the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) and [classical field theory](wikipedia://en/1293340) over [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) which treats the space and time coordinates on equal footing. In this framework, the [Hamiltonian formalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_formalism) in [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics) is generalized to field theory in the way that a [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29) is represented as a system that varies both in space and in time. This generalization is different from the [canonical](wikipedia://en/515096) [Hamiltonian formalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_formalism) in field theory which treats space and time variables differently and describes classical fields as infinite-dimensional systems evolving in time.

## De Donder–Weyl formulation of field theory {#De_Donder–Weyl_formulation_of_field_theory}

The De Donder–Weyl theory is based on a change of variables known as [Legendre transformation](wikipedia://en/390273). Let *x<sup>i</sup>* be [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) coordinates, for *i* = 1 to *n* (with *n* = 4 representing 3 + 1 dimensions of space and time), and *y<sup>a</sup>* field variables, for *a* = 1 to *m*, and *L* the [Lagrangian density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_density)

$$L = L(y^{a},\partial_i y^{a},x^{i})$$

With the **polymomenta** *p<sup>i</sup><sub>a</sub>* defined as

$$p^{i}_a = \partial L / \partial (\partial_i y^{a})$$

and the **De Donder–Weyl Hamiltonian function** *H* defined as

$$H = p^{i}_a \partial_i y^{a} - L$$

the **De Donder–Weyl equations** are:[^o3]

$$\partial p^{i}_a / \partial x^{i} = -\partial H / \partial y^{a} \, , \, \partial y^{a} / \partial x^{i} = \partial H / \partial p^{i}_a$$

This De Donder-Weyl Hamiltonian form of field equations is [covariant](wikipedia://en/202886) and it is equivalent to the [Euler-Lagrange equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler-Lagrange_equations) when the Legendre transformation to the variables *p<sup>i</sup><sub>a</sub>* and *H* is not singular. The theory is a formulation of a [covariant Hamiltonian field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariant_Hamiltonian_field_theory) which is different from the [canonical](wikipedia://en/515096) [Hamiltonian formalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_formalism) and for *n* = 1 it reduces to [Hamiltonian mechanics](wikipedia://en/198319) (see also [action principle in the calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882#Action_principle)).

[Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) in 1935 has developed the [Hamilton-Jacobi theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton-Jacobi_theory) for the De Donder–Weyl theory.[^o4]

Similarly to the [Hamiltonian formalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_formalism) in mechanics formulated using the [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298) of [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) the De Donder-Weyl theory can be formulated using the [multisymplectic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multisymplectic_geometry) or [polysymplectic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysymplectic_geometry) and the geometry of [jet bundles](wikipedia://en/928060).

A generalization of the [Poisson brackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_brackets) to the De Donder–Weyl theory and the representation of De Donder–Weyl equations in terms of generalized [Poisson brackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_brackets) satisfying the [Gerstenhaber algebra](wikipedia://en/883034) was found by Kanatchikov in 1993.[^o5]

## History {#History}

The formalism, now known as De Donder–Weyl (DW) theory, was developed by [Théophile De Donder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Théophile_De_Donder)[^o7][^o8] and [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544). Hermann Weyl made his proposal in 1934 being inspired by the work of [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625), which in turn was founded on the work of [Vito Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Volterra). The work of De Donder on the other hand started from the theory of integral [invariants](wikipedia://en/363762) of [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274).[^o9] The De Donder–Weyl theory has been a part of the calculus of variations since the 1930s and initially it found very few applications in physics. Recently it was applied in theoretical physics in the context of [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267)[^o10] and [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312).[^o11]

In 1970, Jedrzej Śniatycki, the author of *Geometric quantization and quantum mechanics*, developed an invariant geometrical formulation of [jet bundles](wikipedia://en/928060), building on the work of De Donder and Weyl.[^o12] In 1999 Igor Kanatchikov has shown that the De Donder–Weyl covariant Hamiltonian field equations can be formulated in terms of [Duffin–Kemmer–Petiau matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duffin–Kemmer–Petiau_algebra).[^o13]
