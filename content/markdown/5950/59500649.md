# Simple Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simple Lie algebra"}

In algebra, a **simple Lie algebra** is a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) that is [non-abelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_Lie_algebra) and contains no nonzero proper [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977). The classification of [real simple Lie algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_simple_Lie_algebra) is one of the major achievements of [Wilhelm Killing](wikipedia://en/1531824) and [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274).

:::

Over a field of characteristic 0, a direct sum of simple Lie algebras is called a [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587).

A [simple Lie group](wikipedia://en/292831) is a connected [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) whose Lie algebra is simple.

## Complex simple Lie algebras {#Complex_simple_Lie_algebras}

A finite-dimensional simple [complex Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/62595897) is isomorphic to either of the following: $\mathfrak{sl}_n \mathbb{C}$, $\mathfrak{so}_n \mathbb{C}$, $\mathfrak{sp}_{2n} \mathbb{C}$ ([classical Lie algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Lie_algebra)) or one of the five [exceptional Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/6573043).[^o3]

To each finite-dimensional complex [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587) $\mathfrak{g}$, there exists a corresponding diagram (called the [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109)) where the nodes denote the simple roots, the nodes are jointed (or not jointed) by a number of lines depending on the angles between the simple roots and the arrows are put to indicate whether the roots are longer or shorter.[^o4] The Dynkin diagram of $\mathfrak{g}$ is connected if and only if $\mathfrak{g}$ is simple. All possible connected Dynkin diagrams are the following:[^o5]

where *n* is the number of the nodes (the simple roots). The correspondence of the diagrams and complex simple Lie algebras is as follows:[^o4]

(A<sub>*n*</sub>) $\quad \mathfrak{sl}_{n+1} \mathbb{C}$

(B<sub>*n*</sub>) $\quad \mathfrak{so}_{2n+1} \mathbb{C}$

(C<sub>*n*</sub>) $\quad \mathfrak{sp}_{2n} \mathbb{C}$

(D<sub>*n*</sub>) $\quad \mathfrak{so}_{2n} \mathbb{C}$

The rest, [exceptional Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/6573043).

## Real simple Lie algebras {#Real_simple_Lie_algebras}

If $\mathfrak{g}_0$ is a finite-dimensional real simple Lie algebra, its complexification is either (1) simple or (2) a product of a simple complex Lie algebra and its [conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_of_a_complex_Lie_algebra). For example, the complexification of $\mathfrak{sl}_n \mathbb{C}$ thought of as a real Lie algebra is $\mathfrak{sl}_n \mathbb{C} \times \overline{\mathfrak{sl}_n \mathbb{C}}$. Thus, a real simple Lie algebra can be classified by the classification of complex simple Lie algebras and some additional information. This can be done by [Satake diagrams](wikipedia://en/20514575) that generalize [Dynkin diagrams](wikipedia://en/277109). See also [Table of Lie groups#Real Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1387689#Real_Lie_algebras) for a partial list of real simple Lie algebras.
