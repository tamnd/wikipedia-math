# Bell-shaped function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bell-shaped function"}

*Image: Normal\_Distribution\_PDF.svg.* The [Gaussian function](wikipedia://en/245552) is the archetypal example of a bell shaped function

A **bell-shaped function** or simply 'bell curve' is a [mathematical function](wikipedia://en/185427) having a characteristic "[bell](wikipedia://en/761242)"-shaped curve. These functions are typically continuous or smooth, asymptotically approach zero for large negative/positive x, and have a single, unimodal maximum at small x. Hence, the integral of a bell-shaped function is typically a [sigmoid function](wikipedia://en/87210). Bell shaped functions are also commonly symmetric.

:::

Many common probability distribution functions are bell curves.

Some bell shaped functions, such as the [Gaussian function](wikipedia://en/245552) and the probability distribution of the [Cauchy distribution](wikipedia://en/7003), can be used to construct sequences of functions with decreasing [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) that approach the [Dirac delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_delta) distribution.[^o2] Indeed, the Dirac delta can roughly be thought of as a bell curve with variance tending to zero.

Some examples include:

- [Gaussian function](wikipedia://en/245552), the probability density function of the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462). This is the archetypal bell shaped function and is frequently encountered in nature as a consequence of the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406).

$$f(x) = a e^{-(x-b)^2/(2c^2)}$$

- [Fuzzy Logic](wikipedia://en/49180) generalized membership bell-shaped function[^o3][^o4]

$$f(x) =\frac 1 {1+\left|\frac{x-c} a \right|^{2b}}$$

- [Hyperbolic secant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_function). This is also the derivative of the [Gudermannian function](wikipedia://en/303274).

$$f(x) = \operatorname{sech}(x)=\frac{2}{e^x+e^{-x}}$$

- [Witch of Agnesi](wikipedia://en/407764), the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) of the [Cauchy distribution](wikipedia://en/7003). This is also a scaled version of the derivative of the [arctangent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctangent) function.

$$f(x) = \frac{8a^3}{x^2+4a^2}$$

- [Bump function](wikipedia://en/815799)

$$\varphi_b(x)=\begin{cases}\exp\frac{b^2}{x^2-b^2} & |x|<b, \\0 & |x|\geq b.\end{cases}$$

- Raised cosines type like the [raised cosine distribution](wikipedia://en/2655605) or the [raised-cosine filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raised-cosine_filter)

$$f(x;\mu,s) = \begin{cases} \frac 1 {2s} \left[ 1 +\cos\left(\frac{x-\mu}s \pi\right)\right] & \text{for }\mu-s \le x \le \mu+s, \\[3pt] 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

- Most of the [window functions](wikipedia://en/244097#Examples_of_window_functions) like the [Kaiser window](wikipedia://en/236782)
- The derivative of the [logistic function](wikipedia://en/84563). This is a scaled version of the derivative of the [hyperbolic tangent function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_tangent_function).

$$f(x)=\frac{e^x}{\left(1+e^x\right)^2}$$

- Some [algebraic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_functions). For example

$$f(x)=\frac{1}{(1+x^2)^{3/2}}$$

- Some [logarithmic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_functions). For example

$$f(x)=\log{\frac {x^2 + e} {x^2 + 1} }.$$
