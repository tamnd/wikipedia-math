# Splitter (geometry)

*Image: Extouch Triangle and Nagel Point.svg.* solid #333333 solid orange solid red solid #1e90ff

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Splitter"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), a **splitter** is a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) through one of the [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) (that is, a [cevian](wikipedia://en/6135790)) that [bisects the perimeter](wikipedia://en/152547#Area_bisectors_and_perimeter_bisectors_of_a_triangle) of the triangle.[^o2][^o3] They are not to be confused with [cleavers](wikipedia://en/14925692), which also bisect the perimeter but instead emanate from the [midpoint](wikipedia://en/437979) of one of the triangle's sides.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The opposite endpoint of a splitter to the chosen triangle vertex lies at the point on the triangle's side where one of the [excircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) of the triangle is tangent to that side.[^o2][^o3] This point is also called a **splitting point** of the triangle.[^o3] It is additionally a vertex of the [extouch triangle](wikipedia://en/11125142) and one of the points where the [Mandart inellipse](wikipedia://en/14974602) is tangent to the triangle side.[^o6]

The three splitters [concur](wikipedia://en/2206157) at the [Nagel point](wikipedia://en/9489914) of the triangle,[^o2] which is also called its splitting center.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

Some authors have used the term "splitter" in a more general sense, for any line segment that bisects the perimeter of the triangle. Other line segments of this type include the [cleavers](wikipedia://en/14925692), which are perimeter-bisecting segments that pass through the midpoint of a triangle side, and the equalizers, segments that bisect both the area and perimeter of a triangle.[^o9]

:::
