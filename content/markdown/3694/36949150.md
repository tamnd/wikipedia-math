# Gennady Belyi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gennady Belyi"}

**Gennadii Vladimirovich Belyi** (1951–2001, Генадій Володимирович Білий, Геннадий Владимирович Белый) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_people), and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_people) mathematician, known for [Belyi's theorem](wikipedia://en/3144280) on the representation of [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) as [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) and for the Belyi functions arising in that theorem.

:::

Belyi was born on February 2, 1951, in [Magnitogorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitogorsk), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia), then part of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). His family moved from there to [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine), and he began his studies at the [Kiev Physics and Mathematics School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Physics_and_Mathematics_Lyceum) but moved from there to [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University). After completing his studies in 1973 he returned to Ukraine, working in [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev) and then [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv). He became a graduate student at the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494) in Moscow in 1975, and studied there under the supervision of [Igor Shafarevich](wikipedia://en/1091815), earning a candidate degree in 1979. He then took a faculty position at [Vladimir State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_State_University), in [Vladimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir,_Russia), Russia, where he remained for the remainder of his career. He died on January 29, 2001, in Vladimir.[^o3][^o4]

Belyi won a prize of the [Moscow Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/9098251) in 1981, and was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 1986.[^o3]
