# Shinichi Mochizuki

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shinichi Mochizuki"}

**Shinichi Mochizuki** is a Japanese mathematician working in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177). He is one of the main contributors to [anabelian geometry](wikipedia://en/27119265). His contributions include his solution of the [Grothendieck conjecture in anabelian geometry](wikipedia://en/27119265#Formulation_of_a_conjecture_of_Grothendieck_on_curves) about hyperbolic curves over number fields. Mochizuki has also worked in [Hodge–Arakelov theory](wikipedia://en/37007490) and [*p*-adic Teichmüller theory](wikipedia://en/36974047). Mochizuki developed [inter-universal Teichmüller theory](wikipedia://en/36968172),[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7] which has attracted attention from non-mathematicians due to claims it provides a resolution of the [*abc* conjecture](wikipedia://en/174108).[^n1]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life {#Early_life}

Shinichi Mochizuki was born to parents Kiichi and Anne Mochizuki.[^o11] When he was five years old, Shinichi Mochizuki and his family left Japan to live in the United States. His father was Fellow of the Center for International Affairs and Center for Middle Eastern Studies at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) (1974–76).[^o12] Mochizuki attended [Phillips Exeter Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Exeter_Academy) and graduated in 1985.[^o13]

Mochizuki entered [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) as an undergraduate student at the age of 16 and graduated as [salutatorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salutatorian) with an A.B. in mathematics in 1988.[^o13] He completed his senior thesis, titled "Curves and their deformations," under the supervision of [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226).[^o14]

He remained at Princeton for graduate studies and received his Ph.D. in mathematics in 1992 after completing his doctoral dissertation, titled "The geometry of the compactification of the [Hurwitz scheme](wikipedia://en/60559003)," also under the supervision of Faltings.[^o15]

After his graduate studies, Mochizuki spent two years at Harvard University and then in 1994 moved back to Japan to join the [Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/1978962) (RIMS) at [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University), and was promoted to professor in 2002.[^o3][^n2]

### Career {#Career}

Mochizuki proved [Grothendieck's conjecture on anabelian geometry](wikipedia://en/27119265#Formulation_of_a_conjecture_of_Grothendieck_on_curves) in 1996. He was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 1998.[^o18] In 2000–2008, he discovered several new theories including the theory of [frobenioids](wikipedia://en/36985868), mono-anabelian geometry and the etale theta theory for line bundles over tempered covers of the [Tate curve](wikipedia://en/35189927).

On August 30, 2012, Mochizuki released four preprints, whose total size was about 500 pages, that developed [inter-universal Teichmüller theory](wikipedia://en/36968172) and applied it in an attempt to prove several very famous problems in [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205).[^o19] These include the strong [Szpiro conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szpiro_conjecture), the hyperbolic [Vojta conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vojta_conjecture) and the [*abc* conjecture](wikipedia://en/174108) over every number field. In September 2018, Mochizuki posted a report on his work by [Peter Scholze](wikipedia://en/39409306) and [Jakob Stix](wikipedia://en/58556057), which asserted that the third preprint contains an irreparable flaw; he also posted several documents containing his rebuttal of their criticism.[^o20] The majority of number theorists have found Mochizuki's preprints very difficult to follow and have not accepted the conjectures as settled, although there are a few prominent exceptions, including Go Yamashita, [Ivan Fesenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Fesenko), and Yuichiro Hoshi, who vouch for the work and have written expositions of the theory.[^o21][^o22]

On April 3, 2020, two Japanese mathematicians, [Masaki Kashiwara](wikipedia://en/4376209) and Akio Tamagawa, announced that Mochizuki's claimed proof of the *abc* conjecture would be published in *Publications of the Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences*, a journal of which Mochizuki is chief editor.[^o23] The announcement was received with skepticism by [Kiran Kedlaya](wikipedia://en/31688876) and [Edward Frenkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Frenkel), as well as being described by [*Nature*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29) as "unlikely to move many researchers over to Mochizuki's camp".[^o23] The special issue containing Mochizuki's articles was published on March 5, 2021.[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]
