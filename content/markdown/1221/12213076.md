# Abel's inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abel's inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Abel's inequality**, named after [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573), supplies a simple bound on the absolute value of the [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) of two vectors in an important special case.

:::

## Mathematical description {#Mathematical_description}

Let {*a<sub>1</sub>, a<sub>2</sub>,...*} be a sequence of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) that is either nonincreasing or nondecreasing, and let {*b<sub>1</sub>, b<sub>2</sub>,...*} be a sequence of real or [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). If {*a<sub>n</sub>*} is nondecreasing, it holds that

$$\left |\sum_{k=1}^n a_k b_k \right | \le \operatorname{max}_{k=1,\dots,n} |B_k| (|a_n| + a_n - a_1),$$

and if {*a<sub>n</sub>*} is nonincreasing, it holds that

$$\left |\sum_{k=1}^n a_k b_k \right | \le \operatorname{max}_{k=1,\dots,n} |B_k| (|a_n| - a_n + a_1),$$

where

$$B_k =b_1+\cdots+b_k.$$

In particular, if the sequence {*a*<sub>n</sub>} is nonincreasing and nonnegative, it follows that

$$\left |\sum_{k=1}^n a_k b_k \right | \le \operatorname{max}_{k=1,\dots,n} |B_k| a_1,$$

## Relation to [Abel's transformation](wikipedia://en/246188) {#Relation_to_Abel's_transformation}

Abel's inequality follows easily from Abel's transformation, which is the discrete version of [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252): If {*a*<sub>1</sub>, *a*<sub>2</sub>, ...} and {*b*<sub>1</sub>, *b*<sub>2</sub>, ...} are sequences of real or complex numbers, it holds that

$$\sum_{k=1}^n a_k b_k = a_n B_n - \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} B_k (a_{k+1} - a_k).$$
