# Seven basic tools of quality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Seven basic tools of quality"}

The **seven basic tools of quality** are a fixed set of visual exercises identified as being most helpful in [troubleshooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troubleshooting) issues related to [quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_%28business%29).[^n1] They are called *basic* because they are suitable for people with little formal training in statistics and because they can be used to solve the vast majority of quality-related issues.[^n2]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The seven tools are:[^n3][^o6][^n4]

1. [Cause-and-effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishikawa_diagram) diagram (also known as the "fishbone diagram" or Ishikawa diagram)
1. [Check sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Check_sheet)
1. [Control chart](wikipedia://en/435754)
1. [Histogram](wikipedia://en/13266)
1. [Pareto chart](wikipedia://en/4060171)
1. [Scatter diagram](wikipedia://en/412544)
1. [Stratification](wikipedia://en/27596) (alternatively, [flow chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_chart) or [run chart](wikipedia://en/6392749))

The designation arose in [postwar Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postwar_Japan), inspired by the [seven famous weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benkei#Seven_weapons) of [Benkei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benkei).[^n5] It was possibly introduced by [Kaoru Ishikawa](wikipedia://en/458907) who in turn was influenced by a series of lectures [W. Edwards Deming](wikipedia://en/70534) had given to Japanese engineers and scientists in 1950.[^o9] At that time, companies that had set about training their workforces in [statistical quality control](wikipedia://en/41613) found that the complexity of the subject intimidated most of their workers and scaled back training to focus primarily on simpler methods which suffice for most quality-related issues.[^n6] The [Project Management Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Management_Institute) references the seven basic tools in *[A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Guide_to_the_Project_Management_Body_of_Knowledge)* as an example of a set of general tools useful for planning or controlling project quality.[^n7]

The seven basic tools stand in contrast to more advanced statistical methods such as [survey sampling](wikipedia://en/27578), [acceptance sampling](wikipedia://en/25719356), [statistical hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing), [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541), [multivariate analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_analysis), and various methods developed in the field of [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476).[^n8]
