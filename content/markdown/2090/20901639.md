# Marcin Śmiglecki

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marcin Śmiglecki"}

**Marcin Śmiglecki** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin): *Martinus Smiglecius* or *Leopolitanus,* Martynas Smigleckis;[^o2] 11 November 1564 – 26 or 28 July 1618) was a Polish [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) philosopher and logician,[^o3][^o4] known for his erudite [scholastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism) *Logica*.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born on 11 November 1564[^o6] in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów) (Leopolis) in the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_%281385–1569%29) (now Lviv, [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)). He used the surname Lwowczyk, or Leopolitanus, and later adopted the name Śmiglecki or Smiglecius (after the town of [Śmigiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Śmigiel), from which his family originated). He attended the Jesuit school in [Pułtusk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pułtusk) and until 1586 studied in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome), where he joined the Jesuit order in 1581. His education was financed by the prominent Polish statesman [Jan Zamojski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Zamojski). He obtained a master's degree in philosophy and a doctor's degree in theology at the [Academy of Vilnius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_University#Polish–Lithuanian_Commonwealth), and taught philosophy and theology there.[^o8]

In 1599 he took part in a public disputation with the Protestants [Marcin Janicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcin_Janicki) and [Daniel Mikołajewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Mikołajewski_%28theologian%29). It was recorded by Martin Gratian Gertich.[^o7]

He spent the last 20 years of his life teaching in the colleges of Pułtusk, [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań), [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków) and [Kalisz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalisz). He died in Kalisz on 26 or 28 July 1618.[^o8][^o6]

## The *Logica* {#The_Logica}

Marcin Śmiglecki's "Logica", first published in 1618 in [Ingolstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingolstadt), was reprinted several times, in particular at [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) in 1634, 1638, and 1658,[^o8] being used there as a textbook.[^o12] It harked back to [Gregory of Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_of_Rimini), discussing [mental propositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_proposition).[^o13] As a textbook author his reputation survived in the satirical poem *The Logicians Refuted*,[^o14] attributed to both [Jonathan Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Swift) and [Oliver Goldsmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Goldsmith). [Samuel Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Johnson), writing in 1751 as a fictitious correspondent in *[The Rambler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rambler)*, claimed that as a student he "slept every night with Śmiglecki on my pillow."[^o15]

## Views {#Views}

In a live controversy of the time, Śmiglecki sided with [Benedict Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_Pereira) against [Giuseppe Biancani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Biancani). The issue was the status of [mathematical proof](wikipedia://en/82285) in physics, where Pereyra denied mathematics an essential status.[^o17]
