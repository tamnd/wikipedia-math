# Axial current

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Axial current"}

In [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics), the **axial current**, also denoted the *pseudo-vector* or *chiral* current, is the [conserved current](wikipedia://en/1097925) associated to the [chiral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral_symmetry) or [axial symmetry](wikipedia://en/6025673) of a system.

:::

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Axial charge"}

## Origin {#Origin}

According to [Noether's theorem](wikipedia://en/150159), each [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) of a system is associated a conserved quantity.[^o4][^o5] For example, the [rotational invariance](wikipedia://en/424202) of a system implies the conservation of its [angular momentum](wikipedia://en/2839), or spacetime invariance implies the conservation of energy–momentum. In [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), [internal symmetries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_symmetries) also result in conserved quantities. For example, the [U(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%281%29) [gauge invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_invariance) of [QED](wikipedia://en/25268) implies the conservation of the [electric charge](wikipedia://en/9804). Likewise, if a theory possesses an internal chiral or axial symmetry, there will be a conserved quantity, which is called the ***axial charge***. Further, just as the motion of an electrically charged particle produces an [electric current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_current), a moving axial charge constitutes an axial current.

:::

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The axial current resulting from the motion of an axially charged moving particle is formally defined as $j_5^\mu = \overline\psi\gamma^5\gamma^\mu\psi$, where $\psi$ is the [particle field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29) represented by [Dirac spinor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_spinor) (since the particle is typically a [spin](wikipedia://en/19593829)-1/2 [fermion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion)) and $\gamma^5$ and $\gamma^\mu$ are the [Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac) [gamma matrices](wikipedia://en/2907966).[^o8]

For comparison, the electromagnetic current produced by an electrically charged moving particle is $j^\mu = \overline\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$.

:::

## Meaning {#Meaning}

As explained above, the axial current is simply the equivalent of the electromagnetic current for the axial symmetry instead of the U(1) symmetry. Another perspective is given by recalling that the [chiral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral_symmetry) is the [invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_%28physics%29) of the theory under the field rotation $\psi_{\rm L}\rightarrow e^{i\theta_{\rm L}}\psi_{\rm L}$ and $\psi_{\rm R}\rightarrow \psi_{\rm R}$ (or alternatively $\psi_{\rm L}\rightarrow \psi_{\rm L}$ and $\psi_{\rm R}\rightarrow e^{i\theta_{\rm R}}\psi_{\rm R}$), where $\psi_{\rm L}$ denotes a [left-handed field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicity_%28particle_physics%29) and $\psi_{\rm R}$ a right-handed one. From this as well as the fact that $\psi=\psi_{\rm L}+\psi_{\rm R}$ and the definition of $j_5^\mu$ above, one sees that the axial current is the difference between the current due to left-handed fermions and that from right-handed ones, whilst the electromagnetic current is the sum.

Chiral symmetry is exhibited by vector [gauge theories](wikipedia://en/23912155) with massless fermions. Since there is no known massless fermion in nature, chiral symmetry is at best an approximate symmetry in fundamental theories, and the axial current is not conserved. (Note: this [explicit breaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explicit_symmetry_breaking) of the chiral symmetry by non-zero masses is not to be confused with the [spontaneous](wikipedia://en/297466) [chiral symmetry breaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral_symmetry_breaking) that plays a dominant role in [hadronic physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadron).) An important consequence of such non-conservation is the neutral [pion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pion) [decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_decay) and the [chiral anomaly](wikipedia://en/312229),[^o10] which is directly related to the pion [decay width](wikipedia://en/1725518).[^o11][^o12]

::: {#o14 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The axial current is an important part of the formalism describing [high-energy scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-energy_physics) reactions. In such reaction, two particles scatter off each other by exchanging a force [boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_boson), e.g., a [photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon) for electromagnetic scattering (see the figure).

*Image: Electron-scattering.png.* A [Feynman diagram](wikipedia://en/11617) of scattering between two electrons by emission of a virtual [photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon).

The [cross-section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_section_%28physics%29) for such reaction is proportional to the square of the [scattering amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_amplitude), which in turn is given by the product of boson [propagator](wikipedia://en/698759) times the two currents associated with the motions two colliding particles.[^o15] Therefore, currents (axial or electromagnetic) are one of the two essential ingredients needed to compute [high-energy scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-energy_physics), the other being the boson [propagator](wikipedia://en/698759).

In electron–nucleon [scattering](wikipedia://en/164483) (or more generally, charged [lepton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepton)–[hadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadron)/[nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus) scattering) the axial current yields the [spin](wikipedia://en/19593829)-dependent part of the [cross-section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_section_%28physics%29).[^o16] (The spin-average part of the cross-section comes from the electromagnetic current.[^o15])

In [neutrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrino)–nucleon scattering, neutrinos couple only via the axial current, thus accessing different nucleon structure information than with charged leptons.[^o17]

Neutral [pions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pion) also couple only via the axial current because pions are [pseudoscalar](wikipedia://en/475035) particles and, to produce amplitudes (scalar quantities), a pion must couple to another pseudoscalar object like the axial current. (Charged pions can also couple via the electromagnetic current.)
