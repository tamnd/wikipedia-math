# Andrea Rotnitzky

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Andrea Rotnitzky"}

**Andrea Gloria Rotnitzky** is an Argentine biostatistician whose research involves [causal inference](wikipedia://en/37103476) on the effects of [medical interventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_intervention) in the face of [missing data](wikipedia://en/7859676). She is Prentice Endowed Professor of Biostatistics in the [University of Washington School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington_School_of_Public_Health).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Rotnitzky earned a [licenciate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licenciate) in mathematics in 1982 at the [University of Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Buenos_Aires). She went to the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) for graduate study in statistics, earning a master's degree in 1986 and completing her Ph.D. in 1988. Her dissertation, *Analysis of Generalized Linear Models for Cluster Correlated Data*, was supervised by Nicholas P. Jewell.

After postdoctoral research in the [Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_T.H._Chan_School_of_Public_Health), Rotnitzky continued at Harvard as an assistant professor of biostatistics from 1989 to 1995, associate professor from 1995 to 2000, and senior lecturer from 2000 to 2005. Meanwhile, she joined the department of economics at [Torcuato di Tella University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torcuato_di_Tella_University) in Buenos Aires as an associate professor from 2000 to 2005, and as a full professor from 2005 to 2022.

In 2022 she became Prentice Endowed Professor of Biostatistics at the [University of Washington School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington_School_of_Public_Health).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Rotnitzky was one of the five inaugural winners of the [Rousseeuw Prize for Statistics](wikipedia://en/72172766), in 2022, given to her with [James Robins](wikipedia://en/16311183), Thomas Richardson, [Miguel Hernán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Hernán), and Eric Tchetgen Tchetgen, "for their pioneering work on causal inference with applications in medicine and public health". In 2023 she was granted the [Konex Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konex_Award) Merit Diploma for her work in Mathematics in the last decade.

In 2025, Rotnitzky was named a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics), "for groundbreaking contributions to semiparametric theory for causal inference and missing data analysis, the development of doubly robust estimation, the integration of semiparametric efficiency theory with causal graphical models".
