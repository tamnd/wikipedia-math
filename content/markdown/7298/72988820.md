# Garnier integrable system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Garnier integrable system"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), the **Garnier integrable system**, also known as the **classical Gaudin model**, is a [classical mechanical system](wikipedia://en/19555586) discovered by René Garnier[^o2] in 1917, and solved using abelian integrals on compact Riemann surfaces (algebraic curves) of arbitrarily high genus. It is obtained by taking the '[Painlevé](wikipedia://en/402504) simplification' or 'autonomous limit' of the [Schlesinger equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlesinger_equations#Schlesinger's_equations)[^o3]. It may be interpreted as the classical limit of the [quantum Gaudin model](wikipedia://en/71851317) due to [Michel Gaudin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Gaudin_%28physicist%29)[^o4]. (Similarly, the [Schlesinger equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlesinger_equations) are the classical limit of the [Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations](wikipedia://en/16119106), expressed in the Heisenberg representation.[^o5][^o6]. )

:::

The Garnier systems were later shown to be of Hamiltonian type [^o7] [^o8] [^o9], defined on a phase space consisting of the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of $N$ copies of the dual $\mathfrak{gl(r)}^*$ of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{gl(r)}^*$, for $N$ a positive integer, and [completely integrable](wikipedia://en/2573213) in the Hamiltonian sense.

They are also a specific case of [Hitchin integrable systems](wikipedia://en/23652892), when the [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) that the theory is defined on is the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799) and the system is [tamely ramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamely_ramified).

## As a limit of the Schlesinger equations {#As_a_limit_of_the_Schlesinger_equations}

The [Schlesinger equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlesinger_equations) are a system of differential equations for $n + 2$ matrix-valued functions $A_i:\mathbb{C}^{n+2} \rightarrow \mathrm{Mat}(m, \mathbb{C})$, given by

$$\frac{\partial A_i}{\partial \lambda_j} = \frac{[A_i,A_j]}{\lambda_i-\lambda_j} \qquad \qquad j\neq i$$

$$\sum_j \frac{\partial A_i}{\partial \lambda_j} = 0.$$

The 'autonomous limit' is given by replacing the $\lambda_i$ dependence in the denominator by constants $\alpha_i$ with $\alpha_{n+1} = 0, \alpha_{n+2} = 1$:

$$\frac{\partial A_i}{\partial \lambda_j} = \frac{[A_i,A_j]}{\alpha_i-\alpha_j} \qquad \qquad j\neq i$$

$$\sum_j \frac{\partial A_i}{\partial \lambda_j} = 0.$$

This is the **Garnier system** in the form originally derived by Garnier.

## As the classical Gaudin model {#As_the_classical_Gaudin_model}

There is a formulation of the Garnier system as a classical mechanical system, the classical Gaudin model, which quantizes to the quantum Gaudin model and whose equations of motion are equivalent to the Garnier system. This section describes this formulation.[^o7][^o8][^o9][^o13]

As for any classical system, the Gaudin model is specified by a [Poisson manifold](wikipedia://en/882902) $M$ referred to as the [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101), and a [smooth function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) on the manifold called the [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/198319).

### Phase space {#Phase_space}

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a quadratic [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944), that is, a Lie algebra with a non-[degenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degenerate_form) [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) $\kappa$. If $\mathfrak{g}$ is [complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_vector_space) and [simple](wikipedia://en/59500649), this can be taken to be the [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386).

The [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_vector_space), denoted $\mathfrak{g}^*$, can be made into a [linear Poisson structure](wikipedia://en/882902#linear_Poisson_structure) by the [Kirillov–Kostant bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coadjoint_orbit).

The phase space $M$ of the classical Gaudin model is then the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of $N$ copies of $\mathfrak{g}^*$ for $N$ a positive integer.

### Sites {#Sites}

Associated to each of these copies is a point in $\mathbb{C}$, denoted $\lambda_1, \cdots, \lambda_N$, and referred to as **sites**.

### Lax matrix {#Lax_matrix}

Fixing a basis of the Lie algebra $\{I^a\}$ with [structure constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_constant) $f^{ab}_c$, there are functions $X^a_{(r)}$ with $r = 1, \cdots, N$ on the phase space satisfying the Poisson bracket

$$\{X^a_{(r)}, X^b_{(s)}\} = \delta_{rs}f^{ab}_c X^c_{(r)}.$$

These in turn are used to define $\mathfrak{g}$-valued functions

$$X^{(r)} = \kappa_{ab}I^a \otimes X^b_{(r)}$$

with [implicit summation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_summation_convention).

Next, these are used to define the [Lax matrix](wikipedia://en/5527029) which is also a $\mathfrak{g}$ valued function on the phase space which in addition depends [meromorphically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meromorphic) on a spectral parameter $\lambda$,

$$\mathcal{L}(\lambda) = \sum_{r = 1}^N \frac{X^{(r)}}{\lambda - \lambda_r} + \Omega,$$

and $\Omega$ is a constant element in $\mathfrak{g}$, in the sense that it Poisson commutes (has vanishing Poisson bracket) with all functions.

### (Quadratic) Hamiltonian {#(Quadratic)_Hamiltonian}

The (quadratic) Hamiltonian is

$$\mathcal{H}(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2}\kappa(\mathcal{L}(\lambda), \mathcal{L}(\lambda))$$

which is indeed a function on the phase space, which is additionally dependent on a spectral parameter $\lambda$. This can be written as

$$\mathcal{H}(\lambda) = \Delta_\infty + \sum_{r = 1}^N\left( \frac{\Delta_r}{(\lambda - \lambda_r)^2} + \frac{\mathcal{H}_r}{\lambda - \lambda_r} \right),$$

with

$$\Delta_r = \frac{1}{2} \kappa(X^{(r)}, X^{(r)}), \Delta_\infty = \frac{1}{2} \kappa(\Omega, \Omega)$$

and

$$\mathcal{H}_r = \sum_{s \neq r} \frac{ \kappa( X^{(r)}, X^{(s)} )}{ \lambda_r - \lambda_s} + \kappa(X^{(r)} , \Omega).$$

From the Poisson bracket relation

$$\{ \mathcal{H}(\lambda), \mathcal{H}(\mu) \} = 0, \forall \lambda, \mu \in \mathbb{C},$$

by varying $\lambda$ and $\mu$ it must be true that the $\mathcal{H}_r$'s, the $\Delta_r$'s and $\Delta_\infty$ are all in involution. It can be shown that the $\Delta_r$'s and $\Delta_\infty$ Poisson commute with all functions on the phase space, but the $\mathcal{H}_r$'s do not in general. These are the conserved charges in involution for the purposes of [Arnol'd Liouville integrability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnol'd–Liouville_theorem).

### Lax equation {#Lax_equation}

One can show

$$\{\mathcal{H}_r, \mathcal{L}(\lambda)\} = \left[\frac{X^{(r)}}{\lambda - \lambda_r}, \mathcal{L}(\lambda)\right],$$

so the Lax matrix satisfies the Lax equation when time evolution is given by any of the Hamiltonians $\mathcal{H}_r$, as well as any linear combination of them.

### Higher Hamiltonians {#Higher_Hamiltonians}

The quadratic Casimir gives corresponds to a quadratic [Weyl invariant](wikipedia://en/296332) polynomial for the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, but in fact many more commuting conserved charges can be generated using $\mathfrak{g}$-invariant polynomials. These invariant polynomials can be found using the [Harish-Chandra isomorphism](wikipedia://en/5352569) in the case $\mathfrak{g}$ is complex, simple and finite.

## Integrable field theories as classical Gaudin models {#Integrable_field_theories_as_classical_Gaudin_models}

Certain integrable classical field theories can be formulated as classical affine Gaudin models, where $\mathfrak{g}$ is an [affine Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1350865). Such classical field theories include the principal [chiral model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral_model), coset [sigma models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_model) and affine [Toda field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toda_field_theory).[^o21] As such, affine Gaudin models can be seen as a 'master theory' for integrable systems, but is most naturally formulated in the Hamiltonian formalism, unlike other master theories like [four-dimensional Chern–Simons theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-dimensional_Chern–Simons_theory) or [anti-self-dual Yang–Mills](wikipedia://en/35544664).

## Quantum Gaudin models {#Quantum_Gaudin_models}

A great deal is known about the integrable structure of [quantum Gaudin models](wikipedia://en/71851317). In particular, [Feigin](wikipedia://en/53823921), [Frenkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Frenkel) and [Reshetikhin](wikipedia://en/7487626) studied them using the theory of [vertex operator algebras](wikipedia://en/1287577), showing the relation of Gaudin models to topics in mathematics including the [Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov_equation) and the [geometric Langlands correspondence](wikipedia://en/10500178).[^o23]
