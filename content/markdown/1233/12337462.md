# John B. Cosgrave

*Image: JBC1.JPG.* Dr. John B. Cosgrave

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John B. Cosgrave"}

Dr. **John B. Cosgrave** (born 5 January 1946) is an Irish mathematician specialising in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). Born in [Bailieborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailieborough),[^o2] County Cavan, he was educated at Royal Holloway College, London, he lectured in [Carysfort College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carysfort_College) ([Blackrock, Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackrock,_Dublin)) and [St Patrick's College of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Patrick's_College_of_Education) (Drumcondra).

:::

## Other {#Other}

In January 1999, while preparing some work for his students, he identified a highly structured prime number with exactly two thousand digits. Dubbing this prime a millennium prime, he wrote an email about it to a niece and nephew, which was subsequently published by Folding Landscapes,[^o4] the publishing house of the cartographer [Tim Robinson.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Robinson_%28cartographer%29) He donated his author royalties to the Irish Cancer Society,[^o5] and subsequently wrote an *Irishman's Diary*[^o6] column about it for the [Irish Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Times) newspaper.

In July 1999 – while a participant in the Proth Search Group – he became the discoverer of the then-largest known composite Fermat number,[^o7] a record which his [St. Patrick's College (Drumcondra)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Patrick's_College_of_Education) based Proth-Gallot Group twice broke in 2003, the 1999 record having stood until then.[^o8] The third of those records continued to stand until it was broken in June 2011.

- Cosgrave, John B. *An Introduction to Number Theory with Talented Youth*, USA School Science and Mathematics, Vol 99, No 6, October 1999 (Special issue devoted to gifted and talented Mathematics and Science students).

- Cosgrave, John B. *An application of Wilson's theorem to prove that 2^p = 2 (mod p) when p is prime*, *A theorem about certain sequences*, and *A theorem about primes of the form a^2 + n^2*, Mathematical Gazette, Oct. 1969.
- Cosgrave, J. B. *A new proof of Wilson's Theorem* and *A new proof of Wilson's theorem for primes of the form (4n + 3)*, Mathematical Gazette, Feb. 1967.
