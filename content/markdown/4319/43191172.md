# Proton Mail

::: {#o9 .subject cue=lead name="Proton Mail"}

**Proton Mail** is an [end-to-end encrypted](wikipedia://en/1065362) [email hosting service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mailbox_provider). It was launched in 2014 by Andy Yen, Jason Stockman and Wei Sun. Proton Mail is headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland.[^o10] It is owned by the non-profit Proton Foundation through its subsidiary [Proton AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_AG), which also operates [Proton VPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_VPN), [Proton Drive](wikipedia://en/71846962), [Proton Calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_Calendar), [Proton Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_Pass), Proton Wallet, Proton Meet, and [Lumo AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumo_%28AI_assistant%29). Proton Mail uses [client-side encryption](wikipedia://en/46420317) to protect email content and user data before they are sent to company servers, unlike other common email providers such as [Gmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmail) and [Outlook.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com).[^o11][^o12]

:::

Proton Mail received its initial funding through a [crowdfunding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowdfunding) campaign, and initial access was by invitation only, but it opened to the public in 2016. There were two million users by 2017,[^o13] almost 70 million by 2022,[^o14] and 100 million users in 2024.

The source code for the back end of Proton Mail remains closed-source,[^o15][^o16] but Proton Mail released the source code for the web interface, iOS and Android apps, and the Proton Mail Bridge app under an [open-source licence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_licence).[^o17][^o18][^o19][^o20]

## History {#History}

On 16 May 2014, Proton Mail entered into [public beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_beta).[^o22] Due to high demand, after three days beta signups were temporarily restricted to expand server capacity.[^o23] Afterwards, Proton Mail implemented an invite-only waiting list.

In summer 2014, Proton Mail received 550377 from 10,576 donors through a crowdfunding campaign on [Indiegogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiegogo), while aiming for 100000.[^o24] During the campaign, [PayPal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PayPal) froze Proton Mail's PayPal account, thereby preventing the withdrawal of 251721 worth of donations. PayPal stated that the account was frozen due to doubts of the legality of encryption, statements that opponents said were unfounded.[^o25][^o26] The restrictions were lifted the following day.[^o27]

On 14 August 2015, Proton Mail released major version 2.0, which included a rewritten [codebase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codebase) for its web interface. On 17 March 2016, Proton Mail released major version 3.0, which saw the official launch of Proton Mail out of beta. With a new interface for the web client, version 3.0 also included the public launch of Proton Mail's iOS and Android beta applications and the removal of the waiting list.[^o28]

On 19 January 2017, Proton Mail announced a [Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_%28anonymity_network%29) [onion](wikipedia://en/3648703) site.[^o29] On 21 November 2017, Proton Mail introduced Proton Mail Contacts, a zero-access encryption contacts manager. Proton Mail Contacts also utilizes [digital signatures](wikipedia://en/59644) to verify the integrity of contacts data.[^o30] On 6 December 2017, Proton Mail launched Proton Mail Bridge, an application that provides end-to-end email encryption to any desktop client that supports [IMAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMAP) and [SMTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMTP), such as [Microsoft Outlook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Outlook), [Mozilla Thunderbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozilla_Thunderbird), and [Apple Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Mail), for Windows and MacOS.[^o31]

On 25 July 2018, Proton Mail introduced address verification and Pretty Good Privacy (specifically the open-source [OpenPGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenPGP) variant) support, making Proton Mail interoperable with other [GPG](wikipedia://en/38809) clients.[^o32]

Around July 2021, Proton Mail's security and cryptographic architecture were both independently audited by Securitum, a European security auditing company, who uncovered no major issues or security vulnerabilities, and the audit results were publicly published.[^o33]

In April 2022, Proton acquired SimpleLogin, a company based in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), France that provides [email aliasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email_alias) addresses. SimpleLogin functionality was subsequently integrated into Proton Mail, but the email masking service is also available independently to use with any email provider.[^o34][^o35] That same month, Proton also announced that users would now be able to create @proton.me email addresses, to complement the @protonmail.com addresses that were previously the default choice.[^o36]

In May 2022, following a rebrand of Proton, a space was added to the official name of the service, transitioning from ProtonMail to Proton Mail.[^o37] In February 2023 a new version of the Proton Mail Bridge was launched that was considered to be a major improvement.[^o38] Proton Mail Bridge allows Proton Mail to be used with any third party email client on Windows, macOS, or Linux, without losing end-to-end encryption.

In April 2024, Proton Mail launched a desktop app for Windows and macOS. A version for [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux) is in beta.[^o39][^o40][^o41] The desktop client is only available for users with a paying subscription, despite Proton AG's earlier comments that it would be "gradually be made available to all users, including *free*".[^o42] The app also allows access to Proton Calendar.[^o39][^o43]

In July 2024, Proton released a private AI writing assistant for Proton Mail, called Scribe, for the mail composer to initiate new emails.[^o44] Scribe is only available on higher-tier paid plans.

## Encryption {#Encryption}

Proton Mail uses a combination of [public-key cryptography](wikipedia://en/24222) and [symmetric encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_encryption) protocols to offer [end-to-end encryption](wikipedia://en/1065362). When a user creates a Proton Mail account, their browser generates a pair of public and private [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28cryptosystem%29) keys:

- The [public key](wikipedia://en/24222) is used to encrypt the user's emails and other user data.
- The [private key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_key) capable of decrypting the user's data is symmetrically encrypted with the user's mailbox password.

This symmetrical encryption happens in the user's web browser using [AES-256](wikipedia://en/1260). Upon account registration, the user is asked to provide a login password for their account.

Proton Mail also offers users an option to log in with a two-password mode that requires a login password and a mailbox password.

- The login password is used for authentication.
- The mailbox password encrypts the user's mailbox that contains received emails, contacts, and user information as well as a private encryption key.

Upon logging in, the user has to provide both passwords. This is to access the account and the encrypted mailbox and its private encryption key. The decryption takes place client-side either in a web browser or in one of the apps. The public key and the encrypted private key are both stored on Proton Mail servers. Thus Proton Mail stores decryption keys only in their encrypted form so Proton Mail developers are unable to retrieve user emails or reset user mailbox passwords.[^o46] This system absolves Proton Mail from:

- Storing either the unencrypted data or the mailbox password.
- Divulging the contents of past emails but not future emails.
- Decrypting the mailbox if requested or compelled by a court order.[^o47]

Proton Mail exclusively supports [HTTPS](wikipedia://en/13586) and uses [TLS](wikipedia://en/187813) with ephemeral [key exchange](wikipedia://en/7903) to encrypt all Internet traffic between users and Proton Mail servers.

In September 2015, Proton Mail added native support to their web interface and mobile app for PGP. This allows a user to export their Proton Mail PGP-encoded public key to others outside of Proton Mail, enabling them to use the key for [email encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email_encryption). The Proton Mail also supports PGP encryption from Proton Mail to outside users.[^o48]

### Email sending {#Email_sending}

An email message sent from one Proton Mail account to another is automatically encrypted with the public key of the recipient. Once encrypted, only the private key of the recipient can decrypt the message. When the recipient logs in, their mailbox password decrypts their private key and unlocks their inbox.

Email messages sent from Proton Mail to non-Proton Mail email addresses may optionally be sent in [plain text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_text) or with end-to-end encryption. With encryption, the message is encrypted with AES under a user-supplied password. The recipient receives a link to the official and limited version of Proton Mail website, on which they can enter the password, read the decrypted message, and reply back securely end-to-end encrypted. Proton Mail assumes that the sender and the recipient have exchanged this password through a [backchannel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backchannel).[^o46] Such email messages can be optionally set to self-destruct after a period of time.[^o50]

## Data centers {#Data_centers}

Proton Mail is hosted in [data centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_centers) maintained by Proton AG in three countries: [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) (one in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne) and another in [Attinghausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attinghausen) in the former K7 military bunker under 1000 m of granite), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) and [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway).[^o52] Each data center uses [load balancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Load_balancing_%28computing%29) across web, mail, and [SQL](wikipedia://en/29004) servers, redundant power supply, [hard drives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Drives) with [full disk encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_disk_encryption), and exclusive use of Linux and other open-source software.

## Controversies {#Controversies}

Proton has faced criticism and user backlash over allegations of political bias and associations with far-right groups.[^o54][^o55] In January 2025, the company became the subject of controversy after Proton CEO Andy Yen praised the US Republican Party’s stance on antitrust enforcement, specifically regarding the "Big Tech" lobby. Yen wrote:

> 10 years ago, Republicans were the party of big business and Dems stood for the little guys, but today the tables have completely turned.

The tweet went viral, and Proton’s official Reddit account posted a comment stating that “Until corporate Dems are thrown out, the reality is that Republicans remain more likely to tackle Big Tech abuses.” [^o54] Following criticism that the statement aligned the privacy-focused company with the MAGA movement, Yen posted on Reddit that the post was "not intended to be a political statement" and declared that "officially Proton must always be politically neutral."[^o54]

Concerns regarding Proton’s proximity to extremism have extended beyond political statements. In August 2025, two individuals of [Phrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrack) had their Proton email accounts disabled while working on an article describing an [advanced persistent threat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_persistent_threat) in South Korean computer networks. The individuals speculated their accounts being disabled were a co-ordinated attack by [Kimsuky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimsuky) since this occurred after they used the emails to notify the affected parties. After the individuals contacted Proton's team for an appeal, the response was that their accounts were disabled for unauthorized activities which they concluded with "We consider that allowing access to your account will cause further damage to our service, therefore we will keep the account suspended." Phrack's editors contacted Proton regarding the suspensions on August 22, clarifying hacked information was not passed through the email exchanges using Proton Mail infrastructure. A follow-up email later in September was sent, but no response came from Proton. When the incident was posted on social media, an official response by Proton on [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter) on 10 September 2025, claimed the suspensions were due to an alert given by a [Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_emergency_response_team) about the accounts violating terms of service and that they were investigating. The accounts were then reinstated after Proton's CEO Andy Yen made a post on X about it, clarifying that the individuals were suspended due to being [hacktivists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacktivists), which violated the [terms of service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terms_of_service), explaining there was a distinction between that and a journalist and that their suspensions were not targeting journalists as Phrack claimed. He said this reinstatement was an exception for reasons he could not disclose further for privacy reasons of the parties involved.[^o56][^o57]

## Reception {#Reception}

Technological reviews of Proton Mail are generally positive. *IT Pro*'s review scores it 4 out of 5 stars. It lauds the end-to-end encryption of emails, including for non-Proton Mail users, a robust security, and the interfaces of both the web and mobile versions of the email client, with criticisms of the client's search function and the service's price versus the competition.[^o59] *[PCMag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCMag)* also rates the service 4 out of 5 stars, praising the security, in addition to loading embedded images without returning the IP address to senders and setting expirations for messages, but questioning the cap on daily messages for free accounts.[^o60] *[TechRadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TechRadar)* gives the service 4.5 out of 5 stars, calling it one of the best secure email services, although it noted limitations such as that it is not suited for voluminous messaging, the reluctance of websites to adopt the service, and that email subject lines are not encrypted.[^o61] User review website *[Trustpilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trustpilot)* scored the service 2.2 out of 5 stars based on 1,025 reviews.[^o62]

Proton Mail was the recipient of three [Lovie Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovie_Award), one gold and The People's Lovie in 2016,[^o63] and one silver in 2017.[^o64]

## Legal status {#Legal_status}

According to Proton Mail's transparency report[^o66][^o67] it is legally obliged to follow Swiss court orders if Switzerland law is broken. Due to the encryption utilized, Proton Mail is unable to hand over the contents of encrypted emails under any circumstances, but according to Proton's 2021 privacy policy Proton Mail can be legally compelled to log IP addresses as part of a Swiss criminal investigation.[^o68]2025 For this reason, the company strongly suggests that users who need to hide their identity from the Switzerland government use their Tor hidden service/onion site or Proton VPN, noting in particular that under Swiss law, VPNs are treated differently from email, and Proton VPN is not obliged to keep data retention logs of online activity and cannot be forced to log user activity.[^o69] In July 2025, Proton began steps to diversify its infrastructure, starting with its AI component, to the EU due to concerns over proposed Swiss privacy legislation. The AI infrastructure will be based in Germany with an additional datacenter located in Norway.[^o70]

In 2020, Proton Mail received 3,572 orders from Swiss authorities and contested 750 of them.[^o71] Notable court orders include a case involving death threats made against immunologist [Anthony Fauci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Fauci), a case involving French activists accused of property damage, and a case involving a person allegedly involved in [Catalan independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_independence_movement) activism.[^o72][^o73][^o74] In all three cases, Swiss authorities issued court orders to Proton in response to international requests for assistance. In October 2021, Proton won a Switzerland court case that confirmed that email services cannot be considered telecommunications providers, and consequently are not subject to the data retention requirements imposed on telecommunications providers.[^o75]

On 15 November 2019, Proton confirmed that the government of the [Republic of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus) had issued a block across the country of Proton Mail and [Proton VPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProtonVPN) IP addresses. The block was no longer in place four days later. No explanation was given to Proton Mail for the block, nor for the block being lifted.[^o76]

On 29 January 2020, the Russian [Federal Service for Supervision of Communications, Information Technology and Mass Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Service_for_Supervision_of_Communications,_Information_Technology_and_Mass_Media) reported that it had implemented a complete block of Proton Mail services within the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia).[^o77] As a reason for the block, it cited Proton Mail's refusal to give up information relating to accounts that allegedly sent out spam with terror threats.[^o78][^o79] However, Proton Mail claimed that it did not receive any requests from Russian authorities regarding any such accounts.[^o80][^o81] In response to the block, the Proton Mail [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter) account recommended legitimate users circumvent the block via VPNs or Tor.[^o82]

In March 2020, the company announced that even though the Russia ban was not particularly successful, and the service continues to be largely available in Russia without using a VPN, Proton Mail will be releasing new anti-censorship features in both Proton Mail and Proton VPN desktop and mobile apps which will allow more block attempts to be automatically circumvented.[^o83]

In April 2025, the [Karnataka High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnataka_High_Court) directed the [Indian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_government) to block Proton Mail following a [legal complaint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_complaint) filed by [New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Delhi)-based M Moser Design Associates who alleged that its employees had received sexually explicit content in emails sent via Proton Mail.[^o84]
