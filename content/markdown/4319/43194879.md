# Geometric transformation

Transformation (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geometric transformation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **geometric transformation** is any [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) of a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) to itself (or to another such set) with some salient [geometrical](wikipedia://en/18973446) underpinning, such as preserving distances, [angles](wikipedia://en/1196), or ratios (scale). More specifically, it is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) whose [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) and [range](wikipedia://en/275991) are sets of [points](wikipedia://en/593693) – most often a [real coordinate space](wikipedia://en/1482083), $\mathbb{R}^2$ or $\mathbb{R}^3$ – such that the function is bijective so that its [inverse](wikipedia://en/14907) exists.[^o2] The study of geometry may be approached by the study of these transformations, such as in [transformation geometry](wikipedia://en/473774).[^o3]

:::

## Classifications {#Classifications}

Geometric transformations can be classified by the dimension of their operand sets (thus distinguishing between, say, planar transformations and spatial transformations). They can also be classified according to the properties they preserve:

- [Displacements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_%28vector%29) preserve [distances](wikipedia://en/39378) and [oriented angles](wikipedia://en/1196) (e.g., [translations](wikipedia://en/204682));[^o5]
- [Isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) preserve angles and distances (e.g., [Euclidean transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_transformation));[^o6][^o7]
- [Similarities](wikipedia://en/39377) preserve angles and ratios between distances (e.g., resizing);[^o8]
- [Affine transformations](wikipedia://en/38449) preserve [parallelism](wikipedia://en/664497) (e.g., [scaling](wikipedia://en/651752), [shear](wikipedia://en/799405));[^o7][^o9]
- [Projective transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_transformation) preserve [collinearity](wikipedia://en/3189581);[^o10]

Each of these classes contains the previous one.[^o10]

- [Möbius transformations](wikipedia://en/314493) using complex coordinates on the plane (as well as [circle inversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_inversion)) preserve the set of all lines and circles, but may interchange lines and circles.
- [Conformal transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_transformation) preserve angles, and are, in the first order, similarities.
- [Equiareal transformations](wikipedia://en/8953468), preserve areas in the planar case or volumes in the three dimensional case.[^o11] and are, in the first order, affine transformations of [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) 1.
- [Homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) (bicontinuous transformations) preserve the neighborhoods of points.
- [Diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) (bidifferentiable transformations) are the transformations that are affine in the first order; they contain the preceding ones as special cases, and can be further refined.

Transformations of the same type form [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) that may be sub-groups of other transformation groups.

## Opposite group actions {#Opposite_group_actions}

Many geometric transformations are expressed with linear algebra. The bijective linear transformations are elements of a [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564). The [linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) *A* is non-singular. For a [row vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Row_vector) *v*, the [matrix product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_product) *vA* gives another row vector *w* = *vA*.

The [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844) of a row vector *v* is a column vector *v*<sup>T</sup>, and the transpose of the above equality is $w^T = (vA)^T = A^T v^T .$ Here *A*<sup>T</sup> provides a left action on column vectors.

In transformation geometry there are [compositions](wikipedia://en/7472170) *AB*. Starting with a row vector *v*, the right action of the composed transformation is *w* = *vAB*. After transposition,

$$w^T = (vAB)^T = (AB)^Tv^T = B^T A^T v^T .$$

Thus for *AB* the associated left [group action](wikipedia://en/12781) is $B^T A^T .$ In the study of [opposite groups](wikipedia://en/31366779), the distinction is made between opposite group actions because [commutative groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_group) are the only groups for which these opposites are equal.
