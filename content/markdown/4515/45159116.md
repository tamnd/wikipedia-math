# Reflexive sheaf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reflexive sheaf"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **reflexive sheaf** is a [coherent sheaf](wikipedia://en/603780) that is isomorphic to its second dual (as a [sheaf of modules](wikipedia://en/44887605)) via the canonical map. The second dual of a coherent sheaf is called the **reflexive hull** of the sheaf. A basic example of a reflexive sheaf is a [locally free sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_free_sheaf) of finite rank. The notion is important both in [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory) and [complex algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_algebraic_geometry).

:::

For the theory of reflexive sheaves, one works over an [integral](wikipedia://en/15462) [noetherian](wikipedia://en/2783891) [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754).

A reflexive sheaf is torsion-free. The dual of a coherent sheaf is reflexive.[^o2] Usually, the product of reflexive sheaves is defined as the reflexive hull of their tensor products (so the result is reflexive).

A coherent sheaf *F* is said to be "normal" in the sense of Barth if the restriction $F(U) \to F(U - Y)$ is bijective for every open subset *U* and a closed subset *Y* of *U* of codimension at least 2. With this terminology, a coherent sheaf on an integral [normal scheme](wikipedia://en/2629423) is reflexive if and only if it is [torsion-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_sheaf) and normal in the sense of Barth.[^o3] A reflexive sheaf of rank one on an integral [locally factorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_factorial_scheme) scheme is invertible.[^o4]

A **divisorial sheaf** on a scheme *X* is a rank-one reflexive sheaf that is locally free at the generic points of the [conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductor_%28algebraic_geometry%29) *D*<sub>*X*</sub> of *X*.[^o5] For example, a [canonical sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_sheaf) ([dualizing sheaf](wikipedia://en/47021614)) on a normal projective variety is a divisorial sheaf.
