# Bernard Maskit

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bernard Maskit"}

**Bernard (Bernie) Maskit** (27 May 1935 – 15 March 2024) was an American mathematician who worked on [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652), low dimensional geometry and topology, and related topics.

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Maskit studied for both his bachelors and doctoral degrees at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), earning his Ph.D. in 1964 under the supervision of [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972), with the thesis *On Klein's Combination Theorem*. After postdoctoral studies at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), he held an assistant professorship at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) from 1965 to 1972.[^o5] He then moved to the mathematics department at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University), where he retired in 2008[^o6] and was then a professor emeritus until his death.[^o7] In 2012, he became one of the inaugural [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]

Maskit's main area of mathematical expertise was the study of Kleinian groups acting on low dimensional hyperbolic spaces, where he made fundamental contributions. His works include the [planarity theorem](https://mathscinet.ams.org/mathscinet/article?mr=172252), the significant development of the Klein-Maskit combination theorems, the study of Schottky groups including [their characterization](https://mathscinet.ams.org/mathscinet/article?mr=220929), and the [Poincare polyhedron theorem](https://mathscinet.ams.org/mathscinet/article?mr=297997). Of particular note is the Maskit slice of Teichmueller space. He is the author of the book *Kleinian Groups* (Grundlehren der Mathematischen Wissenschaften 287, Springer-Verlag, 1988)[^o9][^o10] and gave an invited talk about Kleinian groups at the 1974 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842).[^o11]
