# Weighted space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weighted space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a **weighted space** is a [function space](wikipedia://en/340630) equipped with a *weighted norm*, which is a finite [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) (or [semi-norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-norm)) that involves multiplication by a particular [weight function](wikipedia://en/245523).

:::

Weights can be used to expand or reduce a space of considered functions. For example, in the space of functions from a set $U\subset\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{R}$ under the norm $\|\cdot\|_U$ defined by $\textstyle\|f\|_U=\sup_{x\in U}{|f(x)|}$, functions that have infinity as a [limit point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_point) are excluded. However, the weighted norm

$$\|f\|=\sup_{x\in U}{\left|\frac{f(x)}{1+x^2}\right|}$$

is finite for many more functions, so the associated space contains more functions. Alternatively, the weighted norm

$$\|f\|=\sup_{x\in U}{\left|f(x)(1 + x^4)\right|}$$

is finite for many fewer functions. When the weight is of the form $\tfrac{1}{1+x^m}$, the weighted space is called *polynomial-weighted*.[^o2]
