# Free ideal ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Free ideal ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the field of [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a (right) **free ideal ring**, or **fir**, is a ring in which all [right ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) are [free modules](wikipedia://en/326454) with unique [rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rank_of_a_free_module). A ring such that all right ideals with at most *n* generators are free and have unique rank is called an **n-fir**. A [semifir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semifir) is a ring in which all [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) right ideals are free modules of unique rank. (Thus, a ring is semifir if it is *n*-fir for all *n* ≥ 0.) The semifir property is left-right symmetric, but the fir property is not.

:::

## Properties and examples {#Properties_and_examples}

It turns out that a left and right fir is a [domain](wikipedia://en/487627). Furthermore, a [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346) fir is precisely a [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039), while a commutative semifir is precisely a [Bézout domain](wikipedia://en/3131407). These last facts are not generally true for noncommutative rings, however (Cohn 1971).

Every [principal right ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039) *R* is a right fir, since every nonzero principal right ideal of a domain is isomorphic to *R*. In the same way, a right [Bézout domain](wikipedia://en/3131407) is a semifir.

Since all right ideals of a right fir are free, they are projective. So, any right fir is a right [hereditary ring](wikipedia://en/5123176), and likewise a right semifir is a right [semihereditary ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semihereditary_ring). Because [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488) over [local rings](wikipedia://en/153391) are free, and because local rings have [invariant basis number](wikipedia://en/3327998), it follows that a local, right hereditary ring is a right fir, and a local, right semihereditary ring is a right semifir.

Unlike a principal right ideal domain, a right fir is not necessarily right [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129), however in the commutative case, *R* is a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132) since it is a hereditary domain, and so is necessarily Noetherian.

Another important and motivating example of a free ideal ring are the free associative (unital) *k*-algebras for division rings *k*, also called [non-commutative polynomial rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative_polynomial_ring) (Cohn 2000, §5.4).

Semifirs have [invariant basis number](wikipedia://en/3327998) and every semifir is a [Sylvester domain](wikipedia://en/33813990).
