# Szász–Mirakyan operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Szász–Mirakyan operator"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a discipline within [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Szász–Mirakyan operators** (also spelled "Mirakjan" and "Mirakian") are generalizations of [Bernstein polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernstein_polynomials) to infinite intervals, introduced by [Otto Szász](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Szász) in 1950 and [G. M. Mirakjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._M._Mirakjan) in 1941. They are defined by

:::

$$\left[\mathcal{S}_n(f)\right](x) := e^{-nx}\sum_{k=0}^\infty{\frac{(nx)^k}{k!}f\left(\tfrac{k}{n}\right)}$$

where $x\in[0,\infty)\subset\mathbb{R}$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$.[^o2][^o3]

## Basic results {#Basic_results}

In 1964, Cheney and Sharma showed that if $f$ is convex and non-linear, the sequence $(\mathcal{S}_n(f))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ decreases with $n$ ($\mathcal{S}_n(f)\geq f$).[^o5] They also showed that if $f$ is a polynomial of degree $\leq m$, then so is $\mathcal{S}_n(f)$ for all $n$.

A converse of the first property was shown by Horová in 1968 (Altomare & Campiti 1994:350).

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Theorem on convergence"}

## Theorem on convergence {#Theorem_on_convergence}

In Szász's original paper, he proved the following as Theorem 3 of his paper:

If $f$ is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) on $[0,\infty)$, having a finite limit at infinity, then $\mathcal{S}_n(f)$ [converges uniformly](wikipedia://en/50652) to $f$ as $n\rightarrow\infty$.[^o2]

This is analogous to [a theorem stating that Bernstein polynomials approximate continuous functions on \[0,1](wikipedia://en/340136#Approximating_continuous_functions)\].

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

A [Kantorovich](wikipedia://en/194660)-type generalization is sometimes discussed in the literature. These generalizations are also called the [Szász–Mirakjan–Kantorovich operators](wikipedia://en/14943850).

In 1976, C. P. May showed that the [Baskakov operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baskakov_operators) can reduce to the Szász–Mirakyan operators.[^o10]

:::
