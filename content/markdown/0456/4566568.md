# Tameness theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tameness theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **tameness theorem** states that every complete [hyperbolic 3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1237700) with [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) is [topologically tame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically_tame), in other words [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the interior of a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) 3-manifold.

:::

The tameness theorem was [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) by (Marden 1974). It was [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by (Agol 2004) and, independently, by [Danny Calegari](wikipedia://en/21907908) and [David Gabai](wikipedia://en/6129609). It is one of the fundamental properties of geometrically infinite hyperbolic 3-manifolds, together with the [density theorem for Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/31202113) and the [ending lamination theorem](wikipedia://en/30997240). It also implies the [Ahlfors measure conjecture](wikipedia://en/31197103).

## History {#History}

Topological tameness may be viewed as a property of the [ends](wikipedia://en/6862105) of the manifold, namely, having a local product structure. An analogous statement is well known in two dimensions, that is, for [surfaces](wikipedia://en/27865). However, as the example of [Alexander horned sphere](wikipedia://en/685665) shows, there are wild embeddings among 3-manifolds, so this property is not automatic.

The conjecture was raised in the form of a question by [Albert Marden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Marden), who proved that any *geometrically finite* hyperbolic 3-manifold is topologically tame. The conjecture was also called the **Marden conjecture** or the **tame ends conjecture**.

There had been steady progress in understanding tameness before the conjecture was resolved. Partial results had been obtained by [Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190), Brock, Bromberg, Canary, Evans, Minsky, Ohshika. An important sufficient condition for tameness in terms of splittings of the fundamental group had been obtained by [Bonahon](wikipedia://en/56780028).

The conjecture was proved in 2004 by [Ian Agol](wikipedia://en/32084718), and independently, by Danny Calegari and David Gabai. Agol's proof relies on the use of manifolds of pinched negative curvature and on Canary's trick of "diskbusting" that allows to replace a compressible end with an incompressible end, for which the conjecture has already been proved. The Calegari–Gabai proof is centered on the existence of certain closed, non-positively curved surfaces that they call "shrinkwrapped".
