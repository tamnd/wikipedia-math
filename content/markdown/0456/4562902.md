# Radian per second

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Radian per second"}

The **radian per second** (symbol: **rad⋅s<sup>−1</sup>** or **rad/s**) is the unit of [angular velocity](wikipedia://en/65927) in the [International System of Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units) (SI). The [radian](wikipedia://en/26003) per [second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second) is also the SI unit of [angular frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_frequency) (symbol *ω*, omega). The radian per second is defined as the angular frequency that results in the [angular displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_displacement) increasing by one radian every second.[^o3]

:::

## Relation to other units {#Relation_to_other_units}

A [frequency](wikipedia://en/10779) of one [hertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz) (1 Hz), or one [cycle per second](wikipedia://en/3577159) (1 cps), corresponds to an angular frequency of 2$\pi$ radians per second. This is because one [cycle of rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_%28rotational_unit%29) corresponds to an [angular](wikipedia://en/1196) rotation of 2$\pi$ radians.[^o5]

Since the radian is a [dimensionless unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_unit) in the [SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI), the radian per second is dimensionally equivalent to the hertz—both can be expressed as [reciprocal seconds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_seconds), s<sup>−1</sup>. So, context is necessary to specify which [kind of quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kind_of_quantity) is being expressed, angular frequency or ordinary frequency.

One radian per second also corresponds to about 9.55 [revolutions per minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_per_minute) (rpm).[^o6] *Degrees per second* may also be defined, based on [degree of arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degrees_%28angle%29), where 1 degree per second (°/s) is equivalent to $\tfrac{\pi}{180}$ rad⋅s<sup>−1</sup>.

## Coherent units {#Coherent_units}

A use of the unit radian per second is in calculation of the power transmitted by a shaft. In the [International System of Quantities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Quantities) (SI) and the [International System of Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units), widely used in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), the power *p* is equal to the angular speed *ω* multiplied by the [torque](wikipedia://en/30400) *τ* applied to the shaft: *p* = *ω* ⋅ *τ*. When [coherent units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherence_%28units_of_measurement%29) are used for these quantities, which are respectively the [watt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watt), the radian per second, and the [newton-metre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton-metre), and thus [W](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watt) = rad/s × [N·m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton-metre), no numerical factor is needed when performing the numerical calculation. When the units are not coherent (e.g. [horsepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower), [turn](wikipedia://en/855329)/min, and [pound-foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound-foot_%28torque%29)), an additional factor will generally be necessary.
