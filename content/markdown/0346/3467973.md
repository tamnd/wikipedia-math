# Erdős–Graham problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erdős–Graham problem"}

In [combinatorial number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_number_theory), the **Erdős–Graham problem** is the problem of proving that, if the set $\{2,3,4,\dots\}$ of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) greater than one is [partitioned](wikipedia://en/340240) into finitely many subsets, then one of the subsets can be used to form an [Egyptian fraction](wikipedia://en/336349) representation of unity. That is, for every $r > 0$, and every $r$-coloring of the integers greater than one, there is a finite monochromatic subset $S$ of these integers such that.

:::

$$\sum_{n\in S}\frac{1}{n} = 1.$$

In more detail, [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Ronald Graham](wikipedia://en/240694) conjectured that, for sufficiently large $r$, the largest member of $S$ could be bounded by $b^r$ for some constant $b$ independent of $r$. It was known that, for this to be true, $b$ must be at least [Euler's constant](wikipedia://en/9633) $e$.

[Ernie Croot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernie_Croot) proved the conjecture as part of his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) thesis, and later (while a [post-doctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-doctoral) researcher at [UC Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_Berkeley)) published the proof in the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)*. The value Croot gives for $b$ is very large: it is at most $e^{167000}$. Croot's result follows as a corollary of a more general theorem stating the existence of Egyptian fraction representations of unity for sets $C$ of [smooth numbers](wikipedia://en/1035039) in intervals of the form $[X,X^{1+\delta}]$, where $C$ contains sufficiently many numbers so that the sum of their reciprocals is at least six. The Erdős–Graham conjecture follows from this result by showing that one can find an interval of this form in which the sum of the reciprocals of all smooth numbers is at least $6r$; therefore, if the integers are $r$-colored there must be a monochromatic subset $C$ satisfying the conditions of Croot's theorem.

A stronger form of the result, that any set of integers with positive [upper density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_density) includes the denominators of an Egyptian fraction representation of one, was announced in 2021 by [Thomas Bloom](wikipedia://en/71404791), a postdoctoral researcher at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o2][^o3][^o4]
