# Local system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local system"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **local system** (or a system of **local coefficients**) on a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* is a tool from [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) which interpolates between [cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) with coefficients in a fixed [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) *A*, and general [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) in which coefficients vary from point to point. Local coefficient systems were introduced by [Norman Steenrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Steenrod) in 1943.[^o2]

:::

Local systems are the building blocks of more general tools, such as [constructible](wikipedia://en/26131119) and [perverse](wikipedia://en/1714012) sheaves.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *X* be a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). A **local system** (of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974)/[modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_over_a_ring)...) on *X* is a [locally constant sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_constant_sheaf) (of abelian groups/[of modules](wikipedia://en/44887605)...) on *X*. In other words, a sheaf $\mathcal{L}$ is a local system if every point has an open neighborhood $U$ such that the restricted sheaf $\mathcal{L}|_U$ is isomorphic to the sheafification of some constant presheaf.

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Locally constant sheaf"}

### Locally constant sheaf {#Locally_constant_sheaf}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), a **locally constant sheaf** on a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* is a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) $\mathcal{F}$ on *X* such that for each *x* in *X*, there is an open neighborhood *U* of *x* such that the restriction $\mathcal{F}|_U$ is a [constant sheaf](wikipedia://en/2783581) on *U*. It is also called a **local system**. When *X* is a [stratified space](wikipedia://en/25886609), a [constructible sheaf](wikipedia://en/26131119) is roughly a sheaf that is locally constant on each member of the stratification.

A basic example is the [orientation sheaf](wikipedia://en/49112440) on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) since each point of the manifold admits an *[orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable_manifold)* open neighborhood (while the manifold itself may not be orientable).

For another example, let $X = \mathbb{C}$, $\mathcal{O}_X$ be the sheaf of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) on *X* and $P: \mathcal{O}_X \to \mathcal{O}_X$ given by $P = z {\partial \over \partial z} - {1 \over 2}$. Then the kernel of *P* is a locally constant sheaf on $X - \{0\}$ but not constant there (since it has no nonzero global section).[^o7]

If $\mathcal{F}$ is a locally constant sheaf of sets on a space *X*, then each [path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_%28mathematics%29) $p: [0, 1] \to X$ in *X* determines a bijection $\mathcal{F}_{p(0)} \overset{\sim}\to \mathcal{F}_{p(1)}.$ Moreover, two [homotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopic) paths determine the same bijection. Hence, there is the well-defined [functor](wikipedia://en/10987)

$$\Pi_1 X \to \mathbf{Set}, \, x \mapsto \mathcal{F}_x$$

where $\Pi_1 X$ is the [fundamental groupoid](wikipedia://en/55264190) of *X*: the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) whose objects are points of *X* and whose morphisms are homotopy classes of paths. Moreover, if *X* is [path-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path-connected), [locally path-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_path-connected) and [semi-locally simply connected](wikipedia://en/518118) (so *X* has a [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover)), then every functor $\Pi_1 X \to \mathbf{Set}$ is of the above form; i.e., the [functor category](wikipedia://en/407039) $\mathbf{Fct}(\Pi_1 X, \mathbf{Set})$ is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the category of locally constant sheaves on *X*.

If *X* is [locally connected](wikipedia://en/6959754), the [adjunction](wikipedia://en/53991) between the category of [presheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presheaves) and [bundles](wikipedia://en/827658) restricts to an equivalence between the category of locally constant sheaves and the category of [covering spaces](wikipedia://en/250323) of *X*.[^o8][^o9]

:::

### Equivalent definitions {#Equivalent_definitions}

#### Path-connected spaces {#Path-connected_spaces}

If *X* is [path-connected](wikipedia://en/6233#Path_connectedness), a local system $\mathcal{L}$ of abelian groups has the same stalk $L$ at every point. There is a bijective correspondence between local systems on *X* and group homomorphisms

$$\rho: \pi_1(X,x) \to \text{Aut}(L)$$

and similarly for local systems of modules. The map $\pi_1(X,x) \to \text{Aut}(L)$ giving the local system $\mathcal{L}$ is called the **[monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250) representation** of $\mathcal{L}$.

::: {#o13 .proof cue=math-proof name="Proof of equivalence"}

**Proof of equivalence.**

Take local system $\mathcal{L}$ and a loop $\gamma$ at *x*. It's easy to show that any local system on $[0,1]$ is constant. For instance, $\gamma^* \mathcal{L}$ is constant. This gives an isomorphism $(\gamma^*\mathcal{L})_0\simeq \Gamma([0,1], \mathcal{L}) \simeq (\gamma^*\mathcal{L})_1$, i.e. between $L$ and itself. Conversely, given a homomorphism $\rho: \pi_1(X,x)\to \text{Aut}(L)$, consider the *constant* sheaf $\underline{L}$ on the universal cover $\widetilde{X}$ of *X*. The deck-transform-invariant sections of $\underline{L}$ gives a local system on *X*. Similarly, the deck-transform-*ρ*-equivariant sections give another local system on *X*: for a small enough open set *U*, it is defined as

$\mathcal{L}(\rho)_U\ = \ \left\{ \text{sections }s \in \underline{L}_{\pi^{-1}(U)} \text{ with }\theta\circ s=\rho(\theta) s \text{ for all }\theta \in\text{ Deck}(\widetilde{X}/X)=\pi_1(X,x) \right\}$ where $\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X$ is the universal covering.

:::

This shows that (for *X* path-connected) a local system is precisely a sheaf whose pullback to the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of *X* is a constant sheaf.

This correspondence can be upgraded to an [equivalence of categories](wikipedia://en/379051) between the category of local systems of abelian groups on *X* and the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748) endowed with an action of $\pi_1(X,x)$ (equivalently, $\mathbb{Z}[\pi_1(X,x)]$-modules).[^o14]

#### Stronger definition on non-connected spaces {#Stronger_definition_on_non-connected_spaces}

A stronger nonequivalent definition that works for non-connected *X* is the following: a local system is a [covariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariant_functor)

$$\mathcal{L}\colon \Pi_1(X) \to \textbf{Mod}(R)$$

from the fundamental groupoid of $X$ to the category of modules over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $R$, where typically $R = \mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}$. This is equivalently the data of an assignment to every point $x\in X$ a module $M$ along with a group representation $\rho_x: \pi_1(X,x) \to \text{Aut}_R(M)$ such that the various $\rho_x$ are compatible with change of basepoint $x \to y$ and the induced map $\pi_1(X, x) \to \pi_1(X, y)$ on [fundamental groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_groups).

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

- [Constant sheaves](wikipedia://en/2783581) such as $\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X$. This is a useful tool for computing cohomology since in good situations(e.g. [paracompact](wikipedia://en/48631) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) which is [locally contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_contractible)), there is an isomorphism between sheaf cohomology and singular cohomology[^o18]:

$$H^k(X,\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X) \cong H^k_\text{sing}(X,\mathbb{Q})$$

- Let $X=\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0,0)\}$. Since $\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{(0,0)\})=\mathbb{Z}$, there is an $S^1$ family of local systems on *X* corresponding to the maps $n \mapsto e^{in\theta}$:

$$\rho_\theta: \pi_1(X; x_0) \cong \mathbb{Z} \to \text{Aut}_\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{C})$$

- Horizontal sections of vector bundles with a flat connection. If $E\to X$ is a vector bundle with flat connection $\nabla$, then there is a local system given by $$E^\nabla_U=\left\{\text{sections }s\in \Gamma(U,E) \text{ which are horizontal: }\nabla s=0\right\}$$ For instance, take $X=\mathbb{C} \setminus 0$ and $E = X \times \mathbb{C}^n$, the trivial bundle. Sections of *E* are *n*-tuples of functions on *X*, so $\nabla_0(f_1,\dots,f_n)= (df_1,\dots,df_n)$ defines a flat connection on *E*, as does $\nabla(f_1,\dots,f_n)= (df_1,\dots,df_n)-\Theta(x)(f_1,\dots,f_n)^t$ for any matrix of one-forms $\Theta$ on *X*. The horizontal sections are then\</p> $$E^\nabla_U= \left\{(f_1,\dots,f_n)\in E_U: (df_1,\dots,df_n)=\Theta (f_1,\dots,f_n)^t\right\}$$ i.e., the solutions to the linear differential equation $df_i = \sum \Theta_{ij} f_j$.If $\Theta$ extends to a one-form on $\mathbb{C}$ the above will also define a local system on $\mathbb{C}$, so will be trivial since $\pi_1(\mathbb{C}) = 0$. So to give an interesting example, choose one with a pole at *0*: $$\Theta= \begin{pmatrix} 0 & dx/x \\ dx & e^x dx \end{pmatrix}$$ in which case for $\nabla= d+ \Theta$, $$E^\nabla_U =\left\{ f_1,f_2: U \to \mathbb{C} \ \ \text{ with } f'_1= f_2/x \ \ f_2'=f_1+ e^x f_2\right\}$$
- An *n*-sheeted covering map $X\to Y$ is a local system with fibers given by the set $\{1,\dots,n\}$. Similarly, a fibre bundle with discrete fibre is a local system, because each path lifts uniquely to a given lift of its basepoint. (The definition adjusts to include set-valued local systems in the obvious way).
- A local system of *k*-vector spaces on *X* is equivalent to a *k*-linear [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) of $\pi_1(X,x)$.
- If *X* is a variety, local systems are the same thing as [D-modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-modules) which are additionally coherent *O\_X*-modules (see [O modules](wikipedia://en/44887605)).
- If the connection is not flat (i.e. its [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) is nonzero), then [parallel transport](wikipedia://en/285595) of a fibre *F\_x* over *x* around a contractible loop based at *x*\_0 may give a nontrivial automorphism of *F\_x*, so locally constant sheaves can not necessarily be defined for non-flat connections.
- The [Gauss–Manin connection](wikipedia://en/4550529) is a prominent example of a connection whose horizontal sections are studied in relation to [variation of Hodge structures](wikipedia://en/10297305#Variation_of_Hodge_structure).

:::

## Cohomology {#Cohomology}

There are several ways to define the cohomology of a local system, called **cohomology with local coefficients**, which become equivalent under mild assumptions on *X*.

- Given a locally constant sheaf $\mathcal{L}$ of abelian groups on *X*, we have the [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) groups $H^j(X,\mathcal{L})$ with coefficients in $\mathcal{L}$.
- Given a locally constant sheaf $\mathcal{L}$ of abelian groups on *X*, let $C^n(X;\mathcal{L})$ be the group of all functions *f* which map each [singular *n*-simplex](wikipedia://en/431041#Singular_simplices) $\sigma\colon\Delta^n\to X$ to a global section $f(\sigma)$ of the [inverse-image sheaf](wikipedia://en/7086661) $\sigma^{-1}\mathcal{L}$. These groups can be made into a cochain complex with differentials constructed as in usual singular cohomology. Define $H^j_\mathrm{sing}(X;\mathcal{L})$ to be the cohomology of this complex.
- The group $C_n(\widetilde{X})$ of singular *n*-chains on the universal cover of *X* has an action of $\pi_1(X,x)$ by [deck transformations](wikipedia://en/250323#Deck_transformation). Explicitly, a deck transformation $\gamma\colon\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{X}$ takes a singular *n*-simplex $\sigma\colon\Delta^n\to\widetilde{X}$ to $\gamma\circ\sigma$. Then, given an abelian group *L* equipped with an action of $\pi_1(X,x)$, one can form a cochain complex from the groups $\operatorname{Hom}_{\pi_1(X,x)}(C_n(\widetilde{X}),L)$ of $\pi_1(X,x)$-equivariant homomorphisms as above. Define $H^j_\mathrm{sing}(X;L)$ to be the cohomology of this complex.

If *X* is [paracompact](wikipedia://en/48631) and [locally contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414#Locally_contractible_spaces), then $H^j(X,\mathcal{L})\cong H^j_\mathrm{sing}(X;\mathcal{L})$.[^o20] If $\mathcal{L}$ is the local system corresponding to *L*, then there is an identification $C^n(X;\mathcal{L})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\pi_1(X,x)}(C_n(\widetilde{X}),L)$ compatible with the differentials,[^o21] so $H^j_\mathrm{sing}(X;\mathcal{L})\cong H^j_\mathrm{sing}(X;L)$.

::: {#o23 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

Local systems have a mild generalization to [constructible sheaves](wikipedia://en/26131119) -- a constructible sheaf on a locally path connected topological space $X$ is a sheaf $\mathcal{L}$ such that there exists a stratification of

$$X = \coprod X_\lambda$$

where $\mathcal{L}|_{X_\lambda}$ is a local system. These are typically found by taking the cohomology of the derived pushforward for some continuous map $f:X \to Y$. For example, if we look at the complex points of the morphism

$$f:X = \text{Proj}\left(\frac{\mathbb{C}[s,t][x,y,z]}{(st\cdot h(x,y,z))}\right) \to \text{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[s,t])$$

then the fibers over

$$\mathbb{A}^2_{s,t} - \mathbb{V}(st)$$

are the plane curve given by $h$, but the fibers over $\mathbb{V}= \mathbb{V}(st)$ are $\mathbb{P}^2$. If we take the derived pushforward $\mathbf{R}f_!(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X)$ then we get a constructible sheaf. Over $\mathbb{V}$ we have the local systems

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{R}^0f_!(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X)|_{\mathbb{V}(st)} &= \underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathbb{V}(st)} \\ \mathbf{R}^2f_!(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X)|_{\mathbb{V}(st)} &= \underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathbb{V}(st)} \\ \mathbf{R}^4f_!(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X)|_{\mathbb{V}(st)} &= \underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathbb{V}(st)} \\ \mathbf{R}^kf_!(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X)|_{\mathbb{V}(st)} &= \underline{0}_{\mathbb{V}(st)} \text{ otherwise} \end{aligned}$$

while over $\mathbb{A}^2_{s,t} - \mathbb{V}(st)$ we have the local systems

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{R}^0f_!(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X)|_{\mathbb{A}^2_{s,t} - \mathbb{V}(st)} &= \underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathbb{A}^2_{s,t} - \mathbb{V}(st)} \\ \mathbf{R}^1f_!(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X)|_{\mathbb{A}^2_{s,t} - \mathbb{V}(st)} &= \underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathbb{A}^2_{s,t} - \mathbb{V}(st)}^{\oplus 2g} \\ \mathbf{R}^2f_!(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X)|_{\mathbb{A}^2_{s,t} - \mathbb{V}(st)} &= \underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\mathbb{A}^2_{s,t} - \mathbb{V}(st)} \\ \mathbf{R}^kf_!(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X)|_{\mathbb{A}^2_{s,t} - \mathbb{V}(st)} &= \underline{0}_{\mathbb{A}^2_{s,t} - \mathbb{V}(st)} \text{ otherwise} \end{aligned}$$

where $g$ is the genus of the plane curve (which is $g = (\deg(f) - 1)(\deg(f) - 2)/2$).

:::

::: {#o25 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The cohomology with local coefficients in the module corresponding to the [orientation covering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation_covering) can be used to formulate [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) for non-orientable manifolds: see [Twisted Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/32182870).
