# László Babai

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="László Babai"}

**László "Laci" Babai** (born July 20, 1950, in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest))[^o3] is a Hungarian-American professor of computer science and mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). His research focuses on [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), [algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithms), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), and [finite groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_groups), with an emphasis on the interactions between these fields.

:::

## Life {#Life}

In 1968, Babai won a gold medal at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208). Babai studied mathematics at [Faculty of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELTE_Faculty_of_Science) of the [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) from 1968 to 1973, received a PhD from the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1975, and received a DSc from the Hungarian Academy of Sciences in 1984.[^o3] He held a teaching position at Eötvös Loránd University since 1971; in 1987 he took joint positions as a professor in [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) at Eötvös Loránd and in computer science at the University of Chicago. In 1995, he began a joint appointment in the mathematics department at Chicago and gave up his position at Eötvös Loránd.[^o3]

## Work {#Work}

He is the author of over 180 academic papers.[^o3] His notable accomplishments include the introduction of [interactive proof systems](wikipedia://en/160255),[^o7] the introduction of the term [Las Vegas algorithm](wikipedia://en/537519),[^o8] and the introduction of [group theoretic](wikipedia://en/41890) methods in [graph isomorphism](wikipedia://en/247577) testing.[^o8] In November 2015, he announced a [quasipolynomial time](wikipedia://en/405944#Quasi-polynomial_time) algorithm for the [graph isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/1950766).[^o9][^o10]

He is editor-in-chief of the refereed online journal *[Theory of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_Computing_%28journal%29)*.[^o11] Babai was also involved in the creation of the [Budapest Semesters in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/8520286) program and coined the name.

### Graph isomorphism in quasipolynomial time {#Graph_isomorphism_in_quasipolynomial_time}

After announcing the result in 2015,[^o10][^o13][^o14] Babai presented a paper proving that the [graph isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/1950766) can be solved in [quasi-polynomial time](wikipedia://en/25269049) in 2016, at the ACM [Symposium on Theory of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symposium_on_Theory_of_Computing).[^o15] In response to an error discovered by [Harald Helfgott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Helfgott), he posted an update in 2017.[^o16]

## Honors {#Honors}

In 1988, Babai won the Hungarian State Prize, in 1990 he was elected as a corresponding member of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences, and in 1994 he became a full member.[^o3] In 1999 the [Budapest University of Technology and Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_University_of_Technology_and_Economics) awarded him an honorary doctorate.[^o3]

In 1993, Babai was awarded the [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize) together with [Shafi Goldwasser](wikipedia://en/842178), [Silvio Micali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Micali), [Shlomo Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo_Moran), and [Charles Rackoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Rackoff), for their papers on interactive proof systems.[^o21]

In 2005, he received the [Quantrell Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantrell_Award).[^o22]

In 2015, he was elected[^o23] a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), and won the [Knuth Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knuth_Prize).

Babai was an invited speaker at the [International Congresses of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto) (1990), [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) (1994, plenary talk), and [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro) (2018).
