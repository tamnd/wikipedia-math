# Banach bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Banach bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Banach bundle** is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) each of whose fibres is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989), i.e. a [complete](wikipedia://en/7832) [normed vector space](wikipedia://en/21538), possibly of infinite dimension.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition of a Banach bundle"}

## Definition of a Banach bundle {#Definition_of_a_Banach_bundle}

Let *M* be a [Banach manifold](wikipedia://en/2504397) of class *C*<sup>*p*</sup> with *p* ≥ 0, called the **base space**; let *E* be a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), called the **total space**; let *π* : *E* → *M* be a [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29). Suppose that for each point *x* ∈ *M*, the [fibre](wikipedia://en/6603892) *E*<sub>*x*</sub> = *π*<sup>−1</sup>(*x*) has been given the structure of a Banach space. Let

$$\{ U_{i} | i \in I \}$$

be an [open cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover) of *M*. Suppose also that for each *i* ∈ *I*, there is a Banach space *X*<sub>*i*</sub> and a map *τ*<sub>*i*</sub>

$$\tau_{i} : \pi^{-1} (U_{i}) \to U_{i} \times X_{i}$$

such that

- the map *τ*<sub>*i*</sub> is a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) commuting with the projection onto *U*<sub>*i*</sub>, i.e. the following [diagram commutes](wikipedia://en/138484):

*Image: CommDiag Local Triv Banach Bundle.png.*

and for each *x* ∈ *U*<sub>*i*</sub> the induced map *τ*<sub>*ix*</sub> on the fibre *E*<sub>*x*</sub>

$$\tau_{ix} : \pi^{-1} (x) \to X_{i}$$

is an [invertible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertible_function) [continuous linear map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_linear_map), i.e. an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752);

- if *U*<sub>*i*</sub> and *U*<sub>*j*</sub> are two members of the open cover, then the map

$$U_{i} \cap U_{j} \to \mathrm{Lin}(X_{i}; X_{j})$$

$$x \mapsto (\tau_{j} \circ \tau_{i}^{-1})_{x}$$

is a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) (a differentiable map of class *C*<sup>*p*</sup>), where Lin(*X*; *Y*) denotes the space of all continuous linear maps from a topological vector space *X* to another topological vector space *Y*.

The collection {(*U*<sub>*i*</sub>, *τ*<sub>*i*</sub>)|*i*∈*I*} is called a **trivialising covering** for *π* : *E* → *M*, and the maps *τ*<sub>*i*</sub> are called **trivialising maps**. Two trivialising coverings are said to be **equivalent** if their union again satisfies the two conditions above. An [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) of such trivialising coverings is said to determine the structure of a **Banach bundle** on *π* : *E* → *M*.

If all the spaces *X*<sub>*i*</sub> are isomorphic as topological vector spaces, then they can be assumed all to be equal to the same space *X*. In this case, *π* : *E* → *M* is said to be a **Banach bundle with fibre** *X*. If *M* is a [connected space](wikipedia://en/6233) then this is necessarily the case, since the set of points *x* ∈ *M* for which there is a trivialising map

$$\tau_{ix} : \pi^{-1} (x) \to X$$

for a given space *X* is both [open](wikipedia://en/39358) and [closed](wikipedia://en/47279).

In the finite-dimensional case, the second condition above is implied by the first.

:::

## Examples of Banach bundles {#Examples_of_Banach_bundles}

- If *V* is any Banach space, the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) T<sub>*x*</sub>*V* to *V* at any point *x* ∈ *V* is isomorphic in an obvious way to *V* itself. The [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) T*V* of *V* is then a Banach bundle with the usual projection

$$\pi : \mathrm{T} V \to V;$$

$$(x, v) \mapsto x.$$

This bundle is "trivial" in the sense that T*V* admits a globally defined trivialising map: the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069)

$$\tau = \mathrm{id} : \pi^{-1} (V) = \mathrm{T} V \to V \times V;$$

$$(x, v) \mapsto (x, v).$$

- If *M* is any Banach manifold, the tangent bundle T*M* of *M* forms a Banach bundle with respect to the usual projection, but it may not be trivial.
- Similarly, the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) T\**M*, whose fibre over a point *x* ∈ *M* is the [topological dual space](wikipedia://en/7988#Continuous_dual_space) to the tangent space at *x*:

$$\pi^{-1} (x) = \mathrm{T}_{x}^{*} M = (\mathrm{T}_{x} M)^{*};$$

also forms a Banach bundle with respect to the usual projection onto *M*.

- There is a connection between [Bochner spaces](wikipedia://en/6751583) and Banach bundles. Consider, for example, the Bochner space *X* = *L*²(\[0, *T*\]; *H*<sup>1</sup>(Ω)), which might arise as a useful object when studying the [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415) on a domain Ω. One might seek solutions *σ* ∈ *X* to the heat equation; for each time *t*, *σ*(*t*) is a function in the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) *H*<sup>1</sup>(Ω). One could also think of *Y* = \[0, *T*\] × *H*<sup>1</sup>(Ω), which as a [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) also has the structure of a Banach bundle over the manifold \[0, *T*\] with fibre *H*<sup>1</sup>(Ω), in which case elements/solutions *σ* ∈ *X* are [cross sections](wikipedia://en/367621) of the bundle *Y* of some specified regularity (*L*², in fact). If the differential geometry of the problem in question is particularly relevant, the Banach bundle point of view might be advantageous.

## Morphisms of Banach bundles {#Morphisms_of_Banach_bundles}

The collection of all Banach bundles can be made into a category by defining appropriate morphisms.

Let *π* : *E* → *M* and *π*′ : *E*′ → *M*′ be two Banach bundles. A **Banach bundle morphism** from the first bundle to the second consists of a pair of morphisms

$$f_{0} : M \to M';$$

$$f : E \to E'.$$

For *f* to be a morphism means simply that *f* is a continuous map of topological spaces. If the manifolds *M* and *M*′ are both of class *C*<sup>*p*</sup>, then the requirement that *f*<sub>0</sub> be a morphism is the requirement that it be a *p*-times continuously [differentiable function](wikipedia://en/330206). These two morphisms are required to satisfy two conditions (again, the second one is redundant in the finite-dimensional case):

- the diagram

*Image: CommDiag Banach Bundle Morphism.png.*

commutes, and, for each *x* ∈ *M*, the induced map

$$f_{x} : E_{x} \to E'_{f_{0} (x)}$$

is a continuous linear map;

- for each *x*<sub>0</sub> ∈ *M* there exist trivialising maps

$$\tau : \pi^{-1} (U) \to U \times X$$

$$\tau' : \pi'^{-1} (U') \to U' \times X'$$

such that *x*<sub>0</sub> ∈ *U*, *f*<sub>0</sub>(*x*<sub>0</sub>) ∈ *U*′,

$$f_{0} (U) \subseteq U'$$

and the map

$$U \to \mathrm{Lin}(X; X')$$

$$x \mapsto \tau'_{f_{0} (x)} \circ f_{x} \circ \tau^{-1}$$

is a morphism (a differentiable map of class *C*<sup>*p*</sup>).

## Pull-back of a Banach bundle {#Pull-back_of_a_Banach_bundle}

One can take a Banach bundle over one manifold and use the [pull-back](wikipedia://en/1665392) construction to define a new Banach bundle on a second manifold.

Specifically, let *π* : *E* → *N* be a Banach bundle and *f* : *M* → *N* a differentiable map (as usual, everything is *C*<sup>*p*</sup>). Then the **pull-back** of *π* : *E* → *N* is the Banach bundle *f*\**π* : *f*\**E* → *M* satisfying the following properties:

- for each *x* ∈ *M*, (*f*\**E*)<sub>*x*</sub> = *E*<sub>*f*(*x*)</sub>;
- there is a commutative diagram

*Image: CommDiag Pullback Banach Bundle 1.png.*

with the top horizontal map being the identity on each fibre;

- if *E* is trivial, i.e. equal to *N* × *X* for some Banach space *X*, then *f*\**E* is also trivial and equal to *M* × *X*, and

$$f^{*} \pi : f^{*} E = M \times X \to M$$

is the projection onto the first coordinate;

- if *V* is an open subset of *N* and *U* = *f*<sup>−1</sup>(*V*), then

$$f^{*} (E_{V}) = (f^{*} E)_{U}$$

and there is a commutative diagram

*Image: CommDiag Pullback Banach Bundle 2.png.*

where the maps at the "front" and "back" are the same as those in the previous diagram, and the maps from "back" to "front" are (induced by) the inclusions.
