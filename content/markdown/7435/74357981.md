# Philomena Nyarko

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Philomena Nyarko"}

**Philomena Efua Nyarko** is a [Ghanaian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana) statistician and academic, and the former Government Statistician for Ghana, chief executive of the [Ghana Statistical Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Statistical_Service) (GSS).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Nyarko has a PhD in [social statistics](wikipedia://en/285943), awarded by the [University of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southampton), United Kingdom.[^o4] She also studied at the [University of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ghana), and was awarded a graduate diploma in [population studies](wikipedia://en/16081079).[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Nyarko started working for the Ghana Statistical Service in 1987.[^o4] During her time there, she worked on the Ghana Demographic and Health Survey 1995.[^o8] She left the organisation in 1996 and worked at the Navrongo Health Research Centre, leading the Demographic Surveillance System.[^o4][^o5] She taught at the University of Ghana from 2001 to 2010, focusing on technical demography, basic statistics, and advanced quantitative analysis.[^o4][^o5] During her time there she also worked for the [Population Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_Council) on [reproductive health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_and_reproductive_health).[^o4][^o5]

Nyarko was appointed acting Government Statistician in 2010.[^o4][^o5] She was appointed to the post permanently in 2013.[^o4][^o5][^o9][^o10][^o11] Her predecessor had been [Grace Bediako](wikipedia://en/51451669).[^o10] Nyarko remained in post until 2016.[^o4][^o5][^o10]

As Government Statistician, she was responsible for the 2014 Ghana Demographic and Health Survey report.[^o3] She also worked on the National Strategy for the Development of Statistics and the Strategic Plan for the Improvement of the [Civil Registration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_registration) and Vital Statistics System in Ghana.[^o4] She set up collaboration between the GSS and [Accra Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_Technical_University) to give students access to statistical training.[^o12]

On leaving the Ghana Statistical Service, Nyarko worked as a consultant and was on the Expert Group of the Regional Report of the African [Gender and Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_Development_Index).[^o4][^o5] She is also on the board of the African Digital Rights Hub, as vice-chair.[^o4][^o5] She is on the board of the [University for Development Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_for_Development_Studies).[^o4] Nyarko is also working on the Second Phase of the Global Strategy to Improve Agricultural and Rural Statistics.[^o5]

She has been listed by the *[Graphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Graphic_%28Ghana%29)* as an example of women participating in governance in Ghana, and in the *Junior Graphic* as a successful female leader.[^o9][^o13]

Nyarko has said that access to and take-up of [contraception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_control) should be improved in Ghana, or there will be adverse effects for the country's development.[^o14] She has also argued for an increase in the [legal age of marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriageable_age) in Ghana from 18 to 23, in order to decrease population growth and improve women's health.[^o15]

With extensive teaching and research expertise, Dr. Philomena Efua Nyarko has offered technical guidance and strategic leadership to numerous public and private sector organizations.[^o16]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Nyarko is married and lives in [Kumasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumasi).[^o18] In 2021 she was reported missing.[^o18]
