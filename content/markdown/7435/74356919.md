# Paul Butzer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Butzer"}

**Paul Leo Butzer** (15 April 1928 – 9 September 2026) was a German mathematician who specialised in [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) ([approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713), [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147)). He was born in Germany but studied in Montreal and Toronto, Canada. He returned to Germany for his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) and then taught at the [RWTH Aachen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWTH_Aachen_University) from 1958. Besides mathematics, he was interested in its history and studied the life and work of mathematicians, beginning in the Carolingian era, publishing a biography of [Elwin Bruno Christoffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elwin_Bruno_Christoffel). Some of his scientific works were translated.

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Butzer was born in [Mülheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mülheim) on 15 April 1928,[^o4] the son of an engineer and a mother who studied mathematics at [RWTH Aachen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWTH_Aachen_University) who had met as students.[^o4] Paul grew up with a younger brother, [Karl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_W._Butzer), who also became a scientist.[^o4][^o5] As opponents of the Nazis, Butzer's parents left Germany with their children in 1937 and moved to England. During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), they relocated to Canada, where Butzer attended school in [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal) and studied mathematics at Loyola College (later [Concordia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_University)), completing his bachelor's degree in 1948.[^o4] He then pursued further studies at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto), including studying under [Harold Scott MacDonald Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter) and [William Tutte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tutte), and obtained his Ph.D. in 1951 under the supervision of [George G. Lorentz](wikipedia://en/58614550) (on [Bernstein polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernstein_polynomials)).[^o4][^o6] In 1952, he became a lecturer and then an assistant professor at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University). In 1955-56, he lived in Paris and then in [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz). He decided to stay in Germany, where he completed his habilitation at the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg), taught [in Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Würzburg), and starting in 1958, at RWTH Aachen University. In 1962, he was appointed professor there. In 1963, he began organizing international conferences on [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713) at the [Oberwolfach Research Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1368669), later together with [Béla Szőkefalvi-Nagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Béla_Szőkefalvi-Nagy).[^o4][^o6]

In addition to approximation theory and its connections to [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659) and semigroups of operators in [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), Butzer also worked on probability theory ([central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406) and related [convergence tests](wikipedia://en/4576951) issues), [sampling theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_Theory), and [signal analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_analysis).[^o7]

He also delved into the history of mathematics, particularly in its connection with [Aachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aachen). He studied figures such as [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002), [Eduard Helly](wikipedia://en/2202570), [Eugène Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugène_Catalan), [Pafnuty Chebyshev](wikipedia://en/149568), [Charles Jean de la Vallée Poussin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Jean_de_la_Vallée_Poussin), the history of [splines](wikipedia://en/457680), [Otto Blumenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Blumenthal), mathematics in the [Carolingian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian-era), and [Elwin Bruno Christoffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elwin_Bruno_Christoffel), on whom he published a book.[^o4]

Butzer was a member of the Royal Society of Sciences in Liège and the [Royal Belgian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Belgian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o8] He was an honorary member of the [Mathematical Society in Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Mathematical_Society).[^o9] He received honorary doctorates from three universities: [Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liège), [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_York), and [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_University_of_Timișoara).[^o4]

Butzer died on 9 September 2026, at the age of 98.[^o10]
