# Hopf manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hopf manifold"}

In [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), a **Hopf manifold** (Hopf 1948) is obtained as a quotient of the complex [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) (with zero deleted) $({\mathbb C}^n\backslash 0)$ by a [free action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $\Gamma \cong {\mathbb Z}$ of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), with the generator $\gamma$ of $\Gamma$ acting by holomorphic [contractions](wikipedia://en/6239). Here, a *holomorphic contraction* is a map $\gamma:\; {\mathbb C}^n \to {\mathbb C}^n$ such that a sufficiently big iteration $\;\gamma^N$ maps any given [compact subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_subset) of ${\mathbb C}^n$ onto an arbitrarily small [neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) of 0.

:::

Two-dimensional Hopf manifolds are called [Hopf surfaces](wikipedia://en/7016769).

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

In a typical situation, $\Gamma$ is generated by a linear contraction, usually a [diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/174080) $q\cdot Id$, with $q\in {\mathbb C}$ a [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826), $0<|q|<1$. Such manifold is called *a classical Hopf manifold*.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A Hopf manifold $H:=({\mathbb C}^n\backslash 0)/{\mathbb Z}$ is [diffeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphic) to $S^{2n-1}\times S^1$. For $n\geq 2$, it is non-[Kähler](wikipedia://en/390538). In fact, it is not even symplectic because the second cohomology group is zero.

:::

## Hypercomplex structure {#Hypercomplex_structure}

Even-dimensional Hopf manifolds admit [hypercomplex structure](wikipedia://en/6990840). The Hopf surface is the only compact [hypercomplex manifold](wikipedia://en/6990840) of quaternionic dimension 1 which is not [hyperkähler](wikipedia://en/646120).
