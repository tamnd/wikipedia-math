# Lax equivalence theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lax equivalence theorem"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), the **Lax equivalence theorem** is a fundamental theorem in the analysis of linear [finite difference methods](wikipedia://en/6054681) for the numerical solution of linear [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). It states that for a linear [consistent](wikipedia://en/272829#Consistency_and_order) finite difference method for a [well-posed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-posed) linear [initial value problem](wikipedia://en/498383), the method is [convergent](wikipedia://en/272829#Convergence) if and only if it is [stable](wikipedia://en/233807#Stability_in_numerical_differential_equations).[^o2]

:::

The importance of the theorem is that while the convergence of the solution of the linear finite difference method to the solution of the linear partial differential equation is what is desired, it is ordinarily difficult to establish because the numerical method is defined by a [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806) while the [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309) involves a [differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206) function. However, consistency—the requirement that the linear finite difference method approximates the correct linear partial differential equation—is straightforward to verify, and stability is typically much easier to show than convergence (and would be needed in any event to show that [round-off error](wikipedia://en/432450) will not destroy the computation). Hence convergence is usually shown via the Lax equivalence theorem.

Stability in this context means that a [matrix norm](wikipedia://en/1543735) of the matrix used in the iteration is at most [unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_%28mathematics%29), called (practical) Lax–Richtmyer stability.[^o3] Often a [von Neumann stability analysis](wikipedia://en/20625645) is substituted for convenience, although von Neumann stability only implies Lax–Richtmyer stability in certain cases.

This theorem is due to [Peter Lax](wikipedia://en/486365). It is sometimes called the **Lax–Richtmyer theorem**, after Peter Lax and [Robert D. Richtmyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_D._Richtmyer).[^o4]
