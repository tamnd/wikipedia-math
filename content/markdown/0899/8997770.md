# List of graphs

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of graphs"}

This partial **list of [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806)** contains definitions of graphs and graph families. For collected definitions of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) terms that do not refer to individual graph types, such as *vertex* and *path*, see [Glossary of graph theory](wikipedia://en/325802). For links to existing articles about particular kinds of graphs, see . Some of the finite structures considered in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) have names, sometimes inspired by the graph's topology, and sometimes after their discoverer. A famous example is the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926), a concrete graph on 10 vertices that appears as a minimal example or counterexample in many different contexts.

:::

## Individual graphs {#Individual_graphs}

## Highly symmetric graphs {#Highly_symmetric_graphs}

### Strongly regular graphs {#Strongly_regular_graphs}

The [strongly regular graph](wikipedia://en/1399856) on *v* vertices and rank *k* is usually denoted srg(*v,k*,λ,μ).

### Symmetric graphs {#Symmetric_graphs}

A [symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/1577896) is one in which there is a symmetry ([graph automorphism](wikipedia://en/15094186)) taking any ordered pair of adjacent vertices to any other ordered pair; the [Foster census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_census) lists all small symmetric 3-regular graphs. Every strongly regular graph is symmetric, but not vice versa.

## Graph families {#Graph_families}

### Complete graphs {#Complete_graphs}

The [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) on $n$ vertices is often called the *$n$-clique* and usually denoted $K_n$, from German *komplett*.[^o9]

### Complete bipartite graphs {#Complete_bipartite_graphs}

The [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) is usually denoted $K_{n,m}$. For $n=1$ see the section on star graphs. The graph $K_{2,2}$ equals the 4-cycle $C_4$ (the square) introduced below.

### Cycles {#Cycles}

The [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) on $n$ vertices is called the *n-cycle* and usually denoted $C_n$. It is also called a *cyclic graph*, a *polygon* or the *n-gon*. Special cases are the *triangle* $C_3$, the *square* $C_4$, and then several with Greek naming *pentagon* $C_5$, *hexagon* $C_6$, etc.

### Friendship graphs {#Friendship_graphs}

The [friendship graph](wikipedia://en/24363442) *F<sub>n</sub>* can be constructed by joining *n* copies of the [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) *C*<sub>3</sub> with a common vertex.[^o13]

*Image: Friendship graphs.svg.* The friendship graphs *F*<sub>2</sub>, *F*<sub>3</sub> and *F*<sub>4</sub>.

### Fullerene graphs {#Fullerene_graphs}

In graph theory, a **[fullerene](wikipedia://en/10628)** is any [polyhedral graph](wikipedia://en/24656270) with all faces of size 5 or 6 (including the external face). It follows from [Euler's polyhedron formula](wikipedia://en/148420), *V* − *E* + *F* = 2 (where *V*, *E*, *F* indicate the number of vertices, edges, and faces), that there are exactly 12 pentagons in a fullerene and *h* = *V*/2 − 10 hexagons. Therefore *V* = 20 + 2*h*; *E* = 30 + 3*h*. Fullerene graphs are the [Schlegel representations](wikipedia://en/7201595) of the corresponding fullerene compounds.

An algorithm to generate all the non-isomorphic fullerenes with a given number of hexagonal faces has been developed by G. Brinkmann and A. Dress.[^o15] G. Brinkmann also provided a freely available implementation, called [fullgen](http://cs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/plantri/).

### Platonic solids {#Platonic_solids}

The [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) on four vertices forms the skeleton of the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606), and more generally the complete graphs form skeletons of [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781). The [hypercube graphs](wikipedia://en/6706815) are also skeletons of higher-dimensional regular [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471).

### Truncated solids {#Truncated_solids}

### Snarks {#Snarks}

A [snark](wikipedia://en/727811) is a [bridgeless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeless_graph) [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) that requires four colors in any proper [edge coloring](wikipedia://en/690647). The smallest snark is the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926), already listed above.

### Star {#Star}

A [star](wikipedia://en/19310129) *S*<sub>*k*</sub> is the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) *K*<sub>1,*k*</sub>. The star *S*<sub>3</sub> is called the claw graph.

*Image: Star graphs.svg.* The star graphs *S*<sub>3</sub>, *S*<sub>4</sub>, *S*<sub>5</sub> and *S*<sub>6</sub>.

### Wheel graphs {#Wheel_graphs}

The [wheel graph](wikipedia://en/1655876) *W<sub>n</sub>* is a graph on *n* vertices constructed by connecting a single vertex to every vertex in an (*n* − 1)-cycle.

*Image: Wheel graphs.svg.* Wheels $W_4$ – $W_9$.

## Other graphs {#Other_graphs}

This partial list contains definitions of graphs and graph families which are known by particular names, but do not have a Wikipedia article of their own.

::: {#o23 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Gear graph"}

### Gear {#Gear}

*Image: Gear graph.svg.* *G*<sub>*4*</sub>

A **gear graph**, denoted *G*<sub>*n*</sub>, is a graph obtained by inserting an extra vertex between each pair of adjacent vertices on the perimeter of a [wheel graph](wikipedia://en/1655876) *W*<sub>*n*</sub>. Thus, *G*<sub>*n*</sub> has 2*n*+1 vertices and 3*n* edges. Gear graphs are examples of [squaregraphs](wikipedia://en/20741076), and play a key role in the [forbidden graph characterization](wikipedia://en/13547826) of squaregraphs.[^o25] Gear graphs are also known as **cogwheels** and **bipartite wheels**.

:::

::: {#o27 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Helm graph"}

### Helm {#Helm}

A **helm graph**, denoted ***H<sub>n</sub>***, is a graph obtained by attaching a single edge and node to each node of the outer circuit of a [wheel graph](wikipedia://en/1655876) ***W<sub>n</sub>***.[^o29]

:::

::: {#o31 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Lobster graph"}

### Lobster {#Lobster}

A **lobster** graph is a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) in which all the vertices are within distance 2 of a central [path](wikipedia://en/638889).[^o32] Compare [*caterpillar*](wikipedia://en/27258886).

:::

### Web {#Web}

*Image: Cube graph.svg.* The web graph *W*<sub>4,2</sub> is a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285).

The **web** graph *W*<sub>*n*,*r*</sub> is a graph consisting of *r* concentric copies of the [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) *C*<sub>*n*</sub>, with corresponding vertices connected by "spokes". Thus *W*<sub>*n*,1</sub> is the same graph as *C*<sub>*n*</sub>, and *W*<sub>n,2</sub> is a [prism](wikipedia://en/283007).

A web graph has also been defined as a prism graph ***Y*<sub>*n*+1, 3</sub>**, with the edges of the outer cycle removed.[^o29]
