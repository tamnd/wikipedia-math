# Hypsometric equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hypsometric equation"}

The **hypsometric equation**, also known as the **thickness equation**, relates an [atmospheric pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_pressure) ratio to the equivalent thickness of an atmospheric layer considering the layer mean of [virtual temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_temperature), [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity), and occasionally [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind). It is derived from the [hydrostatic equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrostatic_equation) and the [ideal gas law](wikipedia://en/59881).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

The hypsometric equation is expressed as:[^o4]

$$h = z_2 - z_1 = \frac{R \cdot \overline{T_v}}{g} \, \ln \left(\frac{p_1}{p_2}\right),$$

where:

- $h$ = thickness of the layer \[m\],
- $z$ = geometric height \[m\],
- $R$ = specific [gas constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_constant) for dry air,
- $\overline{T_v}$ = mean [virtual temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_temperature) in [Kelvin](wikipedia://en/19593121) \[K\],
- $g$ = [gravitational acceleration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gravitational_acceleration) \[m/s<sup>2</sup>\],
- $p$ = [pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure) \[[Pa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_%28unit%29)\].

In [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904), $p_1$ and $p_2$ are isobaric surfaces. In [radiosonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiosonde) observation, the hypsometric equation can be used to compute the height of a pressure level given the height of a reference pressure level and the mean virtual temperature in between. Then, the newly computed height can be used as a new reference level to compute the height of the next level given the mean virtual temperature in between, and so on.

:::

## Derivation {#Derivation}

The hydrostatic equation:

$$p = \rho \cdot g \cdot z,$$

where $\rho$ is the [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) \[kg/m<sup>3</sup>\], is used to generate the equation for [hydrostatic equilibrium](wikipedia://en/51776), written in [differential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_%28infinitesimal%29) form:

$$dp = - \rho \cdot g \cdot dz.$$

This is combined with the [ideal gas law](wikipedia://en/59881):

$$p = \rho \cdot R \cdot T_v$$

to eliminate $\rho$:

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}p}{p} = \frac{-g}{R \cdot T_v} \, \mathrm{d}z.$$

This is integrated from $z_1$ to $z_2$:

$$\int_{p(z_1)}^{p(z_2)} \frac{\mathrm{d}p}{p} = \int_{z_1}^{z_2}\frac{-g}{R \cdot T_v} \, \mathrm{d}z.$$

*R* and *g* are constant with *z*, so they can be brought outside the integral. If temperature varies linearly with *z* (e.g., given a small change in *z*), it can also be brought outside the integral when replaced with $\overline{T_v}$, the average virtual temperature between $z_1$ and $z_2$.

$$\int_{p(z_1)}^{p(z_2)} \frac{\mathrm{d}p}{p} = \frac{-g}{R \cdot \overline{T_v}}\int_{z_1}^{z_2} \, \mathrm{d}z.$$

Integration gives

$$\ln \left( \frac{p(z_2)}{p(z_1)} \right) = \frac{-g}{R \cdot \overline{T_v}} (z_2 - z_1),$$

simplifying to

$$\ln \left( \frac{p_1}{p_2} \right) = \frac{g}{R \cdot \overline{T_v}} (z_2 - z_1).$$

Rearranging:

$$z_2 - z_1 = \frac{R \cdot \overline{T_v}}{g} \ln \left( \frac{p_1}{p_2} \right),$$

or, eliminating the natural log:

$$\frac{p_1}{p_2} = e^{\frac{g}{R \cdot \overline{T_v}} \cdot (z_2 - z_1)}.$$

## Correction {#Correction}

The [Eötvös effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_effect) can be taken into account as a correction to the hypsometric equation. Physically, using a [frame of reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_of_reference) that rotates with Earth, an [air mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_mass) moving eastward effectively weighs less, which corresponds to an increase in thickness between pressure levels, and vice versa. The corrected hypsometric equation follows:[^o7]

$$h = z_2 - z_1 = \frac{R \cdot \overline{T_v}}{g(1+A)} \cdot \ln \left(\frac{p_1}{p_2}\right),$$

where the correction due to the [Eötvös effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_effect), A, can be expressed as follows:

$$A = -\frac{1}{g} \left(2 \Omega \overline{u} \cos \phi + \frac{\overline{u}^2 + \overline{v}^2}{r}\right),$$

where

- $\Omega$ = Earth rotation rate,
- $\phi$ = latitude,
- $r$ = distance from Earth center to the air mass,
- $\overline{u}$ = mean velocity in longitudinal direction (east-west), and
- $\overline{v}$ = mean velocity in latitudinal direction (north-south).

This correction is considerable in tropical large-scale atmospheric motion.
