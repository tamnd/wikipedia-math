# John of Palermo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John of Palermo"}

**John of Palermo** (1221–1240) was a translator of mathematical works from [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) to [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) who lived in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo), Sicily. He worked in the court of [Emperor Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor).

:::

John had been introduced into the court of Frederick II through the mathematician Domenico Ispano. John is mentioned by [Leonardo Fibonacci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Fibonacci) in his *[Liber quadratorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Squares)* (1225) and several problems from Arab texts by [Omar Khayyam](wikipedia://en/92550) were posed to Fibonacci.[^o2] Some court documents mention a Johannes de Panormo who is thought to be the same person. John translated an Arab [manuscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuscript), possibly by [Ibn al-Haytham](wikipedia://en/1645), on the parabola into Latin as the *De duabus lineis semper approximantibus sibi invicem et nunquam concurrentibus*.[^o3] John as noted as a "notarius" and there are indications that Frederick was to send him to [Tunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunis) in 1240.[^o4]
