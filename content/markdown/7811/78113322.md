# Ewa Ligocka

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ewa Ligocka"}

**Ewa Ligocka** (13 October 1947 – 28 October 2022) was a Polish mathematician specializing in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), and also a [political activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_activist).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Ligocka was born in [Katowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katowice) on 13 October 1947, the daughter of Polish photography critic and historian Alfred Ligocki. As a high school student under the tutelage of Teodor Paliczka, she competed for Poland in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) in 1965.

Ligocka earned a master's degree at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) in 1970 and completed a Ph.D. there in 1973 under the supervision of [Wiesław Żelazko](wikipedia://en/81590569). During this period, her research concerned the theory of [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) on [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752). In 1972, she reportedly plucked and cooked the goose given to [Per Enflo](wikipedia://en/7180897) as the prize for solving [Mazur's goose problem](wikipedia://en/4233115#History).

## Career and later life {#Career_and_later_life}

After completing her doctorate, Ligocka continued as a researcher at the University of Warsaw. As an assistant professor in 1976, she signed an [open letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_letter) regarding the [June 1976 Polish protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_1976_in_Polish_protests) in [Radom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radom) and [Ursus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursus,_Warsaw). Despite the efforts of other mathematicians to protect her, this act of protest led to her transfer to a branch campus of the university in [Białystok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Białystok), and then to her dismissal from the university in 1977.

Meanwhile, Ligocka had begun working with Maciej Skwarczyński on the [Bergman kernel](wikipedia://en/3205923). By 1978, she began her research with [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) student Steven R. Bell on the [Feferman–Vaught theorem](wikipedia://en/69955049) on the smooth extension of [biholomorphisms](wikipedia://en/6077963) to the boundaries of their domains. This work, which was published in *[Inventiones Mathematicae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventiones_Mathematicae)* in 1980,[^o5] had already created a stir in Polish mathematics in the late 1970s, and in 1979 she was hired by [Czesław Olech](wikipedia://en/31633829) as a researcher at the [Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences](wikipedia://en/67042302) without any political restrictions.

Ligocka completed a [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1986, and in 1992 returned to the University of Warsaw as an associate professor. She was given the degree of professor in 1994. She retired in 2008, and died on 28 October 2022.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Ligocka was the 1986 recipient of the Stanisław Zaremba Grand Prize of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791). She and Steven R. Bell received the 1991 [Stefan Bergman Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Bergman_Prize) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for their work on the [Feferman–Vaught theorem](wikipedia://en/69955049).
