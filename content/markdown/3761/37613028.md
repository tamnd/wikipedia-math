# Pseudo-finite field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pseudo-finite field"}

In mathematics, a **pseudo-finite field** *F* is an infinite [model](wikipedia://en/4055928) of the [first-order](wikipedia://en/10983) [theory](wikipedia://en/3512103) of [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615). This is equivalent to the condition that *F* is [quasi-finite](wikipedia://en/4644135) (perfect with a unique [extension](wikipedia://en/11634) of every positive degree) and [pseudo algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo_algebraically_closed) (every absolutely [irreducible variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_variety) over *F* has a point defined over *F*).

:::

Every [hyperfinite field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperfinite_field) is pseudo-finite and every pseudo-finite field is quasifinite. Every non-principal [ultraproduct](wikipedia://en/273642) of finite fields is pseudo-finite.

Pseudo-finite fields were introduced by [James Ax](wikipedia://en/6165715) in 1968.[^o2]
