# Michael Christopher Wendl

::: {#o5 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Christopher Wendl"}

**Michael Christopher Wendl** is a mathematician and biomedical engineer who has worked on [DNA sequencing theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_sequencing_theory),[^o6] covering and matching problems in probability, theoretical fluid mechanics, and co-wrote [Phred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phred_base_calling).[^o7] He was a scientist on the [Human Genome Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project) and has done bioinformatics and biostatistics work in cancer. Wendl is of ethnic German heritage and is the son of the aerospace engineer [Michael J. Wendl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Wendl).[^o8]

:::

## Research Work {#Research_Work}

### Theoretical Fluid Mechanics {#Theoretical_Fluid_Mechanics}

The problem of low Reynolds number flow in the gap between 2 infinite cylinders, so-called [Couette flow](wikipedia://en/2041980), was solved in 1845 by [Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gabriel_Stokes).[^o11] Wendl reported the generalization of this solution for finite-length cylinders,[^o4][^o12] which can actually be built for experimental work, in 1999, as a series of [modified Bessel functions](wikipedia://en/4700) $I_1$ and $K_1$. He also examined a variety of other low Reynolds number rotational devices and shear-driven devices, including a general form of the unsteady disk flow problem, for which the velocity profile is:[^o13]

$$u(r,z,t) = \Re\left(e^{i\sigma t}\left[\frac{r\cdot\sinh(1+i)\beta z}{\sinh(1+i)\beta \phi}\right] + \frac{2}{\phi}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^j\cdot\alpha_j^2\cdot\sin(\alpha_j z)\cdot I_1\left(\sqrt{i R \sigma + \alpha_j^2} r\right)}{(i R \sigma + \alpha_j^2)\cdot I_1\left(\sqrt{i R \sigma + \alpha_j^2}\right)}\right)$$

where $\sigma$, $R$, $\beta$, and $\phi$ are physical parameters, $\alpha_j$ are eigen-values, and $(r, z, t)$ are coordinates. This result united prior-published special cases for steady flow, infinite disks, etc.[^o13]

### Covering and Matching Problems in Probability {#Covering_and_Matching_Problems_in_Probability}

Wendl examined a number of matching and covering problems in combinatorial probability, especially as these problems apply to molecular biology. He determined the distribution of match counts of pairs of integer [multisets](wikipedia://en/305303) in terms of [Bell polynomials](wikipedia://en/572498),[^o15] a problem directly relevant to [physical mapping of DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_mapping). Prior to this, investigators had used a number of ad-hoc quantifiers, like the [Sulston score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulston_score), which idealized match trials as being independent. His result for the [multiple-group birthday proposition](wikipedia://en/73242)[^o16] solves various related "collision problems", e.g. some types of [P2P searching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer).[^o17] He has also examined a variety of 1-dimensional covering problems (see review by [Cyril Domb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Domb)[^o18]), generalizing the basic configuration to forms relevant to molecular biology.[^o19][^o20] His covering investigation of rare DNA variants with [Richard K. Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_K._Wilson)[^o21] played a role in designing the [1000 Genomes Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1000_Genomes_Project).[^o22]

### Bioinformatics and Biostatistics {#Bioinformatics_and_Biostatistics}

Wendl co-wrote [Phred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phred_base_calling), a widely used DNA trace analyzer that converted raw output stream of early DNA sequence machines to sequence strings.[^o24][^o25] He has contributed extensively to biostatistical analysis of cancer studies[^o26][^o27] and to the bioinformatics toolbase,[^o28] collaborating frequently with [Li Ding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Ding), [Elaine Mardis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaine_Mardis), and [Richard K. Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_K._Wilson).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Wendl's heritage is ethnic German, originating from the [Banat region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat_Swabians) of the old [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) and is a historian of [Danube-Swabian folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_folk_music).[^o30] He is the son of the aerospace engineer [Michael J. Wendl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Wendl).[^o8] He is married to the former Pamela Bjerkness of [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago)[^o31]
