# Nagata's compactification theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nagata's compactification theorem"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **Nagata's compactification theorem**, introduced by (txt), implies that every [abstract variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_variety) can be embedded in a [complete variety](wikipedia://en/1047627), and more generally shows that a [separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#separated) and [finite type morphism](wikipedia://en/2139357#Morphisms_of_finite_type) to a [Noetherian scheme](wikipedia://en/2783891) *S* can be factored into an [open immersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#immersion) followed by a [proper morphism](wikipedia://en/603782).

:::

Nagata's original proof used the older terminology of [Zariski–Riemann spaces](wikipedia://en/27902093) and [valuation theory](wikipedia://en/347049), which sometimes made it hard to follow. [Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) showed, in unpublished notes expounded by [Conrad](wikipedia://en/4099038), that Nagata's proof can be translated into scheme theory and that the condition that *S* is Noetherian can be replaced by the much weaker condition that *S* is [quasi-compact](wikipedia://en/6042) and quasi-separated. (Lütkebohmert 1993) gave another scheme-theoretic proof of Nagata's theorem.

An important application of Nagata's theorem is in defining the analogue in algebraic geometry of [cohomology with compact support](wikipedia://en/20626603), or more generally higher [direct image functors with proper support](wikipedia://en/13910217). The idea is that given a compactifiable morphism $f: X \to S,$ one defines $R f_!$ by choosing a factorization $f = p \circ j$ by an open immersion *j* and proper morphism *p*, and then setting

$$Rf_! = Rp_* \circ j_{\sharp},$$

where $j_{\sharp}$ is the extension by zero functor. One then shows the independence of the definition from the choice of compactification.

In the context of [étale sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_sheaf), this idea was carried out by Deligne in [SGA 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SGA_4), Exposé XVII. In the context of [coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_sheaves), the statements are more delicate since for an open immersion *j*, the [inverse image](wikipedia://en/7086661) functor $j^*$ does not usually admit a left adjoint. Nonetheless, $j_{\sharp}$ exists as a [pro-left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nlab:pro-left+adjoint), and Deligne was able to define the functor $R f_!$ as valued in the pro-derived category of coherent sheaves.[^o4]
