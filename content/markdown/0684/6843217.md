# Formation matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Formation matrix"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), the **expected formation matrix** and the **observed formation matrix** are concepts used to quantify the uncertainty associated with parameter estimates derived from a [likelihood function](wikipedia://en/44968) $L(\theta)$. They are the matrix inverses of the [Fisher information matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_information_matrix) and the [observed information matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observed_information_matrix), respectively.[^o2]

:::

Because Fisher information measures the amount of information that an observable [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) carries about an unknown [parameter](wikipedia://en/25065) $\theta$, its inverse represents a measure of the dispersion or variance for an [estimator](wikipedia://en/10043) of $\theta$. The formation matrix is therefore related to the [covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/191752) of an estimator and is central to the [Cramér–Rao bound](wikipedia://en/581124), which establishes a lower bound on the variance of unbiased estimators. These matrices appear naturally in the [asymptotic expansion](wikipedia://en/642090) of the distribution of many statistics related to the [likelihood ratio](wikipedia://en/45035).

Currently, no single notation for formation matrices is universally used. In works by [Ole E. Barndorff-Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_E._Barndorff-Nielsen) and [Peter McCullagh](wikipedia://en/11740023), the symbol $j^{ij}$ denotes the element in the i-th row and j-th column of the observed formation matrix. An alternative notation, $g^{ij}$, arises from the [geometric interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Geometry) of the Fisher information matrix as a [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795), denoted $g_{ij}$. Following [Einstein notation](wikipedia://en/195407), these are related by $g_{ik}g^{kj} = \delta_i^j$.
