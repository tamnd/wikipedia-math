# Metric derivative

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Metric derivative"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **metric derivative** is a notion of [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) appropriate to [parametrized](wikipedia://en/576108) [paths](wikipedia://en/1189553) in [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018). It generalizes the notion of "speed" or "absolute velocity" to spaces which have a notion of distance (i.e. metric spaces) but not direction (such as [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370)).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $(M, d)$ be a metric space. Let $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ have a [limit point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_point) at $t \in \mathbb{R}$. Let $\gamma : E \to M$ be a path. Then the **metric derivative** of $\gamma$ at $t$, denoted $| \gamma' | (t)$, is defined by

$$| \gamma' | (t) := \lim_{s \to 0} \frac{d (\gamma(t + s), \gamma (t))}{| s |},$$

if this [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602) exists.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Recall that [AC<sup>*p*</sup>(*I*; *X*)](wikipedia://en/252670) is the space of curves *γ* : *I* → *X* such that

$$d \left( \gamma(s), \gamma(t) \right) \leq \int_{s}^{t} m(\tau) \, \mathrm{d} \tau \mbox{ for all } [s, t] \subseteq I$$

for some *m* in the [*L*<sup>*p*</sup> space](wikipedia://en/45194) *L*<sup>*p*</sup>(*I*; **R**). For *γ* ∈ AC<sup>*p*</sup>(*I*; *X*), the metric derivative of *γ* exists for [Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/18198)-[almost all](wikipedia://en/1309) times in *I*, and the metric derivative is the smallest *m* ∈ *L*<sup>*p*</sup>(*I*; **R**) such that the above inequality holds.

If [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ is equipped with its usual Euclidean norm $\| - \|$, and $\dot{\gamma} : E \to V^{*}$ is the usual [Fréchet derivative](wikipedia://en/2735727) with respect to time, then

$$| \gamma' | (t) = \| \dot{\gamma} (t) \|,$$

where $d(x, y) := \| x - y \|$ is the Euclidean metric.

:::
