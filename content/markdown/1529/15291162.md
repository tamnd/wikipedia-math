# Judith Roitman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Judith Roitman"}

**Judith A. "Judy" Roitman** (born November 12, 1945) is a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), a retired professor at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas). She specializes in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959), and [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471).[^o20]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Roitman was born in 1945 in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City). She attended [Oberlin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberlin_College), followed by [Sarah Lawrence College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Lawrence_College), graduating in 1966 with a degree in [English literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_literature). Next, she became interested in [mathematical linguistics](wikipedia://en/78756037). As she had little formal mathematical education, Roitman started taking [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) classes at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and [San Francisco State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_State_University). She had enjoyed mathematics as a [high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school) student and found her interest renewed. In 1969 she started graduate studies in mathematics at Berkeley. During graduate school, she spent some time teaching mathematics in [elementary schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_school) as a Community Teaching Fellow with [Project SEED](wikipedia://en/33639818). Roitman received her [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1974 from UC Berkeley with a thesis in topology; her thesis advisor was [Robert M. Solovay](wikipedia://en/3160110).[^o21] She taught at [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College) for three years, then spent a semester at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). She has been at the University of Kansas since then.

She has been involved in the field of [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471) for much of her career, running workshops for elementary school teachers and high school teachers and observing them in the classroom. She has encouraged individual mathematicians and the mathematical community at large to get involved and take mathematics education more seriously. She was in the [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1561691) writing group that produced *[Principles and Standards for School Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principles_and_Standards_for_School_Mathematics)*. Dismayed at the [politicization](wikipedia://en/7717612) of U.S. mathematics education, Roitman has insisted, "There is no math war."

Roitman has been active in the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) since its early years, and she served as president for the term 1979–1981.[^o22] She has been a [Zen Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen_Buddhist) since 1976, and is currently the guiding [teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen_teacher) of the Kansas Zen Center, of which she and her husband [Stanley Lombardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Lombardo) were founding members.[^o23]

Roitman is also a [poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet). Her poetry has appeared in a number of magazines, seven [chapbooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapbook), and two books.[^o24]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1996, she received the [Louise Hay Award](wikipedia://en/51270001) as recognition for her role as a math educator.[^o26] In 2012 she became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o27] In 2017, she was selected as a fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in the inaugural class.[^o25]
