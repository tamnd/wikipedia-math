# List of forcing notions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of forcing notions"}

In mathematics, [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) is a method of constructing new [models](wikipedia://en/19858#Set_theory) *M*\[*G*\] of [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) by adding a [generic](wikipedia://en/2213247) subset *G* of a [poset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poset) *P* to a model *M*. The poset *P* used will determine what statements hold in the new universe (the 'extension'); to force a statement of interest thus requires construction of a suitable *P*. This article lists some of the posets *P* that have been used in this construction.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

- *P* is a poset with order \<

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="universe"}

- *V* is the [universe](wikipedia://en/296838) of all sets

:::

- *M* is a countable transitive model of set theory
- *G* is a generic subset of *P* over *M*.

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="Countable chain condition"}

- *P* satisfies the **[countable chain condition](wikipedia://en/1550771)** if every antichain in *P* is at most countable. This implies that *V* and *V*\[*G*\] have the same cardinals (and the same cofinalities).

:::

- A subset *D* of *P* is called **dense** if for every *p* ∈ *P* there is some *q* ∈ *D* with *q* ≤ *p*.

::: {#o8 .entry cue=list-entry name="filter"}

- A **[filter](wikipedia://en/19719)** on *P* is a nonempty subset *F* of *P* such that if *p* \< *q* and *p* ∈ *F* then *q* ∈ *F*, and if *p* ∈ *F* and *q* ∈ *F* then there is some *r* ∈ *F* with *r* ≤ *p* and *r* ≤ *q*.

:::

- A subset *G* of *P* is called **generic** over *M* if it is a filter that meets every dense subset of *P* in *M*.

:::

## Amoeba forcing {#Amoeba_forcing}

Amoeba forcing is forcing with the [amoeba order](wikipedia://en/43515862), and adds a measure 1 set of random reals.

## Cohen forcing {#Cohen_forcing}

In Cohen forcing (named after [Paul Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Cohen_%28mathematician%29)) *P* is the set of functions from a finite subset of ω<sub>2</sub> × ω to {0,1} and *p* \< *q* if *p* ⊇ *q*.

This poset satisfies the countable chain condition. Forcing with this poset adds ω<sub>2</sub> distinct reals to the model; this was the poset used by Cohen in his original proof of the independence of the continuum hypothesis.

More generally, one can replace ω<sub>2</sub> by any cardinal κ so construct a model where the continuum has size at least κ. Here, there is no restriction. If κ has cofinality ω, the cardinality of the reals ends up bigger than κ.

::: {#o12 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Grigorieff forcing"}

## Grigorieff forcing {#Grigorieff_forcing}

Grigorieff forcing (after Serge Grigorieff) destroys a free [ultrafilter](wikipedia://en/31911) on ω.

:::

::: {#o14 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Hechler forcing"}

## Hechler forcing {#Hechler_forcing}

Hechler forcing (after Stephen Herman Hechler) is used to show that [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783) implies that every family of less than *c* functions from ω to ω is eventually dominated by some such function.

*P* is the set of pairs (*s*, *E*) where *s* is a finite sequence of natural numbers (considered as functions from a finite ordinal to ω) and *E* is a finite subset of some fixed set *G* of functions from ω to ω. The element (*s*, *E*) is stronger than (*t*, *F*) if *t* is contained in *s*, *F* is contained in *E*, and if *k* is in the domain of *s* but not of *t* then *s*(*k*) > *h*(*k*) for all *h* in *F*.

:::

## Iterated forcing {#Iterated_forcing}

Iterated forcing with finite supports was introduced by [Solovay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Solovay) and [Tennenbaum](wikipedia://en/33807699) to show the consistency of [Suslin's hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin's_hypothesis). [Easton](wikipedia://en/48754551) introduced another type of iterated forcing to determine the [possible values of the continuum function](wikipedia://en/4691250) at regular cardinals. Iterated forcing with countable support was investigated by [Laver](wikipedia://en/22280145) in his proof of the consistency of Borel's conjecture, [Baumgartner](wikipedia://en/5825279), who introduced Axiom A forcing, and [Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211), who introduced proper forcing. Revised countable support iteration was introduced by [Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) to handle semi-proper forcings, such as Prikry forcing, and generalizations, notably including Namba forcing.

## Jockusch–Soare forcing {#Jockusch–Soare_forcing}

Forcing with $\Pi^0_1$ classes was invented by [Robert Soare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Soare) and [Carl Jockusch](wikipedia://en/9859617) to prove, among other results, the [low basis theorem](wikipedia://en/9767227). Here *P* is the set of nonempty $\Pi^0_1$ subsets of [$2^{\omega}$](wikipedia://en/352376) (meaning the sets of paths through infinite, [computable](wikipedia://en/1139338) [subtrees](wikipedia://en/2190732) of $2^{<\omega}$), ordered by inclusion. While most forcing notions on this page relate to set theory, this one relates to recursion theory.

::: {#o18 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Laver forcing"}

## Laver forcing {#Laver_forcing}

Laver forcing was used by [Laver](wikipedia://en/22280145) to show that Borel's conjecture, which says that all [strong measure zero sets](wikipedia://en/45200516) are countable, is consistent with ZFC. (Borel's conjecture is not consistent with the continuum hypothesis.)

- *P* is the set of Laver trees, ordered by inclusion.

A **Laver tree** *p* is a subset of the finite sequences of natural numbers such that

- *p* is a tree: *p* contains any initial sequence of any element of *p*, equivalently stated as *p* is closed under initial segments
- *p* has a stem: a maximal node *s*(*p*) = *s* ∈ *p* such that *s* ≤ *t* or *t* ≤ *s* for all *t* in *p*,
- If *t* ∈ *p* and *s* ≤ *t* then *t* has an infinite number of immediate successors *tn* in *p* for *n* ∈ ω.

If *G* is generic for (*P*, ≤), then the real {*s*(*p*) : p ∈ *G*}, called a *Laver-real*, uniquely determines *G*.

Laver forcing satisfies the [Laver property](wikipedia://en/45136432).

:::

## Laver preparation {#Laver_preparation}

The Laver preparation was introduced by [Laver](wikipedia://en/22280145)[^o20] in the context of forcing while preserving [large cardinal](wikipedia://en/383644) axioms. Specifically, if κ is a [supercompact cardinal](wikipedia://en/248116), then after forcing with the Laver preparation at κ, κ remains a supercompact cardinal, and moreover will still remain so after any further κ-directed-closed forcing.

The Laver preparation *P<sub>κ</sub>* on κ is an Easton support [iteration](wikipedia://en/15550368#Iterated_forcing) of length κ, guided by a *Laver function* *f*. This is a function such that for any *x* in *V*, there is a λ-supercompactness embedding *j* with critical point κ (for suitable λ) such that *j(f)(κ)=x*; Laver shows that such a function exists for every supercompact cardinal κ. The iteration *P<sub>κ</sub>* only has non-trivial forcing at certain stages α for which *f(α)* is a suitable name for an α-directed-closed forcing, in which case *f(α)* is used as the stage α forcing.

::: {#o22 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Levy collapsing"}

## Levy collapsing {#Levy_collapsing}

These posets will collapse various cardinals, in other words force them to be equal in size to smaller cardinals.

- **Collapsing a cardinal to ω:** *P* is the set of all finite sequences of ordinals less than a given cardinal λ. If λ is uncountable then forcing with this poset collapses λ to ω.
- **Collapsing a cardinal to another:** *P* is the set of all functions from a subset of κ of cardinality less than κ to λ (for fixed cardinals κ and λ). Forcing with this poset collapses λ down to κ.
- **Levy collapsing:** If κ is regular and λ is inaccessible, then *P* is the set of functions *p* on subsets of λ × κ with domain of size less than κ and *p*(α, ξ) \< α for every (α, ξ) in the domain of *p*. This poset collapses all cardinals less than λ onto κ, but keeps λ as the successor to κ.

Levy collapsing is named for [Azriel Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azriel_Levy).

:::

::: {#o24 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Magidor forcing"}

## Magidor forcing {#Magidor_forcing}

Amongst many forcing notions developed by [Magidor](wikipedia://en/17115328), one of the best known is a generalization of Prikry forcing used to change the cofinality of a cardinal to a given smaller [regular cardinal](wikipedia://en/374128).

:::

## Mathias forcing {#Mathias_forcing}

::: {#o26 .entry cue=list-entry name="Finite set"}

- An element of *P* is a pair consisting of a [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742) *s* of natural numbers and an [infinite set](wikipedia://en/142679) *A* of natural numbers such that every element of *s* is less than every element of *A*. The order is defined by

:::

(*t*, *B*) is stronger than (*s*, *A*) ((*t*, *B*) \< (*s*, *A*)) if *s* is an initial segment of *t*, *B* is a subset of *A*, and *t* is contained in *s* ∪ *A*.

Mathias forcing is named for [Adrian Mathias](wikipedia://en/63066808).

::: {#o28 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Namba forcing"}

## Namba forcing {#Namba_forcing}

Namba forcing (after Kanji Namba) is used to change the cofinality of ω<sub>2</sub> to ω without collapsing ω<sub>1</sub>.

- *P* is the set of all trees $T \subseteq \omega_2^{<\omega}$ (nonempty downward closed subsets of the set of finite sequences of ordinals less than ω<sub>2</sub>) which have the property that any *s* in *T* has an extension in *T* which has $\aleph_2$ immediate successors. *P* is ordered by inclusion (i.e., subtrees are stronger conditions). The intersection of all trees in the generic filter defines a countable sequence which is cofinal in ω<sub>2</sub>.

Namba′ forcing is the subset of *P* such that there is a node below which the ordering is linear and above which each node has $\aleph_2$ immediate successors.

[Magidor](wikipedia://en/17115328) and [Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) proved that if CH holds then a generic object of Namba forcing does not exist in the generic extension by Namba′, and vice versa.[^o29][^o30]

:::

::: {#o32 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Prikry forcing"}

## Prikry forcing {#Prikry_forcing}

In Prikry forcing (after Karel Prikrý) *P* is the set of pairs (*s*, *A*) where *s* is a finite subset of a fixed measurable cardinal κ, and *A* is an element of a fixed normal measure *D* on κ. A condition (*s*, *A*) is stronger than (*t*, *B*) if *t* is an initial segment of *s*, *A* is contained in *B*, and *s* is contained in *t* ∪ *B*. This forcing notion can be used to change the cofinality of κ while preserving all cardinals.

:::

::: {#o34 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Product forcing"}

## Product forcing {#Product_forcing}

Taking a product of forcing conditions is a way of simultaneously forcing all the conditions.

- **Finite products**: If *P* and *Q* are posets, the product poset *P* × *Q* has the partial order defined by (*p*<sub>1</sub>, *q*<sub>1</sub>) ≤ (*p*<sub>2</sub>, *q*<sub>2</sub>) if *p*<sub>1</sub> ≤ *p*<sub>2</sub> and *q*<sub>1</sub> ≤ *q*<sub>2</sub>.
- **Infinite products**: The product of a set of posets *P*<sub>*i*</sub>, *i* ∈ *I*, each with a largest element 1 is the set of functions *p* on *I* with *p*(*i*) ∈ *P*(*i*) and such that *p*(*i*) = 1 for all but a finite number of *i*. The order is given by *p* ≤ *q* if *p*(*i*) ≤ *q*(*i*) for all *i*.
- The **Easton product** (after William Bigelow Easton) of a set of posets *P*<sub>*i*</sub>, *i* ∈ *I*, where *I* is a set of cardinals is the set of functions *p* on *I* with *p*(*i*) ∈ *P*(*i*) and such that for every regular cardinal γ the number of elements α of γ with *p*(α) ≠ 1 is less than γ.

:::

::: {#o36 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Radin forcing"}

## Radin forcing {#Radin_forcing}

Radin forcing (after Lon Berk Radin), a technically involved generalization of Magidor forcing, adds a closed, unbounded subset to some regular cardinal λ.

If λ is a sufficiently large cardinal, then the forcing keeps λ regular, [measurable](wikipedia://en/248102), [supercompact](wikipedia://en/248116), etc.

:::

## Random forcing {#Random_forcing}

- *P* is the set of Borel subsets of \[0,1\] of positive measure, where *p* is called stronger than *q* if it is contained in *q*. The generic set *G* then encodes a "random real": the unique real *x*<sub>*G*</sub> in all rational intervals \[*r*, *s*\]<sup>*V*\[*G*\]</sup> such that \[*r*, *s*\]<sup>*V*</sup> is in *G*. This real is "random" in the sense that if *X* is any subset of \[0, 1\]<sup>*V*</sup> of measure 1, lying in *V*, then *x*<sub>*G*</sub> ∈ *X*.

## Sacks forcing {#Sacks_forcing}

::: {#o39 .entry cue=list-entry name="Gerald Enoch Sacks"}

- *P* is the set of all perfect trees contained in the set of finite {0, 1} sequences. (A tree *T* is a set of finite sequences containing all initial segments of its members, and is called perfect if for any element *t* of *T* there is a segment *s* extending *t* so that both *s*0 and *s*1 are in *T*.) A tree *p* is stronger than *q* if *p* is contained in *q*. Forcing with perfect trees was used by [Gerald Enoch Sacks](wikipedia://en/3050504) to produce a real *a* with minimal degree of constructibility.

:::

Sacks forcing has the [Sacks property](wikipedia://en/44516416).

## Shooting a fast club {#Shooting_a_fast_club}

For *S* a stationary subset of $\omega_1$ we set $P=\{\langle \sigma, C\rangle\,\colon\sigma$ is a closed sequence from *S* and *C* is a closed unbounded subset of $\omega_1\}$, ordered by $\langle \sigma',C'\rangle\leq\langle\sigma, C\rangle$ iff $\sigma'$ end-extends $\sigma$ and $C'\subseteq C$ and $\sigma'\subseteq\sigma\cup C$. In $V[G]$, we have that $\bigcup\{\sigma\,\colon(\exists C)(\langle\sigma,C\rangle\in G)\}$ is a closed unbounded subset of *S* almost contained in each club set in *V*. $\aleph_1$ is preserved. This method was introduced by [Ronald Jensen](wikipedia://en/3785996) in order to show the consistency of the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705) and the [Suslin hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_hypothesis).

## Shooting a club with countable conditions {#Shooting_a_club_with_countable_conditions}

For *S* a stationary subset of $\omega_1$ we set *P* equal to the set of closed countable sequences from *S*. In $V[G]$, we have that $\bigcup G$ is a closed unbounded subset of *S* and $\aleph_1$ is preserved, and if CH holds then all cardinals are preserved.

## Shooting a club with finite conditions {#Shooting_a_club_with_finite_conditions}

For *S* a stationary subset of $\omega_1$ we set *P* equal to the set of finite sets of pairs of countable ordinals, such that if $p\in P$ and $\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\in p$ then $\alpha\leq\beta$ and $\alpha\in S$, and whenever $\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle$ and $\langle\gamma,\delta\rangle$ are distinct elements of *p* then either $\beta<\gamma$ or $\delta<\alpha$. *P* is ordered by reverse inclusion. In $V[G]$, we have that $\{\alpha\,\colon(\exists\beta)(\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\in\bigcup G)\}$ is a closed unbounded subset of *S* and all cardinals are preserved.

## Silver forcing {#Silver_forcing}

Silver forcing (after [Jack Howard Silver](wikipedia://en/21626512)) is the set of all those partial functions from the natural numbers into {0, 1} whose domain is coinfinite; or equivalently the set of all pairs (*A*, *p*), where *A* is a subset of the natural numbers with infinite complement, and *p* is a function from *A* into a fixed 2-element set. A condition *q* is stronger than a condition *p* if *q* extends *p*.

Silver forcing satisfies Fusion, the [Sacks property](wikipedia://en/44516416), and is minimal with respect to reals (but not minimal).

## Vopěnka forcing {#Vopěnka_forcing}

Vopěnka forcing (after [Petr Vopěnka](wikipedia://en/3105466)) is used to generically add a set $A$ of ordinals to [${\color{blue}\text{HOD}}$](wikipedia://en/14566906). Define first $P'$ as the set of all non-empty $\text{OD}$ subsets of the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) $\mathcal{P}(\alpha)$ of $\alpha$, where $A\subseteq\alpha$, ordered by inclusion: $p\leq q$ iff $p\subseteq q$. Each condition $p\in P'$ can be represented by a [tuple](wikipedia://en/132729) $(\beta,\gamma,\varphi)$ where $x\in p\Leftrightarrow V_\beta\models\varphi(\gamma,x)$, for all $x\subseteq\alpha$. The translation between $p$ and its least representation is $\text{OD}$, and hence $P'$ is isomorphic to a poset $P\in\text{HOD}$ (the conditions being the minimal representations of elements of $P'$). This poset is the Vopenka forcing for subsets of $\alpha$. Defining $G_A$ as the set of all representations for elements $p\in P'$ such that $A\in p$, then $G_A$ is $\text{HOD}$-generic and $A\in\text{HOD}[G_A]$.
