# ÉLECTRE

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="ÉLECTRE"}

**ÉLECTRE** is a family of [multi-criteria decision analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-criteria_decision_analysis) (MCDA) methods that originated in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) in the mid-1960s. The [acronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acronym) ÉLECTRE stands for: ÉLimination Et Choix Traduisant la REalité ("Elimination and Choice Translating Reality").

:::

The method was first proposed by [Bernard Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Roy) and his colleagues at [SEMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sema_Group) consultancy company. A team at SEMA was working on the concrete, multiple criteria, real-world problem of how firms could decide on new activities and had encountered problems using a [weighted sum technique](wikipedia://en/27914444). Roy was called in as a consultant and the group devised the ELECTRE method. As it was first applied in 1965, the ELECTRE method was to choose the best action(s) from a given set of actions, but it was soon applied to three main problems: choosing, ranking and sorting. The method became more widely known when a paper by B. Roy appeared in a French [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476) journal.[^o2] It evolved into ELECTRE I (*electre one*) and the evolutions have continued with ELECTRE II, ELECTRE III, ELECTRE IV, ELECTRE IS and ELECTRE TRI (*electre tree*), to mention a few.[^o3] They are used in the fields of business, development,[^o4] design,[^o5] and small hydropower.[^o6]

Roy is widely recognized as the father of the ELECTRE method, which was one of the earliest approaches in what is sometimes known as the French school of [decision making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_making). It is usually classified as an "outranking method" of decision making.

There are two main parts to an ELECTRE application: first, the construction of one or several outranking relations, which aims at comparing in a comprehensive way each pair of actions; second, an exploitation procedure that elaborates on the recommendations obtained in the first phase. The nature of the recommendation depends on the problem being addressed: choosing, ranking or sorting.

Criteria in ELECTRE methods have two distinct sets of parameters: the importance [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) and the veto thresholds. ELECTRE method cannot determine the weights of the criteria. In this regard, it can be combined with other approaches such as [Ordinal Priority Approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_Priority_Approach), [Analytic Hierarchy Process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_hierarchy_process), etc.
