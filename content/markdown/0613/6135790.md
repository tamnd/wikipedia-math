# Cevian

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cevian"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **cevian** is a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) which joins a [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) to a point on the opposite side of the triangle.[^o2][^o3] [Medians](wikipedia://en/1072006), [symmedians](wikipedia://en/320997), [angle bisectors](wikipedia://en/152547), [altitudes](wikipedia://en/161241) are all special cases of cevians. The name *cevian* comes from the Italian mathematician [Giovanni Ceva](wikipedia://en/152426), who proved a [theorem about cevians](wikipedia://en/152428) which also bears his name.[^o4]

:::

## Length {#Length}

*Image: stewarts theorem.svg.* A triangle with a cevian of length $d$

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Stewart's theorem"}

### Stewart's theorem {#Stewart's_theorem}

The length of a cevian can be determined by [Stewart's theorem](wikipedia://en/5732881): in the diagram, the cevian length $d$ is given by the formula

$$\,b^2m + c^2n = a(d^2 + mn).$$

Less commonly, this is also represented (with some rearrangement) by the following [mnemonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mnemonic):

$\underset{\text{A }man\text{ and his }dad}{man\ +\ dad} = \!\!\!\!\!\! \underset{\text{put a }bomb\text{ in the }sink.}{bmb\ +\ cnc}$[^o8]

:::

### Median {#Median}

If the cevian happens to be a [median](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_%28triangle%29) (thus [bisecting a side](wikipedia://en/152547#Bisectors_of_the_sides_of_a_polygon)), its length can be determined from the formula

$$\,m(b^2 + c^2) = a(d^2 + m^2)$$

or

$$\,2(b^2 + c^2) = 4d^2 + a^2$$

since

$$\,a = 2m.$$

Hence in this case

$$d= \frac\sqrt{2 b^2 + 2 c^2 - a^2}2 .$$

### Angle bisector {#Angle_bisector}

If the cevian happens to be an [angle bisector](wikipedia://en/152547#Angle_bisector), its length obeys the formulas

$$\,(b + c)^2 = a^2 \left( \frac{d^2}{mn} + 1 \right),$$

and[^o11]

$$d^2+mn = bc$$

and

$$d= \frac{2 \sqrt{bcs(s-a)}}{b+c}$$

where the [semiperimeter](wikipedia://en/886617) $s = \tfrac{a+b+c}{2}.$

The side of length $a$ is divided in the proportion $b : c$.

### Altitude {#Altitude}

If the cevian happens to be an [altitude](wikipedia://en/161241) and thus [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to a side, its length obeys the formulas

$$\,d^2 = b^2 - n^2 = c^2 - m^2$$

and

$$d=\frac{2\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}}{a},$$

where the semiperimeter $s = \tfrac{a+b+c}{2}.$

## Ratio properties {#Ratio_properties}

*Image: Ceva's theorem 1.svg.* Three cevians passing through a common point

There are various properties of the ratios of lengths formed by three cevians all passing through the same arbitrary interior point:[^o14] Referring to the diagram at right,

$$\begin{aligned} & \frac{\overline{AF}}{\overline{FB}} \cdot \frac{\overline{BD}}{\overline{DC}} \cdot \frac{\overline{CE}}{\overline{EA}} = 1 \\ & \\ & \frac{\overline{AO}}{\overline{OD}} = \frac{\overline{AE}}{\overline{EC}} + \frac{\overline{AF}}{\overline{FB}}; \\ & \\ & \frac{\overline{OD}}{\overline{AD}} + \frac{\overline{OE}}{\overline{BE}} + \frac{\overline{OF}}{\overline{CF}} = 1; \\ & \\ & \frac{\overline{AO}}{\overline{AD}} + \frac{\overline{BO}}{\overline{BE}} + \frac{\overline{CO}}{\overline{CF}} = 2. \end{aligned}$$

The first property is known as [Ceva's theorem](wikipedia://en/152428). The last two properties are equivalent because summing the two equations gives the [identity](wikipedia://en/298428) $1 + 1 + 1 = 3$.

## Splitter {#Splitter}

A [splitter](wikipedia://en/14961391) of a triangle is a cevian that [bisects](wikipedia://en/152547) the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636#Polygons). The three splitters [concur](wikipedia://en/2206157) at the [Nagel point](wikipedia://en/9489914) of the triangle.

## Area bisectors {#Area_bisectors}

Three of the [area bisectors](wikipedia://en/152547#Area_bisectors_and_perimeter_bisectors) of a triangle are its medians, which connect the vertices to the opposite side midpoints. The medians all contain the [centroid](wikipedia://en/1072006#Relation_to_center_of_mass), which happens to lie at their common point of intersection, so that a uniform-density triangle would in principle balance on a razor supporting any of the medians.

## Angle trisectors {#Angle_trisectors}

If from each vertex of a triangle two cevians are drawn so as to [trisect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trisection) the angle (divide it into three equal angles), then the six cevians intersect in pairs to form an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285), called the [Morley triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morley_triangle).

## Area of inner triangle formed by cevians {#Area_of_inner_triangle_formed_by_cevians}

[Routh's theorem](wikipedia://en/4639094) determines the ratio of the area of a given triangle to that of a triangle formed by the pairwise intersections of three cevians, one from each vertex.
