# Charles Newton Little

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Newton Little"}

**Charles Newton Little** (1858–1923) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [civil engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering). He was known for his expertise in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), including the construction of a [table of knots](wikipedia://en/17647177) with ten or fewer crossings.[^o2][^o3]

:::

Little's father was a missionary to [Madurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurai), in India, where Little was born in 1858;[^o4] his family returned with him to America in 1859.[^o2] He earned an A.B. from the [University of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska–Lincoln) in 1879, and continued at Nebraska's Institute of Mathematics and Civil Engineering, where he earned an M.A. in 1884.[^o2][^o3] After this, he entered graduate study at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), and completed his Ph.D. in 1885 under the supervision of [Hubert Anson Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Anson_Newton), with a dissertation concerning knot theory.[^o2][^o3]

He returned to the University of Nebraska as an associate professor of civil engineering, and was promoted to full professor in 1889. In 1893 he joined [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) as a professor of pure mathematics, after turning down a chair of mathematics at Nebraska.[^o2] In 1899–1900 he went on leave from Stanford, and traveled to Germany to study mathematics with [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) and [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302).[^o3][^o6] He moved again in 1901 to the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho), as a professor of civil engineering, and in 1911 was appointed as dean of engineering there.[^o3][^o6]

He died on September 7, 1923, of heart failure, in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California).[^o3]
