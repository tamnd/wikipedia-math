# Max Brückner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Max Brückner"}

*Image: Model of the final stellation of the icosahedron.JPG.* Brückner's photo of the [final stellation of the icosahedron](wikipedia://en/2968303), a stellated polyhedron first studied by Brückner

*Image: Brückner Vielflache Tafel 02.jpg.* Drawings in *Vielecke und Vielflache* from 1900, including a [stellation of the icosahedron](wikipedia://en/13181245) and [stereographic projections](wikipedia://en/143431) of the disdyakis [dodeca-](wikipedia://en/724752) and [triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/725111)

*Image: Brückner Achtzelle 2.jpg.* Drawings of [4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-polytopes) in an essay from 1909

**Johannes Max Brückner** (5 August 1860 – 1 November 1934) was a German [geometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometer), known for his collection of [polyhedral](wikipedia://en/23470) models.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Brückner was born in Hartau, in the [Kingdom of Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Saxony), a town that is now part of [Zittau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zittau), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany).[^o4] He completed a Ph.D. at [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University) in 1886, supervised by [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) and Wilhelm Scheibner, with a dissertation concerning [conformal maps](wikipedia://en/50627).[^o4] After teaching at a grammar school in [Zwickau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zwickau), he moved to the [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29) in [Bautzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bautzen).[^o4]

Brückner is known for making many geometric models, particularly of [stellated](wikipedia://en/180253) and [uniform polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polyhedra), which he documented in his book *Vielecke und Vielflache: Theorie und Geschichte* (*Polygons and polyhedra: Theory and History*, Leipzig: B. G. Teubner, 1900).[^o6][^o7] The shapes first studied in this book include the [final stellation of the icosahedron](wikipedia://en/2968303) and the [compound of three octahedra](wikipedia://en/33808818), made famous by [M. C. Escher](wikipedia://en/20127)'s print [*Stars*](wikipedia://en/1170784).[^o8] Joseph Malkevitch lists the publication of this book, which documented all that was known on polyhedra at the time, as one of 25 milestones in the history of polyhedra. Malkevitch writes that the book's "beautiful pictures of uniform polyhedra ... served as an inspiration to people later".[^o9]

Brückner died on November 1, 1934, in [Bautzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bautzen).[^o4]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Brückner was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 1904, 1908, 1912, and 1928.[^o11] In 1930–1931 he donated his model collection to [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University),[^o4] and the university in turn gave him an honorary doctorate in 1931.[^o4][^o5]
