# Niven's constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Niven's constant"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Niven's constant**, named after [Ivan Niven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Niven), is the largest exponent appearing in the [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorization) of a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) "on average". More precisely, if we set $H(1) = 1$ and define $H(n)$ to be the largest exponent appearing in the prime factorization of $n > 1$, then Niven's constant is given by

:::

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^n H(j) = 1+\sum_{k=2}^\infty \bigg(1-\frac{1}{\zeta(k)}\bigg) = 1.7052111401\dots$$

where $\zeta$ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).[^n1] Niven also proved that

$$\sum_{j=1}^n h(j) = n + c\sqrt{n} + o(\sqrt{n}),$$

where $h(1) = 1$, $h(n)$ is defined to be the smallest exponent appearing in the prime factorization of $n > 1$, $o$ is [little-o notation](wikipedia://en/44578#Little-o_notation), and $c = \zeta(\textstyle\frac{3}{2})/\zeta(3)$. Consequently,

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^n h(j) = 1.$$
