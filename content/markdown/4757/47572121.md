# Reinhold Hoppe

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reinhold Hoppe"}

**Ernst Reinhold Eduard Hoppe** (November 18, 1816 – May 7, 1900) was a German mathematician who worked as a professor at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin).[^o15][^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hoppe was a student of Johann August Grunert at the [University of Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greifswald),[^o4] graduating in 1842 and becoming an English and mathematics teacher. He completed his doctorate in 1850 in Halle and his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in mathematics in 1853 in Berlin under [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002). He also tried to obtain a habilitation in philosophy at the same time, but was denied until a later re-application in 1871. He worked at Berlin as a [privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent), and then after 1870 as a professor, but with few students and little remuneration.[^o2]

When Grunert died in 1872, Hoppe took over the editorship of the mathematical journal founded by Grunert, the *Archiv der Mathematik und Physik*. Hoppe in turn continued as editor until his own death, in 1900.[^o4] In 1890, Hoppe was one of the 31 founding members of the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973).[^o5]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Hoppe wrote over 250 scientific publications, including one of the first textbooks on [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).[^o2]

His accomplishments in geometry include rediscovering the higher-dimensional [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693) (previously discovered by [Ludwig Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408)),[^o7] and coining the term "polytope".[^o8] In 1880 he published a [closed-form expression](wikipedia://en/585143) for all triangles with consecutive integer sides and rational area, also known as [almost-equilateral Heronian triangles](wikipedia://en/731959#Almost-equilateral_Heronian_triangles).[^o9] He is sometimes credited with having proven [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627)'s [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) on the [kissing number problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kissing_number_problem), that at most twelve congruent balls can touch a central ball of the same radius, but his proof was incorrect, and a valid proof was not found until 1953.[^o10]

Hoppe published several works on a formula for the *m*-fold [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of a [composition of functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composition_of_functions). The formula, now known as "Hoppe's formula", is a variation of [Faà di Bruno's formula](wikipedia://en/425943). Hoppe's publication of his formula in 1845 predates Faà di Bruno's in 1852, but is later than some other independent discoveries of equivalent formulas.[^o11]

In his work on [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110), Hoppe belonged to the Königsberg school of thought, led by [Carl Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340).[^o12] He also published research in [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988).[^o13]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

He was elected to the [Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina) in 1890.[^o15]
