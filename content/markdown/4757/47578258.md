# Wakeby distribution

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wakeby distribution"}

The **Wakeby distribution**[^o3] is a five-parameter [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) defined by its quantile function,

:::

$$W(p) =\xi + \frac{\alpha}{\beta}(1 - (1-p)^{\beta}) - \frac{\gamma}{\delta}(1 - (1-p)^{-\delta}),$$

and by its quantile density function,

$$W'(p) = w(p) = \alpha (1-p)^{\beta - 1} + \gamma (1-p)^{-\delta - 1},$$

where $0 \le p \le 1$, ξ is a [location parameter](wikipedia://en/17516), α and γ are scale parameters and β and δ are [shape parameters](wikipedia://en/9210865).[^o3]

This distribution was first proposed by John C. Houghton while under the supervision of Harold A. Thomas Jr.,[^o4] who named it after [Wakeby Pond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakeby_Pond) in [Cape Cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod).[^o5][^o6]

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Wakeby distribution has been used for modeling distributions of

- flood flows,[^o9][^o10]
- citation counts,[^o11]
- extreme [rainfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainfall),[^o12][^o13]
- [tidal current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_current) speeds,[^o14]
- peak [flows of rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_flow),[^o15]
- and extreme wind speeds.[^o16]

:::

## Parameters and domain {#Parameters_and_domain}

The following restrictions apply to the parameters of this distribution:

- $$\beta + \delta \ge 0$$
- Either $\beta + \delta > 0$ or $\beta = \gamma = \delta = 0$
- If $\gamma > 0$, then $\delta > 0$
- $$\gamma \ge 0$$
- $$\alpha + \gamma \ge 0$$

The domain of the Wakeby distribution is

- $\xi$ to $\infty$, if $\delta \ge 0$ and $\gamma > 0$
- $\xi$ to $\xi + (\alpha/ \beta) - (\gamma/ \delta)$, if $\delta < 0$ or $\gamma = 0$

With two shape parameters, the Wakeby distribution can model a wide variety of shapes.[^o3]

## CDF and PDF {#CDF_and_PDF}

The [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) is computed by numerically inverting the quantile function given above. The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) is then found by using the following relation (given on page 46 of Johnson, Kotz, and Balakrishnan[^o19]):

$$f(x) = \frac{(1 - F(x))^{(\delta+1)}}{\alpha t + \gamma}$$

where F is the cumulative distribution function and

$$t = (1 - F(x))^{(\beta + \delta)}$$

An implementation that computes the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) of the Wakeby distribution is included in the [Dataplot](wikipedia://en/16025102) scientific computation library, as routine WAKPDF.[^o3]

An alternative to the above method is to define the PDF parametrically as $(W(p),1/w(p)), \ 0\le p \le 1$. This can be set up as a [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487), $f(x)$, by solving for the unique $p$ in the equation $W(p)=x$ and returning $1/w(p)$.
