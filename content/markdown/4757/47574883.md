# Olivier Pironneau

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Olivier Pironneau"}

**Olivier Pironneau** (born 1945) is a [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who is a professor at the [Université Pierre et Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Pierre_et_Marie_Curie) and member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o4]

:::

Pironneau is a worldwide recognized expert in [computational fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/305924), [scientific computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_computing), [computational engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_engineering), [optimal design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_design), [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). He is a highly cited author, having written 8 books and 693 papers. He is a member of French Academy of Sciences since 2002.[^o5] He was awarded the Marcel Dassault Prize by the French Academy of Sciences in 2000. He is also the recipient of the [Blaise Pascal Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Pascal_Prize) of the French Academy of Sciences in 1983, the Ordre National du Mérite (1989) and is an Associate member of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) since 2004. His group has developed the software [FreeFem++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FreeFem++) which is used by researchers worldwide for [finite element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_element) computations.
