# Complex lamellar vector field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complex lamellar vector field"}

In [vector calculus](wikipedia://en/32640), a **complex lamellar vector field** is a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) which is orthogonal to a family of surfaces. In the broader context of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), complex lamellar vector fields are more often called **hypersurface-orthogonal vector fields.** They can be characterized in a number of different ways, many of which involve the [curl](wikipedia://en/6123). A **lamellar vector field** is a special case given by vector fields with zero curl.

:::

The adjective "lamellar" derives from the noun "lamella", which means a thin layer. The *lamellae* to which "lamellar vector field" refers are the surfaces of constant potential, or in the complex case, the surfaces orthogonal to the vector field.

## Complex lamellar vector fields {#Complex_lamellar_vector_fields}

In [vector calculus](wikipedia://en/32640), a **complex lamellar vector field** is a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) in three dimensions which is [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/102221) to its own [curl](wikipedia://en/6123). That is,

$$\mathbf{F}\cdot (\nabla\times \mathbf{F}) = 0.$$

The term **lamellar vector field** is sometimes used as a synonym for the special case of an [irrotational vector field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrotational_vector_field), meaning that

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{0}.$$

Complex lamellar vector fields are precisely those that are normal to a family of surfaces. An irrotational vector field is locally the [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461) of a function, and is therefore orthogonal to the family of [level surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_surface) (the [equipotential surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equipotential_surface)). Any vector field can be decomposed as the sum of an irrotational vector field and a complex lamellar field.

## Hypersurface-orthogonal vector fields {#Hypersurface-orthogonal_vector_fields}

In greater generality, a vector field $\mathbf{F}$ on a [pseudo-Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/287555) is said to be **hypersurface-orthogonal** if through an arbitrary point there is a [smoothly embedded hypersurface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_submanifold) which, at all of its points, is orthogonal to the vector field. By the [Frobenius theorem](wikipedia://en/602480) this is equivalent to requiring that the [Lie bracket](wikipedia://en/10282799) of any smooth vector fields orthogonal to $\mathbf{F}$ is still orthogonal to $\mathbf{F}$.

The condition of hypersurface-orthogonality can be rephrased in terms of the [differential 1-form](wikipedia://en/221519) $\omega$ which is dual to $\mathbf{F}$. The previously given Lie bracket condition can be reworked to require that the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) $d\omega$, when evaluated on any two tangent vectors which are orthogonal to $\mathbf{F}$, is zero. This may also be phrased as the requirement that there is a smooth 1-form whose [wedge product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedge_product) with $\omega$ equals $d\omega$.

Alternatively, this may be written as the condition that the [differential 3-form](wikipedia://en/221519) $\omega \land d\omega$ is zero. This can also be phrased, in terms of the [Levi-Civita connection](wikipedia://en/249176) defined by the metric, as requiring that the totally anti-symmetric part of the 3-tensor field $\omega_{i}\nabla_{j}\omega_{k}$ is zero. Using a different formulation of the Frobenius theorem, it is also equivalent to require that $\omega$ is locally expressible as $\lambda du$ for some functions $\lambda$ and $u$.

In the special case of vector fields on [three-dimensional Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), the hypersurface-orthogonal condition is equivalent to the complex lamellar condition, as seen by rewriting $\omega \land d\omega$ in terms of the [Hodge star operator](wikipedia://en/310914) as $\ast\langle\omega, \ast d\omega\rangle$, with $\ast d\omega$ being the 1-form dual to the curl vector field.

Hypersurface-orthogonal vector fields are particularly important in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), where (among other reasons) the existence of a [Killing vector field](wikipedia://en/1025455) which is hypersurface-orthogonal is one of the requirements of a [static spacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_spacetime). In this context, hypersurface-orthogonality is sometimes called *irrotationality*, although this is in conflict with the standard usage in three dimensions. Another name is **rotation-freeness**.

An even more general notion, in the language of [Pfaffian systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfaffian_system), is that of a [completely integrable 1-form](wikipedia://en/968734) $\omega$, which amounts to the condition $\omega \land d\omega = 0$ as given above. In this context, there is no metric and so there is no notion of "orthogonality".
