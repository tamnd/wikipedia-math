# Infra-exponential

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Infra-exponential"}

A growth rate is said to be **infra-exponential** or **subexponential** if it is dominated by all [exponential growth](wikipedia://en/191933) rates, however great the [doubling time](wikipedia://en/6713437). A [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) with infra-exponential growth rate will have a [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) that is a Fourier [hyperfunction](wikipedia://en/1678626).[^o2]

:::

Examples of subexponential growth rates arise in the [analysis of algorithms](wikipedia://en/2230), where they give rise to [sub-exponential time complexity](wikipedia://en/405944#Sub-exponential_time), and in the [growth rate of groups](wikipedia://en/498727), where a subexponential growth rate implies that a group is [amenable](wikipedia://en/657430).

A positive-valued, unbounded [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) $\cal D$ may be called **subexponential** if its tails are heavy enough so that

$$\lim_{x\to+\infty} \frac{{\mathbb P}(X_1+X_2>x)}{{\mathbb P}(X>x)}=2,\qquad X_1, X_2, X\sim {\cal D},\qquad X_1, X_2 \hbox{ independent.}$$

See Heavy-tailed distribution. Contrariwise, a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) may also be called **subexponential** if [its tails are sufficiently light to fall off at an exponential or faster rate](wikipedia://en/75586835).
