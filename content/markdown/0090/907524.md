# Von Neumann regular ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Von Neumann regular ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **von Neumann regular ring** is a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* (associative, with 1, not necessarily commutative) such that for every element *a* in *R* there exists an *x* in *R* with *a* = *axa*. One may think of *x* as a "weak inverse" of the element *a*; in general *x* is not uniquely determined by *a*. Von Neumann regular rings are also called **absolutely flat rings**, because these rings are characterized by the fact that every left [*R*-module](wikipedia://en/276410) is [flat](wikipedia://en/492616).

:::

Von Neumann regular rings were introduced by (txt) under the name of "regular rings", in the course of his study of [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185) and [continuous geometry](wikipedia://en/32590824). Von Neumann regular rings should not be confused with the unrelated [regular rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_ring) and [regular local rings](wikipedia://en/506959) of [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990).

An element *a* of a ring is called a **von Neumann regular element** if there exists an *x* such that *a* = *axa*.[^n1] An ideal $\mathfrak{i}$ is called a (von Neumann) [regular ideal](wikipedia://en/24159446) if for every element *a* in $\mathfrak{i}$ there exists an element *x* in $\mathfrak{i}$ such that *a* = *axa*.[^n2]

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Every [field](wikipedia://en/10603) (and every [skew field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skew_field)) is von Neumann regular: for *a* ≠ 0 we can take *x* = *a*<sup>−1</sup>.[^n1] An [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) is von Neumann regular if and only if it is a field. Every [direct product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_product_of_rings) of von Neumann regular rings is again von Neumann regular.

Another important class of examples of von Neumann regular rings are the rings M<sub>*n*</sub>(*K*) of *n*-by-*n* [square matrices](wikipedia://en/166022) with entries from some field *K*. If *r* is the [rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rank_of_a_matrix) of *A* ∈ M<sub>*n*</sub>(*K*), [Gaussian elimination](wikipedia://en/13035) gives [invertible matrices](wikipedia://en/217122) *U* and *V* such that[^o8]

$$A = U \begin{pmatrix}I_r &0\\ 0 &0\end{pmatrix} V$$

(where *I*<sub>*r*</sub> is the *r*-by-*r* [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718)). If we set *X* = *V*<sup>−1</sup>*U*<sup>−1</sup>, then

$$AXA= U \begin{pmatrix}I_r &0\\ 0 &0\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}I_r &0\\ 0 &0\end{pmatrix} V = U \begin{pmatrix}I_r &0\\ 0 &0\end{pmatrix} V = A.$$

More generally, the *n* × *n* matrix ring over any von Neumann regular ring is again von Neumann regular.[^n1]

If *V* is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over a field (or [skew field](wikipedia://en/9067)) *K*, then the [endomorphism ring](wikipedia://en/59623) End<sub>*K*</sub>(*V*) is von Neumann regular, even if *V* is not finite-dimensional.[^n3]

Generalizing the above examples, suppose *S* is some ring and *M* is an *S*-module such that every [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) of *M* is a direct summand of *M* (such modules *M* are called *[semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple)*). Then the [endomorphism ring](wikipedia://en/59623) End<sub>*S*</sub>(*M*) is von Neumann regular. In particular, every [semisimple ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring) is von Neumann regular. Indeed, the semisimple rings are precisely the [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) von Neumann regular rings.[^o11]

The ring of [affiliated operators](wikipedia://en/5123403) of a finite [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) is von Neumann regular.

A [Boolean ring](wikipedia://en/54356) is a ring in which every element satisfies *a*<sup>2</sup> = *a*. Every Boolean ring is von Neumann regular.

## Facts {#Facts}

The following statements are equivalent for the ring *R*:

- *R* is von Neumann regular
- every [principal](wikipedia://en/145126) [left ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_ideal) is generated by an [idempotent element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent_element_%28ring_theory%29)
- every [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) left ideal is generated by an idempotent
- every principal left ideal is a [direct summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_summand) of the left *R*-module *R*
- every finitely generated left ideal is a direct summand of the left *R*-module *R*
- every finitely generated [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) of a [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) left *R*-module *P* is a direct summand of *P*
- every left *R*-module is [flat](wikipedia://en/492616): this is also known as *R* being **absolutely flat**, or *R* having **[weak dimension](wikipedia://en/38263686)** 0
- every [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) of left *R*-modules is [pure exact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_exact).

The corresponding statements for right modules are also equivalent to *R* being von Neumann regular.

Every von Neumann regular ring has [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) {0} and is thus [semiprimitive](wikipedia://en/485855) (also called "Jacobson semi-simple").

In a commutative von Neumann regular ring, for each element *x* there is a unique element *y* such that *xyx* = *x* and *yxy* = *y*, so there is a canonical way to choose the "weak inverse" of *x*.

The following statements are equivalent for the commutative ring *R*:

- *R* is von Neumann regular.
- *R* has [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) 0 and is [reduced](wikipedia://en/539749).
- Every [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) of *R* at a [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) is a field.
- *R* is a subring of a product of fields closed under taking "weak inverses" of *x* ∈ *R* (the unique element *y* such that *xyx* = *x* and *yxy* = *y*).
- *R* is a [V-ring](wikipedia://en/48340978).[^n4]
- *R* has the [right-lifting property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-lifting-property) against the ring homomorphism **Z**\[*t*\] → **Z**\[*t*<sup>±</sup>\] × **Z** determined by *t* ↦ (*t*, 0), or said geometrically, every [regular function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_function) $\mathrm{Spec}(R) \to \mathbb{A}^1$ factors through the [morphism of schemes](wikipedia://en/7482797) $\{0\} \sqcup \mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{A}^1$.[^n5]

Also, the following are equivalent: for a commutative ring *A*

- *R* = *A* / nil(*A*) is von Neumann regular.
- The [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) of *A* is Hausdorff (in the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744)).
- The [constructible topology](wikipedia://en/30525205) and Zariski topology for [Spec(*A*)](wikipedia://en/37291) coincide.

## Generalizations and specializations {#Generalizations_and_specializations}

Special types of von Neumann regular rings include *unit regular rings* and *strongly von Neumann regular rings* and [rank rings](wikipedia://en/32593841).

A ring *R* is called **unit regular** if for every *a* in *R*, there is a unit *u* in *R* such that *a* = *aua*. Every [semisimple ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring) is unit regular, and unit regular rings are [directly finite rings](wikipedia://en/38244183). An ordinary von Neumann regular ring need not be directly finite.

A ring *R* is called **strongly von Neumann regular** if for every *a* in *R*, there is some *x* in *R* with *a* = *aax*. The condition is left-right symmetric. Strongly von Neumann regular rings are unit regular. Every strongly von Neumann regular ring is a [subdirect product](wikipedia://en/17395221) of [division rings](wikipedia://en/9067). In some sense, this more closely mimics the properties of commutative von Neumann regular rings, which are subdirect products of fields. For commutative rings, von Neumann regular and strongly von Neumann regular are equivalent. In general, the following are equivalent for a ring *R*:

- *R* is strongly von Neumann regular
- *R* is von Neumann regular and [reduced](wikipedia://en/539749)
- *R* is von Neumann regular and every idempotent in *R* is [central](wikipedia://en/361940#Types_of_ring_idempotents)
- Every principal left ideal of *R* is generated by a central idempotent

Generalizations of von Neumann regular rings include **π**-regular rings, left/right [semihereditary rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semihereditary_ring), left/right [nonsingular rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonsingular_ring) and [semiprimitive rings](wikipedia://en/485855).
