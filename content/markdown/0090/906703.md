# Clenshaw algorithm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clenshaw algorithm"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), the **Clenshaw algorithm**, also called **Clenshaw summation**, is a [recursive](wikipedia://en/25407) method to evaluate a [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of [Chebyshev polynomials](wikipedia://en/184539).[^o2][^o11] The method was published by [Charles William Clenshaw](wikipedia://en/68251012) in 1955. It is a generalization of [Horner's method](wikipedia://en/14263) for evaluating a linear combination of [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416).

:::

It generalizes to more than just Chebyshev polynomials; it applies to any class of functions that can be defined by a three-term [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806).[^o3]

## Clenshaw algorithm {#Clenshaw_algorithm}

In full generality, the Clenshaw algorithm computes the weighted sum of a finite series of functions $\phi_k(x)$:

$$S(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k \phi_k(x)$$

where $\phi_k,\; k=0, 1, \ldots$ is a sequence of functions that satisfy the linear recurrence relation

$$\phi_{k+1}(x) = \alpha_k(x)\,\phi_k(x) + \beta_k(x)\,\phi_{k-1}(x),$$

where the coefficients $\alpha_k(x)$ and $\beta_k(x)$ are known in advance.

The algorithm is most useful when $\phi_k(x)$ are functions that are complicated to compute directly, but $\alpha_k(x)$ and $\beta_k(x)$ are particularly simple. In the most common applications, $\alpha(x)$ does not depend on $k$, and $\beta$ is a constant that depends on neither $x$ nor $k$.

To perform the summation for given series of coefficients $a_0, \ldots, a_n$, compute the values $b_k(x)$ by the "reverse" recurrence formula:

$$\begin{aligned} b_{n+1}(x) &= b_{n+2}(x) = 0, \\ b_k(x) &= a_k + \alpha_k(x)\,b_{k+1}(x) + \beta_{k+1}(x)\,b_{k+2}(x). \end{aligned}$$

Note that this computation makes no direct reference to the functions $\phi_k(x)$. After computing $b_2(x)$ and $b_1(x)$, the desired sum can be expressed in terms of them and the simplest functions $\phi_0(x)$ and $\phi_1(x)$:

$$S(x) = \phi_0(x)\,a_0 + \phi_1(x)\,b_1(x) + \beta_1(x)\,\phi_0(x)\,b_2(x).$$

See Fox and Parker[^o5] for more information and stability analyses.

## Examples {#Examples}

### Horner as a special case of Clenshaw {#Horner_as_a_special_case_of_Clenshaw}

A particularly simple case occurs when evaluating a polynomial of the form

$$S(x) = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k x^k.$$

The functions are simply

$$\begin{aligned} \phi_0(x) &= 1, \\ \phi_k(x) &= x^k = x\phi_{k-1}(x) \end{aligned}$$

and are produced by the recurrence coefficients $\alpha(x) = x$ and $\beta = 0$.

In this case, the recurrence formula to compute the sum is

$$b_k(x) = a_k + x b_{k+1}(x)$$

and, in this case, the sum is simply

$$S(x) = a_0 + x b_1(x) = b_0(x),$$

which is exactly the usual [Horner's method](wikipedia://en/14263).

### Special case for Chebyshev series {#Special_case_for_Chebyshev_series}

Consider a truncated [Chebyshev series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chebyshev_series)

$$p_n(x) = a_0 + a_1 T_1(x) + a_2 T_2(x) + \cdots + a_n T_n(x).$$

The coefficients in the recursion relation for the [Chebyshev polynomials](wikipedia://en/184539) are

$$\alpha(x) = 2x, \quad \beta = -1,$$

with the initial conditions

$$T_0(x) = 1, \quad T_1(x) = x.$$

Thus, the recurrence is

$$b_k(x) = a_k + 2xb_{k+1}(x) - b_{k+2}(x)$$

and the final results are

$$b_0(x) = a_0 + 2xb_1(x) - b_2(x),$$

$$p_n(x) = \tfrac{1}{2} \left[a_0+b_0(x) - b_2(x)\right].$$

An equivalent expression for the sum is given by

$$p_n(x) = a_0 + xb_1(x) - b_2(x).$$

### Meridian arc length on the ellipsoid {#Meridian_arc_length_on_the_ellipsoid}

Clenshaw summation is extensively used in [geodetic](wikipedia://en/12608) applications.[^o11] A simple application is summing the [trigonometric series](wikipedia://en/4099125) to compute the [meridian arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_arc) distance on the surface of an ellipsoid. These have the form

$$m(\theta) = C_0\,\theta + C_1\sin \theta + C_2\sin 2\theta + \cdots + C_n\sin n\theta.$$

Leaving off the initial $C_0\,\theta$ term, the remainder is a summation of the appropriate form. There is no leading term because $\phi_0(\theta) = \sin 0\theta = \sin 0 = 0$.

The [recurrence relation for $\sin k\theta$](wikipedia://en/25804104#Chebyshev_method) is

$$\sin (k+1)\theta = 2 \cos\theta \sin k\theta - \sin (k-1)\theta,$$

making the coefficients in the recursion relation

$$\alpha_k(\theta) = 2\cos\theta, \quad \beta_k = -1.$$

and the evaluation of the series is given by

$$\begin{aligned} b_{n+1}(\theta) &= b_{n+2}(\theta) = 0, \\ b_k(\theta) &= C_k + 2\cos \theta \,b_{k+1}(\theta) - b_{k+2}(\theta),\quad\mathrm{for\ } n\ge k \ge 1. \end{aligned}$$

The final step is made particularly simple because $\phi_0(\theta) = \sin 0 = 0$, so the end of the recurrence is simply $b_1(\theta)\sin(\theta)$; the $C_0\,\theta$ term is added separately:

$$m(\theta) = C_0\,\theta + b_1(\theta)\sin \theta.$$

Note that the algorithm requires only the evaluation of two trigonometric quantities $\cos \theta$ and $\sin \theta$.

### Difference in meridian arc lengths {#Difference_in_meridian_arc_lengths}

Sometimes it necessary to compute the difference of two meridian arcs in a way that maintains high relative accuracy. This is accomplished by using trigonometric identities to write

$$m(\theta_1)-m(\theta_2) = C_0(\theta_1-\theta_2) + \sum_{k=1}^n 2 C_k \sin\bigl({\textstyle\frac12}k(\theta_1-\theta_2)\bigr) \cos\bigl({\textstyle\frac12}k(\theta_1+\theta_2)\bigr).$$

Clenshaw summation can be applied in this case[^o13] provided we simultaneously compute $m(\theta_1)+m(\theta_2)$ and perform a matrix summation,

$$\mathsf M(\theta_1,\theta_2) = \begin{bmatrix} (m(\theta_1) + m(\theta_2)) / 2\\ (m(\theta_1) - m(\theta_2)) / (\theta_1 - \theta_2) \end{bmatrix} = C_0 \begin{bmatrix} \mu \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + \sum_{k=1}^n C_k \mathsf F_k(\theta_1,\theta_2),$$

where

$$\begin{aligned} \delta &= \tfrac{1}{2}(\theta_1-\theta_2), \\[1ex] \mu &= \tfrac{1}{2}(\theta_1+\theta_2), \\[1ex] \mathsf F_k(\theta_1,\theta_2) &= \begin{bmatrix} \cos k \delta \sin k \mu \\ \dfrac{\sin k \delta}\delta \cos k \mu \end{bmatrix}. \end{aligned}$$

The first element of $\mathsf M(\theta_1,\theta_2)$ is the average value of $m$ and the second element is the average slope. $\mathsf F_k(\theta_1,\theta_2)$ satisfies the recurrence relation

$$\mathsf F_{k+1}(\theta_1,\theta_2) = \mathsf A(\theta_1,\theta_2) \mathsf F_k(\theta_1,\theta_2) - \mathsf F_{k-1}(\theta_1,\theta_2),$$

where

$$\mathsf A(\theta_1,\theta_2) = 2\begin{bmatrix} \cos \delta \cos \mu & -\delta\sin \delta \sin \mu \\ - \displaystyle\frac{\sin \delta}\delta \sin \mu & \cos \delta \cos \mu \end{bmatrix}$$

takes the place of $\alpha$ in the recurrence relation, and $\beta=-1$. The standard Clenshaw algorithm can now be applied to yield

$$\begin{aligned} \mathsf B_{n+1} &= \mathsf B_{n+2} = \mathsf 0, \\[1ex] \mathsf B_k &= C_k \mathsf I + \mathsf A \mathsf B_{k+1} - \mathsf B_{k+2}, \qquad\mathrm{for\ } n\ge k \ge 1, \\[1ex] \mathsf M(\theta_1,\theta_2) &= C_0 \begin{bmatrix}\mu\\1\end{bmatrix} + \mathsf B_1 \mathsf F_1(\theta_1,\theta_2), \end{aligned}$$

where $\mathsf B_k$ are 2×2 matrices. Finally we have

$$\frac{m(\theta_1) - m(\theta_2)}{\theta_1 - \theta_2} = \mathsf M_2(\theta_1, \theta_2).$$

This technique can be used in the [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602) $\theta_2 = \theta_1 = \mu$ and $\delta = 0$ to simultaneously compute $m(\mu)$ and the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) $dm(\mu)/d\mu$, provided that, in evaluating $\mathsf F_1$ and $\mathsf A$, we take $\lim_{\delta \to 0} (\sin k \delta)/\delta = k$.
