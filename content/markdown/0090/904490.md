# Pure submodule

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pure submodule"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the field of [module theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_theory), the concept of **pure submodule** provides a generalization of [direct summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_summand), a type of particularly well-behaved piece of a [module](wikipedia://en/276410). Pure modules are complementary to [flat modules](wikipedia://en/492616) and generalize Prüfer's notion of [pure subgroups](wikipedia://en/5739585). While flat modules are those modules which leave [short exact sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) exact after [tensoring](wikipedia://en/30844), a pure submodule defines a short exact sequence (known as a **pure exact sequence**) that remains exact after tensoring with any module. Similarly a flat module is a [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488), and a pure exact sequence is a direct limit of [split exact sequences](wikipedia://en/1531404).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $R$ be a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) (associative, with $1$), let $M$ be a (left) [module](wikipedia://en/276410) over $R$, and let $P$ be a [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) of $M$ with $\iota\colon P\hookrightarrow M$ be the natural [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) map. Then $P$ is a **pure submodule of $M$** if, for any (right) $R$-module $X$, the natural induced map $\mathrm{id}_X\otimes_R \iota\colon X\otimes_R P\to X\otimes_R M$ is injective.

Analogously, a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence)

$$0 \longrightarrow A\,\ \stackrel{f}{\longrightarrow}\ B\,\ \stackrel{g}{\longrightarrow}\ C \longrightarrow 0$$

of (left) $R$-modules is **pure exact** if the sequence stays exact when tensored with any (right) $R$-module $X$. This is equivalent to saying that $f(A)$ is a pure submodule of $B$.

:::

## Equivalent characterizations {#Equivalent_characterizations}

Purity of a submodule can also be expressed element-wise; it is really a statement about the solvability of certain systems of linear equations. Specifically, $P$ is pure in $M$ if and only if the following condition holds: for any $m$-by-$n$ [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) $(a_{ij})$ with entries in $R$, and any set $y_1,\cdots,y_m$ of elements of $P$, if there exist elements $x_1,\cdots,x_n$ **in $M$** such that

$$\sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}x_j = y_i \qquad\mbox{ for } i=1,\ldots,m$$

then there also exist elements $x'_1,\cdots,x'_n$ **in $P$** such that

$$\sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}x'_j = y_i \qquad\mbox{ for } i=1,\ldots,m$$

Another characterization is: a sequence is pure exact if and only if it is the [filtered colimit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_colimit) (also known as [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566)) of [split exact sequences](wikipedia://en/1531404)

$0 \longrightarrow A_i \longrightarrow B_i \longrightarrow C_i \longrightarrow 0.$[^o5]

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every [direct summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_summand) of *M* is pure in *M*. Consequently, every [subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) is pure.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Suppose $0 \longrightarrow A\,\ \stackrel{f}{\longrightarrow}\ B\,\ \stackrel{g}{\longrightarrow}\ C \longrightarrow 0$ is a short exact sequence of $R$-modules, then:

1. $C$ is a [flat module](wikipedia://en/492616) if and only if the exact sequence is pure exact for every $A$ and $B$. From this we can deduce that over a [von Neumann regular ring](wikipedia://en/907524), *every* submodule of *every* $R$-module is pure. This is because *every* module over a von Neumann regular ring is flat. The converse is also true.[^n1]
1. Suppose $B$ is flat. Then the sequence is pure exact if and only if $C$ is flat. From this one can deduce that pure submodules of flat modules are flat.
1. Suppose $C$ is flat. Then $B$ is flat if and only if $A$ is flat.

If $0 \longrightarrow A\,\ \stackrel{f}{\longrightarrow}\ B\,\ \stackrel{g}{\longrightarrow}\ C \longrightarrow 0$ is pure-exact, and $F$ is a [finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_module) $R$-module, then every homomorphism from $F$ to $C$ can be lifted to $B$, i.e. to every $u\colon F\to C$ there exists $v\colon F\to B$ such that $gv=u$.

:::
