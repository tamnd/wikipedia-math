# Proprism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Proprism"}

*Image: 3-generalized-2-cube\_skew.svg.* The [{3}×{3} duoprism](wikipedia://en/41745711) is a proprism as the product of two orthogonal triangles, having 9 squares between pairs of edges of 2 sets of 3 triangles, and 18 vertices, as seen in this skew orthogonal projection.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of 4 dimensions or higher, a **proprism** is a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) resulting from the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two or more polytopes, each of two dimensions or higher. The term was coined by [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) for *product prism*. The dimension of the space of a proprism equals the sum of the dimensions of all its product elements. Proprisms are often seen as *k*-face elements of [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The number of [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) in a proprism is equal to the product of the number of vertices in all the polytopes in the product.

The minimum [symmetry order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_order) of a proprism is the product of the symmetry orders of all the polytopes. A higher symmetry order is possible if polytopes in the product are identical.

A proprism is [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885) if all its product polytopes are convex.

:::

## f-vectors {#f-vectors}

An [f-vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-vector) is a number of *k*-face elements in a polytope from *k*=0 (points) to *k*=*n*−1 (facets). An extended f-vector can also include *k*=−1 (nullitope), or *k*=*n* (body). Prism products include the body element. (The dual to prism products includes the nullitope, while pyramid products include both.)

The f-vector of prism product, A×B, can be computed as (f<sub>A</sub>,**1**)\*(f<sub>B</sub>,**1**), like [polynomial multiplication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_multiplication) polynomial coefficients.

For example for product of a triangle, f=(3,3), and dion, f=(2) makes a [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584) with 6 vertices, 9 edges, and 5 faces:

f<sub>A</sub>(x) = (3,3,**1**) = 3 + 3x + x<sup>2</sup> (triangle)

f<sub>B</sub>(x) = (2,**1**) = 2 + x (dion)

f<sub>A∨B</sub>(x) = f<sub>A</sub>(x) \* f<sub>B</sub>(x)

\= (3 + 3x + x<sup>2</sup>) \* (2 + x)

\= 6 + 9x + 5x<sup>2</sup> + x<sup>3</sup>

\= (6,9,5,**1**)

[Hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) f-vectors can be computed as Cartesian products of *n* dions, { }<sup>n</sup>. Each { } has f=(2), extended to f=(2,**1**).

For example, an [8-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-cube) will have extended f-vector power product: f=(2,**1**)<sup>8</sup> = (4,4,**1**)<sup>4</sup> = (16,32,24,8,**1**)<sup>2</sup> = (256,1024,1792,1792,1120,448,112,16,**1**). If equal lengths, this doubling represents { }<sup>8</sup>, a square tetra-prism {4}<sup>4</sup>, a tesseract duo-prism {4,3,3}<sup>2</sup>, and regular 8-cube {4,3,3,3,3,3,3}.

## Double products or duoprisms {#Double_products_or_duoprisms}

In geometry of 4 dimensions or higher, **duoprism** is a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) resulting from the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two polytopes, each of two dimensions or higher. The Cartesian product of an *a*-polytope, a *b*-polytope is an *(a+b)*-polytope, where *a* and *b* are 2-polytopes ([polygon](wikipedia://en/23621)) or higher.

Most commonly this refers to the product of two polygons in 4-dimensions. In the context of a product of polygons, Henry P. Manning's 1910 work explaining the [fourth dimension](wikipedia://en/1364622) called these **double prisms**.[^o7]

The [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621) is the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of points:

$$P_1 \times P_2 = \{ (x,y,u,v) | (x,y)\in P_1, (u,v)\in P_2 \}$$

where *P<sub>1</sub>* and *P<sub>2</sub>* are the sets of the points contained in the respective polygons.

The smallest is a [3-3 duoprism](wikipedia://en/41745711), made as the product of 2 triangles. If the triangles are regular it can be written as a product of [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394), {3} × {3}, and is composed of 9 vertices.

The [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112), can be constructed as the duoprism {4} × {4}, the product of two equal-size orthogonal [squares](wikipedia://en/659939), composed of 16 vertices. The [5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215) can be constructed as a duoprism {4} × {4,3}, the product of a square and cube, while the [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462) can be constructed as the product of two cubes, {4,3} × {4,3}.

## Triple products {#Triple_products}

*Image: 333-prism.png.* The prism {3} × {3} × {3} can be seen in orthogonal projection within a regular enneagon.

In geometry of 6 dimensions or higher, a triple product is a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) resulting from the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of three polytopes, each of two dimensions or higher. The Cartesian product of an *a*-polytope, a *b*-polytope, and a *c*-polytope is an (*a* + *b* + *c*)-polytope, where *a*, *b* and *c* are 2-polytopes ([polygon](wikipedia://en/23621)) or higher.

The lowest-dimensional forms are [6-polytopes](wikipedia://en/26021427) being the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of three [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621). The smallest can be written as {3} × {3} × {3} in [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394) if they are regular, and contains 27 vertices. This is the product of three [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) and is a [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392). The f-vectors can be computed by (3,3,**1**)<sup>3</sup> = (27,81,108,81,36,9,**1**).

The [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462), can be constructed as a triple product {4} × {4} × {4}. The f-vectors can be computed by (4,4,**1**)<sup>3</sup> = (64,192,240,160,60,12,**1**).
