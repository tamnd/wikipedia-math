# John Horvath (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Horvath"}

**John Michael Horvath** (born János Horváth; 30 July 1924 – 12 March 2015) was a Hungarian-American mathematician. He is noted for his contributions to [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), especially in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [distribution theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29).[^o2]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Horvath was born János Horváth on 30 July 1924 in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest).

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Horvath received his doctorate in 1947 from the [University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Budapest) as a student of [Lipót Fejér](wikipedia://en/3934357) and [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126). Four other talented mathematicians also graduated in the class of 1947: [János Aczél](wikipedia://en/22717925), [Ákos Császár](wikipedia://en/22603037), [László Fuchs](wikipedia://en/34809998) and [István Gál](wikipedia://en/24774553). Together with Horvath, they were referred to as the Big Five.[^o2]

After obtaining his doctorate, he went to the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) (CNRS) to do research. At the recommendation of [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) and [Salomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384), in 1951 he came to the newly founded [University of Los Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Los_Andes_%28Colombia%29) in [Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogotá), where he became the first head of the mathematics department and established modern mathematics in [Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombia). At his invitation, [Laurent Schwartz](wikipedia://en/317485) and [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084) visited the university. His later work on analytic continuations and a general definition of the [Convolution](wikipedia://en/53268#Convolution_theorem_for_tempered_distributions) of distributions spread the ideas of Schwartz's [theory of distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29).[^o5] In 1957 Horvath finally went to the United States, where he taught at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland) until 1994 and was then awarded the title of Professor Emeritus.[^o6]

It was Schwartz, who recommended him to write elementary textbook on distribution theory. [MathSciNet](wikipedia://en/2580683) called his book *Topological Vector Spaces and Distributions* (1966), "The most readable introduction to the theory of vector spaces available in English and possibly any other language."[^o7] In 2006, Horvath edited and wrote one of the chapters (*Holomorphic Functions*) for *A Panorama of Hungarian Mathematics in the Twentieth Century*.[^o2]

He was an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) since 1958.[^o8] In 1997, he received an honorary doctorate of the Universidad de los Andes. And in 1998, he became a member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences), in 2001 he was elected to the [Colombian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Academy_of_Exact,_Physical,_and_Natural_Sciences).[^o2]
