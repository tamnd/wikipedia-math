# Numbers: The Universal Language

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Numbers: The Universal Language"}

***Numbers: The Universal Language*** (**L'empire des nombres**) is a 1996 [illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livre_d'art) [monograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph) on [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690) and [their history](wikipedia://en/21690#History). Written by the French historian of science [Denis Guedj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Guedj), and published in [pocket format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_edition) by [Éditions Gallimard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Éditions_Gallimard) as the 300 volume in their "[Découvertes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Découvertes_Gallimard)" collection[^o3] (known as "Abrams Discoveries" in the United States, and "New Horizons" in the United Kingdom). The book was adapted into a documentary film of the same title in 2001.[^o4]

:::

## Introduction {#Introduction}

*Image: Numbers - The Universal Language ("Abrams Discoveries" and "New Horizons").jpg.* From left: US and UK editions. The covers featuring the ten digits interpreted by the computer artist Julio Bravo, 1997.

The book is part of the Sciences et techniques series (formerly belonging to Sciences series) in the "[Découvertes Gallimard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Découvertes_Gallimard)" collection. As a work of popularisation of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), it uses simple language to describe the basics of numbers—[arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118), [integer](wikipedia://en/14563), [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474), concepts of [zero](wikipedia://en/34513) and [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824)—as well as how numbers and their symbolism came to be used in art and other disciplines.

According to the tradition of "Découvertes", which is based on an abundant pictorial documentation and a way of bringing together visual documents and texts, enhanced by printing on coated paper, as commented in *[L'Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Express)*, "genuine monographs, published like [art books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livre_d'art)".[^o6] It's almost like a "[graphic novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_novel)", replete with colour plates.

## Contents {#Contents}

The book opens with a "trailer" (1), that is, a series of full-page photographs showing *The [Powers of Ten](wikipedia://en/1161104)*. The body text is divided into seven chapters:

- Chapter 1: "How Many?" (13);
- Chapter 2: "From Numbers to Figures" (25);
- Chapter 3: "Positional Notation" (45);
- Chapter 4: "Natural Numbers" (61);
- Chapter 5: "The Universe Expands" (79);
- Chapter 6: "From Zero to Infinity" (105);
- Chapter 7: "The Impossible Definition" (123).

The second part of the book, the "Documents", containing a compilation of excerpts divided into nine parts:

1. Counting (130);
1. Against Pythagoras, Against Zeno (132);
1. Numbers and religion (136);
1. Numbers, philosophy, and poetry (138);
1. The science of measurement (142);
1. The abacus and calculator (146);
1. Music and mathematics (148);
1. Number and psychology (152);
1. The wit and wisdom of numbers (156).

<!-- -->

- Amusing Puzzle (164);
- Glossary (165);
- Chronology (168);
- Further Reading (170);
- List of Illustrations (171);
- Index (173).

## Reception {#Reception}

On [Babelio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babelio), the book gets an average of 3.0/5 based on 11 ratings.[^o9] [Goodreads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodreads) reported, based on 66 ratings, an average of 3.61 out of 5,[^o10] indicating "generally positive opinions".

## Adaptation {#Adaptation}

In 2001, the book was adapted as a documentary film of the same name. A co-production between La Sept-Arte and Trans Europe Film, with the collaboration of Éditions Gallimard and [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) Images Média, the film was directed by [Philippe Truffault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Truffault), with [voice-over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice-over) narration by [Denis Guedj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Guedj) himself. It was broadcast on [Arte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arte) as part of the television programme *[The Human Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Human_Adventure_%28TV_series%29)*,[^o4][^o13] and released on DVD by Arte vidéo, with English dubbed audio.[^o14] The film has also been dubbed into German by the title Im Reich der Zahlen.[^o15]
