# Majority minority

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Majority minority"}

A **majority-minority** or **minority-majority area** is a term used to refer to a [subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division) in which one or more [racial, ethnic, and/or religious minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group) (relative to the whole country's population) make up a majority of the local population.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Terminology"}

## Terminology {#Terminology}

The exact terminology used differs from place to place and language to language. In many large, contiguous countries like [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) or the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom), a minority population (for the whole state) is often the majority in a subdivision. For example, [Tibetan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) are the majority in the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) and [Scottish people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people) are the majority in [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland). The demographics in these regions are generally the result of historical population distributions, not because of recent immigration or recent differences in birth and fertility rates between various groups. As a result, it is rare for a Chinese [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_region) or a British [home nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_nation) to be described as 'majority minority', even though they would fit the definition.

:::

## Background {#Background}

Majority minority areas exist in two main forms. One form is when a homogeneous grouping residing within an area make up a majority of the local population. This grouping would otherwise be a minority in the broader jurisdiction. The other type occurs when several disparate groupings, when counted together, form a percentage-share majority of the local population, outnumbering the historically dominant group as a composite of diverse minority groups.[^o5]

Whether distinctions between groups are religious, ethnic, linguistic or racial; these different forms of majority-minority scenarios, or areas, tend to contribute towards different socio-political and cultural environments. For example, a study of the 2006 [European Social Survey](wikipedia://en/18591783) found that people of localized majority-minority status across 21 [EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU) countries were more supportive of stronger political [European integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_integration) than existing national native majorities,[^o6] and a 2019 [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) study found that 46 percent of [white Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans) believed national majority-minority demography would negatively impact [American culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_culture).[^o7]

There has also been study on groupings said to have 'old' and 'new' majority-minority status in specific areas. In research funded by the EU's [Framework Programmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framework_Programmes_for_Research_and_Technological_Development), a 2015 study explored this difference, finding that, for example, [ethnic Austrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrians) living in [South Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tyrol) manifest a culture which tends to oblige [ethnic Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Italians) to learn the [German language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) for advancement in the province, such as access to the administration of local government. This was contrasted with 'new' immigration-derived majority-minority populaces in Europe.[^o8]

## Schools {#Schools}

In the United States, the vast majority of [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans) and [Hispanic and Latino Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) attend schools where [white Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans) are in the minority.[^o10] 2006 research from [The Civil Rights Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Civil_Rights_Project) found that, on average, white students attend schools that are 78% white, while black and Hispanic students attend schools which are 29% white. A study on this suggested that; "This data is important because "majority minority" schools have the worst facilities (buildings, labs, libraries, athletic facilities), the least qualified teachers, the worst overcrowding, and the least financial support."[^o11] In regards to racial classification at a national level, [public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_United_States#Public_and_private_schools) in the US obtained majority minority status in 2014.[^o12] At the university level, [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University)'s first case of a majority-minority [freshman class](wikipedia://en/11541500#United_States) was reported in 2017.[^o13]

In the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands), majority-minority schools emerged in the post-war period, starting as a phenomenon in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam) with [immigration from Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinamese_people_in_the_Netherlands) and [from Curaçao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curaçaoans_in_the_Netherlands), right after [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). In the 1970 and 80s, second-generation [black Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Dutch) students with ancestry from the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles), were joined in classes by the children of workers [emigrating from Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_in_the_Netherlands) and [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco), creating [ethnic Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Dutch) minorities in some schools within the country's capital.[^o25] In a 2020 study of school classes in European cities, research on [Turks in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_in_Austria) and [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_in_Belgium) found that "a 'majority minority' school environment may empower minority group members so that relative numbers would protect them from becoming the target of discrimination."[^o14]

## Observation by settlement {#Observation_by_settlement}

### Towns and cities {#Towns_and_cities}

Many cities in [North America](wikipedia://en/21139) have majority-minority scenarios (based upon racial classifications in the [US census](wikipedia://en/447689) and the [census in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_in_Canada)).[^o17] Since the late 20th century, areas of Northern and [Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe) have been undergoing demographic transformation resulting in majority minority cities.[^o18] A 2018 study in *[Frontiers in Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontiers_in_Psychology)* analyzed:[^o19]

> In the United States and Canada racial minorities already comprise a larger share of the population than Whites in dozens of major cities (e.g., [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver) and [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City)). These cities have been dubbed majority-minority areas—or places where the racial/ethnic majority comprise less than half the population (Frey, 2011; Jedwab, 2016). Western Europe is also becoming more diverse, albeit more slowly (Browne, 2000). [London, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_England) is one of the few major European cities that has been designated a majority–minority area.

Based upon the UK's [Office for National Statistics](wikipedia://en/324029) racial or ethnic categorization, demographic research suggests that [Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester) and [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham) will each join London in majority minority status in the 2020s.[^o20][^o21] [University of Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Antwerp)'s professor Dirk Geldof, writing in 2016, noted that "within a matter of years, [Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerp) will also become a majority-minority city, as will many other [European cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_cities)."[^o22] An [education inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_%28disability_rights%29) project at [Hague University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_University) published that; "In superdiverse cities like [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague) and [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels) there is no majority anymore. These are so-called majority minority cities".[^o23] According to a study at the [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission)'s research repository [CORDIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Research_and_Development_Information_Service):[^o24]

> In cities like [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam), now only one in three youngsters under age fifteen is of native descent. This situation, referred to as a majority-minority context, is a new phenomenon in Western Europe and it presents itself as one of the most important societal and psychological transformations of our time.

In the course of two generations places in [Northwestern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Europe), such as [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam) and [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels), have become majority minority, with [ethnic Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Dutch), [Flemings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemings) and [Walloons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walloons), respectively, representing less than 50 percent of the population of the capitals.[^o25]

### States and regions {#States_and_regions}

In 2010, the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC) reported that "America's two largest states - [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California) and [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas) - became "majority-minority" states (with an overall minority population outnumbering the white majority) in 1998 and 2004 respectively."[^o27] Demographers [Dudley L. Poston Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_L._Poston_Jr.) and [Rogelio Sáenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogelio_Sáenz) have noted how "nonwhites account for more than half of the populations of [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii), the [District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Columbia), California, [New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico), Texas and [Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada). In the next 10 to 15 years, these half-dozen “majority-minority” states will likely be joined by as many as eight other states where whites now make up less than 60 percent of the population."[^o28]

In Europe, various national medias report on the social situation in the French suburbs with regards to disproportionate poverty and unrest. Known as [banlieues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banlieues), these outer-city regions across France are often majority-minority areas, in terms of race or ethnicity in relation to the [ethnic French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_French).[^o29]

### Nations and countries {#Nations_and_countries}

The meaning of "majority-minority" or "minority-majority", in relation to a whole country, is not well defined and may not be consistent between different users of the terms. A multitude of scholars have designated countries, or sovereign states, particularly in the developed or [Western world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world), which are projected to obtain majority-minority demography between 2040 and 2050. This includes the United States, Canada and New Zealand, with [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia), and nations in [Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe), estimated to follow this trend toward the end of the century.[^o17][^o28] In this usage, "majority-minority" usually means that a previously majority group becomes a plurality group, less than 50% of the population but still larger than any other group. Occasionally, it may mean a change of the majority group, with the previously majority group becoming a minority group and a previously minority group becoming the new majority group.

This will not be the first time that the status of majority ethnic group has changed in these countries: it is estimated that Australia became a "majority-minority" country in the 1840s, when arriving Europeans first outnumbered [Indigenous Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Australians).[^o31][^o32] New Zealand became "majority-minority" slightly later, with non-Māori first outnumbering the Māori population around 1858.[^o33] [David Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Coleman_%28demographer%29) has studied a similar statistical projection in Britain. Coleman, a professor of demography at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), estimates that by 2060 [the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people_in_the_United_Kingdom) will reach majority-minority status (where the "white British" ethnic group is taken to be the current "majority", excluding "white Irish", "other white" and "mixed" groups).[^o20][^o21]

In the [developing world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_world), the South American nation of [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil) has been described as a majority-minority country. This is with regards to [white Brazilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Brazilians) being the historically largest group, and while remaining culturally dominant, have since become a national minority.[^o34]

## Distinctions between groups {#Distinctions_between_groups}

There are multiple axis points of difference, and distinction, between groupings of people that can contribute towards the attribution of majority-minority status upon a particular area or within a societal scenario. With cultural, linguistic or religious differences, there is usually a corresponding difference in ethnicity, whether related in a central or peripheral way, to said distinctions. For instance, there are examples of this throughout [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe). Where racial distinctions are made, this is most often in relation to [white people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people), and most usually in [European nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_nations) or nations derived from [European colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonialism), such as Brazil or the United States. Other countries, such as Australia, do not collect statistics based on racial categories such as "white people" or "black people", preferring to categorise ancestries by reference to self-identified country of ancestral origin, such as "English", "German" or "Australian".[^o36]

### Cultural and linguistic {#Cultural_and_linguistic}

Where religion does not significantly influence designations of majority minority labels, certain cultural and linguistic differences may be emphasized in that particular society, such as in [South Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tyrol). Whereas the majority of residents in the [northern Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italian) province are [ethnic Austrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrians) and speak the [German language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) (in comparison with the [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language)-speaking [ethnic Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Italians) majority of the broader nation), the population's adherence to [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) is similar to the rest of the country.[^o38]

### Ethnic {#Ethnic}

[Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo) has a history of being a majority-minority area via the historic borders of [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia) in the 20th century, and prior to its independence declaration. While this phenomenon may predominantly be observed to be ethnicity-based (upon distinctions between ethnic [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians) and [ethnic Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Serbs)), contributing factors involve the [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic) religiosity of Albanians and [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) of Serbians, as well as the ethno-linguistic considerations of the [Albanian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language) and [Serbian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language).[^o40]

### Racial {#Racial}

When majority minority status is designated or predicted in terms of racial groups, many scholarly and journalistic works make this distinction with reference to [white people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people).[^o42] Based upon nation-based racial classifications, academics [Eric Kaufmann](wikipedia://en/52270193) and [Matthew Goodwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Goodwin_%28author%29) have suggested that white people will be minorities in the United States, Canada and New Zealand, in what they define as "the ‘majority-minority’ point", by approximately 2050.[^o43]

### Religious {#Religious}

While majority-minority status for Catholics in areas of Northern Ireland, in contrast with historical Protestant majorities across the territory as a whole, can be described to be based on religion; there are ethno-linguistic factors (such as [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language)-speaking Catholics and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language)-speaking Protestants), as well as broader overlapping factors of ethnicity (Catholic [Gaels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaels) and Protestant people of mainly [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people), [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people) and [Huguenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot) descent) which can contribute toward religiously defined majority minority attribution.[^o45]

#### Georgia {#Georgia}

- The Georgian province of [Samtskhe-Javakheti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samtskhe-Javakheti) has an [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians) majority, while the Georgian province of [Kvemo Kartli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kvemo_Kartli) has an [Azeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis) plurality.[^o55] No ethnic group composed a majority of the population in [Abkhazia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abkhazia) from at least 2003 until around 2011.[^o56][^o57]

#### India {#India}

*Image: Muslim in India.png.* The [Muslim population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_India) in various parts of India in 2011

- [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_India) are a majority in the [Lakshadweep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshadweep) and [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_%28union_territory%29) [states/territories of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_territories_of_India), and in some other [districts of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_India). However, Muslims are a minority in India overall.
- [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_India) currently make up the majority of the Northeast Indian states of [Nagaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagaland) at 90%, [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram) at 88% and [Meghalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meghalaya) at 83.3%, although Christians do not even make up more than 3% of India's total population.[^o59]
- [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism_in_India) make the majority of state of [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India), although once again they do not form a majority in India overall.[^o60]
- In the Northeast Indian state of [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh), no religious or ethnic group constitutes more than 30% of its population,[^o60] owing to the state's great cultural diversity.

#### Indonesia {#Indonesia}

- Indonesia is the world's most populous Muslim country, with Islam being practiced by around 88% of the population, or over 200 million people.[^o62] Despite this there are several areas of Indonesia where Muslims are the minority:
  - 84% of the population of [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali) practice a form of Hinduism called [Balinese Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_Hinduism),[^o63] although Hindus only form 1.7% of Indonesia's overall population.[^o62]
  - [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Indonesia) form the majority of [East Nusa Tenggara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Nusa_Tenggara) at 51%,[^o63] although Catholics only form about 3% of Indonesia's overall population.[^o62]
  - [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Indonesia) form the majority in three Indonesian provinces: [West Papua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Papua_%28province%29) at 60%, [Papua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_%28province%29) at 68%, and [North Sulawesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sulawesi) at 64%.[^o63] Overall though Protestants only form about 7% of Indonesia's population.[^o62]
- Several neighborhoods and communities in major Indonesian cities such as [Jakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta), [Batam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batam), [Pontianak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontianak), [Singkawang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singkawang), [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan) and [Bagansiapiapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagansiapiapi) are of predominantly of [Chinese origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesian), although people of Chinese descent do not form more than 10% in any of these cities overall population and only form about 1-2% of Indonesia's overall population.[^o64]

#### United States {#United_States}

*Image: US states districts and territories in 2020 in which non-Hispanic whites are less than 50%.png.*

In the United States of America, **majority-minority area** or **minority-majority area** is a term describing a [U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state) or jurisdiction whose population is composed of less than 50% [non-Hispanic whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_whites). [White Hispanic and Latino Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) are excluded in many definitions. Racial data is derived from self-identification questions on the [U.S. census](wikipedia://en/447689) and on [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census_Bureau) estimates. (See [Race and ethnicity in the United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_ethnicity_in_the_United_States_census)). The term is often used in [voting rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_rights_in_the_United_States) law to designate voting districts which are altered under the [Voting Rights Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_Rights_Act_of_1965) to enable ethnic or language minorities "the opportunity to elect their candidate of choice."[^o144] In that context, the term is first used by the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States) in 1977.[^o145] The Court had previously used the term in employment discrimination and labor relations cases.[^o146]

### Oceania {#Oceania}

#### Australia {#Australia}

It is estimated that Europeans first outnumbered [Indigenous Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Australians) in Australia in the 1840s.[^o31][^o32] There are still a number of rural and regional towns and communities where Indigenous Australians outnumber Europeans, but nationally Indigenous Australians constitute only 3.3% of the population. The state-level jurisdiction with the highest proportion of Indigenous Australians is the [Northern Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory), where people of Indigenous ancestry make up 30.3% of the population, the largest single ethnic group by reported ancestry (although a smaller proportion, 25.5%, *identify as* Indigenous).[^o149]

Of the other ethnic groups in Australia, no single ethnic group constitutes a majority overall. [English Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Australians) make up the largest single ethnic group by ancestry, being reported by 36.1% of the population in the 2016 census. The next largest ancestry group is "Australian", at 33.5%.[^o150] As ancestry is self-reported and each person can nominate two ancestries (and through a separate question report whether they identify as Indigenous Australian), there is no certainty as to the ethnic make-up of the ancestry group who identify as "Australian". It is commonly speculated however that the majority of the "Australian" ancestry group have some ancestral origin from the British Isles, and as a result when people with ethnic origin in the British Isles are considered as a single group ("[Anglo-Celtic Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Celtic_Australians)"), the numbers for the "Australian" ancestry group is added to that of the English, Scottish, Welsh, and Irish ethnic groups, as well as (sometimes) [Manx Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manx_Australian) and [Cornish Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornish_Australians). When "Anglo-Celtic" ethnic groups are considered together, they make up a majority of Australia's population overall (58% estimated in 2018[^o151]). When considered as one group, [European Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Australians) make up 57.2% of the population (including 46% [North-West European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Europe#Ethnographic_definitions) and 11.2% [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe) and [Eastern European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe)).[^o152][^o153] (This figure excludes those who nominate their ancestry as simply "Australian", who are therefore categorised as part of the [Oceanian](wikipedia://en/28989907) ancestry group.)[^o153]

Reflecting the diversity of ancestries at the national level, in most Australian towns and suburbs, no single ancestry group constitutes a majority of the population. In many places, if the "Australians" ancestry group is counted as part of an "Anglo-Celtic" ethnic group together with English, Scottish, Welsh and Irish Australians, this group constitutes a majority of the local population. However, in many other places, even when these ancestry groups are counted as one, there is no single majority ethnic group. It is rare for any non-Anglo-Celtic and non-Indigenous ethnic group to make up a majority in a suburb. For example, in the 2016 census, in the Sydney metropolitan area, [Chinese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Australians) in the suburbs [Burwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burwood,_New_South_Wales) and [Hurstville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurstville,_New_South_Wales) made up just over 50% of the population (although reports of Chinese ancestry are less than 50% of all ancestries reported in the census in each suburb, as each person is permitted to nominate more than one ancestry).[^o154][^o155] However, by the 2021 census the Chinese-ancestry population in both suburbs had dropped below 50%.[^o156][^o157] [Chinese Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Australians) make up 5.6% of the national population, making them the fifth largest ancestry group overall (after the English, Australian, Scottish and Irish ancestry groups).[^o150]

### South America {#South_America}

#### Brazil {#Brazil}

Brazil has become a majority "non-White" country as of the 2010 census,[^o164] together with the [federative units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Brazil) of [Espírito Santo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espírito_Santo), the [Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_District_%28Brazil%29), [Goiás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goiás), and [Minas Gerais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minas_Gerais).

Those identifying as White declined to 47.7% (about 91 million people) in the 2010 census from 52.9% (about 93 million people) in 2000 in the entire country.[^o164] However, in Brazil, this is not simply a matter of origin and birthrate, but identity changes as well. The Black minority did not enlarge its representation in the population to more than 1.5% in the period, while it was mostly the growth in the number of *[pardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardo)* people (~38% in 2000, 42.4% in 2010) that caused the demographic plurality of Brazil.

#### Colombia {#Colombia}

[Afro-Colombians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Colombians) make up roughly about 10–12% of country's overall population, but make up a majority in many areas in the [Colombia's Pacific region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific/Chocó_natural_region),[^o166] especially in [Chocó Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocó_Department), where they make up 80–90% of the population.[^o167]
