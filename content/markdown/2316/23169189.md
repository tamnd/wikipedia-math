# Irreducible ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Irreducible ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the field of [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), the term **irreducible ring** is used in a few different ways.

:::

- A **(meet-)irreducible ring** is a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) in which the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of two non-[zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_ideal) [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) is always non-zero.
- A **directly irreducible ring** is a ring which cannot be written as the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of two non-[zero](wikipedia://en/1872874) rings.
- A **subdirectly irreducible ring** is a ring with a unique, non-zero minimum two-sided ideal.
- A ring with an **irreducible spectrum** is a ring whose [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/7556651) as a topological space.

"Meet-irreducible" rings are referred to as "irreducible rings" in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990). This article adopts the term "meet-irreducible" in order to distinguish between the several types being discussed.

Meet-irreducible rings play an important part in commutative algebra, and directly irreducible and subdirectly irreducible rings play a role in the general theory of structure for rings. [Subdirectly irreducible algebras](wikipedia://en/17398087) have also found use in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

This article follows the convention that rings have [multiplicative identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicative_identity), but are not necessarily [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

The terms "meet-reducible", "directly reducible" and "subdirectly reducible" are used when a ring is *not* meet-irreducible, or *not* directly irreducible, or *not* subdirectly irreducible, respectively.

The following conditions are equivalent for a commutative ring *R*:

- *R* is meet-irreducible;
- the zero ideal in *R* is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/22260381), i.e. the intersection of two non-zero ideals of *A* always is non-zero.

The following conditions are equivalent for a ring *R*:

- *R* is directly irreducible;
- *R* has no [central idempotents](wikipedia://en/361940#Types_of_ring_idempotents) except for 0 and 1.

The following conditions are equivalent for a ring *R*:

- *R* is subdirectly irreducible;
- when *R* is written as a [subdirect product](wikipedia://en/17395221) of rings, then one of the projections of *R* onto a ring in the subdirect product is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828);
- The intersection of all non-zero ideals of *R* is non-zero.

The following conditions are equivalent for a commutative ring *R*:[^o4]

- the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) of *R* is [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_space).
- *R* possesses exactly one [minimal prime ideal](wikipedia://en/16143832) (this [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) may be the zero ideal).

:::

## Examples and properties {#Examples_and_properties}

If *R* is subdirectly irreducible or meet-irreducible, then it is also directly irreducible, but the [converses](wikipedia://en/155941) are not true.

- All [integral domains](wikipedia://en/15462) are meet-irreducible, but not all integral domains are subdirectly irreducible (e.g. **Z**). In fact, a commutative ring is a domain if and only if it is both meet-irreducible and [reduced](wikipedia://en/539749).
- A commutative ring is a domain if and only if its spectrum is irreducible and it is [reduced](wikipedia://en/539749).[^o6][^o7][^o8]
- The [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) **Z**/4**Z** is a ring which has all three senses of irreducibility, but it is not a domain. Its only proper ideal is 2**Z**/4**Z**, which is [maximal](wikipedia://en/48164), hence prime. The ideal is also [minimal](wikipedia://en/34233472).
- The [direct product](wikipedia://en/25063) of two non-zero rings is never directly irreducible, and hence is never meet-irreducible or subdirectly irreducible. For example, in **Z** × **Z** the intersection of the non-zero ideals {0} × **Z** and **Z** × {0} is equal to the zero ideal {0} × {0}.
- Commutative directly irreducible rings are [connected rings](wikipedia://en/23169183); that is, their only [idempotent elements](wikipedia://en/361940) are 0 and 1.

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Commutative meet-irreducible rings play an elementary role in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), where this concept is generalized to the concept of an [irreducible scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_scheme).
