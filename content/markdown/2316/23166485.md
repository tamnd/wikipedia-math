# Benjamin Finkel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Benjamin Finkel"}

**Benjamin Franklin Finkel** (July 5, 1865 – February 5, 1947) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and educator most remembered today as the founder of the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)* magazine.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Fairfield County, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield_County,_Ohio) and educated in small country schools, Finkel received both bachelor's and master's degrees from [Ohio Northern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Northern_University), then known as Ohio Normal University (1888 and 1891, respectively).

In 1888 he copyrighted *A Mathematical Solution Book*. The purpose of the book was to provide mathematics teachers a text utilizing a systematic method of problem solving, "The Step Method", representing a chain of reasoning, in logical order, to arrive at the correct result.[^o4] The first edition was postponed until 1893, due to financial problems of the original publisher.[^o5] The book's preface stated that the work was based upon eight years of teaching in the public schools. Following editions were published in 1897, 1899 and 1902.[^o6]

In 1895 he became professor of mathematics and physics at [Drury University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drury_University), then known as Drury College. He was a University Scholar in Mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) from 1895–1896.[^o6] In 1906 he was awarded a doctorate from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), where he had earlier earned an additional master's degree in 1904 and a Harrison fellow appointment in 1905.[^o5]

He was a member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), 1891; the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097), 1898; and [Circolo Matematico di Palermo](wikipedia://en/32146789), 1902.[^o6] He retained his professorship at Drury College until his death in 1947.
