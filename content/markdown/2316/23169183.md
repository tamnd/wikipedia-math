# Connected ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Connected ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the field of [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), a **connected ring** is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *A* that satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions:[^n1]

:::

- *A* possesses no non-trivial (that is, not equal to 1 or 0) [idempotent elements](wikipedia://en/361940);
- the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) of *A* with the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) is a [connected space](wikipedia://en/6233).

## Examples and non-examples {#Examples_and_non-examples}

Connectedness defines a fairly general class of commutative rings. For example, all [local rings](wikipedia://en/153391) and all (meet-)[irreducible rings](wikipedia://en/23169189) are connected. In particular, all [integral domains](wikipedia://en/15462) are connected. Non-examples are given by [product rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_ring) such as **Z** × **Z**; here the element (1, 0) is a non-trivial idempotent.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), connectedness is generalized to the concept of a [connected scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_scheme).

:::
