# Topological dynamics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Topological dynamics"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **topological dynamics** is a branch of the theory of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) in which qualitative, asymptotic properties of dynamical systems are studied from the viewpoint of [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649).

:::

## Scope {#Scope}

The central object of study in topological dynamics is a **topological dynamical system**, i.e. a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), together with a [continuous transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map_%28topology%29), a continuous flow, or more generally, a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/19313303) of continuous transformations of that space. The origins of topological dynamics lie in the study of asymptotic properties of [trajectories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajectory) of systems of [autonomous](wikipedia://en/312398) [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753), in particular, the behavior of [limit sets](wikipedia://en/5492198) and various manifestations of "repetitiveness" of the motion, such as periodic trajectories, recurrence and minimality, stability, [non-wandering points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-wandering_point). [George Birkhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Birkhoff) is considered to be the founder of the field. A structure theorem for minimal distal flows proved by [Hillel Furstenberg](wikipedia://en/6450152) in the early 1960s inspired much work on classification of minimal flows. A lot of research in the 1970s and 1980s was devoted to topological dynamics of one-dimensional maps, in particular, [piecewise linear](wikipedia://en/444224) self-maps of the interval and the circle.

Unlike the theory of smooth dynamical systems, where the main object of study is a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) with a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) or a smooth flow, [phase spaces](wikipedia://en/191101) considered in topological dynamics are general [metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_spaces) (usually, [compact](wikipedia://en/6042)). This necessitates development of entirely different techniques but allows an extra degree of flexibility even in the smooth setting, because [invariant subsets of a manifold](wikipedia://en/29763944) are frequently very complicated topologically (cf [limit cycle](wikipedia://en/452950), [strange attractor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_attractor)); additionally, [shift spaces](wikipedia://en/15470598) arising via symbolic representations can be considered on an equal footing with more geometric actions. Topological dynamics has intimate connections with [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986) of dynamical systems, and many fundamental concepts of the latter have topological analogues (cf [Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov–Sinai_entropy) and [topological entropy](wikipedia://en/5463978)).
