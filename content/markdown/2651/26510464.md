# Jan Mycielski

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jan Mycielski"}

**Jan Mycielski** (Polish: j; February 7, 1932 – January 18, 2025) was a Polish-American mathematician, [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) and philosopher, who was a professor of mathematics at the [University of Colorado at Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_at_Boulder).[^o3] He is known for contributions to [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Mycielski was born in [Wiśniowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiśniowa,_Strzyżów_County), [Podkarpackie Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podkarpackie_Voivodeship), Poland on February 2, 1932.[^o5]

Mycielski received his Ph.D. in mathematics from the [University of Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław) in 1957 under the supervision of Stanisław Hartman. His dissertation was entitled "Applications of [Free Groups](wikipedia://en/59735) to Geometrical Constructions". Following positions at the [Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_National_de_la_Recherche_Scientifique) in Paris, the Institute of Mathematics of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences), the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), and [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University), he took a permanent faculty position at Colorado in 1969.[^o5]

Mycielski died in January 2025, at the age of 92.[^o7]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Among the mathematical concepts named after Mycielski are:

- The [Ehrenfeucht–Mycielski sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehrenfeucht–Mycielski_sequence), a sequence of [binary digits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_digit) with [pseudorandom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudorandom) properties
- The [Mycielskian](wikipedia://en/7685346), a construction for embedding any [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) into a larger graph with strictly higher [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) without creating any additional [triangles](wikipedia://en/27071812).
- The [Mycielski–Grötzsch graph](wikipedia://en/7685783), the Mycielskian of the 5-[cycle](wikipedia://en/168609), an 11-vertex [triangle-free graph](wikipedia://en/7667996) that is the smallest possible triangle-free graph requiring four colors.
- [Mycielski's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycielski's_theorem) that there exist triangle-free graphs with arbitrarily large chromatic number.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1965, he received the [Stefan Banach Prize](wikipedia://en/2121791#Stefan_Banach_Prize) of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791).

In 1990, he was awarded the [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122) Medal and Lecture by the Polish Mathematical Society.[^o10]

In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o11]
