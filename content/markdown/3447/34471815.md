# Edwin Plimpton Adams

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edwin Plimpton Adams"}

**Edwin Plimpton Adams** ([Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague), 23 January 1878[^o3] – [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), 31 December 1956) was an American physicist known for translating [Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s lectures. [Clinton Joseph Davisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Joseph_Davisson) attended his lectures. Adams was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1915.[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Edwin Plimpton Adams was born in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague), at the time part of Bohemia, on 23 January 1878 to missionary parents. His father, Rev. Edwin Augustus Adams (1837–1927), was an American pastor from Connecticut, who spent 10 years as a representative of the [American Board of Foreign Missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Board_of_Commissioners_for_Foreign_Missions) in Prague, before moving back the family back to Northboro, Connecticut, and then Chicago.[^o7] His mother was Caroline Amelia Plimpton (1842–1928).

Adams earned his bachelor's degree at [Beloit College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beloit_College) in 1899 and PhD in physics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1904. During his graduate studies he was a Tyndall fellow at Harvard which allowed him to study at Berlin, Göttingen, and Trinity College, Cambridge.

### Career {#Career}

In 1903, Adams was hired as an instructor for the [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) Physics Department, helping expand the four person department originally consisting of [Cyrus Fogg Brackett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Fogg_Brackett_Chair_of_Physics), [William Francis Magie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Francis_Magie), E. H. Loomis, and Howard McClenahan.[^o9] During his first few years, Adams taught courses in a number of subjects at the undergraduate and graduate level and took on graduate students to work on experimental research in electricity and magnetism. He became a full professor in 1909 and his research interests later shifted to theory when he took over teaching advanced theoretical physics and applied mathematics courses when [James H Jeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Jeans) left Princeton to return to England.[^o10][^o9]

During World War I, Adams took leave from Princeton to join the Royal Engineers of the British Army. He was in active service in France with a sound-ranging company from 1917 to 1919, and he was later made an Officer of the Order of the British Empire.[^o9]
