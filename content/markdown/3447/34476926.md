# Monod equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monod equation"}

The **Monod equation** is a [mathematical model](wikipedia://en/20590) for the growth of microorganisms. It is named for French biochemist [Jacques Monod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Monod), who proposed using an equation of this form to relate microbial growth rates in an aqueous environment to the concentration of a limiting nutrient.[^o2][^o3][^o4] The Monod equation has the same form as the [Michaelis–Menten equation](wikipedia://en/253272), but differs in that it is [empirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_relationship) while the latter is based on theoretical considerations.

:::

The Monod equation is commonly used in [environmental engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_engineering). For example, it is used in the [activated sludge model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activated_sludge_model) for [sewage treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment).

## Equation {#Equation}

*Image: Monod 3.svg.* The growth rate *μ* of a considered micro-organism as a function of the limiting substrate concentration \[*S*

\]

The empirical Monod equation is[^o6]

$$\mu = \mu_\max \frac{[S]}{K_s + [S]}$$

where:

*μ* is the growth rate of a considered microorganism,

*μ*<sub>max</sub> is the maximum growth rate of this microorganism,

\[*S*\] is the concentration of the [limiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limiting_factor) [substrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme_substrate_%28biology%29) *S* for growth,

*K*<sub>*s*</sub> is the "half-velocity constant"—the value of \[*S*\] when *μ*/*μ*<sub>max</sub> = 0.5.

*μ*<sub>max</sub> and *K*<sub>*s*</sub> are empirical (experimental) coefficients to the Monod equation. They will differ between microorganism species and will also depend on the ambient environmental conditions, e.g., on the temperature, on the pH of the solution, and on the composition of the culture medium.[^o7]

## Application notes {#Application_notes}

The rate of substrate utilization is related to the specific growth rate as[^o9]

$$r_s = \mu X/Y,$$

where

*X* is the total biomass (since the specific growth rate *μ* is normalized to the total biomass),

*Y* is the yield coefficient.

*r*<sub>*s*</sub> is negative by convention.

In some applications, several terms of the form \[*S*\] / (*K*<sub>*s*</sub> + \[*S*\]) are multiplied together where more than one nutrient or growth factor has the potential to be limiting (e.g. [organic matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_matter) and [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) are both necessary to [heterotrophic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterotrophic) bacteria). When the yield coefficient, being the ratio of mass of microorganisms to mass of substrate utilized, becomes very large, this signifies that there is deficiency of substrate available for utilization.

As with the [Michaelis–Menten equation](wikipedia://en/253272) graphical methods may be used to fit the coefficients of the Monod equation:[^o6]
