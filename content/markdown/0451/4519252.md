# Phase portrait

*Image: Pendulum phase portrait.svg.* [Potential energy](wikipedia://en/23703) and phase portrait of a [simple pendulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_pendulum). Note that the x-axis, being angular, wraps onto itself after every 2π radians.

*Image: Phase portrait of damped oscillator, with increasing damping strength.gif.* Phase portrait of damped oscillator, with increasing damping strength. The equation of motion is $\ddot x + 2\gamma \dot x + \omega^2 x = 0.$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Phase portrait"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **phase portrait** is a geometric representation of the [orbits](wikipedia://en/252250) of a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) in the [phase plane](wikipedia://en/2110756). Each set of initial conditions is represented by a different [point](wikipedia://en/593693) or [curve](wikipedia://en/89246).

:::

Phase portraits are an invaluable tool in studying dynamical systems. They consist of a [plot](wikipedia://en/19774918) of typical trajectories in the [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101). This reveals information such as whether an [attractor](wikipedia://en/191094), a [repellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repellor) or [limit cycle](wikipedia://en/452950) is present for the chosen parameter value. The concept of [topological equivalence](wikipedia://en/5506149) is important in classifying the behaviour of systems by specifying when two different phase portraits represent the same qualitative dynamic behavior. An attractor is a stable point which is also called a "sink". The repeller is considered as an unstable point, which is also known as a "source".

A phase portrait graph of a dynamical system depicts the system's trajectories (with arrows) and stable [steady states](wikipedia://en/1326107) (with dots) and unstable steady states (with circles) in a phase space. The axes are of [state variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_variable).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Visualizing the behavior of ordinary differential equations {#Visualizing_the_behavior_of_ordinary_differential_equations}

A phase portrait represents the directional behavior of a system of [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753) (ODEs). The phase portrait can indicate the stability of the system.[^o5]

*Stability[^o5]*

::: {#o6 .table cue=table name="Stability"}

| Unstable | Most of the system's solutions tend towards ∞ over time |
|---|---|
| Asymptotically stable | All of the system's solutions tend to 0 over time |
| Neutrally stable | None of the system's solutions tend towards ∞ over time, but most solutions do not tend towards 0 either |

:::

The phase portrait behavior of a system of ODEs can be determined by the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) or the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) and [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) (trace = λ<sub>1</sub> + λ<sub>2</sub>, determinant = λ<sub>1</sub>λ<sub>2</sub>) of the system.[^o5]

*Phase portrait behavior[^o5]*

::: {#o7 .table cue=table name="Phase portrait behavior"}

| Eigenvalue, trace, determinant | Phase portrait shape |
|---|---|
| λ<sub>1</sub> and λ<sub>2</sub> are real and of opposite sign; Determinant \< 0 | Saddle (unstable) |
| λ<sub>1</sub> and λ<sub>2</sub> are real and of the same sign, and λ<sub>1</sub> ≠ λ<sub>2</sub>; 0 \< determinant \< (trace<sup>2</sup> / 4) | Node (stable if trace \< 0, unstable if trace > 0) |
| λ<sub>1</sub> and λ<sub>2</sub> have both a real and imaginary component; (trace<sup>2</sup> / 4) \< determinant | Spiral (stable if trace \< 0, unstable if trace > 0) |

:::
