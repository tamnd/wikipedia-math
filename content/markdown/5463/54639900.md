# Grassmann graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Grassmann graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), **Grassmann graphs** are a special class of [simple graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph) defined from systems of [subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357). The [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) of the Grassmann graph $J_{q}(n, k)$ are the $k$-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) subspaces of an $n$-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) of order $q$; two vertices are adjacent when their [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) is $(k - 1)$-dimensional.

:::

Many of the parameters of Grassmann graphs are [$q$-analogs](wikipedia://en/1619396) of the parameters of [Johnson graphs](wikipedia://en/2948666), and Grassmann graphs have several of the same [graph properties](wikipedia://en/1655142) as Johnson graphs.

## Graph-theoretic properties {#Graph-theoretic_properties}

- $J_{q}(n, k)$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) to $J_{q}(n, n - k)$.
- For all $0 \le d \le \operatorname{diam}(J_{q}(n,k))$, the intersection of any pair of vertices at distance $d$ is $(k - d)$-dimensional.
- The [clique number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clique_number) of $J_{q}(n,k)$ is given by an expression in terms its least and greatest [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) $\lambda_{\,\min}$ and $\lambda_{\,\max}$:

$$\omega \left( J_q(n,k) \right) = 1 - \frac{\lambda_{\max}}{\lambda_{\min}}$$

## Automorphism group {#Automorphism_group}

There is a [distance-transitive](wikipedia://en/7768222) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of $\operatorname{Aut}(J_q(n, k))$ [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to the [projective linear group](wikipedia://en/382667) $\operatorname{P\Gamma L}(n, q)$.

In fact, unless $n = 2k$ or $k \in \{ 1, n - 1 \}$, $\operatorname{Aut}(J_q(n,k)) \cong \operatorname{P\Gamma L}(n, q)$; otherwise $\operatorname{Aut}(J_q(n,k)) \cong \operatorname{P\Gamma L}(n, q) \times C_2$ or $\operatorname{Aut}(J_q(n,k)) \cong \operatorname{Sym}([n]_q)$ respectively.[^o5]

## Intersection array {#Intersection_array}

As a consequence of being distance-transitive, $J_q(n,k)$ is also [distance-regular](wikipedia://en/7768943). Letting $d$ denote its [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341), the intersection array of $J_q(n,k)$ is given by $\left\{ b_0, \ldots, b_{d-1}; c_1, \ldots c_d \right \}$ where:

- $b_j := q^{2j + 1} [k - j]_q [n - k - j]_q$ for all $0 \leq j < d$.
- $c_j := ([j]_q)^2$ for all $0 < j \leq d$.

## Spectrum {#Spectrum}

- The characteristic polynomial of $J_q(n,k)$ is given by

$\varphi(x) := \prod\limits_{j=0}^{\operatorname{diam}(J_q(n, k))} \left ( x - \left ( q^{j+1} [k - j]_q [n - k - j]_q - [j]_q \right ) \right )^{\left ( \binom{n}{j}_q - \binom{n}{j-1}_q \right )}$.[^o5]
