# Primorial prime

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Primorial prime"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **primorial prime** is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) of the form *p<sub>n</sub>*# ± 1, where *p<sub>n</sub>*# is the [primorial](wikipedia://en/422786) of *p<sub>n</sub>* (i.e. the product of the first *n* primes).[^o3]

:::

[Primality tests](wikipedia://en/183751) show that:

*p<sub>n</sub>*# − 1 is prime for *n* = 2, 3, 5, 6, 13, 24, 66, 68, 167, 287, 310, 352, 564, 590, 620, 849, 1552, 1849, 67132, 85586, 234725, 334023, 435582, 446895, ... . (*p<sub>n</sub>* = 3, 5, 11, 13, 41, 89, 317, 337, 991, 1873, 2053, 2377, 4093, 4297, 4583, 6569, 13033, 15877, 843301, 1098133, 3267113, 4778027, 6354977, 6533299, ... )

*p<sub>n</sub>*# + 1 is prime for *n* = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 11, 75, 171, 172, 384, 457, 616, 643, 1391, 1613, 2122, 2647, 2673, 4413, 13494, 31260, 33237, 304723, 365071, 436504, 498865, ... . (*p<sub>n</sub>* = 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 31, 379, 1019, 1021, 2657, 3229, 4547, 4787, 11549, 13649, 18523, 23801, 24029, 42209, 145823, 366439, 392113, 4328927, 5256037, 6369619, 7351117, 9562633, ..., )

The first term of the third sequence is 0 because *p*<sub>0</sub># = 1 (we also let *p*<sub>0</sub> = 1, see [Primality of one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primality_of_one), hence the first term of the fourth sequence is 1) is the [empty product](wikipedia://en/181289), and thus *p*<sub>0</sub># + 1 = 2, which is prime. Similarly, the first term of the first sequence is not 1 (hence the first term of the second sequence is also not 2), because *p*<sub>1</sub># = 2, and 2 − 1 = 1 is not prime.

The first few primorial primes are [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_%28number%29), [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_%28number%29), [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_%28number%29), [7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_%28number%29), [29](wikipedia://en/362212), [31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31_%28number%29), [211](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/211_%28number%29), [2309](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2309_%28number%29), [2311](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2311_%28number%29), 30029, 200560490131, 304250263527209, 23768741896345550770650537601358309 .

, it is not known whether there are infinitely many primorial primes, and it is also not known whether infinitely many numbers of the form *p<sub>n</sub>*# ± 1 are [composite numbers](wikipedia://en/82289).

, the largest known prime of the form *p*<sub>*n*</sub># − 1 is 6533299# − 1 (*n* = 446,895) with 2,835,864 digits, found by the [PrimeGrid](wikipedia://en/3255603) project.[^o5]

, the largest known prime of the form *p*<sub>*n*</sub># + 1 is 9562633# + 1 (*n* = 637,491) with 4,151,498 digits, also found by the PrimeGrid project.[^o5]

[Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) of the [infinitude of the prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitude_of_the_prime_numbers) is commonly misinterpreted as defining the primorial primes, in the following manner:[^o6]

Assume that the first *n* consecutive primes including 2 are the only primes that exist. If either *p<sub>n</sub>*# + 1 or *p<sub>n</sub>*# − 1 is a primorial prime, it means that there are larger primes than the *n*th prime (if neither is a prime, that also proves the infinitude of primes, but less directly; each of these two numbers has a remainder of either *p* − 1 or 1 when divided by any of the first *n* primes, and hence all its prime factors are larger than *p*<sub>*n*</sub>).
