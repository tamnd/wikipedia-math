# Neil Sloane

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Neil Sloane"}

**Neil James Alexander Sloane** [FLSW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_Society_of_Wales) (born October 10, 1939) is a British-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o4] His major contributions are in the fields of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [error-correcting codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error-correcting_code), and [sphere packing](wikipedia://en/368621). Sloane is best known for being the creator and maintainer of the [On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences](wikipedia://en/500004) (OEIS).[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Sloane was born in [Beaumaris, Anglesey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaumaris,_Anglesey), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales), in 1939, moving to [Cowes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowes), [Isle of Wight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Wight), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) in 1946. The family emigrated to [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia), arriving at the start of 1949. Sloane then moved from [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne) to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in 1961.[^o7]

He studied at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) under Nick DeClaris, [Frank Rosenblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Rosenblatt), [Frederick Jelinek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Jelinek) and [Wolfgang Heinrich Johannes Fuchs](wikipedia://en/5397705), receiving his Ph.D. in 1967. His doctoral dissertation was titled *Lengths of Cycle Times in [Random Neural Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_neural_network)*. Sloane joined [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) in 1968 and retired from its successor [AT&T Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T_Labs) in 2012. He became an AT&T [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) in 1998. He is also a [fellow of the Learned Society of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Learned_Society_of_Wales),[^o9] an [IEEE fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_fellow), a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822),[^o10] and a member of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering).

He is a winner of a [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) in 1978[^o11] and the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) in 1979.[^o11] In 1998 he was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin).[^o12] In 2005 Sloane received the [IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Richard_W._Hamming_Medal).[^o13] In 2008 he received the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) [David P. Robbins Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_P._Robbins_Prize), and in 2013 the [George Pólya Award](wikipedia://en/36409098).

In 2014, to celebrate his 75th birthday, Sloane shared some of his favorite integer sequences.[^o14] Besides mathematics, he loves [rock climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_climbing) and has authored two rock-climbing guides to [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey).[^o15]

He regularly appears in videos for [Brady Haran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brady_Haran)'s YouTube channel [Numberphile](wikipedia://en/61716514).[^o16]
