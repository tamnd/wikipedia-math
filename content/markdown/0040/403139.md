# Point at infinity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Point at infinity"}

*Image: Real projective line.svg.* The real line with the point at infinity; it is called the [real projective line](wikipedia://en/46379102).

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **point at infinity** or **ideal point** is an idealized limiting point at the "end" of each line.

:::

In the case of an [affine plane](wikipedia://en/30764139) (including the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410)), there is one ideal point for each [pencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil_%28mathematics%29) of parallel lines of the plane. Adjoining these points produces a [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350), in which no point can be distinguished, if we "forget" which points were added. This holds for a geometry over any [field](wikipedia://en/10603), and more generally over any [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067).

In the real case, a point at infinity completes a line into a topologically closed curve. In higher dimensions, all the points at infinity form a projective subspace of one dimension less than that of the whole projective space to which they belong. A point at infinity can also be added to the [complex line](wikipedia://en/7975294) (which may be thought of as the complex plane), thereby turning it into a closed surface known as the complex projective line, **C**P<sup>1</sup>, also called the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799) (when complex numbers are mapped to each point).

In the case of a [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742), each line has two distinct [ideal points](wikipedia://en/4995922). Here, the set of ideal points takes the form of a [quadric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadric_%28projective_geometry%29).

## Affine geometry {#Affine_geometry}

In an [affine](wikipedia://en/298834) or [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) of higher dimension, the **points at infinity** are the points which are added to the space to get the [projective completion](wikipedia://en/242135). The set of the points at infinity is called, depending on the dimension of the space, the [line at infinity](wikipedia://en/403142), the [plane at infinity](wikipedia://en/403143) or the [hyperplane at infinity](wikipedia://en/713946), in all cases a projective space of one less dimension.

As a projective space over a field is a [smooth algebraic variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_algebraic_variety), the same is true for the set of points at infinity. Similarly, if the ground field is the real or the complex field, the set of points at infinity is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470).

### Perspective {#Perspective}

In artistic drawing and technical perspective, the projection on the picture plane of the point at infinity of a class of parallel lines is called their [vanishing point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanishing_point).

## Hyperbolic geometry {#Hyperbolic_geometry}

In [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), **points at infinity** are typically named [ideal points](wikipedia://en/4995922). Unlike [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9417) and [elliptic](wikipedia://en/236020) geometries, each line has two points at infinity: given a line *l* and a point *P* not on *l*, the right- and left-[limiting parallels](wikipedia://en/31547181) [converge](wikipedia://en/1743431) [asymptotically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotically) to different points at infinity.

All points at infinity together form the [Cayley absolute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley_absolute) or boundary of a [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane).

## Projective geometry {#Projective_geometry}

A symmetry of points and lines arises in a projective plane: just as a pair of points determine a line, so a pair of lines determine a point. The existence of parallel lines leads to establishing a point at infinity which represents the intersection of these parallels. This axiomatic symmetry grew out of a study of [graphical perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphical_perspective) where a [parallel projection](wikipedia://en/2503009) arises as a [central projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_projection) where the center *C* is a point at infinity, or **figurative point**. The axiomatic symmetry of points and lines is called [duality](wikipedia://en/539925).

Though a point at infinity is considered on a par with any other point of a [projective range](wikipedia://en/34644484), in the representation of points with [projective coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_coordinates), distinction is noted: finite points are represented with a 1 in the final coordinate while a point at infinity has a 0 there. To represent points at infinity requires one extra coordinate beyond what is needed for the space of finite points.

## Other generalizations {#Other_generalizations}

This construction can be generalized to [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450). Different compactifications may exist for a given space, but arbitrary topological space admits [Alexandroff extension](wikipedia://en/55611), also called the *one-point [compactification](wikipedia://en/6617)* when the original space is not itself [compact](wikipedia://en/6042). Projective line (over arbitrary field) is the Alexandroff extension of the corresponding field. Thus, the circle is the one-point compactification of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line), and the sphere is the one-point compactification of the plane. [Projective spaces](wikipedia://en/242135) **P**<sup>$n$</sup> for $n$ > 1 are not *one-point* compactifications of corresponding affine spaces for the reason mentioned above under , and completions of hyperbolic spaces with ideal points are also not one-point compactifications.
