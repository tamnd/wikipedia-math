# Abram Besicovitch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abram Besicovitch"}

**Abram Samoilovitch Besicovitch** (or **Besikovitch**;[^o4] Абра́м Само́йлович Безико́вич; 23 January 1891 – 2 November 1970) was a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was professor at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) and worked mainly in [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Abram Besicovitch was born in [Berdyansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berdyansk) on the [Sea of Azov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Azov) (now in [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)) to a [Karaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaite_Judaism) Jewish family.[^o6][^o7][^o8][^o11] He studied under the supervision of [Andrey Markov](wikipedia://en/915) at the [Saint Petersburg Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University), graduating with a PhD in 1912,[^o3] and then began research in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). He converted to [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy), joining the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church), on marrying in 1916. Besicovitch was appointed professor at the [University of Perm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_State_University) in 1917, and was caught up in the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War) over the next two years. In 1920, he took a position at his alma mater, now renamed the [Petrograd State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University).

In 1924, he went to [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen) on a [Rockefeller Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Fellowship), where he worked on [almost periodic functions](wikipedia://en/405512) under [Harald Bohr](wikipedia://en/638305). A type of function space in that field now bears his name. After a visit to [G.H. Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.H._Hardy) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), he had appointments at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool) in 1926, and the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1927.

Besicovitch moved to Cambridge University in 1927, where he was made a Fellow of [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). He was appointed Lecturer in the Faculty of Mathematics, and therefore received recognition as a Cambridge MA by 'Special Grace' on 24 November 1928. In 1950, he became [J.E. Littlewood's](wikipedia://en/158371) successor as the [Rouse Ball Chair of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouse_Ball_Chair_of_Mathematics). He retired in 1958 and toured the US for eight years. After returning to Trinity, he died in 1970 in Cambridge.

## Work {#Work}

Besicovitch worked mainly on [combinatorial methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_methods) and questions in [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), such as the [Kakeya needle problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakeya_needle_problem) and the [Hausdorff–Besicovitch dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausdorff–Besicovitch_dimension), two areas that have grown in importance in recent years. The [Kovner–Besicovitch measure](wikipedia://en/65472779) of the central symmetry of planar convex sets is also named after him.

He was also a major influence on the economist [Piero Sraffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_Sraffa), after 1940, when they were both Fellows of Trinity, and on [Dennis Lindley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Lindley), one of the founders of the [Bayesian](wikipedia://en/49571) movement in the United Kingdom.

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Besicovitch was in 1934 made [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o4] and in 1952 won the [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal) from the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society).[^o14] He received in 1950 the *[De Morgan Medal](wikipedia://en/1096532)* of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097). He was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in the fall of 1954.[^o15]
