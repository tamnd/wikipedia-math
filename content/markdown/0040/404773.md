# Trefoil knot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trefoil knot"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **trefoil knot** is the simplest example of a nontrivial [knot](wikipedia://en/366808). The trefoil can be obtained by joining the two loose ends of a common [overhand knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhand_knot), resulting in a knotted [loop](wikipedia://en/1189560). As the simplest knot, the trefoil is fundamental to the study of mathematical knot theory.

:::

The trefoil knot is named after the three-leaf [clover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clover) (or trefoil) plant.

## Descriptions {#Descriptions}

The trefoil knot can be defined as the [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) obtained from the following [parametric equations](wikipedia://en/576108):

$$\begin{aligned} x &= \sin t + 2 \sin 2t \\ y &= \cos t - 2 \cos 2t \\ z &= -\sin 3t \end{aligned}$$

The (2,3)-[torus knot](wikipedia://en/1165182) is also a trefoil knot. The following parametric equations give a (2,3)-torus knot lying on [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) $(r-2)^2+z^2 = 1$:

$$\begin{aligned} x &= (2+\cos 3t) \cos 2t \\ y &= (2+\cos 3t )\sin 2t \\ z &= \sin 3t \end{aligned}$$

Any continuous deformation of the curve above is also considered a trefoil knot. Specifically, any curve [isotopic](wikipedia://en/212250#Isotopy) to a trefoil knot is also considered to be a trefoil. In addition, the [mirror image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image) of a trefoil knot is also considered to be a trefoil. In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and knot theory, the trefoil is usually defined using a [knot diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_diagram) instead of an explicit parametric equation.

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the trefoil can also be obtained as the intersection in **C**<sup>2</sup> of the unit [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) *S*<sup>3</sup> with the [complex plane curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_plane_curve) of zeroes of the complex [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) *z*<sup>2</sup> + *w*<sup>3</sup> (a [cuspidal cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuspidal_cubic)).

If one end of a tape or belt is turned over three times and then pasted to the other, the edge forms a trefoil knot.[^o4]

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

The trefoil knot is [chiral](wikipedia://en/1170160), in the sense that a trefoil knot can be distinguished from its own mirror image. The two resulting variants are known as the **left-handed trefoil** and the **right-handed trefoil**. It is not possible to deform a left-handed trefoil continuously into a right-handed trefoil, or vice versa. (That is, the two trefoils are not [ambient isotopic](wikipedia://en/7404467).)

Though chiral, the trefoil knot is also invertible, meaning that there is no distinction between a [counterclockwise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterclockwise)-oriented and a clockwise-oriented trefoil. That is, the chirality of a trefoil depends only on the over and under crossings, not the orientation of the curve.

But the knot has rotational symmetry. The axis is about a line perpendicular to the page for the 3-coloured image.

## Nontriviality {#Nontriviality}

The trefoil knot is nontrivial, meaning that it is not possible to "untie" a trefoil knot in three dimensions without cutting it. Mathematically, this means that a trefoil knot is not isotopic to the [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075). In particular, there is no sequence of [Reidemeister moves](wikipedia://en/1152374) that will untie a trefoil.

Proving this requires the construction of a [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) that distinguishes the trefoil from the unknot. The simplest such invariant is [tricolorability](wikipedia://en/7280493): the trefoil is tricolorable, but the unknot is not. In addition, virtually every major [knot polynomial](wikipedia://en/313735) distinguishes the trefoil from an unknot, as do most other strong knot invariants.

## Classification {#Classification}

In knot theory, the trefoil is the first nontrivial knot, and is the only knot with [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298598) three. It is a [prime knot](wikipedia://en/315030), and is listed as 3<sub>1</sub> in the [Alexander-Briggs notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander-Briggs_notation). The [Dowker notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowker_notation) for the trefoil is 4 6 2, and the [Conway notation](wikipedia://en/17066309) is \[3\].

The trefoil can be described as the (2,3)-[torus knot](wikipedia://en/1165182). It is also the knot obtained by closing the [braid](wikipedia://en/550138) σ<sub>1</sub><sup>3</sup>.

The trefoil is an [alternating knot](wikipedia://en/1236143). However, it is not a [slice knot](wikipedia://en/4158126), meaning it does not bound a smooth 2-dimensional disk in the 4-dimensional ball; one way to prove this is to note that its [signature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_of_a_knot) is not zero. Another proof is that its Alexander polynomial does not satisfy the [Fox-Milnor condition](wikipedia://en/4158126).

The trefoil is a [fibered knot](wikipedia://en/1358592), meaning that its [complement](wikipedia://en/1350120) in $S^3$ is a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) over the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) $S^1$. The trefoil **K** may be viewed as the set of pairs $(z,w)$ of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) such that $|z|^2+|w|^2=1$ and $z^2+w^3=0$. Then this [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) has the [Milnor map](wikipedia://en/2696842) $\phi(z, w) = (z^2+w^3) / |z^2+w^3|$ as the fibre bundle projection of the knot complement $S^3 \setminus \mathbf{K}$ to the circle $S^1$. The fibre is a [once-punctured](wikipedia://en/71038216) [torus](wikipedia://en/74800). Since the knot complement is also a [Seifert fibred](wikipedia://en/1631931) with boundary, it has a horizontal incompressible surface—this is also the fiber of the [Milnor map](wikipedia://en/2696842). (This assumes the knot has been thickened to become a solid torus N<sub>ε</sub>(**K**), and that the interior of this solid torus has been removed to create a compact knot complement $S^3 \setminus \operatorname{int}(\mathrm{N}_\varepsilon(\mathbf{K})$.)

## Invariants {#Invariants}

The [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) of the trefoil knot is

$$\Delta(t) = t - 1 + t^{-1},$$

since $\begin{pmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ is a possible [Seifert matrix](wikipedia://en/1343951) (for the left-hand one), or because of its [Conway polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647#Alexander–Conway_polynomial),[^o10] which is

$$\nabla(z) = z^2 + 1.$$

The [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642) is

$$V(q) = q^{-1} + q^{-3} - q^{-4},$$

and the [Kauffman polynomial](wikipedia://en/9565831) of the trefoil is

$$L(a,z) = za^5 + z^2a^4 - a^4 + za^3 + z^2a^2-2a^2.$$

The [HOMFLY polynomial](wikipedia://en/1039260) of the trefoil is

$$L(\alpha,z) = -\alpha^4 + \alpha^2z^2 + 2\alpha^2.$$

The [knot group](wikipedia://en/1164126) of the trefoil is given by the presentation

$$\langle x,y\mid x^2=y^3\rangle,$$

or equivalently[^o11]

$$\langle x,y \mid xyx=yxy \rangle.$$

This group is isomorphic to the [braid group](wikipedia://en/550138) with three strands.

## In religion and culture {#In_religion_and_culture}

As the simplest nontrivial knot, the trefoil is a common [motif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motif_%28visual_arts%29) in [iconography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iconography) and the [visual arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts). For example, the common form of the [triquetra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triquetra) symbol is a trefoil, as are some versions of the Germanic [Valknut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valknut).

In modern art, the woodcut *Knots* by [M. C. Escher](wikipedia://en/20127) (1965) depicts three trefoil knots whose solid forms are twisted in different ways.[^o13]
