# Mahler's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mahler's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Mahler's theorem**, introduced by (txt), expresses any continuous [*p*-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) function as an [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) of certain special [polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomials). This series is referred to as a *Mahler series*[^o3] or *Mahler expansion*,[^o4] or as *Mahler's expansion*.[^o5] It is the *p*-adic counterpart to the [Stone–Weierstrass theorem](wikipedia://en/28858) for continuous real-valued functions on a closed interval.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $(\Delta f)(x)=f(x+1)-f(x)$ be the forward [difference operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_operator). Then for any *p*-adic function $f: \mathbb{Z}_p \to \mathbb{Q}_p$, Mahler's theorem states that $f$ is continuous [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its [Newton series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_series) converges everywhere to $f$, so that for all $x \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ we have

$$f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty (\Delta^n f)(0){x \choose n},$$

where

$${x \choose n}=\frac{x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n+1)}{n!}$$

is the $n$th [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668) polynomial. Here, the $n$th forward difference is computed by the [binomial transform](wikipedia://en/3877474), so that

$$(\Delta^n f)(0) = \sum^n_{k=0} (-1)^{n-k} \binom{n}{k} f(k).$$

Moreover, we have that

$$f(x):=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n\binom{x}{n}$$

is continuous if and only if the coefficients $a_n=(\Delta^n f)(0) \to 0$ in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ as $n \to \infty$.

It is remarkable that as weak an assumption as continuity is enough in the *p*-adic setting to establish convergence of Newton series. By contrast, Newton series on the field of [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) are far more tightly constrained, and require [Carlson's theorem](wikipedia://en/4065365) to hold.

:::
