# Legendre form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Legendre form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Legendre forms of [elliptic integrals](wikipedia://en/9960)** are a canonical set of three elliptic integrals to which all others may be reduced. [Legendre](wikipedia://en/49695) chose the name *elliptic integrals* because[^o2] the second kind gives the [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) of unit semi-major axis and [eccentricity](wikipedia://en/1239472) $k$ (the ellipse being defined parametrically by $x = \sqrt{1 - k^{2}} \cos(t)$, $y = \sin(t)$).

:::

In modern times the Legendre forms have largely been supplanted by an alternative canonical set, the [Carlson symmetric forms](wikipedia://en/406919). A more detailed treatment of the Legendre forms is given in the main article on [elliptic integrals](wikipedia://en/9960).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The **incomplete elliptic integral of the first kind** is defined as

$$F(\phi,k) = \int_0^\phi \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - k^2 \sin^2(t)}} dt,$$

the **second kind** as

$$E(\phi,k) = \int_0^\phi \sqrt{1 - k^2 \sin^2(t)}\,dt,$$

and the **third kind** as

$$\Pi(\phi,n,k) = \int_0^\phi \frac{1}{\big(1 - n \sin^2(t)\big)\sqrt{1 - k^2 \sin^2(t)}}\,dt.$$

The argument *n* of the third kind of integral is known as the **characteristic**, which in different notational conventions can appear as either the first, second or third argument of *Π* and furthermore is sometimes defined with the opposite sign. The argument order shown above is that of [Gradshteyn and Ryzhik](wikipedia://en/49343041)[^o5] as well as [Numerical Recipes](wikipedia://en/632539).[^o6] The choice of sign is that of [Abramowitz and Stegun](wikipedia://en/1206236)[^o7] as well as [Gradshteyn and Ryzhik](wikipedia://en/49343041),[^o5] but corresponds to the $\Pi(\phi, -n, k)$ of [Numerical Recipes](wikipedia://en/632539).[^o6]

The respective **complete elliptic integrals** are obtained by setting the **amplitude**, $\phi$, the upper limit of the integrals, to $\pi/2$.

The Legendre form of an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) is given by

$$y^2 = x(x - 1)(x - \lambda).$$

:::

## Numerical evaluation {#Numerical_evaluation}

The classic method of evaluation is by means of **[Landen's transformations](wikipedia://en/21424424)**. Descending Landen transformation decreases the **modulus** $k$ towards zero, while increasing the amplitude $\phi$. Conversely, ascending transformation increases the modulus towards unity, while decreasing the amplitude. In either limit of $k$ approaching zero or one, the integral is readily evaluated.

Most modern authors recommend evaluation in terms of the [Carlson symmetric forms](wikipedia://en/406919), for which there exist efficient, robust and relatively simple algorithms. This approach has been adopted by [Boost C++ Libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boost_C++_Libraries), [GNU Scientific Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_Scientific_Library) and [Numerical Recipes](wikipedia://en/632539).[^o6]
