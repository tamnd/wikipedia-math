# Higman–Sims group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Higman–Sims group"}

In the area of modern algebra known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Higman–Sims group** HS is a [sporadic simple group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_simple_group) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527)

:::

44,352,000 = 2<sup>9</sup>3<sup>2</sup>5<sup>3</sup>711

≈ 4.

The [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587) has order 2, the [outer automorphism group](wikipedia://en/628466) has order 2, and the group 2.HS.2 appears as an involution centralizer in the [Harada–Norton group](wikipedia://en/1196772).

## History {#History}

HS is one of the 26 sporadic groups and was found by (txt). They were attending a presentation by [Marshall Hall](wikipedia://en/7758584) on the [Hall–Janko group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall–Janko_group) J<sub>2</sub>. It happens that J<sub>2</sub> acts as a permutation group on the [Hall–Janko graph](wikipedia://en/1110667) of 100 points, the [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_subgroup) of one point being a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) with two other [orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) of lengths 36 and 63. Inspired by this they decided to check for other rank 3 permutation groups on 100 points. They soon focused on a possible one containing the [Mathieu group](wikipedia://en/380207) M<sub>22</sub>, which has [permutation representations](wikipedia://en/59487764) on 22 and 77 points. (The latter representation arises because the M<sub>22</sub> [Steiner system](wikipedia://en/28016) has 77 blocks.) By putting together these two representations, they found HS, with a one-point stabilizer isomorphic to M<sub>22</sub>.

HS is the simple subgroup of [index](wikipedia://en/230581) two in the group of automorphisms of the [Higman–Sims graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higman–Sims_graph). The Higman–Sims graph has 100 nodes, so the Higman–Sims group HS is a transitive [group of permutations](wikipedia://en/24634) of a 100 element set. The smallest faithful complex representation of HS has dimension 22.[^o4]

(txt) independently discovered the group as a [doubly transitive permutation group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubly_transitive_permutation_group) acting on a certain "geometry" on 176 points.

## Relationship to Conway groups {#Relationship_to_Conway_groups}

(Conway 1968) identified the Higman–Sims group as a subgroup of the [Conway group](wikipedia://en/345511) Co<sub>0</sub>. In Co<sub>0</sub> HS arises as a pointwise stabilizer of a [2-3-3 triangle](wikipedia://en/345511#Sublattice_groups), one whose edges (differences of vertices) are type 2 and 3 vectors. HS thus is a subgroup of each of the Conway groups Co<sub>0</sub>, Co<sub>2</sub> and Co<sub>3</sub>.

(Wilson 2009) (p. 208) shows that the group HS is well-defined. In the [Leech lattice](wikipedia://en/344919), suppose a [type 3](wikipedia://en/345511) point **v** is fixed by an instance of Co<sub>3</sub>. Count the type 2 points **w** such that the inner product **v**·**w** = 2 (and thus **v**-**w** is type 3). He shows that their number is 11,178 = 2⋅3<sup>5</sup>⋅23 and that this Co<sub>3</sub> is transitive on these **w**.

$$\lvert \operatorname{HS}\rvert = \lvert \operatorname{Co}_{3}\rvert / 11,178 = 44,352,000.$$

In fact, $\lvert \operatorname{HS}\rvert = 100 \lvert M_{22}\rvert$, and there are instances of HS including a permutation matrix representation of the Mathieu group M<sub>22</sub>.

If an instance of HS in Co<sub>0</sub> fixes a particular point of type 3, this point is found in 276 triangles of type 2-2-3 that this copy of HS permutes in orbits of 176 and 100. This fact leads to Graham Higman's construction as well as to the Higman–Sims graph. HS is [doubly transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubly_transitive) on the 176 and [rank 3](wikipedia://en/29763000) on the 100.

A 2-3-3 triangle defines a 2-dimensional subspace fixed pointwise by HS. The standard representation of HS can thus be reduced to a 22-dimensional one.

### A Higman-Sims graph {#A_Higman-Sims_graph}

(Wilson 2009) (p. 210) gives an example of a Higman-Sims graph within the [Leech lattice](wikipedia://en/344919), permuted by the representation of M<sub>22</sub> on the last 22 coordinates:

- 22 points of shape (1, 1, −3, 1<sup>21</sup>)
- 77 points of shape (2, 2, 2<sup>6</sup>, 0<sup>16</sup>)
- A 100th point (4, 4, 0<sup>22</sup>)

Differences of adjacent points are of type 3; those of non-adjacent ones are of type 2.

Here, HS fixes a 2-3-3 triangle with vertices **x** = (5, 1<sup>23</sup>), **y** = (1, 5, 1<sup>22</sup>), and **z** the origin. **x** and **y** are of type 3 while **x**-**y** = (4, −4, 0<sup>22</sup>) is of type 2. Any vertex of the graph differs from **x**, **y**, and **z** by vectors of type 2.

### Two classes of involutions {#Two_classes_of_involutions}

An involution in the subgroup M<sub>22</sub> transposes 8 pairs of co-ordinates. As a permutation matrix in Co<sub>0</sub> it has trace 8. It can shown that it moves 80 of the 100 vertices of the Higman-Sims graph. No transposed pair of vertices is an [edge](wikipedia://en/12401) in the graph.

There is another class of involutions, of trace 0, that move all 100 vertices.[^o15] As permutations in the alternating group A<sub>100</sub>, being products of an odd number (25) of double transpositions, these involutions lift to elements of order 4 in the [double cover](wikipedia://en/25118486) 2.A<sub>100</sub>. HS thus has a double cover 2.HS, which is not related to the double cover of the subgroup M<sub>22</sub>.

## Generalized Monstrous Moonshine {#Generalized_Monstrous_Moonshine}

Conway and Norton suggested in their 1979 paper that [monstrous moonshine](wikipedia://en/728168) is not limited to the [monster group](wikipedia://en/47422), but that similar phenomena may be found for other groups. Larissa Queen and others subsequently found that one can construct the expansions of many Hauptmoduln from simple combinations of dimensions of sporadic groups. For HS, the McKay-Thompson series is $T_{10A}(\tau)$ where one can set a(0) = 4 (),

$$\begin{aligned} j_{10A}(\tau) &= T_{10A}(\tau) + 4 \\ &= \left( \left( \frac{ \eta(\tau) \; \eta(5\tau) }{ \eta(2\tau) \; \eta(10\tau) } \right)^{2} + 2^2 \left( \frac{ \eta(2\tau) \; \eta(10\tau) }{ \eta(\tau) \; \eta(5\tau) } \right)^{2} \right)^2 \\ &= \left( \left( \frac{ \eta(\tau) \; \eta(2\tau) }{ \eta(5\tau) \; \eta(10\tau) } \right) + 5 \left( \frac{ \eta(5\tau) \; \eta(10\tau) }{ \eta(\tau) \; \eta(2\tau) } \right) \right)^2 - 4 \\ &= \frac{1}{q} + 4 + 22q + 56q^2 + 177q^3 + 352q^4 + 870q^5 + 1584q^6 + \cdots \end{aligned}$$
