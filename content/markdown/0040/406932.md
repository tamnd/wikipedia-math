# Polygamma function

*Image: Mplwp polygamma03.svg.* Graphs of the polygamma functions $\psi$, $\psi^{(1)}$, $\psi^{(2)}$ and $\psi^{(3)}$ of real arguments

*Image: Plot of polygamma function in the complex plane from -2-2i to 2+2i with colors created with Mathematica 13.1.svg.* Plot of the [digamma function](wikipedia://en/406935), the first polygamma function, in the complex plane from −2−2i to 2+2i with colors created by Mathematica's function ComplexPlot3D showing one cycle of phase shift around each pole and the zero

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polygamma function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **polygamma function of order $m$** is a [meromorphic function](wikipedia://en/80857) on the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) $\mathbb{C}$ defined as the $(m + 1)$th [derivative of the logarithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_of_the_logarithm) of the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316):

:::

$$\psi^{(m)}(z) := \frac{\mathrm{d}^m}{\mathrm{d}z^m} \psi(z) = \frac{\mathrm{d}^{m+1}}{\mathrm{d}z^{m+1}} \ln\Gamma(z).$$

Thus

$$\psi^{(0)}(z) = \psi(z) = \frac{\Gamma'(z)}{\Gamma(z)}$$

holds where $\psi(z)$ is the [digamma function](wikipedia://en/406935) and $\Gamma(z)$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316). They are [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) on $\mathbb{C} \backslash\mathbb{Z}_{\le0}$. At all the nonpositive integers these polygamma functions have a [pole](wikipedia://en/334325) of order $m + 1$. The function $\psi^{(1)}(z)$ is sometimes called the [trigamma function](wikipedia://en/2036750).

## Integral representation {#Integral_representation}

When $m > 0$ and $\operatorname{Re} z > 0$, the polygamma function equals

$$\begin{aligned} \psi^{(m)}(z) &= (-1)^{m+1}\int_0^\infty \frac{t^m e^{-zt}}{1-e^{-t}}\,\mathrm{d}t \\ &= -\int_0^1 \frac{t^{z-1}}{1-t}(\ln t)^m\,\mathrm{d}t\\ &= (-1)^{m+1}m!\zeta(m+1,z) \end{aligned}$$

where $\zeta(s,q)$ is the [Hurwitz zeta function](wikipedia://en/406952).

This expresses the polygamma function as the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) of $\tfrac{(-1)^{m+1} t^{m}}{1 - e^{-t}}$. It follows from [Bernstein's theorem on monotone functions](wikipedia://en/3526626) that, for $m > 0$ and $x$ real and non-negative, $(-1)^{m+1} \psi^{(m)}(x)$ is a completely monotone function.

Setting $m = 0$ in the above formula does not give an integral representation of the digamma function. The digamma function has an integral representation, due to Gauss, which is similar to the $m = 0$ case above but which has an extra term $\tfrac{e^{-t}}{t}$.

## Recurrence relation {#Recurrence_relation}

It satisfies the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)

$$\psi^{(m)}(z+1)= \psi^{(m)}(z) + \frac{(-1)^m\,m!}{z^{m+1}}$$

which – considered for positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) argument – leads to a presentation of the sum of reciprocals of the powers of the natural numbers:

$$\frac{\psi^{(m)}(n)}{(-1)^{m+1}\,m!} = \zeta(1+m) - \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \frac{1}{k^{m+1}} = \sum_{k=n}^\infty \frac{1}{k^{m+1}} \qquad m \ge 1$$

and

$$\psi^{(0)}(n) = -\gamma\ + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k}$$

for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, where $\gamma$ is the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant). Like the log-gamma function, the polygamma functions can be generalized from the domain [$\mathbb{N}$](wikipedia://en/21474) [uniquely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unique_%28mathematics%29) to [positive real numbers](wikipedia://en/47172571) only due to their recurrence relation and one given function-value, say $\psi^{(m)}(1)$, except in the case $m = 0$ where the additional condition of strict [monotonicity](wikipedia://en/48260) on $\mathbb{R}^{+}$ is still needed. This is a trivial consequence of the [Bohr–Mollerup theorem](wikipedia://en/780918) for the gamma function where strictly logarithmic convexity on $\mathbb{R}^{+}$ is demanded additionally. The case $m = 0$ must be treated differently because $\psi^{(0)}$ is not normalizable at infinity (the sum of the reciprocals doesn't converge).

## Reflection relation {#Reflection_relation}

$$(-1)^m \psi^{(m)} (1-z) - \psi^{(m)} (z) = \pi \frac{\mathrm{d}^m}{\mathrm{d} z^m} \cot{\pi z} = \pi^{m+1} \frac{P_m(\cos{\pi z})}{\sin^{m+1}(\pi z)}$$

where $P_{m}$ is alternately an odd or even polynomial of degree $\lvert m - 1\rvert$ with integer coefficients and leading coefficient $(-1)^{m}\lceil2^{m - 1}\rceil$. They obey the recursion equation

$$\begin{aligned} P_0(x) &= x \\ P_{m+1}(x) &= - \left( (m+1)xP_m(x)+\left(1-x^2\right)P'_m(x)\right).\end{aligned}$$

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Multiplication theorem"}

## Multiplication theorem {#Multiplication_theorem}

The [multiplication theorem](wikipedia://en/8720712) gives

$$k^{m+1} \psi^{(m)}(kz) = \sum_{n=0}^{k-1} \psi^{(m)}\left(z+\frac{n}{k}\right)\qquad m \ge 1$$

and

$$k \psi^{(0)}(kz) = k\ln{k} + \sum_{n=0}^{k-1} \psi^{(0)}\left(z+\frac{n}{k}\right)$$

for the [digamma function](wikipedia://en/406935).

:::

## Series representation {#Series_representation}

The polygamma function has the series representation

$$\psi^{(m)}(z) = (-1)^{m+1}\, m! \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{1}{(z+k)^{m+1}}$$

which holds for integer values of $m > 0$ and any complex $z$ not equal to a negative integer. This representation can be written more compactly in terms of the [Hurwitz zeta function](wikipedia://en/406952) as

$$\psi^{(m)}(z) = (-1)^{m+1}\, m!\, \zeta (m+1,z).$$

This relation can for example be used to compute the special values[^o8]

$$\psi^{(2n-1)}\left(\frac14\right) = \frac{4^{2n-1}}{2n}\left(\pi^{2n}(2^{2n}-1)|B_{2n}|+2(2n)!\beta(2n)\right);$$

$$\psi^{(2n-1)}\left(\frac34\right) = \frac{4^{2n-1}}{2n}\left(\pi^{2n}(2^{2n}-1)|B_{2n}|-2(2n)!\beta(2n)\right);$$

$$\psi^{(2n)}\left(\frac14\right) = -2^{2n-1}\left(\pi^{2n+1}|E_{2n}|+2(2n)!(2^{2n+1}-1)\zeta(2n+1)\right);$$

$$\psi^{(2n)}\left(\frac34\right) = 2^{2n-1}\left(\pi^{2n+1}|E_{2n}|-2(2n)!(2^{2n+1}-1)\zeta(2n+1)\right).$$

Alternately, the Hurwitz zeta can be understood to generalize the polygamma to arbitrary, non-integer order.

One more series may be permitted for the polygamma functions. As given by [Schlömilch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlömilch),

$$\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)} = z e^{\gamma z} \prod_{n=1}^\infty \left(1 + \frac{z}{n}\right) e^{-\frac{z}{n}}.$$

This is a result of the [Weierstrass factorization theorem](wikipedia://en/1585155). Thus, the gamma function may now be defined as:

$$\Gamma(z) = \frac{e^{-\gamma z}}{z} \prod_{n=1}^\infty \left(1 + \frac{z}{n}\right)^{-1} e^\frac{z}{n}.$$

Now, the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) of the gamma function is easily representable:

$$\ln \Gamma(z) = -\gamma z - \ln(z) + \sum_{k=1}^\infty \left( \frac{z}{k} - \ln\left(1 + \frac{z}{k}\right) \right).$$

Finally, we arrive at a summation representation for the polygamma function:

$$\psi^{(n)}(z) = \frac{\mathrm{d}^{n+1}}{\mathrm{d}z^{n+1}}\ln \Gamma(z) = -\gamma \delta_{n0} - \frac{(-1)^n n!}{z^{n+1}} + \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{1}{k} \delta_{n0} - \frac{(-1)^n n!}{(k+z)^{n+1}}\right)$$

Where $\delta_{n0}$ is the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890).

Also the [Lerch transcendent](wikipedia://en/1342978)

$$\Phi(-1, m+1, z) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^k}{(z+k)^{m+1}}$$

can be denoted in terms of polygamma function

$$\Phi(-1, m+1, z)=\frac1{(-2)^{m+1}m!}\left(\psi^{(m)}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)-\psi^{(m)}\left(\frac{z+1}{2}\right)\right)$$

## Taylor series {#Taylor_series}

The [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) at $z = -1$ is

$$\psi^{(m)}(z+1)= \sum_{k=0}^\infty (-1)^{m+k+1} \frac {(m+k)!}{k!} \zeta (m+k+1) z^k \qquad m \ge 1$$

and

$$\psi^{(0)}(z+1)= -\gamma +\sum_{k=1}^\infty (-1)^{k+1}\zeta (k+1) z^k$$

which converges for $\lvert z\rvert < 1$. Here, $\zeta$ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809). This series is easily derived from the corresponding Taylor series for the Hurwitz zeta function. This series may be used to derive a number of [rational zeta series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_zeta_series).

## Asymptotic expansion {#Asymptotic_expansion}

These non-converging series can be used to get quickly an approximation value with a certain numeric at-least-precision for large arguments:[^o11]

$$\psi^{(m)}(z) \sim (-1)^{m+1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+m-1)!}{k!}\frac{B_k}{z^{k+m}} \qquad m \ge 1$$

and

$$\psi^{(0)}(z) \sim \ln(z) - \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{B_k}{k z^k}$$

where we have chosen $B_{1} = \tfrac{1}{2}$, i.e. the [Bernoulli numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_numbers) of the second kind.

## Inequalities {#Inequalities}

The [hyperbolic cotangent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_cotangent) satisfies the inequality

$$\frac{t}{2}\operatorname{coth}\frac{t}{2} \ge 1,$$

and this implies that the function

$$\frac{t^m}{1 - e^{-t}} - \left(t^{m-1} + \frac{t^m}{2}\right)$$

is non-negative for all $m \ge 1$ and $t \ge 0$. It follows that the Laplace transform of this function is completely monotone. By the integral representation above, we conclude that

$$(-1)^{m+1}\psi^{(m)}(x) - \left(\frac{(m-1)!}{x^m} + \frac{m!}{2x^{m+1}}\right)$$

is completely monotone. The convexity inequality $e^{t} \ge 1 + t$ implies that

$$\left(t^{m-1} + t^m\right) - \frac{t^m}{1 - e^{-t}}$$

is non-negative for all $m \ge 1$ and $t \ge 0$, so a similar Laplace transformation argument yields the complete monotonicity of

$$\left(\frac{(m-1)!}{x^m} + \frac{m!}{x^{m+1}}\right) - (-1)^{m+1}\psi^{(m)}(x).$$

Therefore, for all $m \ge 1$ and $x > 0$,

$$\frac{(m-1)!}{x^m} + \frac{m!}{2x^{m+1}} \le (-1)^{m+1}\psi^{(m)}(x) \le \frac{(m-1)!}{x^m} + \frac{m!}{x^{m+1}}.$$

Since both bounds are *strictly* positive for $x>0$, we have:

- $\ln\Gamma(x)$ is strictly [convex](wikipedia://en/245568).
- For $m=0$, the digamma function, $\psi(x)=\psi^{(0)}(x)$, is strictly monotonic increasing and strictly [concave](wikipedia://en/263321).
- For $m$ odd, the polygamma functions, $\psi^{(1)},\psi^{(3)},\psi^{(5)},\ldots$, are strictly positive, strictly monotonic decreasing and strictly convex.
- For $m$ even the polygamma functions, $\psi^{(2)},\psi^{(4)},\psi^{(6)},\ldots$, are strictly negative, strictly monotonic increasing and strictly concave.

This can be seen in the first plot above.

### Trigamma bounds and asymptote {#Trigamma_bounds_and_asymptote}

For the case of the [trigamma function](wikipedia://en/2036750) ($m=1$) the final inequality formula above for $x>0$, can be rewritten as:

$$\frac{x+\frac12}{x^2} \le \psi^{(1)}(x)\le \frac{x+1}{x^2}$$

so that for $x\gg1$: $\psi^{(1)}(x)\approx\frac1x$.
