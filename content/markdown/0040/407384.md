# 100

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="100"}

**100** or **one hundred** ([Roman numeral](wikipedia://en/25657): **C**) is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/99_%28number%29) and preceding [101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101_%28number%29).

:::

## In mathematics {#In_mathematics}

*Image: cube-sum-100.png.* 100 as the sum of the first positive cubes

100 is the square of [10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_%28number%29) (in [scientific notation](wikipedia://en/59715) it is written as 10<sup>2</sup>). The standard [SI prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI_prefix) for a hundred is "[hecto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecto-)-".

100 is the basis of [percentages](wikipedia://en/64493) (per centum meaning "by the hundred" in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin)), with 100% being a full amount.

100 is a [Harshad number](wikipedia://en/641307) in [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214), and also in base-four, a base in-which it is also a [self-descriptive number](wikipedia://en/1991116).[^o4]

100 is the sum of the first nine [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666), from [2](wikipedia://en/64516) through [23](wikipedia://en/362203). It is also divisible by the number of primes below it, [25](wikipedia://en/362204).

100 cannot be expressed as the difference between any integer and the total of [coprimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) below it, making it a [noncototient](wikipedia://en/758718).

100 has a [reduced totient](wikipedia://en/1181756) of 20, and an [Euler totient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_totient) of 40. A totient value of 100 is obtained from four numbers: [101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101_%28number%29), [125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/125_%28number%29), [202](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/202_%28number%29), and [250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/250_%28number%29).

100 can be expressed as a sum of some of its divisors, making it a [semiperfect number](wikipedia://en/321918). The [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) of its nine divisors is [10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_%28number%29).

100 is the sum of the [cubes](wikipedia://en/451445) of the first four positive [integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integers) (100 = 1<sup>3</sup> + 2<sup>3</sup> + 3<sup>3</sup> + 4<sup>3</sup>). This is related by [Nicomachus's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicomachus's_theorem) to the fact that 100 also equals the square of the sum of the first four positive integers: 100 = 10<sup>2</sup> = (1 + 2 + 3 + 4)<sup>2</sup>.

100 = 2<sup>6</sup> + 6<sup>2</sup>, thus 100 is the seventh [Leyland number](wikipedia://en/4618420).[^o14] 100 is also the seventeenth [Erdős–Woods number](wikipedia://en/2616413), and the fourth 18-[gonal number](wikipedia://en/213996).[^o16]

## In history {#In_history}

- In medieval contexts, it may be described as the **short hundred** or five [score](wikipedia://en/208259) in order to differentiate the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages) use of "hundred" to describe the [long hundred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_hundred) of six [score](wikipedia://en/208259) or [120](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/120_%28number%29).

## In money {#In_money}

*Image: Hundred rupee note India.jpg.* Hundred rupee note India

Most of the world's [currencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency) are divided into 100 subunits. For example, one [euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro) is one hundred cents and one [pound sterling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_sterling) is one hundred pence.

*Image: Obverse of the \$100 Federal Reserve Note.jpg.* The [U.S. hundred-dollar bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_one-hundred-dollar_bill), Series 2025

The [United States one-hundred-dollar bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_one-hundred-dollar_bill) has [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin)'s portrait; this "Benjamin" is the largest American [banknote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banknote) in circulation.

### Science and measurement {#Science_and_measurement}

In [zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology), the group of [centipedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centipedes) ([Chilopoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilopoda)) is classified as a subclass of [millipedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millipedes). Centipedes are characterised by having between 15 and 191 pairs of legs. Although the name refers to "hundred", the actual number varies gradually.

With regard to the biological value of [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein), the number 100 is used as a reference value: the biological value of a protein is measured by how well the [dietary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietary) protein can be converted into the body's own protein. The [chicken egg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_egg) was arbitrarily set at a value of 100, and other proteins are evaluated relative to this.

[Anders Celsius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Celsius) defined 100° as the boiling point and 0° as the melting point of water for his temperature scale. In 1744, shortly after Celsius' death, the modern [Celsius scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius_scale) was introduced by [Carl von Linné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_von_Linné), in which the [boiling point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiling_point) of water is assigned the value 100°C and the freezing point the value 0°C.

- 100 centimetres **\=** 1 meter: Used in [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_system) measurements.
- 100 is the [atomic number](wikipedia://en/673) of [Fermium (Fm)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermium): A synthetic chemical element in the periodic table.
