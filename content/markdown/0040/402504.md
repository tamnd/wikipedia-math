# Paul Painlevé

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Painlevé"}

**Paul Painlevé** (; 5 December 1863 – 29 October 1933) was a French mathematician and statesman who served as [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_France) of the [French Third Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic) in 1917 and 1925. After working as a professor at the [Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University), he entered politics in 1906.

:::

His first term as prime minister lasted only nine weeks but dealt with weighty issues, such as the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution), the [American entry into World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_in_World_War_I), the failure of the [Nivelle Offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nivelle_Offensive), quelling the [French Army Mutinies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Army_Mutinies) and relations with the British. In the 1920s as Minister of War he was a key figure in building the [Maginot Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maginot_Line).[^o3] In his second term as prime minister he dealt with the outbreak of rebellion in Syria's Jabal Druze in July 1925 which had excited public and parliamentary anxiety over the general crisis of France's empire.[^o4]

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life {#Early_life}

Painlevé was born in Paris. Brought up in a family of skilled artisans (his father and grandfather were [lithographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithography) [draughtsmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drafter))[^o8] Painlevé showed early promise across the range of elementary studies and was initially attracted by either an engineering or political career. However, he finally entered the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in 1883 to study mathematics, receiving his doctorate in 1887 following a period of study at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen), Germany with [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) and [Hermann Amandus Schwarz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Amandus_Schwarz). Intending an academic career he became professor at [Université de Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Lille_Nord_de_France), returning to Paris in 1892 to teach at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) and later at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) and the École Normale Supérieure. He was elected a member of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences) in 1900.[^o7]

He married Marguerite Petit de Villeneuve in 1901. She died during the birth of their son [Jean Painlevé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Painlevé) in the following year.[^o7]

Painlevé's mathematical work on [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) led him to encounter their application to the theory of flight and, as ever, his broad interest in engineering topics fostered an enthusiasm for the emerging field of aviation. In 1908, he became [Wilbur Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilbur_Wright)'s first airplane passenger in France and in 1909 created the first university course in [aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautics).[^o7]

### Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

*Image: Paul Painlevé in 1915.jpg.* Paul Painlevé as a young man

Some [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) can be solved using elementary algebraic operations that involve the [trigonometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_function) and [exponential functions](wikipedia://en/9678) (sometimes called [elementary functions](wikipedia://en/10412)). Many interesting [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110) arise as solutions of linear second [order](wikipedia://en/5930652) [ordinary differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_differential_equations). Around the turn of the century, Painlevé, [É. Picard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Émile_Picard), and B. Gambier showed that of the class of [nonlinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear) second order ordinary differential equations with [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) [coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficients), those that possess a certain desirable technical property, shared by the linear equations (nowadays commonly referred to as the '[Painlevé property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painlevé_property)') can always be transformed into one of fifty canonical forms. Of these fifty equations, just six require 'new' transcendental functions for their solution.[^o10] These new [transcendental functions](wikipedia://en/297021), solving the remaining six equations, are called the [Painlevé transcendents](wikipedia://en/2033003), and interest in them has revived recently due to their appearance in modern geometry, integrable systems[^o11] and [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481).[^o12][^o13][^o14]

In 1895 he gave a series of lectures at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University) on differential equations, at the end stating the [Painlevé conjecture](wikipedia://en/43825561) about singularities of the [n-body problem](wikipedia://en/23859945).[^o15] In the same year he published work on the [Painlevé paradox](wikipedia://en/12068350), an apparent contradiction in simple models of [friction](wikipedia://en/11062).[^o16]

In the 1920s, Painlevé briefly turned his attention to the new theory of gravitation, [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), which had recently been introduced by [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736). In 1921, Painlevé proposed the [Gullstrand–Painlevé coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gullstrand–Painlevé_coordinates) for the [Schwarzschild metric](wikipedia://en/264606). The modification in the coordinate system was the first to reveal clearly that the [Schwarzschild radius](wikipedia://en/206115) is a mere [coordinate singularity](wikipedia://en/25269252) (with however, profound global significance: it represents the [event horizon](wikipedia://en/29320146) of a [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650)). This essential point was not generally appreciated by physicists until around 1963. In his diary, [Harry Graf Kessler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Graf_Kessler) recorded that during a later visit to Berlin, Painlevé discussed [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifism) [international politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_politics) with Einstein, but there is no reference to discussions concerning the significance of the Schwarzschild radius.[^o17]

### Early political career {#Early_political_career}

Between 1915 and 1917, Painlevé served as French Minister for Public Instruction and Inventions. In December 1915, he requested a scientific exchange agreement between France and Britain, resulting in Anglo-French collaboration that ultimately led to the parallel development by [Paul Langevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Langevin) in France and [Robert Boyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_William_Boyle) in Britain of the first active [sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonar).[^o19] He also established the [Directorate of Inventions for National Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorate_of_Inventions_for_National_Defense), the predecessor of the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research).

### First period as French Prime Minister {#First_period_as_French_Prime_Minister}

Painlevé took his aviation interests, along with those in naval and military matters, with him when he became, in 1906, Deputy for Paris's [5th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_arrondissement_of_Paris), the so-called [Latin Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Quarter). By 1910, he had vacated his academic posts and World War I led to his active participation in military committees, joining [Aristide Briand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristide_Briand)'s cabinet in 1915 as Minister for Public Instruction and Inventions.[^o7]

On his appointment as War Minister in March 1917 he was immediately called upon to give his approval, albeit with some misgivings, to [Robert Georges Nivelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Georges_Nivelle)'s wildly optimistic plans for a breakthrough offensive in [Champagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champagne,_France). Painlevé reacted to the disastrous public failure of the plan by dismissing [Nivelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Georges_Nivelle) and controversially replacing him with [Henri Philippe Pétain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Philippe_Pétain).[^o21] He was also responsible for isolating the [Russian Expeditionary Force in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Expeditionary_Force_in_France) in the [La Courtine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Courtine) camp, located in a remote spot on the plateau of [Millevaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau_de_Millevaches).[^o22]

On 7 September 1917, Prime Minister [Alexandre Ribot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Ribot) lost the support of the Socialists and Painlevé was called upon to form a new government.[^o7]

*Image: 1918 Painlevé.jpg.* [Autochrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autochrome_Lumière) portrait by Auguste Léon, 1918

Painlevé was a leading voice at the [Rapallo conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapallo_conference) that led to the establishment of the [Supreme Allied Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Allied_Council), a consultative body of [Allied powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) that anticipated the unified Allied command finally established in the following year. He appointed [Ferdinand Foch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Foch) as French representative knowing that he was the natural Allied commander. On Painlevé's return to Paris he was defeated and resigned on 13 November 1917 to be succeeded by [Georges Clemenceau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Clemenceau). [Foch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Foch) was finally named Allied *generalissimo* in March 1918, eventually becoming commander-in-chief of all Allied armies on the Western and Italian fronts.[^o7][^o21][^o23]

### Second period as French Prime Minister {#Second_period_as_French_Prime_Minister}

Painlevé then played little active role in politics until the election of November 1919 when he emerged as a leftist critic of the right-wing [Bloc National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bloc_%28France%29). By the time the next election approached in May 1924 his collaboration with [Édouard Herriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Édouard_Herriot), a fellow member of Briand's 1915 cabinet, had led to the formation of the [Cartel des Gauches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartel_des_Gauches). Winning the election, Herriot became Prime Minister in June, while Painlevé became President of the Chamber of Deputies. Though Painlevé ran for [President of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_France) in 1924 he was defeated by [Gaston Doumergue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Doumergue). Herriot's administration publicly recognised the Soviet Union, accepted the [Dawes Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawes_Plan) and agreed to [evacuate the Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Ruhr). However, a financial crisis arose from the ensuing devaluation of the [franc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franc) and in April 1925, Herriot fell and Painlevé became Prime Minister for a second time on 17 April. Unfortunately, he was unable to offer convincing remedies for the financial problems and was forced to resign on 21 November.[^o7][^o21][^o25]

*Image: Paul Painlevé Wellcome.jpg.* Paul Painlevé in the 1920s

### Later political career {#Later_political_career}

Following Painlevé's resignation, Briand formed a new government with Painlevé as Minister for War. Though Briand was defeated by [Raymond Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Poincaré) in 1926, Painlevé continued in office. Poincaré stabilised the franc with a return to the [gold standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_standard), but ultimately acceded power to Briand.[^o7] During his tenure as Minister of War, Painlevé was instrumental in the creation of the [Maginot Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maginot_Line). This line of military fortifications along France's Eastern border was largely designed by Painlevé, yet named for [André Maginot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Maginot), owing to Maginot's championing of public support and funding. Painlevé remained in office as Minister for War until July 1929.[^o7]

From 1925 to 1933, Painlevé represented France in the [International Committee on Intellectual Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_on_Intellectual_Cooperation) of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) (he replaced [Henri Bergson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson) and was himself replaced by [Édouard Herriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Édouard_Herriot)).[^o27]

Though he was proposed for [President of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_France) in 1932, Painlevé withdrew before the election. He became Minister of Air later that year, making proposals for an international treaty to ban the manufacture of bomber aircraft and to establish an international air force to enforce global peace. On the fall of the government in January 1933, his political career ended.[^o7]

Painlevé died in Paris in October of the same year.[^o21] On 4 November, after a eulogy by Prime Minister [Albert Sarraut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Sarraut), he was interred in the [Panthéon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthéon).[^o28]

## Honours {#Honours}

- Painlevé was elected an International Member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1918.[^o30]
- The aircraft carrier *[Painlevé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_aircraft_carrier_Painlevé)* was named in his honour.[^o31]
- The asteroid [953 Painleva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/953_Painleva) was named in his honour.[^o32]
- The [Laboratoire Paul Painlevé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratoire_Paul_Painlevé) (fr), a French mathematics research lab, is named in his honour.
- [Maurice Ravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Ravel) dedicated the second of his *[Trois Chansons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trois_Chansons_%28Ravel%29)* to him in 1915.
