# Surface of constant width

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Surface of constant width"}

::: {#o2 .problem cue=unsolved}

What is the minimum volume among all shapes of the same constant width?

:::

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **surface of constant width** is a [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) form whose width, measured by the distance between two opposite [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497) [planes touching its boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_plane), is the same regardless of the direction of those two parallel planes. One defines the width of the surface in a given direction to be the perpendicular distance between the parallels [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to that direction. Thus, a surface of constant width is the three-dimensional analogue of a [curve of constant width](wikipedia://en/580238), a two-dimensional shape with a constant distance between pairs of parallel [tangent lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_line).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

More generally, any [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) convex body *D* has one pair of parallel supporting planes in a given direction. A supporting plane is a plane that intersects the boundary of *D* but not the interior of *D*. One defines the width of the body as before. If the width of *D* is the same in all directions, then one says that the body is of constant width and calls its boundary a surface of constant width, and the body itself is referred to as a *spheroform*.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

A [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859), a surface of constant radius and thus diameter, is a surface of constant width.

Contrary to common belief the [Reuleaux tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5158942) is *not* a surface of constant width. However, there are two different ways of smoothing subsets of the edges of the Reuleaux tetrahedron to form [Meissner tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meissner's_tetrahedron), surfaces of constant width. These shapes were [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) by (Bonnesen Fenchel 1934) to have the minimum [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) among all shapes with the same constant width, but this conjecture remains unsolved.

Among all [surfaces of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549) with the same constant width, the one with minimum volume is the shape swept out by a [Reuleaux triangle](wikipedia://en/580252) rotating about one of its axes of symmetry,[^n1] while the one with maximum volume is the sphere.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Every [parallel projection](wikipedia://en/2503009) of a surface of constant width is a [curve of constant width](wikipedia://en/580238). By [Barbier's theorem](wikipedia://en/580264), the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of this projection is [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601) times the width, regardless of the direction of projection. It follows that every surface of constant width is also a surface of constant [girth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girth_%28geometry%29), where the girth of a shape is the perimeter of one of its parallel projections. [Conversely](wikipedia://en/155941), [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537) [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that every surface of constant girth is also a surface of constant width.[^n2]

The shapes whose parallel projections have constant [area](wikipedia://en/1209) (rather than constant perimeter) are called [bodies of constant brightness](wikipedia://en/67714643).

:::

## Higher dimensions {#Higher_dimensions}

Many 3-dimensional questions have n-dimensional analogues still seeking definitive answers.[^o13] For example, [Oded Schramm](wikipedia://en/9605580) asked for bounds on the volume of a convex $n$-dimensional body of constant width. He provided a lower bound,[^o14] and asked if there exists an example whose volume is significantly smaller than the [$n$-ball](wikipedia://en/21067650) $\mathbb{B}^{n}$ of the same width. In 2025, Arman, Bondarenko, Nazarov, Prymak and Radchenko provided such an estimate: for sufficiently large $n$, there exists an $n$-body $K$ of constant width with $\text{Vol}(K)\leq (0.9)^n\text{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{n} )$.[^o15]
