# Riemann invariant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Riemann invariant"}

**Riemann invariants** are [mathematical transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_transformations) made on a system of [conservation equations](wikipedia://en/6956) to make them more easily solvable. Riemann invariants are constant along the [characteristic curves](wikipedia://en/751933) of the partial differential equations where they obtain the name [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638). They were first obtained by [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) in his work on plane waves in gas dynamics.[^o2]

:::

## Mathematical theory {#Mathematical_theory}

Consider the set of [conservation equations](wikipedia://en/6956):

$$l_i\left(A_{ij} \frac{\partial u_j}{\partial t} +a_{ij}\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x} \right)+l_j b_j=0$$

where $A_{ij}$ and $a_{ij}$ are the [elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Element_%28mathematics%29) of the [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{a}$ where $l_{i}$ and $b_{i}$ are elements of [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_vector). It will be asked if it is possible to rewrite this equation to

$$m_j\left(\beta\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial t} +\alpha\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x} \right)+l_j b_j=0$$

To do this curves will be introduced in the $(x,t)$ plane defined by the [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) $(\alpha,\beta)$. The term in the brackets will be rewritten in terms of a [total derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_derivative) where $x,t$ are parametrized as $x=X(\eta),t=T(\eta)$

$$\frac{d u_j}{d \eta}=T'\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial t}+X'\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x}$$

comparing the last two equations we find

$$\alpha=X'(\eta), \beta=T'(\eta)$$

which can be now written in [characteristic form](wikipedia://en/31050469)

$$m_j\frac{du_j }{ d \eta }+l_jb_j = 0$$

where we must have the conditions

$$l_iA_{ij}=m_jT'$$

$$l_ia_{ij}=m_jX'$$

where $m_j$ can be eliminated to give the necessary condition

$$l_i(A_{ij}X'-a_{ij}T')=0$$

so for a [nontrivial solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_solution) is the determinant

$$|A_{ij}X'-a_{ij}T'|=0$$

For Riemann invariants we are concerned with the case when the matrix $\mathbf{A}$ is an [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718) to form

$$\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t} +a_{ij}\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial x}=0$$

notice this is [homogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous) due to the vector $\mathbf{n}$ being zero. In characteristic form the system is

$l_i\frac{du_i }{dt }=0$ with $\frac{dx }{dt }=\lambda$

Where $l$ is the left [eigenvector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector) of the matrix $\mathbf{A}$ and $\lambda 's$ is the [characteristic speeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_speed) of the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues) of the matrix $\mathbf{A}$ which satisfy

$$|A -\lambda\delta_{ij}|=0$$

To simplify these [characteristic equations](wikipedia://en/31050469) we can make the transformations such that $\frac{dr}{dt}=l_i\frac{du_i}{dt}$

which form

$$\mu l_idu_i =dr$$

An [integrating factor](wikipedia://en/1912874) $\mu$ can be multiplied in to help integrate this. So the system now has the characteristic form

$\frac{dr}{dt }=0$ on $\frac{dx}{dt}=\lambda_i$

which is equivalent to the [diagonal system](wikipedia://en/174080)[^o4]

$r_t^k +\lambda_kr_x^k=0,$ $k=1,...,N.$

The solution of this system can be given by the generalized [hodograph method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodograph_method).[^o5][^o6]

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Consider the one-dimensional [Euler equations](wikipedia://en/396022) written in terms of density $\rho$ and velocity $u$ are

$$\rho_t+\rho u_x+u\rho_x=0$$

$$u_t+uu_x+(c^2/\rho)\rho_x=0$$

with $c$ being the [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853) is introduced on account of isentropic assumption. Write this system in matrix form

$$\left( \begin{matrix} \rho\\ u \end{matrix}\right)_t +\left( \begin{matrix} u&\rho\\ \frac{c^2 }{\rho }&u \end{matrix}\right) \left( \begin{matrix} \rho\\ u \end{matrix}\right)_x=\left( \begin{matrix} 0\\ 0 \end{matrix}\right)$$

where the matrix $\mathbf{a}$ from the analysis above the eigenvalues and eigenvectors need to be found. The eigenvalues are found to satisfy

$$\lambda^2-2u\lambda+u^2-c^2=0$$

to give

$$\lambda=u\pm c$$

and the eigenvectors are found to be

$$\left( \begin{matrix} 1\\ \frac{c }{\rho } \end{matrix}\right),\left( \begin{matrix} 1\\ -\frac{c }{\rho } \end{matrix}\right)$$

where the Riemann invariants are

$$r_1=J_+=u+\int \frac{c}{\rho}d\rho,$$

$$r_2=J_-=u-\int \frac{c}{\rho}d\rho,$$

($J_+$ and $J_-$ are the widely used notations in [gas dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_dynamics)). For perfect gas with constant specific heats, there is the relation $c^2=\text{const}\, \gamma \rho^{\gamma-1}$, where $\gamma$ is the [specific heat ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_heat_ratio), to give the Riemann invariants[^o9][^o10]

$$J_+=u+\frac{2}{\gamma-1}c,$$

$$J_-=u-\frac{2}{\gamma-1}c,$$

to give the equations

$$\frac{\partial J_+}{\partial t}+(u+c)\frac{\partial J_+}{\partial x}=0$$

$$\frac{\partial J_-}{\partial t}+(u-c)\frac{\partial J_-}{\partial x}=0$$

In other words,

$$\begin{aligned} &dJ_+ = 0, \, J_+=\text{const}\quad \text{along}\,\, C_+\, :\, \frac{dx}{dt}=u+c, \\ &dJ_- = 0, \, J_-=\text{const}\quad \text{along}\,\, C_-\, :\, \frac{dx}{dt}=u-c, \end{aligned}$$

where $C_+$ and $C_-$ are the characteristic curves. This can be solved by the [hodograph transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodograph_transformation). In the hodographic plane, if all the characteristics collapses into a single curve, then we obtain [simple waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_wave). If the matrix form of the system of pde's is in the form

$$A\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+B\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}=0$$

Then it may be possible to multiply across by the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$ so long as the matrix [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of $\mathbf{A}$ is not zero.
