# Mehler–Heine formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mehler–Heine formula"}

In mathematics, the **Mehler–Heine formula** introduced by [Gustav Ferdinand Mehler](wikipedia://en/31395561)[^o2] and [Eduard Heine](wikipedia://en/608289)[^o3] describes the asymptotic behavior of the [Legendre polynomials](wikipedia://en/100349) as the index tends to infinity, near the edges of the support of the weight. There are generalizations to other [classical orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/336568), which are also called the Mehler–Heine formula. The formula complements the Darboux formulae which describe the [asymptotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotics) in the interior and outside the support.

:::

## Legendre polynomials {#Legendre_polynomials}

The simplest case of the Mehler–Heine formula states that

$$\lim _{n\to\infty}P_n\left(\cos{\frac{z}{n}}\right) = \lim _{n\to\infty}P_n\left(1-\frac{z^2}{2n^2}\right) = J_0(z),$$

where $P_{n}$ is the Legendre polynomial of order $n$, and $J_{0}$ the [Bessel function](wikipedia://en/4700) of order 0. The limit is uniform over $z$ in an arbitrary bounded [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628).

## Jacobi polynomials {#Jacobi_polynomials}

The generalization to [Jacobi polynomials](wikipedia://en/30863587) $P^{(\alpha, \beta)}_{n}$ is given by [Gábor Szegő](wikipedia://en/46065)[^o6] as follows

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} n^{-\alpha}P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}\left(\cos \frac{z}{n}\right) = \lim_{n \to \infty} n^{-\alpha}P_n^{(\alpha,\beta)}\left(1-\frac{z^2}{2n^2}\right) = \left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{-\alpha} J_\alpha(z),$$

where $J_{\alpha}$ is the Bessel function of [order $\alpha$](wikipedia://en/4700#Bessel_functions_of_the_first_kind:_Jα).

## Laguerre polynomials {#Laguerre_polynomials}

Using generalized [Laguerre polynomials](wikipedia://en/943917) and [confluent hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/1516095), they can be written as

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} n^{-\alpha}L_n^{(\alpha)}\left(\frac{z^2}{4n}\right) = \left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{-\alpha} J_\alpha(z),$$

where $L^{(\alpha)}_{n}$ is the Laguerre function.

## Hermite polynomials {#Hermite_polynomials}

Using the expressions relating [Hermite polynomials](wikipedia://en/195984) and Laguerre polynomials where [two equations](wikipedia://en/195984#Laguerre_polynomials) exist,[^o9] they can be written as

$$\begin{aligned}\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{4^nn!}\sqrt{n}H_{2n}\left(\frac{z}{2\sqrt{n}}\right) &=\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{-\frac{1}{2}}(z) \\ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{(-1)^n}{4^nn!}H_{2n+1}\left(\frac{z}{2\sqrt{n}}\right) &=(2z)^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{\frac{1}{2}}(z),\end{aligned}$$

where $H_{n}$ is the Hermite function.
