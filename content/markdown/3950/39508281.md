# Julius Borcea

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Julius Borcea"}

**Julius Bogdan Borcea** (8 June 1968 – 8 April 2009) was a [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania) [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). His scientific work included [vertex operator algebra](wikipedia://en/1287577) and zero distribution of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) and entire [functions](wikipedia://en/185427), via [correlation inequalities](wikipedia://en/20286769) and [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Bacău](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacău), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania), by a math teacher who instilled in her son's intellect the [beauty of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_of_mathematics), he studied in 1982-1984 at the [Lycée Descartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Descartes_%28Rabat%29) in [Rabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabat), [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco), and he completed his Baccalaureat at the [Lycée Français Prins Henrik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Français_Prins_Henrik) of [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen). In 1987–1989 he attended the [Lycée Louis-le-Grand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Louis-le-Grand) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989). He obtained his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in Mathematics in 1998, at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University), under the direction of [Arne Meurman](wikipedia://en/5033047). After defending his PhD thesis in 1998, he embarked in postdoctoral studies at the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452) for six months and at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) for two years. He was appointed Associate Professor in 2001, and Lecturer in 2005 at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University). A year later he was granted the Swedish Mathematical Society's Wallenberg Prize. Promoted to Full Professor in 2008, he was awarded a [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) Fellowship, in 2009, and the Crafoord prize research grant diploma.[^o5]

## Professional profile {#Professional_profile}

Borcea's scientific work ranged from vertex [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) to zero distribution of polynomials and entire functions, via correlation inequalities and statistical mechanics. His thesis consists of two seemingly independent parts: one in vertex operator theory and the other devoted to the geometry of zeros of complex polynomials in one variable.

In vertex operator theory, Julius generalized results of Mirko Primc and [Arne Meurman](wikipedia://en/5033047)[^o7] and gave a classification of annihilated fields. As concerns complex polynomials, he tackled Sendov’s conjecture on zeros and critical points of complex polynomials in one variable. Using novel techniques, he proved the conjecture for polynomials of degree not exceeding 7. Earlier (1969) the conjecture had been proven for polynomials of degree not exceeding 5. At [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University), Julius had a steady collaboration with Rikard Bøgvad and Boris Shapiro. They worked on rational approximations of algebraic equations, [piecewise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise) harmonic functions and positive Cauchy transforms, and the geometry of zeros of polynomials in one variable. Borcea and Petter Brändén collaborated on a project on the geometry of zeros of polynomials and entire functions. They characterized all linear operators on polynomials preserving the property of having only real zeros, a problem that goes back to [Edmond Laguerre](wikipedia://en/1011080) and to [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) and [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522). These results were subsequently extended to several variables, and a connection to the [Lee–Yang theorem](wikipedia://en/19661214) on phase-transitions in statistical physics was made. Together with Tom Liggett ([UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA)) they applied their methods to problems in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and were able to prove an important conjecture about the preservation of negative dependence properties in the symmetric exclusion process.

Borcea had a comprehensive project on the distribution of positive charges and the Hausdorff geometry of complex polynomials. One of the motivations for the project was to bring Sendov’s conjecture into a larger and more natural context. He formulated several interesting conjectures, and in the summer of 2008 he was the driving force of two meetings, one at the [American Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/3248005) in [San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California) and the other at the [Banff International Research Station](wikipedia://en/39151817)[^o8] together with Dmitry Khavinson, Rajesh Pereira, Mihai Putinar, Edward B. Saff, and Serguei Shimorin. These two encounters were focused on structuring and expanding Julius’ program. His continuous and vivid interest in the Hausdorff geometry of polynomials was triggered by an [École normale supérieure (Paris)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29) exam he took in 1989.
