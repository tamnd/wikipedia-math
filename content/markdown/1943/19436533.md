# Hurwitz quaternion order

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hurwitz quaternion order"}

The **Hurwitz quaternion order** is a specific [order](wikipedia://en/1114171) in a [quaternion algebra](wikipedia://en/2776890) over a suitable [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field). The order is of particular importance in [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) theory, in connection with surfaces with maximal [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741), namely the [Hurwitz surfaces](wikipedia://en/12807841).[^o2] The Hurwitz quaternion order was studied in 1967 by [Goro Shimura](wikipedia://en/174724),[^o3] but first explicitly described by [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999) in 1998.[^o4] For an alternative use of the term, see [Hurwitz quaternion](wikipedia://en/772241) (both usages are current in the literature).

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $K$ be the maximal real subfield of $\mathbb{Q}$$(\rho)$ where $\rho$ is a 7th-primitive [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950). The [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of $K$ is $\mathbb{Z}[\eta]$, where the element $\eta=\rho+ \bar\rho$ can be identified with the positive real $2\cos(\tfrac{2\pi}{7})$. Let $D$ be the [quaternion algebra](wikipedia://en/2776890), or symbol algebra

$$D:=\,(\eta,\eta)_{K},$$

so that $i^2=j^2=\eta$ and $ij=-ji$ in $D.$ Also let $\tau=1+\eta+\eta^2$ and $j'=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\eta i + \tau j)$. Let

$$\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{Hur}}=\mathbb{Z}[\eta][i,j,j'].$$

Then $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{Hur}}$ is a maximal [order](wikipedia://en/1114171) of $D$, described explicitly by [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999).[^o7]

:::

## Module structure {#Module_structure}

The order $Q_{\mathrm{Hur}}$ is also generated by elements

$$g_2= \tfrac{1}{\eta}ij$$

and

$$g_3=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+(\eta^2-2)j+(3-\eta^2)ij).$$

In fact, the order is a free $\mathbb Z[\eta]$-module over the basis $\,1,g_2,g_3, g_2g_3$. Here the generators satisfy the relations

$$g_2^2=g_3^3= (g_2g_3)^7=-1,$$

which descend to the appropriate relations in the [(2,3,7) triangle group](wikipedia://en/12659803), after quotienting by the center.

## Principal congruence subgroups {#Principal_congruence_subgroups}

The principal congruence subgroup defined by an ideal $I \subset \mathbb{Z}[\eta]$ is by definition the group

$\mathcal{Q}^1_{\mathrm{Hur}}(I) = \{x \in \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{Hur}}^1 : x \equiv 1 ($mod $I\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{Hur}})\},$

namely, the group of elements of [reduced norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_norm) 1 in $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{Hur}}$ equivalent to 1 modulo the ideal $I\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{Hur}}$. The corresponding Fuchsian group is obtained as the image of the principal congruence subgroup under a representation to P[SL(2,R)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SL%282,R%29).

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

The order was used by Katz, Schaps, and Vishne[^o12] to construct a family of Hurwitz surfaces satisfying an asymptotic lower bound for the systole: $sys > \frac{4}{3}\log g$ where g is the genus, improving an earlier result of [Peter Buser](wikipedia://en/64453197) and [Peter Sarnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sarnak);[^o13] see [systoles of surfaces](wikipedia://en/19060246).
