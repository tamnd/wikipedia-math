# Nelson–Aalen estimator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nelson–Aalen estimator"}

The **Nelson–Aalen estimator** is a [non-parametric estimator](wikipedia://en/223369) of the [cumulative hazard rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_rate) function in case of [censored data](wikipedia://en/11548952) or [incomplete data](wikipedia://en/7859676).[^o2] It is used in [survival theory](wikipedia://en/419259), [reliability engineering](wikipedia://en/1724836) and [life insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_insurance) to estimate the cumulative number of expected events. An "event" can be the failure of a non-repairable component, the death of a human being, or any occurrence for which the experimental unit remains in the "failed" state (e.g., death) from the point at which it changed on. The [estimator](wikipedia://en/10043) is given by

:::

$$\tilde{H}(t)=\sum_{t_i\leq t}\frac{d_i}{n_i},$$

with $d_i$ the number of events at time $t_i$ and $n_i$ the total individuals at risk at $t_i$.[^o3]

The curvature of the Nelson–Aalen estimator gives an idea of the hazard rate shape. A concave shape is an indicator for [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathtub_curve) while a convex shape indicates [wear out mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathtub_curve).

It can be used for example when testing the homogeneity of [Poisson processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process).[^o4]

It was constructed by [Wayne Nelson](wikipedia://en/67877140) and [Odd Aalen](wikipedia://en/7755735).[^o5][^o6][^o7] The Nelson-Aalen estimator is directly related to the [Kaplan-Meier estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaplan-Meier_estimator) and both maximize the [empirical likelihood](wikipedia://en/37281260).[^o8]
