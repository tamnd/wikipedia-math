# Moffat distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moffat distribution"}

The **Moffat distribution**, named after the [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) [Anthony Moffat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Moffat), is a [continuous probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_probability_distribution) based upon the [Lorentzian distribution](wikipedia://en/7003). Its particular importance in [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics) is due to its ability to accurately reconstruct [point spread functions](wikipedia://en/865348), whose wings cannot be accurately portrayed by either a [Gaussian](wikipedia://en/245552) or [Lorentzian](wikipedia://en/7003) function.

:::

## Characterisation {#Characterisation}

### Probability density function {#Probability_density_function}

The Moffat distribution can be described in two ways. Firstly as the distribution of a bivariate random variable (*x*,*y*) centred at zero, and secondly as the distribution of the corresponding radii

$$r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}.$$

In terms of the random vector (*x*,*y*), the distribution has the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) (pdf)

$$f(x,y; \alpha,\beta) = \frac{\beta-1}{\pi\alpha^2} \left[1+\left(\frac{x^2+y^2}{\alpha^2}\right)\right]^{-\beta} ,$$

where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are [seeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_seeing) dependent parameters. In this form, the distribution is a reparameterisation of a [bivariate Student distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bivariate_Student_distribution) with zero correlation.

In terms of the radius *r*, the distribution has density

$$f(r; \alpha,\beta)=\frac{\beta-1}{\pi\alpha^2} \left[1+\left(\frac{r^2}{\alpha^2}\right)\right]^{-\beta} .$$

## Relation to other distributions {#Relation_to_other_distributions}

- [Pearson distribution](wikipedia://en/1804023)
- [Student's t-distribution](wikipedia://en/105375) for $\beta = \frac{\alpha^2+1}{2}$
- [Normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) for $\beta = \frac{\alpha^2}{2} \rightarrow \infty$, since for the [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) $\exp x = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{x}{n}\right)^n.$
