# Hexastix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hexastix"}

**Hexastix** is a symmetric arrangement of non-intersecting [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) that, when extended infinitely, fill exactly 3/4 of space. The prisms in a hexastix arrangement are all [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497) to 4 directions on the [body-centered cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body-centered_cubic) lattice. In *[The Symmetries of Things](wikipedia://en/69062204)*, [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807), Heidi Burgiel, and [Chaim Goodman-Strauss](wikipedia://en/56181508) named this structure hexastix.

:::

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The hexastix arrangement has found use in mathematics, [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography), reticular chemistry, puzzle design, and art. [Michael O'Keeffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_O'Keeffe_%28chemist%29) and associates define this structure as one of the 6 possible invariant cubic rod packing arrangements. O'Keefe classifies this arrangement as the *Γ* or Garnet rod packing, and describes it as the densest possible cubic rod packing. Rod packings are used to classify chains of atoms in crystal structures, and in the develop of materials like metal–organic frameworks. It has been proposed that [stratum corneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratum_corneum)'s structure could be modeled using the hexastix cylinder packing geometry. Hexastix geometry has also found use in architecture, being used to construct a 3-story bamboo structure in Ecuador. In [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446), the hexastix arrangement can be found in the design of mechanical [burr puzzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burr_puzzle). [Stewart Coffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Coffin) has used this geometry in the creation of complex non-rectilinear wooden puzzles. In art, hexastix is used by artist [Anduriel Widmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anduriel_Widmark) to create complex glass knots. Hexastix is also seen in the sculpture titled "72 Pencils", made by math artist [George W. Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Hart).

:::

## Related structures {#Related_structures}

Non-intersecting prism arrangements with prime cubic symmetry make up the family "polystix". Related square and triangular prism structures in three and four directions are named by Conway as [tetrastix](wikipedia://en/64254647) and "tristix". If the ends of the prisms in a hexastix arrangement are pointed, the directionality modifies the symmetry, and the related structure is known as hexastakes. Rod packings with more directions are also possible, as in the quasi-periodic 6 directional rod packing. The [Hexahemioctacron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexahemioctacron) is similarly made from hexagonal prisms but unlike hexastix, the prisms are intersecting.
