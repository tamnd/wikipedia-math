# F. Thomas Farrell

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="F. Thomas Farrell"}

**Francis Thomas Farrell** (born November 14, 1941) is an American mathematician who has made contributions in the area of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). Farrell is a distinguished professor emeritus of mathematics at [Binghamton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binghamton_University).[^o3] He also holds a position at the Yau Mathematical Sciences Center, [Tsinghua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsinghua_University).

:::

## Biographical data {#Biographical_data}

Farrell got his bachelor's degree in 1963 from the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame) and earned his [Ph.D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D) in Mathematics from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1967. His Ph.D. advisor was [Wu-Chung Hsiang](wikipedia://en/50980728), and his doctoral thesis title was "The Obstruction to Fibering a Manifold over a Circle". He was a NSF Post-doctoral Fellow at the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley) from 1968 to 1969, and became an assistant professor there from 1969 to 1972. He then went to [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University), where he was promoted to professor in 1978. Later he joined the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) (1979–1985) and [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) (1984–1992). Since 1990 Farrell has been a faculty member at SUNY Binghamton.

In 1970, Farrell was invited to give a 50-minute address at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) about his thesis in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), France.[^o6][^o7] In 1990, for their joint work on Rigidity in Geometry and Topology, his co-author [Lowell E. Jones](wikipedia://en/48870101) was invited to give a 45-minute address at the International Congress of Mathematicians in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto), Japan.[^o6][^o8]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Much of Farrell's work lies around the [Borel conjecture](wikipedia://en/7181855). He and his co-authors have verified the conjecture for various cases, most notably [flat manifolds](wikipedia://en/12756616),[^o10] [nonpositively curved](wikipedia://en/12989803) manifolds.[^o11]

In his thesis, Farrell solved the problem of determining when a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) (of dimension greater than 5) can [fiber](wikipedia://en/316826) over a circle.[^o12]

In 1977, he introduced [Tate–Farrell cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farrell_cohomology),[^o13] which is a generalization to infinite groups of the [Tate cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate_cohomology) theory for finite groups.

In 1993, he and his co-author Lowell E. Jones introduced the [Farrell–Jones conjecture](wikipedia://en/20643804)[^o14] and made contributions on it. The conjecture plays an important role in manifold topology.
