# Obligationes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Obligationes"}

***Obligationes*** or **disputations *de obligationibus*** were a medieval [disputation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disputation) format common in the 13th and 14th centuries.[^o2] Despite the name, they had nothing to do with ethics or morals but rather dealt with logical formalisms;[^o3] the name comes from the fact that the participants were "obliged" to follow the rules.[^o4] Typically, there were two disputants, one *Opponens* and one *Respondens*. At the start of a debate, both the disputants would agree on a ‘*positum*’, usually a false statement. The task of *Respondens* was to answer rationally to the questions from the *Opponens*, assuming the truth of the *positum* and without contradicting himself. On the opposite, the task of the *Opponens* was to try to force the *Respondens* into contradictions.[^o2]

:::

Several styles of *Obligationes* were distinguished in the medieval literature with the most widely studied being called "*positio*" (positing). "Obligational" disputations resemble recent theories of counterfactual reasoning and are believed to precede the modern practice of the academic "thesis defense." *Obligationes* also resembles a stylized, highly formalized, version of [Socratic dialogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socratic_dialogues). It can also be a form a Aristotelian dialectical situation with an Answerer and a Questioner.[^o5][^o6] It precedes other more modern dialogical accounts of logic such as [Lorenzen](wikipedia://en/2326847) games, [Hintikka](wikipedia://en/1710903) games and [game semantics](wikipedia://en/617121).

William of Ockham said *Obligationes*:

> ...consists of this that in the beginning some proposition has to be posited, and then propositions have to be proposed as pleases the opponent, and to these the respondent has to answer by granting or denying or doubting or distinguishing. When these answers are given, the opponent, when it pleases him, has to say: “time is finished”. This is, the time of the obligation is finished. And then it is seen whether the respondent has answered well or not.[^o7]
