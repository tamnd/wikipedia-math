# Comb space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Comb space"}

In mathematics, particularly [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a **comb space** is a particular [subspace](wikipedia://en/910926) of $\mathbb{R}^2$ that resembles a [comb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comb). The comb space has properties that serve as a number of [counterexamples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterexamples). The [topologist's sine curve](wikipedia://en/292224) has similar properties to the comb space. The **deleted comb space** is a variation on the comb space.

:::

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Consider $\mathbb{R}^2$ with its [standard topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_topology) and let *K* be the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $\{1/n ~|~ n \in \mathbb N\}$. The set *C* defined by:

$$(\{0\} \times [0,1] ) \cup (K \times [0,1]) \cup ([0,1] \times \{0\})$$

considered as a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$ equipped with the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) is known as the comb space. The deleted comb space, D, is defined by:

$$(\{0\} \times \{1 \}) \cup (K \times [0,1]) \cup ([0,1] \times \{0\}).$$

This is the comb space with the line segment $\{0\} \times (0,1)$ deleted.

:::

## Topological properties {#Topological_properties}

The comb space and the deleted comb space have some interesting topological properties mostly related to the notion of [connectedness](wikipedia://en/6959754).

- The comb space, C, is path connected and [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414), but not locally contractible, [locally path connected](wikipedia://en/6959754), or locally connected.
- The deleted comb space, D, is connected:

Let E be the comb space without $\{0\} \times (0,1]$. E is also path connected and the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of E is the comb space. As E $\subset$ D $\subset$ the closure of E, where E is connected, the deleted comb space is also connected.

- The deleted comb space is not path connected since there is no [path](wikipedia://en/1189553) from (0,1) to (0,0):

Suppose there is a path from *p* = (0, 1) to the point (0, 0) in *D*. Let *f* : \[0, 1\] → *D* be this path. We shall prove that *f*<sup>−1</sup>{*p*} is both [open](wikipedia://en/39358) and [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) in \[0, 1\] contradicting the [connectedness](wikipedia://en/6233) of this set. Clearly we have *f*<sup>−1</sup>{*p*} is closed in \[0, 1\] by the [continuity](wikipedia://en/6122) of *f*. To prove that *f*<sup>−1</sup>{*p*} is open, we proceed as follows: Choose a [neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) *V* (open in **R**<sup>2</sup>) about *p* that doesn't intersect the *x*–axis. Suppose *x* is an arbitrary point in *f*<sup>−1</sup>{*p*}. Clearly, *f*(*x*) = *p*. Then since *f*<sup>−1</sup>(*V*) is open, there is a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29) element *U* containing *x* such that *f*(*U*) is a subset of *V*. We assert that *f*(*U*) = {*p*} which will mean that *U* is an open subset of *f*<sup>−1</sup>{*p*} containing *x*. Since *x* was arbitrary, *f*<sup>−1</sup>{*p*} will then be open. We know that *U* is connected since it is a basis element for the [order topology](wikipedia://en/213650) on \[0, 1\]. Therefore, *f*(*U*) is connected. Suppose *f*(*U*) contains a point *s* other than *p*. Then *s* = (1/*n*, *z*) must belong to *D*. Choose *r* such that 1/(*n* + 1) \< *r* \< 1/*n*. Since *f*(*U*) does not intersect the *x*-axis, the sets *A* = (−∞, *r*) × $\mathbb{R}$ and *B* = (*r*, +∞) × $\mathbb{R}$ will form a [separation](wikipedia://en/148911) on *f*(*U*); contradicting the connectedness of *f*(*U*). Therefore, *f*<sup>−1</sup>{*p*} is both open and closed in \[0, 1\]. This is a contradiction.

- The comb space is [homotopic](wikipedia://en/212250) to a point but does not admit a [strong deformation retract](wikipedia://en/2120001) onto a point for every choice of basepoint that lies in the segment $\{0\} \times (0,1]$
