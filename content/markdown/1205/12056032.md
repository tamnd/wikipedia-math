# Polygonal chain

*Image: Chainline.svg.* A simple polygonal chain

*Image: Self crossed polygonal chain.svg.* A self-intersecting polygonal chain

*Image: Closed polygonal line.svg.* A closed polygonal chain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polygonal chain"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **polygonal chain** is a connected series of [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860). More formally, a polygonal chain $P$ is a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) specified by a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of points $(A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n)$ called its [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417). The curve itself consists of the line segments connecting the consecutive vertices.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variations"}

## Variations {#Variations}

### Simple {#Simple}

A **simple polygonal chain** is one in which only consecutive segments intersect and only at their endpoints.

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Closed polygonal chain"}

### Closed {#Closed}

A **closed polygonal chain** is one in which the first vertex coincides with the last one, or, alternatively, the first and the last vertices are also connected by a line segment.[^o7] A simple closed polygonal chain in [the plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) is the boundary of a [simple polygon](wikipedia://en/1059530). Often the term "[polygon](wikipedia://en/23621)" is used in the meaning of "closed polygonal chain", but in some cases it is important to draw a distinction between a [polygonal area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygonal_area) and a polygonal chain. A [space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_curve) closed polygonal chain is also known as a [skew "polygon"](wikipedia://en/6545461).

:::

### Monotone {#Monotone}

*Image: Monotone-subseq-17-5.svg.* A set of *n*=17 points has a polygonal path with 4 same-sign slopes

A polygonal chain is called ***monotone*** if there is a [straight line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_line) *L* such that every line perpendicular to *L* intersects the chain at most once. Every nontrivial monotone polygonal chain is open. In comparison, a [monotone polygon](wikipedia://en/12003237) is a polygon (a closed chain) that can be partitioned into exactly two monotone chains.[^o9] The graphs of [piecewise linear functions](wikipedia://en/444224) form monotone chains with respect to a horizontal line.

:::

## Parametrization {#Parametrization}

Each segment of a polygonal chain is typically [parametrized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_curve) linearly, using [linear interpolation](wikipedia://en/160010) between successive vertices. For the whole chain, two parametrizations are common in practical applications: Each segment of the chain can be assigned a unit [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) of the parameter corresponding to the index of the first vertex; alternately, each segment of the chain can be assigned an interval of the parameter corresponding to the length of the segment, so that the parameter corresponds uniformly to arclength along the whole chain.

## From point sets {#From_point_sets}

Every set of at least $n$ points contains a polygonal path of at least $\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor$ edges in which all slopes have the same sign. This is a corollary of the [Erdős–Szekeres theorem](wikipedia://en/3115543).

::: {#o13 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Polygonal chains can often be used to approximate more complex curves. In this context, the [Ramer–Douglas–Peucker algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramer–Douglas–Peucker_algorithm) can be used to find a polygonal chain with few segments that serves as an accurate approximation.[^o14][^o15]

In [graph drawing](wikipedia://en/325813), polygonal chains are used to represent the edges of graphs, in drawing styles where drawing the edges as straight line segments would cause crossings, edge-vertex collisions, or other undesired features. In this context, it is often desired to draw edges with as few segments and bends as possible, to reduce the visual clutter in the drawing; the problem of minimizing the number of bends is called [bend minimization](wikipedia://en/43176911).[^o16]

*Image: Bézier 4 big.svg.* A red Bézier curve is defined by the control points $P_{0},\,...,\,P_{4}$. The gray polygonal chain connecting the control points is called the control polygon.

In [computer-aided geometric design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_geometric_design), smooth curves are often defined by a list of [control points](wikipedia://en/8124425), e.g. in defining [Bézier curve](wikipedia://en/4664) segments. When connected together, the control points form a polygonal chain called a *control polygon*.

Polygonal chains are also a fundamental data type in [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927). For instance, a [point location](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_location) algorithm of [Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der-Tsai_Lee) and [Preparata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_P._Preparata) operates by decomposing arbitrary [planar subdivisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_subdivision) into an ordered sequence of monotone chains, in which a point location query problem may be solved by [binary search](wikipedia://en/4266); this method was later refined to give optimal time bounds for the point location problem.[^o17]

With [geographic information system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_information_system), linestrings may represent any linear geometry, and can be described using the [well-known text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-known_text_representation_of_geometry) markup as a `LineString` or `MultiLineString`.[^o25] Linear rings (or `LinearRing`) are closed and simple polygonal chains used to build polygon geometries.

:::
