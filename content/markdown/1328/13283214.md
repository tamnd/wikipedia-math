# Jurjen Ferdinand Koksma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jurjen Ferdinand Koksma"}

*Image: Jurjen Koksma, 1930.jpg.* Jurjen Koksma, 1930

*Image: Koksma Titelblatt.jpg.* *Diophantische Approximationen* (1936), title page.

**Jurjen Ferdinand Koksma** (21 April 1904, [Schoterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heerenveen) – 17 December 1964, [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam)) was a Dutch [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specialized in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513).[^o2]

:::

Koksma received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) degree (*[cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cum_laude)*) in 1930 at the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen) under supervision of [Johannes van der Corput](wikipedia://en/3791412), with a thesis on *Systems of Diophantine Inequalities*. Around the same time, aged 26, he was invited to become full professor at the *[Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrije_Universiteit_Amsterdam)*. He accepted and in 1930 became the first professor in mathematics at this university.[^o4] Koksma is also one of the founders of the Dutch *Mathematisch Centrum* (today *[Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica](wikipedia://en/506063)*).

One of Koksma's main works was the book *Diophantische Approximationen*, published in 1936 by Springer. He also wrote several papers with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284).

In 1950 he became member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o5]

Koksma had two brothers, Jan and Marten, who were also mathematicians.
