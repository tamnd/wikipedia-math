# Christopher Deninger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Christopher Deninger"}

**Christopher Deninger** (born 8 April 1958) is a German mathematician at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster). Deninger's research focuses on [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), including applications to [*L*-functions](wikipedia://en/245283).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Deninger obtained his doctorate from the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne) in 1982, under the supervision of [Curt Meyer](wikipedia://en/32348478). In 1992 he shared a [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize) with [Michael Rapoport](wikipedia://en/26629548), [Peter Schneider](wikipedia://en/64772763) and [Thomas Zink](wikipedia://en/33323824). In 1998 he was a [plenary speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 1998 in Berlin.[^o5] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o6]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

### Artin–Verdier duality {#Artin–Verdier_duality}

In a series of papers between 1984 and 1987, Deninger studied extensions of [Artin–Verdier duality](wikipedia://en/33899975). Broadly speaking, Artin–Verdier duality, a consequence of [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695), is an arithmetic analogue of [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732), a [duality](wikipedia://en/609737) for [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) on a compact manifold. In this parallel, the ([spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) of the) ring of integers in a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) corresponds to a [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257). Following work of [Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679), Deninger (1984) extended Artin–Verdier duality to [function fields](wikipedia://en/447645). Deninger then extended these results in various directions, such as non-torsion sheaves (1986), [arithmetic surfaces](wikipedia://en/31776898) (1987), as well as higher-dimensional [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375) (with Wingberg, 1986). The appearance of [Bloch's](wikipedia://en/25794633) [motivic complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivic_complex) considered in the latter papers influenced work of several authors including (Geisser 2010), who identified Bloch's complexes to be the dualizing complexes over higher-dimensional schemes.

### Special values of *L*-functions {#Special_values_of_L-functions}

Another group of Deninger's papers studies [*L*-functions](wikipedia://en/245283) and their special values. A classical example of an *L*-function is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) ζ(*s*), for which formulas such as

[ζ(2) = π<sup>2</sup> / 6](wikipedia://en/490303)

are known since Euler. In a landmark paper, (Beilinson 1984) had proposed a set of far-reaching conjectures describing the special values of *L*-functions, i.e., the values of *L*-functions at integers. In very rough terms, [Beilinson's conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beilinson_conjecture) assert that for a smooth projective [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *X* over **Q**, [motivic cohomology](wikipedia://en/809314) of *X* should be closely related to [Deligne cohomology](wikipedia://en/31615715) of *X*. In addition, the relation between these two cohomology theories should explain, according to Beilinson's conjecture, the pole orders and the values of

*L*(*h*<sup>*n*</sup>(*X*), *s*)

*Image: BorromeanRings.svg.* Any two of the three [Borromean rings](wikipedia://en/873118) can be pulled apart, yet the three rings are linked. The Massey product of the three cohomology classes given by winding around each circle can be used to capture this phenomenon algebraically.

at integers *s*. Bloch and Beilinson proved essential parts of this conjecture for *h*<sup>1</sup>(*X*) in the case where *X* is an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) with [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183) and *s*=2. In 1988, Deninger & Wingberg gave an exposition of that result. In 1989 and 1990, Deninger extended this result to certain elliptic curves considered by Shimura, at all *s*≥2. Deninger & Nart (1995) expressed the [height pairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_pairing), a key ingredient of Beilinson's conjecture, as a natural pairing of [Ext-groups](wikipedia://en/840758) in a certain category of motives. In 1995, Deninger studied [Massey products](wikipedia://en/2676948) in Deligne cohomology and conjectured therefrom a formula for the special value for the *L*-function of an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) at *s*=3, which was subsequently confirmed by (Goncharov 1996). As of 2018, Beilinson's conjecture is still wide open, and Deninger's contributions remain some of the few cases where Beilinson's conjecture has been successfully attacked (surveys on the topic include Deninger & Scholl (1991), (Nekovář 1994)).

### *L*-functions via regularized determinants {#L-functions_via_regularized_determinants}

The Riemann ζ-function is defined using a [product of Euler factors](wikipedia://en/398540)

$$\zeta_p(s) := \frac 1 {1-p^{-s}}$$

for each prime number *p*. In order to obtain a functional equation for ζ(*s*), one needs to multiply them with an additional term involving the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316):

$$\zeta_\infty(s) := 2^{-1/2} \pi^{-s/2} \Gamma(s/2).$$

More general *L*-functions are also defined by Euler products, involving, at each finite place, the determinant of the [Frobenius endomorphism](wikipedia://en/1088270) acting on [l-adic cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-adic_cohomology) of some [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *X* / **Q**, while the Euler factor for the infinite place are, according to [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), products of Gamma functions depending on the [Hodge structures](wikipedia://en/10297305) attached to *X* / **Q**. (Deninger 1991) expressed these Γ-factors in terms of [regularized determinants](wikipedia://en/2703380) and moved on, in 1992 and in greater generality in 1994, to unify the Euler factors of *L*-functions at both finite and infinite places using regularized determinants. For example, for the Euler factors of the Riemann zeta-function this uniform description reads

$$\zeta_p(s) = \det{}_\infty \left (\frac 1{2 \pi}(s - \Theta) | R_p ) \right)^{-1}.$$

Here *p* is either a prime number or infinity, corresponding to the non-Archimedean Euler factors and the Archimedean Euler factor respectively, and *R*<sub>*p*</sub> is the space of finite real valued Fourier series on **R**/log(*p*)**Z** for a prime number *p*, and *R*<sub>∞</sub> = **R**\[exp(−2*y*)\]. Finally, Θ is the derivative of the **R**-action given by shifting such functions. Deninger (1994) also exhibited a similar unifying approach for ε-factors (which express the ratio between completed *L*-functions at *s* and at 1−*s*).

### The arithmetic site {#The_arithmetic_site}

These results led Deninger to propose a program concerning the existence of an "arithmetic site" *Y* associated to the [compactification](wikipedia://en/6617) of [Spec](wikipedia://en/37291) **Z**. Among other properties, this site would be equipped with an [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of **R**, and each prime number *p* would correspond to a closed orbit of the **R**-action of length log(*p*). Moreover, analogies between formulas in analytic number theory and dynamics on [foliated spaces](wikipedia://en/371227) led Deninger to conjecture the existence of a foliation on this site. Moreover, this site is supposed to be endowed with an infinite-dimensional cohomology theory such that the *L*-function of a motive *M* is given by

$$L(M, s) = \prod_{i=0}^2 \det{}_\infty \left (\frac 1 {2\pi}(s-\Theta)|H^i_c(Y, F(M)) \right ).$$

Here *M* is a [motive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motive_%28mathematics%29), such as the motives *h*<sup>*n*</sup>(*X*) occurring in Beilinson's conjecture, and *F*(*M*) is conceived to be the sheaf on *Y* attached to the motive *M*. The operator Θ is the [infinitesimal generator](wikipedia://en/17945) of the [flow](wikipedia://en/2619735) given by the **R**-action. The [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125) would be, according to this program, a consequence of properties parallel to the positivity of the intersection pairing in [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590). A version of the [Lefschetz trace formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefschetz_trace_formula) on this site, which would be part of this conjectural setup, has been proven by other means by Deninger (1993). In 2010, Deninger proved that classical conjectures of Beilinson and Bloch concerning the [intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602) of [algebraic cycles](wikipedia://en/9520954) would be further consequences of his program.

This program was surveyed by Deninger in his talks at the [European Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in 1992, at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1998, and also by (Leichtnam 2005). In 2002, Deninger constructed a foliated space which corresponds to an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), and (Hesselholt 2016) showed that the Hasse-Weil zeta-function of a smooth proper variety over **F**<sub>*p*</sub> can be expressed using regularized determinants involving [topological Hochschild homology](wikipedia://en/10499629). In addition, the analogy between knots and primes has been fruitfully studied in [arithmetic topology](wikipedia://en/23834912). However, as of 2018, the construction of a foliated space corresponding to Spec **Z** remains elusive.

### Vector bundles on *p*-adic curves {#Vector_bundles_on_p-adic_curves}

A series of joint papers with Annette Werner examines [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) on *p*-adic curves. A classical result motivating this study is the [Narasimhan–Seshadri theorem](wikipedia://en/35098864), a cornerstone of the [Simpson correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpson_correspondence). It asserts that a vector bundle on a compact [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) *X* is [stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_bundle) if it arises from a [unitary representation](wikipedia://en/292788) of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) π<sub>1</sub>(*X*).

In Deninger & Werner (2005) established a [*p*-adic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_numbers) analogue thereof: for a smooth projective [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) over **C**<sub>*p*</sub>, obtained by base change from $X / \overline \mathbf Q_p$, they constructed an action of the [etale fundamental group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etale_fundamental_group) π<sub>1</sub>(X) on the fibers on certain vector bundles, including those of degree 0 and having potentially strongly semistable reduction. In another paper of 2005, they related the resulting representations of the fundamental group of the curve *X* with representations of the [Tate module](wikipedia://en/3094921) of the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) of *X*. In 2007 and 2010 they continued this work by showing that such vector bundles form a [Tannakian category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannakian_category) which amounts to identifying this class of vector bundles as a category of representations of a certain group.

### Foliations and the Heisenberg group {#Foliations_and_the_Heisenberg_group}

In several joint papers, Deninger and Wilhelm Singhof studied quotients of the *n*-dimensional [Heisenberg group](wikipedia://en/448518) *H* by the standard [lattice](wikipedia://en/15782871) consisting of integer-valued matrices,

*X* = *H* / Γ,

from various points of view. In 1984, they computed the [e-invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-invariant) of *X* in terms of ζ(−*n*), which leads to a construction of elements in the [stable homotopy groups of spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_homotopy_groups_of_spheres) of arbitrarily large order. In 1988, they used methods of [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) to give estimates on the dimension of the [cohomology](wikipedia://en/2096669) of [nilpotent Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/3358867).

The classical fact from [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590) that any cohomology class on a Kähler manifold admits a unique [harmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_form) had been generalized by (Álvarez López Kordyukov 2001) to Riemannian [foliations](wikipedia://en/371227). Deninger & Singhof (2001) show that foliations on the above space *X*, which satisfy only slightly weaker conditions, do not admit such Hodge theoretic properties. In another joint paper from 2001, they established a dynamical Lefschetz trace formula: it relates the trace of an operator on harmonic forms the local traces appearing at the closed orbits (on certain foliated spaces with an **R**-action). This result serves as a corroboration of Deninger's program mentioned above in the sense that it verifies a prediction made by this program on the analytic side, i.e., the one concerning dynamics on foliated spaces.

### Entropy and Mahler measures {#Entropy_and_Mahler_measures}

Another group of Deninger's papers revolves around the space

$$X_f := (\mathbf Z \Gamma / \mathbf Z \Gamma f)\widehat{\ }\ ,$$

where Γ is a discrete group, *f* is an element of its [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) **Z**Γ, and the hat denotes the [Pontryagin dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_dual). For Γ = **Z**<sup>*n*</sup> and $f \in \mathbb Z[x_1^{\pm 1}, \dots, x_n^{\pm n}]$, (Lind Schmidt Ward 1990) had shown that the entropy of the Γ-action on *X*<sub>*f*</sub> is given by the [Mahler measure](wikipedia://en/1088425)

$$m(f) := (2 \pi i)^{-n} \int_{\mathbb R^n / \Gamma} \log |f(z_1, \dots, z_n)| \frac{ dz_1}{z_1} \dots \frac{ dz_n}{z_n}.$$

Moreover, it had been known that Mahler measures of certain polynomials were known to be expressible in terms of special values of certain L-functions. In 1997, Deninger observed that the integrand in the definition of the Mahler measure has a natural explanation in terms of Deligne cohomology. Using known cases of the Beilinson conjecture, he deduced that *m*(*f*) is the image of the symbol {*f*, *t*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *t*<sub>*n*</sub>} under the Beilinson regulator, where the variety is the complement in the *n*-dimensional [torus](wikipedia://en/504697) of the zero set of *f*. This led to a conceptual explanation for the afore-mentioned formulas for Mahler measures. (Besser Deninger 1999) and Deninger later in 2009 carried over these ideas to the *p*-adic world, by replacing the Beilinson regulator map to Deligne cohomology by a regulator map to [syntomic cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntomic_cohomology), and the logarithm appearing in the definition of the entropy by a [*p*-adic logarithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_logarithm).

In 2006 and 2007, Deninger and [Klaus Schmidt](wikipedia://en/39443367) pushed the parallel between entropy and Mahler measures beyond abelian groups, namely residually finite, countable discrete [amenable groups](wikipedia://en/657430) Γ. They showed that the Γ-action on *X*<sub>*f*</sub> is [expansive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansive_action) if and only if *f* is invertible in the *L*<sup>1</sup>-[convolution algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convolution_algebra) of Γ. Moreover, the logarithm of the [Fuglede-Kadison determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuglede-Kadison_determinant) on the [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) NΓ associated to Γ (which replaces the Mahler measure for **Z**<sup>*n*</sup>) agrees with the [entropy](wikipedia://en/9891) of the above action.

### Witt vectors {#Witt_vectors}

[Joachim Cuntz](wikipedia://en/38543091) and Deninger worked together on [Witt vectors](wikipedia://en/5573710). In two papers around 2014, they simplified the theory by giving a presentation of the ring of Witt vectors in terms of a completion of the [monoid algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid_algebra) **Z***R*. This approach avoids the universal polynomials used in the classical definition of the addition of Witt vectors.
