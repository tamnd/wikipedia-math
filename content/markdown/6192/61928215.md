# Nonabelian algebraic topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nonabelian algebraic topology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **nonabelian algebraic topology** studies an aspect of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) that involves (inevitably noncommutative) [higher-dimensional algebras](wikipedia://en/19515158).

:::

Many of the higher-dimensional algebraic structures are [noncommutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative) and, therefore, their study is a very significant part of nonabelian [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), and also of Nonabelian Algebraic Topology (NAAT),[^o12] which generalises to higher dimensions ideas coming from the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004).[^o2] Such algebraic structures in dimensions greater than 1 develop the nonabelian character of the fundamental group, and they are in a precise sense *‘more nonabelian than the groups**.[^o12][^o3] These noncommutative, or more specifically, [nonabelian](wikipedia://en/4791400) structures reflect more accurately the geometrical complications of higher dimensions than the known homology and [homotopy groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_groups) commonly encountered in classical [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).***

An important part of nonabelian algebraic topology is concerned with the properties and applications of [homotopy groupoids](wikipedia://en/297506) and [filtered spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_space). Noncommutative [double groupoids](wikipedia://en/25981912) and double [algebroids](wikipedia://en/310921) are only the first examples of such higher-dimensional structures that are nonabelian. The new methods of Nonabelian Algebraic Topology (NAAT) *"can be applied to determine [homotopy invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_invariant) of spaces, and [homotopy classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_classification) of maps, in cases which include some classical results, and allow results not available by classical methods"*. Cubical omega-groupoids, higher [homotopy groupoids](wikipedia://en/297506), [crossed modules](wikipedia://en/1977281), [crossed complexes](wikipedia://en/19351165) and [Galois groupoids](wikipedia://en/12673) are key concepts in developing applications related to homotopy of filtered spaces, higher-dimensional space structures, the construction of the [fundamental groupoid](wikipedia://en/11004) of a [topos](wikipedia://en/21391464) *E* in the general theory of topoi, and also in their physical applications in nonabelian quantum theories, and recent developments in [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312), as well as categorical and [topological dynamics](wikipedia://en/16379713).[^o4] Further examples of such applications include the generalisations of [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917) formalizations of the [noncommutative standard models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_standard_model) *via* fundamental double groupoids and [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) structures even more general than [topoi](wikipedia://en/21391464) or the lower-dimensional [noncommutative spacetimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_spacetime) encountered in several [topological quantum field theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_quantum_field_theories) and noncommutative geometry theories of quantum gravity.

A fundamental result in NAAT is the generalised, higher homotopy [van Kampen theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Kampen_theorem) proven by R. Brown, which states that *"the homotopy type of a topological space can be computed by a suitable [colimit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit) or [homotopy colimit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_colimit) over homotopy types of its pieces'**. A related example is that of van Kampen theorems for categories of [covering morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_morphism) in [lextensive categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lextensive_categories).[^o5] Other reports of generalisations of the van Kampen theorem include statements for [2-categories](wikipedia://en/2740708)[^o6] and a topos of topoi <http://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/stevel/papers/vkt.ps.gz> (2005-07-20). <https://web.archive.org/web/20050720094804/http://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/stevel/papers/vkt.ps.gz>.*** Important results in higher-dimensional algebra are also the extensions of the [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) in categories and [variable categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_categories), or indexed/'parametrized' categories.[^o7] The [Joyal–Tierney representation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyal–Tierney_representation_theorem) for topoi is also a generalisation of the Galois theory.[^o8] Thus, indexing by bicategories in the sense of Benabou one also includes here the [Joyal–Tierney theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyal–Tierney_theory).[^o9]
