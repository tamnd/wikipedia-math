# Tolerance graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tolerance graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **tolerance graph** is an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) in which every vertex can be represented by a [closed interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_interval) and a real number called its tolerance, in such a way that two vertices are adjacent in the graph whenever their intervals overlap in a length that is at least the minimum of their two tolerances. This class of graphs was introduced in 1982 by [Martin Charles Golumbic](wikipedia://en/14227148) and Clyde Monma, who used them to model [scheduling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduling_%28production_processes%29) problems in which the tasks to be modeled can share resources for limited amounts of time.

:::

Every [interval graph](wikipedia://en/363423) is a tolerance graph. The [complement graph](wikipedia://en/1175649) of every tolerance graph is a [perfectly orderable graph](wikipedia://en/21051205), from which it follows that the tolerance graphs themselves are [perfect graphs](wikipedia://en/670531).

It is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) to determine whether a given graph is a tolerance graph. However, because tolerance graphs are perfect graphs, many algorithmic problems that are hard on other classes of graphs, including [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) and the [clique problem](wikipedia://en/249254), can be solved in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) on tolerance graphs.
