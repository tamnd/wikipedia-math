# Griffith Baley Price

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Griffith Baley Price"}

**G. Baley Price** (14 March 1905, [Brookhaven, Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookhaven,_Mississippi) – 7 November 2006, [Lawrence, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Kansas)) was an American mathematician and historian of American mathematics. He was a president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).[^o2]

:::

## Career {#Career}

After graduating with an A.B. from [Mississippi College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_College) in 1925, G. B. Price went to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he received his M.A. in 1928 and his Ph.D. in 1932 under [G. D. Birkhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._D._Birkhoff) with thesis *Double Pendulum and Similar Dynamical Systems*. Apart from a period of service with the U.S. Army Air Force in England in World War II, Price was a mathematics professor at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas) from 1937 to 1975 and chair of the mathematics department from 1951 to 1970. [John L. Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_L._Kelley) recalled Price's assistance when Kelley refused a [loyalty oath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyalty_oath) at Berkeley.[^o5]

Price was the co-author of two textbooks (published in 1966 and 1968), the author of a history of the department of mathematics of the University of Kansas and several articles related to the role of mathematics and mathematicians in World War II. He was president of the Mathematical Association of America (MAA) for the two years 1957–1958 and received the MAA's distinguished service award in 1970.[^o2]

Price based his book about [multicomplex spaces](wikipedia://en/6339361) and functions on [Corrado Segre](wikipedia://en/2422061)'s work where $\mathbb{C}_n$ has n [imaginary units](wikipedia://en/52358) $i_1, \ i_2, \dots, i_n$ all of which commute. But the book primarily treats [bicomplex numbers](wikipedia://en/4802961) $\mathbb{C}_2 \ .$

Since $(i_1 i_2)^2 = +1$ the numbers $e_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + i_1i_2)$ and $e_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - i_1i_2)$ are [idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent). The idempotents provide an alternate basis for the bicomplexes:

$z_1 + z_2 i_2 = (z_1 - i_1 z_2)e_1 + (z_1 + i_1 z_2)e_2 .$ (page 19)

Differentiable bicomplex functions f are shown to correspond to a pair of differentiable complex functions f<sub>1</sub> and f<sub>2</sub>:

$f(z_1-i_1z_2) = f_1(z_1-i_1z_2)e_1 + f_2(z_1+i_1z_2)e_2 .$ (page 131)
