# Cauchy process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cauchy process"}

In [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) theory, a **Cauchy process** is a type of [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895). There are [symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric) and [asymmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetry) forms of the Cauchy process.[^o2] The unspecified term "Cauchy process" is often used to refer to the symmetric Cauchy process.[^o3]

:::

The Cauchy process has a number of properties:

1. It is a [Lévy process](wikipedia://en/1025748)[^o4][^o5][^o6]
1. It is a [stable process](wikipedia://en/38455452)[^o2][^o3]
1. It is a [pure jump process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_jump_process)[^o7]
1. Its [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) are [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824).

## Symmetric Cauchy process {#Symmetric_Cauchy_process}

*Image: CauchyProcess.png.*

The symmetric Cauchy process can be described by a [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) or [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984) subject to a [Lévy](wikipedia://en/1346871) [subordinator](wikipedia://en/38445971).[^o9] The Lévy subordinator is a process associated with a [Lévy distribution](wikipedia://en/1346871) having location parameter of $0$ and a scale parameter of $t^2/2$.[^o9] The Lévy distribution is a special case of the [inverse-gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/1653565). So, using $C$ to represent the Cauchy process and $L$ to represent the Lévy subordinator, the symmetric Cauchy process can be described as:

$$C(t; 0, 1) \;:=\;W(L(t; 0, t^2/2)).$$

The Lévy distribution is the probability of the first hitting time for a Brownian motion, and thus the Cauchy process is essentially the result of two [independent](wikipedia://en/27593) Brownian motion processes.[^o9]

The [Lévy–Khintchine representation](wikipedia://en/1025748#Lévy–Khintchine_representation) for the symmetric Cauchy process is a triplet with zero drift and zero diffusion, giving a Lévy–Khintchine triplet of $(0,0, W)$, where $W(dx) = dx / (\pi x^2)$.[^o10]

The marginal [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401) of the symmetric Cauchy process has the form:[^o2][^o10]

$$\operatorname{E}\Big[e^{i\theta X_t} \Big] = e^{-t |\theta |}.$$

The marginal [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of the symmetric Cauchy process is the [Cauchy distribution](wikipedia://en/7003) whose density is[^o10][^o11]

$$f(x; t) = { 1 \over \pi } \left[ { t \over x^2 + t^2 } \right].$$

## Asymmetric Cauchy process {#Asymmetric_Cauchy_process}

The asymmetric Cauchy process is defined in terms of a parameter $\beta$. Here $\beta$ is the [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) parameter, and its [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) must be less than or equal to 1.[^o2] In the case where $|\beta|=1$ the process is considered a completely asymmetric Cauchy process.[^o2]

The Lévy–Khintchine triplet has the form $(0,0, W)$, where $W(dx) = \begin{cases} Ax^{-2}\,dx & \text{if } x>0 \\ Bx^{-2}\,dx & \text{if } x<0 \end{cases}$, where $A \ne B$, $A>0$ and $B>0$.[^o2]

Given this, $\beta$ is a function of $A$ and $B$.

The characteristic function of the asymmetric Cauchy distribution has the form:[^o2]

$$\operatorname{E}\Big[e^{i\theta X_t} \Big] = e^{-t (|\theta | + i \beta \theta \ln|\theta| / (2 \pi))}.$$

The marginal probability distribution of the asymmetric Cauchy process is a [stable distribution](wikipedia://en/1117869) with index of stability (i.e., α parameter) equal to 1.
