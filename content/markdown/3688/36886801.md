# Vámos matroid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vámos matroid"}

*Image: Vamos matroid.svg.* The Vámos matroid; the shaded parallelograms depict its five circuits of size four

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Vámos matroid** or **Vámos cube** is a [matroid](wikipedia://en/244321) over a set of eight elements that cannot be [represented as a matrix](wikipedia://en/37165960) over any [field](wikipedia://en/10603). It is named after English mathematician [Peter Vámos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Vámos), who first described it in an unpublished manuscript in 1968.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Vámos matroid has eight elements, which may be thought of as the eight vertices of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) or [cuboid](wikipedia://en/156802). The matroid has rank 4: all sets of three or fewer elements are independent, and 65 of the 70 possible sets of four elements are also independent. The five exceptions are four-element circuits in the matroid. Four of these five circuits are formed by faces of the cuboid (omitting two opposite faces). The fifth circuit connects two opposite edges of the cuboid, each of which is shared by two of the chosen four faces.

Another way of describing the same structure is that it has two elements for each vertex of the [diamond graph](wikipedia://en/20796238), and a four-element circuit for each edge of the diamond graph.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The Vámos matroid is a [paving matroid](wikipedia://en/37729421), meaning that all of its circuits have size at least equal to its [rank](wikipedia://en/37123010).[^o7]
- The Vámos matroid is isomorphic to its [dual matroid](wikipedia://en/23515837), but it is not identically self-dual (the isomorphism requires a nontrivial permutation of the matroid elements).[^o7]
- The Vámos matroid cannot be represented over any field. That is, it is not possible to find a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), and a system of eight vectors within that space, such that the matroid of [linear independence](wikipedia://en/101863) of these vectors is isomorphic to the Vámos matroid.[^o8] Indeed, it is one of the smallest non-representable matroids,[^o9] and served as a counterexample to a conjecture of [Ingleton](wikipedia://en/43130429) that the matroids on eight or fewer elements were all representable.[^o10]
- The Vámos matroid is a [forbidden minor](wikipedia://en/36749660) for the matroids representable over a field $F$, whenever $F$ has five or more elements.[^o11] However, it is not possible to test in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) whether it is a minor of a given matroid $M$, given access to $M$ through an [independence oracle](wikipedia://en/36878315).[^o12]
- The Vámos matroid is not algebraic. That is, there do not exist a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) $L/K$ and a set of eight elements of $L$, such that the [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree) of sets of these eight elements equals the rank function of the matroid.[^o13]
- The Vámos matroid is not a secret-sharing matroid.[^o14] Secret-sharing matroids describe "ideal" [secret sharing](wikipedia://en/458253) schemes in which any coalition of users who can gain any information about a secret key can learn the whole key (these coalitions are the dependent sets of the matroid), and in which the shared information contains no more information than is needed to represent the key.[^o15] These matroids also have applications in [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869).[^o16]
- The Vámos matroid has no adjoint. This means that the [dual lattice](wikipedia://en/600618) of the [geometric lattice](wikipedia://en/17243191) of the Vámos matroid cannot be [order-embedded](wikipedia://en/762203) into another geometric lattice of the same rank.[^o17]
- The Vámos matroid can be [oriented](wikipedia://en/24338041).[^o18] In oriented matroids, a form of the [Hahn–Banach theorem](wikipedia://en/13860) follows from a certain intersection property of the flats of the matroid; the Vámos matroid provides an example of a matroid in which the intersection property is not true, but the Hahn–Banach theorem nevertheless holds.[^o19]
- The [Tutte polynomial](wikipedia://en/3892303) of the Vámos matroid is[^o20]

$$x^4+4x^3+10x^2+15x+5xy+15y+10y^2+4y^3+y^4.$$
