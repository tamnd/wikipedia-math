# Race and ethnicity in censuses

*Image: Ethnicity and race in censuses.png.* Map showing countries where the ethnicity or race of people was enumerated in at least one census since 1991 map needs updating

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Race and ethnicity in censuses"}

Many [countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countries) and national [censuses](wikipedia://en/6889) currently enumerate or have previously enumerated their populations by [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_%28human_classification%29), [ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity), [nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationality), or a combination of these characteristics.[^o2][^o3] Different countries have different classifications and census options for race and ethnicity/nationality which are not comparable with data from other countries.[^o2][^o4] In addition, many of the race and ethnicity concepts that appear on national censuses worldwide have their origins in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) or in the views of [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europeans), rather than in the views of the locals of these countries.[^o5]

:::

## Africa {#Africa}

### Angola {#Angola}

*Image: Angola Ethnic map 1970.svg.* Ethnic composition of Angola in 1970

The [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal) asked about race in colonial censuses when they controlled Angola, and they provided three options: White, Mestiço, or African/Black. Africans had to then pick either "Assimilado" (assimilated) or "Indigenato" (indigenous).[^o8] Angola has not used any racial categories since its independence in 1975.[^o8] Tribe and language for Africans were recorded only in 1950 and 1960.[^o8]

### Benin {#Benin}

The people in [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin) were enumerated by ethnicity in 1979, 1992, and 2013 but not in 2002.[^o166]

### Botswana {#Botswana}

[Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana) enumerated people by race in all censuses between 1904 and 1946, and again in the 1964 census (though not in 1956), but in no censuses after 1964.[^o11] People were enumerated by language use in 1946 and 1956 and again in 2001 and 2011.[^o11]

### Kenya {#Kenya}

Since 1945, [Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya) enumerated people by ethnicity from 1948 all the way up to its most recent census in 2009 (however, the 1999 census ethnicity figures were not made public).[^o166][^o26][^o27][^o28][^o29][^o30] There were some concerns about asking an ethnicity equation in the 2009 census since it came just a year after the disputed Kenyan presidential election of 2007 and the riots that followed it. Kenya, nevertheless, went through with asking about ethnicity in 2009.[^o31][^o32]

The number of ethnic categories and sub-categories recorded in the country's census has changed significantly over time, expanding from 42 in 1969 to more than 120 in 2019.[^o33]

### Lesotho {#Lesotho}

People in [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho) were enumerated by race between 1904 and 1976, but not after 1976.[^o35] Language was only recorded in 1946 and 1956, tribe and language for Africans were recorded only in 1950 and 1960.[^o35]

### Somalia {#Somalia}

In the pre-independence period in [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia), the colonial administrations in [British Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Somaliland) and [Italian Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somaliland) officially classified the [ethnic Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people) majority within the two territories as "[Hamitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamitic)".[^o56][^o57] The Italian Somaliland census of 1935 reported that the remaining 6.2% of the area's population consisted of "[Negroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negroid) groups", including the [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Bantu) ethnic minorities inhabiting the southern riverine region.[^o58] These census designations were at the time pre-assigned rather than based on self-reporting. Following independence in 1960, no official census was taken until 1975. This first post-independence national census only collected information on district of birth.[^o59] However, the general government literature maintained that the country's ethnic Somali majority was of Hamitic stock, or alternately of [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs) heritage.[^o60] Similarly, Article 3 of the [Provision Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Somalia) adopted in 2012 indicates that Somalia is an Arab nation.[^o61] In 2014, the Ministry of Planning and International Cooperation also announced that it would release a new national population census before 2016.[^o62]

### South Africa {#South_Africa}

*Image: South Africa 2011 White population proportion map.svg.* Whites as a percentage of the total South African population in 2011

[South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa) has enumerated people by race in all of its censuses since 1904.[^o64] Five population group options are provided in response to the question "How would *(name)* describe him/herself in terms of population group?": "Black African", "Coloured", "Indian or Asian", "White" and "Other".[^o65] South Africa has also asked about language use since 1921 for [white South Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_South_African) and since 1936 for all races.[^o64]

### Uganda {#Uganda}

### Zambia {#Zambia}

People in [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia) were not continuously counted by race before independence, and when they were counted by race it were primarily the non-Africans/non-blacks who were counted.[^o77] People were counted by language only in 1931, while they were counted for ethnicity at various points since 1969.[^o77] The 1969 census asked about language but not ethnic group, and the 1974 census asked about ethnic group, but not language. The 1980 census asked about ethnic group, mother tongue, and language of most frequent use, while the 1990 census asked about ethnic group and mother tongue. The 2000 census asked about ethnic group, language of most frequent use, and second language.[^o77]

### Zimbabwe {#Zimbabwe}

People in [Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe) were enumerated by race between 1901 and 2012, but many censuses were done separately for Whites/Europeans and Blacks/Africans before the 1970s.[^o79] People were enumerated by language only in 1982 (when they were enumerated by "\[their\] father's dialect").[^o79]

### India {#India}

When it was a [British colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India), India enumerated its population by race between 1872 and 1941.[^o98]

In 1872 the first British Indian colonial census classified the population into racial groups through their religion and castes. Later the census also included the categories of tribe and race, although Political Scientist Ram B. Bhagat states that “the distinction between them was often obscure”.[^o111] Bhagat also claims that these classes were efforts to try to “identify a race within religion and religion within a race”.[^o112] After the 1872 census, racial categories were only used for the [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians) population. The five main categories used for this group were Native Indians, [Asiatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatics), non- Asiatics, Mixed Races, and [Mohammadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammedan).

Drawing from the 1891 British Indian census, the top religious practices were [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) at 72.32% of the population, [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam) at 19.96%, and [tribal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal_religions_in_India) at 3.23%.[^o113] Although these primary religious groups are connected to various races and castes, they created a foundation for enumerating racial groups in India. Writing in 1908, British [anthropologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropologist) and [physiognomist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiognomy) [Herbert Hope Risley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hope_Risley) stated that “the population of India consisted of seven basic types: [Mongoloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongoloid), [Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_peoples), [Indo-Aryans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_peoples), [Turko-Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turko-Iranian), Mongolo-Dravidian, Aryo-Dravidian’ Scytho-Dravidian".[^o114] These do not just highlight the classifications for race but also how British India viewed these groups as racially distinctive.

### Russia/Soviet Union {#Russia/Soviet_Union}

*Image: Ethnic map of Russia 2010.png.* Dominant ethnicity in each [Russian oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_oblast) in 2010

The [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) began counting people by native tongue with [its first modern census in 1897](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1897_census).[^o82] In 1920, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) replaced this question with a question about ethnicity.[^o138] The Soviet government organized lists of ethnicities and wanted to shape these various ethnic groups in the mold of [Communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism).[^o139] In the words of Soviet politician [Anastas Mikoyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastas_Mikoyan), the Soviet Union was "creating and organising new nations"[^o139] The government of the U.S.S.R. was eager to get ethnic data for many ethnic groups in order to create republics and autonomous regions for many of these ethnic groups and nationalities, and later on (under [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)) in order to deport some of them.[^o140][^o141] The [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) also wanted to get more support from ethnic minority groups within the Soviet Union, many of whom were previously oppressed under [Tsarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Tsars) rule.[^o141] The Soviet Union continued to ask about ethnicity for the rest of its existence, and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) also asked about ethnicity in its two post-Soviet censuses of 2002 and 2010.[^o142] Unlike in 1989, the 2002 census did not require respondents to prove their ethnicity/nationality when they responded to this question.[^o143] The number of ethnicity/nationality options available on Soviet censuses was enormous—the Soviet Union offered 194 different choices for ethnicity/nationality in its 1926 census.[^o144] There were 97 options in 1939, 126 options in 1959, 122 options in 1970, 123 options in 1979, and 128 options for the Soviet Union in 1989.[^o144] There were 192 ethnicity/nationality options for Russia in 2002.[^o144]

### France {#France}

[France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) has not counted individuals by race or ethnicity since at least 1978, when a law was passed that prevented individuals from being enumerated by these categories without their consent or a state committee waiver.[^o193][^o194] The reasons for this are that many French people consider asking people about race and ethnicity to be a contradiction of their principle of [equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_equality) and equal treatment for all French people.[^o84] Also, there is a desire to avoid repeating what [Vichy France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France) [did in regards to its Jewish population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_occupied_France) and to prevent the [National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_%28France%29) from getting more popularity.[^o84][^o193] Former [French President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_President) [Nicolas Sarkozy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Sarkozy) and his administration supported and proposed counting French people by race and ethnicity, though this was never implemented.[^o195][^o196]

### United States {#United_States}

*Image: Census-2000-Data-Top-US-Ancestries-by-County.svg.* The plurality (not [majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority)) ethnic ancestry in each [county in the United States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_%28United_States%29)

Before the [U.S. acquired independence from Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), many of the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) conducted [censuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censuses) of their own where they enumerated their populations by [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_%28human_classification%29).[^o306][^o307] The [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) as a whole [enumerated people by race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_ethnicity_in_the_United_States_Census) in every [census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census) since [its first census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census,_1790) in 1790.[^o308] Part of the reason why the United States began counting people by race much sooner than many other countries was due to the [Three Fifths Compromise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Fifths_Compromise), which determined the amount of representation that Southern states had based on how many slaves they had (slaves were counted as three-fifths of a person for representation purposes, and a new census every ten years was needed to enumerate the current slave population).[^o309] Initially, the [United States Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census) only counted Americans as either [white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_American) or [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American), with blacks classified as either [free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_people_of_color) or [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaves) until 1860 ([slavery in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States) was [abolished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) in 1865).[^o310] Over time, the United States has also added some other racial categories as well.[^o310] [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian-American) and [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) categories were added in 1860.[^o310] However, the first census to enumerate all Native Americans occurred in 1890.[^o310] Before that point Native Americans living on [reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_reservation) were not counted, and thus most Native Americans in the U.S. were not counted in census data before 1890.[^o310] Between 1850 and 1870, and in 1890, 1910, and 1920, the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census_Bureau) also enumerated [Mulattoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulattoes) and sometimes other partially black groups ([Quadroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadroons) and [Octoroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octoroons)) separately.[^o310]

The [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census_Bureau) counted [Mexicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexicans) as a separate race in 1930[^o311] and tried to retroactively determine the number of Mexicans in the U.S. in 1910 and 1920 as well.[^o310]

President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) promoted a ["good neighbor" policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Neighbor_policy) that sought better relations with Mexico. In 1935, a [federal judge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_judge) ruled that three Mexican immigrants were ineligible for citizenship because they were not white, as required by federal law. Mexico protested, and Roosevelt decided to circumvent the decision and make sure the federal government treated Hispanics as white. The [State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._State_Department), the Census Bureau, the [Labor Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Labor_Department), and other [government agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_agencies) therefore made sure to uniformly classify people of Mexican descent as white. This policy encouraged the [League of United Latin American Citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_United_Latin_American_Citizens) in its quest to minimize discrimination by asserting their whiteness.[^o312]

Hispanics were counted as whites in 1940, but for the first time ever, the U.S. made an attempt to measure the size of the Hispanic population that year.[^o310] The U.S. resumed enumerating its [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic)/Latino population in 1970, with Hispanics being enumerated in every U.S. census since then.[^o310] The U.S. Census Bureau also began offering Hispanics several sub-group options from which they could identify themselves, such as Mexican, Puerto Rican, Cuban, et cetera (these Hispanic sub-group options have changed over time).[^o313][^o314] The Other race category was added in 1950, and in 2000 multi-race responses were tabulated for the first time (instructions now explicitly inform respondents that they can choose multiple race boxes).[^o310] Because multiracial respondents are now allowed, U.S. Census data since 2000 is not directly comparable with that of previous censuses.[^o310] While people nowadays are enumerated by race based on self-identification, until 1950 their race on the census was mainly determined by their census enumerator.[^o310] During this time multiracial people who were White and of another race were usually marked down as belonging to the other race due to the [One drop rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_drop_rule).[^o310] The instructions provided to enumerators endorsed this practice.

It is worth noting that some of the definitions of race in the United States changed over time. For instance, [Indian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Americans) were previously marked as Hindu in 1920–1940, Other race in 1950–1960, and White in 1970 before being marked as Asian (Indian) since 1980.[^o310] The [U.S. census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._census) counted certain [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_people) ethnic groups separately since 1870, initially counting only [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Americans) and [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Americans), but having other categories as well since 1910.[^o310] It only began counting all people by ethnic ancestry since [the 1980 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census,_1980), though.[^o315] In addition to a Hispanic/Latino *ethnicity* question that has appeared on the census short form since 1980 (based on the US [Office of Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Management_and_Budget)'s official categories specified in 1977). In addition, at various times the Census Bureau has also used an *ancestry* question. Before 1970, [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska) and [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii) had different choices for race on their censuses in contrast to the [continental United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_United_States).[^o310]

The United States has also used language as a way to classify people by race or ethnicity.[^o316] From 1910 to 1940, the Census recorded the mother tongue of the foreign-born population and their children.[^o317] In 1960 and 1970 the Census Bureau also used mother tongue to classify respondents by ethnicity, but the definition of mother tongue was not consistent. Since 1980, the Census Bureau has tabulated [language spoken at home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_Spoken_at_Home) for people aged five or older.[^o318]

The [2020 United States Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_Census) might allow [Middle Easterners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Easterners) and [North Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_American) to write in their ethnicity/race instead of merely marking them as White.[^o319][^o320] Right now, and in the past, [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Americans) have been marked in the U.S. Census as White.[^o319] This began in the early twentieth century when [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs) [coming to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_the_United_States) successfully petitioned to be marked as White in order to avoid entry quotas and have a greater chance of achieving success and avoiding discrimination.[^o319][^o321]

The United States is one of the countries that uses racial and ethnic census data in order to create [minority-majority districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority-majority_districts), as is required by the 1965 [Voting Rights Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_Rights_Act).[^o322] These districts were created in order to increase minority representation in the [United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress) following the end of legal [segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation) and racial discrimination in the [Southern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_United_States).[^o322]

Since 1850, the United States enumerated its population by their country of birth of its population.[^o323] The whole U.S. population was enumerated by country of birth between 1850 and 1930 and again from 1960 to the present day.[^o323] Meanwhile, only the [White population of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans) was enumerated by their country of birth in 1940 and 1950.[^o323]

## Oceania {#Oceania}

### Australia {#Australia}

Australia enumerated its population by *race* between 1911 and 1966, by *racial origin* in 1971 and 1976, by *ethnic* origin in 1981, and by *ancestry* since 1986.[^o326]

### Fiji {#Fiji}

The population of [Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiji) was enumerated by ethnicity from 1881 all the way up to Fiji's most recent census in 2007.[^o328]

### Nauru {#Nauru}

The population of [Nauru](wikipedia://en/21302) was enumerated by ethnicity between 1947 and 1977.[^o166]

### New Zealand {#New_Zealand}

*Image: MaoriPopulationPercentage.png.* Maoris as a percentage of the total population of New Zealand in 2006

[New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand) enumerated people by ethnicity from 1858 to the present day.[^o331] People were enumerated by tribal affiliation in 1901 and again from 1991 to the present day.[^o331]

### Palau {#Palau}

[Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau) enumerated people by ethnicity in 1995.[^o166]

### Papua New Guinea {#Papua_New_Guinea}

[Papua New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea) only enumerated its population by ethnicity in 1971.[^o166]

## South America {#South_America}

### Argentina {#Argentina}

Argentina enumerated its population by their country of birth from 1869 to the present day.[^o336]

### Bolivia {#Bolivia}

*Image: Pueblos originarios de Bolivia.png.* Ethnic composition of Bolivia

[Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivia) enumerated people by ethnicity in 1950,[^o166] 2001,[^o338] and 2012.[^o166][^o339] The 2012 census included 40 ethnic group options.[^o339] However, it did not include [Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) as an option, which critics of current [Bolivian President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_President) [Evo Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evo_Morales) say was because he wants more Bolivians to identify as one of the indigenous groups in order to lend more legitimacy to his indigenous-friendly policies.[^o339]

### Brazil {#Brazil}

*Image: Brancos no Brasil.png.* Brazilian states according to the percentage of [Whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whites) in 2009

The [Brazilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil) census [enumerated people by race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_ethnicity_in_Brazil) in all censuses since [1872](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1872_Brazilian_Census) with the exception of 1900, 1920, and 1970.[^o342] The Brazilian census classifies people by race as either white, black, pardo (brown), yellow (Asian), or indigenous.

### Chile {#Chile}

[Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile) enumerated people by ethnicity in 1992.[^o166] The 2017 census included an option to identify as part of [one of the country's indigenous groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_in_Chile).

### Guyana {#Guyana}

[Guyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyana) enumerated people by ethnicity from at least 1946 until at least 2002.[^o166][^o345]
