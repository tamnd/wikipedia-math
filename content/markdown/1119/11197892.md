# Simplicial volume

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simplicial volume"}

In the mathematical field of [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), the **simplicial volume** (also called **Gromov norm**) is a measure of the topological complexity of a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). More generally, the **simplicial norm** measures the complexity of [homology classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_class).

:::

Given a [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) and oriented manifold, one defines the simplicial norm by minimizing the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients over all singular chains homologous to a given cycle. The simplicial volume is the simplicial norm of the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440).[^o2][^o3]

It is named after [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29), who introduced it in 1982. With [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190), he proved that the simplicial volume of a finite volume [hyperbolic manifold](wikipedia://en/1589135) is proportional to the [hyperbolic volume](wikipedia://en/8158628).[^o2]

The simplicial volume is equal to twice the [Thurston norm](wikipedia://en/51024718).[^o4]

Thurston also used the simplicial volume to prove that hyperbolic volume decreases under [hyperbolic Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/4450009).[^o5]
