# First Hardy–Littlewood conjecture

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="First Hardy–Littlewood conjecture"}

**First Hardy–Littlewood conjecture.**

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="First Hardy–Littlewood conjecture"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **first Hardy–Littlewood conjecture**[^n1] states the asymptotic formula for the number of [prime *k*-tuples](wikipedia://en/11421646) less than a given magnitude by generalizing the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692). It was first proposed by [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) and [J. E. Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371) in 1923.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $m_1, m_2, \ldots, m_k$ be positive even integers such that the numbers of the sequence $P = (p, p + m_1, p + m_2, \ldots , p + m_k)$ do not form a complete residue class with respect to any prime and let $\pi_{P}(n)$ denote the number of primes $p$ less than $n$ so that $p + m_1, p + m_2, \ldots , p + m_k$ are all prime. Then[^n1][^n2]

$$\pi_P(n)\sim C_P\int_2^n \frac{dt}{\log^{k+1}t},$$

where

$$C_P=2^k \prod_{q \text{ prime,} \atop q \ge 3}\frac{1-\frac{w(q; m_1, m_2, \ldots , m_k)}q}{\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{k+1}}$$

is a product over odd primes and $w(q; m_1, m_2, \ldots , m_k)$ denotes the number of distinct residues of $0, m_1, m_2, \ldots , m_k$ modulo $q$.

The case $k=1$ and $m_1=2$ is related to the [twin prime conjecture](wikipedia://en/41997). Specifically if $\pi_2(n)$ denotes the number of twin primes less than *n* then

$$\pi_2(n)\sim C_2 \int_2^n \frac{dt}{\log^2 t},$$

where

$$C_2 = 2\prod_{\textstyle{q \text{ prime,}\atop q \ge 3}} \left(1 - \frac{1}{(q-1)^2} \right) \approx 1.320323632\ldots$$

is the twin prime constant.[^n2]

:::

## Skewes' number {#Skewes'_number}

The [Skewes' numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skewes'_number) for prime *k*-tuples are an extension of the definition of Skewes' number to prime *k*-tuples based on the first Hardy–Littlewood conjecture. The first prime *p* that violates the Hardy–Littlewood inequality for the *k*-tuple *P*, i.e., such that

$$\pi_P(p)>C_P \operatorname {li}_P(p),$$

(if such a prime exists) is the Skewes number for *P*.[^n2]

## Consequences {#Consequences}

The conjecture has been shown to be inconsistent with the [*second* Hardy–Littlewood conjecture](wikipedia://en/361598).[^o13]

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The [Bateman–Horn conjecture](wikipedia://en/1085053) generalizes the first Hardy–Littlewood conjecture to [polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomials) of degree higher than 1.[^n1]

:::
