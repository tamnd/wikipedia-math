# Lyudmyla Nazarova

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lyudmyla Nazarova"}

Oleksandrivna

**Lyudmyla Oleksandrivna Nazarova** (Людмила Олександрівна Назарова, published as L. A. Nazarova and also spelled Liudmila, Ludmila, or Lyudmila; born 14 May 1938 in [Vologda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vologda), [RSFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSFSR)[^o2]) is a [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine) mathematician specializing in [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).

:::

## Research {#Research}

With her husband, [Andrei Vladimirovich Roiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Vladimirovich_Roiter), Nazarova founded the theory of representations of and differentiation of [partially ordered sets](wikipedia://en/23572),A and solved the second Brauer–Thrall conjecture, [proving](wikipedia://en/82285) what became known as the Nazarova–Roiter theorem.B Her research has also included pioneering work on representations of [quivers](wikipedia://en/355100),C and on the [wild problem](wikipedia://en/49690041) in [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) classification.D

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Lyudmila Nazarova was born in a family of mathematician Olxander Nazarov. Nazarova began her studies at [Taras Shevchenko National University of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko_National_University_of_Kyiv), where she met Roiter. Together they transferred to [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_State_University), where Nazarova completed her doctorate as a student of [Dmitry Faddeev](wikipedia://en/48446570). They returned to Kiev, and Nazarova became a researcher in the Institute of Mathematics of the Academy of Sciences of Ukraine, now the [National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences_of_Ukraine). She has since retired.
