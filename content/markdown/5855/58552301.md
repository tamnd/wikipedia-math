# Σ-Algebra of τ-past

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Σ-Algebra of τ-past"}

The **σ-algebra of τ-past**, (also named **stopped σ-algebra**, **stopped σ-field**, or **σ-field of τ-past**) is a [σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/29586) associated with a [stopping time](wikipedia://en/1202193) in the theory of [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895), a branch of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).[^o10][^o11]

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\tau$ be a [stopping time](wikipedia://en/1202193) on the [filtered probability space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_probability_space) $(\Omega, \mathcal A, (\mathcal F_t)_{t \in T}, P )$. Then the [σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/29586)

$$\mathcal F_\tau:= \{ A \in \mathcal A \mid \forall t \in T \colon \{ \tau \leq t \} \cap A \in \mathcal F_t\}$$

is called the σ-algebra of τ-past.[^o10][^o11]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Monotonicity {#Monotonicity}

If $\sigma, \tau$ are two stopping times and

$$\sigma \leq \tau$$

[almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908), then

$$\mathcal F_\sigma \subset \mathcal F_\tau.$$

### Measurability {#Measurability}

A stopping time $\tau$ is always $\mathcal F_\tau$-[measurable](wikipedia://en/44055).

:::

## Intuition {#Intuition}

The same way $\mathcal{F}_t$ is all the information up to time $t$, $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ is all the information up to time $\tau$. The only difference is that $\tau$ is random. For example, if you had a [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451), and you wanted to ask, “How many times did the random walk hit −5 before it first hit 10?”, then letting $\tau$ be the first time the random walk hit 10, $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ would give you the information to answer that question.[^o12]
