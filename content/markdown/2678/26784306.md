# George Henry Livens

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Henry Livens"}

**George Henry Livens** (1886–1950) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best known for his work on [electromagnetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetics), elasticity and [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/29952).[^o2]

:::

He graduated from [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) in 1910 and was awarded the [Smith Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_Prize) in 1911 for an essay entitled "The influence on density on the position of the emission and absorption lines in a gas spectrum" and was elected fellow of [Jesus College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Cambridge). He went on to an appointment as Lecturer in Geometry at [Sheffield University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_University),[^o3] a post he held until 1919 when he was appointed [Fielden Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fielden_Chair_of_Pure_Mathematics) in [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Manchester). He left this post for a chair at the [University College of South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cardiff) in 1922 where he remained for the rest of his career. One of his students was [Rosa M. Morris](wikipedia://en/67025149).[^o4]

*Image: George Henry Livens cropped.jpg.* Livens in academic robes in Cambridge 1909

Livens's main work was in electrical theory publishing a book "The Theory of Electricity" in 1918. His work included problems concerned with the motion of charged spheres, the connection between optical properties and electron theory, leading to a new form of the electron theory of the rotatory power in solutions and isotropic media; another topic was the electron theory of metallic conduction.[^o3]
