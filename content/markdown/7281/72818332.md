# Colloquium Lectures (AMS)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Colloquium Lectures"}

The **Colloquium Lecture** of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) is a special annual session of lectures.[^o2]

:::

## History {#History}

The origins of the Colloquium Lectures date back to the 1893 International Congress of Mathematics, held in connection with the [Chicago World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_World's_Fair_%281893%29), where the German mathematician [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) gave the opening address.[^o4] After the Congress, Klein was invited by one of its organiser, his former student [Henry Seely White](wikipedia://en/5687416), to deliver a two-week-long series of lectures at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) in [Evanston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanston,_Illinois).[^o5]

In February 1896, White proposed in a letter to [Thomas Fiske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Fiske) to repeat the experience of the Evanston lectures, by organising a series of longer talks "for increasing the utility of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)".[^o6][^o2] The two of them, together with [E. H. Moore](wikipedia://en/1532860), [William Osgood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Fogg_Osgood), [Frank Cole](wikipedia://en/3417701), Alexander Ziwet, and [Frank Morley](wikipedia://en/589167), wrote later an open letter to the AMS, asking the society to sponsor an annual week-long series of Colloquium lectures focussing on a specific mathematical area, in order to complement the traditional shorter talks.[^o2]

The first official Colloquium Lectures were held in September 1896, after the AMS Summer Meetings in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), and consisted of two independent series of lectures given by [James Pierpont](wikipedia://en/2594993) and [Maxime Bôcher](wikipedia://en/3180285).[^o7] A synopse of their lectures was published in the [Bulletin of the AMS](wikipedia://en/2668304); starting from the second Colloquium in 1898, the lectures have been published entirely in book form in the *AMS Colloquium Publications* series.[^o8]
