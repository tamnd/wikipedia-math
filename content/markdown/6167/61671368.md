# Elizabeth Meckes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elizabeth Meckes"}

**Elizabeth Samantha Meckes** (1980–2020) was an American mathematician specializing in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). Her research included work on [Stein's method](wikipedia://en/12933953) for bounding the distance between probability distributions and on [random matrices](wikipedia://en/1648765). She was a professor of mathematics, applied mathematics, and statistics at [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University). She died in December 2020 after a brief battle with cancer.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Meckes went to Case Western Reserve University as an undergraduate, and graduated *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honors)* in 2001 with a bachelor's degree in mathematics and a minor in German. She remained at Case for a master's degree, which she completed in 2002. Her master's thesis, *Harmonic Maps Between Graphs*, was supervised by E. Jerome Benveniste.

Next, Meckes became a doctoral student of [Persi Diaconis](wikipedia://en/65467) at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). She completed her Ph.D. there in 2006; her dissertation was *An Infinitesimal Version of Stein’s Method*.

After postdoctoral research at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) and the [American Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/3248005), working with [Laurent Saloff-Coste](wikipedia://en/45608368), Meckes returned to Case as a faculty member in 2007.[^o4] She was tenured in 2013 and promoted to full professor in 2018.

## Books {#Books}

With her husband Mark W. Meckes, Elizabeth Meckes wrote the textbook *Linear Algebra* (Cambridge University Press, 2018).[^o4] She is also the author of *The Random Matrix Theory of the Classical Compact Groups* (Cambridge University Press, 2019).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2019, the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) (IMS) recognized Meckes as an IMS Fellow, "for contributions to Stein’s method and to random matrix theory". She was twice named a Simons Fellow in Mathematics, in 2013 and 2020. She was a Fellow of the American Institute of Mathematics, 2006–2011.

In 2023, Case Western Reserve University began hosting an annual seminar series in her honor.
