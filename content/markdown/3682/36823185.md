# Quadratically closed field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quadratically closed field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **quadratically closed field** is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) not equal to 2 in which every element has a [square root](wikipedia://en/29208).[^o2][^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The field of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) is quadratically closed; more generally, any [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) is quadratically closed.
- The field of [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) is not quadratically closed as it does not contain a square root of −1.
- The union of the [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) $\mathbb F_{5^{2^n}}$ for *n* ≥ 0 is quadratically closed but not algebraically closed.[^o8]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A field is quadratically closed if and only if it has [universal invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_invariant) equal to 1.
- Every quadratically closed field is a [Pythagorean field](wikipedia://en/31032774) but not conversely (for example, **R** is Pythagorean); however, every non-[formally real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formally_real) Pythagorean field is quadratically closed.[^o3]
- A field is quadratically closed if and only if its [Witt–Grothendieck ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witt–Grothendieck_ring) is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/26411) to **Z** under the dimension mapping.[^o8]
- A formally real [Euclidean field](wikipedia://en/12756005) *E* is not quadratically closed (as −1 is not a square in *E*) but the quadratic extension *E*($\sqrt{-1}$) is quadratically closed.[^o9]
- Let *E*/*F* be a finite [extension](wikipedia://en/11634) where *E* is quadratically closed. Either −1 is a square in *F* and *F* is quadratically closed, or −1 is not a square in *F* and *F* is Euclidean. This "going-down theorem" may be deduced from the [Diller–Dress theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diller–Dress_theorem).[^o10]

:::

## Quadratic closure {#Quadratic_closure}

A **quadratic closure** of a field *F* is a quadratically closed field containing *F* which [embeds](wikipedia://en/52119) in any quadratically closed field containing *F*. A quadratic closure for any given *F* may be constructed as a subfield of the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) *F*<sup>alg</sup> of *F*, as the union of all iterated quadratic extensions of *F* in *F*<sup>alg</sup>.[^o9]

::: {#o13 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

- The quadratic closure of **R** is **C**.[^o9]
- The quadratic closure of $\mathbb F_5$ is the union of the $\mathbb F_{5^{2^n}}$.[^o9]
- The quadratic closure of **Q** is the field of complex [constructible numbers](wikipedia://en/7439).

:::
