# U-invariant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="U-invariant"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **universal invariant** or ***u*-invariant** of a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) describes the structure of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) over the field.

:::

The universal invariant *u*(*F*) of a field *F* is the largest dimension of an [anisotropic quadratic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropic_quadratic_space) over *F*, or ∞ if this does not exist. Since [formally real fields](wikipedia://en/1067223) have anisotropic quadratic forms (sums of squares) in every dimension, the invariant is only of interest for other fields. An equivalent formulation is that *u* is the smallest number such that every form of dimension greater than *u* is [isotropic](wikipedia://en/10917170), or that every form of dimension at least *u* is [universal](wikipedia://en/5479431).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- For the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), *u*(**C**) = 1.
- If *F* is [quadratically closed](wikipedia://en/36823185) then *u*(*F*) = 1.
- The function field of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) has *u* ≤ 2; this follows from [Tsen's theorem](wikipedia://en/10129446) that such a field is [quasi-algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-algebraically_closed).[^o4]
- If *F* is a non-real [global](wikipedia://en/288358) or [local field](wikipedia://en/145375), or more generally a [linked field](wikipedia://en/39227440), then *u*(*F*) = 1, 2, 4 or 8.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- If *F* is not formally real and the characteristic of *F* is not *2* then *u*(*F*) is at most $q(F) = \left|{F^\star / F^{\star2}}\right|$, the index of the squares in the multiplicative [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of *F*.[^o8]
- *u*(*F*) cannot take the values 3, 5, or 7.[^o9] Fields exist with *u* = 6[^o10][^o11] and *u* = 9.[^o12]
- [Merkurjev](wikipedia://en/31902178) has shown that every [even](wikipedia://en/143135) integer occurs as the value of *u*(*F*) for some *F*.[^o13][^o14]
- [Alexander Vishik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Vishik) proved that there are fields with *u*-invariant $2^r+1$ for all $r > 3$.[^o15]
- The *u*-invariant is bounded under finite-[degree](wikipedia://en/4382697) [field extensions](wikipedia://en/11634). If *E*/*F* is a field extension of degree *n* then

$$u(E) \le \frac{n+1}{2} u(F) \ .$$

In the case of quadratic extensions, the *u*-invariant is bounded by

$$u(F) - 2 \le u(E) \le \frac{3}{2} u(F) \$$

and all values in this range are achieved.[^o16]

:::

## The general *u*-invariant {#The_general_u-invariant}

Since the *u*-invariant is of little interest in the case of formally real fields, we define a **general** ***u*-invariant** to be the maximum dimension of an anisotropic form in the [torsion subgroup](wikipedia://en/144052) of the [Witt ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witt_ring_%28forms%29) of **F**, or ∞ if this does not exist.[^o18] For non-formally-real fields, the Witt ring is torsion, so this agrees with the previous definition.[^o19] For a formally real field, the general *u*-invariant is either even or ∞.

::: {#o21 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

- *u*(*F*) ≤ 1 if and only if *F* is a [Pythagorean field](wikipedia://en/31032774).[^o19]

:::
