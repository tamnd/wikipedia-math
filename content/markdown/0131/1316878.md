# Destructive dilemma

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Destructive dilemma"}

**Destructive dilemma.**

If $P$ implies $Q$ and $R$ implies $S$ and either $Q$ is false or $S$ is false, then either $P$ or $R$ must be false.

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Destructive dilemma"}

**Destructive dilemma**[^o3][^o4] is the name of a [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742) [rule of inference](wikipedia://en/252311) of [propositional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus). It is the [inference](wikipedia://en/317465) that, if *P* implies *Q* and *R* implies *S* and either *Q* is false or *S* is false, then either *P* or *R* must be false. In sum, if two [conditionals](wikipedia://en/658808) are true, but one of their [consequents](wikipedia://en/804778) is false, then one of their [antecedents](wikipedia://en/804755) has to be false. *Destructive dilemma* is the [disjunctive](wikipedia://en/7962) version of *[modus tollens](wikipedia://en/18901)*. The disjunctive version of *[modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900)* is the [constructive dilemma](wikipedia://en/1316648). The destructive dilemma rule can be stated:

:::

$$\frac{P \to Q, R \to S, \neg Q \lor \neg S}{\therefore \neg P \lor \neg R}$$

where the rule is that wherever instances of "$P \to Q$", "$R \to S$", and "$\neg Q \lor \neg S$" appear on lines of a proof, "$\neg P \lor \neg R$" can be placed on a subsequent line.

## Formal notation {#Formal_notation}

The *destructive dilemma* rule may be written in [sequent](wikipedia://en/252355) notation:

$$(P \to Q), (R \to S), (\neg Q \lor \neg S) \vdash (\neg P \lor \neg R)$$

where $\vdash$ is a [metalogical](wikipedia://en/387934) symbol meaning that $\neg P \lor \neg R$ is a [syntactic consequence](wikipedia://en/37531624) of $P \to Q$, $R \to S$, and $\neg Q \lor \neg S$ in some [logical system](wikipedia://en/396102);

and expressed as a truth-functional [tautology](wikipedia://en/4495335) or [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of propositional logic:

$$(((P \to Q) \land (R \to S)) \land (\neg Q \lor \neg S)) \to (\neg P \lor \neg R)$$

where $P$, $Q$, $R$ and $S$ are propositions expressed in some [formal system](wikipedia://en/396102).

## Natural language example {#Natural_language_example}

If it rains, we will stay inside.

If it is sunny, we will go for a walk.

Either we will not stay inside, or we will not go for a walk, or both.

Therefore, either it will not rain, or it will not be sunny, or both.

## Proof {#Proof}

::: {#o9 .table cue=table}

| *Step* | *Proposition* | *Derivation* |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $P \to Q$ | Given |
| 2 | $R \to S$ | Given |
| 3 | $\neg Q \lor \neg S$ | Given |
| 4 | $\neg Q \to \neg P$ | [Transposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transposition_%28logic%29) (1) |
| 5 | $\neg S \to \neg R$ | Transposition (2) |
| 6 | $(\neg Q \to \neg P) \land (\neg S \to \neg R)$ | [Conjunction introduction](wikipedia://en/6563) (4,5) |
| 7 | $\neg P \lor \neg R$ | [Constructive dilemma](wikipedia://en/1316648) (6,3) |

:::

:::

## Example proof {#Example_proof}

The validity of this argument structure can be shown by using both [conditional proof](wikipedia://en/6562) (CP) and [reductio ad absurdum](wikipedia://en/22456146) (RAA) in the following way:

| \1. | $((P\to Q)\land (R\to S))\land(\neg Q\lor\neg S)$ | (CP assumption) |
|---|---|---|
| \2. | $(P\to Q)\land(R\to S)$ | (1: simplification) |
| \3. | $P\to Q$ | (2: simplification) |
| \4. | $R\to S$ | (2: simplification) |
| \5. | $\neg Q\lor\neg S$ | (1: simplification) |
| \6. | $\neg(\neg P\lor\neg R)$ | (RAA assumption) |
| \7. | $\neg\neg P\land\neg\neg R$ | (6: [De Morgan's Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Morgan's_Law)) |
| \8. | $\neg\neg P$ | (7: simplification) |
| \9. | $\neg\neg R$ | (7: simplification) |
| \10. | $P$ | (8: [double negation](wikipedia://en/379833)) |
| \11. | $R$ | (9: double negation) |
| \12. | $Q$ | (3,10: modus ponens) |
| \13. | $S$ | (4,11: modus ponens) |
| \14. | $\neg\neg Q$ | (12: double negation) |
| \15. | $\neg S$ | (5, 14: [disjunctive syllogism](wikipedia://en/7963)) |
| \16. | $S\land\neg S$ | (13,15: [conjunction](wikipedia://en/18152)) |
| \17. | $\neg P\lor\neg R$ | (6-16: RAA) |
| \18. | $(((P\to Q)\land(R\to S))\land(\neg Q\lor\neg S)))\to\neg P\lor\neg R$ | (1-17: CP) |
