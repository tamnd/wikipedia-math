# Advanced z-transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Advanced z-transform"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), the **advanced z-transform** is an extension of the [z-transform](wikipedia://en/171589), to incorporate ideal delays that are not multiples of the [sampling time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_rate). The advanced z-transform is widely applied, for example, to accurately model processing delays in [digital control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_control). It is also known as the **modified z-transform**.

:::

It takes the form

$$F(z, m) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} f(k T + m)z^{-k}$$

where

- *T* is the sampling period
- *m* (the "delay parameter") is a fraction of the sampling period $[0, T].$

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

If the delay parameter, *m*, is considered fixed then all the properties of the z-transform hold for the advanced z-transform.

### Linearity {#Linearity}

$$\mathcal{Z} \left\{ \sum_{k=1}^{n} c_k f_k(t) \right\} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} c_k F_k(z, m).$$

### Time shift {#Time_shift}

$$\mathcal{Z} \left\{ u(t - n T)f(t - n T) \right\} = z^{-n} F(z, m).$$

### Damping {#Damping}

$$\mathcal{Z} \left\{ f(t) e^{-a\, t} \right\} = e^{-a\, m} F(e^{a\, T} z, m).$$

### Time multiplication {#Time_multiplication}

$$\mathcal{Z} \left\{ t^y f(t) \right\} = \left(-T z \frac{d}{dz} + m \right)^y F(z, m).$$

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Final value theorem"}

### Final value theorem {#Final_value_theorem}

$$\lim_{k \to \infty} f(k T + m) = \lim_{z \to 1} (1-z^{-1})F(z, m).$$

:::

:::

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Consider the following example where $f(t) = \cos(\omega t)$:

$$\begin{aligned} F(z, m) & = \mathcal{Z} \left\{ \cos \left(\omega \left(k T + m \right) \right) \right\} \\ & = \mathcal{Z} \left\{ \cos (\omega k T) \cos (\omega m) - \sin (\omega k T) \sin (\omega m) \right\} \\ & = \cos(\omega m) \mathcal{Z} \left\{ \cos (\omega k T) \right\} - \sin (\omega m) \mathcal{Z} \left\{ \sin (\omega k T) \right\} \\ & = \cos(\omega m) \frac{z \left(z - \cos (\omega T) \right)}{z^2 - 2z \cos(\omega T) + 1} - \sin(\omega m) \frac{z \sin(\omega T)}{z^2 - 2z \cos(\omega T) + 1} \\ & = \frac{z^2 \cos(\omega m) - z \cos(\omega(T - m))}{z^2 - 2z \cos(\omega T) + 1}. \end{aligned}$$

If $m=0$ then $F(z, m)$ reduces to the transform

$$F(z, 0) = \frac{z^2 - z \cos(\omega T)}{z^2 - 2z \cos(\omega T) + 1},$$

which is clearly just the *z*-transform of $f(t)$.

:::
