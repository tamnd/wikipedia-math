# Wolfgang Spohn

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wolfgang Spohn"}

**Wolfgang Konrad Spohn** (born 20 March 1950, in [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tübingen)) is a German philosopher.[^o2] He is professor emeritus of philosophy and [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science) at the [University of Konstanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Konstanz).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Wolfgang Spohn studied philosophy, [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and philosophy of science and mathematics at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich) and acquired there the [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magister_%28degree%29) (1973) and the PhD (1976) with a thesis on the *Grundlagen der Entscheidungstheorie*.[^o5][^o6] In his time as an assistant professor, he earned the [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) (1984) with a thesis about *Eine Theorie der Kausalität*.[^o6] He held professorships at the [University of Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Regensburg) (1986–91), [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University) (1991–96), and the University of Konstanz (1996–2018).[^o5] From 2019 to 2025, he was a senior professor at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen).[^o7][^o8]

Spohn is editor of the philosophical journal *[Erkenntnis](wikipedia://en/18761111)* and was its editor-in-chief from 1988 to 2001.[^o9] [^o10] He is a founding member of the *Gesellschaft für Analytische Philosophie* and was its vice-president from 2006 to 2012.[^o11] He was a Fellow at the *[Wissenschaftskolleg zu Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study,_Berlin)* (1985/86) and a [Distinguished Visiting Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Visiting_Professor) at the [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine) (1988).[^o6] Since 2002, he is a member of the *[Deutsche Akademie der Naturforscher Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina)*[^o12] and since 2015 a member of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea)[^o6]. In 2012, he was the first outside Anglosaxon academia to win the *[Lakatos Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakatos_Award)* of the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) for his book *The Laws of Belief. Ranking Theory and its Philosophical Applications*.[^o13] In 2015, he received the Frege Prize of the *Gesellschaft für Analytische Philosophie* for outstanding achievements of a German-speaking philosopher in the field of analytic philosophy.[^o14][^o6] In 2023, he received an honorary doctorate from [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich).[^o15]

Spohn was speaker of two [DFG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Research_Foundation) Research Units *Logic in Philosophy* (1997–2003) and *What if?* (2012–2018), PI of the Collaborative Research Center 471 Variation in the Lexicon (2000–2008) und co-initiator of the DFG Priority Program SPP 1516 New Frameworks of Rationality (2011–2018), from which the interdisciplinary, philosophical-psychological Handbook of Rationality (MIT Press 2021), co-edited with Markus Knauff, emerged. He was a member of the Excellence Cluster EXC 2064 Machine Learning: New Perspectives for Science from 2019 to 2025, and since 2020 he has been directing his [Reinhart Koselleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhart_Koselleck) Project Reflexive Decision and Game Theory.[^o16][^o17]

Spohn is the youngest brother of the historical sociologist Willfried Spohn and of the mathematical physicist [Herbert Spohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Spohn).

## Research {#Research}

Spohn's work is particularly associated with his contributions to formal [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology), in particular for comprehensively developing ranking theory[^o19] since 1982, which is his theory of the dynamics of belief.[^o15] It is an alternative to [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and has similarly great philosophical significance for many related epistemological topics (such as the problem of induction and the [theory of causation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality)). Spohn's research extends to [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science), [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) and [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology), [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language) and [mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_mind), [two-dimensional semantics](wikipedia://en/11388276), [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939), and [decision](wikipedia://en/446216) and [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) (see the collection of papers[^o20]). His dissertation[^o21] and his paper "Stochastic Independence, Causal Independence, and Shieldability"[^o22] are precursors of the theory of [Bayesian networks](wikipedia://en/203996) and their causal interpretation, which became the dominating statistical theory of causality after 1990.[^o23] His paper *How to Make Sense of Game Theory*[^o24] is a forerunner of epistemic game theory, which developed into an important branch of game theory.[^o25] The novel game theoretic concept of a dependency equilibrium, which generalizes the basic notion of a [Nash equilibrium](wikipedia://en/45337), is first introduced in his paper *Dependency Equilibria and the Causal Structure of Decision and Game Situations*.[^o26] [^o27]
