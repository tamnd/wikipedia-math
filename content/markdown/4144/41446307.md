# Marty Golubitsky

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marty Golubitsky"}

**Martin Aaron Golubitsky** is an American Distinguished [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) and the former director of the [Mathematical Biosciences Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Biosciences_Institute).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Education {#Education}

Marty Golubitsky was born on April 5, 1945, in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia), [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania). He graduated with bachelor's degree in 1966 from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) and the same year got his master's there as well. He obtained his Ph.D. from [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1970 where his advisor was [Victor Guillemin](wikipedia://en/13236636).[^o5]

### Career {#Career}

#### Full-time {#Full-time}

From September 1974 to December 1976 he was an assistant professor at the [Queens College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_College) and from January of next year to August 1979 served as an associate professor there. Starting from the same month of 1979 he relocated himself to the [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University) where he became a professor and served there till August 1983. In September of the same year he held the same position at the [University of Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Houston) where he remained till November 2008. From then until 2016 he served as the director of the [Mathematical Biosciences Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Biosciences_Institute) at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) where he retains a distinguished professorship in mathematics. He affiliates himself with such organizations as the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) and [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376).[^o5] He served as the president of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) 2005–2006.[^o8] In 2012 he became an inaugural fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o9] and in 2009 a SIAM Fellow.[^o10]

#### Visiting {#Visiting}

From January 1980 to June of the same year he worked at [University of Nice Sophia Antipolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nice_Sophia_Antipolis) as a visiting professor and then from September to December of the next year worked at the [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University). Following that he worked at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), with the same position which lasted him for two months in summer of 1982 and then from January to June 1989 he worked at the [Institute for Mathematics and Applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Mathematics_and_Applications), a division of the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota). He continued to hold that position even four years later when from January to June 1993 he was working at the division of [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) called [Fields Institute](wikipedia://en/2495768). From August to November 2005 he worked at both [Newton Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_Institute) and [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) and then from January to June 2006 worked at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) as a distinguished professor. As of July 2005 he works as an adjunct professor at the [Computational and Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_and_Applied_Mathematics) division of [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University).[^o5]

## Publications {#Publications}

In 1992, he and [Ian Stewart](wikipedia://en/332183) wrote a book called *Fearful Symmetry: Is God a Geometer?* which was published by [Blackwell Publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwell_Publishers) in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford). In 1994 it was translated into Dutch by [Hans van Cuijlenborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_van_Cuijlenborg) where it came out under a title of *Turings tijger* by [Epsilon Uitgaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epsilon_Uitgaven) in [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht). In 1995 the same work was translated into [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language) by [Libero Sosio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libero_Sosio) as *Terribili simmetrie: Dio è un geometra?* and was published in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy). His second book, on which he worked with [M. Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Field) called *Symmetry in Chaos: A Search for Pattern in Mathematics, Art, and Nature* was released by [Oxford University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press), in 1992 and was followed by German translation by [Micha Lotrovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micha_Lotrovsky) in 1993 and the French one the same year by [Christian Jeanmougin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Jeanmougin) which was published by [Inter´Editions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter´Editions) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989). Besides books he also has numerous [peer-reviewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-review) articles and even was a co-editor of the *Multiparameter Bifurcation Theory, Contemporary Mathematics* which was published by [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) in 1986.[^o5]
