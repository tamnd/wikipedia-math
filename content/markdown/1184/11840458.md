# John ellipsoid

*Image: Lowner\_Ellipse.webm.* Outer Löwner–John ellipsoid containing a set of a points in $\mathbb{R}^2$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John ellipsoid"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **John ellipsoid** or **Löwner–John ellipsoid** $E(K)$ associated to a [convex body](wikipedia://en/8652020) $K$ in $n$-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$ can refer to the $n$-dimensional [ellipsoid](wikipedia://en/145381) of maximal [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) contained within $K$ or the ellipsoid of minimal volume that contains $K$.

:::

Often, the minimal volume ellipsoid is called the [Löwner](wikipedia://en/9337483) ellipsoid, and the maximal volume ellipsoid is called the John ellipsoid (although John worked with the minimal volume ellipsoid in his original paper).[^o2] One can also refer to the minimal volume circumscribed ellipsoid as the **outer** **Löwner–John ellipsoid**, and the maximum volume inscribed ellipsoid as the **inner** **Löwner–John ellipsoid**.[^o3]

The German-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Fritz John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_John) proved in 1948 that each convex body in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is circumscribed by a unique ellipsoid of minimal volume, and that the dilation of this ellipsoid by factor $1/n$ is contained inside the convex body.[^o4] That is, the outer Lowner-John ellipsoid is larger than the inner one by a factor of at most $n$. For a [balanced](wikipedia://en/1734238) body, this factor can be reduced to $\sqrt{n}.$

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The inner Löwner–John ellipsoid $E(K)$ of a convex body $K \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is a [closed unit ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_unit_ball) $B$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $B \subseteq K$ and there exists an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $m \ge n$ and, for $i = 1, ..., m$, [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) $c_{i} > 0$ and [unit vectors](wikipedia://en/167053) $u_i \in S^{n-1} \cap \partial K$ (where $S$ is the unit [n-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782)) such that[^o7]

$$\sum_{i = 1}^m c_i u_i = 0$$

and, for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^n:$

$$x = \sum_{i = 1}^m c_i (x \cdot u_i) u_i.$$

:::

## Computation {#Computation}

In general, computing the John ellipsoid of a given convex body is a hard problem. However, for some specific cases, explicit formulas are known. Some cases are particularly important for the [ellipsoid method](wikipedia://en/4331203).p. 70–73

Let $E(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{a})$ be an ellipsoid in $\mathbb{R}^n,$ defined by a matrix $\mathbf{A}$ and center $\mathbf{a}$. Let $\mathbf{c}$ be a nonzero vector in $\mathbb{R}^n.$ Let $E(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c})$ be the half-ellipsoid derived by cutting $E(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{a})$ at its center using the hyperplane defined by $\mathbf{c}$. Then, the Lowner-John ellipsoid of $E(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c})$ is an ellipsoid $E(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{a})$ defined by:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{a'} &= \mathbf{a} - \frac{1}{n+1} \mathbf{b} \\ \mathbf{A'} &= \frac{n^2}{n^2-1} \left(\mathbf{A} - \frac{2}{n+1} \mathbf{bb}^\mathrm{T} \right) \end{aligned}$$

where $\mathbf{b}$ is a vector defined by:

$$\mathbf{b} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathbf{c}^\mathrm{T} \mathbf{Ac}}} \mathbf{Ac}$$

Similarly, there are formulas for other sections of ellipsoids, not necessarily through its center.

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

The computation of Löwner–John ellipsoids (and in more general, the computation of minimal-volume polynomial level sets enclosing a set) has found many applications in [control](wikipedia://en/7039) and [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics).[^o13] In particular, computing Löwner–John ellipsoids has applications in [obstacle collision detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collision_detection) for robotic systems, where the distance between a robot and its surrounding environment is estimated using a best ellipsoid fit.[^o14]

Löwner–John ellipsoids has also been used to approximate the optimal policy in [portfolio optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_optimization) problems with transaction costs.[^o15]
