# Bonnet theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bonnet theorem"}

In the mathematical field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **fundamental theorem of surface theory** deals with the problem of prescribing the geometric data of a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). Originally proved by [Pierre Ossian Bonnet](wikipedia://en/703244) in 1867, it has since been extended to higher dimensions and non-Euclidean contexts.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Bonnet's theorem"}

## Bonnet's theorem {#Bonnet's_theorem}

Any [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) has a [first](wikipedia://en/510355) and [second fundamental form](wikipedia://en/652078), which automatically are interrelated by the [Gauss–Codazzi equations](wikipedia://en/9633335). Bonnet's theorem asserts a local converse to this result.

Given an open region $D$ in $\mathbf{R}^{2}$, let $g$ and $h$ be symmetric 2-tensors on $D$, with $g$ additionally required to be positive-definite. If these are smooth and satisfy the Gauss–Codazzi equations, then Bonnet's theorem says that $D$ is covered by open sets which can be smoothly embedded into $\mathbf{R}^{3}$ with first fundamental form $g$ and second fundamental form (relative to one of the two choices of unit normal vector field) $h$. Furthermore, each of these embeddings is uniquely determined up to a [rigid motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_motion) of $\mathbf{R}^{3}$.

Bonnet's theorem is a corollary of the [Frobenius theorem](wikipedia://en/602480), upon viewing the Gauss–Codazzi equations as a system of first-order [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) for the two coordinate derivatives of the position vector of an embedding, together with the normal vector.

:::

## Related results {#Related_results}

*Image: Non-spherical\_Bonnet\_Pair.png.* A compact Bonnet pair: two non-congruent immersed real analytic tori that are related by a mean curvature preserving isometry.[^n1]

[H. Blaine Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Blaine_Lawson) and Renato de Azevedo Tribuzy showed that for a [compact surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_surface), prescribing the first fundamental form (the [induced metric](wikipedia://en/1253303)) together with a nonconstant [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210) function on the surface (the average of the principal curvatures, equivalently half the trace of the [shape operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_operator)) yields at most two compact smooth immersions in $\mathbf{R}^{3}$ up to congruence, and at most one for [genus](wikipedia://en/61891)-zero surfaces.[^n2] When two noncongruent immersions realize the same first fundamental form and the same mean curvature function, they form a compact Bonnet pair. In 2025, Bobenko, Hoffmann, and Sageman-Furnas constructed the first explicit compact Bonnet pairs, consisting of two immersed tori in $\mathbf{R}^{3}$.[^n1][^n3]

:::

## General formulations {#General_formulations}

Bonnet's theorem can be naturally formulated for hypersurfaces in a Euclidean space of any dimension, and the result remains true in this context. Furthermore, the theorem can be extended from Bonnet's local formulation to a *global formulation*, allowing $D$ to be any connected and [simply-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply-connected) [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold), with the result asserting the existence and uniqueness (up to a rigid motion) of a smooth immersion of $D$ as a hypersurface of Euclidean space with first fundamental form $g$ and second fundamental form $h$. The idea of the proof is to use the existence theory from the local formulation to construct the immersion along arbitrary curves emanating from a single point. Simple-connectedness is used to say that any two such curves with a common endpoint are [homotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopic) (through paths fixing the endpoints), and uniqueness from the local formulation implies that the value of the immersion at the endpoint must be fixed through the homotopy, so that an immersion results which is well-defined on the entire manifold.

In this global formulation, existence would not hold in general if the condition of simple-connectedness were removed. This can be seen from the nonexistence of a hypersurface immersion of the [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) whose first fundamental form is [flat](wikipedia://en/12756616) and whose second fundamental form is zero.

The theorem can also be extended, beyond the context of hypersurfaces, to the theory of [submanifolds](wikipedia://en/525095) of arbitrary [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149). This is more complicated to formulate, because in addition to the first and second fundamental forms, there is also the (generally nontrivial) connection in the [normal bundle](wikipedia://en/2370200) which must be taken into account. In this generality, the fundamental theorem of surface theory subsumes the [fundamental theorem of curves](wikipedia://en/1056003).

In this general context, the ambient Euclidean space can also be replaced by any connected and [geodesically complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesically_complete) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) of [constant curvature](wikipedia://en/3638803), which (as with the more special case of higher codimension) requires a suitably extended formulation of the Gauss–Codazzi equations.
