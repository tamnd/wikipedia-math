# Böhmer integral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Böhmer integral"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Böhmer integrals** are [integrals](wikipedia://en/15532) introduced by Paul Böhmer generalizing [Fresnel integrals](wikipedia://en/271143).[^n1]

:::

The integrals are defined for all $x\geq 0$ and $\alpha < 1$ as[^n2]

$$\operatorname{S}(x,\alpha) = \int_x^\infty t^{\alpha-1} \sin(t) \, dt, \quad \operatorname{C}(x,\alpha) = \int_x^\infty t^{\alpha-1} \cos(t) \, dt.$$

When $\alpha=1/2$, Böhmer integrals reduce to Fresnel integrals $S(x), C(x)$ by

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{S}(x) &= \frac1{2}-\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdot\operatorname{S}\left(\frac1{2},x^2\right), \\[1ex] \operatorname{C}(x) &= \frac1{2}-\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cdot\operatorname{C}\left(\frac1{2},x^2\right). \end{aligned}$$

The [sine integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine_integral) and [cosine integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine_integral) can also be expressed in terms of the Böhmer integrals by[^n2]

$$\operatorname{Si}(x) = \frac{\pi}{2} -\operatorname{S}(x,0), \quad \operatorname{Ci}(x) = - \operatorname{C}(x,0).$$
