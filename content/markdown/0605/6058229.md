# Tetradecagon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tetradecagon"}

Regular polygon stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **tetradecagon** or **tetrakaidecagon** or 14-gon is a fourteen-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621).

:::

## Regular tetradecagon {#Regular_tetradecagon}

A *[regular](wikipedia://en/333306) tetradecagon* is represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {14} and can be constructed as a quasiregular [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [heptagon](wikipedia://en/365349), t{7}, which alternates two types of edges.

The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) tetradecagon of side length *a* is given by

$$A = \frac{14}{4}a^2\cot\frac{\pi}{14} \approx 15.3345a^2$$

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

As 14 = 2 × 7, a regular tetradecagon cannot be [constructed](wikipedia://en/523430) using a [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge).[^o5] However, it is constructible using [neusis](wikipedia://en/3752339) with use of the [angle trisector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_trisector),[^o6] or with a marked ruler,[^o7] as shown in the following two examples.

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Symmetries\_of\_tetradecagon.png.* Symmetries of a regular tetradecagon. Vertices are colored by their symmetry positions. Blue mirrors are drawn through vertices, and purple mirrors are drawn through edge. Gyration orders are given in the center.

The *regular tetradecagon* has [Dih<sub>14</sub> symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry), order 28. There are 3 subgroup dihedral symmetries: Dih<sub>7</sub>, Dih<sub>2</sub>, and Dih<sub>1</sub>, and 4 [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) symmetries: Z<sub>14</sub>, Z<sub>7</sub>, Z<sub>2</sub>, and Z<sub>1</sub>.

These 8 symmetries can be seen in 10 distinct symmetries on the tetradecagon, a larger number because the lines of reflections can either pass through vertices or edges. [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) labels these by a letter and group order.[^o10] Full symmetry of the regular form is **r28** and no symmetry is labeled **a1**. The dihedral symmetries are divided depending on whether they pass through vertices (**d** for diagonal) or edges (**p** for perpendiculars), and **i** when reflection lines path through both edges and vertices. Cyclic symmetries in the middle column are labeled as **g** for their central gyration orders.

Each subgroup symmetry allows one or more degrees of freedom for irregular forms. Only the **g14** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

The highest symmetry irregular tetradecagons are **d14**, an [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560) tetradecagon constructed by seven mirrors which can alternate long and short edges, and **p14**, an [isotoxal](wikipedia://en/6289189) tetradecagon, constructed with equal edge lengths, but vertices alternating two different internal angles. These two forms are [duals](wikipedia://en/19865549) of each other and have half the symmetry order of the regular tetradecagon.

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) states that every [zonogon](wikipedia://en/55312103) (a 2*m*-gon whose opposite sides are parallel and of equal length) can be dissected into *m*(*m*-1)/2 parallelograms.[^o14] In particular this is true for [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) with evenly many sides, in which case the parallelograms are all rhombi. For the *regular tetradecagon*, *m*=7, and it can be divided into 21: 3 sets of 7 rhombs. This decomposition is based on a [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) projection of a [7-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-cube), with 21 of 672 faces. The list defines the number of solutions as 24698, including up to 14-fold rotations and chiral forms in reflection.

## Numismatic use {#Numismatic_use}

The regular tetradecagon is used as the shape of some commemorative gold and silver [Malaysian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia) coins, the number of sides representing the 14 states of the Malaysian Federation.[^o17]

## Related figures {#Related_figures}

*Image: Flag of Malaysia.svg.* The flag of Malaysia, featuring a fourteen-pointed star

A **tetradecagram** is a 14-sided star polygon, represented by symbol {14/n}. There are two regular [star polygons](wikipedia://en/313303): {14/3} and {14/5}, using the same vertices, but connecting every third or fifth points. There are also three compounds: {14/2} is reduced to 2{7} as two [heptagons](wikipedia://en/365349), while {14/4} and {14/6} are reduced to 2{7/2} and 2{7/3} as two different [heptagrams](wikipedia://en/180673), and finally {14/7} is reduced to seven [digons](wikipedia://en/5442728).

A notable application of a fourteen-pointed star is in the [flag of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Malaysia), which incorporates a yellow {14/6} tetradecagram in the top-right corner, representing the unity of the thirteen [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Malaysia) with the [federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Malaysia).

### Isotoxal forms {#Isotoxal_forms}

An [isotoxal polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotoxal_polygon) can be labeled as {p<sub>α</sub>} with outer most internal angle α, and a star polygon {(*p*/*q*)<sub>α</sub>}, with *q* is a [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113), and gcd(*p*,*q*)=1, *q*\<*p*. Isotoxal tetradecagons have *p*=7, and since 7 is prime all solutions, q=1..6, are polygons.
