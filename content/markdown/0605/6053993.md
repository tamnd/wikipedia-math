# Distributive category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Distributive category"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_%28category_theory%29) is **distributive** if it has finite [products](wikipedia://en/237868) and finite [coproducts](wikipedia://en/310889) and such that for every choice of [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) $A,B,C$, the canonical map

:::

$$[\mathit{id}_A \times\iota_1, \mathit{id}_A \times\iota_2] : A\!\times\!B \,+ A\!\times\!C \to A\!\times\!(B+C)$$

is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828), and for all objects $A$, the canonical map $0 \to A\times 0$ is an isomorphism (where 0 denotes the [initial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_object)). Equivalently, if for every object $A$ the [endofunctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endofunctor) $A \times -$ defined by $B\mapsto A\times B$ preserves coproducts up to isomorphisms $f$.[^o2] It follows that $f$ and aforementioned canonical maps are equal for each choice of objects.

In particular, if the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $A \times -$ has a right [adjoint](wikipedia://en/53991) (i.e., if the category is [cartesian closed](wikipedia://en/237037)), it necessarily preserves all [colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit), and thus any cartesian closed category with finite coproducts (i.e., any [bicartesian closed category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicartesian_closed_category)) is distributive.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) is distributive. Let $A$, $B$, and $C$ be [sets](wikipedia://en/26691). Then

$$\begin{aligned} A\times (B\amalg C) &= \{(a,d) \mid a \in A\text{ and }d \in B \amalg C\} \\ &\cong \{(a,d) \mid a \in A\text{ and }d \in B\} \amalg \{(a,d) \mid a \in A\text{ and }d \in C\} \\ &= (A \times B) \amalg (A \times C) \end{aligned}$$

where $\amalg$ denotes the coproduct in **Set**, namely the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946), and $\cong$ denotes a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942). In the case where $A$, $B$, and $C$ are [finite sets](wikipedia://en/11742), this result reflects the [distributive property](wikipedia://en/103118): the above sets each have [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) $|A|\cdot (|B|+|C|)=|A|\cdot|B| + |A|\cdot|C|$.

The categories **[Grp](wikipedia://en/800092)** and **[Ab](wikipedia://en/454748)** are not distributive, even though they have both products and coproducts.

An even simpler category that has both products and coproducts but is not distributive is the category of [pointed sets](wikipedia://en/1242444).[^o5]
