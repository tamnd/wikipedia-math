# Robert Tijdeman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Tijdeman"}

**Robert Tijdeman** (born 30 July 1943 in [Oostzaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oostzaan), [North Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Holland)) is a Dutch mathematician. Specializing in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), he is best known for his [Tijdeman's theorem](wikipedia://en/5001103). He is a professor of mathematics at the [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) since 1975, and was chairman of the department of mathematics and computer science at Leiden from 1991 to 1993. He was also president of the [Royal Dutch Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/43504918) from 1984 to 1986.[^o3]

:::

Tijdeman received his PhD in 1969 from the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), and received an honorary doctorate from [Kossuth Lajos University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Debrecen) in 1999. In 1987 he was elected to the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o3][^o5]

## Research {#Research}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **[Tijdeman's theorem](wikipedia://en/5001103)**[^o7] states that there are at most a finite number of consecutive powers. Stated another way, the set of solutions in integers *x*, *y*, *n*, *m* of the [exponential diophantine equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_diophantine_equation)

$$y^m = x^n + 1,$$

for exponents *n* and *m* greater than one, is finite.[^o8][^o9] (This was a significant step towards resolving [Catalan’s conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan’s_conjecture), which [Preda Mihăilescu](wikipedia://en/639556) accomplished in 2002.)

Tijdeman worked closely with [T.N. Shorey](wikipedia://en/39762362) on various arithmetic problems. He has also worked on algorithms in the area of [discrete tomography](wikipedia://en/6778984).
