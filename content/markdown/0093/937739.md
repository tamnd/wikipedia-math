# Time evolution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Time evolution"}

**Time evolution** is the change of state brought about by the passage of [time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time), applicable to systems with internal state (also called *stateful systems*). In this formulation, *time* is not required to be a continuous parameter, but may be [discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_time) or even finite. In [classical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_physics), time evolution of a collection of [rigid bodies](wikipedia://en/455769) is governed by the principles of [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586). In their most rudimentary form, these principles express the relationship between forces acting on the bodies and their acceleration given by [Newton's laws of motion](wikipedia://en/55212). These principles can be equivalently expressed more abstractly by [Hamiltonian mechanics](wikipedia://en/198319) or [Lagrangian mechanics](wikipedia://en/23371726).

:::

The concept of time evolution may be applicable to other stateful systems as well. For instance, the operation of a [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403) can be regarded as the time evolution of the machine's control state together with the state of the tape (or possibly multiple tapes) including the position of the machine's read-write head (or heads). In this case, time is considered to be discrete steps.

Stateful systems often have dual descriptions in terms of states or in terms of [observable](wikipedia://en/294248) values. In such systems, time evolution can also refer to the change in observable values. This is particularly relevant in [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) where the [Schrödinger picture](wikipedia://en/419200) and [Heisenberg picture](wikipedia://en/306706) are (mostly) equivalent descriptions of time evolution.

## Time evolution operators {#Time_evolution_operators}

Consider a system with state space *X* for which evolution is [deterministic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic) and [reversible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reversible_dynamics). For concreteness let us also suppose time is a parameter that ranges over the set of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) **R**. Then time evolution is given by a family of [bijective](wikipedia://en/3942) state transformations

$$(\operatorname{F}_{t, s} \colon X \rightarrow X)_{s, t \in \mathbb{R}}.$$

F<sub>*t*, *s*</sub>(*x*) is the state of the system at time *t*, whose state at time *s* is *x*. The following identity holds

$$\operatorname{F}_{u, t} (\operatorname{F}_{t, s} (x)) = \operatorname{F}_{u, s}(x).$$

To see why this is true, suppose *x* ∈ *X* is the state at time *s*. Then by the definition of F, F<sub>*t*, *s*</sub>(*x*) is the state of the system at time *t* and consequently applying the definition once more, F<sub>*u*, *t*</sub>(F<sub>*t*, *s*</sub>(*x*)) is the state at time *u*. But this is also F<sub>*u*, *s*</sub>(*x*).

In some contexts in mathematical physics, the mappings F<sub>*t*, *s*</sub> are called *propagation operators* or simply [propagators](wikipedia://en/698759). In [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586), the propagators are functions that operate on the [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) of a physical system. In [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), the propagators are usually [unitary operators](wikipedia://en/225999) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932). The propagators can be expressed as [time-ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-ordered) exponentials of the integrated Hamiltonian. The asymptotic properties of time evolution are given by the [scattering matrix](wikipedia://en/622053).[^o3]

A state space with a distinguished propagator is also called a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087).

To say time evolution is homogeneous means that

$\operatorname{F}_{u, t} = \operatorname{F}_{u - t,0}$ for all $u,t \in \mathbb{R}$.

In the case of a homogeneous system, the mappings G<sub>*t*</sub> = F<sub>*t*,0</sub> form a one-parameter [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of transformations of *X*, that is

$$\operatorname{G}_{t+s} = \operatorname{G}_{t}\operatorname{G}_{s}.$$

For non-reversible systems, the propagation operators F<sub>*t*, *s*</sub> are defined whenever *t* ≥ *s* and satisfy the propagation identity

$\operatorname{F}_{u, t} (\operatorname{F}_{t, s} (x)) = \operatorname{F}_{u, s}(x)$ for any $u \geq t \geq s$.

In the homogeneous case the propagators are exponentials of the Hamiltonian.

### In quantum mechanics {#In_quantum_mechanics}

In the [Schrödinger picture](wikipedia://en/419200), the [Hamiltonian operator](wikipedia://en/14381#Schrödinger_equation) generates the time evolution of quantum states. If $\left| \psi (t) \right\rangle$ is the state of the system at time $t$, then

$$H \left| \psi (t) \right\rangle = i \hbar {\partial\over\partial t} \left| \psi (t) \right\rangle.$$

This is the [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874).

#### Time-independent Hamiltonian {#Time-independent_Hamiltonian}

If $H$ is independent of time, then a state at some initial time ($t = 0$) can be expressed using the [unitary](wikipedia://en/225999) time evolution operator $U(t)$ is the [exponential operator](wikipedia://en/672731) as

$$\left| \psi (t) \right\rangle = U(t)\left| \psi (0) \right\rangle = e^{-iHt/\hbar} \left| \psi (0) \right\rangle,$$

or more generally, for some initial time $t_0$

$\left| \psi (t) \right\rangle = U(t, t_0)\left| \psi (t_0) \right\rangle = e^{-iH(t-t_0)/\hbar} \left| \psi (t_0) \right\rangle.$[^o6]
