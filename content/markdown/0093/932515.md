# Hermann Hankel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hermann Hankel"}

**Hermann Hankel** (14 February 1839 – 29 August 1873) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Having worked on [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) during his career, he is best known for introducing the [Hankel transform](wikipedia://en/1452960) and the [Hankel matrix](wikipedia://en/377537).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Hankel was born on 14 February 1839 in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle,_Saxony-Anhalt), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Confederation). His father, [Wilhelm Gottlieb Hankel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Gottlieb_Hankel), was a physicist. Hankel studied at Nicolai Gymnasium in [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig) before entering [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University) in 1857, where he studied with [Moritz Drobisch](wikipedia://en/31322407), [August Ferdinand Möbius](wikipedia://en/37875) and his father. In 1860, he started studying at [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where he acquired an interest in function theory under the tutelage of [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980). Following the publication of an award winning article, he proceeded to study under [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459) and [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450) in Berlin. He received his doctorate in 1862 at Leipzig University. Receiving his teaching qualifications a year after, he was promoted to an associate professor at Leipzig University in 1867. At the same year, he received his full professorship in [University of Erlangen–Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen–Nuremberg) and spent his last four years in [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen). He died from an illness on 29 August 1873 in [Schramberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schramberg), near Tübingen. He was married to [Marie Hankel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Hankel).[^o4]

In 1867, he published *Theorie der Complexen Zahlensysteme*, a treatise on [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759). His works on the theory of functions include 1870's *Untersuchungen über die unendlich oft oscillirenden und unstetigen functionen* and his 1871 article “Grenze” for the [Ersch-Gruber Encyklopädie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allgemeine_Encyclopädie_der_Wissenschaften_und_Künste). His work for *[Mathematische Annalen](wikipedia://en/1423381)* has highlighted the importance of [Bessel functions of the third kind](wikipedia://en/4700#Hankel_functions:_H%281%29α,_H%282%29α), which were later known as Hankel functions.[^o4]

His 1867 exposition on [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) and [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) is particularly memorable. For example, Fischbein notes that he solved the problem of products of [negative numbers](wikipedia://en/154616) by proving the following theorem: "The only multiplication in R which may be considered as an extension of the usual multiplication in R<sup>\+</sup> by *respecting the law of distributivity* to the left and the right is that which conforms to the rule of signs."[^o5] Furthermore, Hankel draws attention[^o6] to the [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) that [Hermann Grassmann](wikipedia://en/99242) had developed in his *Extension Theory* in two publications. This was the first of many references later made to Grassmann's early insights on the nature of [space](wikipedia://en/5308894).
