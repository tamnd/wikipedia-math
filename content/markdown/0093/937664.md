# Chebyshev's sum inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chebyshev's sum inequality"}

*Image: Chebyshev.jpg.* Chebyshev, after which the inequality is named

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Chebyshev's sum inequality**, named after [Pafnuty Chebyshev](wikipedia://en/149568), states that if

:::

$a_1 \geq a_2 \geq \cdots \geq a_n \quad$ and $\quad b_1 \geq b_2 \geq \cdots \geq b_n,$

then

$${1 \over n} \sum_{k=1}^n a_k b_k \geq \left({1 \over n}\sum_{k=1}^n a_k\right)\!\!\left({1 \over n}\sum_{k=1}^n b_k\right)\!.$$

In words, if we are given two sequences that are both non-increasing or non-decreasing, then the product of their averages is less than the average of their (termwise) product.

Similarly, if

$a_1 \leq a_2 \leq \cdots \leq a_n \quad$ and $\quad b_1 \geq b_2 \geq \cdots \geq b_n,$

then

${1 \over n} \sum_{k=1}^n a_k b_k \leq \left({1 \over n}\sum_{k=1}^n a_k\right)\!\!\left({1 \over n}\sum_{k=1}^n b_k\right)\!.$[^o2]

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Consider the sum

$$S = \sum_{j=1}^n \sum_{k=1}^n (a_j - a_k) (b_j - b_k).$$

The two [sequences](wikipedia://en/27838) are [non-increasing](wikipedia://en/27838#Increasing_and_decreasing), therefore $a_{j} - a_{k}$ and $b_{j} - b_{k}$ have the same sign for any $j, k$. Hence $S \ge 0$.

Opening the brackets, we deduce:

$$0 \leq 2 n \sum_{j=1}^n a_j b_j - 2 \sum_{j=1}^n a_j \, \sum_{j=1}^n b_j,$$

hence

$$\frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^n a_j b_j \geq \left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^n a_j\right)\!\!\left(\frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^n b_j\right)\!.$$

An alternative [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) is simply obtained with the [rearrangement inequality](wikipedia://en/937598), writing that

$$\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} b_j = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} a_i b_j =\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1} a_i b_{i+k~\text{mod}~n} = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_i b_{i+k~\text{mod}~n} \leq \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_ib_i = n \sum_i a_ib_i.$$

:::

## Continuous version {#Continuous_version}

There is also a continuous version of Chebyshev's sum inequality:

If *f* and *g* are [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued, [integrable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_function) over \[*a*, *b*\], both non-increasing or both non-decreasing, then

$$\frac{1}{b-a} \int_a^b f(x)g(x) \,dx \geq\! \left(\frac{1}{b-a} \int_a^b f(x) \,dx\right)\!\!\left(\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^b g(x) \,dx\right)$$

with the [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) reversed if one is non-increasing and the other is non-decreasing.
