# Harold Hotelling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harold Hotelling"}

**Harold Hotelling** (; September 29, 1895 – December 26, 1973) was an American [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) [statistician](wikipedia://en/26685) and an influential [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) theorist, known for [Hotelling's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotelling's_law), [Hotelling's lemma](wikipedia://en/3394448), and [Hotelling's rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotelling's_rule) in economics, as well as [Hotelling's T-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/934711) in statistics.[^o3][^o4] He also developed and named the [principal component analysis](wikipedia://en/76340) method widely used in finance, statistics and computer science.

:::

He was associate professor of mathematics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) from 1927 until 1931, a member of the faculty of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) from 1931 until 1946, and a professor of Mathematical Statistics at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) from 1946 until his death. A street in [Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina) bears his name. In 1972, he received the [North Carolina Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Award) for contributions to science.

## Statistics {#Statistics}

Hotelling is known to statisticians because of [Hotelling's T-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/934711) which is a generalization of the [Student's t-distribution](wikipedia://en/105375) in multivariate setting, and its use in statistical [hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing) and confidence regions. He also introduced [canonical correlation](wikipedia://en/363900) analysis.

At the beginning of his statistical career Hotelling came under the influence of [R.A. Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824), whose *[Statistical Methods for Research Workers](wikipedia://en/1645218)* had "revolutionary importance", according to Hotelling's review. Hotelling was able to maintain professional relations with Fisher, despite the latter's temper tantrums and polemics. Hotelling suggested that Fisher use the English word "[cumulants](wikipedia://en/359684)" for [Thiele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._N._Thiele)'s Danish "semi-invariants". Fisher's emphasis on the sampling distribution of a statistic was extended by [Jerzy Neyman](wikipedia://en/2386106) and [Egon Pearson](wikipedia://en/1643759) with greater precision and wider applications, which Hotelling recognized. Hotelling sponsored refugees from European anti-semitism and Nazism, welcoming [Henry Mann](wikipedia://en/30545778) and [Abraham Wald](wikipedia://en/1239430) to his research group at Columbia. While at Hotelling's group, Wald developed [sequential analysis](wikipedia://en/3509530) and [statistical decision theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_decision_theory), which Hotelling described as "pragmatism in action".

In the United States, Hotelling is known for his leadership of the statistics profession, in particular for his vision of a statistics department at a university, which convinced many universities to start statistics departments. Hotelling was known for his leadership of departments at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and the [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina).

## Economics {#Economics}

Hotelling has a crucial place in the growth of mathematical economics; several areas of active research were influenced by his economics papers. While at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington), he was encouraged to switch from pure mathematics toward mathematical economics by the famous mathematician [Eric Temple Bell](wikipedia://en/255236). Later, at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) (where during 1933-34 he taught [Milton Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Friedman) statistics) in the '40s, Hotelling in turn encouraged young [Kenneth Arrow](wikipedia://en/49894) to switch from mathematics and statistics applied to actuarial studies towards more general applications of mathematics in general economic theory. Hotelling is the [eponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym) of [Hotelling's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotelling's_law), [Hotelling's lemma](wikipedia://en/3394448), and [Hotelling's rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotelling's_rule) in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics).

Hotelling was influenced by the writing of [Henry George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_George) and was an editorial adviser for the [Georgist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgist) journal [AJES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Journal_of_Economics_and_Sociology).[^o7]

### Spatial economics {#Spatial_economics}

One of Hotelling's most important contributions to economics was his conception of "[spatial economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_economics)" in his 1929 article.[^o9] Space was not just a barrier to moving goods around, but rather a field upon which competitors jostled to be nearest to their customers.[^o10]

Hotelling considers a situation in which there are two sellers at point A and B in a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) of size l. The buyers are [distributed uniformly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29) in this line segment and carry the merchandise to their home at cost c. Let p<sub>1</sub> and p<sub>2</sub> be the prices charged by A and B, and let the line segment be divided in 3 parts of size a, x+y and b, where x+y is the size of the segment between A and B, *a* the portion of segment to the left of A and *b* the portion of segment to the right of B. Therefore, a+x+y+b=l. Since the product being sold is a [commodity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_%28type_of_good%29), the point of indifference to buying is given by p<sub>1</sub>+cx=p<sub>2</sub>+cy. Solving for x and y yields:

$$x=\frac{1}{2}\left( l-a-b+\frac{p_{2}-p_{1}}{c} \right)$$

$$y=\frac{1}{2}\left( l-a-b+\frac{p_{1}-p_{2}}{c} \right)$$

Let q<sub>1</sub> and q<sub>2</sub> indicate the quantities sold by A and B. The sellers profit are:

$$\pi_{1}=p_{1}q_{1}=p_{1}\left( a+x \right)=\frac{1}{2}\left( l+a-b \right)p_{1}-\frac{p_{1}^{2}}{2c}+\frac{p_{1}p_{2}}{2c}$$

$$\pi_{2}=p_{2}q_{2}=p_{2}\left( b+y \right)=\frac{1}{2}\left( l-a+b \right)p_{2}-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2c}+\frac{p_{1}p_{2}}{2c}$$

By imposing [profit maximization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profit_maximization):

$$\frac{\partial \pi_{1}}{\partial p_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\left( l+a-b \right)-\frac{p_{1}}{c}+\frac{p_{2}}{2c}=0$$

$$\frac{\partial \pi_{2}}{\partial p_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left( l-a+b \right)-\frac{p_{1}}{2c}+\frac{p_{2}}{c}=0$$

Hotelling obtains the [economic equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_equilibrium). Hotelling argues this equilibrium is [stable](wikipedia://en/363360) even though the sellers may try to establish a price [cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartel).

Hotelling extrapolates from his findings about spatial economics and links it to not just physical distance, but also similarity in products. He describes how, for example, some factories might make shoes for the poor and others for the rich, but they end up alike. He also quips that, "Methodists and Presbyterian churches are too much alike; cider too homogenous."[^o9]

### Market socialism and Georgism {#Market_socialism_and_Georgism}

intellectuals As an extension of his research in spatial economics, Hotelling realized that it would be possible and socially optimal to finance investment in public goods through a [Georgist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgist) [land value tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_value_tax) and then provide such goods and services to the public at marginal cost (in many cases for free). This is an early expression of the [Henry George theorem](wikipedia://en/13766990) that [Joseph Stiglitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stiglitz) and others expanded upon. Hotelling pointed out that when local public goods like roads and trains become congested, users create an additional marginal cost of excluding others. Hotelling became an early advocate of Georgist [congestion pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestion_pricing) and stated that the purpose of this unique type of [toll fee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_fee) was in no way to recoup investment costs, but was instead a way of changing behavior and compensating those who are excluded. Hotelling describes how human attention is also in limited supply at any given time and place, which produces a rental value; he concludes that billboards could be regulated or taxed on similar grounds as other scarcity rents. Hotelling reasoned that rent and taxation were analogous, the public and private versions of a similar thing. Therefore, the social optimum would be to put taxes directly on rent.[^o12] [Kenneth Arrow](wikipedia://en/49894) described this as [market socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_socialism), but [Mason Gaffney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason_Gaffney) points out that it is actually Georgism.[^o13] Hotelling added the following comment about the ethics of Georgist [value capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_capture): "The proposition that there is no ethical objection to the confiscation of the site value of land by taxation, if and when the nonlandowning classes can get the power to do so, has been ably defended by \[the Georgist\] [H. G. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Gunnison_Brown)."[^o12]

### Non-convexities {#Non-convexities}

Hotelling made pioneering studies of [non-convexity in economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-convexity_in_economics). In [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), *non-convexity* refers to violations of the [convexity assumptions of elementary economics](wikipedia://en/30643278). Basic economics textbooks concentrate on consumers with [convex preferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_preferences) and convex [budget sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_set) and on producers with convex [production sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_set); for convex models, the predicted economic behavior is well understood.[^o33][^o15] When convexity assumptions are violated, then many of the good properties of competitive markets need not hold: Thus, non-convexity is associated with [market failures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_failure),[^o27][^o16] where [supply and demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_and_demand) differ or where [market equilibria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_equilibrium) can be [inefficient](wikipedia://en/45468).[^o33][^o16][^o28][^o25][^o26][^o18]

#### Producers with increasing returns to scale: marginal cost pricing {#Producers_with_increasing_returns_to_scale:_marginal_cost_pricing}

In "[oligopolies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligopoly)" (markets dominated by a few producers), especially in "[monopolies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly)" (markets dominated by one producer), non-convexities remain important.[^o18] Concerns with large producers exploiting market power initiated the literature on non-convex sets, when [Piero Sraffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_Sraffa) wrote about firms with increasing [returns to scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Returns_to_scale) in 1926,[^o19] after which Hotelling wrote about [marginal&nbsp;cost pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal&nbsp;cost_pricing) in 1938.[^o20] Both Sraffa and Hotelling illuminated the [market power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_power) of producers without competitors, clearly stimulating a literature on the supply-side of the economy.[^o21]

#### Consumers with non-convex preferences {#Consumers_with_non-convex_preferences}

When the consumer's preference set is non-convex, then (for some prices) the consumer's demand is not [connected](wikipedia://en/6233). A disconnected demand implies some discontinuous behavior by the consumer as discussed by Hotelling: If indifference curves for purchases be thought of as possessing a wavy character, convex to the origin in some regions and concave in others, we are forced to the conclusion that it is only the portions convex to the origin that can be regarded as possessing any importance, since the others are essentially unobservable. They can be detected only by the discontinuities that may occur in demand with variation in price-ratios, leading to an abrupt jumping of a point of tangency across a chasm when the straight line is rotated. But, while such discontinuities may reveal the existence of chasms, they can never measure their depth. The concave portions of the indifference curves and their many-dimensional generalizations, if they exist, must forever remain in unmeasurable obscurity.[^o23][^o24]

Following Hotelling's pioneering research on non-convexities in economics, research in economics has recognized non-convexity in new areas of economics. In these areas, non-convexity is associated with [market failures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_failure), where any [equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_equilibrium) need not be [efficient](wikipedia://en/45468) or where no equilibrium exists because [supply and demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_and_demand) differ.[^o33][^o16][^o28][^o25][^o26][^o18] Non-convex sets arise also with [environmental goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_economics) and other [externalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Externality),[^o25][^o26] and with [market failures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_failure),[^o27] and [public economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_economics).[^o28][^o29] Non-convexities occur also with [information economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_economics),[^o30] and with [stock markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market)[^o18] (and other [incomplete markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incomplete_markets)).[^o31][^o32] Such applications continued to motivate economists to study non-convex sets.[^o33]
