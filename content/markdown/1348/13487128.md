# Horosphere

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Horosphere"}

*Image: 633 honeycomb one cell horosphere.png.* A horosphere within the [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836) tangent to the edges of a [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719) cell of a [hexagonal tiling honeycomb](wikipedia://en/40281224)

*Image: Apollonian spheres.jpg.* [Apollonian sphere packing](wikipedia://en/13129755) can be seen as showing horospheres that are tangent to an outer sphere of a [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836)

In [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), a **horosphere** (or **parasphere**) is a specific [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) in [hyperbolic *n*-space](wikipedia://en/318742). It is the boundary of a **horoball**, the limit of a sequence of increasing balls sharing (on one side) a tangent hyperplane and its point of tangency. For *n* = 2 a horosphere is called a [horocycle](wikipedia://en/11084460).

:::

A horosphere can also be described as the limit of the hyperspheres that share a tangent hyperplane at a given point, as their radii go towards infinity. In Euclidean geometry, such a "hypersphere of infinite radius" would be a hyperplane, but in hyperbolic geometry it is a horosphere (a curved surface).

## History {#History}

The concept has its roots in a notion expressed by [F. L. Wachter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Ludwig_Wachter) in 1816 in a letter to his teacher [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125). Noting that in Euclidean geometry the limit of a sphere as its radius tends to infinity is a plane, Wachter affirmed that even if the [fifth postulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_fifth_postulate) were false, there would nevertheless be a geometry on the surface identical with that of the ordinary plane.[^o3] The terms *horosphere* and *horocycle* are due to [Lobachevsky](wikipedia://en/648527), who established various results showing that the geometry of horocycles and the horosphere in hyperbolic space were equivalent to those of lines and the plane in Euclidean space.[^o4] The term "horoball" is due to [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190), who used it in his work on [hyperbolic 3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1237700). The terms horosphere and horoball are often used in 3-dimensional hyperbolic geometry.

## Models {#Models}

In the [conformal ball model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_disc), a horosphere is represented by a sphere tangent to the horizon sphere. In the [upper half-space model](wikipedia://en/461942), a horosphere can appear either as a sphere tangent to the horizon plane, or as a plane parallel to the horizon plane. In the [hyperboloid model](wikipedia://en/4771189), a horosphere is represented by a plane whose normal lies in the asymptotic cone.

## Curvature {#Curvature}

A horosphere has a critical amount of (isotropic) curvature: if the curvature were any greater, the surface would close, yielding a sphere, and if the curvature were any less, the surface would be an (*N* − 1)-dimensional [hypercycle](wikipedia://en/7516582).
