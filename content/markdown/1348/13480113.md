# Stein–Strömberg theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stein–Strömberg theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Stein–Strömberg theorem** or **Stein–Strömberg inequality** is a result in [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) concerning the [Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Littlewood_maximal_operator). The result is foundational in the study of the problem of [differentiation of integrals](wikipedia://en/13478284). The result is named after the [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) [Elias M. Stein](wikipedia://en/418073) and [Jan-Olov Strömberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Olov_Strömberg).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *λ*<sup>*n*</sup> denote *n*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) on *n*-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>*n*</sup> and let *M* denote the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator: for a function *f* : **R**<sup>*n*</sup> → **R**, *Mf* : **R**<sup>*n*</sup> → **R** is defined by

$$Mf(x) = \sup_{r > 0} \frac1{\lambda^{n} \big( B_{r} (x) \big)} \int_{B_{r} (x)} | f(y) | \, \mathrm{d} \lambda^{n} (y),$$

where *B*<sub>*r*</sub>(*x*) denotes the [open ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_ball) of [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) *r* with center *x*. Then, for each *p* > 1, there is a constant *C*<sub>*p*</sub> > 0 such that, for all [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) *n* and functions *f* ∈ *L*<sup>*p*</sup>(**R**<sup>*n*</sup>; **R**),

$$\| Mf \|_{L^{p}} \leq C_{p} \| f \|_{L^{p}}.$$

In general, a maximal operator *M* is said to be of **strong type** (*p*, *p*) if

$$\| Mf \|_{L^{p}} \leq C_{p, n} \| f \|_{L^{p}}$$

for all *f* ∈ *L*<sup>*p*</sup>(**R**<sup>*n*</sup>; **R**). Thus, the Stein–Strömberg theorem is the statement that the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator is of strong type (*p*, *p*) uniformly with respect to the dimension *n*.
