# George C. Papanicolaou

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George C. Papanicolaou"}

**George C. Papanicolaou** (; born January 23, 1943) is a Greek-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specializes in [applied](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833), [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), and [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895).[^o3] He is currently the Robert Grimmett Professor in Mathematics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Papanicolaou was born on January 23, 1943, in [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece). He received his [B.E.E.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.E.E.) from [Union College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_College) and his [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.S.) and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) (NYU) in 1969. His PhD thesis, supervised by [Joseph Bishop Keller](wikipedia://en/4454126) was entitled "On Stochastic Differential Equations and Applications".[^o5] At NYU, he started out as an assistant professor in 1969 before moving up to associate professor in 1973 and finally professor in 1976. Later, in 1993, he relocated to Stanford.[^o6]

He has had 42 doctoral students[^o7] and 220 descendants. He is married, with three children.[^o6]

## Publications {#Publications}

Papanicolaou has more than 250 publications[^o9][^o10] on a wide range of topics, including [imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaging), communications and [time reversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_reversal_signal_processing), waves in random media, convection-diffusion, nonlinear waves, high contrast materials, [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680), and [homogenization](wikipedia://en/23852047).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

George Papanicolaou is a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o12] and he is a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences),[^o13] the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS), and the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM). He was a plenary speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1998 and the [International Congress on Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13606431) (ICIAM) in 2003. He was awarded a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) (1974), a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) (1983), the [von Neumann Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann_Lecture) from SIAM (2006), the William Benter Prize in Applied Mathematics[^o14] (2010), the [Gibbs Lectureship](wikipedia://en/45715334) of the AMS (2011), and the Lagrange Prize[^o15] from ICIAM (2019). He received an Honorary Doctor of Science, [University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_and_Kapodistrian_University_of_Athens) in 1987 and a Doctor Honoris Causa, [University of Paris VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) in 2011.
