# Dual bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dual bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **dual bundle** is an operation on [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) extending the operation of [duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_vector_space) for [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The **dual bundle** of a vector bundle $\pi: E \to X$ is the vector bundle $\pi^*: E^* \to X$ whose fibers are the [dual spaces](wikipedia://en/7988) to the fibers of $E$.

Equivalently, $E^*$ can be defined as the [Hom bundle](wikipedia://en/276524#Operations_on_vector_bundles) *$\mathrm{Hom}(E,\mathbb{R} \times X),$* that is, the vector bundle of morphisms from *$E$* to the trivial line bundle *$\mathbb{R} \times X \to X.$*

:::

## Constructions and examples {#Constructions_and_examples}

Given a local trivialization of *$E$* with [transition functions](wikipedia://en/276526#Structure_groups_and_transition_functions) $t_{ij},$ a local trivialization of $E^*$ is given by the same open cover of *$X$* with transition functions $t_{ij}^* = (t_{ij}^T)^{-1}$ (the [inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_matrix) of the [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844)). The dual bundle $E^*$ is then constructed using the [fiber bundle construction theorem](wikipedia://en/7762521). As particular cases:

- The dual bundle of an [associated bundle](wikipedia://en/398874) is the bundle associated to the [dual representation](wikipedia://en/1442471) of the [structure group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_group).
- The dual bundle of the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) is its [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600).

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

If the base space *$X$* is [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact) and [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) then a real, finite-rank vector bundle *$E$* and its dual $E^*$ are [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) as vector bundles. However, just as for [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), there is no [natural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) choice of isomorphism unless *$E$* is equipped with an [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product).

This is not true in the case of [complex vector bundles](wikipedia://en/1538109): for example, the [tautological line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tautological_line_bundle) over the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799) is not isomorphic to its dual. The dual $E^*$ of a complex vector bundle *$E$* is indeed isomorphic to the [conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) bundle *$\overline{E},$* but the choice of isomorphism is non-canonical unless *$E$* is equipped with a [hermitian product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_product).

The [Hom bundle](wikipedia://en/276524#Operations_on_vector_bundles) *$\mathrm{Hom}(E_1,E_2)$* of two vector bundles is canonically isomorphic to the [tensor product bundle](wikipedia://en/46418707) *$E_1^* \otimes E_2.$*

Given a morphism *$f : E_1 \to E_2$* of vector bundles over the same space, there is a morphism *$f^*: E_2^* \to E_1^*$* between their dual bundles (in the converse order), defined fibrewise as the [transpose](wikipedia://en/1442470) of each linear map *$f_x: (E_1)_x \to (E_2)_x.$* Accordingly, the dual bundle operation defines a [contravariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) from the category of vector bundles and their morphisms to itself.
