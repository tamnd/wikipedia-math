# Frank Duckworth

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Frank Duckworth"}

**Frank Carter Duckworth** [MBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire) (26 December 1939 – 21 June 2024) was an English [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who co-developed the [Duckworth–Lewis method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duckworth–Lewis_method) of resetting targets in [limited overs cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_overs_cricket) matches interrupted by weather or other circumstances.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Duckworth was born on 26 December 1939 in [Lytham St Annes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lytham_St_Annes), [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire).[^o7] He attended King Edward VII School, Lytham, now part of [King Edward VII and Queen Mary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VII_and_Queen_Mary_School),[^o8] then went on to study physics (BSc Hons 1961) and earned a PhD (1965) in metallurgy, both at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool).[^o9]

## Career {#Career}

Prior to his retirement, he worked as a mathematical scientist for the English [nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power) industry.[^o11] He was a consultant statistician to the [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council),[^o12][^o13] and the editor of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664)'s monthly news magazine, *RSS News*, until he retired from both these roles in 2014.[^o14] He also served on the editorial board of *[Significance](wikipedia://en/27589249)* before stepping down in 2010. In 2004 he delivered the Royal Statistical Society Schools Lecture, entitled *Lies and Statistics*.[^o15]

In 1962, Duckworth was a tenant of [John Lennon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennon)'s aunt.[^o16]

Duckworth is also known for developing a system of quantifying personal [risk perception](wikipedia://en/2308722),[^o17] now known as the "Duckworth scale".[^o18]

## Death {#Death}

Duckworth died of cancer on 21 June 2024, at the age of 84.[^o20][^o21]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

Duckworth was appointed [Member of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (MBE) in the [2010 Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Birthday_Honours) for services to the Royal Statistical Society and to Cricket. He was also the recipient of the Degree of Doctor of Science, honoris causa. from the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath) in 2015.[^o24][^o25]
