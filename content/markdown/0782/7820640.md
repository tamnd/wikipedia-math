# Tetrated dodecahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tetrated dodecahedron"}

*Image: Tetrated dodecahedron.stl.* 3D model of a tetrated dodecahedron

*Image: Physical model of tetrated dodecahedron.png.* Model built with [polydron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polydron)

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **tetrated dodecahedron** is a [near-miss Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/3881930). It was first discovered in 2002 by Alex Doskey. It was then independently rediscovered in 2003, and named, by Robert Austin.[^o3]

:::

It has 28 [faces](wikipedia://en/149926): twelve regular [pentagons](wikipedia://en/20741014) arranged in four panels of three pentagons each, four [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) (shown in blue), and six pairs of [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428) (shown in yellow). All edges of the tetrated [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) have the same length, except for the shared bases of these isosceles triangles, which are approximately 1.07 times as long as the other edges. This [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) has [tetrahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2904472).

Topologically, as a near-miss Johnson solid, the four triangles corresponding to the face planes of a tetrahedron are always equilateral, while the pentagons and the other triangles only have reflection symmetry.

## Dual Polyhedron {#Dual_Polyhedron}

The dual polyhedron of the *tetrated dodecahedron* consists of triangular, trapezoidal, and rhombic faces. It has 28 faces.
