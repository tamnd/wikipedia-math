# Lipschitz domain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lipschitz domain"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Lipschitz domain** (or **domain with Lipschitz boundary**) is a [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) whose [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) is "sufficiently regular" in the sense that it can be thought of as locally being the graph of a [Lipschitz continuous function](wikipedia://en/51731). The term is named after the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Rudolf Lipschitz](wikipedia://en/339965).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $n \in \mathbb N$. Let $\Omega$ be a [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) of $\mathbb R^n$ and let $\partial\Omega$ denote the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of $\Omega$. Then $\Omega$ is called a **Lipschitz domain** if for every point $p \in \partial\Omega$ there exists a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) $H$ of dimension $n-1$ through $p$, a Lipschitz-continuous function $g : H \rightarrow \mathbb R$ over that hyperplane, and reals $r > 0$ and $h > 0$ such that

- $$\Omega \cap C = \left\{x + y \vec{n} \mid x \in B_r(p) \cap H,\ -h < y < g(x) \right\}$$
- $$(\partial\Omega) \cap C = \left\{x + y \vec{n} \mid x \in B_r(p) \cap H,\ g(x) = y \right\}$$

where

$\vec{n}$ is one of the two [unit vectors](wikipedia://en/167053) that are [normal](wikipedia://en/173224) to $H,$

$B_{r} (p) := \{x \in \mathbb{R}^{n} \mid \| x - p \| < r \}$ is the open ball of radius $r$,

$$C := \left\{x + y \vec{n} \mid x \in B_r(p) \cap H,\ {-h} < y < h \right\}.$$

In other words, at each point of its boundary, $\Omega$ is locally the set of points located above the graph of some Lipschitz function.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

A more general notion is that of **weakly Lipschitz** domains, which are domains whose boundary is locally flattenable by a bilipschitz mapping. Lipschitz domains in the sense above are sometimes called **strongly Lipschitz** by contrast with weakly Lipschitz domains.

A domain $\Omega$ is **weakly Lipschitz** if for every point $p \in \partial\Omega,$ there exists a radius $r > 0$ and a map $\ell_p : B_r(p) \rightarrow Q$ such that

- $\ell_p$ is a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942);
- $\ell_p$ and $\ell_p^{-1}$ are both Lipschitz continuous functions;
- $$\ell_p\left( \partial\Omega \cap B_r(p) \right) = Q_0;$$
- $$\ell_p\left( \Omega \cap B_r(p) \right) = Q_+;$$

where $Q$ denotes the unit ball $B_1(0)$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and

$$Q_{0} := \{(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in Q \mid x_n = 0 \};$$

$$Q_{+} := \{(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in Q \mid x_n > 0 \}.$$

A (strongly) Lipschitz domain is always a weakly Lipschitz domain but the converse is not true. An example of weakly Lipschitz domains that fails to be a strongly Lipschitz domain is given by the **two-bricks** domain [^o6]

:::

## Applications of Lipschitz domains {#Applications_of_Lipschitz_domains}

Many of the [Sobolev embedding theorems](wikipedia://en/4665038) require that the domain of study be a Lipschitz domain. Consequently, many [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [variational problems](wikipedia://en/171882) are defined on Lipschitz domains.
