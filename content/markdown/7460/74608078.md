# JeAnne Burg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="JeAnne Burg"}

*Image: JeAnneBurgUniform3.jpg.* Dr. Burg in dress uniform.

**JeAnne Rae Burg** (December 23, 1937 – October 3, 2020) was an American [environmental statistician](wikipedia://en/8919339) who worked for many years in the [United States Public Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Public_Health_Service).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Burg was born on December 23, 1937, in [Coleridge, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coleridge,_Nebraska), one of three children in a farming family. She graduated at age 15 from high school and at age 19 from the [Nebraska State Teachers College at Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_State_Teachers_College_at_Wayne). After a year as a high school mathematics and science teacher, she returned to school for a master's degree in chemistry at the [University of Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska–Lincoln). She continued to teach at the high school level while her husband earned a Ph.D. in chemistry at [Kent State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_State_University). After he took a faculty position at the [University of Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cincinnati), she earned a Ph.D. in environmental science there in 1975, while continuing to raise their two children.

## Career and later life {#Career_and_later_life}

After completing her doctorate, Burg joined the United States Public Health Service, working for the [National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Occupational_Safety_and_Health) in Cincinnati. She later moved to the [Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agency_for_Toxic_Substances_and_Disease_Registry) in [Atlanta, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta,_Georgia) and then to the [Centers for Disease Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centers_for_Disease_Control), where she retired in 2000 as a scientist and captain in the USPHS after 24 years of service.

After struggles with [Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson's_disease), she died on October 3, 2020, in [Atlantic Beach, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Beach,_Florida).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1997, Burg was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).
