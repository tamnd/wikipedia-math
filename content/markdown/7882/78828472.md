# Approximately continuous function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Approximately continuous function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), an **approximately continuous function** is a concept that generalizes the notion of [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) by replacing the [ordinary limit](wikipedia://en/285759) with an [approximate limit](wikipedia://en/27100784).[^o2] This generalization provides insights into [measurable functions](wikipedia://en/44055) with applications in real analysis and geometric measure theory.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ be a [Lebesgue measurable set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_measurable_set), $f\colon E \to \mathbb{R}^k$ be a [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055), and $x_0 \in E$ be a point where the [Lebesgue density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_density) of $E$ is 1. The function $f$ is said to be *approximately continuous* at $x_0$ if and only if the [approximate limit](wikipedia://en/27100784) of $f$ at $x_0$ exists and equals $f(x_0)$.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A fundamental result in the theory of approximately continuous functions is derived from [Lusin's theorem](wikipedia://en/2257262), which states that every measurable function is approximately continuous at almost every point of its domain.[^o9] The concept of approximate continuity can be extended beyond measurable functions to arbitrary functions between metric spaces. The **Stepanov-Denjoy theorem** provides a remarkable characterization:

> **Stepanov-Denjoy theorem:** A function is [measurable](wikipedia://en/44055) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is approximately continuous [almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688). [^o10]

Approximately continuous functions are intimately connected to [Lebesgue points](wikipedia://en/1519594). For a function $f \in L^1(E)$, a point $x_0$ is a Lebesgue point if it is a point of Lebesgue density 1 for $E$ and satisfies

$$\lim_{r\downarrow 0} \frac{1}{\lambda (B_r (x_0))} \int_{E\cap B_r (x_0)} |f(x)-f(x_0)|\, dx = 0$$

where $\lambda$ denotes the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) and $B_r(x_0)$ represents the ball of radius $r$ centered at $x_0$. Every Lebesgue point of a function is necessarily a point of approximate continuity.[^o11] The converse relationship holds under additional constraints: when $f$ is [essentially bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essentially_bounded), its points of approximate continuity coincide with its Lebesgue points.[^o12]
