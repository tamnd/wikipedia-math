# Hardy–Littlewood Tauberian theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hardy–Littlewood Tauberian theorem"}

In [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), the **Hardy–Littlewood Tauberian theorem** is a [Tauberian theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauberian_theorem) relating the [asymptotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotics) of the partial sums of a [series](wikipedia://en/15287) with the asymptotics of its [Abel summation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_summation). In this form, the theorem asserts that if the sequence $a_n\geq 0$ is such that there is an [asymptotic equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_equivalence)

:::

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n e^{-ny} \sim \frac{1}{y}\ \text{as}\ y\downarrow 0$$

then there is also an asymptotic equivalence

$$\sum_{k=0}^n a_k \sim n$$

as $n\to\infty$. The [integral](wikipedia://en/15532) formulation of the theorem relates in an analogous manner the asymptotics of the [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) of a function with the asymptotics of its [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610).

The theorem was [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) in 1914 by [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) and [J. E. Littlewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._E._Littlewood).[^o2] In 1930, [Jovan Karamata](wikipedia://en/3130497) gave a new and much simpler proof.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

### Series formulation {#Series_formulation}

This formulation is from Titchmarsh.[^o2] Suppose $a_n\geq 0$ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$, and we have

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n x^n \sim \frac{1}{1-x}\ \text{as}\ x\uparrow 1 .$$

Then as $n\to\infty$ we have

$$\sum_{k=0}^n a_k \sim n.$$

The theorem is sometimes quoted in equivalent forms, where instead of requiring $a_n\geq 0$, we require $a_n=O(1)$, or we require $a_n\geq -K$ for some constant $K$.[^o6] The theorem is sometimes quoted in another equivalent formulation (through the change of variable $x=1/e^y$).[^o6] If,

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n e^{-ny} \sim \frac{1}{y}\ \text{as}\ y\downarrow 0$$

then

$$\sum_{k=0}^n a_k \sim n.$$

### Integral formulation {#Integral_formulation}

The following more general formulation is from Feller.[^o8] Consider a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued function $F:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$ of [bounded variation](wikipedia://en/330673).[^o9] The [Laplace–Stieltjes transform](wikipedia://en/365929) of $F$ is defined by the [Stieltjes integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stieltjes_integral)

$$\omega(s) = \int_0^\infty e^{-st}\,dF(t).$$

The theorem relates the asymptotics of ω with those of $F$ in the following way. If $\rho$ is a non-negative real number, then the following statements are equivalent

- $$\omega(s)\sim C s^{-\rho},\quad\rm{as\ }s\to 0$$
- $$F(t)\sim \frac{C}{\Gamma(\rho+1)}t^\rho, \ \text{as}\ t\to\infty.$$

Here $\Gamma$ denotes the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316). One obtains the theorem for series as a special case by taking $\rho=1$ and $F(t)$ to be a piecewise constant function with value $\textstyle{\sum_{k=0}^n a_k}$ between $t=n$ and $t=n+1$.

A slight improvement is possible. According to the definition of a [slowly varying function](wikipedia://en/17053182), $L(x)$ is slow varying at infinity iff

$$\frac{L(tx)}{L(x)}\to 1,\quad x\to\infty$$

for every $t>0$. Let $L$ be a function slowly varying at infinity and $\rho\geq 0$. Then the following statements are equivalent

- $$\omega(s)\sim s^{-\rho}L(s^{-1}),\quad\text{as}\ s\to 0$$
- $$F(t)\sim \frac{1}{\Gamma(\rho+1)}t^\rho L(t), \quad\text{as}\ t\to\infty.$$

:::

## Karamata's proof {#Karamata's_proof}

(Karamata 1930) found a short proof of the theorem by considering the functions $g$ such that

$$\lim_{x\to 1} (1-x)\sum a_nx^ng(x^n) = \int_0^1 g(t)dt$$

An easy calculation shows that all [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416) $g(x)=x^k$ have this property, and therefore so do all [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) $g$. This can be extended to a function $g$ with simple (step) [discontinuities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discontinuity_%28mathematics%29) by approximating it by polynomials from above and below (using the [Weierstrass approximation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_approximation_theorem) and a little extra fudging) and using the fact that the coefficients $a_n$ are positive. In particular the function given by $g(t)=1/t$ if $1/e<t<1$ and $0$ otherwise has this property. But then for $x=e^{-1/N}$ the sum $\sum a_n x^n g(x^n)$ is $a_0 + \cdots + a_N$ and the integral of $g$ is $1$, from which the Hardy–Littlewood theorem follows immediately.

## Examples {#Examples}

### Non-positive coefficients {#Non-positive_coefficients}

The theorem can fail without the condition that the coefficients are non-negative. For example, the function

$$\frac{1}{(1+x)^2(1-x)} = 1-x+2x^2-2x^3+3x^4-3x^5+\cdots$$

is asymptotic to $1/4(1-x)$ as $x\to 1$, but the partial sums of its coefficients are 1, 0, 2, 0, 3, 0, 4, ... and are not asymptotic to any linear function.

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Littlewood's extension of Tauber's theorem"}

### Littlewood's extension of Tauber's theorem {#Littlewood's_extension_of_Tauber's_theorem}

In 1911 [Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371) proved an extension of [Tauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Tauber)'s [converse](wikipedia://en/155941) of [Abel's theorem](wikipedia://en/237305). Littlewood showed the following: If $a_n=O(1/n)$, and we have

$$\sum a_n x^n \to s \ \text{as}\ x\uparrow 1$$

then

$$\sum a_n = s.$$

This came historically before the Hardy–Littlewood Tauberian theorem, but can be proved as a simple application of it.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Prime number theorem"}

### Prime number theorem {#Prime_number_theorem}

In 1915 Hardy and Littlewood developed a proof of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) based on their Tauberian theorem; they proved

$$\sum_{n=2}^\infty \Lambda(n) e^{-ny} \sim \frac{1}{y},$$

where $\Lambda$ is the [von Mangoldt function](wikipedia://en/1928862), and then conclude

$$\sum_{n \le x} \Lambda(n) \sim x,$$

an equivalent form of the prime number theorem.[^o19][^o20] Littlewood developed a simpler proof, still based on this Tauberian theorem, in 1971.[^o20]
