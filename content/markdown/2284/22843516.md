# Schützenberger group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schützenberger group"}

In [semigroup theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_theory), a **Schützenberger group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) associated with a [Green *H*-class](wikipedia://en/1192012#The_H_and_D_relations) of a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799).[^o2] The Schützenberger groups associated with different *H*-classes are distinct, but the groups associated with two different *H*-classes contained in the same *D*-class of a semigroup are [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic). Moreover, if the *H*-class itself were a group, the Schützenberger group of the *H*-class would be isomorphic to the *H*-class. In fact, there are two Schützenberger groups associated with a given *H*-class, with each being [antiisomorphic](wikipedia://en/2152465) to the other.

:::

The Schützenberger group was discovered by [Marcel-Paul Schützenberger](wikipedia://en/2817337) in 1957[^o3][^o4], and the terminology was coined by [A. H. Clifford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._H._Clifford).[^o5]

## The Schützenberger group {#The_Schützenberger_group}

Let *S* be a semigroup and let *S*<sup>1</sup> be the semigroup obtained by adjoining an [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) 1 to *S* (if *S* already has an identity element, then *S*<sup>1</sup> = *S*). Green's *H*-relation in *S* is defined as follows: If *a* and *b* are in *S* then

*a* *H* *b* ⇔ there are *u*, *v*, *x*, *y* in *S*<sup>1</sup> such that *ua* = *ax* = *b* and *vb* = *by* = *a*.

For *a* in *S*, the set of all *b**s in*****S*such that*aHb*is the Green *H*-class of*S*containing*a*, denoted by*H*<sub>*a*</sub>.***

Let *H* be an *H*-class of the semigroup *S*. Let *T*(*H*) be the set of all elements *t* in *S*<sup>1</sup> such that *Ht* is a subset of *H* itself. Each *t* in *T*(*H*) defines a transformation, denoted by γ<sub>*t*</sub>, of *H* by mapping *h* in *H* to *ht* in *H*. The set of all these transformations of *H*, denoted by Γ(*H*), is a group under [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of mappings (taking functions as right operators). The group Γ(*H*) is the Schützenberger group associated with the *H*-class *H*.

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

If *H* is a maximal subgroup of a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) *M*, then *H* is an H-class, and it is naturally isomorphic to its own Schützenberger group.

In general, one has that the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of *H* and its Schützenberger group coincide for any H-class *H*.

:::

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

It is known that a monoid with finitely many left and right [ideals](wikipedia://en/27799#Subsemigroups_and_ideals) is [finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_monoid) (or just [finitely generated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_generated_monoid)) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) all of its Schützenberger groups are [finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group) (respectively, [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042)). Similarly such a monoid is [residually finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residually_finite) if and only if all of its Schützenberger groups are residually finite.
