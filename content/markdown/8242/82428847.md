# Richard S. Pierce

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard S. Pierce"}

**Richard Scott Pierce** was an American logician and mathematician. His books on [abstract algebras](wikipedia://en/19616384) have become a standard references and graduate textbooks.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Richard Scott Pierce was born on February 26, 1927 in [Los Angeles County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County,_California).[^o3] He studied at [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), earning his bachelor degree in 1950 and Ph.D. in 1952. This thesis, *Homomorphisms of a Modular Lattice*, was supervised by [Robert P. Dilworth](wikipedia://en/9581646), a proponent of [lattice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_theory).

For post-graduate study Pierce went to [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), where he was a fellow in the Office of Naval Research. From 1953 to 1955, he studied at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) as a Jewette Research Fellow. There he became acquainted with [Garrett Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/1180916). Together they made the [Pierce-Birkhoff conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierce-Birkhoff_conjecture), which concerned a [minimax approximation algorithm](wikipedia://en/4882595) for [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000).[^o4]

In 1955, he commenced his teaching career at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) where he wrote his first [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook). He served as thesis advisor for [Gloria Conyers Hewitt](wikipedia://en/38543875) in 1962.

In 1970, he moved to the [University of Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawaii), and in 1975 to the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona).[^o5]

Pierce died on March 15, 1992. After his death, family and friends established The Richard Scott Memorial Endowment to support outstanding undergraduate math majors at University of Arizona.[^o6]

## Books {#Books}

Richard Pierce wrote two books about [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384):

### *Introduction to the theory of Abstract Algebras* (1968) {#Introduction_to_the_theory_of_Abstract_Algebras_(1968)}

According to the preface, the book is intended to provide a systematic presentation of the basic results of [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200), a missing part of the published literature, except for books by [P. M. Cohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._M._Cohn) (1965) and [George Grätzer](wikipedia://en/42456445) (1967), each with the title *Universal Algebra*. The scope includes "partial algebras with (possibly) infinitary operations or relations ... an effort is made to present all results at their maximal level of generality." Four chapters each present a particular [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977): the subdirect decomposition theorem, [Øystein Ore](wikipedia://en/1643733)'s theorem on the uniqueness of direct decomposition, an existence theorem for free extensions, and [Birkhoff's variety theorem](wikipedia://en/1021753#Birkhoff's_theorem). Regarding exercises, "some of the material presented in \[the\] problems is of recent vintage, and a few of the results have not been previously published." With a significant attention given to [lattices](wikipedia://en/469304), an alternative title for the book is "An introduction to the theory and uses of lattices". Pierce states, "This monograph was developed from lectures given over a number of years", and he acknowledges a decade of support from [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) and the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation).

Page one begins: "The theory of abstract algebras is close enough to the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358) that some care must be exercised using [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553)." The [Von\_Neumann%E2%80%93Bernays%E2%80%93G%C3%B6del set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann%E2%80%93Bernays%E2%80%93G%C3%B6del_set_theory) is cited, with *class* and *set* as [primitive notions](wikipedia://en/23706). A *class* is the totality of things satisfying a property, and a *set* is a class of specific objects. He writes that proper classes are "so enormous that the intuition is incapable of assigning a magnitude to them." After defining a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) and its domain, he defines a *partial mapping* φ as a relation in which $<a,b>\in \phi \ \text{and}\ <a,c> \in \phi \ \text{implies}\ a = b .$ Then a partial mapping φ on *A* x *B* for which the domain is all of *A* is a *mapping* or *function*. "The definition is exact and convenient for all mathematical uses." (page 3)

The [restriction](wikipedia://en/1589032) of a relation to a subset *A*<sub>1</sub> of *A* includes \<a,b> in φ only if a is in *A*<sub>1</sub>. The [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) is necessary to show that, if a mapping φ is onto, then there is a mapping ψ such that the [composition of mappings](wikipedia://en/7472170) φ ψ is the identity mapping on *B*. (page 5)

### *Associative Algebras* (1982) {#Associative_Algebras_(1982)}

According to the preface, "the classical results of the subject are explored more deeply than in most student-oriented expositions of [associative algebras](wikipedia://en/2112), and the recent developments in the theory of algebras are liberally sampled." Classical theorems of [Joseph Wedderburn](wikipedia://en/2969494) and [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) are covered in the first part, and the second part addresses [central simple algebras](wikipedia://en/404082) and the [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078) of a [field](wikipedia://en/10603). The development follows the [Jacobson density theorem](wikipedia://en/488391), the [Skolem%E2%80%93Noether theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skolem%E2%80%93Noether_theorem), and the [double centralizer theorem](wikipedia://en/32329370).

The book is dedicated to Marilyn Pierce, who’s "patience and impatience kept the project moving from its beginning to the end."
