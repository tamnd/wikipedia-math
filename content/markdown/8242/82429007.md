# Maria Westdickenberg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maria Westdickenberg"}

**Maria G. Westdickenberg** (also published as **Maria G. Reznikoff**) is an American applied mathematician and [mathematical analyst](wikipedia://en/48396) who works in Germany as University Professor in the chair for applied analysis at [RWTH Aachen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWTH_Aachen_University). She lists her research interests as being in [nonlinear partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/16678376) and the complex energy landscapes of [gradient flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradient_flow) methods for solving these equations.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Westdickenberg received a master's degree in applied mathematics in 2000 from the [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute). She continued her studies at the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), completing her Ph.D. in 2004. Her doctoral dissertation, *Rare Events in Finite and Infinite Dimensions*, was jointly supervised by [Robert V. Kohn](wikipedia://en/48477231) and [Eric Vanden-Eijnden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Vanden-Eijnden).

After postdoctoral research at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), she became an assistant professor at [Georgia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech) in 2006, and was tenured there as an associate professor in 2011. Since 2012 she has been a full professor at RWTH Aachen University.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Westdickenberg was a 2010 recipient of the [Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Early_Career_Award_for_Scientists_and_Engineers), given "for excellent research in applied mathematics, and leadership in recruiting, training, and mentoring students from underrepresented groups".
