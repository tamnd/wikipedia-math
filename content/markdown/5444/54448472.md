# Glasser's master theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glasser's master theorem"}

In [integral calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_calculus), **Glasser's master theorem** explains how a certain broad class of substitutions can simplify certain integrals over the whole [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) from $-\infty$ to $+\infty.$ The integrals in question must be construed as [Cauchy principal values](wikipedia://en/408195), and *a fortiori* it is applicable when the integral [converges absolutely](wikipedia://en/61532). It is named after M. L. Glasser, who introduced it in 1983.[^o2]

:::

## A special case: the Cauchy–Schlömilch transformation {#A_special_case:_the_Cauchy–Schlömilch_transformation}

A special case called the Cauchy–Schlömilch substitution or Cauchy–Schlömilch transformation[^o4] was known to [Cauchy](wikipedia://en/1842) in the early 19th century.[^o5] It states that

$$\text{P.V.} \int_{-\infty}^\infty F\left(x-\frac{1}{x}\right)\mathrm{d}x = \text{P.V.} \int_{-\infty}^\infty F(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$$

where PV denotes the Cauchy principal value and $F$ is a function which is integrable on the real line at least in the sense of the Cauchy principal value.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="The master theorem"}

## The master theorem {#The_master_theorem}

If $a$, $a_n$, and $b_n$ are real numbers and

$$\phi(x) = x - a - \sum_{n=1}^N \frac{|a_n|}{x-b_n}$$

then

$$\operatorname{P.V.} \int_{-\infty}^\infty F(\phi(x))\,\mathrm{d}x = \operatorname{P.V.} \int_{-\infty}^\infty F(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.$$

:::

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

$$\begin{aligned} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{x^2}{x^4+1}\,\mathrm{d}x &=\int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x^2+\frac{1}{x^2}} \\ &= \int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\left( x-\frac 1 x \right)^2 + 2} \\ &= \int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x^2 + 2} \\ &=\frac \pi {\sqrt 2} \end{aligned}$$

where the first equality comes from cancelling $x^2$, the second from Cauchy–Schlömilch, and the last one from a substitution and the [integral of the arctangent function](wikipedia://en/374220#Derivatives_of_inverse_trigonometric_functions).

:::
