# Victor J. Katz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Victor J. Katz"}

Victor Kac

**Victor Joseph Katz** (born December 31, 1942, [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia))[^o3] is an American mathematician, [historian of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian_of_mathematics) specialising in [islamic Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Mathematics), and teacher known for using the history of mathematics in teaching mathematics.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Katz received in 1963 from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) a bachelor's degree and in 1968 from [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) a Ph.D. in mathematics under [Maurice Auslander](wikipedia://en/30337642) with thesis *The Brauer group of a regular local ring*. He became at Federal City College an assistant professor and then in 1973 an associate professor and, after the merger of Federal City College into the [University of the District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_District_of_Columbia) in 1977, a full professor there in 1980. He retired there as professor emeritus in 2005.

As a mathematician Katz specializes in algebra, but he is mainly known for his work on the history of mathematics and its uses in teaching. He wrote a textbook *History of Mathematics: An Introduction* (1993), for which he won in 1995 the [Watson Davis and Helen Miles Davis Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_Davis_and_Helen_Miles_Davis_Prize). He organized workshops and congresses for the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA) and the [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1561691). The MAA published a collection of teaching materials by Katz as a compact disk with the title *Historical Modules for the Teaching and Learning of Mathematics*. With Frank Swetz, he was a founding editor of a free online journal on the history of mathematics under the aegis of the MAA; the journal is called *Convergence: Where Mathematics, History, and Teaching Interact*.[^o6] In the journal *Convergence*, Katz and Swetz published a series *Mathematical Treasures*.[^o7][^o8] For a study of the possibilities for using mathematical history in schools, Katz received a grant from the National Science Foundation.

## Personal {#Personal}

He has been married since 1969 to Phyllis Katz (née Friedman), a science educator who developed and directed the U.S. national nonprofit organization *[Hands On Science Outreach, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hands_On_Science_Outreach,_Inc.)* (HOSO). The couple have three children.

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

### As author {#As_author}

- History of Mathematics: An Introduction, New York: Harper Collins, 1993, 3rd edition Pearson 2008 (a shortened edition was published in 2003 by Pearson)
- with [Karen Hunger Parshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Hunger_Parshall): [Taming the Unknown: A History of Algebra from Antiquity to the Early Twentieth Century](https://books.google.com/books/about/Taming_the_Unknown.html?id=nQLHAgAAQBAJ), Princeton University Press 2014[^o12][^o13]
- with John B. Fraleigh: A first course in abstract algebra, Addison-Wesley 2003

### As editor {#As_editor}

- [The Mathematics of Egypt, Mesopotamia, China, India and Islam: A Sourcebook](https://books.google.com/books?id=3ullzl036UEC), Princeton University Press 2007[^o15]
- with Bengt Johansson, Frank Swetz, Otto Bekken, John Fauvel: [Learn from the Masters](https://books.google.com/books?id=gqGLoh-WYrEC), MAA 1994 (contribution by Katz: Historical ideas in teaching linear algebra, Napier's logarithms adapted for today's classroom)
- [Using History to Teach Mathematics: An International Perspective](https://books.google.com/books?id=CbZ_YsdCmP0C), MAA 2000, MAA Notes (No. 51)[^o16][^o17]
- with Marlow Anderson, [Robin Wilson](wikipedia://en/2715277): [Sherlock Holmes in Babylon and other Tales of Mathematical History](https://books.google.com/books/about/Sherlock_Holmes_in_Babylon.html?id=BKRE5AjRM3AC), (collection of reprints from the journal [Mathematics Magazine](wikipedia://en/4230329) of MAA; contribution by Katz: Ideas of calculus in Islam and India), MAA 2004[^o18]
- with Marlow Anderson, Robin Wilson: [Who gave you the epsilon? and other tales of mathematical history](https://books.google.com/books?id=WwFMjsym9JwC), MAA 2009 (continuation of the collection of essays on the history of mathematics from MAA journal; contribution by Katz: The history of Stokes' theorem)[^o19]
- with Constantinos Tzanakis: Recent Developments on Introducing a Historical Dimension in Mathematics Education, MAA 2011
