# Domina Eberle Spencer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Domina Eberle Spencer"}

**Domina Eberle Spencer** (September 26, 1920 – 2022) was an American mathematician who was Professor at the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Spencer was born on September 26, 1920,[^o3] and earned her Ph.D. in 1942 from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) under the supervision of [Dirk Jan Struik](wikipedia://en/932453). She worked on [electrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrodynamics) and [field theory](wikipedia://en/1293340).[^o5]

Spencer made significant contributions to the field of lighting design research, particularly through her research on the visual perception of light.[^o6] Working alongside her husband, [Parry Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parry_Moon), Spencer co-authored several books and papers that explored various aspects of electrodynamics, field theory and introduced [holors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holor). Their collaborative work, such as *The Photic Field* (1981), delved into the physical and physiological effects of light, providing a foundational understanding that has influenced modern lighting standards and practices.[^o7][^o8]

Spencer's research extended to practical applications in lighting, contributing to the development of more efficient and visually comfortable lighting systems.[^o9][^o10] Her work is recognized for integrating rigorous scientific principles with practical engineering solutions, enhancing the quality and functionality of lighting in various environments.[^o11] Additionally, her contributions are noted in discussions on lighting design innovations, as highlighted by peers in the [Illuminating Engineering Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminating_Engineering_Society) (IES), who acknowledge her influence on contemporary lighting research and standards.[^o6][^o12] She was awarded the distinction of IES Fellow in 1962 for valuable contributions to the art and science of illumination.[^o13] Her death, at age 101, was reported by the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut) in May 2022.[^o14]
