# Macbeath region

*Image: MacBeathRegionSWAT2018.png.* The Macbeath region around a point x in a convex body K and the scaled Macbeath region around a point x in a convex body K

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Macbeath region"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **MacBeath region** is an explicitly defined region in [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201) on a bounded [convex subset](wikipedia://en/6292) of *d*-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^d$. The idea was introduced by (txt)[^o3] and dubbed by G. Ewald, D. G. Larman and C. A. Rogers in 1970.[^o4] MacBeath regions have been used to solve certain complex problems in the study of the boundaries of [convex bodies](wikipedia://en/8652020).[^o5] Recently they have been used in the study of convex approximations and other aspects of [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927).[^o6][^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *K* be a bounded [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) set in a [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). Given a point *x* and a scalar λ the λ-scaled MacBeath region around a point *x* is:

$${M_K}(x)=K \cap (2x - K) = x + ((K-x)\cap(x-K)) = \{k'\in K| \exists k \in K \text{ and } k'-x=x-k \}$$

The scaled MacBeath region at *x* is defined as:

$$M_K^{\lambda}(x)=x + \lambda ((K-x)\cap(x-K)) = \{(1-\lambda)x+\lambda k'|k'\in K, \exists k\in K \text{ and } k'-x=x-k \}$$

This can be seen to be the intersection of *K* with the reflection of *K* around *x* scaled by λ.

:::

## Example uses {#Example_uses}

- MacBeath regions can be used to create $\epsilon$ approximations, with respect to the [Hausdorff distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausdorff_metric), of convex shapes within a factor of $O(\log^{\frac{d+1}{2}}(\frac{1}{\epsilon}))$ combinatorial complexity of the lower bound.[^o7]
- MacBeath regions can be used to approximate balls in the [Hilbert metric](wikipedia://en/22154619), e.g. given any convex *K*, containing an *x* and a $0\leq\lambda<1$ then:[^o6][^o11]

$$B_H\left(x,\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+\lambda)\right)\subset M^\lambda (x) \subset B_H\left(x,\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{1+ \lambda}{1-\lambda}\right)$$

- Dikin’s Method

## Properties {#Properties}

- The $M_K^{\lambda}(x)$ is centrally symmetric around x.
- MacBeath regions are [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292).
- If $x,y \in K$ and $M^{\frac{1}{2}}(x) \cap M^{\frac{1}{2}}(y) \neq \emptyset$ then $M^{1}(y)\subset M^{5}(x)$.[^o5][^o6] Essentially if two MacBeath regions intersect, you can scale one of them up to contain the other.
- If some convex *K* in $R^d$ containing both a ball of radius r and a half-space *H*, with the half-space disjoint from the ball, and the cap $K \cap H$ of our convex set has a width less than or equal to $\frac{r}{2}$, we get $K \cap H \subset M^{3d(x)}$ for *x*, the center of gravity of K in the bounding hyper-plane of *H*.[^o5]
- Given a convex body $K \subset R^d$ in canonical form, then any cap of *K* with width at most $\frac{1}{6d}$ then $C \subset M^{3d}(x)$, where x is the centroid of the base of the cap.[^o7]
- Given a convex *K* and some constant $\lambda>0$, then for any point *x* in a cap *C* of *K* we know $M^{\lambda}(x) \cap K \subset C^{1+\lambda}$. In particular when $\lambda\leq 1$, we get $M^{\lambda}(x) \subset C^{1+\lambda}$.[^o7]
- Given a convex body *K*, and a cap *C* of *K*, if x is in *K* and $C \cap M'(x) \neq \emptyset$ we get $M'(x)\subset C^2$.[^o7]
- Given a small $\epsilon>0$ and a convex $K\subset R^d$ in canonical form, there exists some collection of $O\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\right)$ centrally symmetric disjoint convex bodies $R_1,....,R_k$ and caps $C_1,....,C_k$ such that for some constant $\beta$ and $\lambda$ depending on d we have:[^o7]
  - Each $C_i$ has width $\beta\epsilon$, and $R_i \subset C_i \subset R_i^{\lambda}$
  - If C is any cap of width $\epsilon$ there must exist an *i* so that $R_i \subset C$ and $C_i^{\frac{1}{\beta^2}} \subset C \subset C_i$
