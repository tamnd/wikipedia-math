# Chuu-Lian Terng

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chuu-Lian Terng"}

Terng Chuu-Lian

**Chuu-Lian Terng** (; born 1949) is a Taiwanese-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Her research areas are [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [integrable systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_systems), with particular interests in completely integrable Hamiltonian [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) and their relations to differential geometry, the geometry and topology of submanifolds in [symmetric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_spaces), and the geometry of isometric actions.[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

She received her [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) from [National Taiwan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Taiwan_University) in 1971 and her [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) in 1976[^o6] under the supervision of [Richard Palais](wikipedia://en/34779708),[^o7] whom she later married.[^o8] She was a postdoctoral researcher at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) from 1976 to 1978, an assistant professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1978 to 1982, and was faculty at [Northeastern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_University) from 1982 to 2004. She was the first female assistant professor in mathematics at Princeton University.[^o9] She is currently a professor emerita at the [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine), which she joined in 2004.[^o10] She also spent two years at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (IAS) in Princeton and two years at the [Max-Planck Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck_Institute) in [Bonn, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn,_Germany).[^o4]

Terng has been an active member of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) (AWM). She served as AWM President from 1995 to 1997, chaired the Julia Robinson Celebration of Women in Math Conference, which was held July 1–3, 1996,[^o11] and chaired the Michler Prize and Travel/Mentoring Grant Committees.[^o12]

Terng has served on the editorial boards of the [Transactions of the AMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transactions_of_the_AMS), the Taiwanese Journal of Mathematics, Communications of Analysis and Geometry, the [Proceedings of the AMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_AMS), and the Journal of Fixed Point Theory and its Applications.[^o4]

In 1999, she was selected as the [AWM/MAA Falconer Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecturer). Her citation reads:[^o13]

> Her early research concerned the classification of [natural](wikipedia://en/45022) [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) and natural [differential operators](wikipedia://en/263886) between them. She then became interested in [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) geometry. Her main contributions are developing a structure theory for [isoparametric](wikipedia://en/16174280) submanifolds in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and constructing [soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) equations from special submanifolds. Recently, Terng and [Karen Uhlenbeck](wikipedia://en/4384796) ([University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin)) have developed a general approach to [integrable](wikipedia://en/2573213) PDEs that explains their hidden symmetries in terms of [loop group](wikipedia://en/1390288) actions. She is co-author of the book Submanifold Geometry and Critical Point Theory and an editor of the Journal of Differential Geometry survey volume 4 on "Integrable systems".
>
> Professor Terng served as president of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) (AWM) from 1995 to 1997 and as Member-at-Large of the Council of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) from 1989 to 1992. She is currently on the Advisory Board of the National Center for Theoretical Sciences in Taiwan, the Steering Committee of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) Park City Summer Institute, and the Editorial Board of the Transactions of the AMS.

In 2006, she was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Madrid.[^o14] In 2011, she gave an AMS-MAA Invited Address at the [Joint Mathematics Meeting](wikipedia://en/14985143) in New Orleans.

## Honors {#Honors}

- [Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) in 1980.
- [Humboldt Senior Scientist Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Senior_Scientist_Award) in 1997.
- [AWM/MAA Falconer Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecturer) in 1999
- Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), 2012.[^o16]
- Fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698), 2018 (inaugural class).[^o17]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

With [Sun-Yung Alice Chang](wikipedia://en/24637925), [Fan Chung](wikipedia://en/246562), [Winnie Li](wikipedia://en/38403990), [Jang-Mei Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jang-Mei_Wu), and [Mei-Chi Shaw](wikipedia://en/45351961), Terng is one of a group of six women mathematicians from [National Taiwan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Taiwan_University) called by [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905) "a miracle in Chinese history; the glory of the Chinese people".[^o19]
