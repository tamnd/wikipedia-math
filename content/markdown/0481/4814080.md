# Primary cyclic group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Primary cyclic group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **primary cyclic group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) that is both a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) and a [*p*-primary group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-primary_group) for some [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*. That is, it is a cyclic group of order *p*${}^{m}$, C${}_{p^{m}}$, for some prime number *p*, and [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *m*.

:::

Every finite [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) *G* may be written as a finite direct sum of primary cyclic groups, as stated in the [fundamental theorem of finite abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974#Classification):

$G=\bigoplus_{1\leq i \leq n}\mathrm{C}_{{p_i}^{m_i}} .$[^o2]

This expression is essentially unique: there is a bijection between the sets of groups in two such expressions, which maps each group to one that is isomorphic.

Primary cyclic groups are characterised among [finitely generated abelian groups](wikipedia://en/58527) as the [torsion groups](wikipedia://en/3568695) that cannot be expressed as a direct sum of two non-trivial groups. As such they, along with the group of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), form the building blocks of finitely generated abelian groups.

The subgroups of a primary cyclic group are linearly ordered by inclusion. The only other groups that have this property are the [quasicyclic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicyclic_group).
