# Eduard Heis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eduard Heis"}

*Image: EduardHeis.jpg.* Eduard Heis

**Eduard Heis** (18 February 1806, [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) – 30 June 1877 in [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Münster)) was a German mathematician and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer).

:::

He completed his education at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) in 1827, then taught mathematics at a school in Cologne. In 1832 he taught at [Aachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aachen), and remained there until 1852. He was then appointed by [King Frederick William IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_William_IV_of_Prussia) to a chair position at the [Academy of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster) in 1852. In 1869 he became [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) of the academy.

While at the academy he made a series of observations of the night sky, including the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way), [zodiacal light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zodiacal_light), [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star), and [shooting stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteor). These were published in the following works, among others:

- *Atlas Coelestis Novus*, Cologne, 1872.
- *Zodiakal-Beobachtungen*.
- *Sternschnuppen-Beobachtungen*.
- *De Magnitudine*, 1852.

His star atlas, which was based on [Argelander's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_Argelander) *Uranometria Nova* (1843), helped define the selection of [constellations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation) in the northern sky that was officially adopted by the International Astronomical Union in 1922.[^o2] His other publications included a treatise on the eclipses during the [Peloponnesian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnesian_War), [Halley's Comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halley's_Comet), and some mathematical text books.

He was also the first person to record a count of the [Perseid meteor shower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perseid_meteor_shower) in 1839, giving an hourly rate of 160. Observers have recorded the hourly count every year since that time.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- [Order of the Red Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Red_Eagle), 1870.
- Awarded doctor *[honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa)* by Bonn University, 1852.
- Foreign associate, [Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society) of London, 1874.
- Honorary member, Leopoldine Academy, 1877.
- Honorary member, Scientific Society of [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels), 1877.
- The crater [Heis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heis_%28crater%29) on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) is named after him.
