# Amy Tsui

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Amy Tsui"}

**Amy Ong Tsui** (born 1949) is an American demographer.

:::

Due to the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), Tsui's father emigrated to the United States to pursue doctoral studies in agricultural economics. Tsui was born in [Pullman, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullman,_Washington) in 1949. The elder Tsui worked for the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) and moved his family to Bangladesh and Thailand. The Tsui family later returned to the United States. Amy complted high school, attended [Carlton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton_College) in Minnesota, transferred to the [University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawaiʻi_at_Mānoa), completing a BA (1970) and MA (1972), and a PhD (1977) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago); [Donald Bogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Bogue) was her advisor).[^o5][^o2][^o3]

Tsui remained on faculty at Chicago until 1982, before joining faculty at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill).[^o4][^o5] From 2002 to 2013, Tsui was the director of Johns Hopkins University's Bill and Melinda Gates Institute for Population and Reproductive Health.[^o6][^o7] While affiliated with Johns Hopkins, Tsui was elected a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 2004[^o8][^o9] and served as president of the [Population Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_Association_of_America) in 2017.[^o10] She also collaborated with universities in various [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa) countries in improving their own population and reproductive health departments.[^o11] In the 2020 Performance Monitoring and Accountability Project, Tsui was a Senior Technical Advisor.[^o12]

Early in her career, she and Bogue challenged the notion of the "[population bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_bomb)," predicting that increasing contraceptive use would cause the global population to balance at 8 billion by 2050.[^o13] In most recent years, Tsui has focused her research on the topic of women's reproductive activities, prenatal care, and contraceptive usage in various countries around the world.[^o14][^o15]
