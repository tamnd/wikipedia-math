# John Kieffer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Kieffer"}

**John Cronan Kieffer** (born 1945) is an American mathematician best known for his work in [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), and [stationary process theory](wikipedia://en/329898).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Kieffer received his elementary and high school education in [St Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Louis,_Missouri), a bachelor's degree in applied mathematics in 1967 from [University of Missouri Rolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_University_of_Science_and_Technology), and a master's degree in mathematics in 1968 from [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign). In 1970, under Robert B. Ash, he received the Ph.D. degree in mathematics from [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign) with thesis *A Generalization of the Shannon-McMillan Theorem and Its Application to Information Theory*.[^o4]

## Work history {#Work_history}

In 1970 Kieffer became an assistant professor at [Missouri University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_University_of_Science_and_Technology), where he eventually became a full professor.[^o6] In 1986 he became a full professor at [University of Minnesota Twin Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota).[^o7] Kieffer held visiting appointments at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign), [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich), and [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona). He has been the supervisor for 6 Ph.D. theses.[^o3]

## Professional activities {#Professional_activities}

During the 1980s, Kieffer was Associate Editor of the [IEEE Transactions on Information Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Transactions_on_Information_Theory).[^o9] In 2004, Kieffer was co-editor of a special issue of the IEEE Transactions on Information Theory entitled "Problems on Sequences: Information Theory and Computer Science Interface". [^o10] He is a Life Fellow of the [Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers) "for contributions to information theory, particularly coding theory and quantization".[^o11]

## Impact {#Impact}

Kieffer has over 70 journal publications in the mathematical sciences.[^o28] His research work has attracted over 3000 Google Scholar citations, over 500 MathSciNet citations[^o30] and over 1000 IEEE Xplore citations.[^o6] Some of these works have been cited as prior art on various United States patents.[^o31] In 1998, the IEEE Transactions on Information Theory published a special issue consisting of articles that survey research in information theory during 1948–1998. Two of these articles include discussions of Kieffer's work, namely, the article *Lossy Source Coding*[^o32] by [Toby Berger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Berger) and Jerry Gibson, and the article *Quantization*[^o33] by [Robert M. Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Gray) and David Neuhoff. In addition, the textbook *Transmitting and Gaining Data*[^o34] by [Rudolf Ahlswede](wikipedia://en/18251788) presents several aspects of Kieffer's work.
