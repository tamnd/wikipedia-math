# Margarethe Kahn

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Margarethe Kahn"}

**Margarethe Kahn**[^o3] (known as **Grete Kahn**,[^o4] also **Margarete Kahn**,[^o5] born 27 August 1880, missing after deportation to [Piaski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaski,_Świdnik_County), Poland on 28 March 1942) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [Holocaust victim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_victim).[^o6] She was among the first women to obtain a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in Germany. Her doctoral work was on the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of [algebraic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_curves).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Margarethe Kahn was the daughter of [Eschwege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschwege) merchant and [flannel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flannel) factory owner Albert Kahn (1853–1905) and his wife Johanne (née Plaut, 1857–1882). She had an older brother Otto (1879–1932). Five years after the untimely death of his wife Johanne, their father married her younger sister Julie (1860–1934), with whom he had a daughter, Margarethe's half-sister Martha (1888–1942).[^o8]

After attending elementary school from 1887, and the Higher School for Girls from 1889 to 1896, Kahn until 1904 took private lessons to prepare for her *[Abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur)*, because few high schools for girls existed at that time in [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany). In 1904 she was given permission to take her *Abitur* at the Royal *[Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29)* in [Bad Hersfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Hersfeld). Thus she belonged to the small elite of young women in Germany at the beginning of the 20th century who were allowed to take the *Abitur* externally at boys' schools. [Konrad Duden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Duden) signed her *Abitur* certificate as school principal.

Since [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) began to allow women to formally attend university only from the winter semester of 1908–09, Kahn and her friend [Klara Löbenstein](wikipedia://en/41642433) first attended the universities of [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) as guest students. In addition, Kahn attended lectures and tutorials in mathematics at the [Technische Hochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule) in Charlottenburg (now [Technische Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Berlin)). They studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), and [propaedeutics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaedeutics) at [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen). At the University of Göttingen she attended lectures given by, among others, [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887), [Woldemar Voigt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woldemar_Voigt), and [Georg Elias Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Elias_Müller); in Berlin she attended lectures by [Hermann Amandus Schwarz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Amandus_Schwarz) and [Paul Drude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Drude) at the [Royal Prussian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences). Her field of expertise was [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). Together with Löbenstein she made a contribution to [Hilbert's sixteenth problem](wikipedia://en/381750).[^o8] Hilbert's sixteenth problem concerned the topology of [algebraic curves in the complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/253260#Complex_curves_and_realsurfaces); as a difficult special case in his formulation of the problem Hilbert proposed that there are no [algebraic curves of degree 6](wikipedia://en/468884#Degree_6) consisting of 11 separate ovals. Kahn and Löbenstein developed methods to address this problem.

Against opposition in particular from the Berlin faculty, but supported by the University of Göttingen and Felix Klein, Kahn obtained a doctorate in 1909 under David Hilbert in Göttingen, with a dissertation titled *Eine allgemeine Methode zur Untersuchung der Gestalten algebraischer Kurven* \[A general method to investigate the shapes of algebraic curves\] and was therefore one of the first German women to obtain a doctorate in mathematics (the mathematics division was part of the faculty of philosophy then). She took her oral examination – again, along with Löbenstein – on 30 June 1909.

Kahn could not pursue a scientific career because women in Germany were not admitted to [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) before 1920. She therefore sought a career as a schoolteacher, and in October 1912 she obtained a job in the Prussian school system, where she worked as a teacher for secondary schools in [Katowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katowice), [Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dortmund), and from 1929 in [Berlin-Tegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-Tegel) at today's Gabriele-von-Bülow-Gymnasium, and later in [Berlin-Pankow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-Pankow) at today's Carl-von-Ossietzky-Gymnasium.[^o9]

As a [Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew), she was forced to go on leave by the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) in 1933, and was dismissed from the school in 1936. She was [forced to work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) as a factory worker at the *Nordland Schneeketten* (Nordland snow chains) factory. On 28 March 1942, Kahn and her by then widowed sister Martha were deported to Piaski and are considered missing since then.[^o10]

On 13 September 2008, a *[Stolperstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolperstein)* was laid at 127 *Rudolstädter Straße* in [Wilmersdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmersdorf) in memory of Margarethe Kahn, as well as on 26 May 2010 in front of her parents' former house at Stad 29 in [Eschwege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschwege), where additionally a commemorative plaque was attached on 13 December 2017.[^o11] In 2013, a street in [Leverkusen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverkusen) was named after her.[^o12]
