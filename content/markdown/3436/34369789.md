# Donald McIntosh (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donald McIntosh"}

**Donald Cameron McIntosh** (13 January 1868 – 1 July 1957) was a Scottish educator and mathematician.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

McIntosh was born at [Tomintoul, Banffshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkmichael,_Banffshire), Scotland on 13 January 1868. He was educated at Tomintoul Parish School and [Aberdeen Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Grammar_School). He studied at the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen) graduating with a MA in 1890, a BSc in [marine zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biology) 1906 and a DSc in 1912 with a thesis entitled *Studies on Echinodermata and on Variation*.[^o4][^o5]

From 1890 to 1899 he was a teacher at George Watson's College, in Edinburgh, and Head of Mathematics at [Edinburgh Ladies' College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mary_Erskine_School) from 1899 to 1918. From 1918 to 1933 he was Director of Education in Moray and Nairn.[^o4][^o5]

He was a member of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940) and the [Royal Physical Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Physical_Society_of_Edinburgh).[^o4]

He was elected as a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1903. His proposers were [John Sturgeon Mackay](wikipedia://en/54492344), [Sir Francis Grant Ogilvie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Grant_Ogilvie), Sir John Murray and [Alexander Morgan](wikipedia://en/55490791).[^o4]
