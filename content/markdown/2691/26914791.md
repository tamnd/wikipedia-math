# De prospectiva pingendi

De prospectiva pingendi

*Image: Piero, proiezioni di una testa scorciata dal de prospectiva pingendi, ante 1482, milano, biblioteca ambrosiana.jpg.* Perspective of anatomy from *De prospectiva Pingendi*.

*Image: De la perspective en peinture.jpg.* An [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) in perspective from *De prospectiva pingendi*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="De prospectiva pingendi"}

***De prospectiva pingendi*** (*On the Perspective of Painting*) is the earliest and only pre-1500 Renaissance treatise solely devoted to the subject of perspective.[^o2] It was written by the Italian master [Piero della Francesca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_della_Francesca) in the mid-1470s to 1480s,[^o3] and possibly by about 1474.[^o4] Despite its Latin title, the text is written in Italian.

:::

## The book {#The_book}

The subjects covered by Piero della Francesca in these writings include [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and innovative work in both [solid geometry](wikipedia://en/507960) and [perspective](wikipedia://en/380405).[^o6][^o10]

The script consists of three parts:

- Part One *Disegno*, describing techniques for painting faces
- Part Two *Commensurazio*, describing perspectives
- Part Three *Coloro*, describing techniques for creating perspectives by using colours

## History {#History}

*De prospectiva pingendi* was probably created in the years between 1474 until 1482.[^o8][^o9][^o10]

The writings were inspired by the book *[De pictura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_pictura)* by [Leon Battista Alberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Battista_Alberti)[^o9] and references [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s *[Elements](wikipedia://en/244107)* and *[Optics](wikipedia://en/24877937)*.[^o3][^o10] The manuscript later came into the possession of the [Biblioteca Palatina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Palatina) in [Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parma)[^o10] before it was transferred to the [Biblioteca Ambrosiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblioteca_Ambrosiana).

Much of Piero's work was later absorbed into the writing of others, notably [Luca Pacioli](wikipedia://en/18473), whose *[Divina proportione](wikipedia://en/16175342)* (1509) discusses Piero's use of perspective, as well featuring an uncredited translation of Piero's entire work on solid geometry, *[De quinque corporibus regularibus](wikipedia://en/65240274)*.

In 1899 the work was first published in book form.[^o11]
