# Stickelberger's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stickelberger's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Stickelberger's theorem** is a result of [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), which gives some information about the [Galois module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module) structure of [class groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_group) of [cyclotomic fields](wikipedia://en/30872462). A special case was first proven by [Ernst Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117) (1847) while the general result is due to [Ludwig Stickelberger](wikipedia://en/18870377) (1890).[^o2]

:::

## The Stickelberger element and the Stickelberger ideal {#The_Stickelberger_element_and_the_Stickelberger_ideal}

Let $K_m$ denote the $m$th [cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462), i.e. the [extension](wikipedia://en/11634) of the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) obtained by [adjoining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunction_%28field_theory%29) the $m$th [roots of unity](wikipedia://en/171950) to $\mathbb{Q}$ (where $m\ge 2$ is an integer). It is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of $\mathbb{Q}$ with [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) $G_m$ isomorphic to the [multiplicative group of integers modulo $m$](wikipedia://en/1195234) $(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$. The **Stickelberger element** (of level $m$ or of $K_m$) is an element in the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) $\mathbb{Q}[G_m]$ and the **Stickelberger ideal** (of level $m$ or of $K_m$) is an ideal in the group ring $\mathbb{Z}[G_m]$. They are defined as follows. Let $\zeta_m$ denote a [primitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_root_of_unity) $m$th root of unity. The isomorphism from $(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ to $G_m$ is given by sending an element $a$ to $\sigma_a$ defined by the relation

$$\sigma_a(\zeta_m) = \zeta_m^a.$$

The Stickelberger element of level $m$ is defined as

$$\theta(K_m)=\frac{1}{m}\underset{(a,m)=1}{\sum_{a=1}^m}a\cdot\sigma_a^{-1}\in\mathbb{Q}[G_m].$$

The Stickelberger ideal of level $m$, denoted $I(K_m)$, is the set of integral multiples of $\theta(K_m)$ which have integral coefficients, i.e.

$$I(K_m)=\theta(K_m)\mathbb{Z}[G_m]\cap\mathbb{Z}[G_m].$$

More generally, if $F$ be any [Abelian number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_number_field) whose Galois group over $\mathbb{Q}$ is denoted $G_F$, then the **Stickelberger element of** $F$ and the **Stickelberger ideal of** $F$ can be defined. By the [Kronecker–Weber theorem](wikipedia://en/1082550) there is an integer $m$ such that $F$ is contained in $K_m$. Fix the least such $m$ (this is the (finite part of the) [conductor](wikipedia://en/26355302) of $F$ over $\mathbb{Q}$). There is a natural [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) $G_m\to G_F$ given by restriction, i.e. if $\sigma_\in G_m$, its image in $G_F$ is its restriction to $F$ denoted $\operatorname{res}_m\sigma$. The Stickelberger element of $F$ is then defined as

$$\theta(F)=\frac{1}{m}\underset{(a,m)=1}{\sum_{a=1}^m}a\cdot\mathrm{res}_m\sigma_a^{-1}\in\mathbb{Q}[G_F].$$

The Stickelberger ideal of $F$, denoted $I(F)$, is defined as in the case of $K_m$, i.e.

$$I(F)=\theta(F)\mathbb{Z}[G_F]\cap\mathbb{Z}[G_F].$$

In the special case where $F=K_m$, the Stickelberger ideal $I(K_m)$ is generated by $(a-\sigma_a)\theta(K_m)$ as $a$ varies over $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$. This is not true for general $F$.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

If $F$ is a [totally real field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_real_field) of conductor $m$, then[^o7]

$$\theta(F)=\frac{\varphi(m)}{2[F:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{\sigma\in G_F}\sigma,$$

where $\varphi$ is the [Euler totient function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_totient_function) and $[F:\mathbb{Q}]$ is the [degree](wikipedia://en/4382697) of $F$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

> **Stickelberger's Theorem**[^o10] Let $F$ be an abelian number field. Then, the Stickelberger ideal of $F$ [annihilates](wikipedia://en/328684) the class group of $F$.

Note that $\theta(F)$ itself need not be an annihilator, but any multiple of it in $\mathbb{Z}[G_F]$ is.

Explicitly, the theorem is saying that if $\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}[G_F]$ is such that

$$\alpha\theta(F)=\sum_{\sigma\in G_F}a_\sigma\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}[G_F]$$

and if $J$ is any [fractional ideal](wikipedia://en/460700) of $F$, then

$$\prod_{\sigma\in G_F}\sigma\left(J^{a_\sigma}\right)$$

is a [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126).

:::
