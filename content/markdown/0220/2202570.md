# Eduard Helly

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eduard Helly"}

**Eduard Helly** (June 1, 1884 in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866) – 28 November 1943 in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago)) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) after whom [Helly's theorem](wikipedia://en/1409506), [Helly families](wikipedia://en/670453), [Helly's selection theorem](wikipedia://en/6740565), [Helly metric](wikipedia://en/19088928), and the [Helly–Bray theorem](wikipedia://en/1518742) were named.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Helly earned his doctorate from the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) in 1907, with two advisors, [Wilhelm Wirtinger](wikipedia://en/2044921) and [Franz Mertens](wikipedia://en/41999).[^o3] He then continued his studies for another year at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238), also studying there at the same time, tells a story of Helly disrupting one of Courant's talks, which fortunately did not prevent [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) from eventually hiring Courant as an assistant.[^o6] After returning to Vienna, Helly worked as a tutor, gymnasium teacher, and textbook editor until [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), when he enlisted in the Austrian army.[^o3] He was shot in 1915, and spent the rest of the war as a prisoner of the Russians.[^o3] In one prison camp in [Berezovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryozovka,_Russia), [Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia), he organized a mathematical seminar in which [Tibor Radó](wikipedia://en/1490393), then an engineer, began his interest in pure mathematics.[^o7] While held in another camp at [Nikolsk-Ussuriysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ussuriysk), also in Siberia, Helly wrote important contributions on [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180).[^o8]

After a complicated return trip, Helly finally came back to Vienna in 1920, married his wife (mathematician Elise Bloch) in 1921, and also in 1921 earned his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation). Unable to obtain a paid position at the university because he was seen as too old and too Jewish, he worked at a bank until the financial collapse of 1929, and then for an insurance company. After the takeover of Austria by the Nazis in 1938, he lost that job as well, and escaped to America. With the assistance of [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736), he found teaching positions at Paterson Junior College and [Monmouth Junior College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monmouth_University) in New Jersey,[^o9] before moving with his wife to Chicago in 1941, to work for the [U.S. Army Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Corps_%28United_States_Army%29). In Chicago, he suffered two [heart attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack), and died from the second one.[^o3]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In the same 1912 paper in which he introduced [Helly's selection theorem](wikipedia://en/6740565) concerning the convergence of sequences of functions, Helly published a proof of a special case of the [Hahn–Banach theorem](wikipedia://en/13860), 15 years before [Hans Hahn](wikipedia://en/1075212) and [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) discovered it independently.[^o11] Helly's proof only covers continuous functions over closed intervals of the real numbers; the more general theorem requires the [ultrafilter lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafilter_lemma), a weakened variant of the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840), which had not yet been invented.[^o3] Along with Hahn, Banach, and [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185), Helly has subsequently been seen as one of the founders of the theory of [normed vector spaces](wikipedia://en/21538).[^o12]

His most famous result, [Helly's theorem](wikipedia://en/1409506) on the intersection patterns of [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292) in [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697), was published in 1923. The theorem states that, if *F* is a family of *d*-dimensional convex sets with the property that every *d* + 1 sets have a nonempty intersection, then the whole family has a nonempty intersection. [Helly families](wikipedia://en/670453), named after this theorem, are a [set-theoretic](wikipedia://en/27553) generalization of this intersection property: they are the [families of sets](wikipedia://en/723043) in which the minimal subfamilies with empty intersection consist of a bounded number of sets.
