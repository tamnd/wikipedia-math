# Thomas R. Kane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas R. Kane"}

**Thomas Reif Kane**[^o3] (March 23, 1924 – February 16, 2019)[^o4] was a professor emeritus of [applied mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mechanics) at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).[^o5]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Kane was born in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria). He immigrated to the United States with his parents in 1938 after Austria fell to [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany). In 1943, he enlisted in the [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army) and was stationed in the [South Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pacific_Ocean) as a [combat photographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_photography). From 1946 to 1953 he attended [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) during which he earned two BS degrees in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering), as well as an MS in civil engineering and a PhD in [applied mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mechanics).[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

In 1953, Dr. Kane joined the engineering faculty at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) as an assistant professor of mechanical engineering and three years later was promoted to associate professor. While at Penn, he served as a research engineer and on the committee whose focus was investigating the question of sabbatical leave.[^o3]

In the 1960s, Kane devised a method for formulating equations of motion for complex mechanical systems that requires less labor and leads to simpler equations than the classical approaches, while avoiding the vagueness of virtual quantities. The method is based on the use of partial angular velocities and partial velocities.[^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11]
