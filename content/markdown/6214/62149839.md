# Ludomir Newelski

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ludomir Newelski"}

**Ludomir Newelski** (born 27 November 1960, [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław)) is a Polish mathematician, specializing in [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358), and [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200).[^o2]

:::

He attended the 14th High School in Wrocław, where in April 1977, as a second-year student, he became one of the first laureates of the Polish Mathematical Olympiad in this school. He studied and graduated in mathematics at the [University of Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław) and then worked at the Mathematical Institute of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) (PAN). At PAN he received his PhD in 1987 and [habilitated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1991. He worked at PAN until 1994 and then moved to the University of Wrocław, where he now works. He obtained the rank of full professor in 1998.[^o2] From 2007 to 2016 Newelski was the director of the Mathematical Institute of the University of Wrocław.

Newelski was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1998 in Berlin.[^o4] He was the winner of the [Prize of the Foundation for Polish Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prize_of_the_Foundation_for_Polish_Science) in the field of exact sciences in 2001 "for work in the field of mathematical logic constituting a breakthrough in model theory and algebra".
