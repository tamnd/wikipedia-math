# Sturm–Picone comparison theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sturm–Picone comparison theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753), the **Sturm–Picone comparison theorem**, named after [Jacques Charles François Sturm](wikipedia://en/1181554) and [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009), is a classical theorem which provides criteria for the [oscillation](wikipedia://en/10144971) and [non-oscillation](wikipedia://en/10144971) of solutions of certain [linear differential equations](wikipedia://en/379868) in the real domain.

:::

Let $p_{i}$, $q_{i}$ for $i = 1, 2$ be real-valued continuous functions on the interval $[a, b]$ and let

1. $$(p_1(x) y^\prime)^\prime + q_1(x) y = 0$$
1. $$(p_2(x) y^\prime)^\prime + q_2(x) y = 0$$

be two homogeneous linear second order differential equations in [self-adjoint form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint_form) with

$$0 < p_2(x) \le p_1(x)$$

and

$$q_1(x) \le q_2(x).$$

Let $u$ be a non-trivial solution of (1) with successive roots at $z_{1}$ and $z_{2}$ and let $v$ be a non-trivial solution of (2). Then one of the following properties holds.

- There exists an $x$ in $(z_{1}, z_{2})$ such that $v(x) = 0;$ or
- there exists a $\lambda$ in **R** such that $v(x) = \lambda\,u(x)$.

The first part of the conclusion is due to Sturm (1836),[^o2] while the second (alternative) part of the theorem is due to Picone (1910)[^o3][^o4] whose simple proof was given using his now famous [Picone identity](wikipedia://en/10163390). In the special case where both equations are identical one obtains the [Sturm separation theorem](wikipedia://en/10165595).[^o5]
