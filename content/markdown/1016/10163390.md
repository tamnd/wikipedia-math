# Picone identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Picone identity"}

In the field of [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753), the **Picone identity**, named after [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009),[^o2] is a classical result about [homogeneous](wikipedia://en/6954092) linear second order differential equations. Since its inception in 1910 it has been used with tremendous success in association with an almost immediate proof of the [Sturm comparison theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturm_comparison_theorem), a theorem whose proof took up many pages in Sturm's original memoir of 1836. It is also useful in studying the [oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillation_%28differential_equation%29) of such equations and has been generalized to other type of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) and [difference equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_equation).

:::

The Picone identity is used to prove the [Sturm–Picone comparison theorem](wikipedia://en/10162277).

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="Picone identity"}

## Picone identity {#Picone_identity}

Suppose that *u* and *v* are solutions of the two homogeneous linear second order differential equations in [self-adjoint form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint_form)

$$(p_1(x) u')' + q_1(x) u = 0$$

and

$$(p_2(x) v')' + q_2(x) v = 0.$$

Then, for all *x* with *v*(*x*) ≠ 0, the following identity holds

$$\left(\frac{u}{v}(p_1 u' v - p_2 u v')\right)' = \left(q_2 - q_1\right) u^2 + \left(p_1 - p_2\right)u'^2 + p_2\left(u'-v'\frac{u}{v}\right)^2.$$

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

$$\left(\frac{u}{v}(p_1 u' v - p_2 u v')\right)'=\left(u p_1 u' -p_2v'u^2 \frac 1 v \right)' =u'p_1u' +u(p_1 u')' -(p_2v')'u^2 \frac 1 v-p_2 v' 2u u' \frac 1 v +p_2 v' u^2 \frac{v'}{v^2}=$$

$$= p_1u'^2-2p_2\frac{ u u' v'} v+p_2\frac{u^2 v'^2}{v^2}+u (p_1u')'- (p_2 v')'\frac{u^2}{v}=$$

$$= p_1u'^2-p_2u'^2+p_2u'^2-2p_2u'\frac {u v'} v+p_2\left(\frac{u v'}{v}\right)^2-u (q_1u)+ (q_2 v)\frac {u^2} v = \left(p_1 - p_2\right)u'^2 + p_2\left(u'-v'\frac{u}{v}\right)^2 + \left(q_2 - q_1\right) u^2$$

:::
