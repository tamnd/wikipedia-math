# Lars Hesselholt

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lars Hesselholt"}

**Lars Hesselholt** (born September 25, 1966) is a [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danes) mathematician who works as a professor of mathematics at [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University) in [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan), as well as holding a temporary position as Niels Bohr Professor at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen).[^o3][^o4] His research interests include [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/212250),[^o10] [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500),[^o10] and arithmetic algebraic geometry.[^o4]

:::

Hesselholt was born in [Vejrumbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vejrumbro), a village in the [Viborg Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viborg_Municipality) of Denmark.[^o3] He studied at [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University), earning a bachelor's degree in 1988, a master's degree in 1992, and a Ph.D. in 1994;[^o3] his dissertation, supervised by [Ib Madsen](wikipedia://en/38416618), concerned [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748). After postdoctoral studies at the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452), he joined the faculty of the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1994 as a [C.L.E. Moore instructor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.L.E._Moore_instructor), and stayed at MIT as an assistant and then associate professor, before moving to Nagoya in 2008.[^o3] Hesselholt's wife is Japanese, and when he joined the Nagoya faculty he became the first westerner with a full professorship in mathematics in Japan.[^o4] He is the managing editor of the *Nagoya Mathematical Journal*.[^o6]

Hesselholt became a [Sloan fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) in 1998,[^o7] and was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2002.[^o8] In 2012, he became one of the inaugural fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822),[^o9] and a foreign member of the [Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters).[^o10][^o11]
