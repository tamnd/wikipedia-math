# Børge Jessen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Børge Jessen"}

**Børge Christian Jessen** (19 June 1907 – 20 March 1993) was a [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best known for his work in [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), specifically on the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809), and in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), specifically on [Hilbert's third problem](wikipedia://en/152703).

:::

## Early years {#Early_years}

Jessen was born on 19 June 1907 in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen) to Hans Jessen and Christine Jessen (née Larsen). He attended [Skt. Jørgens Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skt._Jørgens_Gymnasium), where he was taught by the Hungarian mathematician [Julius Pal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Pal) during his first year.[^o4] In 1925, Jessen graduated from the gymnasium and enrolled at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen). During his time at the university he got to know [Harald Bohr](wikipedia://en/638305),[^o5] then a leading figure in Danish mathematics.[^o6] In 1928, Bohr established a collaboration with Jessen, which would last until Bohr's death in 1951.[^o7]

After receiving his master's degree in the spring of 1929, Jessen embarked on a stay abroad. Supported by the [Carlsberg Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Foundation), he spent the fall of 1929 at the [University of Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged), where he met [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126), [Alfréd Haar](wikipedia://en/491228), and [Lipót Fejér](wikipedia://en/3934357). He then spent the winter semester of 1929–30 at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where he attended lectures by [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) and [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230) while working on his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) thesis. On 1 May 1930 Jessen defended his thesis in Copenhagen. He later elaborated the thesis into an article that was published in *[Acta Mathematica](wikipedia://en/6860708)* in 1934.[^o5] The same year, he was appointed as a [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent#Northern_Europe) at The Royal Veterinary and Agricultural University in Denmark.[^o8]

In 1931,[^o4] Jessen married Ellen Pedersen (1903–1979), [cand. mag.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cand.mag.) in mathematics and the daughter of [Peder Oluf Pedersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peder_Oluf_Pedersen).[^o5] Jessen continued to travel frequently in the early 1930s, visiting [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), [Cambridge, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_England), the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale) and [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in America.

## Career {#Career}

Jessen was a professor of descriptive geometry at the [Technical University of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Denmark) from 1935 till 1942, when he moved back to the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) where he was professor from 1942 to 1977 when he retired. He was the president of the [Carlsberg Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Foundation) in 1955-1963[^o10] and one of the founders of the [Hans Christian Ørsted Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Christian_Ørsted_Institute). He was the Secretary of the Interim Executive Committee of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) (1950–1952), and in September 1951 he officially declared the founding of the Union, with its first [domicile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domicile_%28law%29) in Copenhagen.[^o11] He was also active in the [Danish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/22351308). After his death, the society named an award in his honor (Børge Jessen Diploma Award).
