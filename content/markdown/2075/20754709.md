# Derrick Niederman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Derrick Niederman"}

**Derrick Corson Niederman** is an [author](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Author), [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [game designer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_designer), and national squash champion. His most recent game, [36 Cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36_cube),[^o2] has been described by [Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters) as "a wolf in sheep's clothing" because its simple design belies the sophisticated mathematical intuition required for the solution. He received a B.A. in mathematics from [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale) and a Ph.D. in mathematics from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT).[^o4] Dr. Niederman is a math professor at the [College of Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Charleston).[^o5] In 2025, Niederman founded Wordsmith Games. The flagship game is called Tosswords. [^o6]

:::

***The New York Times Magazine* Sunday Crosswords**

Niederman has created a number of crossword puzzles and cryptic puzzles for *[The New York Times Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Magazine)* since the early 1980s. His October 5, 1997 crossword puzzle "Baseball by the Numbers" is often included in crossword puzzle anthologies.[^o8]

**Puzzle and Game Books**

Dr. Niederman has also published other game and puzzle books.

**36 Cube**

The [36 Cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36_cube) is a puzzle that requires the arrangement of "towers" in a 6x6 square block so that each color appears only once in each row and column, all the same height. The completed puzzle will then be a cube.[^o9] [Jerry Slocum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Slocum), a puzzle historian and one of the world's top puzzle collectors, finds [36 Cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36_cube) one of the most engaging puzzles in his extensive collection. "My first impression was that it was quite a beautiful puzzle. Its three-dimensional character and colors are very striking. Out of the 30,000 puzzles I own, it's quite distinctive and unique."[^o3] Dr. Niederman came up with the idea while writing a book on whole numbers, after unearthing a mathematical supposition from the 18th century. "It struck me as the basis for a potentially great 3-D puzzle, and what eventually became [36 Cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36_cube)."[^o3]

**Wordsmith Games**

Niederman founded Wordsmith Games in early 2025; its flagship game, called Tosswords, began production in the fall of that year.[^o10]
