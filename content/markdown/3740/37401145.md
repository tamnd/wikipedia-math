# Julius Wolff (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Julius Wolff"}

**Julius Wolff** (18 April 1882 – 8 February 1945)[^o3] was a [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people)-[Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for the [Denjoy–Wolff theorem](wikipedia://en/33962500) and for his boundary version of the [Schwarz lemma](wikipedia://en/1432589).[^o7] With his family he was arrested in Utrecht by the [Nazi occupation forces of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_in_World_War_II) on 8 March 1943 and transported to the [Bergen-Belsen concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen-Belsen_concentration_camp) on 13 September 1944, where he died of [epidemic typhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic_typhus) on 8 February 1945, shortly before the camp was liberated.[^o7][^o8]

:::

Wolff studied mathematics and physics at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), where he earned his doctorate in 1908 under [Korteweg](wikipedia://en/1978798) with thesis *Dynamen, beschouwd als duale vectoren*. From 1907 to 1917 he taught at secondary and grammar schools in [Meppel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meppel), [Middelburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middelburg,_Zeeland), and [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam). In 1917 Wolff was appointed [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of differential calculus, theory of functions and higher algebra at the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen) and in 1922 at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University). He was also a statistical advisor for the life insurance company (or co-operative distributive society) "Eigen Hulp," (a predecessor of [AEGON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegon_N.V.)) with offices at [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague).[^o10]
