# Collider (statistics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Collider"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [causal graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causal_graphs), a variable is a **collider** when it is causally influenced by two or more variables. The name "collider" reflects the fact that in [graphical models](wikipedia://en/447298), the arrow heads from variables that lead into the collider appear to "collide" on the [node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Node_%28graph_theory%29) that is the collider.[^o2] They are sometimes also referred to as **inverted forks**.[^o3]

:::

*Image: Collider(statistics).png.* [SEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_Equation_Modeling) model of a collider

The causal variables influencing the collider are themselves not necessarily associated. If they are not adjacent, the collider is *unshielded*. Otherwise, the collider is shielded and part of a triangle.[^o4]

The result of having a collider in the [path](wikipedia://en/590893) is that the collider blocks the association between the variables that influence it.[^o5][^o6][^o7] Thus, the collider does not generate an unconditional association between the variables that determine it.

Conditioning on the collider via [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997), stratification, experimental design, or sample selection based on values of the collider creates a non-causal association between **X** and **Y** ([Berkson's paradox](wikipedia://en/1409541)). In the terminology of causal graphs, conditioning on the collider opens the path between **X** and **Y**. This will introduce bias when estimating the causal association between **X** and **Y**, potentially introducing associations where there are none. Colliders can therefore undermine attempts to test causal theories.

Colliders are sometimes confused with [confounder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confounder) variables. Unlike colliders, confounder variables *should* be controlled for when estimating causal associations.

To detect and manage collider bias, scholars have made use of [directed acyclic graphs](wikipedia://en/204002).[^o8]

Randomization and quasi-experimental research designs are not useful in overcoming collider bias.[^o8]
