# Gustav Rümelin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gustav Rümelin"}

**Gustav Rümelin** (26 March 1815 – 28 October 1889) was a German statistician, [pedagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogue) and author.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Rümelin was born in [Ravensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravensburg). After studying theology at Tübingen, he devoted himself to teaching, became rector of a [Latin school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_school) in 1845, and became professor at the [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) of [Heilbronn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilbronn) in 1849, having in the meanwhile been a delegate to the [Frankfurt Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Parliament) in 1848. Called to [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) in 1850 to serve in the Board of National Education, he was head of a department in the Ministry of Public Instruction from 1856 to 1861, when he became director of the Statistic-Topographical Bureau. In 1867 he established himself as [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent) at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen) and was appointed its [chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_%28education%29) in 1870. Aside from various statistical and miscellaneous publications, he produced *Shakespeare-Studien* (2d ed. 1874), a much valued contribution to the [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare) literature. He died in [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tübingen).
