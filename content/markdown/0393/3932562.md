# E. J. G. Pitman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="E. J. G. Pitman"}

**Edwin James George Pitman** (29 October 1897 – 21 July 1993) was an [Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made significant contributions to [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). In particular, he is remembered primarily as the originator of the [Pitman permutation test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitman_permutation_test), [Pitman nearness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitman_nearness) and [Pitman efficiency](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Efficiency,_asymptotic).

:::

His work *the Pitman measure of closeness* or *Pitman nearness* concerning the [exponential families](wikipedia://en/339174) of [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) has been studied extensively since the 1980s by [C. R. Rao](wikipedia://en/1418009), [Pranab K. Sen](wikipedia://en/32302045), and others.[^o3]

The [Pitman&ndash;Koopman&ndash;Darmois theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitman&ndash;Koopman&ndash;Darmois_theorem) states that only exponential families of probability distributions admit a [sufficient statistic](wikipedia://en/140841) whose dimension remains bounded as the sample size grows.

## Biography {#Biography}

Pitman was born in Melbourne on 29 October 1897, and attended [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne), residing at [Ormond College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormond_College), where he graduated with First Class Honours. In 1926 he was appointed [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Tasmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tasmania), which he held until his retirement in 1962.

He was a founding member and second President of the [Australian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/4579582). He was also active within the [Statistical Society of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_Society_of_Australia), which in 1978 named the Pitman medal in his honour.

## Terminology {#Terminology}

- For "the [sum of squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum_of_squares_of_deviations_from_the_mean) of deviations from the mean," he coined the term **squariance**.
- For "the [logarithm of the likelihood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log-likelihood)" he coined the term **loglihood**.

However, neither of these terms caught on.

:::
