# Marcus du Sautoy

::: {#o5 .subject cue=lead name="Marcus du Sautoy"}

**Marcus Peter Francis du Sautoy** (;[^o6] born 26 August 1965)[^o2][^o7] is a British mathematician, [Simonyi Professor for the Public Understanding of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simonyi_Professor_for_the_Public_Understanding_of_Science) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford),[^o8][^o9] [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of [New College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College,_Oxford),[^o10] and author of [popular mathematics](wikipedia://en/2069923) and [popular science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_science) books.[^o11] He was previously a fellow of [All Souls College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Souls_College,_Oxford), and [Wadham College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadham_College,_Oxford), and served as president of the [Mathematical Association](wikipedia://en/2742403), an [Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_and_Physical_Sciences_Research_Council) (EPSRC) senior media fellow,[^o12][^o13] and a [Royal Society University Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_University_Research_Fellow).[^o14]

:::

In 1996, he was awarded the [title of distinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titles_of_distinction_awarded_by_the_University_of_Oxford) of Professor of Mathematics.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Du Sautoy was born in London to Bernard du Sautoy, employed in the computer industry, and Jennifer ( Deason) du Sautoy, who left the [Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Office) to raise her children.[^o2][^o52] He grew up in [Henley-on-Thames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henley-on-Thames). His grandfather, [Peter du Sautoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_du_Sautoy), was chairman of the publisher [Faber and Faber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faber_and_Faber), and managed the estates of [James Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Joyce) and [Samuel Beckett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Beckett).[^o16][^o52][^o17]

Du Sautoy was educated at [Gillotts Comprehensive School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillotts_School)[^o2] and [King James's Sixth Form College (now Henley College)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henley_College_%28Henley-on-Thames%29) and [Wadham College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadham_College,_Oxford), where he was awarded a [first class honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_honours) degree in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). In 1991 he completed a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics on [discrete groups](wikipedia://en/468536), [analytic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_group) and [Poincaré series](wikipedia://en/24146591), supervised by [Dan Segal](wikipedia://en/9276503).[^o18]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

*Image: Sautoy2 cropped.JPG.* Marcus du Sautoy in 2007

Du Sautoy's research "uses classical tools from number theory to explore the mathematics of symmetry".[^o44] Du Sautoy's academic work concerns mainly [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o20]

Du Sautoy is known for his work [popularising mathematics](wikipedia://en/2069923), and has been named by *[The Independent on Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent_on_Sunday)* as one of the UK's leading scientists. He has also served on the advisory board of Mangahigh.com, an online maths game website. He is a regular contributor to the BBC Radio 4's *[In Our Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Our_Time_%28BBC_Radio_4%29)* programme and has written for *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* and *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*. He has written numerous academic articles and books on mathematics, the most recent being an exploration of the current state of creativity in artificial intelligence, *The Creativity Code*.[^o21]

He is co-director of PRiSM, the Centre for Practice & Research in Science & Music at the [Royal Northern College of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Northern_College_of_Music), which he co-founded with composer [Emily Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Howard).[^o22]

In a 2006 article published in *[Seed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_%28magazine%29)* magazine, du Sautoy discussed the [Hilbert-Pólya conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert-Pólya_conjecture#Possible_connection_with_quantum_mechanics), a way for advances in quantum physics to provide insight into the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125).[^o23][^o24]

### Television {#Television}

Among many other programmes,[^o40] Du Sautoy presented the [BBC Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Four) television programme *Mind Games* and co-hosted the TV series *[School of Hard Sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Hard_Sums)* with [Dara Ó Briain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dara_Ó_Briain). On the latter show, he posed mathematical questions with real-world applications. Ó Briain and a guest then tried to solve the problems, using rigorous and experimental methods, respectively.

In December 2006, du Sautoy delivered the 2006 [Royal Institution Christmas Lectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institution_Christmas_Lectures) under the collective title *The Num8er My5teries*.[^o41] This was only the third time the subject of the lectures had been mathematics – on the first occasion, in 1978, when the lecture was delivered by [Erik Christopher Zeeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Christopher_Zeeman), du Sautoy had been a schoolboy in the audience. Other television work includes:

- *Mindgames* (BBC Four, 2004–5). Presented 20 episodes of puzzle gameshow with regular guests [Kathy Sykes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Sykes) and [Michael Rosen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Rosen_%28author%29).
- *The Music of the Primes* (BBC Four, 2005, BBC 2 2007). One-hour documentary based on his book.
- *Painting with Numbers* (Teachers TV 2006). Four fifteen-minute programmes covering numerous topics from risk and probability to concepts of infinity, from codes and cryptography to flowers and football.
- *The Num8er My5teries: [Royal Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institution) Christmas Lectures* (Channel 5, 2006),[^o41] five lectures about the great unsolved problems of mathematics.
- *[The Story of Maths](wikipedia://en/20029977)* (BBC Four, 2008)[^o42] is a four-part series first broadcast on [BBC Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Four). In this series he discovers techniques and theories from different times and cultures.
- *[Horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_%28BBC_TV_series%29): Alan and Marcus Go Forth and Multiply* ([BBC 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_2), 2009). [Alan Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Davies) embarks on a maths odyssey with the help of mathematician Marcus du Sautoy.
- *Horizon: The Secret You* (BBC 2, 2009). Marcus du Sautoy investigates self-awareness.
- *Horizon: How Long is a Piece of String?* (BBC 2, 2009). Alan Davies attempts to answer the proverbial question: How long is a piece of string? Featuring Marcus du Sautoy.
- *Horizon: What Makes a Genius?* (BBC 2, 2010). Marcus du Sautoy asks if geniuses' brains are fundamentally different from his.
- *The Beauty of Diagrams* (BBC Four, 2010). Produced by Michael Waterhouse and directed by Steven Clarke, Marcus du Sautoy discusses influential scientific diagrams, starting with Vitruvian Man, [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci)'s iconic anatomical drawing which follows the geometrical ideas of the Roman architect [Vitruvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitruvius).
- *[The Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Code_%282011_TV_series%29)* (BBC 2, 2011). A three-part documentary series which began broadcasting on 27 July 2011.
- *Faster Than the Speed of Light?* (BBC 2, 2011). Marcus du Sautoy discusses a recent discovery, the [faster-than-light neutrino anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faster-than-light_neutrino_anomaly), that neutrinos may travel faster than light. First broadcast on 19 October 2011.
- *[Horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_%28BBC_TV_series%29): The Hunt for AI* ([BBC 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_2), 2012). Marcus Du Sautoy asks how close mankind is to creating computers or robots that can think for themselves – artificial intelligence, AI. First broadcast on 3 April 2012.
- *[Dara Ó Briain's School of Hard Sums](wikipedia://en/35607118)* ([Dave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_%28TV_channel%29), 2012). Co-host with [Dara Ó Briain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dara_Ó_Briain). Dara and guests attempt to solve problems posed by Marcus Du Sautoy with mathematics or through trial and error. First broadcast on 16 April 2012.
- *[Precision: The Measure of All Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision:_The_Measure_of_All_Things)* ([BBC Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Four), 2013). Professor Marcus du Sautoy explores why we are driven to measure and quantify the world around us and why we have reduced the universe to just a handful of fundamental units of measurement. First broadcast on 10 June 2013.
- *The Secret Rules of Modern Living: Algorithms* ([BBC Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Four), 2015). Mathematician Professor Marcus du Sautoy demystifies the hidden world of algorithms. First broadcast on 24 September 2015.

### Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Du Sautoy was awarded the [Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prize) in 2001 by the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) for the publication of outstanding mathematical research. In 2009 he won the [Michael Faraday Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Faraday_Prize) from the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London) for "excellence in communicating science to UK audiences".[^o44] Du Sautoy was appointed [Officer of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (OBE) in the [2010 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_New_Year_Honours) "for services to Science". The [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath) awarded du Satoy an [Honorary Doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) in 2010. He was elected a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2012[^o46] and a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) in 2016.[^o44]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Du Sautoy lives in London with his family and plays [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football) (No 17 for Recreativo [Hackney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hackney,_London) FC) and the trumpet.[^o2] He met his wife Shani while a [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem).[^o2] They have three children, who are being raised [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish).[^o52][^o48]

Du Sautoy is an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist) but has stated that as holder of the [Simonyi Chair for the Public Understanding of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simonyi_Professorship_for_the_Public_Understanding_of_Science) he won't be pursuing an anti-religious agenda, perhaps suggesting a difference of emphasis compared with his predecessor in the past, [Richard Dawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dawkins). However, his focus is going to be "very much on the science and less on religion"[^o49] He has described his own religion as being "[Arsenal – football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_F.C.)", as he sees religion as wanting to belong to a community.[^o50] Du Sautoy is a supporter of [Common Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Hope), an organisation that helps people in [Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala).[^o51]

He is the grandson of [Peter du Sautoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_du_Sautoy) and his [godmother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godparent) was [Valerie Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerie_Eliot), the wife and widow of [T. S. Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._S._Eliot).[^o52]
