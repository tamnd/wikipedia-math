# Martin Beale

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Martin Beale"}

**Evelyn Martin Lansdowne Beale** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o3] (8 September 1928 – 23 December 1985) was a British [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician) and statistician who was one of the pioneers of [mathematical programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_programming).[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Beale was born in [Stanwell Moor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanwell_Moor) in the [Middlesex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex) county (now in [Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey)) of England. His father, Evelyn Stewart Lansdowne Beale, was a senior physicist at the [Anglo-Persian Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Persian_Oil_Company).[^o3] His mother, Muriel Rebecca (Betsy) Slade, was a leader in the [Women's Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Institute) and the great-niece of [Edmond Warre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmond_Warre).[^o6]

Beale was educated at [Winchester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_College) and at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), graduating with First Class Honours in mathematics in 1949 and gaining a diploma in mathematical statistics in 1950.[^o7][^o8] He then joined the Mathematics Group at the UK [Admiralty Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_Research_Laboratory), working under [Steven Vajda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Vajda) for 11 years, except for a leave of absence in 1957/58 to assist the Statistical Techniques Research Group at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10] In 1955 he extended [George Dantzig](wikipedia://en/13111)'s [Simplex Algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplex_Algorithm) to minimise a [quadratic function](wikipedia://en/187240).[^o11]

In 1961, Beale became a founder member of a computer services company CEIR (Corporation for Economic and Industrial Research), which [BP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BP) bought and renamed Scicon, and in 1967 he became visiting professor at [Imperial College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College,_London).[^o7][^o12][^o13]

## Honors {#Honors}

Beale was chairman of the [Mathematical Programming Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Programming_Society) from 1974 to 1976, vice-president of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) from 1978 to 1980, a Fellow of the [British Computer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Computer_Society), and a member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute). In 1979, he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) "for his applications of mathematical and statistical techniques to industrial problems and for his contributions to the theory of mathematical programming", and he was elected to the Council of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1984.[^o3] He was awarded the Silver Medal of the Operational Research Society in 1980, and became vice-president of the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139). He was also non-executive chairman of Beale International Technology.[^o9]

*[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* suggested that he "used his blend of theory and state-of-the-art practice to encourage several generations of young mathematicians and computer scientists," and that his "many papers and his seminal book *Mathematical Programming in Practice* were major influences in their field, with their succinctness and clarity."[^o9]

Beale's [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) memoir mentioned his "extraordinary skill" and "substantial contributions to knowledge".[^o3]

## Memorials {#Memorials}

- The [Mathematical Programming Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Programming_Society) awards the "Beale–Orchard-Hays" Prize in memory of him and William Orchard-Hays.[^o16]
- The [Operational Research Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Operational_Research_Society) awards the [Beale Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beale_Medal) for "the most outstanding sustained contribution to Operational Research",[^o17] and hosts the Beale Lectures in his honour[^o18] as "one of OR's true greats".[^o19]
- A two-day symposium was held in his memory at the Royal Society in 1987.[^o20]
- The book *[Questions of Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Questions_of_Truth)* is dedicated to him and Ruth Polkinghorne.[^o21]
- He makes a cameo appearance in [E.M. Delafield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.M._Delafield)'s *A Provincial Lady Goes Further*.[^o22][^o23]

Wiley. isbn:9780471917601. <https://books.google.com/books?id=007vAAAAMAAJ&q=Introduction+to+Optimization++beale> (Based on his lecture notes and working papers at Scicon and edited by his former colleague Lynne Mackley.)

## Family {#Family}

Beale was a son of Muriel Rebecca Beale [OBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OBE), descendant of [General Sir John Slade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Slade,_1st_Baronet), grandniece of [Edmond Warre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmond_Warre), niece of [Henry Adolphus Warre Slade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Adolphus_Warre_Slade), daughter of Marcus Warre Slade [Queen's Counsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Counsel), and first cousin of [Madelaine Slade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirabehn).[^o22]
