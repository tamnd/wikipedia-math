# Superrigidity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Superrigidity"}

In mathematics, in the theory of [discrete groups](wikipedia://en/468536), **superrigidity** is a concept designed to show how a [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) ρ of a discrete group Γ inside an [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) *G* can, under some circumstances, be as good as a representation of *G* itself. That this phenomenon happens for certain broadly defined classes of [lattices](wikipedia://en/15782871) inside [semisimple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_group) was the discovery of [Grigory Margulis](wikipedia://en/1104948), who proved some fundamental results in this direction.

:::

There is more than one result that goes by the name of *Margulis superrigidity*.[^n1] One simplified statement is this: take *G* to be a simply connected semisimple real algebraic group in *GL*<sub>*n*</sub>, such that the [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) of its real points has [real rank](wikipedia://en/1139148) at least 2 and no compact factors. Suppose Γ is an irreducible lattice in G. For a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) *F* and ρ a linear representation of the lattice Γ of the Lie group, into *GL*<sub>*n*</sub> (*F*), assume the image ρ(Γ) is not [relatively compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_compact) (in the topology arising from *F*) and such that its closure in the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) is connected. Then *F* is the real numbers or the complex numbers, and there is a [rational representation](wikipedia://en/5784135) of *G* giving rise to ρ by restriction.
