# Distribution on a linear algebraic group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Distribution on a linear algebraic group"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), given a [linear algebraic group](wikipedia://en/395885) *G* over a field *k*, a **distribution** on it is a linear functional $k[G] \to k$ satisfying some support condition. A [convolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convolution_%28mathematics%29) of distributions is again a distribution and thus they form the [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714) on *G*, denoted by Dist(*G*), which contains the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) Lie(*G*) associated to *G*. Over a field of characteristic zero, Cartier's theorem says that Dist(*G*) is isomorphic to the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) of the Lie algebra of *G* and thus the construction gives no new information. In the positive characteristic case, the algebra can be used as a substitute for the [Lie group–Lie algebra correspondence](wikipedia://en/43302095) and its variant for algebraic groups in the characteristic zero; for example, this approach taken in (Jantzen 1987).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

### The Lie algebra of a linear algebraic group {#The_Lie_algebra_of_a_linear_algebraic_group}

Let *k* be an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) and *G* a [linear algebraic group](wikipedia://en/395885) (that is, affine algebraic group) over *k*. By definition, Lie(*G*) is the Lie algebra of all derivations of *k*\[*G*\] that commute with the left action of *G*. As in the Lie group case, it can be identified with the tangent space to *G* at the identity element.

### Enveloping algebra {#Enveloping_algebra}

There is the following general construction for a Hopf algebra. Let *A* be a Hopf algebra. The [finite dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_dual) of *A* is the space of linear functionals on *A* with kernels containing left ideals of finite codimensions. Concretely, it can be viewed as the space of matrix coefficients.

## Distributions on an algebraic group {#Distributions_on_an_algebraic_group}

::: {#o10 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

Let *X* = Spec *A* be an affine scheme over a field *k* and let *I*<sub>*x*</sub> be the kernel of the restriction map $A \to k(x)$, the residue field of *x*. By definition, a *distribution* *f* supported at *x*'' is a *k*-linear functional on *A* such that $f(I_x^n) = 0$ for some *n*. (Note: the definition is still valid if *k* is an arbitrary ring.)

Now, if *G* is an algebraic group over *k*, we let Dist(*G*) be the set of all distributions on *G* supported at the identity element (often just called distributions on *G*). If *f*, *g* are in it, we define the product of *f* and *g*, demoted by *f* \* *g*, to be the linear functional

$$k[G] \overset{\Delta}\to k[G] \otimes k[G] \overset{f \otimes g}\to k \otimes k = k$$

where Δ is the [comultiplication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comultiplication) that is the homomorphism induced by the multiplication $G \times G \to G$. The multiplication turns out to be associative (use $1 \otimes \Delta \circ \Delta = \Delta \otimes 1 \circ \Delta$) and thus Dist(*G*) is an associative algebra, as the set is closed under the muplication by the formula:

(\*) $\Delta(I_1^n) \subset \sum_{r=0}^n I_1^r \otimes I^{n-r}_1.$

It is also unital with the unity that is the linear functional $k[G] \to k, \phi \mapsto \phi(1)$, the [Dirac's delta measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac's_delta_measure).

The Lie algebra Lie(*G*) sits inside Dist(*G*). Indeed, by definition, Lie(*G*) is the tangent space to *G* at the identity element 1; i.e., the dual space of $I_1/I_1^2$. Thus, a tangent vector amounts to a linear functional on *I*<sub>1</sub> that has no [constant term](wikipedia://en/79861) and kills the square of *I*<sub>1</sub> and the formula (\*) implies $[f, g] = f * g - g * f$ is still a tangent vector.

Let $\mathfrak{g} = \operatorname{Lie}(G)$ be the Lie algebra of *G*. Then, by the universal property, the inclusion $\mathfrak{g} \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Dist}(G)$ induces the algebra homomorphism:

$$U(\mathfrak{g}) \to \operatorname{Dist}(G).$$

When the base field *k* has characteristic zero, this homomorphism is an isomorphism.[^o11]

:::

### Examples {#Examples}

#### Additive group {#Additive_group}

Let $G = \mathbb{G}_a$ be the additive group; i.e., *G*(*R*) = *R* for any *k*-algebra *R*. As a variety *G* is the affine line; i.e., the coordinate ring is *k*\[*t*\] and *I*${}^{n}_{0}$ = (*t*<sup>*n*</sup>).

#### Multiplicative group {#Multiplicative_group}

Let $G = \mathbb{G}_m$ be the multiplicative group; i.e., *G*(*R*) = *R*<sup>\*</sup> for any *k*-algebra *R*. The coordinate ring of *G* is *k*\[*t*, *t*<sup>−1</sup>\] (since *G* is really *GL*<sub>1</sub>(*k*).)

:::

### Correspondence {#Correspondence}

- For any closed subgroups *H*, **K*of*G*, if*k*is perfect and*H*is irreducible, then***

$$H \subset K \Leftrightarrow \operatorname{Dist}(H) \subset \operatorname{Dist}(K).$$

- If *V* is a *G*-module (that is a representation of *G*), then it admits a natural structure of Dist(*G*)-module, which in turns gives the module structure over $\mathfrak{g}$.
- Any action *G* on an [affine algebraic variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_algebraic_variety) *X* induces the representation of *G* on the coordinate ring *k*\[*X*\]. In particular, the conjugation action of *G* induces the action of *G* on *k*\[*G*\]. One can show *I*${}^{n}_{1}$ is stable under *G* and thus *G* acts on (*k*\[*G*\]/*I*${}^{n}_{1}$)<sup>\*</sup> and whence on its union Dist(*G*). The resulting action is called the [adjoint action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_action) of *G*.

## The case of finite algebraic groups {#The_case_of_finite_algebraic_groups}

Let *G* be an algebraic group that is "finite" as a [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523); for example, any [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) may be viewed as a finite algebraic group. There is an equivalence of categories between the category of finite algebraic groups and the category of finite-dimensional cocommutative Hopf algebras given by mapping *G* to *k*\[*G*\]<sup>\*</sup>, the dual of the coordinate ring of *G*. Note that Dist(*G*) is a (Hopf) subalgebra of *k*\[*G*\]<sup>\*</sup>.
