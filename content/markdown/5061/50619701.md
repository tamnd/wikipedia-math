# Geoffrey H. Moore

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Geoffrey H. Moore"}

**Geoffrey Hoyt Moore** (February 28, 1914 – March 9, 2000), whom *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal)* called "the father of leading indicators",[^o3] spent several decades working on business cycles at the [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Economic_Research),[^o4] where he helped build on the work of his mentors, [Wesley Clair Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_Clair_Mitchell) and [Arthur F. Burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_F._Burns).[^o5][^o6] Moore also served as commissioner of the [Bureau of Labor Statistics](wikipedia://en/72528) from March 1969 to January 1973.[^o3]

:::

In 1946 Moore was teaching statistics at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) and one of his students was [Alan Greenspan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Greenspan),[^o7] later chairman of the Federal Reserve, who would tell *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* that Moore was "a major force in economic statistics and business-cycle research for more than a half-century."[^o6] In 1956 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o8] In 1996 Moore founded the [Economic Cycle Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Cycle_Research_Institute) in New York City.[^o6]
