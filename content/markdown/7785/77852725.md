# Osserman manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Osserman manifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), an **Osserman manifold** is a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) in which the [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the [Jacobi operator](wikipedia://en/28868152) of [unit](wikipedia://en/167053) [tangent vectors](wikipedia://en/191999) is a constant on the unit [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794).[^o2] It is named after [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Robert Osserman](wikipedia://en/19663336).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $M^n$ be a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652). For a point $p \in M^n$ and a [unit vector](wikipedia://en/167053) $X \in T_pM^n$, the Jacobi operator $R_X$ is defined by $R_X = R(X,\cdot)X$, where $R$ is the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526).[^o5] A manifold $M^n$ is called **pointwise Osserman** if, for every $p \in M^n$, the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137) of the [Jacobi operator](wikipedia://en/28868152) does not depend on the choice of the unit vector $X$. The manifold is called **globally Osserman** if the spectrum depends neither on $X$ nor on $p$. All two-point [homogeneous spaces](wikipedia://en/363325) are globally Osserman, including [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$, [real projective spaces](wikipedia://en/1014694) $\mathbb{RP}^n$, [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) $\mathbb{S}^n$, [hyperbolic spaces](wikipedia://en/318742) $\mathbb{H}^n$, [complex projective spaces](wikipedia://en/762977) $\mathbb{CP}^n$, [complex hyperbolic spaces](wikipedia://en/69557765) $\mathbb{CH}^n$, [quaternionic projective spaces](wikipedia://en/2808931) $\mathbb{HP}^n$, [quaternionic hyperbolic spaces](wikipedia://en/1071730) $\mathbb{HH}^n$, the [Cayley projective plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley_projective_plane) $\mathbb{C}ayP^2$, and the [Cayley hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/9410951) $\mathbb{C}ayH^2$.[^o5]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

*Clifford structures* are fundamental in studying Osserman manifolds. An [algebraic curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) $R$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ has a $\text{Cliff}(\nu)$-structure if it can be expressed as

$$R(X,Y)Z = \lambda_0(\langle X,Z \rangle Y - \langle Y,Z \rangle X) + \sum_{i=1}^{\nu} \frac{1}{3}(\lambda_i - \lambda_0)(2\langle J_iX,Y \rangle J_iZ + \langle J_iZ,Y \rangle J_iX - \langle J_iZ,X \rangle J_iY)$$

where $J_i$ are [skew-symmetric](wikipedia://en/1176) [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) [operators](wikipedia://en/22213) satisfying the Hurwitz relations $J_iJ_j + J_jJ_i = -2\delta_{ij}I$.[^o8] A Riemannian manifold is said to have $\text{Cliff}(\nu)$-structure if its curvature tensor also does. These structures naturally arise from unitary representations of [Clifford algebras](wikipedia://en/45305) and provide a way to construct examples of Osserman manifolds. The study of Osserman manifolds has connections to [isospectral geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isospectral_geometry), [Einstein manifolds](wikipedia://en/644814), [curvature operators](wikipedia://en/651361#Curvature_operator) in differential geometry, and the classification of [symmetric spaces](wikipedia://en/1642877).[^o5]

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Osserman conjecture"}

## Osserman conjecture {#Osserman_conjecture}

::: {#o11 .problem cue=unsolved}

Are all Osserman manifolds either [flat](wikipedia://en/12756616) or locally rank-one [symmetric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_spaces)?

:::

The **Osserman conjecture** asks whether every Osserman manifold is either a [flat manifold](wikipedia://en/12756616) or locally a rank-one [symmetric space](wikipedia://en/1642877).[^o12]

Considerable progress has been made on this conjecture, with proofs established for manifolds of dimension $n$ where $n$ is not divisible by 4 or $n = 4$. For pointwise Osserman manifolds, the conjecture holds in dimensions $n \neq 2$ not divisible by 4. The case of manifolds with exactly two [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the Jacobi operator has been extensively studied, with the conjecture proven except for specific cases in dimension 16.[^o5]

:::
