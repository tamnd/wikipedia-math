# Marion Beiter

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Marion Beiter"}

[Sister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_sister_%28Catholic%29) **Marion Beiter** [OSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St._Francis) (August 23, 1907 – October 11, 1982), born **Dorothy Katharine Beiter**, was an American mathematician and educator. Her research focused on the area of [cyclotomic polynomials](wikipedia://en/171992).

:::

Beiter was born in [Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York) to Kathryn (Kiel) and Edward Frederick Beiter, where she attended [Sacred Heart Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Academy_of_the_Sacred_Heart). She entered the [Sisters of St. Francis of Penance and Christian Charity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisters_of_St._Francis_of_Penance_and_Christian_Charity) in 1923, and professed her [final vows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_vows) in 1929.

She began her career in 1925 as a teacher in parochial and private schools, continuing in this capacity until 1952, when she was appointed chairwoman of the mathematics department of [Rosary Hill College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daemen_College). She meanwhile graduated from [Canisius College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canisius_College) (1944) and [St. Bonaventure University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Bonaventure_University) (1948), before obtaining a PhD from the [Catholic University of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_America) in 1960. In her work on cyclotomic polynomials and their coefficients she made a conjecture referred to as the [Sister Beiter conjecture](wikipedia://en/73599200). Besides a sabbatical year at the [State University of New York at Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Buffalo) in 1971–1972, Beiter remained at Rosary Hill until her retirement in May 1977.

Beiter died in 1982 of a series of [strokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke).
