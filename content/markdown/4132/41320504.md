# Girth (functional analysis)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Girth"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **girth** of a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) is the [infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum) of lengths of [centrally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrally_symmetric) [simple closed curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_closed_curve) in the [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090) of the space. Equivalently, it is twice the infimum of distances between opposite points of the sphere, as measured within the sphere.[^o2][^o3]

:::

Every finite-dimensional Banach space has a pair of opposite points on the unit sphere that achieves the minimum distance, and a centrally symmetric simple closed curve that achieves the minimum length. However, such a curve may not always exist in infinite-dimensional spaces.[^o2]

The girth is always at least four, because the shortest path on the unit sphere between two opposite points cannot be shorter than the length-two line segment connecting them through the origin of the space. A Banach space for which it is exactly four is said to be *flat*. There exist flat Banach spaces of infinite dimension in which the girth is achieved by a minimum-length curve; an example is the space *C*\[0,1\] of continuous functions from the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) to the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), with the [sup norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sup_norm). The unit sphere of such a space has the counterintuitive property that certain pairs of opposite points have the same distance within the sphere that they do in the whole space.[^o4]

The girth is a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) on the [Banach–Mazur compactum](wikipedia://en/20021256), a space whose points correspond to the normed vector spaces of a given dimension.[^o3] The girth of the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) of a normed vector space is always equal to the girth of the original space.[^o3][^o5]
