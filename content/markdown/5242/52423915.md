# Rick Jardine

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rick Jardine"}

**John Frederick** "**Rick**" **Jardine** (born December 6, 1951, in [Belleville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belleville,_Ontario), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada)) is a Canadian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in the fields of [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Jardine obtained his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) in 1981, with thesis *Algebraic Homotopy* written under the direction of Roy Douglas. Following a research fellowship at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) and a Dickson instructorship at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), he joined the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Western Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Ontario) in 1984, where he is currently an emeritus professor.[^o4][^o5]

From 2002 to 2016, Jardine held a [Canada Research Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Research_Chair) in applied homotopy theory. Since 2008, he is fellow of the [Fields Institute](wikipedia://en/2495768), and has been recognized with the [Coxeter–James Prize](wikipedia://en/18739444) in 1992 by the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055).[^o5] In 2018 the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) listed him in their inaugural class of fellows.[^o6]
