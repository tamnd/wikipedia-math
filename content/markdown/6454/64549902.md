# Birth process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Birth process"}

*Image: Birth process.svg.* A birth process with birth rates $\lambda_0, \lambda_1, \lambda_2, ...$.

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **birth process** or a **pure birth process**[^n1] is a special case of a [continuous-time Markov process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous-time_Markov_process) and a generalisation of a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process). It defines a continuous process which takes values in the [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers) and can only increase by one (a "birth") or remain unchanged. This is a type of [birth–death process](wikipedia://en/2276797) with no deaths. The rate at which births occur is given by an [exponential random variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_random_variable) whose parameter depends only on the current value of the process

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Birth rates definition {#Birth_rates_definition}

A birth process with birth rates $(\lambda_n, n\in \mathbb{N})$ and initial value $k\in \mathbb{N}$ is a minimal right-continuous process $(X_t, t\ge 0)$ such that $X_0=k$ and the interarrival times $T_i = \inf\{t\ge 0: X_t=i+1\} - \inf\{t\ge 0: X_t=i\}$ are independent [exponential random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_random_variable) with parameter $\lambda_i$.[^n2]

### Infinitesimal definition {#Infinitesimal_definition}

A birth process with rates $(\lambda_n, n\in \mathbb{N})$ and initial value $k\in \mathbb{N}$ is a process $(X_t, t\ge 0)$ such that:

- $$X_0=k$$
- $$\forall s,t\ge 0: s<t\implies X_s \le X_t$$
- $$\mathbb{P}(X_{t+h}=X_t+1)=\lambda_{X_t}h+o(h)$$
- $$\mathbb{P}(X_{t+h}=X_t)=o(h)$$
- $\forall s,t\ge 0: s<t\implies X_t-X_s$ is independent of $(X_u, u < s)$

(The third and fourth conditions use [little o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_o) notation.)

These conditions ensure that the process starts at $i$, is non-decreasing and has independent single births continuously at rate $\lambda_n$, when the process has value $n$.[^n3]

### Continuous-time Markov chain definition {#Continuous-time_Markov_chain_definition}

A birth process can be defined as a [continuous-time Markov process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous-time_Markov_process) (CTMC) $(X_t, t\ge 0)$ with the non-zero Q-matrix entries $q_{n,n+1}=\lambda_n=-q_{n,n}$ and initial distribution $i$ (the random variable which takes value $i$ with probability 1).[^n4]

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix} -\lambda_0 & \lambda_0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\ 0 & -\lambda_1 & \lambda_1 & 0 & \cdots \\ 0 & 0 & -\lambda_2 & \lambda_2 & \cdots\\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \ddots \end{pmatrix}$$

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variations"}

### Variations {#Variations}

Some authors require that a birth process start from 0 i.e. that $X_0=0$,[^n3] while others allow the initial value to be given by a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) on the natural numbers.[^n2] The [state space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_space) can include infinity, in the case of an explosive birth process.[^n2] The birth rates are also called intensities.[^n3]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

As for CTMCs, a birth process has the [Markov property](wikipedia://en/306422). The CTMC definitions for communicating classes, irreducibility and so on apply to birth processes. By the conditions for recurrence and transience of a [birth–death process](wikipedia://en/2276797),[^n5] any birth process is transient. The transition matrices $((p_{i,j}(t))_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}), t\ge 0)$ of a birth process satisfy the [Kolmogorov forward and backward equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov_equations_%28Markov_jump_process%29).

The backwards equations are:[^n6]

$p'_{i,j}(t)=\lambda_i (p_{i+1,j}(t)-p_{i,j}(t))$ (for $i,j\in\mathbb{N}$)

The forward equations are:[^n7]

$p'_{i,i}(t)=-\lambda_i p_{i,i}(t)$ (for $i\in\mathbb{N}$)

$p'_{i,j}(t)=\lambda_{j-1}p_{i,j-1}(t)-\lambda_j p_{i,j}(t)$ (for $j\ge i+1$)

From the forward equations it follows that:[^n7]

$p_{i,i}(t)=e^{-\lambda_i t}$ (for $i\in\mathbb{N}$)

$p_{i,j}(t)=\lambda_{j-1}e^{-\lambda_j t}\int_0^t e^{\lambda_j s}p_{i,j-1}(s)\, \text{d} s$ (for $j\ge i+1$)

Unlike a Poisson process, a birth process may have infinitely many births in a finite amount of time. We define $T_\infty=\sup \{T_n:n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ and say that a birth process explodes if $T_\infty$ is finite. If $\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{\lambda_n}<\infty$ then the process is explosive with probability 1; otherwise, it is non-explosive with probability 1 ("honest").[^n8][^n9]

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Poisson process.svg.* A [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) is a special case of a birth process.

A [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) is a birth process where the birth rates are constant i.e. $\lambda_n=\lambda$ for some $\lambda>0$.[^n3]

::: {#o29 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Simple birth process"}

### Simple birth process {#Simple_birth_process}

*Image: Simple birth process.svg.* A simple birth process, where birth rates are equal to the size of the current population.

A **simple birth process** is a birth process with rates $\lambda_n=n\lambda$.[^n10] It models a population in which each individual gives birth repeatedly and independently at rate $\lambda$. [Udny Yule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udny_Yule) studied the processes, so they may be known as **Yule processes**.[^n11]

The number of births in time $t$ from a simple birth process of population $n$ is given by:[^n3]

$$p_{n,n+m}(t)=\binom{n}{m}(\lambda t)^m(1-\lambda t)^{n-m}+o(h)$$

In exact form, the number of births is the [negative binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/45177) with parameters $n$ and $e^{-\lambda t}$. For the special case $n=1$, this is the [geometric distribution](wikipedia://en/45922) with success rate $e^{-\lambda t}$.[^n12]

The [expectation](wikipedia://en/9653) of the process grows exponentially; specifically, if $X_0=1$ then $\mathbb{E}(X_t)=e^{\lambda t}$.[^n10]

A simple birth process with immigration is a modification of this process with rates $\lambda_n=n\lambda+\nu$. This models a population with births by each population member in addition to a constant rate of immigration into the system.[^n3]
