# Herbert Wilf

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Herbert Wilf"}

**Herbert Saul Wilf** (June 13, 1931 – January 7, 2012) was an American mathematician, specializing in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). He was the [Thomas A. Scott Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Scott_Professorship_of_Mathematics) in Combinatorial Analysis and Computing at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania). He wrote numerous books and research papers. Together with [Neil Calkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Calkin) he founded *[The Electronic Journal of Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Electronic_Journal_of_Combinatorics)* in 1994 and was its editor-in-chief until 2001.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Wilf was the author of numerous papers and books, and was adviser and mentor to many students and colleagues. His collaborators include [Doron Zeilberger](wikipedia://en/1724825) and [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095). One of Wilf's former students is [Richard Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Garfield), the creator of the [collectible card game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectible_card_game) *[Magic: The Gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering)*. He also served as a thesis advisor for [E. Roy Weintraub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._Roy_Weintraub) in the late 1960s.

Wilf died of a progressive [neuromuscular disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromuscular_disease) in 2012.[^o4]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 1996, Wilf received the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics).[^o6]

In 1998, Wilf and Zeilberger received the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Seminal Contribution to Research for their joint paper, "Rational functions certify combinatorial identities" (*[Journal of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society)*, 3 (1990) 147–158).[^o7] The prize citation reads: "New mathematical ideas can have an impact on experts in a field, on people outside the field, and on how the field develops after the idea has been introduced. The remarkably simple idea of the work of Wilf and Zeilberger has already changed a part of mathematics for the experts, for the high-level users outside the area, and the area itself." Their work has been translated into computer packages that have simplified [hypergeometric summation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_summation).

In 2002, Wilf was awarded the [Euler Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_Medal) by the [Institute of Combinatorics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/17545919).
