# Mouse (set theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mouse"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), a **mouse** is a small [model](wikipedia://en/19858) of (a fragment of) [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214) with desirable properties. The exact definition depends on the context. In most cases, there is a technical definition of "premouse" and an added condition of iterability (referring to the existence of [wellfounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellfounded) iterated [ultrapowers](wikipedia://en/273642)): a mouse is then an iterable premouse. The notion of mouse generalizes the concept of a level of [Gödel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel)'s [constructible hierarchy](wikipedia://en/343335) while being able to incorporate [large cardinals](wikipedia://en/383644).

:::

Mice are important ingredients of the construction of [core models](wikipedia://en/3781904). The concept was isolated by [Ronald Jensen](wikipedia://en/3785996) in the 1970s and has been used since then in core model constructions of many authors.

Generally, a mouse exists iff [$0^\sharp$ exists](wikipedia://en/248085)[^o2]<sup>p. 661</sup>, although some conventions treat any level of the constructible hierarchy as a *passive* mouse; its iterated ultrapowers are vacuously well-founded because there are no [extenders](wikipedia://en/24047112) on its sequence with which to take an ultrapower.
