# Ronald Jensen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ronald Jensen"}

**Ronald Björn Jensen** (April 1, 1936 – September 16, 2025) was an American mathematician who lived in Germany, primarily known for his work in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Jensen completed a BA in economics at [American University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University) in 1959, and a Ph.D. in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) in 1964. His supervisor was [Gisbert Hasenjaeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gisbert_Hasenjaeger). Jensen taught at [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University), 1969–71, and the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), 1971–73. The balance of his academic career was spent in Europe at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo), the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ludwigs_University_of_Freiburg), the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), and the [Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt-Universität_zu_Berlin), from which he retired in 2001.

He was honored by the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438) as the first [Gödel Lecturer](wikipedia://en/69216431) in 1990.[^o4] In 2015, the European Set Theory Society awarded him and [John R. Steel](wikipedia://en/2290967) the [Hausdorff Medal](wikipedia://en/62036856) for their paper *K without the measurable*.[^o5]

Jensen died on September 16, 2025, at the age of 89.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .statement cue=heading name="Results"}

## Results {#Results}

Jensen's better-known results include the:

- [Axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory) [NFU](wikipedia://en/945957), a variant of [New Foundations](wikipedia://en/945957) (NF) where [extensionality](wikipedia://en/52376) is weakened to allow several sets with no elements, and the proof of NFU's [consistency](wikipedia://en/75802) relative to [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic);
- Fine structure theory of the [constructible universe](wikipedia://en/343335) **L**. This work led to his being awarded in 2003 the [Leroy P. Steele Prize for Seminal Contribution to Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leroy_P_Steele_Prize) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for his 1972 paper titled "The fine structure of the constructible hierarchy";
- Definitions and proofs of various [infinitary combinatorial principles](wikipedia://en/15959032) in **L**, including [diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamondsuit) $\diamondsuit$, [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_square), and [morass](wikipedia://en/20194751);
- [Jensen's covering theorem](wikipedia://en/25646402) for **L**;
- General theory of [core models](wikipedia://en/3781904) and the construction of the [Dodd–Jensen core model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodd–Jensen_core_model);
- Consistency of the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705) plus [Suslin's hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin's_hypothesis);
- Technique of [coding the universe by a real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coding_the_universe_by_a_real).
