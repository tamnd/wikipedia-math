# Petersson inner product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Petersson inner product"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) the **Petersson inner product** is an [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) defined on the space of entire [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000). It was introduced by the German mathematician [Hans Petersson](wikipedia://en/9079432).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathbb{M}_k$ be the space of entire modular forms of weight $k$ and $\mathbb{S}_k$ the space of [cusp forms](wikipedia://en/610165).

The mapping $\langle \cdot , \cdot \rangle : \mathbb{M}_k \times \mathbb{S}_k \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$,

$$\langle f , g \rangle := \int_\mathrm{F} f(\tau) \overline{g(\tau)} (\operatorname{Im}\tau)^k d\nu (\tau)$$

is called Petersson inner product, where

$$\mathrm{F} = \left\{ \tau \in \mathrm{H} : \left| \operatorname{Re}\tau \right| \leq \frac{1}{2}, \left| \tau \right| \geq 1 \right\}$$

is a fundamental region of the [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019) $\Gamma$ and for $\tau = x + iy$

$$d\nu(\tau) = y^{-2}dxdy$$

is the hyperbolic volume form.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The integral is [absolutely convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_convergent) and the Petersson inner product is a [positive definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definite_bilinear_form) [Hermitian form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_form).

For the [Hecke operators](wikipedia://en/521837) $T_n$, and for forms $f,g$ of level $\Gamma_0$, we have:

$$\langle T_n f , g \rangle = \langle f , T_n g \rangle,$$

i.e., the $T_n$ are [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408) with respect to the Petersson inner product. This can be used to show that the space of cusp forms of level $\Gamma_0$ has an orthonormal basis consisting of simultaneous [eigenfunctions](wikipedia://en/228601) for the Hecke operators and the [Fourier coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_coefficients) of these forms are all real.

:::
