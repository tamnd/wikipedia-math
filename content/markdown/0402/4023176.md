::: {#o5 .subject cue=lead name="Truncated tesseract"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **truncated tesseract** is a [uniform 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/1645899) formed as the [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429) of the regular [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112).

:::

There are three truncations, including a [bitruncation](wikipedia://en/6792316), and a tritruncation, which creates the *truncated 16-cell*.

The **truncated tesseract** is bounded by 24 [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29): 8 [truncated cubes](wikipedia://en/280529), and 16 [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606).

### Alternate names {#Alternate_names}

- Truncated tesseract ([Norman W. Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527))
- Truncated tesseract (Acronym: tat) (George Olshevsky and Jonathan Bowers)[^n1]

::: {#o16 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

The truncated tesseract may be constructed by [truncating](wikipedia://en/4224429) the vertices of the [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112) at $1/(\sqrt{2}+2)$ of the edge length. A regular tetrahedron is formed at each truncated vertex.

The [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate) of the vertices of a truncated tesseract having edge length 2 is given by all permutations of:

$$\left(\pm1,\ \pm(1+\sqrt{2}),\ \pm(1+\sqrt{2}),\ \pm(1+\sqrt{2})\right)$$

:::

### Projections {#Projections}

*Image: 3D stereoscopic projection truncated tesseract.PNG.* A [stereoscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereoscopy) 3D projection of a truncated tesseract.

In the truncated cube first parallel projection of the truncated tesseract into 3-dimensional space, the image is laid out as follows:

- The projection envelope is a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285).
- Two of the truncated cube cells project onto a truncated cube inscribed in the cubical envelope.
- The other 6 truncated cubes project onto the square faces of the envelope.
- The 8 tetrahedral volumes between the envelope and the triangular faces of the central truncated cube are the images of the 16 tetrahedra, a pair of cells to each image.

*Image: Tesseractihexadecachoron net.png.* [Net](wikipedia://en/1148129)

The **bitruncated tesseract**, **bitruncated 16-cell**, or **tesseractihexadecachoron** is constructed by a [bitruncation](wikipedia://en/6792316) operation applied to the [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112). It can also be called a **runcicantic tesseract** with half the vertices of a [runcicantellated tesseract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runcicantellated_tesseract) with a construction.

### Alternate names {#Alternate_names}

- Bitruncated tesseract/Runcicantic tesseract ([Norman W. Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527))
- Tesseractihexadecachoron (Acronym: tah) (George Olshevsky and Jonathan Bowers)[^n1]

::: {#o30 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

A tesseract is bitruncated by [truncating](wikipedia://en/4224429) its [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29) beyond their midpoints, turning the eight [cubes](wikipedia://en/6285) into eight [truncated octahedra](wikipedia://en/279654). These still share their square faces, but the hexagonal faces form truncated tetrahedra which share their triangular faces with each other.

The [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate) of the vertices of a bitruncated tesseract having edge length 2 is given by all permutations of:

$$\left(0,\ \pm\sqrt{2},\ \pm2\sqrt{2},\ \pm2\sqrt{2}\right)$$

:::

### Structure {#Structure}

The truncated octahedra are connected to each other via their square faces, and to the truncated tetrahedra via their hexagonal faces. The truncated tetrahedra are connected to each other via their triangular faces.

### Stereographic projections {#Stereographic_projections}

The truncated-octahedron-first projection of the bitruncated tesseract into 3D space has a [truncated cubical](wikipedia://en/280529) envelope. Two of the truncated octahedral cells project onto a truncated octahedron inscribed in this envelope, with the square faces touching the centers of the octahedral faces. The 6 octahedral faces are the images of the remaining 6 truncated octahedral cells. The remaining gap between the inscribed truncated octahedron and the envelope are filled by 8 flattened truncated tetrahedra, each of which is the image of a pair of truncated tetrahedral cells.

The **truncated 16-cell**, **truncated hexadecachoron**, **cantic tesseract** which is bounded by 24 [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29): 8 regular [octahedra](wikipedia://en/80177635), and 16 [truncated tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/279651). It has half the vertices of a [cantellated tesseract](wikipedia://en/3715231) with construction .

It is related to, but not to be confused with, the [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485), which is a [regular 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/505253#Four_dimensional_regular_polytopes) bounded by 24 regular octahedra.

### Alternate names {#Alternate_names}

- Truncated 16-cell/Cantic tesseract ([Norman W. Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527))
- Truncated hexadecachoron (Acronym: thex) (George Olshevsky and Jonathan Bowers)[^n1]

::: {#o46 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

The truncated 16-cell may be constructed from the [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) by truncating its vertices at 1/3 of the edge length. This results in the 16 truncated tetrahedral cells, and introduces the 8 octahedra (vertex figures).

(Truncating a 16-cell at 1/2 of the edge length results in the [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485), which has a greater degree of symmetry because the truncated cells become identical with the vertex figures.)

The [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate) of the vertices of a truncated 16-cell having edge length √2 are given by all permutations, and sign combinations of

(0,0,1,2)

An alternate construction begins with a [demitesseract](wikipedia://en/6833695) with vertex coordinates (±3,±3,±3,±3), having an even number of each sign, and truncates it to obtain the permutations of

(1,1,3,3), with an even number of each sign.

:::

### Structure {#Structure}

The truncated tetrahedra are joined to each other via their hexagonal faces. The octahedra are joined to the truncated tetrahedra via their triangular faces.

### Projections {#Projections}

#### Centered on octahedron {#Centered_on_octahedron}

*Image: Truncated16cell-trunc-tetrahedron-small.gif.* Octahedron-first parallel projection into 3 dimensions, with octahedral cells highlighted

The octahedron-first parallel projection of the truncated 16-cell into 3-dimensional space has the following structure:

- The projection envelope is a [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654).
- The 6 square faces of the envelope are the images of 6 of the octahedral cells.
- An octahedron lies at the center of the envelope, joined to the center of the 6 square faces by 6 edges. This is the image of the other 2 octahedral cells.
- The remaining space between the envelope and the central octahedron is filled by 8 truncated tetrahedra (distorted by projection). These are the images of the 16 truncated tetrahedral cells, a pair of cells to each image.

This layout of cells in projection is analogous to the layout of faces in the projection of the [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654) into 2-dimensional space. Hence, the truncated 16-cell may be thought of as the 4-dimensional analogue of the truncated octahedron.

#### Centered on truncated tetrahedron {#Centered_on_truncated_tetrahedron}

*Image: Truncated 16cell-trunc-tetrahedron-first.gif.* Projection of truncated 16-cell into 3 dimensions, centered on truncated tetrahedral cell, with hidden cells culled

The truncated tetrahedron first parallel projection of the truncated 16-cell into 3-dimensional space has the following structure:

- The projection envelope is a [truncated cube](wikipedia://en/280529).
- The nearest truncated tetrahedron to the 4D viewpoint projects to the center of the envelope, with its triangular faces joined to 4 octahedral volumes that connect it to 4 of the triangular faces of the envelope.
- The remaining space in the envelope is filled by 4 other truncated tetrahedra.
- These volumes are the images of the cells lying on the near side of the truncated 16-cell; the other cells project onto the same layout except in the dual configuration.
- The six octagonal faces of the projection envelope are the images of the remaining 6 truncated tetrahedral cells.
