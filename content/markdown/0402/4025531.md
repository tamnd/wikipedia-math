# Reflection formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reflection formula"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **reflection formula** or **reflection relation** for a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f$ is a relationship between $f(a - x)$ and $f(x)$. It is a special case of a [functional equation](wikipedia://en/364377). It is common in mathematical literature to use the term "functional equation" for what are specifically reflection formulae.

:::

Reflection formulae are useful for [numerical computation](wikipedia://en/21506) of [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function). In effect, an approximation that has greater accuracy or only converges on one side of a reflection point (typically in the positive half of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628)) can be employed for all arguments.

## Known formulae {#Known_formulae}

The [even and odd functions](wikipedia://en/592151) satisfy by definition simple reflection relations around $a = 0$. For all even functions,

$$f(-x) = f(x),$$

and for all odd functions,

$$f(-x) = -f(x).$$

A famous relationship is **Euler's reflection formula**

$$\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z) = \frac{\pi}{\sin{(\pi z)}}, \qquad z \not\in \mathbb Z$$

for the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) $\Gamma(z)$, due to [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902).

There is also a reflection formula for the general $n$-th order [polygamma function](wikipedia://en/406932) $\psi^{(n)}(z)$,

$$\psi^{(n)} (1-z)+(-1)^{n+1}\psi^{(n)} (z) = (-1)^n \pi \frac{d^n}{d z^n} \cot{(\pi z)}$$

which springs trivially from the fact that the polygamma functions are defined as the derivatives of $\ln \Gamma$ and thus inherit the reflection formula.

The [dilogarithm](wikipedia://en/2398437) also satisfies a reflection formula,[^o3][^o4]

$$\operatorname{Li}_2(z)+\operatorname{Li}_2(1-z)=\zeta(2)-\ln (z)\ln(1-z)$$

The [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) $\zeta(z)$ satisfies

$$\frac{\zeta(1-z)}{\zeta(z)} = \frac{2\, \Gamma(z)}{(2\pi)^{z}} \cos\left(\frac{\pi z}{2}\right),$$

and the [Riemann Xi function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_Xi_function) $\xi(z)$ satisfies

$$\xi(z) = \xi(1-z).$$
