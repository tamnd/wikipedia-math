# Tunnel number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tunnel number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **tunnel number** of a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808), as first defined by Bradd Clark, is a [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898), given by the minimal number of arcs (called tunnels) that must be added to the knot so that the complement becomes a [handlebody](wikipedia://en/864472). The tunnel number can equally be defined for [links](wikipedia://en/1161784). The boundary of a regular neighbourhood of the union of the link and its tunnels forms a [Heegaard splitting](wikipedia://en/864418) of the link exterior.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075) is the only knot with tunnel number 0.
- The [trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773) has tunnel number 1. In general, any nontrivial torus knot has tunnel number 1.[^o4]

Every link *L* has a tunnel number. This can be seen, for example, by adding a 'vertical' tunnel at every crossing in a diagram of *L*. It follows from this construction that the tunnel number of a knot is always less than or equal to its [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298598).

:::
