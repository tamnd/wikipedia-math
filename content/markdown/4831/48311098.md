# Loxodromic navigation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Loxodromic navigation"}

*Image: Loxodrome.png.* Rhumb line navigation path: β = constant

**Loxodromic navigation** (from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) *λοξóς*, oblique, and *δρóμος*, path) is a method of navigation by following a [rhumb line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhumb_line), a curve on the surface of the [Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_%28planet%29) that follows the same angle at the intersection with each [meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_%28geography%29). This serves to maintain a steady course in sailing.[^o2]

:::

Navigating on a spherical surface with a fixed [course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Course_%28navigation%29) ($\beta$ in the figure) results in a spiral path that approaches the [North Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pole) for courses ranging from 270º to 090º and the [South Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pole) for courses from 090º to 270º. On a [nautical chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nautical_chart) plotted according to the [Mercator projection](wikipedia://en/20771), a loxodromic course appears as a straight line.
