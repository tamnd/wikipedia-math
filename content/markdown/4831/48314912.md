# Essay d'analyse sur les jeux de hazard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Essay d'analyse sur les jeux de hazard"}

fr **Essay d'analyse sur les jeux de hazard** (*Essay on the Analysis of Games of Chance*) is a book on [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and mathematical [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) written by [Pierre Remond de Montmort](wikipedia://en/2380423) published in 1708 with an expanded second edition in 1713. It was the first comprehensive text published on probability theory.[^o2]

:::

With *Essay*, Montmort intended to incorporate and build upon [Jacob Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/64740)'s unfinished la, which remained unpublished at the time of Jacob's death. Both works applied theories of combinatorics and probability to analyze [games of chance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Games_of_chance) popular at the time. *Essay* additionally solved problems posed by [Christiaan Huygens](wikipedia://en/42127)' treatise De ratiociniis in ludo aleae (*On Reasoning in Games of Chance*, 1657) and proposed the solutions for new and more complex problems. *Essay* greatly influenced the thinking of Montmort's more famous contemporaries, [Nicolaus I Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/1151030) and [Abraham De Moivre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_De_Moivre).[^o2]

## Continuation of Montmort's work {#Continuation_of_Montmort's_work}

In 1710, Montmort began a three-year correspondence with Nicolaus I Bernoulli, Jacob Bernoulli's nephew, which was incorporated into the second edition of *Essay* published in 1713. The correspondence covers many topics, particularly the probability questions that arose from the original edition of the book.[^n1] Also that year, Nicolaus published Jacob's Ars Conjectandi, eight years after Jacob's death.[^o2]

The second edition of *Essay* is twice as long as the first edition and is divided into five sections:

1. A Treatise on Combinations;
1. Problems on Games of Chance;
1. Problem on Quinquenove;
1. Various Problems; and
1. Correspondence

The second section studies the card games: [Pharaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaon), [Lansquenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansquenet), Treize, [Bassette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassette_%28card_game%29), [Piquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piquet), [Triomphe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triomphe), [L'Ombre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Ombre), [Brelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brelan), Imperial and [Quinze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinze). The third section examines games played with dice: Quinquenove, Hazard, Esperance, [TricTrac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TricTrac), Trois Dez, Rafle, Trois Rafles, and Noyaux. The fourth section solves problems posed in Huygen's De ratiociniis in ludo aleae and poses four unsolved problems. The fifth section contains Montmort's correspondence with Nicolaus Bernoulli and introduces the [St. Petersburg paradox](wikipedia://en/466277) and the [Waldegrave problem](wikipedia://en/48545958).[^n1]
