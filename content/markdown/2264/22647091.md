# Albert Muchnik

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Albert Muchnik"}

**Albert Abramovich Muchnik** (2 January 1934 – 14 February 2019) was a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked in the field of foundations and [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He received his Ph.D. from [Moscow State Pedagogical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_Pedagogical_Institute) in 1959 under the advisorship of [Pyotr Novikov](wikipedia://en/1139033). From there, he wrote his dissertation titled *Solution to the Post Reducibility Problem.*[^o4] Muchnik's most significant contribution was on the subject of [relative computability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_computability). He and [Richard Friedberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_M._Friedberg) independently introduced the priority method which gave an affirmative answer to [Post's problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post's_problem) regarding the existence of [recursively enumerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursively_enumerable) [Turing degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_degrees) between **0** and **0'**. This result, now known as the [Friedberg–Muchnik theorem](wikipedia://en/61969687),[^o5][^o6] opened study of the Turing degrees of the recursively enumerable sets which turned out to possess a very complicated and non-trivial structure.

Muchnik also made significant contributions to Medvedev's theory of mass problems, introducing a generalisation of Turing degrees, called "Muchnik degrees", in 1963.[^o7] Muchnik also elaborated [Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov)'s proposal of viewing [intuitionism](wikipedia://en/19513) as "calculus of problems" and proved that the lattice of Muchnik degrees is [Brouwerian](wikipedia://en/19513).

Muchnik was married to the Russian mathematician Nadezhda Ermolaeva. Their son [Andrey Muchnik](wikipedia://en/72647224), who died in 2007, was also a mathematician working in foundations of mathematics.[^o8] Albert Muchnik died in February 2019.
