# Harrop formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harrop formula"}

In [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262), the **Harrop formulae**, named after [Ronald Harrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Harrop), are the class of [formulae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_%28logic%29) [inductively defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_definition) as follows:[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

- [Atomic formulae](wikipedia://en/4472066) are Harrop, including falsity (⊥);
- $A \wedge B$ is Harrop provided $A$ and $B$ are;
- $\neg F$ is Harrop for any well-formed formula $F$;
- $F \rightarrow A$ is Harrop provided $A$ is, and $F$ is any well-formed formula;
- $\forall x. A$ is Harrop provided $A$ is.

By excluding [disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disjunction) and [existential quantification](wikipedia://en/91420) (except in the [antecedent](wikipedia://en/804755) of implication), [non-constructive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_%28mathematics%29) predicates are avoided, which has benefits for computer implementation.

## Discussion {#Discussion}

Harrop formulae are more "well-behaved" also in a constructive context. For example, in [Heyting arithmetic](wikipedia://en/2545815) ${\mathsf{HA}}$, Harrop formulae satisfy a classical equivalence not generally satisfied in constructive logic:[^o2]

$$\neg \neg A \leftrightarrow A.$$

But there are still [$\Pi_1$-statements](wikipedia://en/186475) that are ${\mathsf{PA}}$-independent, meaning these are Harrop formulae $\forall x. A$ for which excluded middle is however not ${\mathsf{HA}}$-provable. The standard example is the arithmetized consistency of ${\mathsf{PA}}$ (or ${\mathsf{HA}}$-equivalently that of ${\mathsf{HA}}$). So

$${\mathsf{HA}}\vdash \neg\neg{\mathrm{Con}}({\mathsf{HA}})\leftrightarrow {\mathrm{Con}}({\mathsf{HA}})$$

but

$${\mathsf{HA}}\nvdash {\mathrm{Con}}({\mathsf{HA}})\lor \neg{\mathrm{Con}}({\mathsf{HA}}).$$

And a plain disjunction is never Harrop itself, in the syntactic sense. So the above is compatible with the fact that intuitionistic logic itself already proves $\neg\neg(P\lor \neg P)$ indeed for any $P$.

Note that Harrop formulea in the syntactic sense are equivalent to non-Harrop formulas, for example through [explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_falso) as $A\leftrightarrow (A\lor\bot)$.

## Hereditary Harrop formulae and logic programming {#Hereditary_Harrop_formulae_and_logic_programming}

A more complex definition of hereditary Harrop formulae is used in [logic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_programming) as a generalisation of [Horn clauses](wikipedia://en/333824), and forms the basis for the language [λProlog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ΛProlog). Hereditary Harrop formulae are defined in terms of two (sometimes three) recursive sets of formulae. In one formulation:[^o8]

- Rigid atomic formulae, i.e. constants $r$ or formulae $r(t_1,...,t_n)$, are hereditary Harrop;
- $A \wedge B$ is hereditary Harrop provided $A$ and $B$ are;
- $\forall x. A$ is hereditary Harrop provided $A$ is;
- $G \rightarrow A$ is hereditary Harrop provided $A$ is rigidly atomic, and $G$ is a *G*-formula.

*G*-formulae are defined as follows:[^o8]

- Atomic formulae are *G*-formulae, including truth(⊤);
- $A \wedge B$ is a *G*-formula provided $A$ and $B$ are;
- $A \vee B$ is a *G*-formula provided $A$ and $B$ are;
- $\forall x. A$ is a *G*-formula provided $A$ is;
- $\exists x. A$ is a *G*-formula provided $A$ is;
- $H \rightarrow A$ is a *G*-formula provided $A$ is, and $H$ is hereditary Harrop.

:::

## History {#History}

Harrop formulae were introduced around 1956 by Ronald Harrop and independently by [Helena Rasiowa](wikipedia://en/3482432).[^o3] Variations of the fundamental concept are used in different branches of [constructive mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_%28mathematics%29) and [logic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_programming).
