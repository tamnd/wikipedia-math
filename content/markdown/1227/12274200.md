# Herbrand structure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Herbrand structure"}

In [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983), a **Herbrand structure** $S$ is a [structure](wikipedia://en/4055928) over a [vocabulary](wikipedia://en/7788156#Other_conventions) $\sigma$ (also sometimes called a *signature*) that is defined solely by the syntactical properties of $\sigma$. The idea is to take the symbol strings of [terms](wikipedia://en/24885593) as their values, e.g. the denotation of a constant symbol $c$ is just "$c$" (the symbol). It is named after [Jacques Herbrand](wikipedia://en/159901).

:::

Herbrand structures play an important role in the foundations of [logic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_programming).[^o2]

## Herbrand universe {#Herbrand_universe}

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

The *Herbrand universe* serves as the universe in a *Herbrand structure*. The *Herbrand universe* of a first-order language $L^\sigma$, is the set of all [ground terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_term) of $L^\sigma$. If the language has no constants, then the language is extended by adding an arbitrary new constant.

The Herbrand universe is [countably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite) if $\sigma$ is [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) and a function symbol of [arity](wikipedia://en/42301) greater than 0 exists.

In the context of first-order languages we also speak simply of the *Herbrand universe* of the vocabulary $\sigma$.

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

Let $L^\sigma$, be a first-order language with the vocabulary

- constant symbols: $c$
- function symbols: $f(\cdot), \, g(\cdot)$

then the Herbrand universe $H$ of $L^\sigma$ (or of $\sigma$) is

$$H = \{ c, f(c), g(c), f(f(c)), f(g(c)), g(f(c)), g(g(c)), \dots\}$$

The [relation symbols](wikipedia://en/10983#Non-logical_symbols) are not relevant for a Herbrand universe since formulas involving only relations do not correspond to elements of the universe.[^o8]

:::

## Herbrand structure {#Herbrand_structure}

A *Herbrand structure* interprets terms on top of a *Herbrand universe*.

::: {#o11 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

Let $S$ be a [structure](wikipedia://en/4055928), with vocabulary $\sigma$ and universe $U$. Let $W$ be the set of all terms over $\sigma$ and $W_0$ be the subset of all variable-free terms. $S$ is said to be a *Herbrand structure* iff

1. $$U = W_0$$
1. $f^S(t_1,\dots,t_n) = f(t_1,\dots,t_n)$ for every $n$-ary function symbol $f \in \sigma$ and $t_1,\dots,t_n \in W_0$
1. $c^S = c$ for every constant $c$ in $\sigma$

:::

### Remarks {#Remarks}

1. $U$ is the Herbrand universe of $\sigma$.
1. A Herbrand structure that is a [model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_%28logic%29) of a [theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_%28logic%29) $T$ is called a *Herbrand model* of $T$.

::: {#o14 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

For a constant symbol $c$ and a unary function symbol $f(\,\cdot\,)$ we have the following interpretation:

- $$U = \{c, fc, ffc, fffc, \dots\}$$
- $$fc \mapsto fc, ffc \mapsto ffc, \dots$$
- $$c \mapsto c$$

:::

## Herbrand base {#Herbrand_base}

In addition to the universe, defined in , and the term denotations, defined in , the *Herbrand base* completes the interpretation by denoting the relation symbols.

::: {#o17 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

A *Herbrand base* $\mathcal B_H$ for a Herbrand structure is the set of all atomic formulas whose argument terms are elements of the Herbrand universe.

:::

::: {#o19 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

For a binary relation symbol $R$, we get with the terms from above:

$$\mathcal B_H = \{R(c,c), R(fc,c), R(c,fc), R(fc,fc), R(ffc,c), \dots\}$$

:::
