# Swift–Hohenberg equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Swift–Hohenberg equation"}

The **Swift–Hohenberg equation** (named after Jack B. Swift and [Pierre Hohenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Hohenberg)) is a [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) noted for its pattern-forming behaviour. It takes the form

:::

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = r u - (1+\nabla^2)^2u + N(u)$$

where *u* = *u*(*x*, *t*) or *u* = *u*(*x*, *y*, *t*) is a [scalar function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_function) defined on the line or the plane, *r* is a real [bifurcation](wikipedia://en/2326042) parameter, and *N*(*u*) is some smooth nonlinearity.

The equation is named after the authors of the paper,[^o2] where it was derived from the equations for thermal [convection](wikipedia://en/47526).

Another example where the equation appears is in the study of wrinkling morphology and pattern selection in curved elastic bilayer materials.[^o3][^o4]

The Swift–Hohenberg equation leads to the [Ginzburg–Landau equation](wikipedia://en/4597084).
