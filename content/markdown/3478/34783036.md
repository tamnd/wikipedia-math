# Decoupling (probability)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Decoupling"}

In [probability](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **decoupling** is a reduction of a [sample statistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_statistic) to an average of the statistic evaluated on several [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_independence) sequences of the [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685). This sum, [conditioned](wikipedia://en/24104134) on all but one of the independent sequences, becomes a sum of independent random variables. Decoupling is used in the study of [U statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_statistic), where decoupling should not be confused with Hoeffding's decomposition, however.[^o2] (Such "decoupling" is unrelated to the use of "[couplings](wikipedia://en/887545)" in the study of [stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_processes).)

:::
