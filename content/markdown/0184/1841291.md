# Powerful p-group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Powerful p-group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), especially in the study of [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664) and [pro-*p*-groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-p-group), the concept of **powerful *p*-groups** plays an important role. They were introduced in (Lubotzky Mann 1987), where a number of applications are given, including results on [Schur multipliers](wikipedia://en/1121587). Powerful *p*-groups are used in the study of [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) of *p*-groups (Khukhro 1998), the solution of the [restricted Burnside problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restricted_Burnside_problem) (Vaughan-Lee 1993), the classification of finite *p*-groups via the [coclass conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coclass_conjectures) (Leedham-Green McKay 2002), and provided an excellent method of understanding analytic pro-*p*-groups (Dixon du Sautoy Mann Segal 1991).

:::

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A finite *p*-group $G$ is called **powerful** if the [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847) $[G,G]$ is contained in the subgroup $G^p = \langle g^p | g\in G\rangle$ for odd $p$, or if $[G,G]$ is contained in the subgroup $G^4$ for $p=2$.

:::

## Properties of powerful *p*-groups {#Properties_of_powerful_p-groups}

Powerful *p*-groups have many properties similar to [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974), and thus provide a good basis for studying *p*-groups. Every finite *p*-group can be expressed as a [section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_%28group_theory%29) of a powerful *p*-group.

Powerful *p*-groups are also useful in the study of [pro-*p* groups](wikipedia://en/1842075) as it provides a simple means for characterising [*p*-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) analytic groups (groups that are [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) over the *p*-adic numbers): A finitely generated pro-*p* group is *p*-adic analytic if and only if it contains an [open](wikipedia://en/39358) [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) that is powerful: this is a special case of a deep result of [Michel Lazard](wikipedia://en/47432466) (1965).

Some properties similar to abelian [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664) are: if $G$ is a powerful *p*-group then:

- The [Frattini subgroup](wikipedia://en/540707) $\Phi(G)$ of $G$ has the property $\Phi(G) = G^p.$
- $G^{p^k} = \{g^{p^k}|g\in G\}$ for all $k\geq 1.$ That is, the *group generated* by $p$th powers is precisely the *set* of $p$th powers.
- If $G = \langle g_1, \ldots, g_d\rangle$ then $G^{p^k} = \langle g_1^{p^k},\ldots,g_d^{p^k}\rangle$ for all $k\geq 1.$
- The $k$th entry of the [lower central series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_central_series) of $G$ has the property $\gamma_k(G)\leq G^{p^{k-1}}$ for all $k\geq 1.$
- Every [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) of a powerful *p*-group is powerful.
- The [Prüfer rank](wikipedia://en/1202488) of $G$ is equal to the minimal number of generators of $G.$

Some less abelian-like properties are: if $G$ is a powerful *p*-group then:

- $G^{p^k}$ is powerful.
- [Subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgroups) of $G$ are not necessarily powerful.
