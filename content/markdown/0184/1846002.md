# Luigi Cremona

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Luigi Cremona"}

Luigi Cremona

**Antonio Luigi Gaudenzio Giuseppe Cremona** (7 December 1830 – 10 June 1903) was an Italian mathematician. His life was devoted to the study of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and reforming advanced [mathematical teaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_teaching) in Italy. He worked on [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) and [algebraic surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311), particularly through his paper *Introduzione ad una teoria geometrica delle curve piane* ("Introduction to a geometrical theory of the plane curves"), and was a founder of the [Italian school of algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/383424).[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Oldest son of Gaudenzio Cremona and Teresa Andreoli, Luigi Cremona was born in [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia) ([Lombardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardy)), then part of the [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire)-controlled [Kingdom of Lombardy–Venetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lombardy–Venetia). His youngest brother, only surviving sibling other than Luigi after Pietro and Francesca passed, was the painter [Tranquillo Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranquillo_Cremona).[^o6]

In 1848, when [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan) and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) rose against Austria, Cremona, then only seventeen, joined the ranks of the Italian volunteers. He remained with them, fighting on behalf of his country's freedom, until, in 1849, the capitulation of [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) put an end to the campaign.[^n1]

He then returned to Pavia, where he pursued his studies at the university under [Francesco Brioschi](wikipedia://en/6421852), and determined to seek a career as teacher of mathematics. He graduated in 1853 as *[dottore negli studi di ingegnere civile e architetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea)*.

Cremona is noted for the important role he played in bringing about the great geometrical advances in Italy. While, at the beginning of the nineteenth century, Italy had very little mathematical standing, the end of the century found Italy in the lead along geometric lines, largely as a result of the work of Cremona. He was very influential in bringing about reforms in the secondary schools of Italy and became a leader in questions of mathematical pedagogy as well as in those relating to the advancement of knowledge. The mathematical advances which Italy made since the middle of the nineteenth century were largely guided by Cremona, [Brioschi](wikipedia://en/6421852), and [Beltrami](wikipedia://en/543760).[^o8]

His first appointment was as elementary mathematical master at the gymnasium and lyceum of [Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremona), and he afterwards obtained a similar post at [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan). In 1860 he was appointed to the professorship of higher geometry at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna), and in 1866 to that of higher geometry and graphical statics at the higher technical college of Milan. In this same year he competed for the [Steiner Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steiner_Prize) of the [Berlin Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences), with a treatise entitled *Memoria sulle superfici del terzo ordine*, and shared the award with [J. C. F. Sturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._C._F._Sturm). Two years later the same prize was conferred on him without competition.[^n1]

As early as 1856 Cremona had begun to contribute to the *Annali di scienze matematiche e fisiche*, and to the *Annali di matematica*, of which he became afterwards joint editor. Papers by him appeared in the mathematical journals of Italy, France, Germany and England, and he published several important works, many of which have been translated into other languages. His manual *Graphical [Statics](wikipedia://en/28767)* and his *Elements of Projective Geometry* (translated by [Thomas Hudson Beare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hudson_Beare) and C. Leudesdorf respectively) were published in English by the [Clarendon Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarendon_Press).

In 1873 he was called to Rome to organize the Royal College of Engineering, and was also appointed professor of higher mathematics at the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome).[^o10] Cremona's reputation had now become European, and in 1879 he was elected a corresponding member of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society). In the same year he became a member of the [senate of the Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Kingdom_of_Italy). In 1898 he was briefly [minister for education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Public_Education_%28Italy%29).[^o11] The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences elected Cremona as member in 1901. The following year, he was awarded the German [Pour le Mérite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pour_le_Mérite) for Sciences and Arts.

He died in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) in 1903.[^o12] [Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremona_%28crater%29) on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) was named after him in 1964.[^o13]
