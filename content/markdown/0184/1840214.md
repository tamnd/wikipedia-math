# Conservative extension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conservative extension"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), a **conservative extension** is a [supertheory](wikipedia://en/3512103#Subtheories_and_extensions) of a [theory](wikipedia://en/3512103) which is often convenient for proving [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977), but proves no new theorems about the language of the original theory. Similarly, a **non-conservative extension**, or **proper extension**, is a supertheory which is not conservative, and can prove more theorems than the original.

:::

More formally stated, a theory *T*<sub>2</sub> is a ([proof theoretic](wikipedia://en/183478)) conservative extension of a theory *T*<sub>1</sub> if every theorem of *T*<sub>1</sub> is a theorem of *T*<sub>2</sub>, and any theorem of *T*<sub>2</sub> in the language of *T*<sub>1</sub> is already a theorem of *T*<sub>1</sub>.

More generally, if *Γ* is a set of [formulas](wikipedia://en/404582) in the common language of *T*<sub>1</sub> and *T*<sub>2</sub>, then *T*<sub>2</sub> is ***Γ*-conservative** over *T*<sub>1</sub> if every formula from *Γ* provable in *T*<sub>2</sub> is also provable in *T*<sub>1</sub>.

Note that a conservative extension of a [consistent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistent) theory is consistent. If it were not, then by the [principle of explosion](wikipedia://en/591394), every formula in the language of *T*<sub>2</sub> would be a theorem of *T*<sub>2</sub>, so every formula in the language of *T*<sub>1</sub> would be a theorem of *T*<sub>1</sub>, so *T*<sub>1</sub> would not be consistent. Hence, conservative extensions do not bear the risk of introducing new inconsistencies. This can also be seen as a [methodology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodology) for writing and structuring large theories: start with a theory, *T*<sub>0</sub>, that is known (or assumed) to be consistent, and successively build conservative extensions *T*<sub>1</sub>, *T*<sub>2</sub>, … of it.

Recently, conservative extensions have been used for defining a notion of [module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_modularization) for [ontologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_%28computer_science%29): if an ontology is formalized as a logical theory, a subtheory is a module if the whole ontology is a conservative extension of the subtheory.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- ACA<sub>0</sub>, a subsystem of [second-order arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3542454) studied in [reverse mathematics](wikipedia://en/326365), is a conservative extension of first-order [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic).
- The [subsystems of second-order arithmetic](wikipedia://en/326365) RCA${}^{\ast}_{0}$ and WKL${}^{\ast}_{0}$ are Π${}^{0}_{2}$-conservative over EFA.[^o4]
- The subsystem WKL<sub>0</sub> is a Π${}^{1}_{1}$-conservative extension of RCA<sub>0</sub>, and a Π${}^{0}_{2}$-conservative over [PRA](wikipedia://en/6637022).[^o4]
- [NBG](wikipedia://en/528491) is a conservative extension of ZFC (= [ZF](wikipedia://en/152214)+[AC](wikipedia://en/840)).
- [Internal set theory](wikipedia://en/865686) is a conservative extension of ZFC.
- [Extensions by definitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extension_by_definitions) are conservative.
- Extensions by unconstrained predicate or function symbols are conservative.
- IΣ<sub>1</sub> (a subsystem of Peano arithmetic with induction only for [Σ${}^{0}_{1}$-formulas](wikipedia://en/186475)) is a Π${}^{0}_{2}$-conservative extension of PRA.[^o5]
- ZFC is a [Π${}^{1}_{4}$](wikipedia://en/634233)-conservative extension of ZF by [Shoenfield's absoluteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absoluteness_%28mathematical_logic%29).[^o6]
- ZFC with the [generalized continuum hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_continuum_hypothesis) is a Π${}^{2}_{1}$-conservative extension of ZFC.[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Conservative extension (model theory)"}

## Model-theoretic conservative extension {#Model-theoretic_conservative_extension}

With [model-theoretic](wikipedia://en/19858) means, a stronger notion is obtained: an extension *T*<sub>2</sub> of a theory *T*<sub>1</sub> is **model-theoretically conservative** if *T*<sub>1</sub> ⊆ *T*<sub>2</sub> and every model of *T*<sub>1</sub> can be expanded to a model of *T*<sub>2</sub>. Each model-theoretic conservative extension also is a (proof-theoretic) conservative extension in the above sense.[^o10] The model theoretic notion has the advantage over the proof theoretic one that it does not depend so much on the language at hand; on the other hand, it is usually harder to establish model theoretic conservativity.

:::
