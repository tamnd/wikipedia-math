# Pro-p group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pro-p group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **pro-*p* group** (for some [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*) is a [profinite group](wikipedia://en/24266) $G$ such that for any [open](wikipedia://en/39358) [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) $N\triangleleft G$ the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) $G/N$ is a [*p*-group](wikipedia://en/24664). Note that, as profinite groups are [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), the open subgroups are exactly the [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) subgroups of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581), so that the [discrete](wikipedia://en/56061) quotient group is always finite.

:::

Alternatively, one can define a pro-*p* group to be the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of an [inverse system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_system) of discrete finite *p*-groups.

The best-understood (and historically most important) class of pro-*p* groups is the [*p*-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) analytic groups: groups with the structure of an analytic [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ such that group multiplication and inversion are both analytic functions. The work of [Lubotzky](wikipedia://en/22754130) and Mann, combined with [Michel Lazard](wikipedia://en/47432466)'s solution to [Hilbert's fifth problem](wikipedia://en/152760) over the *p*-adic numbers, shows that a pro-*p* group is *p*-adic analytic if and only if it has finite [rank](wikipedia://en/1202488), i.e. there exists a positive integer $r$ such that any closed subgroup has a topological generating set with no more than $r$ elements. More generally it was shown that a finitely generated profinite group is a compact *p*-adic [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) if and only if it has an open subgroup that is a uniformly powerful pro-*p* group.

The [Coclass Theorems](wikipedia://en/42823620#Pro-p_groups_and_coclass_trees) have been proved in 1994 by A. Shalev and independently by C. R. Leedham-Green. Theorem D is one of these theorems and asserts that, for any prime number *p* and any positive integer *r*, there exist only finitely many pro-*p* groups of coclass *r*. This finiteness result is fundamental for the classification of finite *p*-groups by means of [directed coclass graphs](wikipedia://en/42823620#Multifurcation_and_coclass_graphs).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The canonical example is the [*p*-adic integers](wikipedia://en/51423)

$$\mathbb{Z}_{p} = \displaystyle \varprojlim \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}.$$

- The group $\ GL_{n}( \mathbb{Z}_{p})$ of invertible *n* by *n* [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) over $\ \mathbb{Z}_{p}$ has an open subgroup *U* consisting of all matrices congruent to the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718) modulo $\ p\mathbb{Z}_{p}$. This *U* is a pro-*p* group. In fact the *p*-adic analytic groups mentioned above can all be found as closed subgroups of $\ GL_{n}( \mathbb{Z}_{p})$ for some integer *n*.
- Any finite [*p*-group](wikipedia://en/24664) is also a pro-*p* group (with respect to the constant inverse system).
- Fact: A finite homomorphic image of a pro-p group is a p-group. (due to [J.P. Serre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.P._Serre))
