# Exponential dichotomy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exponential dichotomy"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) theory of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), an **exponential dichotomy** is a property of an [equilibrium point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_point) that extends the idea of [hyperbolicity](wikipedia://en/3948604) to non-[autonomous systems](wikipedia://en/312398).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If

$$\dot{\mathbf{x}} = A(t)\mathbf{x}$$

is a [linear](wikipedia://en/722503) non-autonomous dynamical system in **R**<sup>*n*</sup> with [fundamental solution matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_solution_matrix) Φ(*t*), Φ(0) = *I*, then the equilibrium point **0** is said to have an *exponential dichotomy* if there exists a (constant) [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) *P* such that *P*<sup>2</sup> = *P* and positive constants *K*, *L*, α, and β such that

$$|| \Phi(t) P \Phi^{-1}(s) || \le Ke^{-\alpha(t - s)}\mbox{ for }s \le t < \infty$$

and

$$|| \Phi(t) (I - P) \Phi^{-1}(s) || \le Le^{-\beta(s - t)}\mbox{ for }s \ge t > -\infty.$$

If furthermore, *L* = 1/*K* and β = α, then **0** is said to have a *uniform exponential dichotomy*.

The constants α and β allow us to define the *spectral window* of the equilibrium point, (−α, β).

:::

## Explanation {#Explanation}

The matrix *P* is a projection onto the stable subspace and *I* − *P* is a projection onto the unstable subspace. What the exponential dichotomy says is that the norm of the projection onto the stable subspace of any orbit in the system [decays exponentially](wikipedia://en/330320) as *t* → ∞ and the norm of the projection onto the unstable subspace of any orbit decays exponentially as *t* → −∞, and furthermore that the stable and unstable subspaces are conjugate (because $\scriptstyle P \oplus (I - P) = \mathbb{R}^n$).

An equilibrium point with an exponential dichotomy has many of the properties of a hyperbolic equilibrium point in [autonomous systems](wikipedia://en/312398). In fact, it can be shown that a hyperbolic point has an exponential dichotomy.
