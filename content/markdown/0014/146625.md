# Wreath product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wreath product"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **wreath product** is a special combination of two [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) based on the [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246). It is formed by the [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_%28group_theory%29) of one group on many copies of another group, somewhat analogous to [exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491). Wreath products are used in the classification of [permutation groups](wikipedia://en/24634) and also provide a way of constructing interesting examples of groups.

:::

Given two groups $A$ and $H$ (sometimes known as the *bottom* and *top*[^o2]), there exist two variants of the wreath product: the **unrestricted wreath product** $A \text{ Wr } H$ and the **restricted wreath product** $A \text{ wr } H$. The general form, denoted by $A \text{ Wr}_{\Omega} H$ or $A \text{ wr}_{\Omega} H$ respectively, requires that $H$ [acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on some set $\Omega$; when unspecified, usually $\Omega = H$ (a **regular wreath product**), though a different $\Omega$ is sometimes implied. The two variants coincide when $A$, $H$, and $\Omega$ are all finite. Either variant is also denoted as $A \wr H$ (with **\\wr** for the LaTeX symbol) or *A* ≀ *H* ([Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) U+2240).

The notion generalizes to [semigroups](wikipedia://en/27799) and, as such, is a central construction in the [Krohn–Rhodes structure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krohn-Rhodes_theory) of finite semigroups.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $A$ be a group and let $H$ be a group [acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on a set $\Omega$ (on the left). The [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) $A^{\Omega}$ of $A$ with itself indexed by $\Omega$ is the set of sequences $\overline{a} = (a_{\omega})_{\omega \in \Omega}$ in $A$, indexed by $\Omega$, with a group operation given by pointwise multiplication. The action of $H$ on $\Omega$ can be extended to an action on $A^{\Omega}$ by *reindexing*, namely by defining

$$h \cdot (a_{\omega})_{\omega \in \Omega} := (a_{h^{-1} \cdot \omega})_{\omega \in \Omega}$$

for all $h \in H$ and all $(a_{\omega})_{\omega \in \Omega} \in A^{\Omega}$.

Then the **unrestricted wreath product** $A \text{ Wr}_{\Omega} H$ of $A$ by $H$ is the [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) $A^{\Omega} \rtimes H$ with the action of $H$ on $A^{\Omega}$ given above. The subgroup $A^{\Omega}$ of $A^{\Omega} \rtimes H$ is called the **base** of the wreath product.

The **restricted wreath product** $A \text{ wr}_{\Omega} H$ is constructed in the same way as the unrestricted wreath product except that one uses the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/106341) as the base of the wreath product. In this case, the base consists of all sequences in $A^{\Omega}$ with finitely many non-[identity](wikipedia://en/14962) entries. The two definitions coincide when $\Omega$ is finite.

In the most common case, $\Omega = H$, and $H$ acts on itself by left multiplication. In this case, the unrestricted and restricted wreath product may be denoted by $A \text{ Wr } H$ and $A \text{ wr } H$ respectively. This is called the **regular** wreath product.

:::

## Notation and conventions {#Notation_and_conventions}

The structure of the wreath product of $A$ by $H$ depends on the $H$-set $\Omega$ and in case $\Omega$ is infinite it also depends on whether one uses the restricted or unrestricted wreath product. However, in literature the notation used may be deficient and one needs to pay attention to the circumstances.

- In literature, $A\wr_\Omega H$ may stand for the unrestricted wreath product $A\operatorname{Wr}_\Omega H$ or the restricted wreath product $A\operatorname{wr}_\Omega H$.
- In literature, the $H$-set $\Omega$ may be omitted from the notation even if $\Omega\neq H$.
- In the special case that $H=S_n$ is the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) of degree $n$, it is common in the literature to assume that $\Omega=\{1,\dots,n\}$ (with the natural action of $S_n$) and then omit $\Omega$ from the notation. That is, $A\wr S_n$ commonly denotes $A\wr_{\{1,\dots,n\}} S_n$ instead of the regular wreath product $A\wr_{S_n} S_n$. In the first case the base group is the product of $n$ copies of $A$, in the latter it is the product of [*n*!](wikipedia://en/10606) copies of $A$.

## Properties {#Properties}

### Agreement of unrestricted and restricted wreath product on finite Ω {#Agreement_of_unrestricted_and_restricted_wreath_product_on_finite_Ω}

Since the finite direct product is the same as the finite direct sum of groups, it follows that the unrestricted wreath product $A\operatorname{Wr}_\Omega H$ and the restricted wreath product $A\operatorname{wr}_\Omega H$ are equal if $\Omega$ is finite. In particular, this is true when $\Omega = H$ and $H$ is finite.

### Subgroup {#Subgroup}

$A\operatorname{wr}_\Omega H$ is always a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of $A\operatorname{Wr}_\Omega H$.

### Cardinality {#Cardinality}

If $A$, $H$ and $\Omega$ are finite, then

$|A\wr_\Omega\! H| = |A|^{|\Omega|}|H|$.[^o11]

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Universal embedding theorem"}

### Universal embedding theorem {#Universal_embedding_theorem}

If $G$ is an [extension](wikipedia://en/345807) of $A$ by $H$, then there exists a subgroup of the unrestricted wreath product $A\wr H$ which is isomorphic to $G$.[^o14] This is also known as the *Krasner–Kaloujnine embedding theorem*. The [Krohn–Rhodes theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krohn–Rhodes_theorem) involves what is basically the semigroup equivalent of this.[^o15]

:::

## Canonical actions of wreath products {#Canonical_actions_of_wreath_products}

If the group $A$ acts on a set $\Lambda$ then there are two canonical ways to construct sets from $\Omega$ and $\Lambda$ on which $A\operatorname{Wr}_\Omega H$ (and therefore also $A\operatorname{wr}_\Omega H$) can act.

- The **imprimitive** wreath product action on $\Lambda\times\Omega$:

If $((a_\omega), h)\in A \operatorname{Wr}_\Omega H$ and $(\lambda,\omega')\in \Lambda\times \Omega$, then

$$((a_\omega), h) \cdot (\lambda,\omega') := (a_{h(\omega')}\lambda, h\omega').$$

- The **primitive** wreath product action on $\Lambda^\Omega$:

An element in $\Lambda^\Omega$ is a sequence $(\lambda_\omega)$ indexed by the $H$-set $\Omega$. Given an element $((a_\omega), h)\in A \operatorname{Wr}_\Omega H$, its operation on $(\lambda_\omega) \in \Lambda^\Omega$ is given by

$$((a_\omega), h) \cdot (\lambda_\omega) := (a_{h^{-1}\omega}\lambda_{h^{-1}\omega}).$$

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [lamplighter group](wikipedia://en/5491540) is the restricted wreath product $C_2 \wr \mathbb{Z}$.
- The [generalized symmetric group](wikipedia://en/25186376) is $C_m \wr S_n$. The base of this wreath product is the $n$-fold direct product $C_m^n = C_m\times\cdots\times C_m$, where the action $\phi:S_n \to \text{Aut}(C_m^n)$ of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_n$ is given by $\phi(\sigma)(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)=(\alpha_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\alpha_{\sigma(n)})$.[^o19]
  - As a special case, we have the [hyperoctahedral group](wikipedia://en/24268961) $S_2 \wr S_n$ (since $S_2$ is isomorphic to $C_2$).[^o20]
- The smallest non-trivial wreath product is $C_2 \wr C_2$, which is the two-dimensional case of the above hyperoctahedral group. It is the symmetry group of the square, also called $D_8$, the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) of order 8.
- Let $p$ be a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) and let $n \geq 1$. Let $P$ be a [Sylow *p*-subgroup](wikipedia://en/53993) of the symmetric group $S_{p^n}$. Then $P$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to the iterated regular wreath product $W_n = C_p \wr \cdots \wr C_p$ of $n$ copies of $C_p$. Here $W_1=C_p$ and $W_k=W_{k - 1} \wr C_p$ for all $k \geq 2$.[^o21][^o22] For instance, the Sylow 2-subgroup of $S_4$ is the above $C_2 \wr C_2$ group.
- The [Rubik's Cube group](wikipedia://en/2467690) is a normal subgroup of index 12 in the product of wreath products, $(C_3 \wr S_8) \times (C_2 \wr S_{12})$, the factors corresponding to the symmetries of the 8 corners and 12 edges.
- The [Sudoku validity-preserving transformations (VPT) group](wikipedia://en/2912292#The_sudoku_symmetry_group) contains the double wreath product $(S_3\wr S_3)\wr S_2$, where the factors are the permutation of rows/columns within a 3-row or 3-column *band* or *stack* ($S_3$), the permutation of the bands/stacks themselves ($S_3$) and the transposition, which interchanges the bands and stacks ($S_2$). Here, the two index sets $\Omega_1,\Omega_2$ are firstly the set of bands (resp. stacks), so $|\Omega_1|=3$, and secondly the set {bands, stacks} (so $|\Omega_2|=2$). Accordingly, $|S_3\wr S_3|=|S_3|^3|S_3|=6^4$ and $|(S_3\wr S_3)\wr S_2|=|S_3\wr S_3|^2|S_2|=6^8\times 2$.
- Wreath products arise naturally in the symmetries of complete rooted [trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_%28data_structure%29) and their [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806). For example, the repeated (iterated) wreath product $S_2\wr S_2\wr\cdots\wr S_2$ is the automorphism group of a complete [binary tree](wikipedia://en/4321).
