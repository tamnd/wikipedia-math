# Row and column vectors

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Row and column vectors"}

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), a **column vector** with $m$ elements is an $m \times 1$ [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859)[^o2] consisting of a single column of $m$ entries. Similarly, a **row vector** is a $1 \times n$ matrix, consisting of a single row of $n$ entries. For example, $\boldsymbol x$ is a column vector and $\boldsymbol a$ is a row vector:

:::

$$\boldsymbol{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_m \end{bmatrix}, \quad \boldsymbol a = \begin{bmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & \dots & a_n \end{bmatrix}.$$

(Throughout this article, boldface is used for both row and column vectors.)

The [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844) (indicated by $T$) of any row vector is a column vector, and the transpose of any column vector is a row vector:

$$\begin{bmatrix} x_1 \; x_2 \; \dots \; x_m \end{bmatrix}^{\rm T} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_m \end{bmatrix}, \quad \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_m \end{bmatrix}^{\rm T} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \; x_2 \; \dots \; x_m \end{bmatrix}.$$

Taking the transpose twice returns the original (row or column) vector: $\textstyle \bigl(\boldsymbol{x}^{\rm T}\bigr)\vphantom)^{\rm T} = \boldsymbol x$.

The set of all row vectors with $n$ entries in a given [field](wikipedia://en/10603) (such as the [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers)) forms an $n$-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370); similarly, the set of all column vectors with $m$ entries forms an $m$-dimensional vector space.

The space of row vectors with $n$ entries can be regarded as the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) of the space of column vectors with $n$ entries, since any linear functional on the space of column vectors can be represented as the left-multiplication of a unique row vector.

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

To simplify writing column vectors in-line with other text, sometimes they are written as row vectors with the transpose operation applied to them.

$$\boldsymbol{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \; x_2 \; \dots \; x_m \end{bmatrix}^{\rm T}$$

Some authors also use the convention of writing both column vectors and row vectors as rows, but separating row vector elements with [commas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comma) and column vector elements with [semicolons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semicolon) (see alternative notation 2 in the table below).

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

|  | Row vector | Column vector |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard matrix notation** (array spaces, no commas, transpose signs) | $\begin{bmatrix} x_1 \; x_2 \; \dots \; x_m \end{bmatrix}$ | $\begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_m \end{bmatrix} \text{ or } \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \; x_2 \; \dots \; x_m \end{bmatrix}^{\rm T}$ |
| **Alternative notation 1** (commas, transpose signs) | $\begin{bmatrix} x_1, x_2, \dots, x_m \end{bmatrix}$ | $\begin{bmatrix} x_1, x_2, \dots, x_m \end{bmatrix}^{\rm T}$ |
| **Alternative notation 2** (commas and semicolons, no transpose signs) | $\begin{bmatrix} x_1, x_2, \dots, x_m \end{bmatrix}$ | $\begin{bmatrix} x_1; x_2; \dots; x_m \end{bmatrix}$ |

:::

:::

## Operations {#Operations}

[Matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280) involves the action of multiplying each row vector of one matrix by each column vector of another matrix.

The [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) of two column vectors $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}$, considered as elements of a coordinate space, is equal to the matrix product of the transpose of $\mathbf{a}$ with $\mathbf{b}$,

$$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{a}^{\rm T} \mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix} a_1 & \cdots & a_n \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ \vdots \\ b_n \end{bmatrix} = a_1 b_1 + \cdots + a_n b_n \,,$$

By the symmetry of the dot product, the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) of two column vectors $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}$ is also equal to the matrix product of the transpose of $\mathbf{b}$ with $\mathbf{a}$,

$$\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{b}^{\rm T} \mathbf{a} = \begin{bmatrix} b_1 & \cdots & b_n \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} a_1 \\ \vdots \\ a_n \end{bmatrix} = a_1 b_1 + \cdots + a_n b_n\,.$$

The matrix product of a column and a row vector gives the [outer product](wikipedia://en/103109) of two vectors $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}$, an example of the more general [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844). The matrix product of the column vector representation of $\mathbf{a}$ and the row vector representation of $\mathbf{b}$ gives the components of their dyadic product,

$$\mathbf{a} \otimes \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{a} \mathbf{b}^{\rm T} = \begin{bmatrix} a_1 \\ a_2 \\ a_3 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} a_1 b_1 & a_1 b_2 & a_1 b_3 \\ a_2 b_1 & a_2 b_2 & a_2 b_3 \\ a_3 b_1 & a_3 b_2 & a_3 b_3 \\ \end{bmatrix} \,,$$

which is the [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844) of the matrix product of the column vector representation of $\mathbf{b}$ and the row vector representation of $\mathbf{a}$,

$$\mathbf{b} \otimes \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{b} \mathbf{a}^{\rm T} = \begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ b_3 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} b_1 a_1 & b_1 a_2 & b_1 a_3 \\ b_2 a_1 & b_2 a_2 & b_2 a_3 \\ b_3 a_1 & b_3 a_2 & b_3 a_3 \\ \end{bmatrix} \,.$$

## Matrix transformations {#Matrix_transformations}

An $n \times n$ matrix $M$ can represent a [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102) and act on row and column vectors as the linear map's [transformation matrix](wikipedia://en/692458). For a row vector $\mathbf{v}$, the product $\mathbf{v}M$ is another row vector $\mathbf{p}$:

$$\mathbf{v} M = \mathbf{p} \,.$$

Another $n \times n$ matrix $Q$ can act on $\mathbf{p}$,

$$\mathbf{p} Q = \mathbf{t} \,.$$

Then one can write $\mathbf{t} = \mathbf{p}Q = \mathbf{v}MQ$, so the [matrix product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_product) transformation $MQ$ maps $\mathbf{v}$ directly to $\mathbf{t}$. Continuing with row vectors, matrix transformations further reconfiguring $n$-space can be applied to the right of previous outputs.

When a column vector is transformed to another column vector under an $n \times n$ matrix action, the operation occurs to the left,

$$\mathbf{p}^\mathrm{T} = M \mathbf{v}^\mathrm{T} \,,\quad \mathbf{t}^\mathrm{T} = Q \mathbf{p}^\mathrm{T},$$

leading to the algebraic expression $QM \mathbf{v}^{T}$ for the composed output from $\mathbf{v}^{T}$ input. The matrix transformations mount up to the left in this use of a column vector for input to matrix transformation.
