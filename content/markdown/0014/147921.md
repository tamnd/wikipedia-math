# Lucasian Professor of Mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lucasian Professor of Mathematics"}

The **Lucasian Chair of Mathematics** () is a mathematics professorship in the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), England; its holder is known as the **Lucasian Professor**. The post was founded in 1663 by [Henry Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Lucas_%28politician%29), who was [Cambridge University's Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_%28UK_Parliament_constituency%29) in 1639–1640, and it was officially established by [King Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) on 18 January 1664. It has been called the most celebrated professorship in the world, and the most famous academic chair in the world due to the prestige of many of its holders and the groundbreaking work done by them.[^o2] It was said by *[The Daily Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Telegraph)* to be one of the most prestigious academic posts in the world.[^o3] Since its establishment, the professorship has been held by, among others, [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627), [Charles Babbage](wikipedia://en/5698), [George Stokes](wikipedia://en/212838), [Joseph Larmor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Larmor), [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742) and [Stephen Hawking](wikipedia://en/19376148).

:::

## History {#History}

[Henry Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Lucas_%28politician%29), in his [will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_%28law%29), bequeathed his library of 4,000 volumes to the university and left instructions for the purchase of land whose yielding should provide £100 a year for the founding of a professorship.[^o5]

It is the third oldest chair of mathematics in [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain), after the [Gresham Professor of Geometry](wikipedia://en/4470052) at [Gresham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham_College) and the [Savilian Professor of Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savilian_Professor_of_Geometry) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o6]

In the 1800s and following decades, the Lucasian professors "played important roles in making Britain the preeminent scientific state and in changing the university from a ‘gentleman’s club’ to a research institution."[^o7]

[Babbage](wikipedia://en/5698) applied for the vacancy in 1826, after [Turton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Turton), but [Airy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Biddell_Airy) was appointed. [William Whewell](wikipedia://en/205488) (who considered applying, but preferred both [Herschel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Herschel) and Babbage to himself) remarked that he would be the best professor, but that the heads of the colleges would not see that. Nonetheless, Babbage was appointed when the chair became free again two years later.[^o8]

The 19th and current Lucasian Professor is [Michael Cates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Cates), starting from 1 July 2015.[^o9]

Recently, the University of Cambridge requires holders of the chair to retire from the post at the age of 67, as was the case for Stephen Hawking.[^o10]

## Cultural references {#Cultural_references}

In the [final episode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Good_Things..._%28Star_Trek:_The_Next_Generation%29) of the science-fiction television series *[Star Trek: The Next Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek:_The_Next_Generation)*, one of the main characters, the [android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_%28robot%29) [Data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_%28Star_Trek%29), holds the Lucasian Chair in the late 24th century, albeit in an alternate reality.[^o14][^o15][^o16]
