# Ultrafinitism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ultrafinitism"}

In the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439), **ultrafinitism**, **ultraintuitionism**,[^o2] **strict formalism**,[^o3] **strict finitism**,[^o3] **actualism**,[^o2] **predicativism**,[^o3][^o4] and **strong finitism**[^o3] are various philosophies of mathematics with aspects of [finitism](wikipedia://en/152900) and [intuitionism](wikipedia://en/19513). Common to these philosophies is their objection to the [totality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_function) of number theoretic functions like [exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491) over [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474).

:::

## Main ideas {#Main_ideas}

Like other [finitists](wikipedia://en/152900), ultrafinitists deny the existence of the [infinite set](wikipedia://en/142679) $\mathbb{N}$ of [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers), on the basis that it can never be completed.

In addition, some ultrafinitists are concerned with acceptance of objects in mathematics that no one can construct in practice because of physical restrictions in constructing large finite mathematical objects. Thus some ultrafinitists will deny or refrain from accepting the existence of large numbers, for example, the [floor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floor_function) of the first [Skewes's number](wikipedia://en/250178), which is a huge number defined using the [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) as exp(exp(exp(79))), or

$$e^{e^{e^{79}}}.$$

The reason is that nobody has yet calculated what [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) is the [floor](wikipedia://en/54267) of this [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438), and it may not even be physically possible to do so. Similarly, $2\uparrow\uparrow\uparrow 6$ (in [Knuth's up-arrow notation](wikipedia://en/258733)) would be considered only a formal expression that does not correspond to a natural number. The brand of ultrafinitism concerned with physical realizability of mathematics is often called [actualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actualism).

[Edward Nelson](wikipedia://en/387561) criticized the classical conception of natural numbers because of the circularity of its definition. In classical mathematics the natural numbers are defined as 0 and numbers obtained by the iterative applications of the [successor function](wikipedia://en/325637) to 0. But the concept of natural number is already assumed for the iteration. In other words, to obtain a number like $2\uparrow\uparrow\uparrow 6$ one needs to perform the successor function iteratively (in fact, exactly $2\uparrow\uparrow\uparrow 6$ times) to 0.

Some versions of ultrafinitism are forms of [constructivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_%28mathematics%29), but most constructivists view the philosophy as unworkably extreme. The logical foundation of ultrafinitism is unclear; in his comprehensive survey *Constructivism in Mathematics* (1988), the constructive logician [A. S. Troelstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._S._Troelstra) dismissed it by saying "no satisfactory development exists at present." This was not so much a philosophical objection as it was an admission that, in a rigorous work of [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), there was simply nothing precise enough to include.

## People associated with ultrafinitism {#People_associated_with_ultrafinitism}

Serious work on ultrafinitism was led, from 1959 until his death in 2016, by [Alexander Esenin-Volpin](wikipedia://en/2638312), who in 1961 sketched a program for proving the consistency of [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214) in ultrafinite mathematics. Other mathematicians who have worked in the topic include [Doron Zeilberger](wikipedia://en/1724825), [Edward Nelson](wikipedia://en/387561), [Rohit Jivanlal Parikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohit_Jivanlal_Parikh), and [Jean Paul Van Bendegem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Paul_Van_Bendegem). The philosophy is also sometimes associated with the beliefs of [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741), [Robin Gandy](wikipedia://en/3155895), [Petr Vopěnka](wikipedia://en/3105466), and [Johannes Hjelmslev](wikipedia://en/30961758).

[Shaughan Lavine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaughan_Lavine) has developed a form of set-theoretical ultrafinitism that is consistent with classical mathematics.[^o7] Lavine has shown that the basic principles of arithmetic such as "there is no largest natural number" can be upheld, as Lavine allows for the inclusion of "indefinitely large" numbers.[^o7]

## Computational complexity theory based restrictions {#Computational_complexity_theory_based_restrictions}

Other considerations of the possibility of avoiding unwieldy large numbers can be based on [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), as in [András Kornai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/András_Kornai)'s work on explicit finitism (which does not deny the existence of large numbers)[^o9] and [Vladimir Sazonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Sazonov)'s notion of [feasible numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feasible_number).

There has also been considerable formal development on versions of ultrafinitism that are based on complexity theory, like [Samuel Buss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Buss)'s [bounded arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_arithmetic) theories, which capture mathematics associated with various complexity classes like [P](wikipedia://en/658550) and [PSPACE](wikipedia://en/39351). Buss's work can be considered the continuation of [Edward Nelson](wikipedia://en/387561)'s work on [predicative arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicative_arithmetic) as bounded arithmetic theories like S12 are interpretable in [Raphael Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Robinson)'s theory [Q](wikipedia://en/2544440) and therefore are predicative in [Nelson](wikipedia://en/387561)'s sense. The power of these theories for developing mathematics is studied in bounded reverse mathematics as can be found in the works of [Stephen A. Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_A._Cook) and [Phuong The Nguyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuong_The_Nguyen). However these are not philosophies of mathematics but rather the study of restricted forms of reasoning similar to [reverse mathematics](wikipedia://en/326365).
