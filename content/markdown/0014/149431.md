# Karl Menger

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Karl Menger"}

**Karl Menger** (; January 13, 1902 – October 5, 1985) was an Austrian-born American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), the son of the economist [Carl Menger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Menger). In mathematics, Menger studied the theory of [algebras](wikipedia://en/191788) and the [dimension theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_theory) of low-[regularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regularity_%28smoothness%29) ("rough") curves and regions; as well as topology. In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), he is credited with [Menger's theorem](wikipedia://en/1155738). Outside of mathematics, Menger has substantial contributions to [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) and social sciences.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Karl Menger was a student of [Hans Hahn](wikipedia://en/1075212) and received his PhD from the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) in 1924. [L. E. J. Brouwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luitzen_Egbertus_Jan_Brouwer) invited Menger in 1925 to teach at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam). In 1927, he returned to Vienna to accept a professorship there. In 1930 and 1931 he was visiting lecturer at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and the [Rice Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University). From 1937 to 1946 he was a professor at the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame). From 1946 to 1971, he was a professor at [Illinois Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Institute_of_Technology) (IIT) in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago). In 1983, IIT awarded Menger a Doctor of Humane Letters and Sciences degree.[^o4]

## Contributions to mathematics {#Contributions_to_mathematics}

*Image: Menger sponge (IFS).jpg.* Computer illustration of the "Menger sponge".

His most famous popular contribution was the [Menger sponge](wikipedia://en/208811) (mistakenly known as [Sierpinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waclaw_Sierpinski)'s sponge), a three-dimensional version of the [Sierpiński carpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_carpet). It is also related to the [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172).

With [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034), Menger is considered one of the founders of [distance geometry](wikipedia://en/654387); especially by having formalized definitions of the notions of *angle* and of *curvature* in terms of directly measurable [physical quantities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_quantities), namely ratios of *distance* values. The characteristic mathematical expressions appearing in those definitions are [Cayley–Menger determinants](wikipedia://en/2826108).

He was an active participant of the [Vienna Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Circle), which had discussions in the 1920s on social science and philosophy. During that time, he published an influential result[^o6] on the [St. Petersburg paradox](wikipedia://en/466277#Expected_utility_theory) with applications to the [utility theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_theory) in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics); this result has since been criticised as fundamentally misleading.[^o7] Later he contributed to the development of [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) with [Oskar Morgenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Morgenstern).

Menger's work on [topology without points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_without_points) followed [Whitehead's point-free geometry](wikipedia://en/6290771)'s approach and used shrinking regions of the plane to simulate points.[^o8]

Menger was a founding member of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric_Society).

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Menger's longest and last academic post was at the Illinois Institute of Technology, which hosts an annual IIT Karl Menger Lecture and offers the IIT Karl Menger Student Award to an exceptional student for scholarship each year.[^o10]

Menger's memoirs inspired his granddaughter [Kirsten Menger-Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsten_Menger-Anderson) to write the 2025 novel *[The Expert of Subtle Revisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Expert_of_Subtle_Revisions)*, which featured a fictionalized Vienna Circle.[^o11]
