# Paraboloid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paraboloid"}

*Image: Paraboloid of Revolution.svg.* Paraboloid of revolution

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **paraboloid** is a [quadric surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadric_surface) that has exactly one [axis of symmetry](wikipedia://en/6025673) and no [center of symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_symmetry). The term "paraboloid" is derived from [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231), which refers to a [conic section](wikipedia://en/19008673) that has a similar property of symmetry.

:::

Every [plane section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_section) of a paraboloid made by a plane [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497#A_line_and_a_plane) to the axis of symmetry is a parabola. The paraboloid is **hyperbolic** if every other plane section is either a [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052), or two crossing lines (in the case of a section by a tangent plane). The paraboloid is **elliptic** if every other nonempty plane section is either an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277), or a single point (in the case of a section by a tangent plane). A paraboloid is either elliptic or hyperbolic.

Equivalently, a paraboloid may be defined as a quadric surface that is not a [cylinder](wikipedia://en/859283), and has an [implicit equation](wikipedia://en/1527655) whose part of degree two may be factored over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) into two different linear factors. The paraboloid is hyperbolic if the factors are real; elliptic if the factors are [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918).

An elliptic paraboloid is shaped like an oval cup and has a [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum) or minimum point when its axis is vertical. In a suitable [coordinate system](wikipedia://en/81931) with three axes $x$, $y$, and $z$, it can be represented by the equation[^o2]

$$z = \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2}.$$

where $a$ and $b$ are constants that dictate the level of curvature in the $xz$ and $yz$ planes respectively. In this position, the elliptic paraboloid opens upward.

*Image: HyperbolicParaboloid.svg.* Hyperbolic paraboloid

A hyperbolic paraboloid (not to be confused with a [hyperboloid](wikipedia://en/145844)) is a [doubly ruled surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubly_ruled_surface) shaped like a [saddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddle). In a suitable coordinate system, a hyperbolic paraboloid can be represented by the equation[^o3][^o4]

$$z = \frac{y^2}{b^2} - \frac{x^2}{a^2}.$$

In this position, the hyperbolic paraboloid opens downward along the $x$-axis and upward along the $y$-axis (that is, the parabola in the plane $x = 0$ opens upward and the parabola in the plane $y = 0$ opens downward).

Any paraboloid (elliptic or hyperbolic) is a [translation surface](wikipedia://en/59182989), as it can be generated by a moving parabola directed by a second parabola.

## Properties and applications {#Properties_and_applications}

::: {#o8 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Circular paraboloid"}

### Elliptic paraboloid {#Elliptic_paraboloid}

*Image: Paraboloid-3dmesh.png.* [Polygon mesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygon_mesh) of a circular paraboloid

*Image: Paraboloid3d.png.* Circular paraboloid

In a suitable [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706), an elliptic paraboloid has the equation

$$z = \frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}.$$

If $a = b$, an elliptic paraboloid is a **circular paraboloid** or **paraboloid of revolution**. It is a [surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549) obtained by revolving a [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231) around its axis.

A circular paraboloid contains circles. This is also true in the general case (see [Circular section](wikipedia://en/54435709)).

From the point of view of [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), an elliptic paraboloid is an [ellipsoid](wikipedia://en/145381) that is [tangent](wikipedia://en/30807) to the [plane at infinity](wikipedia://en/403143).

**Plane sections**

The plane sections of an elliptic paraboloid can be:

- a *parabola*, if the plane is parallel to the axis,
- a *point*, if the plane is a [tangent plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_plane).
- an *ellipse* or *empty*, otherwise.

#### Parabolic reflector {#Parabolic_reflector}

On the axis of a circular paraboloid, there is a point called the [*focus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focus_%28optics%29) (or *focal point*), such that, if the paraboloid is a mirror, light (or other waves) from a point source at the focus is reflected into a parallel beam, parallel to the axis of the paraboloid. This also works the other way around: a parallel beam of light that is parallel to the axis of the paraboloid is concentrated at the focal point. For a proof, see Parabola.

Therefore, the shape of a circular paraboloid is widely used in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) for parabolic reflectors and parabolic antennas.

The surface of a rotating liquid is also a circular paraboloid. This is used in [liquid-mirror telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid-mirror_telescope) and in making solid telescope mirrors (see [rotating furnace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotating_furnace)).

### Hyperbolic paraboloid {#Hyperbolic_paraboloid}

*Image: Hyperbolic-paraboloid.svg.* A hyperbolic paraboloid with lines contained in it

*Image: Pringles chips.JPG.* [Pringles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pringles) fried snacks are in the shape of a hyperbolic paraboloid.

The hyperbolic paraboloid is a [doubly ruled surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubly_ruled_surface): it contains two families of mutually [skew lines](wikipedia://en/1975821). The lines in each family are parallel to a common plane, but not to each other. Hence the hyperbolic paraboloid is a [conoid](wikipedia://en/6932317).

These properties characterize hyperbolic paraboloids and are used in one of the oldest definitions of hyperbolic paraboloids: *a hyperbolic paraboloid is a surface that may be generated by a moving line that is parallel to a fixed plane and crosses two fixed [skew lines](wikipedia://en/1975821)*.

This property makes it simple to manufacture a [hyperbolic paraboloid structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_paraboloid_structure) from a variety of materials and for a variety of purposes, from [saddle roofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddle_roof) to snack foods such as [Pringles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pringles).[^o12]

A hyperbolic paraboloid is a [saddle surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddle_surface), as its [Gauss curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss_curvature) is negative at every point. Therefore, although it is a ruled surface, it is not [developable](wikipedia://en/2878111).

From the point of view of [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), a hyperbolic paraboloid is [one-sheet hyperboloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-sheet_hyperboloid) that is [tangent](wikipedia://en/30807) to the [plane at infinity](wikipedia://en/403143).

A hyperbolic paraboloid of equation $z=axy$ or $z=\tfrac a 2(x^2-y^2)$ (this is the same [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) a [rotation of axes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_of_axes)) may be called a *rectangular hyperbolic paraboloid*, by analogy with [rectangular hyperbolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectangular_hyperbola).

**Plane sections**

*Image: ParabHyper.png.* A hyperbolic paraboloid (red) with hyperbolas (yellow) and parabolas (teal)

A plane section of a hyperbolic paraboloid with equation

$$z = \frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2}$$

can be

- a *line*, if the plane is parallel to the $z$-axis, and has an equation of the form $bx \pm ay+b=0$,
- a *parabola*, if the plane is parallel to the $z$-axis, and the section is not a line,
- a pair of *intersecting lines*, if the plane is a [tangent plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_plane),
- a *hyperbola*, otherwise.

*Image: Hyperbolic\_paraboloid.stl.* [STL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STL_%28file_format%29) hyperbolic paraboloid model

## Cylinder between pencils of elliptic and hyperbolic paraboloids {#Cylinder_between_pencils_of_elliptic_and_hyperbolic_paraboloids}

*Image: Parabol-el-zy-hy-s.svg.* elliptic paraboloid, parabolic cylinder, hyperbolic paraboloid

The [pencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil_%28mathematics%29) of elliptic paraboloids

$$z=x^2 + \frac{y^2}{b^2}, \ b>0,$$

and the pencil of hyperbolic paraboloids

$$z=x^2 - \frac{y^2}{b^2}, \ b>0,$$

approach the same surface

$$z=x^2$$

for $b \rightarrow \infty$, which is a *parabolic cylinder* (see image).

## Curvature {#Curvature}

The elliptic paraboloid, parametrized simply as

$$\vec \sigma(u,v) = \left(u, v, \frac{u^2}{a^2} + \frac{v^2}{b^2}\right)$$

has [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623)

$$K(u,v) = \frac{4}{a^2 b^2 \left(1 + \frac{4u^2}{a^4} + \frac{4v^2}{b^4}\right)^2}$$

and [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210)

$$H(u,v) = \frac{a^2 + b^2 + \frac{4u^2}{a^2} + \frac{4v^2}{b^2}}{a^2 b^2 \sqrt{\left(1 + \frac{4u^2}{a^4} + \frac{4v^2}{b^4}\right)^3}}$$

which are both always positive, have their maximum at the origin, become smaller as a point on the surface moves further away from the origin, and tend asymptotically to zero as the said point moves infinitely away from the origin.

The hyperbolic paraboloid,[^o3] when parametrized as

$$\vec \sigma (u,v) = \left(u, v, \frac{u^2}{a^2} - \frac{v^2}{b^2}\right)$$

has Gaussian curvature

$$K(u,v) = \frac{-4}{a^2 b^2 \left(1 + \frac{4u^2}{a^4} + \frac{4v^2}{b^4}\right)^2}$$

and mean curvature

$$H(u,v) = \frac{-a^2 + b^2 - \frac{4u^2}{a^2} + \frac{4v^2}{b^2}}{a^2 b^2 \sqrt{\left(1 + \frac{4u^2}{a^4} + \frac{4v^2}{b^4}\right)^3}}.$$

## Geometric representation of multiplication table {#Geometric_representation_of_multiplication_table}

If the hyperbolic paraboloid

$$z = \frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2}$$

is rotated by an angle of $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$ in the $+z$ direction (according to the [right hand rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_hand_rule)), the result is the surface

$$z = \left(\frac{x^2 + y^2}{2}\right) \left(\frac{1}{a^2} - \frac{1}{b^2}\right) + xy \left(\frac{1}{a^2} + \frac{1}{b^2}\right)$$

and if $a = b$ then this simplifies to

$$z = \frac{2xy}{a^2}.$$

Finally, letting $a = \sqrt{2}$, we see that the hyperbolic paraboloid

$$z = \frac{x^2 - y^2}{2}.$$

is congruent to the surface

$$z = xy$$

which can be thought of as the geometric representation (a three-dimensional [nomograph](wikipedia://en/271164), as it were) of a [multiplication table](wikipedia://en/57122).

The two paraboloidal $\mathbf{R}^{2} \to \mathbf{R}$ functions

$$z_1 (x,y) = \frac{x^2 - y^2}{2}$$

and

$$z_2 (x,y) = xy$$

are [harmonic conjugates](wikipedia://en/910519), and together form the [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478)

$$f(z) = \frac{z^2}{2} = f(x + yi) = z_1 (x,y) + i z_2 (x,y)$$

which is the [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) of the $\mathbf{R} \to \mathbf{R}$ parabolic function $f(x) = \tfrac{x^{2}}{2}$.

## Dimensions of a paraboloidal dish {#Dimensions_of_a_paraboloidal_dish}

The dimensions of a symmetrical paraboloidal dish are related by the equation

$$4FD = R^2,$$

where $F$ is the focal length, $D$ is the depth of the dish (measured along the axis of symmetry from the vertex to the plane of the rim), and $R$ is the radius of the rim. They must all be in the same [unit of length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_of_length). If two of these three lengths are known, this equation can be used to calculate the third.

A more complex calculation is needed to find the diameter of the dish *measured along its surface*. This is sometimes called the "linear diameter", and equals the diameter of a flat, circular sheet of material, usually metal, which is the right size to be cut and bent to make the dish. Two intermediate results are useful in the calculation: $P = 2F$ (or the equivalent: $P = \tfrac{R^{2}}{2D}$) and $Q = \sqrt{P^{2} + R^{2}}$, where $F$, $D$, and $R$ are defined as above. The diameter of the dish, measured along the surface, is then given by

$$\frac{RQ}{P} + P \ln\left(\frac{R+Q}{P}\right),$$

where $\ln x$ means the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) of $x$, i.e. its logarithm to base $e$.

The volume of the dish, the amount of liquid it could hold if the rim were horizontal and the vertex at the bottom (e.g. the capacity of a paraboloidal [wok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wok)), is given by

$$\frac{\pi}{2} R^2 D,$$

where the symbols are defined as above. This can be compared with the formulae for the volumes of a [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29) ($\pi R^{2}D$), a [hemisphere](wikipedia://en/27859) ($\tfrac{2\pi}{3}R^{2}D$, where $D = R$), and a [cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_%28geometry%29) ($\tfrac{\pi}{3}R^{2}D$). $\pi R^{2}$ is the aperture area of the dish, the area enclosed by the rim, which is proportional to the amount of sunlight a reflector dish can intercept. The surface area of a parabolic dish can be found using the area formula for a [surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549#Area_formula) which gives

$$A = \frac{\pi R\left(\sqrt{(R^2+4D^2)^3}-R^3\right)}{6D^2}.$$
