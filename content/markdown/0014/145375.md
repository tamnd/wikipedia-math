# Local field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **local field** is a [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) non-[discrete](wikipedia://en/56061) [topological field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_field).[^n1] Local fields find many applications in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), where they arise naturally as [completions](wikipedia://en/7832#Completion) of [global fields](wikipedia://en/288358).[^n2] Moreover, tools like [integration](wikipedia://en/15532) and [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659) are available for functions defined on local fields.

:::

Given a local field, an [absolute value](wikipedia://en/5042667) can be defined on it which gives rise to a [complete metric](wikipedia://en/7832) that generates its topology. There are two basic types of local field: those called **Archimedean local fields** in which the absolute value is [Archimedean](wikipedia://en/264158#Definition_for_normed_fields), and those called **non-Archimedean local fields** in which it is not. Non-Archimedean local fields can also be defined as those fields which are complete with respect to a [metric](wikipedia://en/20018) induced by a [discrete valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_valuation) whose [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) is finite.[^n3]

Every local field is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) (as a topological field) to one of the following:[^n4]

- Archimedean local fields ([characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero): the [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) $\mathbb{R}$, and the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) $\mathbb{C}$.
- Non-Archimedean local fields of characteristic zero: [finite extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) of the [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423) $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (where $p$ is any [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666)).
- Non-Archimedean local fields of characteristic $p$: the field $\mathbb{F}_q((t))$ of [formal Laurent series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_Laurent_series) in the variable $t$ over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) $\mathbb{F}_q$, where $q$ is a [power](wikipedia://en/99491) of $p$.

## Module, absolute value, metric {#Module,_absolute_value,_metric}

Given a local field $F$, a "module function" on $F$ can be defined as follows. First, consider the [additive group](wikipedia://en/10603#Related_algebraic_structures) of the field. As a locally compact [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315), it has a unique (up to positive scalar multiple) [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) $\mu$. The module of an element $a$ of $F$ is defined so as to measure the change in size of a set after multiplying it by $a$. Specifically, define $\operatorname{mod}_K:F\to\mathbb{R}$ by[^n5]

$$\operatorname{mod}_K(a)=\frac{\mu(aX)}{\mu(X)}$$

for any [measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable) subset $X$ of $F$ (with $0<\mu(X)<\infty$). This module does not depend on $X$ nor on the choice of Haar measure $\mu$ (since the same scalar multiple ambiguity will occur in both the numerator and the denominator). The function $\operatorname{mod}_K$ is continuous and satisfies

$$\operatorname{mod}_K(ab)=\operatorname{mod}_K(a) \operatorname{mod}_K(b),$$

$$\operatorname{mod}_K(a+b)\leq A\sup\left(\operatorname{mod}_K(a), \operatorname{mod}_K(b)\right)$$

for some constant $A$ that only depends on $F$.

Using $\operatorname{mod}_K$, one may then define an absolute value $|\cdot|$ on $F$ that induces a [metric](wikipedia://en/20018) $d$ on $F$ (by setting $d(x,y)=|x-y|$), such that $F$ is complete with respect to this metric, and the metric induces the given topology on $F$.

## Basic features of non-Archimedean local fields {#Basic_features_of_non-Archimedean_local_fields}

For a non-Archimedean local field $F$ with absolute value $|\cdot|$, the following objects are important:

- its **[ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613)** $\mathcal{O} = \{a\in F: |a|\leq 1\}$ which is a [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633), is the closed [unit ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ball) of $F$, and is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042);
- the **units** in its ring of integers $\mathcal{O}^\times = \{a\in F: |a|= 1\}$ which form a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) and is the [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090) of $F$;
- the unique non-zero [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) $\mathfrak{m}=\{a\in F: |a|< 1\}$ in its ring of integers, which is the open unit ball of $F$;
- a [generator](wikipedia://en/145126) $\varpi$ of $\mathfrak{m}$ called a **[uniformizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformizer)** of $F$; and
- its residue field $k=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}$ which is finite (since it is compact and [discrete](wikipedia://en/56061)).

Every non-zero element $a$ of $F$ can be written as $a=\varpi^nu$ with $u$ a unit in $\mathcal{O}^\times$, and $n$ a unique integer. The **normalized valuation** of $F$ is the [surjective function](wikipedia://en/27873) $v:F\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}$ defined by sending a non-zero $a$ to the unique integer $n$ such that $a=\varpi^nu$ with $u$ a unit, and by sending 0 to $\infty$. If $q$ is the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of the residue field, the absolute value on $F$ induced by its structure as a local field is given by:[^n6]

$$|a|=q^{-v(a)}.$$

An equivalent and very important definition of a non-Archimedean local field is that it is a field that is [complete with respect to a discrete valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_valued_field) and whose residue field is finite.

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

- ***p*-adic numbers**: the ring of integers of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is the ring of $p$-adic integers $\mathbb{Z}_p$. Its prime ideal is $p\mathbb{Z}_p$ and its residue field is $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$. Every non-zero element of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ can be written as $up^n$ where $u$ is a unit in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $n$ is an integer, with $v(up^n)=n$ for the normalized valuation.
- **Formal Laurent series over a finite field**: the ring of integers of $\mathbb{F}_q((t))$ is the ring of [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) $\mathbb{F}_q[[t]]$. Its maximal ideal is $(t)$ (i.e. the set of [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) whose [constant terms](wikipedia://en/79861) are zero) and its residue field is $\mathbb{F}_q$. Its normalized valuation is related to the (lower) degree of a formal Laurent series as follows:

$$v\biggl(\sum_{i=-m}^\infty a_iT^i\biggr) = -m$$

where $a_{-m}$ is non-zero.

- The field $\mathbb{C}((t))$ of formal Laurent series over the complex numbers is *not* a local field: its residue field is $\mathbb{C}((t))/(t)=\mathbb{C}$, which is not finite.

:::

### Higher unit groups {#Higher_unit_groups}

The ***n*-th higher unit group** of a non-Archimedean local field $F$ is

$$U^{(n)}=1+\mathfrak{m}^n=\left\{u\in\mathcal{O}^\times:u\equiv1\, (\mathrm{mod}\,\mathfrak{m}^n)\right\}$$

for $n\geq 1$. The group $U^{(1)}$ is called the **group of principal units**. The full unit group $\mathcal{O}^\times$ is denoted $U^{(1)}$.

The higher unit groups form a decreasing [filtration](wikipedia://en/726014) of the unit group

$$\mathcal{O}^\times\supseteq U^{(1)}\supseteq U^{(2)}\supseteq\cdots$$

whose [quotients](wikipedia://en/11526) are given by

$$\mathcal{O}^\times/U^{(n)}\cong\left(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}^n\right)^\times\text{ and }\,U^{(n)}/U^{(n+1)}\approx\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}$$

for $n\geq 1$.[^n7] (Here "$\approx$" means a non-canonical isomorphism.)

### Structure of the unit group {#Structure_of_the_unit_group}

The multiplicative group of non-zero elements of a non-Archimedean local field $F$ is isomorphic to

$$F^\times\cong(\varpi)\times\mu_{q-1}\times U^{(1)}$$

where $q$ is the order of the residue field, and $\mu_{q-1}$ is the group of ($q-1$)-st roots of unity in $F$. Its structure as an abelian group depends on its [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621):

- If $F$ has characteristic $p$, then

$$F^\times\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/{(q-1)}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_p^\mathbb{N}$$

where $\mathbb{N}$ denotes the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474);

- If $F$ has characteristic zero, i.e. it is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $d$, then

$$F^\times\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/(q-1)\oplus\mathbb{Z}/p^a\oplus\mathbb{Z}_p^d$$

where $a\geq 0$ is defined so that the group of $p$-power roots of unity in $F$ is $\mu_{p^a}$.[^n8]

## Theory of local fields {#Theory_of_local_fields}

This theory includes the study of types of local fields, extensions of local fields using [Hensel's lemma](wikipedia://en/1633368), [Galois extensions](wikipedia://en/457064) of local fields, [ramification groups](wikipedia://en/501614), filtrations of [Galois groups](wikipedia://en/12673) of local fields, the behavior of the norm map on local fields, the local reciprocity homomorphism and existence theorem in [local class field theory](wikipedia://en/669532), [local Langlands correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Langlands_correspondence), [Hodge-Tate theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge-Tate_theory) (also called [*p*-adic Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/26086853)), explicit formulas for the [Hilbert symbol](wikipedia://en/5924217) in local class field theory.[^n9]

## Variant definitions {#Variant_definitions}

The definition for "local field" adopted in this article, as a locally compact Hausdorff non-discrete topological field, is common today. Some authors however reserve the term "local field" for what we have called "non-Archimedean local field".

Research papers in modern number theory often consider a more general notion of non-Archimedean local field, requiring only that they be complete with respect to a [discrete valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_valuation) and that the residue field be [perfect](wikipedia://en/505088) of positive characteristic, not necessarily finite.[^n10]

In his book *[Local Fields](wikipedia://en/21934376)*, Serre defines "local fields" as fields that are complete with respect to a discrete valuation, without any restriction on the residue field, leading to a notion that is more general still.[^n11]

## Higher-dimensional local fields {#Higher-dimensional_local_fields}

A local field is sometimes called a *one-dimensional local field*.

A non-Archimedean local field can be viewed as the field of fractions of the completion of the [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) of a one-dimensional arithmetic scheme of rank 1 at its non-singular point.

For a [non-negative integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative_integer) $n$, an $n$-dimensional local field is a complete discrete valuation field whose residue field is an $(n-1)$-dimensional local field.[^n10] Depending on the definition of local field, a *zero-dimensional local field* is then either a finite field (with the definition used in this article), or a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088) of positive characteristic.

From the geometric point of view, $n$-dimensional local fields with last finite residue field are naturally associated to a complete [flag](wikipedia://en/9352497) of subschemes of an $n$-dimensional [arithmetic scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_scheme).
