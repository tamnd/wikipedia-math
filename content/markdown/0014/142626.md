# Normal morphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Normal morphism"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) and its applications to [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **normal monomorphism** or **conormal epimorphism** is a particularly well-behaved type of [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891). A **normal category** is a category in which every [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538) is normal. A **conormal category** is one in which every [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539) is conormal.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A monomorphism is **normal** if it is the [kernel](wikipedia://en/142616) of some morphism, and an epimorphism is **conormal** if it is the [cokernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cokernel_%28category_theory%29) of some morphism.

A category **C** is **binormal** if it's both normal and conormal. But note that some authors will use the word "normal" only to indicate that **C** is binormal.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

In the [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092), a monomorphism *f* from *H* to *G* is normal [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its image is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of *G*. In particular, if *H* is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of *G*, then the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516) *i* from *H* to *G* is a monomorphism, and will be normal if and only if *H* is a normal subgroup of *G*. In fact, this is the origin of the term "normal" for monomorphisms.

On the other hand, every epimorphism in the category of groups is conormal (since it is the cokernel of its own kernel), so this category is conormal.

In an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063), every monomorphism is the kernel of its cokernel, and every epimorphism is the cokernel of its kernel. Thus, abelian categories are always binormal. The category of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) is the fundamental example of an abelian category, and accordingly every subgroup of an abelian group is a normal subgroup.

:::
