# Fubini's theorem on differentiation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fubini's theorem on differentiation"}

In mathematics, **Fubini's theorem on differentiation**, named after [Guido Fubini](wikipedia://en/349051), is a result in [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478) concerning the [differentiation](wikipedia://en/7921) of series of [monotonic functions](wikipedia://en/48260). It can be proven by using [Fatou's lemma](wikipedia://en/291614) and the properties of [null sets](wikipedia://en/21520).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Assume $I \subseteq \mathbb R$ is an [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) and that for every [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *k*, $f_k: I \to \mathbb R$ is an [increasing function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Increasing_function). If,

$$s(x) := \sum_{k=1}^\infty f_k(x)$$

exists for all $x \in I,$ then for [almost any](wikipedia://en/44688) $x \in I,$ the derivatives exist and are related as:[^o2]

$$s'(x) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty f_k'(x).$$

In general, if we don't suppose *f<sub>k</sub>* is increasing for every *k*, in order to get the same conclusion, we need a stricter condition like [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652) of $\sum_{k=1}^n f_k'(x)$ on *I* for every n.[^o5]

:::
