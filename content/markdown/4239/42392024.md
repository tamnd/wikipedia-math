# Robert Ghrist

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Ghrist"}

**Robert W. Ghrist** (born 1969) is an American mathematician, known for his work on topological methods in applied mathematics.

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Ghrist received his bachelor's degree in [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) from the [University of Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toledo) in 1991, and in 1994 his master's degree and in 1995 his PhD from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) under [Philip Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Holmes) with thesis *The link of periodic orbits of a flow*. From 1996 to 1998, he was *R. H. Bing Instructor* at the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas) and from 1998 an assistant professor and then from 2002 an associate professor at the [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology). In 2002 he became an associate professor and in 2004 a professor at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign). From 2007, he was at the *Information Trust Institute*. In 2008, he was appointed *Andrea Mitchell Penn Integrating Knowledge University Professor* in Mathematics and Electrical/Systems Engineering at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania).

Ghrist was a visiting scientist in 1995 at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) and in 2000 at the [Isaac Newton Institute](wikipedia://en/736837) in Cambridge. He works on the application of topological methods to [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087), [robots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot), [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamic), and [information systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_system), such as [sensor networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensor_network).[^o5]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

In 2002, Ghrist received a [Presidential Early Career Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Early_Career_Award_for_Scientists_and_Engineers). In 2013, he received the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) for *Barcodes: The Persistent Topology of Data*[^o7] and in 2014 the [Gauss Lectureship](wikipedia://en/48440954) of the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973).
