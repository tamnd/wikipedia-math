# Barry Loewer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Barry Loewer"}

**Barry Monroe Loewer** (born 1945) is an American [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) who is a distinguished professor of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) and director of the Rutgers Center for Philosophy and the Sciences.[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

He obtained his BA from [Amherst College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst_College) (1965) and his PhD from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1975[^o5] (under the direction of [Jaakko Hintikka](wikipedia://en/1710903)). He taught first at the [University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina) before joining [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) in 1989.[^o3]

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

Loewer has published in many areas of philosophy including [philosophy of mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_mind), [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology), [history of philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_philosophy), [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939), philosophy of language, and the [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science). He is especially known for work on [mental causation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_causation), the metaphysics of laws and chance, and the interpretation of quantum mechanics. Loewer has collaborated with Marvin Belzer (on [deontic logic](wikipedia://en/1343980)), [Ernest Lepore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Lepore) (on [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language)), and [David Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Albert) (on the interpretation of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) and the role of chance in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481)). In his work with Albert he is known for developing the "[many-minds interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Many-minds_interpretation)" of [quantum theory](wikipedia://en/25202). The point of this work was not so much to advocate this view as to argue that many worlds interpretations need to be modified to accommodate probabilities. This has become a much discussed and contended issue.
