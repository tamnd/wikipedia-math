# Irving Stringham

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Irving Stringham"}

**Washington Irving Stringham** (December 10, 1847 – October 5, 1909) was an American mathematician born in [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire,_New_York), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29). He was the first person to denote the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) as $\ln(x)$ where $x$ is its argument. The use of $\ln(x)$ in place of $\log_e(x)$ is commonplace in [digital calculators](wikipedia://en/7593) today.

:::

> "In place of $^{e}\log$ we shall henceforth use the shorter symbol $\ln$, made up of the initial letters of *logarithm* and of *natural* or *Napierian*."[^o3]

Stringham graduated from [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College) in 1877. He earned his PhD from [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) in 1880. His dissertation was titled *Regular Figures in N-dimensional Space*[^o4] under his advisor [James Joseph Sylvester](wikipedia://en/234861).

In 1881 he was in Schwartzbach, [Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxony), when he submitted an article on [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) found in the [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) algebra.[^o5]

Stringham began his professorship in mathematics at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley) in 1882.[^o6] In 1893 in Chicago, his paper *Formulary for an Introduction to Elliptic Functions* was read at the International Mathematical Congress held in connection with the [World's Columbian Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Columbian_Exposition).[^o7] In 1900 he was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in Paris.[^o8]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Irving married Martha Sherman Day. The couple raised a daughter, Martha Sherman Stringham, (March 5, 1891- August 7, 1967).
