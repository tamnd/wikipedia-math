# Peter Scholze

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Scholze"}

**Peter Scholze** (; born 11 December 1987)[^o4] is a German mathematician known for his work in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177). He has been a professor in the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) since 2012 and co-director at the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1386960) since 2018. He has been called one of the leading mathematicians in the world.[^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8] In 2018, he won the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859), an award regarded as the highest professional honor in mathematics.[^o9][^o10][^o11]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Scholze was born in [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden) and grew up in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin).[^o13] His father is a [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), his mother a [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), and his sister studied [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry).[^o14] He attended the Heinrich-Hertz-Gymnasium in [Berlin-Friedrichshain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrichshain), a [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29) devoted to mathematics and science.[^o15] As a student, Scholze participated in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208), winning three gold medals and one silver medal.[^o16]

He studied at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) and completed his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in three semesters and his master's degree in two further semesters.[^o17] He obtained his Ph.D. in 2012 under the supervision of [Michael Rapoport](wikipedia://en/26629548).[^o2]

Scholze was a student of Rapoport, who was a student of [Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544), who was a student of [Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042), who was a student of [Schwartz](wikipedia://en/317485); in this chain, Scholze, Deligne, Grothendieck, and Schwartz are all Fields medallists.

## Career {#Career}

From July 2011 until 2016, Scholze was a research fellow of the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655) in New Hampshire.[^o19] In 2012, shortly after completing his PhD, he was made [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_professor) in the University of Bonn, becoming at the age of 24 the youngest full professor in Germany.[^o5][^o17][^o20][^o21]

In fall 2014, Scholze was appointed a chancellor's professor in the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he taught a course on [*p*-adic geometry](wikipedia://en/291327).[^o22][^o23]

In 2018, Scholze was appointed as a director of the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1386960) in Bonn.[^o24]

## Work {#Work}

Peter Scholze's work focuses on local aspects of *p*-adic algebraic geometry. He presented in a more compact form some of the previous fundamental theories pioneered by [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226), [Jean-Marc Fontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marc_Fontaine) and later by [Kiran Kedlaya](wikipedia://en/31688876). His [PhD thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD_thesis) on [perfectoid spaces](wikipedia://en/39197973)[^o26] yields the solution to a special case of the [weight-monodromy conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight-monodromy_conjecture).[^o27]

Scholze and [Bhargav Bhatt](wikipedia://en/67165881) have developed a theory of [prismatic cohomology](wikipedia://en/83781807), which has been described as progress towards [motivic cohomology](wikipedia://en/809314) by unifying [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology), [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530), [$\ell$-adic cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-adic_cohomology), and [crystalline cohomology](wikipedia://en/10220067).[^o28][^o29]

Scholze and [Dustin Clausen](wikipedia://en/76038574) proposed a program for [condensed mathematics](wikipedia://en/68011960).

## Awards {#Awards}

In 2012, he was awarded the Prix and Cours Peccot.[^o31] He was awarded the 2013 [SASTRA Ramanujan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SASTRA_Ramanujan_Prize).[^o32] In 2014, he received the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167).[^o33] In 2015, he was awarded the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in Algebra,[^o34] and the [Ostrowski Prize](wikipedia://en/3159411).[^o35][^o36]

He received the [Fermat Prize](wikipedia://en/10919195) 2015 from the Institut de Mathématiques de Toulouse.[^o37] In 2016, he was awarded the [Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_Prize) 2016 by the [German Research Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Research_Foundation).[^o38] He declined the \$100,000 "New Horizons in Mathematics Prize" of the 2016 [Breakthrough Prizes](wikipedia://en/43125933).[^o39] His turning down of the prize received some media attention.[^o40][^o41]

In 2017 he became a member of the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina).[^o42]

In 2018, at thirty years old, Scholze, who was at the time serving as a mathematics professor in the University of Bonn, became one of the youngest mathematicians ever to be awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859)[^o43][^o44] for "transforming arithmetic [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) over [*p*-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) fields through his introduction of [perfectoid spaces](wikipedia://en/39197973), with application to [Galois representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_representations), and for the development of new [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) theories."[^o45]

In 2019, Scholze received the Great Cross of Merit of the [Order of Merit of the Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit_of_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany).[^o46][^o47][^o48]

In 2022 he became a foreign member of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society)[^o49] and was awarded the [Pius XI Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_XI_Medal) from the [Pontifical Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o50]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Scholze is married to a fellow mathematician[^o52] and has a daughter.[^o53]
