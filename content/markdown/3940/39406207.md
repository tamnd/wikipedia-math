# J-structure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="J-structure"}

In mathematics, a **J-structure** is an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) over a [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28algebra%29) related to a [Jordan algebra](wikipedia://en/936371). The concept was introduced by (Springer 1973) to develop a theory of Jordan algebras using [linear algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/395885) and axioms taking the Jordan inversion as basic operation and [Hua's identity](wikipedia://en/38023412) as a basic relation. There is a classification of simple structures deriving from the classification of [semisimple algebraic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_algebraic_group). Over fields of [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_of_a_field) not equal to 2, the theory of J-structures is essentially the same as that of Jordan algebras.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *V* be a [finite-dimensional vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-dimensional_vector_space) over a field *K* and *j* a [rational map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_map) from *V* to itself, expressible in the form *n*/*N* with *n* a [polynomial map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_map) from *V* to itself and *N* a polynomial in *K*\[*V*\]. Let *H* be the subset of GL(*V*) × GL(*V*) containing the pairs (*g*,*h*) such that *g*∘*j* = *j*∘*h*: it is a closed [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of the product and the projection onto the first factor, the set of *g* which occur, is the *structure group* of *j*, denoted *G**(*****j*).***

A *J-structure* is a triple (*V*,*j*,*e*) where *V* is a vector space over *K*, *j* is a [birational map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birational_map) from *V* to itself and *e* is a non-zero element of *V* satisfying the following conditions.[^o5]

- *j* is a homogeneous birational [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) of degree −1
- *j* is regular at *e* and *j*(*e*) = *e*
- if *j* is regular at *x*, *e* + *x* and *e* + *j*(*x*) then

$$j(e+x) + j(e+j(x)) = e$$

- the orbit *G* *e* of *e* under the structure group *G* = *G*(*j*) is a [Zariski open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski_open) subset of *V*.

The *norm* associated to a J-structure (*V*,*j*,*e*) is the numerator *N* of *j*, normalised so that *N*(*e*) = 1. The *degree* of the J-structure is the degree of *N* as a homogeneous polynomial map.[^o6]

The *quadratic map* of the structure is a map *P* from *V* to End(*V*) defined in terms of the [differential](wikipedia://en/677191#Algebraic_geometry) d*j* at an invertible *x*.[^o7] We put

$$P(x) = - (d j)_x^{-1} .$$

The quadratic map turns out to be a quadratic polynomial map on *V*.

The subgroup of the structure group *G* generated by the invertible quadratic maps is the *inner structure group* of the J-structure. It is a closed connected normal subgroup.[^o8]

:::

## J-structures from quadratic forms {#J-structures_from_quadratic_forms}

Let *K* have [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) not equal to 2. Let *Q* be a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) on the vector space *V* over *K* with associated [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) *Q*(*x*,*y*) = *Q*(*x*+*y*) − *Q*(*x*) − *Q*(*y*) and distinguished element *e* such that *Q*(*e*,.) is not trivial. We define a reflection map *x*<sup>\*</sup> by

$$x^* = Q(x,e)e - x$$

and an inversion map *j* by

$$j(x) = Q(x)^{-1} x^* .$$

Then (*V*,*j*,*e*) is a J-structure.

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

Let *Q* be the usual sum of squares quadratic function on *K*<sup>*r*</sup> for fixed integer *r*, equipped with the [standard basis](wikipedia://en/462005) *e*<sup>1</sup>,...,*e*<sup>*r*</sup>. Then (*K*<sup>*r*</sup>, *Q*, *e*<sup>*r*</sup>) is a J-structure of degree 2. It is denoted O<sub>2</sub>.[^o12]

:::

## Link with Jordan algebras {#Link_with_Jordan_algebras}

In [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_of_a_field) not equal to 2, which we assume in this section, the theory of J-structures is essentially the same as that of Jordan algebras.

Let *A* be a finite-dimensional commutative [non-associative algebra](wikipedia://en/6533841) over *K* with identity *e*. Let *L*(*x*) denote multiplication on the left by *x*. There is a unique birational map *i* on *A* such that *i*(*x*).*x* = *e* if *i* is regular on *x*: it is homogeneous of degree −1 and an involution with *i*(*e*) = *e*. It may be defined by *i*(*x*) = *L*(*x*)<sup>−1</sup>.*e*. We call *i* the *inversion* on *A*.[^o14]

A Jordan algebra is defined by the identity[^o15][^o16]

$$x(x^2 y) = x^2 (x y) .$$

An alternative characterisation is that for all invertible *x* we have

$$x^{-1}(x y) = x (x^{-1} y) .$$

If *A* is a Jordan algebra, then (*A*,*i*,*e*) is a J-structure. If (*V*,*j*,*e*) is a J-structure, then there exists a unique Jordan algebra structure on *V* with identity *e* with inversion *j*.

## Link with quadratic Jordan algebras {#Link_with_quadratic_Jordan_algebras}

In general characteristic, which we assume in this section, J-structures are related to [quadratic Jordan algebras](wikipedia://en/39323582). We take a quadratic Jordan algebra to be a finite dimensional vector space *V* with a quadratic map *Q* from *V* to End(*V*) and a distinguished element *e*. We let *Q* also denote the bilinear map *Q*(*x*,*y*) = *Q*(*x*+*y*) − *Q*(*x*) − *Q*(*y*). The properties of a quadratic Jordan algebra will be[^o18][^o19]

- *Q*(*e*) = id<sub>*V*</sub>, *Q*(*x*,*e*)*y* = *Q*(*x*,*y*)*e*
- *Q*(*Q*(*x*)*y*) = *Q*(*x*)*Q*(*y*)*Q*(*x*)
- *Q*(*x*)*Q*(*y*,*z*)*x* = *Q*(*Q*(*x*)*y*,*x*)*z*

We call *Q*(*x*)*e* the *square* of *x*. If the squaring is [dominant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant_morphism) (has [Zariski dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski_dense) image) then the algebra is termed *separable*.[^o20]

There is a unique birational involution *i* such that *Q*(*x*)*i* *x* = *x* if *Q* is regular at *x*. As before, *i* is the *inversion*, definable by *i*(*x*) = *Q*(*x*)<sup>−1</sup> *x*.

If (*V*,*j*,*e*) is a J-structure, with quadratic map *Q* then (*V*,*Q*,*e*) is a quadratic Jordan algebra. In the opposite direction, if (*V*,*Q*,*e*) is a separable quadratic Jordan algebra with inversion *i*, then (*V*,*i*,*e*) is a J-structure.[^o21]

### H-structure {#H-structure}

McCrimmon proposed a notion of *H-structure* by dropping the density axiom and strengthening the third (a form of Hua's identity) to hold in all [isotopes](wikipedia://en/41841804). The resulting structure is categorically equivalent to a quadratic Jordan algebra.[^o23][^o24]

## Peirce decomposition {#Peirce_decomposition}

A J-structure has a [Peirce decomposition](wikipedia://en/30189901) into subspaces determined by idempotent elements.[^o26] Let *a* be an idempotent of the J-structure (*V*,*j*,*e*), that is, *a*<sup>2</sup> = *a*. Let *Q* be the quadratic map. Define

$$\phi_a(t,u) = Q(ta + u(e-a)) .$$

This is invertible for non-zero *t*,*u* in *K* and so φ defines a morphism from the [algebraic torus](wikipedia://en/504697) GL<sub>1</sub> × GL<sub>1</sub> to the inner structure group *G*<sub>1</sub>. There are subspaces

$$V_a = \left\lbrace{ x \in V : \phi_a(t,u) x = t^2 x }\right\rbrace$$

$$V'_a = \left\lbrace{ x \in V : \phi_a(t,u) x = tu x }\right\rbrace$$

$$V_{e-a} = \left\lbrace{ x \in V : \phi_a(t,u) x = u^2 x }\right\rbrace$$

and these form a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) decomposition of *V*. This is the Peirce decomposition for the idempotent *a*.[^o27]

## Generalisations {#Generalisations}

If we drop the condition on the distinguished element *e*, we obtain "J-structures without identity".[^o29] These are related to [isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope_%28Jordan_algebra%29) of Jordan algebras.[^o30]
