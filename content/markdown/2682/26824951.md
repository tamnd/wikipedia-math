# David Rivault de Fleurance

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Rivault de Fleurance"}

**David Rivault de Fleurance** (1571–1616) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and royal servant. He was born probably at [La Cropte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Cropte), near [Laval, Mayenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laval,_Mayenne), France.

:::

He is the son of Pierre Rivault and Madeleine Gaultier who contracted marriage in 1550 [^o2]

He was a "[Gentleman of the Bedchamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentleman_of_the_Bedchamber#France)" to [Henry IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_IV_of_France), and a teacher of [Louis XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIII). He discovered that water, if confined in a [bombshell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb) and heated, would explode the shell. He published that fact in his treatise on [artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery) in 1605, where he wrote: "The water is converted into air, and its vaporization is followed by violent explosion." His discovery is seen as one of the steps leading to the invention of the [steam engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine). He died in [Tours, France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tours,_France) in January 1616.
