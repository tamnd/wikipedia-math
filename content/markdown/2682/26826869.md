# Ludwig Schlesinger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ludwig Schlesinger"}

**Ludwig Schlesinger** (Hungarian: Schlesinger Lajos, Slovak: Ľudovít Schlesinger), (1 November 1864 – 15 December 1933) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for the research in the field of [linear differential equations](wikipedia://en/379868).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Schlesinger attended the high school in [Pressburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressburg) and later studied physics and mathematics in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg) and [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin). In 1887 he received his PhD (Über lineare homogene Differentialgleichungen vierter Ordnung, zwischen deren Integralen homogene Relationen höheren als ersten Grades bestehen.) His thesis advisors were [Lazarus Immanuel Fuchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazarus_Immanuel_Fuchs) and [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450). In 1889 he became an associate professor at Berlin; in 1897 an invited professor in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn) and in the same year, a full professor at the [University of Kolozsvár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kolozsvár), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) (now Cluj, Romania). From 1911 he was professor at the [University of Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen), where he taught until 1930. His daughter [Hildegard Lewy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildegard_Lewy) (1903–1969), became an Assyriologist and academic. In 1933 he was forced to retire by the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazis). He died shortly afterwards.[^o5] His doctoral students include [Abraham Plessner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Plessner).

Schlesinger was a [historian of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian_of_science). He wrote an article on the [function theory](wikipedia://en/5759) of [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) and translated [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525)' *[La Géométrie](wikipedia://en/239981)* into German (1894). He was one of the organizers of the celebrations for the hundredth anniversary of [János Bolyai](wikipedia://en/98912) and from 1904 to 1909 with R. Fuchs he collected the works of his teacher Lazarus Fuchs, who was also his father-in-law. In 1902 he became a corresponding member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences). In 1909 he received the [Lobachevsky Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobachevsky_Prize).[^o6]

From 1929 until his death he was co-editor of *[Crelle's Journal](wikipedia://en/973547)*.

Like his teacher Fuchs, he worked primarily on linear [ordinary differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_differential_equations). His two-volume *Handbuch der Theorie der Linearen Differentialgleichungen* was published from 1895 to 1898 in Teubner in Leipzig (Vol.2 in two parts).[^o7] He also published *Einführung in die Theorie der gewöhnlichen Differentialgleichungen auf funktionentheoretischer Grundlage* (Auflage, 1922), *Vorlesungen über lineare Differentialgleichungen* (1908)[^o8] and *Automorphe Funktionen* (Gruyter, 1924). In 1909 he wrote a long report for the annual report of the German Mathematical Society on the history of linear differential equations since 1865.[^o9] He also studied differential geometry, and wrote a book of lectures on [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s [general relativity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity_theory).[^o10]

Today, his best known work is *Über eine Klasse von Differentialsystemen beliebiger Ordnung mit festen kritischen Punkten* (Crelle's Journal, 1912). There he considered the problem of [isomonodromy deformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomonodromy_deformation) for a certain matrix [Fuchsian equation](wikipedia://en/3166061); this is a special case of [Hilbert's 21st Problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert's_21st_Problem) (existence of differential equations with prescribed monodromy). The paper introduced what are today called Schlesinger transformations and [Schlesinger equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlesinger_equations).
