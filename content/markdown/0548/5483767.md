# Monogon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monogon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **monogon** is a curve, considered by some as a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) with one [edge](wikipedia://en/12158034) and one [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417). It has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {1}.[^o3]

:::

## In Euclidean geometry {#In_Euclidean_geometry}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) a *monogon* is a [degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952) polygon because its endpoints must coincide, unlike any Euclidean line segment. Most definitions of a polygon in Euclidean geometry do not admit the monogon.

## In spherical geometry {#In_spherical_geometry}

In [spherical geometry](wikipedia://en/174026), a monogon can be constructed as a vertex on a [great circle](wikipedia://en/48082) ([equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator)). This forms a [dihedron](wikipedia://en/6109457#As_a_tiling_on_a_sphere), {1,2}, with two hemispherical monogonal faces which share one 360° edge and one vertex. Its dual, a [hosohedron](wikipedia://en/2316192), {2,1} has two [antipodal](wikipedia://en/367577) vertices at the poles, one 360° [lune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lune_%28mathematics%29#Spherical_geometry) face, and one edge (meridian) between the two vertices.[^o3]
