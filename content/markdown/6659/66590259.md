# Anthony Joseph Penico

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anthony Joseph Penico"}

**Anthony "Tony" Joseph Penico** (June 11, 1923, Philadelphia – November 19, 2011, Missouri) was an American mathematician and engineer. He is known for the Penico theorem,[^o2] Penico solvability, and Penico series.[^o3][^o4]

:::

After graduating from [South Philadelphia High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Philadelphia_High_School), Penico was awarded scholarships to the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania). There he graduated in 1946 with a bachelor's degree in physics and in 1950 with a Ph.D. in mathematics.[^o5] His dissertation, written under the supervision of [Richard D. Schafer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_D._Schafer), is entitled *The Wedderburn Principal Theorem for Jordan Algebras*. The theorem, which generalizes a theorem of [A. A. Albert](wikipedia://en/3326054), was published in the *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)* in 1951.[^o2] At the 1950 meeting of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) he was an approved (but not an invited) speaker.[^o7] In October 1969 he contributed a paper *Functional-analysis identities for biadditive mappings on modules with non-associative scalars* to the 668th meeting of the *[American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)*.[^o8]

After receiving his Ph.D., Penico moved with his wife to the Boston area, where he taught mathematics at [Tufts College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_College). In the mid-1950s the family moved to Northern California, where he worked as a Senior Engineering Specialist at the [GTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTE)'s Research Laboratories. In the early 1960s, he became a Senior Research Mathematician at the [Stanford Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Research_Institute) and also taught part-time at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). In 1966 Penico became a Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Missouri–Rolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri–Rolla) (which in 2008 was renamed the [Missouri University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_University_of_Science_and_Technology)). He retired as professor emeritus in 1986.[^o5]

In 1948 he married Eva Yaremko (1925–2017). They had two sons, David Anthony Penico (1952–2008) and Stephen John Penico (1956-2025). Anthony J. Penico died in 2011.[^o5]
