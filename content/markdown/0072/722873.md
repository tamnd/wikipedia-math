# Triakis icosahedron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Triakis icosahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **triakis icosahedron** is an [Archimedean dual](wikipedia://en/1847) solid, or a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393), with 60 [isosceles triangle](wikipedia://en/315428) faces. Its dual is the [truncated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/376538). It has also been called the **kisicosahedron**.[^o3] It was first depicted, in a non-convex form with [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) faces, by [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci) in [Luca Pacioli](wikipedia://en/18473)'s *[Divina proportione](wikipedia://en/16175342)*, where it was named the *icosahedron elevatum*.[^o4] The [capsid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsid) of the [Hepatitis A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_A) virus has the shape of a triakis icosahedron.[^o5]

:::

## As a Kleetope {#As_a_Kleetope}

The triakis icosahedron can be formed by gluing triangular pyramids to each face of a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968). Depending on the height of these pyramids relative to their base, the result can be either convex or non-convex. This construction, of gluing pyramids to each face, is an instance of a general construction called the [Kleetope](wikipedia://en/31165912); the triakis icosahedron is the Kleetope of the icosahedron.[^o4] This interpretation is also expressed in the name, [triakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway_kis_operator), which is used for the Kleetopes of polyhedra with triangular faces.[^o3]

When depicted in Leonardo's form, with equilateral triangle faces, it is an example of a non-convex [deltahedron](wikipedia://en/493995), one of the few known deltahedra that are [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) (meaning that all faces are symmetric to each other).[^o7] In another of the non-convex forms of the triakis icosahedron, the three triangles adjacent to each pyramid are coplanar, and can be thought of as instead forming the visible parts of a convex hexagon, in a self-intersecting polyhedron with 20 hexagonal faces that has been called the [small triambic icosahedron](wikipedia://en/18437022).[^o8] Alternatively, for the same form of the triakis icosahedron, the triples of coplanar isosceles triangles form the faces of the first [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of the icosahedron.[^o9] Yet another non-convex form, with [golden isosceles triangle](wikipedia://en/4525417) faces, forms the outer shell of the [great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029878), a [Kepler–Poinsot polyhedron](wikipedia://en/16814) with twelve [pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544) faces.[^o10]

Each edge of the triakis icosahedron has endpoints of total degree at least 13. By [Kotzig's theorem](wikipedia://en/62047186), this is the most possible for any polyhedron. The same total degree is obtained from the Kleetope of any polyhedron with minimum degree five, but the triakis icosahedron is the simplest example of this construction.[^o11] Although this Kleetope has isosceles triangle faces, iterating the Kleetope construction on it produces convex polyhedra with triangular faces that cannot all be isosceles.[^o12]

## As a Catalan solid {#As_a_Catalan_solid}

*Image: Triakis icosahedron.stl.* 3d model of a triakis icosahedron

The triakis icosahedron is a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393), the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) of the [truncated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/376538). The truncated dodecahedron is an [Archimedean solid](wikipedia://en/1847), with faces that are [regular decagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_decagon) and [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285), and with all edges having unit length; its vertices lie on a common sphere, the [circumsphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumsphere) of the truncated decahedron.[^n1] The [polar reciprocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_reciprocation) of this solid through this sphere is a convex form of the triakis icosahedron, with all faces tangent to the same sphere, now an [inscribed sphere](wikipedia://en/991786), with coordinates and dimensions that can be calculated as follows.

Let $\varphi$ denote the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386). The short edges of this form of the triakis icosahedron have length $(5\varphi+15)/11 \approx 2.099$, and the long edges have length $\varphi+2 \approx 3.618$. Its faces are isosceles triangles with one obtuse angle and two acute angles of:[^o16]

$$\begin{aligned} \cos^{-1} \frac{-3\varphi}{10} &\approx 119^{\circ}, \\ \cos^{-1} \frac{\varphi+7}{10} &\approx 30.5^{\circ}. \end{aligned}$$

As a Catalan solid, its [dihedral angles](wikipedia://en/338046) are all equal

$$\cos^{-1} \frac{6\varphi+3}{4\varphi+7} \approx 160.61^\circ.$$

*Image: Triakis\_icosahedron\_hulls.svg.* Combining a dodecahedron and icosahedron to form the triakis icosahedron.

One possible set of 32 [Cartesian coordinates](wikipedia://en/7706) for the vertices of the triakis icosahedron centered at the origin (scaled differently than the one above) can be generated by combining the vertices of two appropriately scaled [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905), the [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968) and a [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361):[^o17]

- Twelve vertices of a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968), scaled to have a unit circumradius, with the coordinates $$\frac{(0, \pm 1, \pm \varphi)}{\sqrt{\varphi^2 + 1}} , \frac{(\pm 1, \pm \varphi, 0)}{\sqrt{\varphi^2 + 1}} , \frac{(\pm \varphi, 0, \pm 1)}{\sqrt{\varphi^2 + 1}}.$$
- Twenty vertices of a [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361), scaled to have circumradius $$\frac{2+\varphi}{3+2\varphi}\sqrt {\frac{3}{2-1/\varphi}}=\frac{1}{11}\sqrt {75 + 6\sqrt{5}}\approx 0.8548,$$ with the coordinates $$(\pm 1, \pm 1,\pm 1)\frac{\sqrt {25 + 2\sqrt{5}}}{11}$$ and $$(0, \pm \varphi, \pm \frac{1}{\varphi})\frac{\sqrt {25 + 2\sqrt{5}}}{11} , (\pm \frac{1}{\varphi}, 0 , \pm \varphi)\frac{\sqrt {25 + 2\sqrt{5}}}{11} , (\pm \varphi, \pm \frac{1}{\varphi},0)\frac{\sqrt {25 + 2\sqrt{5}}}{11}.$$

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

In any of its standard convex or non-convex forms, the triakis icosahedron has the same symmetries as a regular icosahedron.[^o7] The three types of [symmetry axes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_symmetry) of the icosahedron, through two opposite vertices, edge midpoints, and face centroids, become respectively axes through opposite pairs of degree-ten vertices of the triakis icosahedron, through opposite midpoints of edges between degree-ten vertices, and through opposite pairs of degree-three vertices.
