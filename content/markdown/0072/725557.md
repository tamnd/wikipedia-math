# Cryptography newsgroups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cryptography newsgroups"}

There are several [newsgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsgroup) relevant for discussions about [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) and related issues.

:::

- sci.crypt — an unmoderated forum for discussions on technical aspects of [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432).
- sci.crypt.research — a similar, [moderated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usenet_newsgroup#Moderated_newsgroups) group, focusing on research into cryptography. It was founded based on a charter by [Peter Gutmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gutmann_%28computer_scientist%29).
- sci.crypt.random-numbers — discuss [generation of cryptographically secure random numbers](wikipedia://en/182249).
- talk.politics.crypto — discussions of the relationship between cryptography and government. The original charter was by D.J. Silverton.
- alt.security.pgp — discussion of [Pretty Good Privacy](wikipedia://en/23080) (PGP) and related software.

## sci.crypt {#sci.crypt}

In 1995, [Bruce Schneier](wikipedia://en/36732) commented, "It is read by an estimated 100,000 people worldwide. Most of the posts are nonsense, bickering, or both; some are political, and most of the rest are requests for information or basic questions. Occasionally nuggets of new and useful information are posted to this newsgroup."[^o3]

Leaked descriptions of secret algorithms have been posted to the Internet via sci.crypt, for example [RC2](wikipedia://en/632592), [RC4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC4_%28cipher%29) and [Khufu and Khafre](wikipedia://en/634695). Others have been hoaxes: [Iraqi block cipher](wikipedia://en/482331) and [S-1](wikipedia://en/482324), the latter an alleged description of the (then-secret) [Skipjack](wikipedia://en/474702) cipher. The group is also the origin of the term, "[Rubber-hose cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber-hose_cryptanalysis)".
