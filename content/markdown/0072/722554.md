# Tetrakis hexahedron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tetrakis hexahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **tetrakis hexahedron** (also known as a **tetrahexahedron**, **hextetrahedron**, **tetrakis cube**, and **kiscube**) is a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393). Its dual is the [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654), an [Archimedean solid](wikipedia://en/1847).[^o8]

:::

It can be called a **disdyakis hexahedron** or **hexakis tetrahedron** as the [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_%28polyhedron%29) of an [omnitruncated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnitruncated) [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606), and as the [barycentric subdivision](wikipedia://en/583651) of a tetrahedron.

## As a Kleetope {#As_a_Kleetope}

The name "tetrakis" is used for the [Kleetopes](wikipedia://en/31165912) of polyhedra with square faces. Hence, the tetrakis hexahedron can be considered as a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) with [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455) covering each square face, the Kleetope of the cube. The resulting construction can be either convex or non-convex, depending on the square pyramids' height. For the convex result, this solid comprises twenty-four isosceles triangles. A non-convex form of this shape, with [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) faces, has the same surface geometry as the [regular octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458), and a paper octahedron model can be re-folded into this shape. This form of the tetrakis hexahedron was illustrated by [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci) in [Luca Pacioli](wikipedia://en/18473)'s *[Divina proportione](wikipedia://en/16175342)*.

*Image: Pyramid\_augmented\_cube.png.* Non-convex tetrakis hexahedron with equilateral triangle faces

Denoting the edge length of the base cube by $a$, the height of each pyramid summit above the cube is $\tfrac{a}{4}$. The inclination of each triangular face of the pyramid versus the cube face is $\arctan\tfrac{1}{2} \approx 26.565^\circ$ . One edge of the [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) has length $a$, the other two have length $\tfrac{3a}{4},$ which follows by applying the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034) to height and base length. This yields an altitude of $\tfrac{\sqrt 5 a}{4}$ in the triangle (). Its [area](wikipedia://en/30654#Computing_the_area_of_a_triangle) is $\tfrac{\sqrt 5 a^2}{8},$ and the internal angles are $\arccos\tfrac{2}{3} \approx 48.1897^\circ$ and the complementary $180^\circ - 2\arccos\tfrac{2}{3} \approx 83.6206^\circ.$ The [volume of the pyramid](wikipedia://en/3307045#Mensuration) is $\tfrac{a^3}{12};$ so the total volume of the six pyramids and the cube in the hexahedron is $\tfrac{3a^3}{2}.$

This non-convex form of the tetrakis hexahedron can be folded along the square faces of the inner cube as a [net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_%28geometry%29) for a four-dimensional [cubic pyramid](wikipedia://en/36537625).

## As a Catalan solid {#As_a_Catalan_solid}

The tetrakis hexahedron is a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393), the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) of a [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654). The truncated octahedron is an [Archimedean solid](wikipedia://en/1847), constructed by cutting all of a [regular octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458)'s vertices, so the resulting polyhedron has six squares and eight hexagons. The tetrakis hexahedron has the same symmetry as the truncated octahedron, the [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805).

[Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) for the 14 vertices of a tetrakis hexahedron centered at the origin, are the points

$$\bigl( {\pm\tfrac32}, 0, 0 \bigr),\ \bigl( 0, \pm\tfrac32, 0 \bigr),\ \bigl( 0, 0, \pm\tfrac32 \bigr),\ \bigl({\pm1}, \pm1, \pm1 \bigr).$$

The length of the shorter edges of this tetrakis hexahedron equals 3/2, and that of the longer edges equals 2. The faces are acute isosceles triangles. The larger angle of these equals $\arccos\tfrac19 \approx 83.62^{\circ}$ and the two smaller ones equal $\arccos\tfrac23 \approx 48.19^{\circ}$.

The tetrakis hexahedron is [4-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph): every three of the vertices separate from the remaining vertices, and the graph remains connected. Every 4-connected polyhedron can be realized as the [ideal polyhedron](wikipedia://en/64419430), and the tetrakis hexahedron is such.

## Applications {#Applications}

Naturally occurring ([crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal)) formations of tetrahexahedra are observed in [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) and [fluorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorite) systems.[^o8]

[Polyhedral dice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_dice) shaped like the tetrakis hexahedron are occasionally used by [gamers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamer).

A [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485#Projections) viewed under a vertex-first [perspective projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspective_projection) has a surface topology of a tetrakis hexahedron and the geometric proportions of the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), with the rhombic faces divided into two triangles.

The tetrakis hexahedron appears as one of the simplest examples in [building](wikipedia://en/275015) theory. Consider the [Riemannian symmetric space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_symmetric_space) associated to the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) [*SL*<sub>4</sub>(**R**)](wikipedia://en/144823). Its [Tits boundary](wikipedia://en/57665268) has the structure of a [spherical building](wikipedia://en/275015#Spherical_building) whose apartments are 2-dimensional spheres. The partition of this sphere into spherical [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781) (chambers) can be obtained by taking the radial projection of a tetrakis hexahedron.

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

With [tetrahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2904472), the triangular faces represent the 24 fundamental domains of tetrahedral symmetry. This polyhedron can be constructed from six [great circles](wikipedia://en/48082) on a sphere. It can also be seen by a cube with its square faces triangulated by their vertices and face centers, and a tetrahedron with its faces divided by vertices, mid-edges, and a central point.
