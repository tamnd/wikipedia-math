# Borel–Weil–Bott theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Borel–Weil–Bott theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Borel–Weil–Bott theorem** is a basic result in the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), showing how a family of representations can be obtained from holomorphic sections of certain complex [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524), and, more generally, from higher [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) groups associated to such bundles. It is built on the earlier **Borel–Weil theorem** of [Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672) and [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019), dealing just with the space of sections (the zeroth cohomology group), the extension to higher cohomology groups being provided by [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457). One can equivalently, through Serre's [GAGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAGA), view this as a result in [complex algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_algebraic_geometry) in the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Let $G$ be a [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_algebraic_group) Lie group or [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/395885) over $\mathbb C$, and fix a [maximal torus](wikipedia://en/360136) $T$ along with a [Borel subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) $B$ which contains $T$. Let $\lambda$ be an [integral weight](wikipedia://en/277179) of $T$; $\lambda$ defines in a natural way a one-dimensional representation $C_{\lambda}$ of $B$, by pulling back the representation on $T = B/U$, where $U$ is the [unipotent radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unipotent_radical) of $B$. Since we can think of the projection map $G \to G/B$ as a [principal $B$-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950), for each $C_{\lambda}$ we get an [associated fiber bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_fiber_bundle) $L_{-\lambda}$ on $G/B$ (note the sign), which is obviously a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366). Identifying $L_{\lambda}$ with its [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of holomorphic sections, we consider the [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) groups $H^i( G/B, \, L_\lambda )$. Since $G$ acts on the total space of the bundle $L_\lambda$ by bundle automorphisms, this action naturally gives a $G$-module structure on these groups; and the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem gives an explicit description of these groups as $G$-modules.

We first need to describe the [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) action centered at $- \rho$. For any integral weight $\lambda$ and $w$ in the Weyl group $W$, we set $w*\lambda := w( \lambda + \rho ) - \rho \,$, where $\rho$ denotes the half-sum of positive roots of $G$. It is straightforward to check that this defines a [group action](wikipedia://en/12781), although this action is *not* linear, unlike the usual Weyl group action. Also, a weight $\mu$ is said to be *dominant* if $\mu( \alpha^\vee ) \geq 0$ for all simple roots $\alpha$. Let denote the [length function](wikipedia://en/296332#Coxeter_group_structure) on $W$.

Given an integral weight $\lambda$, one of two cases occur:

1. There is no $w \in W$ such that $w*\lambda$ is dominant, equivalently, there exists a nonidentity $w \in W$ such that $w * \lambda = \lambda$; or
1. There is a *unique* $w \in W$ such that $w * \lambda$ is dominant.

The theorem states that in the first case, we have

$H^i( G/B, \, L_\lambda ) = 0$ for all $i$;

and in the second case, we have

$H^i( G/B, \, L_\lambda ) = 0$ for all $i \neq \ell(w)$, while

$H^{ \ell(w) }( G/B, \, L_\lambda )$ is the dual of the irreducible highest-weight representation of $G$ with highest weight $w * \lambda$.

Case (1) above occurs if and only if $(\lambda+\rho)( \beta^\vee ) = 0$ for some positive root $\beta$. Also, we obtain the classical **Borel–Weil theorem** as a special case of this theorem by taking $\lambda$ to be dominant and $w$ to be the identity element $e \in W$.

## Example {#Example}

For example, consider $G = \operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{C})$, for which $G/B$ is the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799), an integral weight is specified simply by an integer $n$, and $\rho = 1$. The line bundle $L_{n}$ is [${\mathcal O}(n)$](wikipedia://en/734893), whose [sections](wikipedia://en/367621) are the [homogeneous polynomials](wikipedia://en/1698977) of degree $n$ (i.e. the *binary forms*). As a representation of $G$, the sections can be written as $\operatorname{Sym}^{n}(\mathbf{C}^{2})*$, and is canonically isomorphic to $\operatorname{Sym}^{n}(\mathbf{C}^{2})$.

This gives us at a stroke the representation theory of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbf{C})$: $\Gamma({\mathcal O}(1))$ is the standard representation, and $\Gamma({\mathcal O}(n))$ is its $n$th [symmetric power](wikipedia://en/57047243). We even have a unified description of the action of the Lie algebra, derived from its realization as vector fields on the Riemann sphere: if $H$, $X$, $Y$ are the standard generators of $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbf{C})$, then

$$\begin{aligned} H & = x\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}, \\[5pt] X & = x\frac{\partial}{\partial y}, \\[5pt] Y & = y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}. \end{aligned}$$

## Positive characteristic {#Positive_characteristic}

One also has a weaker form of this theorem in positive characteristic. Namely, let $G$ be a semisimple algebraic group over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) of characteristic $p > 0$. Then it remains true that $H^i( G/B, \, L_\lambda ) = 0$ for all $i$ if $\lambda$ is a weight such that $w*\lambda$ is non-dominant for all $w \in W$ as long as $\lambda$ is "close to zero".[^o7] This is known as the [Kempf vanishing theorem](wikipedia://en/37552988). However, the other statements of the theorem do not remain valid in this setting.

More explicitly, let $\lambda$ be a dominant integral weight; then it is still true that $H^i( G/B, \, L_\lambda ) = 0$ for all $i > 0$, but it is no longer true that this $G$-module is simple in general, although it does contain the unique highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda$ as a $G$-submodule. If $\lambda$ is an arbitrary integral weight, it is in fact a large unsolved problem in representation theory to describe the cohomology modules $H^i( G/B, \, L_\lambda )$ in general. Unlike over $\mathbb{C}$, Mumford gave an example showing that it need not be the case for a fixed $\lambda$ that these modules are all zero except in a single degree $i$.

## Borel–Weil theorem {#Borel–Weil_theorem}

The Borel–Weil theorem provides a concrete model for [irreducible representations](wikipedia://en/385982) of [compact Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Lie_group) and irreducible holomorphic representations of [complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) [semisimple Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group). These representations are realized in the spaces of global [sections](wikipedia://en/367621) of [holomorphic line bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_line_bundle) on the [flag manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_manifold) of the group. The Borel–Weil–Bott theorem is its generalization to higher cohomology spaces. The theorem dates back to the early 1950s and can be found in (Serre 1954) and (Tits 1955).

::: {#o13 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

### Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

The theorem can be stated either for a complex semisimple Lie group $G$ or for its [compact form](wikipedia://en/22385144) $K$. Let $G$ be a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) complex semisimple Lie group, $B$ a [Borel subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) of $G$, and $X = G/B$ the [flag variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_variety). In this scenario, $X$ is a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) and a nonsingular algebraic $G$-variety. The flag variety can also be described as a compact [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) $K/T$, where $T = K \cap B$ is a (compact) [Cartan subgroup](wikipedia://en/4838153) of $K$. An [integral weight](wikipedia://en/277179#Integral_weight) $\lambda$ determines a $G$-equivariant holomorphic line bundle $L_{\lambda}$ on $X$ and the group $G$ acts on its space of global sections,

$$\Gamma(G/B,L_\lambda).\$$

The Borel–Weil theorem states that if $\lambda$ is a *dominant* integral weight then this representation is a *holomorphic* irreducible [highest weight representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight_representation) of $G$ with highest weight $\lambda$. Its restriction to $K$ is an [irreducible unitary representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_unitary_representation) of $K$ with highest weight $\lambda$, and each irreducible unitary representation of $K$ is obtained in this way for a unique value of $\lambda$. (A holomorphic representation of a [complex Lie group](wikipedia://en/2187847) is one for which the corresponding [Lie algebra representation](wikipedia://en/318712) is *complex* linear.)

:::

### Concrete description {#Concrete_description}

The weight $\lambda$ gives rise to a character (one-dimensional representation) of the Borel subgroup $B$, which is denoted $\chi_{\lambda}$. Holomorphic sections of the holomorphic line bundle $L_{\lambda}$ over $G/B$ may be described more concretely as [holomorphic maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_map)

$$f: G\to \mathbb{C}_{\lambda}: f(gb)=\chi_{\lambda}(b^{-1})f(g)$$

for all $g \in G$ and $b \in B$.

The action of $G$ on these sections is given by

$$g\cdot f(h)=f(g^{-1}h)$$

for $g, h \in G$.

::: {#o16 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

Let $G$ be the complex [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) $\operatorname{SL}(2, \mathbf{C})$, with a Borel subgroup consisting of upper triangular matrices with determinant one. Integral weights for $G$ may be identified with [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), with dominant weights corresponding to nonnegative integers, and the corresponding characters $\chi_{n}$ of $B$ have the form

$$\chi_n \begin{pmatrix} a & b\\ 0 & a^{-1} \end{pmatrix}=a^n.$$

The flag variety $G/B$ may be identified with the [complex projective line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_projective_line) $\mathbf{CP}^{1}$ with [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316) $X, Y$ and the space of the global sections of the line bundle $L_{n}$ is identified with the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $n$ on $\mathbf{C}^{2}$. For $n \ge 0$, this space has dimension $n + 1$ and forms an irreducible representation under the standard action of $G$ on the polynomial algebra $\mathbf{C}[X, Y]$. Weight vectors are given by monomials

$$X^i Y^{n-i}, \quad 0\leq i\leq n$$

of weights $2i - n$, and the highest weight vector $X^{n}$ has weight $n$.
