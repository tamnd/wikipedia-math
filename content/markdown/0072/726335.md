# Compact-open topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Compact-open topology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **compact-open topology** is a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) defined on the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [continuous maps](wikipedia://en/6122) between two [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450). The compact-open topology is one of the commonly used topologies on [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630), and is applied in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). It was introduced by [Ralph Fox](wikipedia://en/1343918) in 1945.[^o2]

:::

If the [codomain](wikipedia://en/50264) of the [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) under consideration has a [uniform structure](wikipedia://en/32339) or a [metric structure](wikipedia://en/20018) then the compact-open topology is the "topology of [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652) on [compact sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set)." That is to say, a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of functions [converges](wikipedia://en/26551602) in the compact-open topology precisely when it converges uniformly on every compact subset of the [domain](wikipedia://en/50263).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $X$ and $Y$ be two [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450), and let $C(X, Y)$ denote the set of all [continuous maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) between $X$ and $Y$. Given a [compact subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) $K$ of $X$ and an [open subset](wikipedia://en/39358) $U$ of $Y$, let $V(K, U)$ denote the set of all functions $\,f\, \in C(X, Y)$ such that $\,f\,(K) \subseteq U.$ In other words, $V(K, U) = C(K, U) \times_{C(K, Y)} C(X, Y)$. Then the collection of all such $V(K, U)$ is a [subbase](wikipedia://en/395395) for the compact-open topology on $C(X, Y)$. (This collection does not always form a [base](wikipedia://en/59220) for a topology on $C(X, Y)$.)

When working in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of [compactly generated spaces](wikipedia://en/899382), it is common to modify this definition by restricting to the subbase formed from those $K$ that are the image of a [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637). Of course, if $X$ is compactly generated and Hausdorff, this definition coincides with the previous one. However, the modified definition is crucial if one wants the convenient category of [compactly generated weak Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/19500167) spaces to be [Cartesian closed](wikipedia://en/237037), among other useful properties.[^o6][^o7][^o8] The confusion between this definition and the one above is caused by differing usage of the word [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set).

If $X$ is [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact), then $X \times -$ from the category of topological spaces has a [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint) $Hom(X, -)$. This adjoint coincides with the compact-open topology and may be used to uniquely define it. The modification of the definition for compactly generated spaces may be viewed as taking the adjoint of the product in the category of compactly generated spaces instead of the category of topological spaces, which ensures that the right adjoint always exists.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- If $*$ is a one-point space then one can identify $C(*, Y)$ with $Y$, and under this identification the compact-open topology agrees with the topology on $Y$. More generally, if $X$ is a [discrete space](wikipedia://en/56061), then $C(X, Y)$ can be identified with the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of $|X|$ copies of $Y$ and the compact-open topology agrees with the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910).
- If $Y$ is $T_{0}$, $T_{1}$, [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637), [regular](wikipedia://en/53058), or [Tychonoff](wikipedia://en/30554), then the compact-open topology has the corresponding [separation axiom](wikipedia://en/40117302).
- If $X$ is Hausdorff and $S$ is a [subbase](wikipedia://en/395395) for $Y$, then the collection $\{V(K, U) : U \in S, K \operatorname{compact}\}$is a [subbase](wikipedia://en/395395) for the compact-open topology on $C(X, Y)$.[^o11]
- If $Y$ is a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) (or more generally, a [uniform space](wikipedia://en/32339)), then the compact-open topology is equal to the [topology of compact convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_of_compact_convergence). In other words, if $Y$ is a metric space, then a sequence $\{\,f_{n}\,\}$[converges](wikipedia://en/26551602) to $\,f\,$ in the compact-open topology if and only if for every compact subset $K$ of $X$, $\{\,f_{n}\,\}$converges uniformly to $\,f\,$ on $K$. If $X$ is compact and $Y$ is a uniform space, then the compact-open topology is equal to the topology of [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652).
- If $X, Y$ and $Z$ are topological spaces, with $Y$ [locally compact Hausdorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_Hausdorff) (or even just locally compact [preregular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preregular_space)), then the [composition map](wikipedia://en/195947) $C(Y, Z) \times C(X, Y) \to C(X, Z),$ given by $(\,f\,, g) \mapsto \,f\,\circ\,g,$ is continuous (here all the function spaces are given the compact-open topology and $C(Y, Z) \times C(X, Y)$ is given the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910)).
- If $X$ is a locally compact Hausdorff (or preregular) space, then the evaluation map $e : C(X, Y) \times X \to Y$, defined by $e(\,f\,, x) = \,f\,(x)$, is continuous. This can be seen as a special case of the above where $X$ is a one-point space.
- If $X$ is compact, and $Y$ is a metric space with [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) $d$, then the compact-open topology on $C(X, Y)$ is [metrizable](wikipedia://en/19738), and a metric for it is given by $e(\,f\,, g) = \sup\{d(\,f\,(x), g(x)) : x\text{ in } X\},$ for $\,f\,, g$ in $C(X, Y)$. More generally, if $X$ is [hemicompact](wikipedia://en/5076058), and $Y$ metric, the compact-open topology is metrizable by the [construction linked here](wikipedia://en/5076058#Applications).

### Applications {#Applications}

The compact open topology (or the k-ification of it) can be used to topologize the following sets:[^o14]

- $\Omega(X,x_0) = \{ f: I \to X \mid f(0) = f(1) = x_0 \}$, the [loop space](wikipedia://en/2174332) of $X$ at $x_0$,
- $$E(X, x_0, x_1) = \{ f: I \to X \mid f(0) = x_0 \text{ and } f(1) = x_1 \},$$
- $$E(X, x_0) = \{ f: I \to X \mid f(0) = x_0 \}.$$

In addition, there is a [homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/212250#Homotopy_equivalence) between the spaces $C(\Sigma X, Y) \cong C(X, \Omega Y)$, where Σ is the [reduced suspension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_suspension).[^o14] The topological spaces $C(X,Y)$ are useful in homotopy theory because they can be used to form a topological space and a model for the homotopy type of the *set* of homotopy classes of maps

$$\pi(X,Y) = \{[f]: X \to Y \mid f \text{ is a homotopy class}\}.$$

This is because $\pi(X,Y)$ is the set of path components in $C(X,Y)$that is, there is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) of sets

$$\pi(X,Y) \to C(I, C(X, Y))/{\sim},$$

where $\sim$ is the homotopy equivalence.

## Fréchet differentiable functions {#Fréchet_differentiable_functions}

Let $X$ and $Y$ be two [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) defined over the same [field](wikipedia://en/10603), and let $C^{\,m}(U, Y)$ denote the set of all $m$-continuously [Fréchet-differentiable](wikipedia://en/2735727) functions from the open subset $U \subseteq X$ to $Y$. The compact-open topology is the [initial topology](wikipedia://en/1197123) induced by the [seminorms](wikipedia://en/292067)

$$p_{K}(f) = \sup \left\{ \left\| D^j f(x) \right\| \ : \ x \in K, 0 \leq j \leq m \right\}$$

where $D^{0}\,f\,(x) = \,f\,(x)$, for each compact subset $K \subseteq U$.
