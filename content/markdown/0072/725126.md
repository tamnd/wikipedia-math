::: {#o9 .subject cue=lead name="Pentagonal icositetrahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **pentagonal icositetrahedron** or **pentagonal icosikaitetrahedron**[^o10] is a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393) which is the [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of the [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249). In [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography) it is also called a **gyroid**.[^o11][^o12]

:::

It has two distinct forms, which are [mirror images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image) (or "[enantiomorphs](wikipedia://en/1170160)") of each other. They are termed *dextro* and *laevo* forms.

::: {#o14 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The pentagonal icositetrahedron can be constructed from a snub cube without taking the dual. Square pyramids are added to the six square faces of the snub cube, and triangular pyramids are added to the eight triangular faces that do not share an edge with a square. The pyramid heights are adjusted to make them coplanar with the other 24 triangular faces of the snub cube. The result is the pentagonal icositetrahedron.

:::

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

Denote the [tribonacci constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_constant) by $t\approx 1.839\,286\,755\,21$. (See [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249) for a geometric explanation of the tribonacci constant.) Then [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) for the 38 vertices of a pentagonal icositetrahedron centered at the origin, are as follows:

- the 12 [even permutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_permutation) of $(\pm1, \pm(2t+1), \pm t^{2})$ with an even number of minus signs
- the 12 [odd permutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_permutation) of $(\pm1, \pm(2t+1), \pm t^{2})$ with an odd number of minus signs
- the 6 points $(\pm t^{3}, 0, 0)$, $(0, \pm t^{3}, 0)$ and $(0, 0, \pm t^{3})$
- the 8 points $(\pm t^{2}, \pm t^{2}, \pm t^{2})$

The [convex hulls](wikipedia://en/40634) for these vertices[^o16] scaled by $t^{-3}$ result in a unit [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) octahedron centered at the origin, a unit cube centered at the origin scaled to $R\approx0.9416969935$, and an irregular chiral [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249) scaled to $R$, as visualized in the figure below:

*Image: Pentagonal Icositetrahedron.svg.* Combining an octahedron and snub cube to form the Pentagonal Icositetrahedron

## Geometry {#Geometry}

The pentagonal faces have four angles of $\arccos((1-t)/2)\approx 114.812\,074\,477\,90^{\circ}$ and one angle of $\arccos(2-t)\approx 80.751\,702\,088\,39^{\circ}$. The pentagon has three short edges of unit length each, and two long edges of length $(t+1)/2\approx 1.419\,643\,377\,607\,08$. The acute angle is between the two long edges. The dihedral angle equals $\arccos(-1/(t^2-2))\approx 136.309\,232\,892\,32^{\circ}$.

If its dual [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249) has unit edge length, its surface area and volume are:

$$\begin{aligned} A &= 3\sqrt{\frac{22(5t-1)}{4t-3}} &&\approx 19.299\,94 \\ V &= \sqrt{\frac{11(t-4)}{2(20t-37)}} &&\approx 7.4474 \end{aligned}$$

### Variations {#Variations}

[Isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) variations with the same chiral octahedral symmetry can be constructed with pentagonal faces having 3 edge lengths.

This variation shown can be constructed by adding pyramids to 6 square faces and 8 triangular faces of a [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249) such that the new triangular faces with 3 coplanar triangles merged into identical pentagon faces.

Among the variations mentioned above, Scott Sherman reported another case in which the pentagonal faces are bilaterally symmetrical in 2014. He called it the Bilateral Pentagonal Icositetrahedron.[^o25] In 2020, Tadaki Takahashi provided a geometric proof for this solid, naming it the Shogihedron because the shape of its faces resembles the pieces in the Japanese board game Shogi.[^o26]

## Related polyhedra and tilings {#Related_polyhedra_and_tilings}

*Image: Spherical pentagonal icositetrahedron.svg.* Spherical pentagonal icositetrahedron

This polyhedron is topologically related as a part of sequence of polyhedra and tilings of pentagons with [face configurations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration) (V3.3.3.3.*n*). (The sequence progresses into tilings the hyperbolic plane to any *n*.) These [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive) figures have (n32) rotational [symmetry](wikipedia://en/2915678).

The *pentagonal icositetrahedron* is second in a series of dual snub polyhedra and tilings with [face configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration) V3.3.4.3.*n*.

The pentagonal icositetrahedron is one of a family of duals to the uniform polyhedra related to the cube and regular octahedron.
