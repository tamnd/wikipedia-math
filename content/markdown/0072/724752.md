::: {#o9 .subject cue=lead name="Disdyakis dodecahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **disdyakis dodecahedron**, (also **hexoctahedron**,[^o10] **hexakis octahedron**, **octakis cube**, **octakis hexahedron**, **kisrhombic dodecahedron**[^o11]) or **d48**, is a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393) with 48 faces and the dual to the [Archimedean](wikipedia://en/1847) [truncated cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/285879). As such it is [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive) but with irregular face polygons. It resembles an augmented [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631). Replacing each face of the rhombic dodecahedron with a flat pyramid results in the [Kleetope](wikipedia://en/31165912) of the rhombic dodecahedron, which looks almost like the disdyakis dodecahedron, and is [topologically](wikipedia://en/29954) equivalent to it. The net of the [rhombic dodecahedral pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombic_dodecahedral_pyramid) also shares the same topology.

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

It has O<sub>h</sub> [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805). Its collective edges represent the reflection planes of the symmetry. It can also be seen in the corner and mid-edge triangulation of the regular cube and octahedron, and rhombic dodecahedron.

The edges of a spherical disdyakis dodecahedron belong to 9 [great circles](wikipedia://en/48082). Three of them form a spherical octahedron (gray in the images below). The remaining six form three square [hosohedra](wikipedia://en/2316192) (red, green and blue in the images below). They all correspond to [mirror planes](wikipedia://en/295829) - the former in [dihedral](wikipedia://en/2922089) \[2,2\], and the latter in [tetrahedral](wikipedia://en/2904472) \[3,3\] symmetry. A spherical disdyakis dodecahedron can be thought of as the [barycentric subdivision](wikipedia://en/583651) of the [spherical cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_cube) or of the [spherical octahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_octahedron).[^o16]

## Dimensions {#Dimensions}

If its smallest edges have length *a*, its surface area and volume are

$$\begin{aligned} A &= \tfrac67\sqrt{783+436\sqrt 2}\,a^2 \\ V &= \tfrac17\sqrt{3\left(2194+1513\sqrt 2\right)}a^3\end{aligned}$$

The faces are scalene triangles. Their angles are $\arccos\biggl(\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{12}\sqrt{2}\biggr) ~{\color{Gray}\approx 87.201^{\circ}}$, $\arccos\biggl(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{2}\biggr) ~{\color{Gray}\approx 55.024^{\circ}}$ and $\arccos\biggl(\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}\biggr) ~{\color{Gray}\approx 37.773^{\circ}}$.

## Orthogonal projections {#Orthogonal_projections}

## Related polyhedra and tilings {#Related_polyhedra_and_tilings}

::: {#o26 .table cue=table}

| *Image: Conway polyhedron m3O.png.* | *Image: Conway polyhedron m3C.png.* |
|---|---|
| Polyhedra similar to the disdyakis dodecahedron are duals to the [Bowtie octahedron and cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetrohedron), containing extra pairs of triangular faces.[^o28] |  |

:::

The disdyakis dodecahedron is one of a family of duals to the uniform polyhedra related to the cube and regular octahedron.

It is a polyhedra in a sequence defined by the [face configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration) V4.6.2*n*. This group is special for having all even number of edges per vertex and form bisecting planes through the polyhedra and infinite lines in the plane, and continuing into the hyperbolic plane for any *n* ≥ 7.

With an even number of faces at every vertex, these polyhedra and tilings can be shown by alternating two colors so all adjacent faces have different colors.

Each face on these domains also corresponds to the fundamental domain of a [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) with order 2,3,*n* mirrors at each triangle face vertex.
