# Family of sets

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Family of sets"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and related branches of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **family** or **collection** is used to mean [set](wikipedia://en/26691), [indexed set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indexed_set), [multiset](wikipedia://en/305303), [tuple](wikipedia://en/132729), or [class](wikipedia://en/20696). It is usually used in phrases like "family of sets" because if one instead uses "set of sets" then the subsequent use of "set" can be confusing as to whether it is the containing set or one of the member sets. A common use is "family of subsets of some set $S$". A family of sets is also called a **set family** or a **set system**. A finite family of subsets of a [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742) $S$ is also called a *[hypergraph](wikipedia://en/232535)*. The subject of [extremal set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremal_set_theory) concerns the largest and smallest examples of families of sets satisfying certain restrictions.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The collection of all subsets of a given set $S$ is called the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of $S$ and is denoted by $\wp(S)$. The [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) $\wp(S)$ of a given set $S$ is a family of sets over $S$.

A subset of $S$ having $k$ elements is called a [$k$-subset](wikipedia://en/11742#Definition_and_terminology) of $S$. The [$k$-subsets](wikipedia://en/11742#Definition_and_terminology) $S^{(k)}$ of a set $S$ form a family of sets.

Let $S = \{{(}}a, b, c, 1, 2\{{)}}$. An example of a family of sets over $S$ (in the [multiset](wikipedia://en/305303) sense) is given by $F = \left\{{(}}A_1, A_2, A_3, A_4\right\{{)}}$, where $A_1 = \{a, b, c\}, A_2 = \{1, 2\}, A_3 = \{{(}}1, 2\{{)}}$, and $A_4 = \{{(}}a, b, 1\{{)}}$.

The class $\operatorname{Ord}$ of all [ordinal numbers](wikipedia://en/26547932) is a *large* family of sets. That is, it is not itself a set but instead a [proper class](wikipedia://en/20696).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Any family of subsets of a set $S$ is itself a subset of the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) $\wp(S)$ if it has no repeated members.

Any family of sets without repetitions is a [subclass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subclass_%28sets%29) of the [proper class](wikipedia://en/20696) of all sets (the [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe_%28math%29)).

[Hall's marriage theorem](wikipedia://en/186969), due to [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033), gives necessary and sufficient conditions for a finite family of non-empty sets (repetitions allowed) to have a [system of distinct representatives](wikipedia://en/897733).

If $\mathcal{F}$ is any family of sets then $\cup \mathcal{F} := {\textstyle \bigcup\limits_{F \in \mathcal{F}}} F$ denotes the union of all sets in $\mathcal F$, where in particular, $\cup \varnothing = \varnothing$. Any family $\mathcal{F}$ of sets is a family over $\cup \mathcal{F}$ and also a family over any superset of $\cup \mathcal F$.

The *trace* of a family $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $S$ on a subset $T \subseteq S$ is $\{A \cap T, A \in \mathcal{F}\}$.

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

Certain types of objects from other areas of mathematics are equivalent to families of sets, in that they can be described purely as a collection of sets of objects of some type:

- A [hypergraph](wikipedia://en/232535), also called a set system, is formed by a set of [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) together with another set of *[hyperedges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperedges)*, each of which may be an arbitrary set. The hyperedges of a hypergraph form a family of sets, and any family of sets can be interpreted as a hypergraph that has the union of the sets as its vertices.
- An [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) is a combinatorial abstraction of the notion of a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686), a shape formed by unions of line segments, triangles, tetrahedra, and higher-dimensional [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781), joined face to face. In an abstract simplicial complex, each simplex is represented simply as the set of its vertices. Any family of finite sets without repetitions in which the subsets of any set in the family also belong to the family forms an abstract simplicial complex.
- An [incidence structure](wikipedia://en/723125) consists of a set of *points*, a set of *lines*, and an (arbitrary) [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931), called the *incidence relation*, specifying which points belong to which lines. An incidence structure can be specified by a family of sets (even if two distinct lines contain the same set of points), the sets of points belonging to each line, and any family of sets can be interpreted as an incidence structure in this way.
- A binary [block code](wikipedia://en/1134659) consists of a set of codewords, each of which is a [string](wikipedia://en/27701) of 0s and 1s, all the same length. When each pair of codewords has large [Hamming distance](wikipedia://en/41227), it can be used as an [error-correcting code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error-correcting_code). A block code can also be described as a family of sets, by describing each codeword as the set of positions at which it contains a 1.
- A [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) consists of a pair $(X, \tau)$ where $X$ is a set (whose elements are called *points*) and $\tau$ is a [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_%28structure%29) on $X$, which is a family of sets (whose elements are called *open sets*) over $X$ that contains both the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) $\varnothing$ and $X$ itself, and is closed under arbitrary set unions and finite set intersections.

### Covers and topologies {#Covers_and_topologies}

A family of sets is said to [cover](wikipedia://en/317552) a set $X$ if every point of $X$ belongs to some member of the family. A subfamily of a cover of $X$ that is also a cover of $X$ is called a [subcover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcover). A family is called a [point-finite collection](wikipedia://en/9856577) if every point of $X$ lies in only finitely many members of the family. If every point of a cover lies in exactly one member of $X$, the cover is a [partition](wikipedia://en/340240) of $X$.

When $X$ is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), a cover whose members are all [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358) is called an [open cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover). A family is called [locally finite](wikipedia://en/11611098) if each point in the space has a [neighborhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) that intersects only finitely many members of the family. A [σ-locally finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Σ-locally_finite) or [countably locally finite collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_locally_finite_collection) is a family that is the union of countably many locally finite families.

A cover $\mathcal{F}$ is said to [refine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refinement_of_an_open_cover) another (coarser) cover $\mathcal{C}$ if every member of $\mathcal{F}$ is contained in some member of $\mathcal C$. A [star refinement](wikipedia://en/982000) is a particular type of refinement.

## Special types of set families {#Special_types_of_set_families}

A **[Sperner family](wikipedia://en/748730)** is a set family in which none of the sets contains any of the others. [Sperner's theorem](wikipedia://en/748844) bounds the maximum size of a Sperner family.

A **[Helly family](wikipedia://en/670453)** is a set family such that any minimal subfamily with empty intersection has bounded size. [Helly's theorem](wikipedia://en/1409506) states that [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292) in [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697) of bounded dimension form Helly families.

An **[abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105)** is a set family $F$ (consisting of finite sets) that is [downward closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downward_closed); that is, every subset of a set in $F$ is also in $F$. A **[matroid](wikipedia://en/244321)** is an abstract simplicial complex with an additional property called the *[augmentation property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentation_property)*.

Every [filter](wikipedia://en/12646549) is a family of sets.

A **[convexity space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convexity_space)** is a set family closed under arbitrary intersections and unions of [chains](wikipedia://en/30330) (with respect to the [inclusion relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_relation)).

Other examples of set families are [independence systems](wikipedia://en/2471386), [greedoids](wikipedia://en/666526), [antimatroids](wikipedia://en/667063), and [bornological spaces](wikipedia://en/1804457).
