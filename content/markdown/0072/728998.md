# Wilhelm Blaschke

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wilhelm Blaschke"}

**Wilhelm Johann Eugen Blaschke** (13 September 1885 – 17 March 1962) was an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in the fields of [differential](wikipedia://en/8625) and [integral geometry](wikipedia://en/349336). In his time he was best known for his influential three-volume treatise *Vorlesungen über Differentialgeometrie* and later for his connection to [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905).[^o6][^o10] He was a member of the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party) and openly supported it.[^o14]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and career {#Early_life_and_career}

Blaschke was the son of mathematician Josef Blaschke (1852–1917), who taught geometry at the Landes Oberrealschule in [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz), and Maria Blaschke (1864–1945), née Edle von Mor zu Sunnegg und Morberg. After studying for two years at the Technische Hochschule in Graz, he went to the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna), and completed a doctorate in 1908 under the supervision of [Wilhelm Wirtinger](wikipedia://en/2044921). His dissertation was *Über eine besondere Art von Kurven vierter Klasse*.[^o9]

After completing his doctorate he spent several years visiting mathematicians at the major universities in Italy and Germany, including [Luigi Bianchi](wikipedia://en/701065) at Pisa, [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887), [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), and [Carl Runge](wikipedia://en/747636) at Göttingen, [Eduard Study](wikipedia://en/663997) at Bonn, and [Friedrich Engel](wikipedia://en/5686283) at Greifswald.[^o6] He then spent two years each in positions in Prague, Leipzig, Königsberg, and Tübingen.

In 1919, he took one of the first professorships in mathematics at the newly formed [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg), along with [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990), where he remained for the rest of his career.[^o5] One of his early hires to the department at Hamburg was [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351). In 1922 he established the journal *[Abhandlungen aus dem Mathematischen Seminar der Universität Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhandlungen_aus_dem_Mathematischen_Seminar_der_Universität_Hamburg)* (English: *Reports from the Mathematical Seminar of the University of Hamburg*), which published articles in pure mathematics, including numerous contributions from famous mathematicians. He was also one of the first editors of the [Springer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Science+Business_Media) series of textbooks *Grundlehren der mathematischen Wissenschaften* (English: *Foundations of Mathematical Sciences*). While at Hamburg, Blaschke continued to travel. He toured the United States in 1930 and 1931 as a visiting lecturer of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822);[^o7] his stops included the Universities of Stanford, Chicago, and Pennsylvania. In 1932 Blaschke visited China, where he lectured at [Peking University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_University).[^o8] Blaschke's students at Hamburg included [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905), [Luis Santaló](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Santaló), and [Emanuel Sperner](wikipedia://en/374404).[^o10]

Blaschke married his second wife, Auguste Meta Anna Röttger (1893–1992) on 10 April 1923; they had one daughter and one son.

### Politics and later life {#Politics_and_later_life}

In 1933 Blaschke signed the *[Vow of allegiance of the Professors of the German Universities and High-Schools to Adolf Hitler and the National Socialistic State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vow_of_allegiance_of_the_Professors_of_the_German_Universities_and_High-Schools_to_Adolf_Hitler_and_the_National_Socialistic_State)*.[^o13][^o12] However, he defended [Kurt Reidemeister](wikipedia://en/520188) against the Nazis and, in the early 1930s, campaigned against [Ludwig Bieberbach](wikipedia://en/616186) for leadership of the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973), arguing that the society should remain international and apolitical in opposition to Bieberbach's wish to "enforce Nazi policies on German mathematics and race". However, by 1934 he was supporting Nazi policies, e.g., overseeing the dismissal of [Max Zorn](wikipedia://en/19740) from Halle.[^o14] He called himself "a Nazi at Heart", and was described by colleagues as "Mussolinetto" for his fascist beliefs.[^o5] He officially joined the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party) in 1937.[^o13]

After the war, Blaschke was removed from his position as Dean at the University of Hamburg by the British on 3 September 1945 for his Nazi affiliation. After an appeal, against strong objections from [Heinrich Behnke](wikipedia://en/22922678), and with support from Reidemeister, [Constantin Caratheodory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Caratheodory), [Hans Zassenhaus](wikipedia://en/1376825), [Konrad Knopp](wikipedia://en/5630788), [Gustav Herglotz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Herglotz), and, crucially, [W.V.D. Hodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W.V.D._Hodge), Blaschke was reinstated on 23 October 1946.[^o5][^o14] He remained at the university until his retirement on 30 September 1953.[^o5] Blaschke spent some time after retirement as a visiting professor in [Istanbul University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_University) in 1953 and 1954.[^o15]

Over the course of his career, Blaschke gained membership to numerous academic societies: The [German National Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_National_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina), the [Austrian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Academy_of_Sciences), the [Academy of Sciences and Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_and_Literature), the [Bavarian Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities), the [Saxon Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxon_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities) in Leipzig, and the [German Academy of Sciences at Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_at_Berlin). He also received honorary doctorates from [Sofia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_University), the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua), the [Karlsruhe Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Institute_of_Technology), and the [University of Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greifswald).[^o5]

Blaschke died on 17 March 1962. He is buried in [Ohlsdorf Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohlsdorf_Cemetery).

## Publications {#Publications}

In 1916 Blaschke published one of the first books devoted to [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292): *Circle and Sphere* (*Kreis und Kugel*). Drawing on dozens of sources, Blaschke made a thorough review of the subject with citations within the text to attribute [credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_%28creative_arts%29) in a classical area of mathematics.

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

Several theorems and mathematical concepts are named for Blaschke:

- Blaschke selection theorem
- Blaschke–Lebesgue theorem
- Blaschke product, for points in a unit disk satisfying the [Blaschke condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaschke_condition)
- Blaschke sum
- [Blaschke–Busemann measure](wikipedia://en/1573991#Blaschke–Busemann_measure) – Generalization of Riemannian metrics on surfaces
- [Blaschke–Santaló inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaschke–Santaló_inequality) – On the Mahler volumes of centrally symmetric convex bodies
- [Blaschke conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaschke_conjecture) – That the only manifold where all geodesics through any point pass through a second point is the Euclidean sphere

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The Wilhelm Blaschke Memorial Foundation of Hamburg, founded by Emanuel Sperner, awards the Wilhelm Blaschke Medal at irregular intervals for outstanding achievements in geometry. Recipients include Kurt Leichtweiss, [Katsumi Nomizu](wikipedia://en/37009604),[^o26] and S.S. Chern.[^o27][^o28]
