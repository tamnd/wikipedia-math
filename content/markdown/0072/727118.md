# Helmut Hasse

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helmut Hasse"}

*Image: EmilArtin HelmutHasse MFO9054.jpg.* Hasse with [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) in 1930

**Helmut Hasse** (; 25 August 1898 – 26 December 1979) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), known for fundamental contributions to [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695), the application of [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423) to [local class field theory](wikipedia://en/669532) and [diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205) ([Hasse principle](wikipedia://en/489707)), and to [local zeta functions](wikipedia://en/396014).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Hasse was born in [Kassel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassel), [Province of Hesse-Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse-Nassau),[^o5] the son of Judge Paul Reinhard Hasse, also written Haße (12 April 1868 – 1 June 1940,[^o6] son of Friedrich Ernst Hasse and his wife Anna Von Reinhard) and his wife Margarethe Louise Adolphine Quentin (born 5 July 1872 in [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee), daughter of retail toy merchant[^o7] Adolph Quentin (b. May 1832, probably [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia)) and Margarethe Wehr (b. about 1840, Prussia),[^o8] then raised in Kassel).[^o9]

After serving in the [Imperial German Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_Navy) in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), he studied at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), and then at the [University of Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Marburg) under [Kurt Hensel](wikipedia://en/2242565), writing a dissertation in 1921 containing the [Hasse–Minkowski theorem](wikipedia://en/1109126), as it is now called, on [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) over [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field). He then held positions at [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel), [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle-Wittenberg) and [Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Marburg). He was [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544)'s replacement at Göttingen in 1934.

Hasse was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in 1932 in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) and a Plenary Speaker of the ICM in 1936 in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo).[^o10]

In 1933 Hasse had signed the *[Vow of allegiance of the Professors of the German Universities and High-Schools to Adolf Hitler and the National Socialistic State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vow_of_allegiance_of_the_Professors_of_the_German_Universities_and_High-Schools_to_Adolf_Hitler_and_the_National_Socialistic_State)*.

In 1937, he applied for membership in the Nazi Party on guidance from a close friend to help hire distinguished mathematicians at Göttingen.[^o11] This application was denied to him allegedly due to his remote [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) ancestry.[^o12] After the war, he briefly returned to Göttingen in 1945, but was excluded by the British authorities. After brief appointments in Berlin, from 1948 on he settled permanently as professor at [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg).

He collaborated with many mathematicians, in particular with [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) and [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134) on [simple algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_algebra), and with [Harold Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111) on [Gauss sums](wikipedia://en/6378204) ([Hasse–Davenport relations](wikipedia://en/14619729)), and with [Cahit Arf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahit_Arf) on the [Hasse–Arf theorem](wikipedia://en/22590101).
