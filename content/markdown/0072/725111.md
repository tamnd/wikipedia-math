::: {#o9 .subject cue=lead name="Disdyakis triacontahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **disdyakis triacontahedron**, **hexakis icosahedron**, **decakis dodecahedron**, **kisrhombic triacontahedron**[^o10] or **d120** is a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393) with 120 faces and the dual to the [Archimedean](wikipedia://en/1847) [truncated icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/286534). As such it is [face-uniform](wikipedia://en/6286120) but with [irregular](wikipedia://en/333306) face [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621). It slightly resembles an inflated [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025): if one replaces each face of the rhombic triacontahedron with a single [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) and four triangles in a regular fashion, one ends up with a disdyakis triacontahedron. That is, the disdyakis triacontahedron is the [Kleetope](wikipedia://en/31165912) of the rhombic triacontahedron. It is also the [barycentric subdivision](wikipedia://en/583651) of the regular [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) and [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084). It has the most faces among the Archimedean and Catalan solids, with the [snub dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/287137), with 92 faces, in second place.

:::

If the [bipyramids](wikipedia://en/4153), the [gyroelongated bipyramids](wikipedia://en/5506771), and the [trapezohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapezohedra) are excluded, the disdyakis triacontahedron has the most faces of any other strictly [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) where every [face](wikipedia://en/149926) of the polyhedron has the same shape.

[Projected](wikipedia://en/243849) into a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859), the edges of a disdyakis triacontahedron define 15 [great circles](wikipedia://en/48082). [Buckminster Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckminster_Fuller) used these 15 great circles, along with 10 and 6 others in two other polyhedra, to define his [31 great circles of the spherical icosahedron](wikipedia://en/54384759).

## Geometry {#Geometry}

Being a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393) with triangular faces, the disdyakis triacontahedron's three face angles $\alpha_4, \alpha_6, \alpha_{10}$ and common dihedral angle $\theta$ must obey the following constraints analogous to other Catalan solids:

$$\sin(\theta/2) = \cos(\pi/4) / \cos(\alpha_4/2)$$

$$\sin(\theta/2) = \cos(\pi/6) / \cos(\alpha_6/2)$$

$$\sin(\theta/2) = \cos(\pi/10) / \cos(\alpha_{10}/2)$$

$$\alpha_4 + \alpha_6 + \alpha_{10} = \pi$$

The above four equations are solved simultaneously to get the following face angles and dihedral angle:

$$\alpha_4 = \arccos \left(\frac{7-4\phi}{30} \right) \approx 88.992^{\circ}$$

$$\alpha_6 = \arccos \left( \frac{17-4\phi}{20} \right) \approx 58.238^{\circ}$$

$$\alpha_{10} = \arccos \left( \frac{2+5\phi}{12} \right) \approx 32.770^{\circ}$$

$$\theta = \arccos \left( -\frac{155 + 48\phi}{241} \right) \approx 164.888^{\circ}$$

where $\phi = \frac{\sqrt{5}+1}{2} \approx 1.618$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).

As with all Catalan solids, the dihedral angles at all edges are the same, even though the edges may be of different lengths.

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

*Image: Disdyakis\_Triacontahedron.svg.* Disdyakis triacontahedron hulls.

The 62 vertices of a disdyakis triacontahedron are given by:[^o13]

- Twelve vertices $\left(0, \frac{\pm 1}{\sqrt{\phi+2}} , \frac{\pm \phi}{\sqrt{\phi+2}} \right)$ and their cyclic permutations,
- Eight vertices $\left(\pm R, \pm R, \pm R\right)$,
- Twelve vertices $\left(0, \pm R\phi, \pm \frac{R}{\phi}\right)$ and their cyclic permutations,
- Six vertices $\left(\pm S, 0, 0\right)$ and their cyclic permutations.
- Twenty-four vertices $\left(\pm \frac{S\phi}{2}, \pm\frac{S}{2}, \pm\frac{S}{2\phi}\right)$ and their cyclic permutations,

where

$$R = \frac{5}{3\phi\sqrt{\phi+2}} = \frac{\sqrt{25 - 10\sqrt{5}}}{3} \approx 0.5415328270548438,$$

$S = \frac{(7\phi - 6) \sqrt{\phi+2}}{11} = \frac{(2\sqrt{5} - 3) \sqrt{25 + 10\sqrt{5}}}{11} \approx 0.9210096876986302$, and

$\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.618$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).

In the above coordinates, the first 12 vertices form a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968), the next 20 vertices (those with *R*) form a [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361), and the last 30 vertices (those with *S*) form an [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023).

Normalizing all vertices to the unit sphere gives a *spherical* disdyakis triacontahedron, shown in the adjacent figure. This figure also depicts the 120 transformations associated with the full [icosahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_group) *Ih*.

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

The edges of the polyhedron projected onto a sphere form 15 [great circles](wikipedia://en/48082), and represent all 15 mirror planes of reflective *I<sub>h</sub>* [icosahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2877844). Combining pairs of light and dark triangles define the fundamental domains of the nonreflective (*I*) icosahedral symmetry. The edges of a [compound of five octahedra](wikipedia://en/8010546) also represent the 10 mirror planes of icosahedral symmetry.

## Uses {#Uses}

*Image: Disdyakis triacontahedron dodecahedral 12-color.png.* *Big Chop* puzzle

The *disdyakis triacontahedron*, as a regular dodecahedron with pentagons divided into 10 triangles each, is considered the "holy grail" for [combination puzzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combination_puzzle) like the [Rubik's Cube](wikipedia://en/25971). Such a puzzle currently has no satisfactory mechanism. It is the most significant unsolved problem in mechanical puzzles, often called the "big chop" problem.[^o25]

This shape was used to make 120-sided dice using 3D printing.[^o26]

Since 2016, the Dice Lab has used the disdyakis triacontahedron to mass-market an injection-moulded 120-sided [die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dice).[^o27] It is claimed that 120 is the largest possible number of faces on a fair die, aside from infinite families (such as right regular [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), [bipyramids](wikipedia://en/4153), and [trapezohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapezohedra)) that would be impractical in reality due to the tendency to roll for a long time.[^o28]

A disdyakis triacontahedron [projected onto a sphere](wikipedia://en/10393861) was previously used as the logo for [Brilliant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brilliant.org), a website containing series of lessons on [STEM](wikipedia://en/3437663)-related topics.[^o29]

Because the disdyakis triacontahedron has the highest [sphericity](wikipedia://en/2628049) of any [isohedral figure](wikipedia://en/6286120), it has been studied for its potential use in constructing [discrete global grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_global_grid) systems for satellite imaging.[^o30]

:::

## Related polyhedra and tilings {#Related_polyhedra_and_tilings}

::: {#o32 .table cue=table}

| *Image: Conway\_polyhedron\_m3I.png.* | *Image: Conway\_polyhedron\_m3D.png.* |
|---|---|
| Polyhedra similar to the disdyakis triacontahedron are duals to the Bowtie icosahedron and dodecahedron, containing extra pairs of triangular faces.[^o33] |  |

:::

It is topologically related to a polyhedra sequence defined by the [face configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration) *V4.6.2n*. This group is special for having all even number of edges per vertex and form bisecting planes through the polyhedra and infinite lines in the plane, and continuing into the hyperbolic plane for any *n* ≥ 7.

With an even number of faces at every vertex, these polyhedra and tilings can be shown by alternating two colors so all adjacent faces have different colors.

Each face on these domains also corresponds to the fundamental domain of a [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) with order 2,3,*n* mirrors at each triangle face vertex. This is \**n*32 in [orbifold notation](wikipedia://en/2915678), and \[*n*,3\] in [Coxeter notation](wikipedia://en/32088502).
