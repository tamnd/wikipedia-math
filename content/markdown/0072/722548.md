# Triakis tetrahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Triakis tetrahedron"}

*Image: Triakis tetrahedron.stl.* 3D model of a triakis tetrahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **triakis tetrahedron** (or **tristetrahedron**, or **kistetrahedron**) is a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393), constructed by attaching four triangular pyramids to a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521).

:::

## As a Kleetope {#As_a_Kleetope}

The triakis tetrahedron is constructed by attaching four triangular pyramids onto the triangular faces of a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521), a [Kleetope](wikipedia://en/31165912) of a tetrahedron. This replaces the equilateral triangular faces of the regular tetrahedron with three [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428) at each face, so there are twelve in total; eight vertices and eighteen edges form them. This interpretation is also expressed in the name, triakis, which is used for [Kleetopes](wikipedia://en/31165912) of polyhedra with triangular faces.

## As a Catalan solid {#As_a_Catalan_solid}

The triakis tetrahedron is a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393), the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) of a [truncated tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/279651), an [Archimedean solid](wikipedia://en/1847) with four hexagonal and four triangular faces, constructed by cutting off the vertices of a regular tetrahedron; it shares the same [symmetry of full tetrahedral](wikipedia://en/2904472) $\mathrm{T}_\mathrm{d}$. Each [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) between triangular faces is $\arccos(-7/11) \approx 129.52^\circ$. Unlike its dual, the triakis tetrahedron is not [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive), but rather [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive), meaning its solid appearance is unchanged by any transformation like reflecting and rotation between two triangular faces. The triakis tetrahedron [can pass through a copy of itself of the same size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_property), but it is an exceptionally tight squeeze: the largest known triakis tetrahedron that can pass through is only about 1.000004 times larger.

The triakis tetrahedron is the [stacked polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stacked_polyhedron) that is a [non-ideal](wikipedia://en/64419430). Combinatorially, it has [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) of exactly half the vertices but is not [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431), so neither can be realized as an ideal polyhedron.

## Related polyhedron {#Related_polyhedron}

A triakis tetrahedron is different from an *augmented tetrahedron* as latter is obtained by [augmenting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentation_%28geometry%29) the four faces of a tetrahedron with four *regular* tetrahedra (instead of nonuniform triangular pyramids) resulting in an equilateral polyhedron which is a concave [deltahedron](wikipedia://en/493995) (whose all faces are congruent equilateral triangles). The convex hull of an augmented tetrahedron is a triakis tetrahedron.[^o6]
