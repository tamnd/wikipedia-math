# Griess algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Griess algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Griess algebra** is a [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative) [non-associative algebra](wikipedia://en/6533841) on a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) 196884 that has the [Monster group](wikipedia://en/47422) *M* as its [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772). It is named after mathematician [R. L. Griess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._L._Griess), who constructed it in 1980 to prove the existence of the Monster group, referring to it as "The Friendly Giant".[^o2]

:::

## Matrix representation size {#Matrix_representation_size}

To visualize the complexity of the Monster group, an individual element $g \in M$, when acting on the 196883-dimensional irreducible subspace $W$, can be represented as a square matrix.

$$g = \begin{pmatrix} g_{1,1} & g_{1,2} & \dots & g_{1,196883} \\ g_{2,1} & g_{2,2} & \dots & g_{2,196883} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ g_{196883,1} & g_{196883,2} & \dots & g_{196883,196883} \end{pmatrix}$$

*Figure: A schematic representation of a single element of the Monster group as a $196883 \times 196883$ matrix. The sheer quantity of entries ($196883^2 \approx 3.8 \times 10^{10}$) means that storing just one such matrix explicitly, even over a small finite field like $\mathbb{F}_2$ (1 bit per entry), requires approximately 4.5 GB of data storage.*

## History and inspiration {#History_and_inspiration}

In 1973, [Bernd Fischer](wikipedia://en/29628162) and Robert Griess independently produced evidence for the existence of the [Monster group](wikipedia://en/47422). However, for several years, the group remained a conjecture because its immense order (approx. $8 \times 10^{53}$) made computer construction impossible at the time. To prove its existence, Griess undertook the task of constructing a specific mathematical object upon which this group would act naturally. This resulted in the 196884-dimensional algebra, announced in 1980 and published in 1982.[^o2]

## Context within the classification {#Context_within_the_classification}

The construction of the Griess algebra was a pivotal moment in the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445). By the 1970s, mathematicians had categorized most simple groups into infinite families, but were left with 26 exceptional cases known as [sporadic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_groups). The Monster was the largest of these. Griess's manual construction of the algebra provided the physical model for the Monster, bridging the gap between theoretical prediction and existence.

## Structure and properties {#Structure_and_properties}

The algebra is equipped with a [positive definite](wikipedia://en/10273855) symmetric [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) (inner product) invariant under the Monster group, satisfying the identity:

$$\langle xy, z \rangle = \langle x, yz \rangle$$

The dimension 196884 arises from the decomposition of the algebra into invariant subspaces:

$$V = \mathbb{R}\mathbf{1} \oplus W$$

- **The 1-dimensional subspace** ($\mathbb{R}\mathbf{1}$): Spanned by a specific element fixed by the group.
- **The 196883-dimensional subspace** ($W$): The orthogonal complement, where the Monster acts [absolutely irreducibly](wikipedia://en/385982).

This dimension relates to [Monstrous moonshine](wikipedia://en/728168), corresponding to the linear term coefficient in the [*j*-function](wikipedia://en/451988) ($196884 = 1 + 196883$).

## Construction {#Construction}

Griess originally constructed the algebra as a direct sum of two vector spaces related to the [Leech lattice](wikipedia://en/344919). This construction was later simplified by [Jacques Tits](wikipedia://en/219570) and [John H. Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_H._Conway).[^o9] The Griess algebra is also identified as the degree 2 piece of the [monster vertex algebra](wikipedia://en/5032859). The Monster fixes (vectorwise) a 1-space in this algebra and acts absolutely irreducibly on the 196883-dimensional [orthogonal complement](wikipedia://en/1058833) of this 1-space. (The Monster preserves the standard [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) on the 196884-space.)
