# Deltoidal hexecontahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Deltoidal hexecontahedron"}

*Image: Deltoidal hexecontahedron.stl.* 3D model of a deltoidal hexecontahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **deltoidal hexecontahedron** (also sometimes called a **trapezoidal hexecontahedron**, a **strombic hexecontahedron**, or a **tetragonal hexacontahedron**[^o3]) is a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393) which is the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) of the [rhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/285486), an [Archimedean solid](wikipedia://en/1847). It is one of six Catalan solids to not have a [Hamiltonian path](wikipedia://en/244437) among its vertices.[^o4]

:::

It is topologically identical to the nonconvex [rhombic hexecontahedron](wikipedia://en/43733185).

## Lengths and angles {#Lengths_and_angles}

The 60 faces are deltoids or [kites](wikipedia://en/84840). The short and long edges of each kite are in the ratio $1:\frac{7+\sqrt{5}}{6}$ ≈ 1:1.539344663...

The angle between two short edges in a single face is $\arccos(\frac{-5-2\sqrt{5}}{20})$ ≈ 118.2686774705°. The opposite angle, between long edges, is $\arccos(\frac{-5+9\sqrt{5}}{40})$ ≈ 67.783011547435°. The other two angles of each face, between a short and a long edge each, are both equal to $\arccos(\frac{5-2\sqrt{5}}{10})$ ≈ 86.97415549104°.

The dihedral angle between any pair of adjacent faces is $\arccos(\frac{-19-8\sqrt{5}}{41})$ ≈ 154.12136312578°.

## Topology {#Topology}

Topologically, the deltoidal hexecontahedron is identical to the nonconvex [rhombic hexecontahedron](wikipedia://en/43733185). The deltoidal hexecontahedron can be derived from a [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) (or [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084)) by pushing the face centers, edge centers and vertices out to different radii from the body center. The radii are chosen so that the resulting shape has planar kite faces each such that vertices go to degree-3 corners, faces to degree-five corners, and edge centers to degree-four points.

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

The 62 vertices of the deltoidal hexecontahedron fall in three sets centered on the origin:

- Twelve vertices are of the form of a unit [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968).
- Twenty vertices are of the form of a $\frac{3}{11}\sqrt {15 - \frac{6}{\sqrt{5}}}\approx 0.9571$ scaled [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361).
- Thirty vertices are of the form of a $3\sqrt {1-\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}}\approx0.9748$ scaled [Icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023).

## Variations {#Variations}

*Image: Perspectiva Corporum Regularium 41b.jpg.* This figure from *[Perspectiva Corporum Regularium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspectiva_Corporum_Regularium)* (1568) by [Wenzel Jamnitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenzel_Jamnitzer) can be seen as a deltoidal hexecontahedron.

The deltoidal hexecontahedron can be constructed from either the [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968) or [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361) by adding vertices mid-edge, and mid-face, and creating new edges from each edge center to the face centers. [Conway polyhedron notation](wikipedia://en/5021705) would give these as oI, and oD, ortho-icosahedron, and ortho-dodecahedron. These geometric variations exist as a continuum along one degree of freedom.

## Related polyhedra and tilings {#Related_polyhedra_and_tilings}

*Image: Spherical deltoidal hexecontahedron.svg.* Spherical deltoidal hexecontahedron

When projected onto a sphere (see right), it can be seen that the edges make up [the edges of an icosahedron and dodecahedron arranged in their dual positions](wikipedia://en/8010901).

This tiling is topologically related as a part of sequence of deltoidal polyhedra with face figure (V3.4.*n*.4), and continues as tilings of the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/318742). These [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive) figures have (\**n*32) reflectional [symmetry](wikipedia://en/2915678).
