# Multiplicative independence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multiplicative independence"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), two positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) *a* and *b* are said to be **multiplicatively independent**[^o2] if their only common integer power is 1. That is, for integers *n* and *m*, $a^n=b^m$ implies $n=m=0$. Two integers which are not multiplicatively independent are said to be multiplicatively dependent.

:::

As examples, 36 and 216 are multiplicatively dependent since $36^3=(6^2)^3=(6^3)^2=216^2$, whereas 2 and 3 are multiplicatively independent.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Being multiplicatively independent admits some other characterizations. *a* and *b* are multiplicatively independent if and only if $\log(a)/\log(b)$ is irrational. This property holds independently of the base of the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860).

Let $a = p_1^{\alpha_1}p_2^{\alpha_2} \cdots p_k^{\alpha_k}$ and $b = q_1^{\beta_1}q_2^{\beta_2} \cdots q_l^{\beta_l}$ be the [canonical representations](wikipedia://en/11556#Canonical_representation_of_a_positive_integer) of *a* and *b*. The integers *a* and *b* are multiplicatively dependent if and only if *k* = *l*, $p_i=q_i$ and $\frac{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}=\frac{\alpha_j}{\beta_j}$ for all *i* and *j*.

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

[Büchi arithmetic](wikipedia://en/36262566) in base *a* and *b* define the same sets if and only if *a* and *b* are multiplicatively dependent.

Let *a* and *b* be multiplicatively dependent integers, that is, there exists *n,m>1* such that $a^n=b^m$. The integers *c* such that the length of its expansion in [base](wikipedia://en/701207) *a* is at most *m* are exactly the integers such that the length of their expansion in base *b* is at most *n*. It implies that computing the base *b* expansion of a number, given its base *a* expansion, can be done by transforming consecutive sequences of *m* base *a* digits into consecutive sequence of *n* base *b* digits.

:::
