# Lipót Klug

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lipót Klug"}

**Lipót**, or **Leopold** (in German), **Klug** (23 January 1854 – 24 March 1945) was a [Jewish-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish-Hungarian) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), professor in the [Franz Joseph University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_University) of [Kolozsvár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolozsvár).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Klug attended the *gymnasium* of his hometown and entered [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) in 1872 where he graduated as docent in 1874.[^n1] Between 1874 and 1893 he taught mathematics in the high school of Pozsony (now [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava) in [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia)). From 1893 to 1897 he was professor in a secondary school in Budapest and he obtained his habilitation in the university of Budapest. In 1897 he was appointed professor of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) in the [University of Kolozsvár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_University).[^n2] He retired in 1917 and moved back to Budapest.

He died in 1944 or 1945 in strange circumstances: in the middle of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) and aged 91 years, he walked out of his home in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest) and he never came back. Likely a victim of racial hate because he was of Jewish descent.[^n3]

His work was greatly influenced by [Gyula Kőnig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyula_Kőnig). His areas of research were [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891) and [synthetic geometry](wikipedia://en/267484).[^n4] During his retirement in Budapest he encouraged the young [Edward Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Teller) (the father of the [hydrogen bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_bomb)).
