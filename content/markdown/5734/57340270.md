# Tasha Inniss

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tasha Inniss"}

**Tasha Rose Inniss** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and the vice [provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provost_%28education%29) for research at [Spelman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelman_College).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Inniss was born in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans) and grew up without a father.[^o5] She became interested in mathematics in fourth grade, and decided she would study it as a freshman in high school.[^o6] She studied mathematics at [Xavier University of Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavier_University_of_Louisiana), graduating [summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude).[^o7] In 1992 she was listed in the *Who's Who Among Colleges and Universities* for her academic achievements.[^o5] She earned a master's degree in applied mathematics from Georgia Institute of Technology.[^o8]

She moved to the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park) for her PhD, funded by the [David and Lucile Packard Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_and_Lucile_Packard_Foundation).[^o5][^o7][^o9][^o10] In 2000, Inniss became the first African American woman to obtain a Ph.D. in mathematics from the University of Maryland, together with Sherry Scott and [Kimberly Weems](wikipedia://en/64324889).[^o11] Her dissertation was *Stochastic Models for the Estimation of Airport Arrival Capacity Distributions*. She was part of the [National Center of Excellence for Aviation Operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_of_Excellence_for_Aviation_Operators) and advised by Michael Owen Ball.[^o8] Her brother, Enos Inniss, also completed his PhD in 2000.[^o5]

## Research and career {#Research_and_career}

In 2001 she was appointed the [Clare Boothe Luce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Boothe_Luce) Professor of Mathematics at [Trinity Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Washington_University).[^o5] Her doctoral thesis described programming methods to calibrate models to estimate airport capacity.[^o14] She remains a consultant for the [Federal Aviation Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Aviation_Administration).[^o15]

She joined the department of mathematics at [Spelman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelman_College) in 2005 as an assistant professor.[^o16][^o17][^o18]

Throughout her career she has worked to recruit, support and mentor underrepresented minority students.[^o19][^o20][^o21][^o22] She led a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) project that looked to *increase the quality and quantity of underrepresented minorities matriculating and completing doctoral degrees.*[^o23] She has contributed to the [EDGE Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDGE_Foundation) (Enhancing Diversity in Graduate Education) program.[^o24]

In 2017 she joined the [Institute for Operations Research and the Management Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences) as Director of Education.[^o8]

Inniss' work earned her recognition by [Mathematically Gifted & Black](wikipedia://en/64552006) as a Black History Month 2017 Honoree.[^o25]

In 2022, Inniss was added to the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) Committee on Professional Ethics.[^o26]
