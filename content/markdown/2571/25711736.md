# Tate–Shafarevich group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tate–Shafarevich group"}

In [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), the **Tate–Shafarevich group** $\text{Ш}(A/K)$ of an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) $A$ (or more generally a [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523)) defined over a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) $K$ consists of the elements of the [Weil–Châtelet group](wikipedia://en/1970852) $\mathrm{WC}(A/K) = H^1(G_K, A)$, where $G_K = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{alg}/K)$ is the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of $K$, that become trivial in all of the completions of $K$ (i.e., the real and complex completions as well as the [$p$-adic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_field) obtained from $K$ by completing with respect to all its Archimedean and non Archimedean [valuations](wikipedia://en/347049) $v$). Thus, in terms of [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315), $\text{Ш}(A/K)$ can be defined as

:::

$$\bigcap_v\mathrm{ker}\left(H^1\left(G_K,A\right)\rightarrow H^1\left(G_{K_v},A_v\right)\right).$$

This group was introduced by [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991) and [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933)[^n1] and [Igor Shafarevich](wikipedia://en/1091815).[^n2] [Cassels](wikipedia://en/1709206) introduced the notation $\text{Ш}(A/K)$, where $\text{Ш}$ is the [Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic) letter "[Sha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sha_%28Cyrillic%29)",[^n3] for Shafarevich, replacing the older notation $\operatorname{TS}$ or $T\text{Š}$.[^n4]

## Elements of the Tate–Shafarevich group {#Elements_of_the_Tate–Shafarevich_group}

Geometrically, the non-trivial elements of the Tate–Shafarevich group can be thought of as the homogeneous spaces of $A$ that have $K_{v}$-[rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200) for every [place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_%28mathematics%29) $v$ of $K$, but no $K$-rational point. Thus, the group measures the extent to which the [Hasse principle](wikipedia://en/489707) fails to hold for rational equations with coefficients in the field $K$. Carl-Erik Lind gave an example of such a homogeneous space, by showing that the [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_of_an_algebraic_curve) 1 curve $x^{4} - 17 = 2y^{2}$ has solutions over the reals and over all $p$-adic fields, but has no rational points.[^n5] [Ernst S. Selmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_S._Selmer) gave many more examples, such as $3x^{3} + 4y^{3} + 5z^{3} = 0$.[^n6]

The special case of the Tate–Shafarevich group for the finite group scheme consisting of points of some given finite order $n$ of an abelian variety is closely related to the [Selmer group](wikipedia://en/1970862).

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Tate–Shafarevich conjecture"}

## Tate–Shafarevich conjecture {#Tate–Shafarevich_conjecture}

The Tate–Shafarevich conjecture states that the Tate–Shafarevich group is finite. [Karl Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Rubin) proved this for some [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) of [rank](wikipedia://en/51244926) at most 1 with [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183).[^n7] [Victor A. Kolyvagin](wikipedia://en/4559839) extended this to modular elliptic curves over the rationals of analytic rank at most 1.[^n8] (The [modularity theorem](wikipedia://en/174475) later showed that the modularity assumption always holds.)

It is known that the Tate–Shafarevich group is a [torsion group](wikipedia://en/3568695),[^o13][^o14] thus the conjecture is equivalent to stating that the group is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/58527).

:::

## Cassels–Tate pairing {#Cassels–Tate_pairing}

The Cassels–Tate pairing is a [bilinear pairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilinear_pairing) $\text{Ш}(A) \times \text{Ш}(\text{Â}) \to \mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}$, where $A$ is an abelian variety and $\text{Â}$ is its dual. Cassels introduced this for [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225), when $A$ can be identified with $\text{Â}$ and the pairing is an alternating form.[^n4] The kernel of this form is the subgroup of divisible elements, which is trivial if the Tate–Shafarevich conjecture is true. Tate extended the pairing to general abelian varieties, as a variation of [Tate duality](wikipedia://en/33900144).[^n9] A choice of polarization on *A* gives a map from $A$ to $\text{Â}$, which induces a bilinear pairing on $\text{Ш}(A)$ with values in $\mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z}$, but unlike the case of elliptic curves this need not be alternating or even skew symmetric.

For an elliptic curve, Cassels showed that the pairing is alternating, and a consequence is that if the order of $\text{Ш}$ is finite then it is a square. For more general abelian varieties it was sometimes incorrectly believed for many years that the order of $\text{Ш}$ is a square whenever it is finite; this mistake originated in a paper by [Swinnerton-Dyer](wikipedia://en/2105540),[^n10] who misquoted one of the results of Tate.[^n9] Poonen and Stoll gave some examples where the order is twice a square, such as the Jacobian of a certain genus 2 curve over the rationals whose Tate–Shafarevich group has order 2,[^n11] and [Stein](wikipedia://en/7920662) gave some examples where the power of an odd prime dividing the order is odd.[^n12] If the abelian variety has a principal polarization then the form on $\text{Ш}$ is skew symmetric which implies that the order of $\text{Ш}$ is a square or twice a square (if it is finite), and if in addition the principal polarization comes from a rational divisor (as is the case for elliptic curves) then the form is alternating and the order of $\text{Ш}$ is a square (if it is finite). On the other hand building on the results just presented Konstantinou showed that for any [squarefree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squarefree) number $n$ there is an abelian variety $A$ defined over $\mathbf{Q}$ and an integer $m$ with $\lvert \text{Ш}\rvert = nm^{2}$.[^n13] In particular $\text{Ш}$ is finite in Konstantinou's examples and these examples confirm a conjecture of Stein. Thus modulo squares any integer can be the order of $\text{Ш}$.
