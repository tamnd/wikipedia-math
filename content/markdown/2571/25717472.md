# Lesley Sibner

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Lesley Sibner"}

**Lesley Millman Sibner** (August 13, 1934 – September 11, 2013)[^o4] was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Polytechnic Institute of New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_Institute_of_New_York_University). She earned her Bachelors at [City College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York) [CUNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUNY) in Mathematics. She completed her doctorate at [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) [NYU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYU) in 1964 under the joint supervision of [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972) and [Cathleen Morawetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathleen_Morawetz). Her thesis concerned [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) of mixed-type.[^o5]

:::

## Research career {#Research_career}

In 1964, Lesley Sibner became an instructor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) for two years. She was a [Fulbright Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Scholar) at the Institut Henri Poincaré in Paris the following year. At this time, in addition to solo work on the [Tricomi equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricomi_equation) and [compressible flows](wikipedia://en/219144), she began working with her husband [Robert Sibner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Sibner) on a problem suggested by [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972): do there exists compressible flows on a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181)? As part of her work in this direction, she studied [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590) eventually proving a nonlinear [Hodge–DeRham theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge–DeRham_theorem) with Robert Sibner based on a physical interpretation of one-dimensional [harmonic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_form) on closed manifolds. The techniques are related to her prior work on compressible flows. They kept working together on related problems and applications of this important work for many years.[^o5]

In 1967 she joined the faculty at Polytechnic University in [Brooklyn, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn,_New_York).[^o5] In 1969 she proved the [Morse index theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_index_theorem) for degenerate [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137) by extending classical [Sturm–Liouville theory](wikipedia://en/490990).[^o5]

In 1971-1972 she spent a year at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) where she met [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457). She realized she could use her knowledge of analysis to solve geometric problems related to the [Atiyah–Bott fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/3636103). In 1974, Lesley and Robert Sibner produced a constructive proof of the [Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/247261).[^o5]

[Karen Uhlenbeck](wikipedia://en/4384796) suggested that Lesley Sibner work on [Yang-Mills equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang-Mills_equation). In 1979-1980 she visited Harvard University where she learned [gauge field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_field_theory) from [Clifford Taubes](wikipedia://en/6893920). This lead results about [point singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_singularity) in the Yang-Mills equation and the [Yang–Mills–Higgs equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang–Mills–Higgs_equation). Her interest in singularities soon brought her deeper into geometry, leading to a classification of singular connections and to a condition for removing two-dimensional singularities in work with Robert Sibner.[^o5]

Realizing that [instantons](wikipedia://en/296077) could under certain circumstances be viewed as [monopoles](wikipedia://en/2415128), the Sibners and Uhlenbeck constructed non-minimal unstable critical points of the Yang-Mills functional over the [four-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) in 1989. She was invited to present this work at the [Geometry Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_Festival). She was a Bunting Scholar at the [Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Institute_for_Advanced_Study) in 1991. For the subsequent decades, Lesley Sibner focussed on [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155) and [gravitational instantons](wikipedia://en/2618270). Although the research sounds very physical, in fact throughout her career, Lesley Sibner applied physical intuition to prove important geometric and topological theorems.

In 2012 she became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]
