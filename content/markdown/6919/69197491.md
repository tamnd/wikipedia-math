# James Devereux Hustler

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Devereux Hustler"}

**James Devereux Hustler** (1784–1849) was an English cleric and academic. He was a mathematician, elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1819.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was the son of Thomas and Ann Hustler of [Bury St Edmunds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury_St_Edmunds); his father (died 1800) was a grocer in Abbeygate Street.[^o2][^o4] William Hustler (died 1832), registrar of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), and Robert Samuel Hustler (died 1835) of the [Royal Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Engineers), who served at the [Battle of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo) as a captain, were his brothers.[^o5][^o6] He was educated at the [King Edward VI Free School, Bury St Edmunds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VI_Free_School,_Bury_St_Edmunds), under the Rev. Michael Becher.[^o2][^o7] [Charles Blomfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_James_Blomfield), who shared his background and education, and became [Bishop of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_London), was a good friend.[^o8]

Hustler matriculated at [Christ's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_College,_Cambridge) in 1801, moving to [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in 1802. He was third [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in 1806, after [Frederick Pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Frederick_Pollock,_1st_Baronet) and [Henry Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Walter_%28antiquary%29), graduating B.A. He became a Fellow of Trinity in 1807.[^o2][^o9] That year, a five-hour interview between [Thomas Smart Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Smart_Hughes), then an undergraduate, and an increasingly drunken [Richard Porson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Porson), a close friend of Hustler's, took place in Hustler's college rooms.[^o10]

In 1809 Hustler graduated M.A., and he graduated B.D. in 1816.[^o2] [Henry Gage, 4th Viscount Gage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Gage,_4th_Viscount_Gage) entered Trinity College in 1811, and graduated M.A. in 1812; he went on to write some mathematical works. Hustler was appointed his domestic chaplain in 1813. In 1813, also, Hustler became tutor to [Spencer Perceval the younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Perceval_%28junior%29).[^o13]

## Trinity tutor {#Trinity_tutor}

Hustler was an assistant tutor at Trinity 1811–1813, and then tutor 1813–1823.[^o15] His period as a tutor overlapped with the years 1815–1825 that have been dubbed the "analytical revolution" at Cambridge. In the teaching and examining of the [infinitesimal calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_calculus), the notations and ideas of [Gottfried Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Leibniz) were introduced, alongside those traditionally taught of [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627).[^o16]

As a tutor, Hustler diverted [George Airy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Airy) away from applying to [Peterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse), which had been recommended by [Thomas Clarkson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Clarkson), a friend of Hustler's, and taught him at Trinity.[^o17] Airy had been taught by a master at his school in [Colchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colchester), Thomas Rogers (1789–1860), a graduate from [Sidney Sussex College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Sussex_College) in 1811, covering the university topics to [fluxions](wikipedia://en/52956308) and the *Principia*. Hustler and his tutorial assistant [George Peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Peacock_%28mathematician%29) were in 1819 passed work by Airy.[^o18] In 1820, Hustler arranged an [exhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_%28scholarship%29) (minor scholarship) for Airy, via the Norwich Member of Parliament [William Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Smith_%28abolitionist%29).[^o20]

Peacock then gave Airy special teaching in the contemporary calculus methods, and Airy responded by working over results of [Samuel Vince](wikipedia://en/3386222) and [Robert Woodhouse](wikipedia://en/564987).[^o18] When Airy began experimenting with the [Argand diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argand_diagram) in 1820, Peacock encouraged him in linking it to [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261), but Hustler disapproved.[^o20]

In 1822, Hustler intervened to place Airy and some of his peers, ahead of the usual schedule, in the "questionist" group of those being prepared for the final stage of the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos). There was resistance from the more senior students. The accelerated group was then taught separately, by Peacock.[^o18]

## Later life {#Later_life}

Hustler's clerical career began in 1823, when he was a curate at [Fornham All Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fornham_All_Saints).[^o2] In 1828 the [Duke of Grafton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Henry_FitzRoy,_4th_Duke_of_Grafton) presented him to the rectory of [Great Fakenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fakenham), and in 1829 to the living of [Euston, Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euston,_Suffolk).[^o5][^o22]

As a benefactor, Hustler gave a [paten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paten) to Fornham All Saints, and an organ to St John's Church, Bury St Edmunds, built 1840–1.[^o22][^o23]

## Works {#Works}

- *The Elements of Conic Sections: with the sections of conoids* (1st edition 1818, 2nd edition 1820, 3rd edition 1826), anonymous.[^o25][^o26] A revised edition of 1845 was published under Hustler's name.[^o27]
- *A Sermon preached \[...\] the first Sunday after the funeral of \[...\] George Henry Duke of Grafton* (1844)[^o28]
- *Unity in the Church: A Sermon Preached at Ixworth on Monday, May 21st, 1849* (1849)[^o29]

## Family {#Family}

Hustler married Elizabeth Mansel, daughter of [William Lort Mansel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lort_Mansel), in 1823.[^o31] Of their children:

- James Devereux Hustler, the eldest son, married in 1852 Isabella Carruthers, eldest daughter of George Edwardes Carruthers, a surgeon, and Anne Smith, daughter of [Sir David William Smith, 1st Baronet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_David_William_Smith,_1st_Baronet).[^o32][^o33]
- Spencer William Hustler, married in 1854 Anne Leach, eldest daughter of the Rev. Francis George Leach of Stackpole.[^o34]
- Elizabeth Juliana, married in 1855 James Robertson Bryant of Pembroke as his second wife.[^o35]
- Augusta Frederica, the youngest daughter, married in 1856 the Rev. Owen Jones.[^o36] In 1888 their daughter Elizabeth Frederica Isabella Owen married the Rev. William Howard Coates, son of William Henry Coates and his wife [Elizabeth Youatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Youatt).[^o37]

Portraits of their children were painted in 1833 by [Samuel Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Lane_%28painter%29).[^o38]
