# Natashia Boland

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Natashia Boland"}

**Natashia Lesley Boland** (born 1967) is a professor of mathematics at [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology). Boland completed a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) at the [University of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Australia) in 1992, and afterwards she pursued postdoctoral research at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) in Canada, at the [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology) in the USA. She spent 13 years at the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne) and then from 2008 to 2014 worked at the [University of Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Newcastle_%28Australia%29). She has made contributions to transportation scheduling, modeling of infrastructure networks, planning pricing strategies for demand, and optimization for environmental modeling.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Boland was influenced as a child by construction toys, and demonstrated an early aptitude for mathematics by reading through the entire year's curriculum during a two-week break for illness. She also cites the inspiration of two teachers, Mrs. Martini in second grade and Janet Hunt at [Churchlands High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchlands_Senior_High_School). She also attended a math camp at the National Mathematics Summer School in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra).[^o4]

Boland pursued degrees in both mathematics and computer science at the [University of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Australia). She at first hated computer science, but later began to love it as she realized how intertwined mathematics and computers were.[^o5] For her honours degree, Boland studied robotics. Boland completed her PhD in 1992 under the supervision of Alistair Iain Mees, and took two postdoctoral fellowships at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) in Canada and at the [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology).

## Awards {#Awards}

In 2013, Boland delivered the [Hanna Neumann](wikipedia://en/7043308) Lecture[^o8] to honour the achievements of women in mathematics. In 2013, Boland was also awarded a Biennial Medal of the Modelling and Simulation Society of Australia and New Zealand.[^o9]
