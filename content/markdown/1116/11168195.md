# Existentially closed model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Existentially closed model"}

In [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), a branch of [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), the notion of an **existentially closed model** (or **existentially complete model**) of a [theory](wikipedia://en/3512103) generalizes the notions of [algebraically closed fields](wikipedia://en/1018) (for the theory of [fields](wikipedia://en/10603)), [real closed fields](wikipedia://en/1066093) (for the theory of [ordered fields](wikipedia://en/22430)), [existentially closed groups](wikipedia://en/472147) (for the theory of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447)), and [dense](wikipedia://en/6320997) [linear orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_order) without endpoints (for the theory of linear orders).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A substructure *M* of a [structure](wikipedia://en/4055928) *N* is said to be **existentially closed in** (or **existentially complete in**) $N$ if for every [quantifier](wikipedia://en/43507260)-free [formula](wikipedia://en/404582) φ(*x*<sub>1</sub>,…,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>,*y*<sub>1</sub>,…,*y*<sub>*n*</sub>) and all elements *b*<sub>1</sub>,…,*b*<sub>*n*</sub> of *M* such that φ(*x*<sub>1</sub>,…,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>,*b*<sub>1</sub>,…,*b*<sub>*n*</sub>) is realized in *N*, then φ(*x*<sub>1</sub>,…,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>,*b*<sub>1</sub>,…,*b*<sub>*n*</sub>) is also realized in *M*. In other words: If there is a tuple *a*<sub>1</sub>,…,*a*<sub>*n*</sub> in *N* such that φ(*a*<sub>1</sub>,…,*a*<sub>*n*</sub>,*b*<sub>1</sub>,…,*b*<sub>*n*</sub>) holds in *N*, then such a tuple also exists in *M*. This notion is often denoted $M \prec_1 N$.

A model *M* of a theory *T* is called existentially closed in *T* if it is existentially closed in every superstructure *N* that is itself a model of *T*. More generally, a structure *M* is called existentially closed in a [class](wikipedia://en/20696) *K* of structures (in which it is contained as a member) if *M* is existentially closed in every superstructure *N* that is itself a member of *K*.

The **existential closure** in *K* of a member *M* of *K*, when it exists, is, up to [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828), the least existentially closed superstructure of *M*. More precisely, it is any extensionally closed superstructure *M*<sup>∗</sup> of *M* such that for every existentially closed superstructure *N* of *M*, *M*<sup>∗</sup> is isomorphic to a substructure of *N* via an isomorphism that is the identity on *M*.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Let *σ* = (+,×,0,1) be the [signature](wikipedia://en/7788156) of fields, i.e. + and × are [binary function](wikipedia://en/3943) symbols and 0 and 1 are constant symbols. Let *K* be the class of structures of signature *σ* that are fields. If *A* is a [subfield](wikipedia://en/10603#Subfields_and_prime_fields) of *B*, then *A* is existentially closed in *B* if and only if every system of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) over *A* that has a solution in *B* also has a solution in *A*. It follows that the existentially closed members of *K* are exactly the algebraically closed fields.

Similarly in the class of [ordered fields](wikipedia://en/22430), the existentially closed structures are the [real closed fields](wikipedia://en/1066093). In the class of [linear orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_order), the existentially closed structures are those that are [dense](wikipedia://en/6320997) without endpoints, while the existential closure of any [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) (including [empty](wikipedia://en/9566)) linear order is, up to isomorphism, the countable dense total order without endpoints, namely the [order type](wikipedia://en/4499021) of the [rationals](wikipedia://en/19727024).
