::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rectified 5-simplexes"}

In five-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **rectified 5-simplex** is a convex [uniform 5-polytope](wikipedia://en/14415338), being a [rectification](wikipedia://en/2192366) of the regular [5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex).

:::

There are three unique degrees of rectifications, including the zeroth, the 5-simplex itself. Vertices of the *rectified 5-simplex* are located at the edge-centers of the *5-simplex*. Vertices of the *birectified 5-simplex* are located in the triangular face centers of the *5-simplex*.

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Rectified 5-simplex"}

## Rectified 5-simplex {#Rectified_5-simplex}

Uniform polyteron stat table

In [five-dimensional](wikipedia://en/2851410) [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **rectified 5-simplex** is a [uniform 5-polytope](wikipedia://en/14415338) with 15 [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), 60 [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), 80 [triangular](wikipedia://en/30654) [faces](wikipedia://en/149926), 45 [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) (30 [tetrahedral](wikipedia://en/30606), and 15 [octahedral](wikipedia://en/80177635)), and 12 [4-faces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-face) (6 [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821) and 6 [rectified 5-cells](wikipedia://en/2720430)). It is also called **0<sub>3,1</sub>** for its branching Coxeter-Dynkin diagram, shown as .

[E. L. Elte](wikipedia://en/27806942) identified it in 1912 as a semiregular polytope, labeling it as S1.

### Alternate names {#Alternate_names}

- Rectified hexateron (Acronym: rix) (Jonathan Bowers)

### Coordinates {#Coordinates}

The vertices of the rectified 5-simplex can be more simply positioned on a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) in 6-space as permutations of (0,0,0,0,1,1) *or* (0,0,1,1,1,1). These construction can be seen as facets of the [rectified 6-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectified_6-orthoplex) or [birectified 6-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birectified_6-cube) respectively.

### Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

The rectified 5-simplex, 0<sub>31</sub>, is second in a dimensional series of uniform polytopes, expressed by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) as 1<sub>3k</sub> series. The fifth figure is a Euclidean honeycomb, [3<sub>31</sub>](wikipedia://en/19593724), and the final is a noncompact hyperbolic honeycomb, 4<sub>31</sub>. Each progressive [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392) is constructed from the previous as its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719).

## Birectified 5-simplex {#Birectified_5-simplex}

Uniform polyteron stat table The **birectified 5-simplex** is [isotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet-transitive), with all 12 of its facets as [rectified 5-cells](wikipedia://en/2720430). It has 20 [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), 90 [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), 120 [triangular](wikipedia://en/30654) [faces](wikipedia://en/149926), 60 [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) (30 [tetrahedral](wikipedia://en/30606), and 30 [octahedral](wikipedia://en/80177635)).

[E. L. Elte](wikipedia://en/27806942) identified it in 1912 as a semiregular polytope, labeling it as S2.

It is also called **0<sub>2,2</sub>** for its branching Coxeter-Dynkin diagram, shown as . It is seen in the [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of the 6-dimensional [1<sub>22</sub>](wikipedia://en/19070548), .

### Construction {#Construction}

The elements of the regular polytopes can be expressed in a [configuration matrix](wikipedia://en/56419724). Rows and columns reference vertices, edges, faces, and cells, with diagonal element their counts ([f-vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-vector)). The nondiagonal elements represent the number of row elements are incident to the column element. The diagonal f-vector numbers are derived through the [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777), dividing the full group order of a subgroup order by removing one mirror at a time.

### Intersection of two 5-simplices {#Intersection_of_two_5-simplices}

The *birectified 5-simplex* is the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of two regular [5-simplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex) in [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope) configuration. The vertices of a [birectification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birectification) exist at the center of the faces of the original polytope(s). This intersection is analogous to the 3D [stellated octahedron](wikipedia://en/2601866), seen as a compound of two regular [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) and intersected in a central [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), while that is a first [rectification](wikipedia://en/2192366) where vertices are at the center of the original edges.

::: {#o24 .table cue=table}

| *Image: Dual\_5-simplex\_intersection\_graphs.png.* |
|---|
| Dual 5-simplexes (red and blue), and their birectified 5-simplex intersection in green, viewed in A5 and A4 Coxeter planes. The simplexes overlap in the A5 projection and are drawn in magenta. |

:::

It is also the intersection of a [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462) with the hyperplane that bisects the 6-cube's long diagonal orthogonally. In this sense it is the 5-dimensional analog of the regular hexagon, [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), and [bitruncated 5-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitruncated_5-cell). This characterization yields simple coordinates for the vertices of a birectified 5-simplex in 6-space: the 20 distinct permutations of (1,1,1,−1,−1,−1).

The vertices of the *birectified 5-simplex* can also be positioned on a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) in 6-space as permutations of (0,0,0,1,1,1). This construction can be seen as facets of the [birectified 6-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birectified_6-orthoplex).

### Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

#### *k*<sub>22</sub> polytopes {#k22_polytopes}

The *birectified 5-simplex*, 0<sub>22</sub>, is second in a dimensional series of uniform polytopes, expressed by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) as k<sub>22</sub> series. The *birectified 5-simplex* is the vertex figure for the third, the [1<sub>22</sub>](wikipedia://en/19070548). The fourth figure is a Euclidean honeycomb, [2<sub>22</sub>](wikipedia://en/19582093), and the final is a noncompact hyperbolic honeycomb, 3<sub>22</sub>. Each progressive [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392) is constructed from the previous as its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719).
