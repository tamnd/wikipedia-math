# Varifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Varifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **varifold** is, loosely speaking, a [measure-theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) generalization of the concept of a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219), by replacing differentiability requirements with those provided by [rectifiable sets](wikipedia://en/2644238), while maintaining the general algebraic structure usually seen in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). Varifolds generalize the idea of a [rectifiable current](wikipedia://en/3021207), and are studied in [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694).

:::

## Historical note {#Historical_note}

Varifolds were first introduced by [Laurence Chisholm Young](wikipedia://en/9620408) in (Young 1951), under the name "*generalized surfaces*".[^o4][^o5] [Frederick J. Almgren Jr.](wikipedia://en/12998276) slightly modified the definition in his mimeographed notes (Almgren 1965) and coined the name *varifold*: he wanted to emphasize that these objects are substitutes for ordinary manifolds in problems of the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882).[^o7] The modern approach to the theory was based on Almgren's notes[^o8] and laid down by [William K. Allard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_K._Allard), in the paper (Allard 1972).

::: {#o11 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given an open subset $\Omega$ of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$, an *m*-dimensional varifold on $\Omega$ is defined as a [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933) on the set

$$\Omega \times G(n,m)$$

where $G(n,m)$ is the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) of all *m*-dimensional linear subspaces of an *n*-dimensional vector space. The Grassmannian is used to allow the construction of analogs to [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) as duals to vector fields in the [approximate tangent space](wikipedia://en/41452559) of the set $\Omega$.

The particular case of a rectifiable varifold is the data of a *m*-rectifiable set *M* (which is measurable with respect to the *m*-dimensional Hausdorff measure), and a density function defined on *M*, which is a positive function θ measurable and locally integrable with respect to the *m*-dimensional Hausdorff measure. It defines a Radon measure *V* on the Grassmannian bundle of $\mathbb{R}^n$

$$V(A) := \int_{\Gamma_{M,A}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\theta(x) \mathrm{d} \mathcal{H}^m(x)$$

where

- $$\Gamma_{M,A}=M \cap \{x : (x, \mathrm{Tan}^m(x,M)) \in A \}$$
- $\mathcal{H}^m(x)$ is the [$m$−dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28mathematics%29) [Hausdorff measure](wikipedia://en/504109)

Rectifiable varifolds are weaker objects than locally rectifiable currents: they do not have any [orientation](wikipedia://en/187446). Replacing *M* with more regular sets, one easily see that [differentiable submanifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219) are particular cases of [rectifiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/2644238).

Due to the [lack of orientation](wikipedia://en/187446), there is no [boundary operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_operator) defined on the space of varifolds.
