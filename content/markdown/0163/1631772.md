# Topological graph theory

*Image: Pappus-graph-animation.webm.* Animation detailing the embedding of the [Pappus graph](wikipedia://en/7250518) and associated map in the torus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Topological graph theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **topological graph theory** is a branch of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). It studies the [embedding of graphs](wikipedia://en/8149170) in [surfaces](wikipedia://en/27865), [spatial embeddings of graphs](wikipedia://en/772031), and [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806) as [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450).[^o2] It also studies [immersions](wikipedia://en/6993953) of graphs.

:::

Embedding a graph in a surface means that we want to draw the graph on a surface, a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) for example, without two [edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29) intersecting. A basic embedding problem often presented as a [mathematical puzzle](wikipedia://en/366445) is the [three utilities problem](wikipedia://en/58862). Other applications can be found in printing [electronic circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_circuit) where the aim is to print (embed) a circuit (the graph) on a [circuit board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_board) (the surface) without two connections crossing each other and resulting in a [short circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_circuit).

## Graphs as topological spaces {#Graphs_as_topological_spaces}

To an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) we may associate an [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) *C* with a single-element set per vertex and a two-element set per edge. The geometric realization |*C*| of the complex consists of a copy of the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) \[0,1\] per edge, with the endpoints of these [intervals](wikipedia://en/49172) glued together at vertices. In this view, embeddings of graphs into a surface or as [subdivisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivision_%28graph_theory%29) of other graphs are both instances of topological embedding, [homeomorphism of graphs](wikipedia://en/353021) is just the specialization of topological [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660), the notion of a [connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph) coincides with [topological connectedness](wikipedia://en/6233), and a connected graph is a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) is trivial.

Other simplicial complexes associated with graphs include the [Whitney complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_complex) or *clique complex*, with a set per [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) of the graph, and the *matching complex*, with a set per [matching](wikipedia://en/581797) of the graph (equivalently, the clique complex of the complement of the [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231)). The matching complex of a [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) is called a *[chessboard complex](wikipedia://en/73384654)*, as it can be also described as the complex of sets of nonattacking rooks on a chessboard.[^o4]

## Example studies {#Example_studies}

[John Hopcroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hopcroft) and [Robert Tarjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Tarjan)[^o6] derived a means of [testing the planarity](wikipedia://en/15972636) of a graph in time linear to the number of edges. Their algorithm does this by constructing a graph embedding which they term a "palm tree". Efficient planarity testing is fundamental to [graph drawing](wikipedia://en/325813).

[Fan Chung](wikipedia://en/246562) et al[^o7] studied the problem of [embedding a graph into a book](wikipedia://en/3987703) with the graph's vertices in a line along the spine of the book. Its edges are drawn on separate pages in such a way that edges residing on the same page do not cross. This problem abstracts layout problems arising in the routing of multilayer printed circuit boards.

[Graph embeddings](wikipedia://en/8149170) are also used to [prove](wikipedia://en/82285) structural results about graphs, via [graph minor theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_%28graph_theory%29) and the [graph structure theorem](wikipedia://en/21318521).
