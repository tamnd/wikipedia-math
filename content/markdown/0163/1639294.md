# Subquotient

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Subquotient"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) fields of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) and [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **subquotient** is a [quotient object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_object) of a [subobject](wikipedia://en/540476). Subquotients are particularly important in [abelian categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_categories), and in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), where they are also known as **sections**, though this conflicts with [a different meaning](wikipedia://en/10630303) in category theory.

:::

So in the [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) of groups, $H$ is a subquotient of $G$ if there exists a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) $G'$ of $G$ and a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) $G''$ of $G'$ so that $H$ is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to $G'/G''$.

In the literature about [sporadic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_groups) wordings like "$H$ is involved in $G$"[^o2] can be found with the apparent meaning of "$H$ is a subquotient of $G$".

As in the context of subgroups, in the context of subquotients the term *trivial* may be used for the two subquotients $G$ and $\{1\}$ which are present in every group $G$.

A quotient of a subrepresentation of a representation (of, say, a group) might be called a subquotient representation; e. g., [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190)'s subquotient theorem.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

There are subquotients of groups which are neither a subgroup nor a quotient of it. For example, according to the article [Sporadic group](wikipedia://en/324806#Third_generation_%288_groups%29:_other_subgroups_of_the_Monster), *Fi*<sub>22</sub> has a double cover which is a subgroup of *Fi*<sub>23</sub>, so it is a subquotient of *Fi*<sub>23</sub> without being a subgroup or quotient of it.

:::

## Order relation {#Order_relation}

The relation *subquotient of* is an [order relation](wikipedia://en/23572#Partial_orders), which shall be denoted by $\preceq$. It shall be proved for groups.

**Notation**

Let $G$ be a group, let $G'$ be a subgroup of $G$, let $G''$ be a normal subgroup of $G'$, and let $H$ be the quotient group $G' / G''$. Then we say that $H$ is a subquotient of $G$. In symbols, let $G'' \text{◃} G' \le G$ and $H = G' / G''$; then $H \preceq G$. This relationship has the following properties:

1. [Reflexivity](wikipedia://en/200458): $G\preceq G$, i. e. every element is related to itself. Indeed, $G$ is isomorphic to the subquotient $G/\{1\}$ of $G$.
1. [Antisymmetry](wikipedia://en/1176): if $G\preceq H$ and $H\preceq G$ then $G\cong H$; that is, no two distinct elements precede each other. Indeed, a comparison of the group orders of $G$ and $H$ then yields $|G| = |H|$ from which $G\cong H$.
1. [Transitivity](wikipedia://en/200463): if $H'/H'' \preceq H$ and $H\preceq G$ then $H'/H'' \preceq G$.

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of transitivity for groups"}

### Proof of transitivity for groups {#Proof_of_transitivity_for_groups}

Let $H'/H''$ be a subquotient of $H$, let $H := G'/G''$ be a subquotient of $G$, and let $\varphi \colon G' \to H$ be the [canonical homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_homomorphism). Then in the following diagram, all vertical ($\downarrow$) maps $\varphi \colon X \to Y, \; x \mapsto x \, G''$

|  |  | $G''$ | $\leq$ | $\varphi^{-1}(H'')$ | $\leq$ | $\varphi^{-1}(H')$ | $\vartriangleleft$ | $G'$ |  |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  | $\varphi\!:$ | $\Big\downarrow$ |  | $\Big\downarrow$ |  | $\Big\downarrow$ |  | $\Big\downarrow$ |  |
|  |  | $\{1\}$ | $\leq$ | $H''$ | $\vartriangleleft$ | $H'$ | $\vartriangleleft$ | $H$ |  |

are [surjective](wikipedia://en/27873) for the respective pairs

|  | $(X,Y) \; \; \; \in$ | $\Bigl\{\bigl(G'', \{1\}\bigr) \Bigr.$ | $,$ | $\bigl(\varphi^{-1}(H''),H''\bigr)$ | $,$ | $\bigl(\varphi^{-1}(H'),H'\bigr)$ | $,$ | $\Bigl.\bigl(G',H\bigr)\Bigr\} .$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The [preimages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimage) $\varphi^{-1}\left(H'\right)$ and $\varphi^{-1}\left(H''\right)$ are both subgroups of $G'$ containing $G'' ,$ and it is $\varphi\left(\varphi^{-1}\left(H'\right)\right) = H'$ and $\varphi\left(\varphi^{-1}\left(H''\right)\right) = H'',$ because every $h \in H$ has a preimage $g \in G'$ with $\varphi(g) = h .$ Moreover, the subgroup $\varphi^{-1}\left(H''\right)$ is normal in $\varphi^{-1}\left(H'\right) .$

As a consequence, the subquotient $H'/H''$ of $H$ is a subquotient of $G$ of the form $H'/H'' \cong \varphi^{-1}\left(H'\right)/\varphi^{-1}\left(H''\right) .$

:::

### Relation to cardinal order {#Relation_to_cardinal_order}

In [constructive set theory](wikipedia://en/5042360), where the [law of excluded middle](wikipedia://en/17637) does not necessarily hold, one can consider the relation *subquotient of* as replacing the usual [order relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_relation)(s) on [cardinals](wikipedia://en/6173). When one has the law of the excluded middle, then a subquotient $Y$ of $X$ is either the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) or there is an onto function $X\to Y$. This order relation is traditionally denoted $\leq^\ast .$ If additionally the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) holds, then $Y$ has a one-to-one function to $X$ and this order relation is the usual $\leq$ on corresponding cardinals.
