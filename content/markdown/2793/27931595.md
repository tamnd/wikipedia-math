# Kramers' opacity law

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kramers' opacity law"}

**Kramers' opacity law** describes the [opacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opacity_%28optics%29) of a medium in terms of the ambient [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) and [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050), assuming that the opacity is dominated by [bound-free absorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionization) (the absorption of light during ionization of a bound [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron)) or [free-free absorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-free_absorption) (the absorption of light when scattering a free ion, inverse of *bremsstrahlung*).[^o2] It is often used to model [radiative transfer](wikipedia://en/2925371), particularly in [stellar atmospheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_atmosphere).[^o3] The relation is named after the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) [Hendrik Kramers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Kramers), who first derived the form in 1923.[^o4][^o5]

:::

The general functional form of the opacity law is $\bar{\kappa}\propto\rho$ $T^{-7/2},$ where

$\bar{\kappa}$ is the resulting average opacity ((kg/m<sup>3</sup>)<sup>−1</sup>/m),

$\rho$ is the density and

$T$ the temperature of the medium.

Often the overall opacity is inferred from observations, and this form of the relation describes how changes in the density or temperature (highly non-linear) will affect the opacity.

## Calculation {#Calculation}

The specific forms for bound-free and free-free absorption are:

- Bound-free $$\bar{\kappa}_\text{bf}=4.34\times10^{25}\frac{g_\text{bf}}{t}\cdot Z(1+X)\cdot\frac{\rho}{\rm g/cm^3}\left(\frac{T}{\rm K}\right)^{-7/2}{\rm\,cm^2\,g^{-1}},$$
- [Free-free](wikipedia://en/54147) $$\bar{\kappa}_\text{ff} = 3.68 \times 10^{22} g_\text{ff} \cdot(1-Z)(1+X)\cdot \frac{\rho}{\mathrm{g/cm^3 }} \left(\frac{T}{\rm K}\right)^{-7/2} \mathrm{cm^2 \, g^{-1}}.$$

By classical [electron-scattering (Thomson)](wikipedia://en/624714) opacity depends on H-ion concentration alone:

$$\bar{\kappa}_\mathrm{es}=0.2(1+X)\mathrm{\,cm^2\,g^{-1}}=0.02(1+X)\mathrm{\,m^2\, kg^{-1}}$$

[Compton scattering](wikipedia://en/55236) of electrons occurs at higher photon energy.

Here, $g_\text{bf}$ and $g_\text{ff}$ are the [Gaunt factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaunt_factor) of circa 1 (quantum-mechanical correction terms) associated with bound-free and free-free transitions respectively. The $t$ is an additional correction factor, typically having a value between 1 and 100. The opacity depends on the number density of electrons and ions in the medium, described by the fractional abundance (by mass):

- of elements heavier than [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium), $Z,$
- and of [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen), $X.$[^o5]

With only helium present (and classical behaviour) $\bar{\kappa}_\text{ff}$ is proportional to mass density and $T^{-3.5},$ valid also for $\bar{\kappa}_\text{bf}$ in lithium etc. medium.
