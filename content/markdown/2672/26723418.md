# Local Euler characteristic formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local Euler characteristic formula"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315), the **local Euler characteristic formula** is a result due to [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) that computes the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of the [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) of the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) *G<sub>K</sub>* of a [non-archimedean local field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-archimedean_local_field) *K*.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *K* be a non-archimedean local field, let *K<sup>s</sup>* denote a [separable closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_closure) of *K*, let *G<sub>K</sub>* = Gal(*K<sup>s</sup>*/*K*) be the absolute Galois group of *K*, and let *H<sup>i</sup>*(*K*, *M*) denote the group cohomology of *G<sub>K</sub>* with coefficients in *M*. Since the [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) of *G<sub>K</sub>* is two,[^o4] *H<sup>i</sup>*(*K*, *M*) = 0 for *i* ≥ 3. Therefore, the Euler characteristic only involves the groups with *i* = 0, 1, 2.

### Case of finite modules {#Case_of_finite_modules}

Let *M* be a [*G<sub>K</sub>*-module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module) of finite [order](wikipedia://en/493527) *m*. The Euler characteristic of *M* is defined to be[^o6]

$$\chi(G_K,M)=\frac{\# H^0(K,M)\cdot\# H^2(K,M)}{\# H^1(K,M)}$$

(the *i*th cohomology groups for *i* ≥ 3 appear tacitly as their sizes are all one).

Let *R* denote the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/145375) of *K*. Tate's result then states that if *m* is [relatively prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_prime) to the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of *K*, then[^o7]

$$\chi(G_K,M)=\left(\#R/mR\right)^{-1},$$

i.e. the inverse of the order of the [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) *R*/*mR*.

Two special cases worth singling out are the following. If the order of *M* is relatively prime to the characteristic of the [residue field](wikipedia://en/145375) of *K*, then the Euler characteristic is one. If *K* is a [finite extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) of the [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423) **Q**<sub>*p*</sub>, and if *v<sub>p</sub>* denotes the [*p*-adic valuation](wikipedia://en/16047618), then

$$\chi(G_K,M)=p^{-[K:\mathbf{Q}_p]v_p(m)}$$

where \[*K*:**Q**<sub>*p*</sub>\] is the [degree](wikipedia://en/4382697) of *K* over **Q**<sub>*p*</sub>.

The Euler characteristic can be rewritten, using [local Tate duality](wikipedia://en/26712429), as

$$\chi(G_K,M)=\frac{\# H^0(K,M)\cdot\# H^0(K,M^\prime)}{\# H^1(K,M)}$$

where *M*<sup>′</sup> is the [local Tate dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Tate_dual) of *M*.
