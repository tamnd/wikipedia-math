# James Hamilton (physicist)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Hamilton"}

**James** "**Jim**" **Hamilton** (29 January 1918 – 6 July 2000) was an [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physicist) who, whilst at Dublin Institute for Advanced Sciences (1941-1943), helped to develop the theory of [cosmic-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic-ray) [mesons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesons) with [Walter Heitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Heitler) and [Hwan-Wu Peng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwan-Wu_Peng).[^o2]

:::

He was born in [Sligo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sligo). His family moved to Belfast in 1920, where after attending the [Royal Academical Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Academical_Institution) he entered [Queen's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_Belfast) in 1935. Following his graduation, Hamilton continued to work at Queen's, and was the first fellow to be enrolled in the School of Theoretical Physics at the [Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies).

After service with the [British Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty) during the Second World War, Hamilton resumed his physics research at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) (1945-1949), under [Patrick Blackett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Blackett), where he worked on [radiation damping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_damping) and associated topics.

At the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), where he lectured in mathematics (1950–1960), he was at the forefront of work on [S-matrix theory](wikipedia://en/14026380) and became known for his sophisticated use of [dispersion relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispersion_relations). His work there included collaborations with [Abdus Salam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdus_Salam) and [Hans Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe). During his last two years he was at the core, along with [Richard Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_J._Eden) and [George Batchelor](wikipedia://en/1763397), of founding the new [Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Applied_Mathematics_and_Theoretical_Physics).

At [University College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_London) (1960-1964) he formed a thriving [high energy physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_energy_physics) research group, before moving to [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen) and [NORDITA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NORDITA), where he led the teaching of [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics) in [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) from 1964 to 1983.
