# Decagram (geometry)

Regular star polygon stat table

*Image: Sultan Barquq's Qur'an decagram.jpg.* Decagrams are common in [Islamic geometric patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_geometric_patterns), here in a Quran from the 14th century.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Decagram"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **decagram** is a 10-point [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303). There is one regular decagram, containing the vertices of a [regular decagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_decagon), but connected by every third point. Its [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) is $\{10/3\}$.[^o2]

:::

The name *decagram* combines a [numeral prefix](wikipedia://en/1705121), *deca-*, with the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) suffix *\-gram* (el).[^o3]

## Isotoxal variations {#Isotoxal_variations}

An [isotoxal polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotoxal_polygon) has two vertices and one edge. There are isotoxal decagram forms, which alternates vertices at two radii. Each form has a freedom of one angle. The first is a variation of a double-wound of a pentagon $\{5\}$, and last is a variation of a double-wound of a pentagram $\{5/2\}$. The middle is a variation of a regular decagram, $\{10/3\}$.

## Related figures {#Related_figures}

A regular decagram is a 10-sided [polygram](wikipedia://en/45296926), represented by symbol $\{10/n\}$, containing the same vertices as regular [decagon](wikipedia://en/327393). Only one of these polygrams, $\{10/3\}$ (connecting every third point), forms a regular [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303), but there are also three ten-vertex polygrams which can be interpreted as regular compounds:

- $\{10/5\}$ is a compound of five degenerate [digons](wikipedia://en/5442728) $5\{2\}$
- $\{10/4\}$ is a compound of two [pentagrams](wikipedia://en/197544) $2\{5/2\}$
- $\{10/2\}$ is a compound of two [pentagons](wikipedia://en/20741014) $2\{5\}$.[^o11][^o12]

$\{10/2\}$ can be seen as the 2D equivalent of the 3D [compound of dodecahedron and icosahedron](wikipedia://en/8010901) and 4D [compound of 120-cell and 600-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_of_120-cell_and_600-cell); that is, the compound of two [pentagonal polytopes](wikipedia://en/33020517) in their respective dual positions.

$\{10/4\}$ can be seen as the two-dimensional equivalent of the three-dimensional [compound of small stellated dodecahedron and great dodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_of_small_stellated_dodecahedron_and_great_dodecahedron) or [compound of great icosahedron and great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8010947) through similar reasons. It has six four-dimensional analogues, with two of these being compounds of two self-dual star polytopes, like the pentagram itself; the [compound of two great 120-cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_of_two_great_120-cells) and the [compound of two grand stellated 120-cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_of_two_grand_stellated_120-cells). A full list can be seen at [Polytope compound#Compounds with duals](wikipedia://en/23901#Compounds_with_duals).

Deeper truncations of the regular pentagon and pentagram can produce intermediate star polygon forms with ten equally spaced vertices and two edge lengths that remain [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) (any two vertices can be transformed into each other by a symmetry of the figure).[^o14][^o15][^o16]
