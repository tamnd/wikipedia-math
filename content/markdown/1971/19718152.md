# Harold Douglas Ursell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harold Douglas Ursell"}

**Harold Douglas Ursell** (16 April 1907 – 8 December 1969), known as **H. D. Ursell**, was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who is best known for the [Ursell function](wikipedia://en/19660624).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Ursell was born in [Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwickshire) in 1907, the youngest of six children, and was raised in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham). He was educated at [Central Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tile_Cross_Academy) and [King Edward's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward's_School,_Birmingham) in the city, before studying mathematics at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where he graduated as a [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) with distinction in Part II of the [tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripos) in 1926 at the unusually young age of 19.[^o3][^o4] It was while still an undergraduate that he made his name with the Ursell function, laying the foundations of a cluster expansion theory through the study of imperfect gases. At Cambridge he was the beneficiary of numerous prizes, including the famed [Smith's Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize), and in 1929 was elected to a fellowship at Trinity after studying almost period functions under the tutelage of [A. S. Besicovitch](wikipedia://en/404566). He was also a Choate Fellow at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) during the years 1930–31.[^o3]

Greatly influenced by both Besicovitch and [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741), Ursell left Cambridge while still a young man, following W. P. Milne to take up a position at [Leeds University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_University) in 1933.[^o3] There he remained for almost the entirety of his career, rising to the position of Reader in 1948. During his time at Leeds he wrote a number of articles on a variety of topics, becoming noted for his ability to rebut existing theorems using counter-examples; he was, however, perhaps best known for his diligent supervision of undergraduates and postgraduates alike. In 1967, he accepted a professorship at [Calgary University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_University) in Canada, but died suddenly of a stroke two years later.[^o3]

Ursell was not related to the German-born fellow Trinity mathematician [Fritz Ursell](wikipedia://en/10408290), who taught at [Manchester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_University).
