# Carlo Rinaldini

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carlo Rinaldini"}

**Carlo Rinaldini**, or **Renaldini** (30 December 1615 – 18 July 1698),[^n1] was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [military engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_engineer), [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), and [metrologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrologist) from [Ancona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancona).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born into an aristocratic family originally from [Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siena), Rinaldini studied at the [University of Macerata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Macerata) and the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna). He was in the service of [Pope Urban VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII) and obtained the supervision of the fortresses of [Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara), [Bondeno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bondeno), and [Comacchio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comacchio) from [Taddeo Barberini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taddeo_Barberini), the pope's nephew. He also tutored Barberini's children.[^n1]

Rinaldini was appointed [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) at the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa), and subsequently a Professor of Mathematics there from 1649 to 1666. Rinaldini was a friend to [Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo) and [Borelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Alfonso_Borelli), who nicknamed him "Simplicio" for his fidelity to traditional [Aristotelianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelianism).[^o6] Rinaldini corresponded with [Vincenzo Viviani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Viviani)[^o7] and with [Leopoldo de' Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_de'_Medici).[^o8] The correspondence with Leopoldo de' Medici helped establish the experimental agenda for the [Accademia del Cimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_del_Cimento), which was founded in 1657.[^o8] Rinaldini was an active member of the Accademia.[^o9]

As part of the Accademia, he proposed an experiment on the diffusion of heat. When a metal ball was frozen in ice, thermometers below the ball had the highest temperature drop; when a metal ball was heated, thermometers above the ball had the highest temperature rise.[^o6] This disproved the idea that heat and cold propagated in a spherically uniform way, which led physicist and meteorologist W. E. Knowles Middleton to credit Rinaldini with the discovery of [convection](wikipedia://en/9662955) in air.[^n1][^o11][^o12]

Rinaldini had numerous disagreements with his friends and with [Francesco Redi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Redi) and [Evangelista Torricelli](wikipedia://en/210413). Rinadini opposed the theory, advanced by Redi, that insects can be born from parent plant [galls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gall).[^o13][^o14] He thus anticipated the hypothesis that [gall insects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gall-inducing_insect) were born from eggs laid by individuals of the same species, promulgated by [Malpighi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malpighi).[^o15][^o16]

In 1667, Rinaldini left [Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscany) to go to the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua), where he held the chair of [Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and published *Philosophia rationalis, atque identità naturalis*. He tried in vain to return to Pisa. He then moved to [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), where he became the teacher of [Elena Cornaro Piscopia](wikipedia://en/5189951), who was the first woman in the world to obtain a [doctoral degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) when Rinaldini declared her to be a Magistra et Doctrix Philosophiae in 1678.[^o17][^o18]

In 1694, Rinaldini was the first person to propose a method for [thermometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermometer) calibration using the freezing and boiling points of water as fixed references, dividing the scale into equal [degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_%28temperature%29).[^o19][^o23] [Christiaan Huygens](wikipedia://en/42127) originally suggested using freezing and boiling points as standards in 1665,[^o22] but scientists were unsure whether those standards would hold across different latitudes.[^o23][^o20] Rinaldini argued that the ice and boiling temperatures are universal,[^o20] and his scale divided the temperature span into 12 degrees.[^o21][^o22] Rinaldini's degrees were calibrated by measuring known mixtures of boiling and freezing water, although that method was subsequently found to be unreliable.[^o23][^o22]
