# Radical of a module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Radical of a module"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the theory of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410), the **radical** of a module is a component in the theory of structure and classification. It is a generalization of the [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) for [rings](wikipedia://en/48404). In many ways, it is the [dual](wikipedia://en/609737) notion to that of the [socle](wikipedia://en/3571350) soc(*M*) of *M*.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $R$ be a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) and $M$ a left $R$-[module](wikipedia://en/276410). A [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) $N$ of $M$ is called **[maximal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_submodule)** or **cosimple** if the [quotient](wikipedia://en/364820) $M/N$ is a [simple module](wikipedia://en/29430). The **radical** of the module $M$ is the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of all maximal submodules of $M$,

$$\mathrm{rad}(M) = \bigcap\, \{N \mid N \mbox{ is a maximal submodule of } M\}$$

Equivalently,

$$\mathrm{rad}(M) = \sum\, \{S \mid S \mbox{ is a superfluous submodule of } M\}$$

These definitions have direct dual analogues for $\mathrm{soc}(M)$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- In addition to the fact that $\mathrm{rad}(M)$ is the sum of superfluous submodules, in a [Noetherian module](wikipedia://en/542431), $\mathrm{rad}(M)$ itself is a [superfluous submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluous_submodule).

In fact, if $M$ is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) over a ring, then $\mathrm{rad}(M)$ itself is a superfluous submodule. This is because any proper submodule of $M$ is contained in a maximal submodule of $M$ when $M$ is finitely generated.

- A ring for which $\mathrm{rad}(M)=\{0\}$ for every right $R$-module $M$ is called a right [V-ring](wikipedia://en/48340978).
- For any module $M$, $\mathrm{rad}(M/\mathrm{rad}(M))$ is zero.
- $M$ is a [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) module [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the [cosocle](wikipedia://en/24762871) $M/\mathrm{rad}(M)$ is finitely generated and $\mathrm{rad}(M)$ is a superfluous submodule of $M$.

:::
