# General set theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="General set theory"}

**General set theory** (**GST**) is [George Boolos](wikipedia://en/340621)'s (1998) name for a fragment of the [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory) [Z](wikipedia://en/322623). GST is sufficient for all mathematics not requiring [infinite sets](wikipedia://en/142679), and is the weakest known set theory whose [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) include the [first-order](wikipedia://en/10983) [Peano axioms](wikipedia://en/25005).

:::

## Ontology {#Ontology}

The ontology of GST is identical to that of [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC), and hence is thoroughly canonical. GST features a single [primitive](wikipedia://en/23706) [ontological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) notion, that of [set](wikipedia://en/26691), and a single ontological assumption, namely that all individuals in the [universe of discourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe_of_discourse) (hence all [mathematical objects](wikipedia://en/19453961)) are sets. There is a single [primitive](wikipedia://en/23706) [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931), [set membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Element_%28mathematics%29); that set *a* is a member of set *b* is written *a ∈ b* (usually read "*a* is an [element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Element_%28mathematics%29) of *b*").

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Axioms"}

## Axioms {#Axioms}

The symbolic axioms below are from Boolos (1998: 196), and govern how sets behave and interact. As with [Z](wikipedia://en/322623), the background logic for GST is [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983) with [identity](wikipedia://en/89532). Indeed, GST is the fragment of Z obtained by omitting the axioms [Union](wikipedia://en/52553), [Power Set](wikipedia://en/52552), Elementary Sets (essentially [Pairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pairing_axiom)) and [Infinity](wikipedia://en/246449) and then taking a theorem of Z, Adjunction, as an axiom. The natural language versions of the axioms are intended to aid the intuition.

\1) [Axiom of Extensionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_of_Extensionality): The sets *x* and *y* are the same set if they have the same members.

$$\forall x \forall y [\forall z [z \in x \leftrightarrow z \in y] \rightarrow x = y].$$

The converse of this axiom follows from the substitution property of equality.

\2) [Axiom Schema of Specification](wikipedia://en/52386) (or *Separation* or *Restricted Comprehension*): If *z* is a set and $\phi$ is any property that may be satisfied by all, some, or no elements of *z*, then there exists a subset *y* of *z* containing just those elements *x* in *z* that satisfy the property $\phi$. The [restriction](wikipedia://en/1589032) to *z* is necessary to avoid [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095) and its variants. More formally, let $\phi(x)$ be any formula in the language of GST in which *x* may occur freely and *y* does not. Then all instances of the following schema are axioms:

$$\forall z \exists y \forall x [x \in y \leftrightarrow ( x \in z \land \phi(x))].$$

\3) [Axiom of Adjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_of_Adjunction): If *x* and *y* are sets, then there exists a set *w*, the *adjunction* of *x* and *y*, whose members are just *y* and the members of *x*.[^o5]

$$\forall x \forall y \exists w \forall z [ z \in w \leftrightarrow (z \in x \lor z=y)].$$

*Adjunction* refers to an elementary operation on two sets, and has no bearing on the use of that term elsewhere in mathematics, including in [category theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor).

ST is GST with the axiom schema of specification replaced by the [axiom of empty set](wikipedia://en/52534):

$$\exists x \forall y [y \notin x].$$

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

### Metamathematics {#Metamathematics}

Note that Specification is an axiom schema. The theory given by these axioms is not [finitely axiomatizable](wikipedia://en/764613). [Montague](wikipedia://en/610182) (1961) showed that [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) is not finitely axiomatizable, and his argument carries over to GST. Hence any axiomatization of GST must include at least one [axiom schema](wikipedia://en/764613). With its simple axioms, GST is also immune to the three great antinomies of [naïve set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naïve_set_theory): [Russell's](wikipedia://en/46095), [Burali-Forti's](wikipedia://en/51653), and [Cantor's](wikipedia://en/2732904).

GST is interpretable in [relation algebra](wikipedia://en/4363670) because no part of any GST axiom lies in the scope of more than three [quantifiers](wikipedia://en/43507260). This is the [necessary and sufficient condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_and_sufficient_condition) given in Tarski and Givant (1987).

### Peano arithmetic {#Peano_arithmetic}

Setting *φ*(*x*) in *Separation* to *x*≠*x*, and assuming that the [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) is nonempty, assures the existence of the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566). *Adjunction* implies that if *x* is a set, then so is $S(x) = x \cup \{x\}$. Given *Adjunction*, the usual construction of the [successor ordinals](wikipedia://en/375256) from the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) can proceed, one in which the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) are defined as $\varnothing,\,S(\varnothing),\,S(S(\varnothing)),\,\ldots$ (see [Set-theoretic definition of natural numbers](wikipedia://en/428484)). GST is mutually interpretable with [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) (thus it has the same proof-theoretic strength as PA).

The most remarkable fact about ST (and hence GST), is that these tiny fragments of set theory give rise to such rich metamathematics. While ST is a small fragment of the well-known canonical set theories [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) and [NBG](wikipedia://en/528491), ST [interprets](wikipedia://en/621176) [Robinson arithmetic](wikipedia://en/2544440) (Q), so that ST inherits the nontrivial metamathematics of Q. For example, ST is [essentially undecidable](wikipedia://en/913118) because Q is, and every consistent theory whose theorems include the ST axioms is also essentially undecidable.[^o9][^o10] This includes GST and every axiomatic set theory worth thinking about, assuming these are consistent. In fact, the [undecidability](wikipedia://en/913118) of ST implies the undecidability of first-order logic with a single [binary predicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_predicate) letter.[^o11]

Q is also incomplete in the sense of [Gödel's incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_incompleteness_theorem). Any axiomatizable theory, such as ST and GST, whose theorems include the Q axioms is likewise incomplete. Moreover, the [consistency](wikipedia://en/75802) of GST cannot be proved within GST itself, unless GST is in fact inconsistent.

### Infinite sets {#Infinite_sets}

Given any model *M* of ZFC, the collection of [hereditarily finite sets](wikipedia://en/332621) in *M* will satisfy the GST axioms. Therefore, GST cannot prove the existence of even a countable [infinite set](wikipedia://en/142679), that is, of a set whose cardinality is $\aleph_0$. Even if GST did afford a countably infinite set, GST could not prove the existence of a set whose [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) is $\aleph_1$, because GST lacks the [axiom of power set](wikipedia://en/52552). Hence GST cannot ground [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), and is too weak to serve as a [foundation for mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_of_mathematics).

## History {#History}

Boolos was interested in GST only as a fragment of [Z](wikipedia://en/322623) that is just powerful enough to interpret [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic). He never lingered over GST, only mentioning it briefly in several papers discussing the systems of [Frege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frege)'s *[Grundlagen](wikipedia://en/8983001)* and *Grundgesetze*, and how they could be modified to eliminate [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095). The system **Aξ'**\[δ<sub>0</sub>\] in Tarski and Givant (1987: 223) is essentially GST with an [axiom schema of induction](wikipedia://en/25005) replacing [Specification](wikipedia://en/52386), and with the existence of an [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) explicitly assumed.

GST is called STZ in Burgess (2005), p. 223.[^o14] Burgess's theory ST[^o15] is GST with [Empty Set](wikipedia://en/52534) replacing the [axiom schema of specification](wikipedia://en/52386). That the letters "ST" also appear in "GST" is a coincidence.
