# Graeme Milton

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graeme Milton"}

**Graeme Milton** is an American mathematician, currently distinguished professor at [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah) and also previously the Eisenbud Professor at [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in 2010 and also a full professor at [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences).[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Graeme W. Milton received B.Sc. and M.Sc degrees in Physics from the [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney) in 1980 and 1982 respectively. He received a Ph.D degree in Physics from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1985, after which he joined the [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech) Physics Department as a Weingart Fellow from 1984 to 1986. He then joined the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) where he stayed until 1994 when he joined the faculty at the University of Utah as a full professor. He has received numerous honors and awards, including an [Alfred P. Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan_Fellowship) and a [Packard Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_and_Lucile_Packard_Foundation#Conservation_and_science), both in 1988. He was an Invited Speaker for the 1998 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842). He was awarded the Ralph E. Kleinman Prize in 2003 by the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) for “his many deep contributions to the modeling and analysis of composite materials.”[^o8]
