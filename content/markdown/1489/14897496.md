# Henri Brocard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henri Brocard"}

**Pierre René Jean Baptiste Henri Brocard** (; 12 May 1845 – 16 January 1922) was a French [meteorologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorologist) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), in particular a [geometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometer).[^o8] His best-known achievement is the invention and discovery of the properties of the [Brocard points](wikipedia://en/7029586), the [Brocard circle](wikipedia://en/14663012), and the [Brocard triangle](wikipedia://en/14884335), all bearing his name.

:::

Contemporary mathematician [Nathan Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Court) wrote that he, along with [Émile Lemoine](wikipedia://en/15956333) and [Joseph Neuberg](wikipedia://en/19178914), was one of the three co-founders of modern [triangle geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_geometry).[^o4] He was awarded the [Ordre des Palmes Académiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_des_Palmes_Académiques), and was an officer of the [Légion d'honneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Légion_d'honneur).[^o5]

He spent most of his life studying [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904) as an officer in the [French Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Navy), but seems to have made no notable original contributions to the subject.[^o8]

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early years {#Early_years}

Pierre René Jean Baptiste Henri Brocard was born on 12 May 1845, in [Vignot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vignot), [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse_%28department%29) to Elizabeth Auguste Liouville and Jean Sebastien Brocard. He attended the [Lycée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée) in [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille) as a young child, and then the Lycée in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg). After graduating from the Lycée he entered the [Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy) in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) where he was prepared for the examination for entrance to the prestigious [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), to which he was accepted in 1865.

#### École Polytechnique and military years {#École_Polytechnique_and_military_years}

*Image: Ecole Polytechnique France seen from lake DSC03389.JPG.* The [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique)

Brocard attended the École Polytechnique from 1865 to 1867.[^o16]

As was the norm at the time, he, after graduation, became a technical [officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_%28armed_forces%29) in the [French military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_military), which had been reorganized in 1866. He acted as a [meteorologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorologist) in the [French navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_navy), and general technician as well.

Brocard soon saw active service, as [Napoleon III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III) declared war upon [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia). Brocard was one of the 120,000 men under [Marshal MacMahon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshal_MacMahon) led to [Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metz) to free the [French army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_army) of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine). The French army, however, was defeated on 31 August at the [Battle of Sedan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sedan), and was taken prisoner along with approximately 83,000 other combatants.[^o8]

### Middle years {#Middle_years}

After Brocard was freed, he returned to his military position and continued teaching, publishing his mathematical articles in the most popular mathematical journal of that time, Nouvelles Correspondances Mathématiques (also called *Nouvelles annales mathématiques*).[^o11][^o12] He joined the [Société Mathématique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_Mathématique_de_France) in 1873, just a year after its founding. In 1875, he was inducted into the French Association for the Advancement of Science as well as the French Meteorological Society. He was shortly after sent to northern [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa), where he served as a military technician for the French forces stationed in [Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers), the seat of French Africa. While in Algiers, Brocard founded the Meteorological Institute of Algiers.[^o13][^o16] Brocard also visited [Oran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oran) while in northern Africa, which was occupied by the French in 1831.[^o14]

### Discovery of Brocard points {#Discovery_of_Brocard_points}

During a meeting of the French Association for the Advancement of Science, Brocard presented a self-written article entitled *Etudes d'un nouveau cercle du plan du triangle*, his first paper on the [Brocard points](wikipedia://en/7029586), the [Brocard triangle](wikipedia://en/14884335), and the [Brocard circle](wikipedia://en/14663012), all of which today bear his name.[^o16]

### Later years {#Later_years}

*Image: Brocardpage.png.* The first page of Henri Brocard's *Notes de bibliographie des courbes géométriques*

In 1884 Brocard returned to France. He served with the Meteorological Commission in [Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier) before moving to [Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenoble) and lastly [Bar-le-duc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar-le-duc). He honorably retired from the French military in 1910 as a [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel). His remaining two major publications were *Notes de bibliographie des courbes géométriques* (1897, 1899, published in two volumes)[^o16][^o18] and the *Courbes géométriques remarquables* (1920, posthumous 1967, also published in two volumes) which was written in collaboration with Timoléon Lemoyne.[^o19]

Brocard attended the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) at [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich) in 1897, [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) in 1900, [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg) in 1904, [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) in 1908, [Cambridge, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_England) in 1912, and [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) in 1920.[^o16]

Brocard spent the last years of his life in [Bar-le-Duc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar-le-Duc). He was offered the presidency of Bar-le-Duc's *Letters, Sciences, and Arts Society*, of which he had been a longtime member and correspondent for several foreign academies of, but declined. He died on 16 January 1922 while on a trip to [Kensington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington), [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England).[^o8]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

### Brocard triangle, Brocard circle, and Brocard points {#Brocard_triangle,_Brocard_circle,_and_Brocard_points}

*Image: Brocard point.svg.* A diagram of the [Brocard point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocard_point)

Brocard's most well-known contributions to mathematics are the [Brocard points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocard_point), the [Brocard circle](wikipedia://en/14663012), and the [Brocard triangle](wikipedia://en/14884335). The positive Brocard point (sometimes known as the first Brocard point) of a [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9417) plane [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is the interior [point](wikipedia://en/593693) of the triangle for which the three angles formed by two of the vertices and the point are equal. Their common value is the Brocard angle of the triangle.[^o22] The Brocard circle of the triangle is a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) having a [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) of the [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) between the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) and [symmedian](wikipedia://en/320997). It contains the [Brocard points](wikipedia://en/7029586). The Brocard triangle of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is a triangle formed by the [intersection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-line_intersection) of [line](wikipedia://en/946975) from a vertex to its corresponding [Brocard point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocard_point) and a line from another vertex to its corresponding Brocard point and the other two points constructed using different combinations of vertices and Brocard points. The Brocard triangle is inscribed in the [Brocard circle](wikipedia://en/14663012).

### Other mathematical contributions {#Other_mathematical_contributions}

Brocard published various other papers on [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) during his time at [Bar-le-duc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar-le-duc), none of which became as well known as *Etudes d'un nouveau cercle du plan du triangle*. He also conjured up a famous unsolved problem thought to have no answers other than the three he provided. This problem is called [Brocard's Problem](wikipedia://en/11301521). One other achievement of his is guessing at the meaning of the cryptic title of one of [Girard Desargues](wikipedia://en/358484)' papers, *DALG*. In his paper *Analyse d'autographes et autres écrits de Girard Desargues*, he surmised that it stood for *Des Argues, Lyonnais, Géometre*, which is the generally accepted title.[^o26]

### Meteorology {#Meteorology}

*Image: Algieruniversity.jpg.* The [University of Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Algiers) administrates the Meteorological Society which Brocard founded.

Though Brocard made no major notable original discoveries in [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904), he founded the Meteorological Institute in [Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers) and served as a meteorological technician during his time in the French military. He also published several notable papers on meteorology.[^o8][^o28]
