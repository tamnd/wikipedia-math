# X̅ and R chart

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="X̅ and R chart"}

In [statistical process control](wikipedia://en/593680) (SPC), the **$\bar x$ and R chart**, also known as an **averages and range chart**, is a type of scheme, popularly known as [control chart](wikipedia://en/435754), used to monitor the mean and range of a normally distributed variables simultaneously, when samples are collected at regular intervals from a [business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process) or [industrial process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_industrial_processes).[^o3] It is often used to monitor the [variables data](wikipedia://en/30423282) but the performance of the **$\bar x$ and R chart** may suffer when the normality assumption is not valid.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The "chart" actually consists of a pair of charts: One to monitor the process standard deviation (as approximated by the sample [moving range](wikipedia://en/27588)) and another to monitor the process mean, as is done with the [$\bar x$ and s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbar_and_s_chart) and [individuals control charts](wikipedia://en/11999755). The $\bar x$ and R chart plots the mean value for the quality characteristic across all units in the sample, $\bar x_i$, plus the range of the quality characteristic across all units in the sample as follows:

R = x<sub>max</sub> - x<sub>min</sub>.

The [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) is the basis for the charts and requires the following assumptions:

- The quality characteristic to be monitored is adequately modeled by a [normally distributed](wikipedia://en/21462) [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685)
- The parameters μ and σ for the random variable are the same for each unit and each unit is independent of its predecessors or successors
- The inspection procedure is same for each sample and is carried out consistently from sample to sample

The control limits for this chart type are:[^o6]

- $D_3 \bar R$ (lower) and $D_4 \bar R$ (upper) for monitoring the process variability
- $\bar \bar x \pm A_2 \bar R$ for monitoring the process mean

where $\bar \bar x$ and $\bar R$ are the estimates of the long-term process mean and range established during control-chart setup and A<sub>2</sub>, D<sub>3</sub>, and D<sub>4</sub> are sample size-specific [anti-biasing](wikipedia://en/10240807) constants. The anti-biasing constants are typically contained in Control Chart Constant tables in the appendices of textbooks on [statistical process control](wikipedia://en/593680). ## Usage of the chart {#Usage_of_the_chart}

The chart is advantageous in the following situations:[^o10]

1. The sample size is relatively small (say, n ≤ 10—[$\bar x$ and s charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbar_and_s_chart) are typically used for larger sample sizes)
1. The sample size is constant
1. Humans must perform the calculations for the chart

As with the [$\bar x$ and s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbar_and_s_chart) and [individuals control charts](wikipedia://en/11999755), the $\bar x$ chart is only valid if the within-sample variability is constant.[^o11] Thus, the R chart is examined before the $\bar x$ chart; if the R chart indicates the sample variability is in statistical control, then the $\bar x$ chart is examined to determine if the sample mean is also in statistical control. If on the other hand, the sample variability is *not* in statistical control, then the entire process is judged to be not in statistical control regardless of what the $\bar x$ chart indicates.

## Limitations {#Limitations}

For monitoring the mean and variance of a normal distribution, the [$\bar x$ and s chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbar_and_s_chart) is usually better than the $\bar{x}$ and R chart.
