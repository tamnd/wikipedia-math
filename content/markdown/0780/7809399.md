# Countably generated space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Countably generated space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is called **countably generated** if the topology of $X$ is determined by the [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) sets in a similar way as the topology of a [sequential space](wikipedia://en/4668395) (or a [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801)) is determined by the convergent sequences.

:::

The countably generated spaces are precisely the spaces having countable [tightness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tightness_%28topology%29)—therefore the name **countably tight** is used as well.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A topological space $X$ is called **countably generated** if the topology on $X$ is [coherent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_%28topology%29) with the family of its countable subspaces. In other words, any subset $V \subseteq X$ is closed in $X$ whenever for each countable [subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_%28topology%29) $U$ of $X$ the set $V \cap U$ is closed in $U;$ or equivalently, any subset $V \subseteq X$ is open in $X$ whenever for each countable [subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_%28topology%29) $U$ of $X$ the set $V \cap U$ is open in $U.$

Equivalently, $X$ is **countably tight**; that is, for every set $A\subseteq X$ and every point $x\in\overline A$, there is a countable set $D\subseteq A$ with $x\in\overline D.$ In other words, the closure of $A$ is the union of the closures of all countable subsets of $A.$

:::

## Countable fan tightness {#Countable_fan_tightness}

A topological space $X$ has **countable fan tightness** if for every point $x \in X$ and every sequence $A_1, A_2, \ldots$ of subsets of the space $X$ such that $x \in {\textstyle\bigcap\limits_n} \, \overline{A_n} = \overline{A_1} \cap \overline{A_2} \cap \cdots,$ there are finite set $B_1\subseteq A_1, B_2 \subseteq A_2, \ldots$ such that $x \in \overline{{\textstyle\bigcup\limits_n} \, B_n} = \overline{B_1 \cup B_2 \cup \cdots}.$

A topological space $X$ has **countable strong fan tightness** if for every point $x \in X$ and every sequence $A_1, A_2, \ldots$ of subsets of the space $X$ such that $x \in {\textstyle\bigcap\limits_n} \, \overline{A_n} = \overline{A_1} \cap \overline{A_2} \cap \cdots,$ there are points $x_1 \in A_1, x_2 \in A_2, \ldots$ such that $x \in \overline{\left\{x_1, x_2, \ldots\right\}}.$ Every [strong Fréchet–Urysohn space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_Fréchet–Urysohn_space) has strong countable fan tightness.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A [quotient](wikipedia://en/237213) of a countably generated space is again countably generated. Similarly, a [topological sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_sum) of countably generated spaces is countably generated. Therefore, the countably generated spaces form a [coreflective subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coreflective_subcategory) of the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051). They are the coreflective hull of all countable spaces.

Any subspace of a countably generated space is again countably generated.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Every [sequential space](wikipedia://en/4668395) (in particular, every [metrizable space](wikipedia://en/19738)) is countably generated.

An example of a space which is countably generated but not sequential can be obtained, for instance, as a subspace of [Arens–Fort space](wikipedia://en/8588347).

:::
