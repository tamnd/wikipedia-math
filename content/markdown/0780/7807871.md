# Basu's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Basu's theorem"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **Basu's theorem** states that any [boundedly complete](wikipedia://en/194618) and [sufficient statistic](wikipedia://en/140841) is [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_independence) of any [ancillary statistic](wikipedia://en/670398). This is a 1955 result of [Debabrata Basu](wikipedia://en/1978708).[^o2]

:::

It is often used in statistics as a tool to prove independence of two [statistics](wikipedia://en/26703), by first demonstrating one is complete sufficient and the other is ancillary, then appealing to the theorem.[^o3] An example of this is to show that the [sample mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_mean) and [sample variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_variance) of a [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) are independent statistics, which is done in the Example section below. This property (independence of sample mean and sample variance) characterizes normal distributions.

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $(P_\theta; \theta \in \Theta)$ be a family of distributions on a [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333) $(X, \mathcal{A})$ and a [statistic](wikipedia://en/26703) $T$ maps from $(X, \mathcal{A})$ to some measurable space $(Y, \mathcal{B})$. If $T$ is a boundedly complete sufficient statistic for $\theta$, and $A$ is ancillary to $\theta$, then conditional on $\theta$, $T$ is independent of $A$. That is, $T\perp\!\!\!\perp A \mid \theta$.

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

Let $P_\theta^T$ and $P_\theta^A$ be the [marginal distributions](wikipedia://en/506077) of $T$ and $A$ respectively.

Denote by $A^{-1}(B)$ the [preimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimage) of a set $B$ under the map $A$. For any measurable set $B \in \mathcal{B}$ we have

$$P_\theta^A(B) = P_\theta (A^{-1}(B)) = \int_{Y} P_\theta(A^{-1}(B) \mid T=t) \ P_\theta^T (dt).$$

The distribution $P_\theta^A$ does not depend on $\theta$ because $A$ is ancillary. Likewise, $P_\theta(\cdot \mid T = t)$ does not depend on $\theta$ because $T$ is sufficient. Therefore

$$\int_Y \big[ P(A^{-1}(B) \mid T=t) - P^A(B) \big] \ P_\theta^T (dt) = 0.$$

Note the integrand (the function inside the integral) is a function of $t$ and not $\theta$. Therefore, since $T$ is boundedly complete the function

$$g(t) = P(A^{-1}(B) \mid T=t) - P^A(B)$$

is zero for $P_\theta^T$ almost all values of $t$ and thus

$$P(A^{-1}(B) \mid T=t) = P^A(B)$$

for almost all $t$. Therefore, $A$ is independent of $T$.

:::

:::

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

### Independence of sample mean and sample variance of a normal distribution {#Independence_of_sample_mean_and_sample_variance_of_a_normal_distribution}

Let *X*<sub>1</sub>, *X*<sub>2</sub>, ..., *X*<sub>*n*</sub> be [independent, identically distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_and_identically-distributed_random_variables) [normal](wikipedia://en/21462) [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) with [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) *μ* and [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) *σ*<sup>2</sup>.

Then with respect to the parameter *μ*, one can show that

$$\widehat{\mu}=\frac{\sum X_i}{n},$$

the sample mean, is a complete and sufficient statistic – it is all the information one can derive to estimate *μ,* and no more – and

$$\widehat{\sigma}^2=\frac{\sum \left(X_i-\bar{X}\right)^2}{n-1},$$

the sample variance, is an ancillary statistic – its distribution does not depend on *μ.*

Therefore, from Basu's theorem it follows that these statistics are independent conditional on $\mu$, conditional on $\sigma^2$.

This independence result can also be proven by [Cochran's theorem](wikipedia://en/574337).

Further, this property (that the sample mean and sample variance of the normal distribution are independent) *[characterizes](wikipedia://en/780886)* the normal distribution – no other distribution has this property.[^o11]

:::
