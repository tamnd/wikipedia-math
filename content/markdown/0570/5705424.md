# Toronto space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toronto space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the realm of [point-set topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-set_topology), a **Toronto space** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) that is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to every proper subspace of the same [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174).

:::

There are five homeomorphism classes of countable Toronto spaces, namely: the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology), the [indiscrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiscrete_topology), the [cofinite topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofinite_topology) and the [upper](wikipedia://en/1017002) and [lower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_topology) topologies on the [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers). The only countable [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) Toronto space is the discrete space.[^o2]

The **Toronto space problem** asks for an uncountable Toronto Hausdorff space that is not discrete.[^o3]
