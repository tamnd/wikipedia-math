# Dowker–Thistlethwaite notation

*Image: Dowker-notation-example.svg.* A knot diagram with crossings labelled for a Dowker sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dowker–Thistlethwaite notation"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **Dowker–Thistlethwaite** (**DT**) **notation** or code, for a [knot diagram](wikipedia://en/366808) is a sequence of even [integers](wikipedia://en/14563). The notation is named after [Clifford Hugh Dowker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Hugh_Dowker) and [Morwen Thistlethwaite](wikipedia://en/16705411), who refined a notation originally due to [Peter Guthrie Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait).[^o8] It is not an [invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) of the associated knot.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

To generate the Dowker–Thistlethwaite notation, traverse the knot using an arbitrary starting point and direction. Label each of the n crossings with the numbers 1, ..., 2*n* in order of traversal (each crossing is visited and labelled twice), with the following modification: if the label is an even number and the strand followed crosses over at the crossing, then change the sign on the label to be a negative. When finished, each crossing will be labelled a pair of integers, one even and one odd.[^o4] The Dowker–Thistlethwaite notation is the sequence of even integer labels associated with the labels 1, 3, ..., 2*n* − 1 in turn.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

For example, a [knot diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_diagram) may have crossings labelled with the pairs (1, 6) (3, −12) (5, 2) (7, 8) (9, −4) and (11, −10). The Dowker–Thistlethwaite notation for this labelling is the sequence: 6 −12 2 8 −4 −10.

:::

## Uniqueness and counting {#Uniqueness_and_counting}

Dowker and Thistlethwaite have proved that the notation specifies [prime knots](wikipedia://en/315030) uniquely, [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) reflection.[^o8]

In the more general case, a knot can be recovered from a Dowker–Thistlethwaite sequence, but the recovered knot may differ from the original by either being a reflection or by having any [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) component reflected in the line between its entry/exit points – the Dowker–Thistlethwaite notation is unchanged by these reflections. Knots tabulations typically consider only [prime knots](wikipedia://en/315030) and disregard [chirality](wikipedia://en/32703814), so this ambiguity does not affect the tabulation.

The [ménage problem](wikipedia://en/21068755), posed by Tait, concerns counting the number of different number sequences possible in this notation.
