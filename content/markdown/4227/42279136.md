# Thomas Malton, the elder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Malton, the elder"}

**Thomas Malton**, the elder (1726–1801) was an English architectural draughtsman and writer on geometry.

*Image: Thomas Malton the Elder's book titled Treatise on Perspective published in Great Britain 1779.jpg.* Thomas Malton the elder's *Treatise on Perspective* contains the first known 3-dimensional pop-up to appear in a commercially produced book.

*Image: Close-up of pop-up from Thomas Malton the Elder's book Treatise on Perspective.jpg.* This pop-up, as well as the others in *Treatise on Perspective* were used to help artists and architects understand and apply the concept of perspective to their work.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in London, Malton originally kept an upholsterer's shop in the [Strand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strand,_London).[^o3] He contributed two drawings of St. Martin's Church to the exhibition of the [Free Society of Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Society_of_Artists) in 1761, and also architectural drawings to the exhibitions of the [Incorporated Society of Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incorporated_Society_of_Artists) in 1766 and 1768. In 1772 and the following years he sent architectural drawings to the [Royal Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Academy). There are drawings by him in the [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_and_Albert_Museum).[^o3]

In 1774 Malton published *The Royal Road to Geometry; or an easy and familiar Introduction to the Mathematics*, a school-book intended as an improvement on Euclid, and in 1775 *A Compleat Treatise on Perspective in Theory and Practice, on the Principles of Dr. Brook Taylor*.[^o3] The *Compleat Treatise* is the earliest known commercially produced [pop-up book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop-up_book). It contains 3-dimensional paper mechanisms. Some of the pop-ups are activated by pulling string and form geometric shapes used to aid the reader in understanding the concept of perspective. It came to replace the pamphlet of [Joshua Kirby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Kirby) as the standard English work on [linear perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_perspective).[^o4] He also gave lectures on perspective at his house in Poland Street, [Soho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soho).[^o3]

Having fallen into financial difficulties in London he moved to Dublin, accompanied by his son [James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Malton). He lived in Dublin for the rest of his life, gaining a reputation there as a lecturer on geometry. He died at Dublin on 18 February 1801[^o3]

## Family {#Family}

His eldest son was [Thomas Malton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Malton), the younger. [James Malton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Malton) was another son.[^o3]
