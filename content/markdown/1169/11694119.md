# Inverse second

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inverse second"}

The **inverse second** or **reciprocal second** (**s<sup>−1</sup>**), also called ***per second***, is a [unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_of_measurement) defined as the [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940) of the [second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second) (a unit of [time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time)). It is applicable for [physical quantities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_quantities) of [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28physics%29) **reciprocal time**, such as [frequency](wikipedia://en/10779) and [strain rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strain_rate).

:::

It is [dimensionally](wikipedia://en/8267) equivalent to:

- [hertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertz) (Hz), historically known as [cycles per second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycles_per_second) – the SI unit for [frequency](wikipedia://en/10779) and [rotational frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_frequency)
- [becquerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Becquerel) (Bq) – the SI unit for the rate of occurrence of aperiodic or stochastic [radionuclide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity) events
- [baud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baud) (Bd) – the unit for [symbol rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol_rate) over a communication link
- [bit per second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit_per_second) (bit/s) – the unit of bit rate

However, the special names and symbols above for s<sup>−1</sup> are recommend for clarity.

Reciprocal second should not be confused with [radian per second](wikipedia://en/4562902) (rad⋅s<sup>−1</sup>), the SI unit for [angular frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_frequency) and [angular velocity](wikipedia://en/65927). As the [radian](wikipedia://en/26003) is a [dimensionless unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_unit), radian per second is dimensionally consistent with reciprocal second. However, they are used for different [kinds of quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kind_of_quantity), frequency and angular frequency, whose numerical value differs by 2$\pi$.

The **inverse minute** or **reciprocal minute** (**min<sup>−1</sup>**), also called ***per minute***, is 60<sup>−1</sup> s<sup>−1</sup>, as 1 min = 60 s; it is used in quantities of type "[counts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable_quantity) per minute", such as:

- [Actions per minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actions_per_minute)
- [Beats per minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beats_per_minute)
- [Counts per minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counts_per_minute)
- [Revolutions per minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_per_minute) (rpm)
- [Words per minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Words_per_minute)

Inverse square second (s<sup>−2</sup>) is involved in the units of linear [acceleration](wikipedia://en/2443), [angular acceleration](wikipedia://en/65929), and [rotational acceleration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_acceleration).
