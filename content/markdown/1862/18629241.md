# On Spirals

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="On Spirals"}

***On Spirals*** (Περὶ ἑλίκων) is a treatise by [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844), written around 225 BC. Notably, Archimedes employed the Archimedean spiral in this book to [square the circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_the_circle) and [trisect an angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trisect_an_angle).

:::

## Contents {#Contents}

### Preface {#Preface}

Archimedes begins *On Spirals* with a message to Dositheus of Pelusium mentioning the death of [Conon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conon_of_Samos) as a loss to mathematics. He then goes on to summarize the results of *[On the Sphere and Cylinder](wikipedia://en/18084436)* (Περὶ σφαίρας καὶ κυλίνδρου) and *On Conoids and Spheroids* (Περὶ κωνοειδέων καὶ σφαιροειδέων). He continues to state his results of *On Spirals*.

### Archimedean spiral {#Archimedean_spiral}

*Image: Archimedean spiral.svg.* The Archimedean spiral with three 360° turnings on one arm

The Archimedean spiral was first studied by [Conon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conon_of_Samos) and was later studied by Archimedes in *On Spirals*. Archimedes was able to find various [tangents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangents) to the spiral.[^o2] He defines the spiral as:

If a straight line one extremity of which remains fixed is made to revolve at a uniform rate in a plane until it returns to the position from which it started, and if, at the same time as the straight line is revolving, a point moves at a uniform rate along the straight line, starting from the fixed extremity, the point will describe a spiral in the plane.[^o6]

### Trisecting an angle {#Trisecting_an_angle}

*Image: Archimedes trisect spiral.svg.* Example of how Archimedes trisected an angle in *On Spirals*.

The construction as to how Archimedes [trisected the angle](wikipedia://en/91111) is as follows:

> Suppose the angle ABC is to be trisected. Trisect the segment BC and find BD to be one third of BC. Draw a circle with center B and radius BD. Suppose the circle with center B intersects the spiral at point E. Angle ABE is one third angle ABC.[^o8]

### Squaring the circle {#Squaring_the_circle}

*Image: Archimedean spiral circle squaring triangle.svg.* The circle and the triangle are equal in area.

To square the circle, Archimedes gave the following construction:

> Let P be the point on the spiral when it has completed one turn. Let the tangent at P cut the line perpendicular to OP at T. OT is the length of the circumference of the circle with radius OP.

Archimedes had already proved as the first proposition of *[Measurement of a Circle](wikipedia://en/18203202)* that the area of a circle is equal to a right-angled triangle having the legs' lengths equal to the radius of the circle and the circumference of the circle. So the area of the circle with radius OP is equal to the area of the triangle OPT.[^o10]
