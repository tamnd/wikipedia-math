# Kuznyechik

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Kuznyechik"}

ru-latn; Cyrillic script: Кузнечик) is a symmetric [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594). It has a block size of 128 bits and key length of 256 bits. It is defined in the National Standard of the Russian Federation **GOST R 34.12-2015**[^o4][^o5] and also in RFC 7801. It has received scrutiny for the structure of its [S-boxes](wikipedia://en/240991), which the authors claim were randomly generated.

:::

The name of the cipher can be translated from Russian as [grasshopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasshopper), however, the standard explicitly says that the English name for the cipher is *Kuznyechik* (). The designers claim that by naming the cipher Kuznyechik they follow the trend of difficult to pronounce algorithm names set up by [Rijndael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijndael) and [Keccak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keccak).[^o6] There is also a rumor that the cipher was named after its creators: A. S. Kuzmin,[^o7] A. A. Nechaev[^o8] and Company (Russian: **Куз**ьмин, **Неч**аев **и** **К**омпания).

The standard **GOST R 34.12-2015** defines the new cipher in addition to the old [GOST block cipher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOST_block_cipher) (now called Magma) as one and does not declare the old cipher obsolete.[^o9]

Kuznyechik is based on a [substitution–permutation network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution–permutation_network), though the [key schedule](wikipedia://en/609603) employs a [Feistel network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feistel_network).

## Description {#Description}

For encryption, decryption and key generation, the following functions:

$Add_2[k](a)=k\oplus a$, where $k$, $a$ are binary strings of the form $a=a_{15}||$...$||a_{0}$ ($||$ is string [concatenation](wikipedia://en/64474)).

$N(a)=S(a_{15})||$...$||S(a_{0}).~~N^{-1}(a)$ is a reversed transformation of $N(a)$.

$G(a)=\gamma(a_{15},$...$,a_0)||a_{15}||$...$||a_{1}.$

$G^{-1}(a)$ — reversed transformation of $G(a)$ , $G^{-1}(a)=a_{14}||a_{13}||$...$||a_{0}||\gamma(a_{14},a_{13},$...$,a_0,a_{15}).$

$H(a)=G^{16}(a)$, where $G^{16}$ — composition of transformations $G^{15}$ and $G$ etc.

$$F[k](a_1,a_0)=(HNAdd_2[k](a_1)\oplus a_0, a_1).$$

The key generation algorithm uses iterative constant $C_i=H(Bin_{128}(i))$, i=1,2,...32 and sets the shared key as follows: $K=k_{255}||$...$||k_0$.

Iterated keys:

$K_1=k_{255}||$...$||k_{128}$

$K_2=k_{127}||$...$||k_{0}$

$(K_{2i+1}, K_{2i+2})=F[C_{8(i-1)+8}]$...$F[C_{8(i-1)+1}](K_{2i-1}, K_{2i}), i=1, 2, 3, 4.$

## Cryptanalysis {#Cryptanalysis}

Riham AlTawy and Amr M. Youssef describe a [meet-in-the-middle attack](wikipedia://en/423418) on the 5-round reduced Kuznyechik which enables recovery of the key with a [time complexity](wikipedia://en/405944) of 2<sup>140</sup>, [memory complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_complexity) of 2<sup>153</sup>, and data complexity of 2<sup>113</sup>.[^o18]

[Alex Biryukov](wikipedia://en/9189491), Leo Perrin, and Aleksei Udovenko published a paper in which they show that the [S-boxes](wikipedia://en/240991) of Kuznyechik and [Streebog](wikipedia://en/47024355) were not created [pseudo-randomly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-random) but by using a hidden algorithm which they were able to [reverse engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_engineer).[^o19]

Later Leo Perrin and Aleksei Udovenko published two alternative decompositions of the S-box and proved its connection to the S-box of the Belarusian cipher BelT.[^o20] The authors of the paper note that while the reason for using such a structure remains unclear, generating S-boxes by a hidden algorithm contradicts the concept of [nothing-up-my-sleeve numbers](wikipedia://en/1249296) which could prove that no weaknesses were intentionally introduced in their design.

Riham AlTawy, Onur Duman, and Amr M. Youssef published two [fault attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_attack) on Kuznyechik which show the importance of protecting the implementations of the cipher.[^o21]

## Adoption {#Adoption}

[VeraCrypt](wikipedia://en/45345491) (a fork of [TrueCrypt](wikipedia://en/905962)) included Kuznyechik as one of its supported encryption algorithms.[^o23]
