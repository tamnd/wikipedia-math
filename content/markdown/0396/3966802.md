# Philip Jourdain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Philip Jourdain"}

**Philip Edward Bertrand Jourdain** (16 October 1879 – 1 October 1919) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) and follower of [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163).

:::

## Background {#Background}

He was born in [Ashbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashbourne,_Derbyshire) in Derbyshire one of a large family belonging to Emily Clay and his father Francis Jourdain (who was the vicar at Ashbourne).[^o6] His sister [Eleanor Jourdain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Jourdain) was an English academic and author. Another sister, [Margaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Jourdain) (1876–1951), was an authority on the history of fine English home-furnishings, and the life-long companion of the novelist [Ivy Compton-Burnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_Compton-Burnett).

## Mathematics and logic {#Mathematics_and_logic}

Jourdain was partly disabled by [Friedreich's ataxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedreich's_ataxia). He corresponded with [Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216) and [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416), and took a close interest in the paradoxes related to [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095), formulating the [card paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Card_paradox) version of the [liar paradox](wikipedia://en/17967).[^o6] He corresponded with [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741), meeting with him in Cambridge to discuss Frege's book [Grundgesetze der Arithmetik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grundgesetze_der_Arithmetik), parts of which Jourdain had prepared a translation. He also worked on [algebraic logic](wikipedia://en/7870034), and the [history of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science) with [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) as a particular study. He was London editor for *[The Monist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Monist)*.

Near the end of his life Jourdain became increasingly obsessed by trying to prove the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840), and published several incorrect proofs of it. (Littlewood 1986, p.129) describes Jourdain on his deathbed still arguing with him about his (incorrect) proof of the axiom of choice. Later, the [independence of the axiom of choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_the_axiom_of_choice) was established.
