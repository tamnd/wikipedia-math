# Ernst Steinitz

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ernst Steinitz"}

**Ernst Steinitz** (13 June 1871 – 29 September 1928) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Steinitz was born in [Laurahütte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurahütte) ([Siemianowice Śląskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemianowice_Śląskie)), [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia_Province), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) (now in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland)), the son of Sigismund Steinitz, a Jewish coal merchant, and his wife Auguste Cohen; he had two brothers. He studied at the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau) and the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin), receiving his Ph.D. from Breslau in 1894. Subsequently, he took positions at [Charlottenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottenburg) (now [Technische Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Berlin)), Breslau, and the [University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel), Germany, where he died in 1928. Steinitz married Martha Steinitz and had one son.

## Mathematical works {#Mathematical_works}

Steinitz's 1894 thesis was on the subject of [projective configurations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_configuration); it contained the result that any abstract description of an [incidence structure](wikipedia://en/723125) of three lines per point and three points per line could be realized as a configuration of straight lines in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) with the possible exception of one of the lines. His thesis also contains the proof of [Kőnig's theorem](wikipedia://en/5465118) for [regular](wikipedia://en/85821) [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431), phrased in the language of configurations.

In 1910 Steinitz published the very influential paper[^o5] *Algebraische Theorie der Körper* ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): Algebraic Theory of Fields, *[Crelle's Journal](wikipedia://en/973547)*). In this paper he axiomatically studies the properties of [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) and defines important concepts like [prime field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_field), [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088) and the [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree) of a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634), and also [normal](wikipedia://en/929925) and [separable](wikipedia://en/363340) extensions (the latter he called *algebraic extensions of the first kind*). Besides numerous, today standard, results in [field theory](wikipedia://en/10603), he proved that every field has an (essentially unique) [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) and a theorem, which characterizes the [existence of primitive elements](wikipedia://en/448933) of a field extension in terms of its intermediate fields. [Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394)[^o6] called this article "a basic paper which may be considered as having given rise to the current conception of Algebra".

Steinitz also made fundamental contributions to the theory of [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra): [Steinitz's theorem](wikipedia://en/19762817) for polyhedra is that the 1-[skeletons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton_%28topology%29) of [convex polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedra) are exactly the 3-[connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-connected_graph) [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314). His work in this area was published posthumously as a 1934 book, *Vorlesungen über die Theorie der Polyeder unter Einschluss der Elemente der Topologie*,[^o7] by [Hans Rademacher](wikipedia://en/1864916).
