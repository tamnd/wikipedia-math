# Mojżesz Presburger

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Mojżesz Presburger"}

**Mojżesz Presburger**, or **Prezburger**,[^o15] (; 27 December 1904 – c. 1943) was a Polish [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), and [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher). He was a student of [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064), [Kazimierz Ajdukiewicz](wikipedia://en/900467), and [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075).[^o15] He is known for, among other things, having invented [Presburger arithmetic](wikipedia://en/23756) as a student in 1929 – a form of arithmetic in which one allows induction but removes multiplication, to obtain a [decidable theory](wikipedia://en/913118).[^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8]

:::

He was born in Warsaw on 27 December 1904 to Abram Chaim Prezburger and Joehwet Prezburger (née Aszenmil).[^o9] On 28 May 1923 he got his [matura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matura) from the School of Commerce of the Merchants' Meeting of Warsaw.[^o10] On 7 October 1930 he was awarded [master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in mathematics from [Warsaw University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University).[^o15][^o11] He died in [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust), probably 1943.[^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15]

In 2010, the [European Association for Theoretical Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Association_for_Theoretical_Computer_Science) began conferring the annual [Presburger Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presburger_Award) named after him to a young scientist (in exceptional cases to several young scientists) for outstanding contributions in theoretical computer science. [Mikołaj Bojańczyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikołaj_Bojańczyk) was the first recipient.
