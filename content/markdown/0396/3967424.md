# Reflective subcategory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reflective subcategory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [full subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_subcategory) *A* of a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) *B* is said to be **reflective** in *B* when the [inclusion functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_functor) from *A* to *B* has a [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint).p. 91 This adjoint is sometimes called a *reflector*, or *localization*.[^o2] Dually, *A* is said to be **coreflective** in *B* when the inclusion functor has a [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint).

:::

Informally, a reflector finds the best possible approximation of an object of the category *B* in the subcategory *A*, in the sense that it does not lose any information about morphisms to objects of the subcategory *A*.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A full subcategory **A** of a category **B** is said to be **reflective in B** if for each **B**-[object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) *B* there exists an **A**-object $A_B$ and a **B**-[morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) $r_B \colon B \to A_B$ such that for each **B**-morphism $f\colon B\to A$ to an **A**-object $A$ there exists a unique **A**-morphism $\overline f \colon A_B \to A$ with $\overline f\circ r_B=f$.

*Image: Refl1.png.*

The pair $(A_B,r_B)$ is called the **A-reflection** of *B*. The morphism $r_B$ is called the **A-reflection arrow.** (Although often, for the sake of brevity, we speak about $A_B$ only as being the **A**-reflection of *B*).

This is equivalent to saying that the embedding functor $E\colon \mathbf{A} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{B}$ is a right adjoint. The left adjoint [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $R \colon \mathbf B \to \mathbf A$ is called the **reflector**. The map $r_B$ is the [unit](wikipedia://en/53991#Definition_via_counit–unit) of this adjunction.

The reflector assigns to $B$ the **A**-object $A_B$ and $Rf$ for a **B**-morphism $f$ is determined by the [commuting diagram](wikipedia://en/138484)

*Image: Reflsq1.png.*

If all **A**-reflection arrows are (extremal) [epimorphisms](wikipedia://en/59539), then the subcategory **A** is said to be **(extremal) epireflective**. Similarly, it is **bireflective** if all reflection arrows are [bimorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bimorphism).

All these notions are special cases of the common generalization **$E$-reflective subcategory,** where $E$ is a [class](wikipedia://en/20696) of morphisms.

The **$E$-reflective hull** of a class **A** of objects is defined as the smallest $E$-reflective subcategory containing **A**. Thus we can speak about the reflective hull, epireflective hull, extremal epireflective hull, etc.

An **anti-reflective subcategory** is a full subcategory **A** such that the only objects of **B** that have an **A**-reflection arrow are those that are already in **A**.

[Dual](wikipedia://en/310885) notions to the above-mentioned notions are coreflection, coreflection arrow, (mono)coreflective subcategory, coreflective hull, anti-coreflective subcategory.

## Examples {#Examples}

### Algebra {#Algebra}

- The [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748) **Ab** is a reflective subcategory of the [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092), **Grp**. The reflector is the functor that sends each group to its [abelianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianization). In its turn, the category of groups is a reflective subcategory of the category of [inverse semigroups](wikipedia://en/1757814).[^o8]
- Similarly, the category of [commutative associative algebras](wikipedia://en/2112) is a reflective subcategory of all associative algebras, where the reflector is [quotienting](wikipedia://en/184635#For_algebras_over_a_ring) out by the commutator [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977). This is used in the construction of the [symmetric algebra](wikipedia://en/654098) from the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628).
- Dually, the category of [anti-commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommutativity) associative algebras is a reflective subcategory of all associative algebras, where the reflector is quotienting out by the anti-commutator ideal. This is used in the construction of the [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) from the tensor algebra.
- The category of [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) is a reflective subcategory of the category of [integral domains](wikipedia://en/15462) (with [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) [ring homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/26411) as morphisms). The reflector is the functor that sends each integral domain to its [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271).
- The category of abelian [torsion groups](wikipedia://en/3568695) is a coreflective subcategory of the category of abelian groups. The coreflector is the functor sending each group to its [torsion subgroup](wikipedia://en/144052).
- The categories of [elementary abelian groups](wikipedia://en/4843202), abelian [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664), and *p*-groups are all reflective subcategories of the category of groups, and the [kernels](wikipedia://en/142616) of the reflection maps are important objects of study; see [focal subgroup theorem](wikipedia://en/25971038).
- The [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092) is a coreflective subcategory of the category of [monoids](wikipedia://en/19652): the right adjoint maps a monoid to its [group of units](wikipedia://en/289666).[^o9]

### Topology {#Topology}

- The category of [Kolmogorov spaces](wikipedia://en/68121) (T<sub>0</sub> spaces) is a reflective subcategory of **Top**, the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051), and the [Kolmogorov quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov_quotient) is the reflector.
- The category of [completely regular spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_regular_space) **CReg** is a reflective subcategory of **Top**. By taking Kolmogorov quotients, one sees that the subcategory of [Tychonoff spaces](wikipedia://en/30554) is also reflective.
- The category of all [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/13637) is a reflective subcategory of the category of all Tychonoff spaces (and of the category of all topological spacesp. 140). The reflector is given by the [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897).
- The category of all [complete metric spaces](wikipedia://en/7832) with [uniformly continuous mappings](wikipedia://en/20018#Uniformly_continuous_maps) is a reflective subcategory of the [category of metric spaces](wikipedia://en/535617). The reflector is the [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28metric_space%29) of a metric space on objects, and the extension by density on arrows.p. 90
- The category of [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) is a reflective subcategory of [presheaves](wikipedia://en/4840944) on a topological space. The reflector is sheafification, which assigns to a presheaf the sheaf of sections of the bundle of its germs.
- The category **Seq** of [sequential spaces](wikipedia://en/4668395) is a coreflective subcategory of **Top**. The sequential coreflection of a topological space $(X,\tau)$ is the space $(X,\tau_{\mathrm{seq}})$, where the topology $\tau_{\text{seq}}$ is a finer topology than $\tau$ consisting of all [sequentially open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequentially_open) sets in $X$ (that is, complements of [sequentially closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequentially_closed) sets).[^n1]

### Functional analysis {#Functional_analysis}

- The category of [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_spaces) is a reflective subcategory of the category of [normed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space) and [bounded linear operators](wikipedia://en/455961). The reflector is the norm completion functor.

### Category theory {#Category_theory}

- For any [Grothendieck site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck_site) (*C*, *J*), the [topos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck_topos) of [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) on (*C*, *J*) is a reflective subcategory of the topos of [presheaves](wikipedia://en/4840944) on *C*, with the special further property that the reflector functor is [left exact](wikipedia://en/453945). The reflector is the [sheafification functor](wikipedia://en/245466#Turning_a_presheaf_into_a_sheaf) *a* : Presh(*C*) → Sh(*C*, *J*), and the adjoint pair (*a*, *i*) is an important example of a [geometric morphism](wikipedia://en/21391464#Geometric_morphisms) in topos theory.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- The components of the [counit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counit_%28category_theory%29) are [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828).p. 140[^o16]
- If *D* is a reflective subcategory of *C*, then the inclusion functor *D* → *C* creates all [limits](wikipedia://en/39248) that are present in *C*.p. 141
- A reflective subcategory has all [colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimits) that are present in the ambient category.p. 141
- The [monad](wikipedia://en/355140) induced by the reflector/localization adjunction is idempotent.p. 158

:::
