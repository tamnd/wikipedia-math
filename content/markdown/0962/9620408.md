# Laurence Chisholm Young

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Laurence Chisholm Young"}

**Laurence Chisholm Young** (14 July 1905 – 24 December 2000) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his contributions to [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), [optimal control theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_control_theory), and [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342). He was the son of [William Henry Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Young) and [Grace Chisholm Young](wikipedia://en/239997), both prominent mathematicians. He moved to the US in 1949 but never sought American citizenship.

:::

The concept of [Young measure](wikipedia://en/9659484) is named after him: he also introduced the concept of the [generalized curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_curve)[^o3] and a concept of generalized surface[^o4] which later evolved in the concept of [varifold](wikipedia://en/6706053).[^o5][^o6] The Young integral also is named after him and has now been generalised in the theory of [rough paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_paths).[^o7]

## Life and academic career {#Life_and_academic_career}

Laurence Chisholm Young was born in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen),[^o9] the fifth of the six children of [William Henry Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Young) and [Grace Chisholm Young](wikipedia://en/239997).[^o10] He held positions of Professor at the University of Cape Town, South Africa, and at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. He was also a chess grandmaster.[^o11]
