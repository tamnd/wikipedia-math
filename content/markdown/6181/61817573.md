# Christine Heitsch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Christine Heitsch"}

**Christine Elizabeth Heitsch** is a mathematician whose research involves the [biomolecular structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomolecular_structure) of [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA). She is a professor of mathematics in the [Georgia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech) School of Mathematics, and the founding director of the Southeast Center for Mathematics and Biology at Georgia Tech.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Heitsch graduated in 1994 from the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign), *[magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honors)* and [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa), with a bachelor's degree in mathematics. She completed her Ph.D. at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 2000. Her dissertation, *Computational Complexity of Generalized Pattern Matching*, was jointly supervised by [John Rhodes](wikipedia://en/31620518) and [John R. Stallings](wikipedia://en/18729795).

After postdoctoral research at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) and the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison), she joined the Georgia Tech faculty in 2006, and was promoted to full professor in 2016. At Georgia Tech, as well as being a professor of mathematics, she also holds courtesy appointments in the School of Computational Science & Engineering, and in the School of Biology.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2019 the University of Illinois Department of Mathematics gave Heitsch their annual Alumni Award for Outstanding Professional Achievement.
