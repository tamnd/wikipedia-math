# Minkowski sausage

*Image: Quadratic Koch.svg.* Higher iteration of type 2

*Image: 6452553 Vicsek Fractal Antenna.png.* Example of a [fractal antenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_antenna): a space-filling curve called a "Minkowski Island"[^o1] or "Minkowski fractal"[^o2]

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Minkowski sausage"}

The **Minkowski sausage**[^o4] or **Minkowski curve** is a [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) first proposed by and named for [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537) as well as its casual resemblance to a [sausage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sausage) or sausage links. The initiator is a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) and the generator is a [broken line](wikipedia://en/12056032) of eight parts one fourth the length.[^o5]

:::

The Sausage has a [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) of $\left ( \ln8/\ln4\ \right ) = 1.5 = 3/2$. It is therefore often chosen when studying the physical properties of non-integer fractal objects. It is strictly [self-similar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-similar).[^o5] It never intersects itself. It is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) everywhere, but [differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206) nowhere. It is not [rectifiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifiable_curve). It has a [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) of 0. The type 1 curve has a dimension of $\tfrac{\ln 5}{\ln 3}$ ≈ 1.46.

Multiple Minkowski Sausages may be arranged in a four sided polygon or [square](wikipedia://en/659939) to create a quadratic [Koch island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_island) or **Minkowski island/\[snow\]flake**:
