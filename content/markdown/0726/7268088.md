# Martin Löb

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Martin Löb"}

**Martin Hugo Löb** (; 31 March 1921 – 21 August 2006) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He settled in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) after the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) and specialised in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). He moved to [the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Netherlands) in the 1970s, where he remained in retirement. He is perhaps best known for having formulated [Löb's theorem](wikipedia://en/693002) in 1955.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Löb grew up in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), but escaped from the [Third Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Reich), arriving in the UK just before the outbreak of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). As an [enemy alien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enemy_alien), he was deported on the *[Dunera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMT_Dunera)* to an [internment camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_camp) at [Hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hay,_New_South_Wales) in [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia) in 1940, where the 19-year-old Löb was taught mathematics by other internees. His teacher, [Felix Behrend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Behrend), was later a professor at [Melbourne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_University).

Löb was allowed to return to the UK in 1943, and he studied at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) after the War. After graduating, he became a research student with [Reuben Goodstein](wikipedia://en/1586970) at the [University of Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leicester). He completed his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) and became an assistant lecturer at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds) in 1951, where he was to remain for 20 years, becoming a [Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_%28academic_rank%29) and ultimately [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of Mathematical Logic from 1967 to 1970. He developed the [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) group at Leeds, making it one of the leading centres in the UK. Löb did research on [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) and [computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414). He formulated [Löb's theorem](wikipedia://en/693002) in 1955, as a formal version of [Löb's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Löb's_paradox), that statements that assert their own provability must be true (similar to [Gödel's incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_incompleteness_theorem)).

Löb's wife, Caroline, was Dutch. They had two daughters together. Löb moved to become a professor at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) in the early 1970s. He remained at the University of Amsterdam until he retired. He then moved to [Annen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annen), where he later died.
