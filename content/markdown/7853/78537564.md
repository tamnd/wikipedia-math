# Forster–Swan theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Forster–Swan theorem"}

The **Forster–Swan theorem** is a result from [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) that states an upper bound for the minimal number of [generators](wikipedia://en/3171371) of a [finitely generated module](wikipedia://en/326430) $M$ over a commutative [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129). The usefulness of the theorem stems from the fact, that in order to form the bound, one only needs the minimum number of generators of all [localizations](wikipedia://en/252216) $M_{\mathfrak{p}}$.

:::

The theorem was proven in a more restrictive form in 1964 by [Otto Forster](wikipedia://en/68051547)[^o2] and then in 1967 generalized by [Richard G. Swan](wikipedia://en/18384954)[^o3] to its modern form.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Forster–Swan theorem"}

## Forster–Swan theorem {#Forster–Swan_theorem}

Let

- $R$ be a commutative Noetherian ring with one,
- $M$ be a finitely generated $R$-module,
- $\mathfrak{p}$ a [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) of $R$.
- $\mu(M)$ and $\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(M)$ are the minimal number of generators needed to generate the $R$-module $M$ and the $R_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module $M_{\mathfrak{p}}$, respectively.

According to [Nakayama's lemma](wikipedia://en/1835001), in order to compute $\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(M)$ one can compute the dimension of $M_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}M$ over the field $k(\mathfrak{p})=R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}$, i.e.

$$\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(M)=\operatorname{dim}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}(M_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}M).$$

::: {#o7 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

### Statement {#Statement}

Define the local $\mathfrak{p}$-bound

$$b_{\mathfrak{p}}(M):=\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(M)+\operatorname{dim}(R/\mathfrak{p}),$$

then the following holds[^o8]

$$\mu(M)\leq \sup_{\mathfrak{p}}\;\{b_{\mathfrak{p}}(M)\;|\;\mathfrak{p}\;\text{is prime},\;M_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\}.$$

:::
