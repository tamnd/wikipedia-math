# Hugo Duminil-Copin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hugo Duminil-Copin"}

**Hugo Duminil-Copin** (born 26 August 1985) is a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specializing in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 2022.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

The son of a [middle school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education_in_France#Collège) sports teacher and a former female dancer who became a primary school teacher, Duminil-Copin grew up in the outer suburbs of Paris, where he played a lot of sports as a child, and initially considered attending a sports-oriented high school to pursue his interest in handball.[^o4] He decided to attend a school focused on mathematics and science,[^o4] and enrolled at the [Lycée Louis-le-Grand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Louis-le-Grand) in Paris earning his [Baccalauréat Scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baccalauréat#Baccalauréat_général_:_série_Scientifique_%28S%29) with a *[mention très bien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_grading_in_France)* in 2003 and was admitted at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29) in 2005 and the [University Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_University). He decided to focus on math instead of physics, because he found the rigour of mathematical proof more satisfying, but developed an interest in [percolation theory](wikipedia://en/187805), which is used in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) to address issues in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481).[^o4] In 2008, he moved to the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva) to write a PhD thesis under [Stanislav Smirnov](wikipedia://en/22752379). Duminil-Copin and Smirnov used percolation theory and the vertices and edges connecting them in a lattice to model fluid flow and with it [phase transitions](wikipedia://en/54423). The pair investigated the number of [self-avoiding walks](wikipedia://en/11186496) that were possible in [hexagonal lattices](wikipedia://en/2868762), connecting [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) to percolation theory. This was published in the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)* in 2012, the same year in which Duminil-Copil was awarded his PhD at the age of 27.[^o4]

In 2013, after his postdoctorate, Duminil-Copin was appointed assistant professor, then full professor in 2014 at the University of Geneva.[^o5] In 2016, he became permanent professor at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques).[^o6] Since 2019, he has been member of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea).[^o7]

Since 2017, Duminil-Copin has been the principal investigator of the [European Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council) – Starting Grant “Critical behavior of lattice models (CriBLam)”. He is a member of the Laboratory Alexander Grothendieck, a [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) joint research unit with IHES.[^o5]

Duminil-Copin's work focuses on the mathematical area of statistical physics. Duminil-Copin uses ideas from probability theory to study the critical behavior of various models on networks.[^o5] His work focuses on identifying the critical point at which phase transitions occur, what happens at the critical point, and the behaviour of the system just above and below the critical point.[^o4] He has been working on dependent percolation models whereby the state of an edge in one part of a lattice will affect the state of edges elsewhere, to shed light of [Ising models](wikipedia://en/292744), which are used to study phase transitions in [ferromagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetic) materials. In collaboration with Vincent Beffara in 2011, he was able to produce a formula for a determining the critical point for many two-dimensional dependent percolation models.[^o4] In 2019, along with Vincent Tassion and Aran Raoufi, he published research on the size of connected components in the lattice when the system is just below and above the critical point. They showed that below the critical point, the probability of having two vertices in the same connected component of the lattice would decay exponentially with separation distance, and that a similar result applies above the critical point, and that there is an infinite connected component above the critical point. Duminil-Copin and his associates proved this characteristic, which they called "sharpness", using [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science).[^o4] He has also shed more light on the nature of the phase transition at the critical point itself, and whether the transition will be continuous or discontinuous, under various circumstances, with a focus on [Potts models](wikipedia://en/1162226).[^o4]

Duminil-Copin is researching [conformal invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_invariance) in dependent percolation models in two dimensions. He said that by proving the existence of these symmetries, a great deal of information about the models would be extracted.[^o4] In 2020, he and his collaborators proved that [rotational invariance](wikipedia://en/424202) exists at the boundary between phases in many physical systems.[^o8][^o9]

Duminil-Copin was awarded the 2017 [New Horizons in Mathematics Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Horizons_in_Mathematics_Prize) for his work on [Ising type models](wikipedia://en/292744).[^n1]

Duminil-Copin was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 2022 for "solving longstanding problems in the probabilistic theory of [phase transitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_transitions) in statistical physics, especially in dimensions three and four".[^o10][^o11] [Wendelin Werner](wikipedia://en/6607797) credited Duminil-Copin with generalising the field of percolation theory, saying that "Everything is easier, streamlined. The results are stronger. … The whole understanding of these physical phenomena has been transformed."[^o4] Werner said that Duminil-Copin has solved "Basically half of the main open questions" in percolation theory.[^o4]

Duminil-Copin's hobbies include sports, which he has stated helps him find inspiration when working.[^o4] He is married and has a daughter.[^o12]
