# + h.c.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="+ h.c."}

**\+ h.c.** is an abbreviation for "**plus** the [**H**ermitian **C**onjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_conjugate)"; it means that there are additional terms which are the [Hermitian conjugates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_conjugate) of all of the preceding terms, and is a convenient shorthand to omit half the terms actually present.[^o4][^o3]

:::

## Context and use {#Context_and_use}

The [notation convention](wikipedia://en/373299) "+ h.c." is common in [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) in the context of writing out formulas for [Lagrangians](wikipedia://en/23371726#Quantum_mechanics) and [Hamiltonians](wikipedia://en/14381), which conventionally are both required to be [Hermitian operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_operator).[^o3]

The expression

$$\mathcal{L} = A + B + C + ~ \text{h.c.} ~$$

means

$\mathcal{L} = A + B + C + A^\dagger + B^\dagger + C^\dagger ~.$[^o3]

The mathematics of quantum mechanics is based on [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), whereas almost all observations (measurements) are only [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438). Adding its own [conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_conjugate) to an operator guarantees that the combination is [Hermitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_operator), which in turn guarantees that the combined operator's eigenvalues will be real numbers, suitable for predicting values of observations / measurements.[^o4]

### Dagger and asterisk notation {#Dagger_and_asterisk_notation}

In the expressions above, $A^\dagger$ is used as the symbol for the [Hermitian conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_conjugate) (also called the *[conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548)*) of $A$, defined as applying both the [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) and the [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844) transformations to the operator $A$, in any order.

The dagger ($\dagger$) is an old notation in mathematics, but is still widespread in quantum-mechanics. In mathematics (particularly [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422)) the Hermitian conjugate of $A$ is commonly written as $A^\ast$, but in quantum mechanics the asterisk ($\ast$) notation is sometimes used for the [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) only, and not the combined [conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548) ([Hermitian conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_conjugate)).
