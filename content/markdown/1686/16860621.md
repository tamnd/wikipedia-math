# Twin circles

*Image: Archimedes' Circles.svg.* The twin circles (red) of an arbelos (grey)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Twin circles"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **twin circles** are two special circles associated with an [arbelos](wikipedia://en/887197). An arbelos is determined by three collinear points $A$, $B$, and $C$, and is the curvilinear triangular region between the three [semicircles](wikipedia://en/617831) that have $AB$, $BC$, and $AC$ as their diameters. If the arbelos is partitioned into two smaller regions by a line segment through the middle point of $A$, $B$, and $C$, perpendicular to line $ABC$, then each of the two twin circles lies within one of these two regions, tangent to its two semicircular sides and to the splitting segment.

:::

These circles first appeared in the *[Book of Lemmas](wikipedia://en/17941791)*, which showed (Proposition V) that the two circles are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330).[^o10] [Thābit ibn Qurra](wikipedia://en/207547), who translated this book into Arabic, attributed it to [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) mathematician [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844). Based on this claim the twin circles, and several other circles in the Arbelos congruent to them, have also been called **Archimedes's circles**. However, this attribution has been questioned by later scholarship.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

*Image: Круги-близнецы.gif.* Animation of twin circles for various positions of point B on AC segment

Specifically, let $A$, $B$, and $C$ be the three corners of the arbelos, with $B$ between $A$ and $C$. Let $D$ be the point where the larger semicircle intercepts the line [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to the $AC$ through the point $B$. The segment $BD$ divides the arbelos in two parts. The twin circles are the two circles inscribed in these parts, each [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to one of the two smaller semicircles, to the segment $BD$, and to the largest semicircle.[^o9]

Each of the two circles is uniquely determined by its three tangencies. Constructing it is a special case of the [Problem of Apollonius](wikipedia://en/2642185).

Alternative approaches to constructing two circles congruent to the twin circles have also been found.[^o11][^o12] These circles have also been called Archimedean circles. They include the [Bankoff circle](wikipedia://en/16857243), [Schoch circles](wikipedia://en/19107031), and [Woo circles](wikipedia://en/17794292).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Let *a* and *b* be the diameters of two inner semicircles, so that the outer semicircle has diameter *a* + *b*. The diameter of each twin circle is then[^o9]

$$d=\frac{ab}{a+b}.$$

Alternatively, if the outer semicircle has unit diameter, and the inner circles have diameters $s$ and $1-s$, the diameter of each twin circle is[^o9]

$$d=s(1-s). \,$$

The smallest circle that encloses both twin circles has the same area as the arbelos.[^o9]
