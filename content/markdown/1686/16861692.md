# Unfolding (functions)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unfolding"}

In mathematics, an **unfolding** of a smooth real-valued [function](wikipedia://en/185427) *ƒ* on a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) is a certain family of functions that includes *ƒ*.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $M$ be a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) and consider a smooth mapping $f : M \to \mathbb{R}.$ Let us assume that for given $x_0 \in M$ and $y_0 \in \mathbb{R}$ we have $f(x_0) = y_0$. Let $N$ be a smooth $k$-dimensional manifold, and consider the family of mappings (parameterised by $N$) given by $F : M \times N \to \mathbb{R} .$ We say that $F$ is a $k$-parameter unfolding of $f$ if $F(x,0) = f(x)$ for all $x.$ In other words the functions $f : M \to \mathbb{R}$ and $F : M \times \{0\} \to \mathbb{R}$ are the same: the function $f$ is contained in, or is unfolded by, the family $F.$

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Let $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$ be given by $f(x,y) = x^2 + y^5.$ An example of an unfolding of $f$ would be $F : \mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}$ given by

$$F((x,y),(a,b,c)) = x^2 + y^5 + ay + by^2 + cy^3.$$

As is the case with unfoldings, $x$ and $y$ are called variables, and $a,$ $b,$ and $c$ are called parameters, since they parameterise the unfolding.

:::

## Well-behaved unfoldings {#Well-behaved_unfoldings}

In practice we require that the unfoldings have certain properties. In $\mathbb{R}$, $f$ is a smooth mapping from $M$ to $\mathbb{R}$ and so belongs to the [function space](wikipedia://en/340630) $C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}).$ As we vary the parameters of the unfolding, we get different elements of the function space. Thus, the unfolding induces a function $\Phi : N \to C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}).$ The space $\operatorname{diff}(M) \times \operatorname{diff}(\mathbb{R})$, where $\operatorname{diff}(M)$ denotes the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) of $M$ etc., [acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on $C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}).$ The action is given by $(\phi,\psi) \cdot f = \psi \circ f \circ \phi^{-1}.$ If $g$ lies in the [orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_orbit) of $f$ under this action then there is a diffeomorphic change of coordinates in $M$ and $\mathbb{R}$, which takes $g$ to $f$ (and vice versa). One property that we can impose is that

$$\operatorname{Im}(\Phi) \pitchfork \operatorname{orb}(f)$$

where "$\pitchfork$" denotes "[transverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transversality_%28mathematics%29) to". This property ensures that as we vary the unfolding parameters we can predict – by knowing how the orbit [foliates](wikipedia://en/371227) $C^\infty (M,\mathbb{R})$ – how the resulting functions will vary.

## Versal unfoldings {#Versal_unfoldings}

There is an idea of a versal unfolding. Every versal unfolding has the property that $\operatorname{Im}(\Phi) \pitchfork \operatorname{orb}(f)$, but the converse is false. Let $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ be [local coordinates](wikipedia://en/304139) on $M$, and let $\mathcal{O}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ denote the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of smooth functions. We define the [Jacobian ideal](wikipedia://en/29321367) of $f$, denoted by $J_f$, as follows:

$$J_f := \left\langle \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}, \ldots, \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n} \right\rangle.$$

Then a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28mathematics%29) for a versal unfolding of $f$ is given by the [quotient](wikipedia://en/184635)

$$\frac{\mathcal{O}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)}{J_f}.$$

This quotient is known as the local algebra of $f$. The dimension of the local algebra is called the [Milnor number](wikipedia://en/18882483) of $f$. The minimum number of unfolding parameters for a versal unfolding is equal to the Milnor number; that is not to say that every unfolding with that many parameters will be versal. Consider the function $f(x,y) = x^2 + y^5$. A calculation shows that

$$\frac{\mathcal{O}(x,y)}{\langle 2x, 5y^4 \rangle} = \{y,y^2,y^3\} \ .$$

This means that $\{y,y^2,y^3\}$ give a basis for a versal unfolding, and that

$$F((x,y),(a,b,c)) = x^2 + y^5 + ay + by^2 + cy^3$$

is a versal unfolding. A versal unfolding with the minimum possible number of unfolding parameters is called a miniversal unfolding.

## Bifurcations sets of unfoldings {#Bifurcations_sets_of_unfoldings}

An important object associated to an unfolding is its bifurcation set. This set lives in the [parameter space](wikipedia://en/2882813) of the unfolding, and gives all parameter values for which the resulting function has degenerate singularities.

## Other terminology {#Other_terminology}

Sometimes unfoldings are called deformations, versal unfoldings are called versal deformations, etc.
