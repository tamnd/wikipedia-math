# Applied Mathematics Panel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Applied Mathematics Panel"}

The **Applied Mathematics Panel** (**AMP**) was created at the end of 1942 as a division of the [National Defense Research Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Research_Committee) (NDRC) within the [Office of Scientific Research and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Scientific_Research_and_Development) (OSRD) in order to solve mathematical problems related to the military effort in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), particularly those of the other NDRC divisions.

:::

The panel's headquarters were in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan), and it was directed by [Warren Weaver](wikipedia://en/473287), formerly of NDRC Division 7, [Fire Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-control_system). It contracted projects out to various research groups, notably at [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) Universities.

In addition to work immediately relevant to the war effort, mathematicians involved with the panel also pursued problems of interest to them without contracts from outside organizations. Most notably, [Abraham Wald](wikipedia://en/1239430) developed the statistical technique of [sequential analysis](wikipedia://en/3509530) while working for AMP.

AMP was formally disbanded in 1946.
