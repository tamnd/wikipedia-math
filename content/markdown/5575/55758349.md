# Jeanne LaDuke

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jeanne LaDuke"}

**Alice Jeanne LaDuke** (born June 27, 1938) is an American mathematician who specialized in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220). She was also a [child actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_actor) who appeared in one film (*[The Green Promise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Green_Promise)*).

:::

## Early life and film career {#Early_life_and_film_career}

LaDuke was raised on a farm in [Posey County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posey_County,_Indiana), in southwest [Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana). Her parents were college-educated and an aunt who taught mathematics in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) frequently visited, bringing mathematics puzzles for LaDuke.

As a child, she was cast from a field of 12,000 [4-H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-H) members to play a small part in *[The Green Promise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Green_Promise)* (1948) as farm girl Jessie Wexford, the sister of [Natalie Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Wood)'s character's love interest. Wood and LaDuke shared a tutor who taught them both string games as well as their school curriculum.

## Education {#Education}

LaDuke studied mathematics at [DePauw University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DePauw_University) in the 1950s, and roomed with another mathematics major from Oregon, who showed her the state on summer camping trips.

She earned a master's degree in mathematics, but was unable to obtain a teaching position with it because the schools she applied to only hired men. She returned to Oregon in 1966 as a doctoral student at the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon), and completed her Ph.D. in 1969 with a dissertation in mathematical analysis supervised by [Kenneth A. Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_A._Ross) on *E<sub>p</sub> Space: Essentially a Product of C<sub>p</sub> Spaces*.

## Mathematics career {#Mathematics_career}

After completing her doctorate, LaDuke spent the following thirty years as a faculty member of the department of mathematical sciences at [DePaul University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DePaul_University). She retired in 2003.

With [Judy Green](wikipedia://en/55756466), she is the author of *[Pioneering Women in American Mathematics: The Pre-1940 PhD’s](wikipedia://en/63513617)* ([American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097), 2009). An annual lecture series on Women in Mathematics, Science, and Technology at DePaul is named after her.
