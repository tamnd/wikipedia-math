# Biconnected graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Biconnected graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **biconnected graph** is a connected and "nonseparable" [graph](wikipedia://en/325806), meaning that if any one [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) were to be removed, the graph will remain connected. Therefore a biconnected graph has no [articulation vertices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articulation_vertex).

:::

The property of being [2-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) is equivalent to biconnectivity, except that the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) of two vertices is usually not regarded as 2-connected.

This property is especially useful in maintaining a graph with a two-fold [redundancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redundancy_%28engineering%29), to prevent disconnection upon the removal of a single [edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29) (or connection).

The use of **biconnected** graphs is very important in the field of networking (see [Network flow](wikipedia://en/645676)), because of this property of redundancy.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **biconnected** [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) is a connected graph that is not broken into disconnected pieces by deleting any single vertex (and its incident edges).

A **biconnected** [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986) is one such that for any two vertices *v* and *w* there are two directed paths from *v* to *w* which have no vertices in common other than *v* and *w*.

:::

## Structure of 2-connected graphs {#Structure_of_2-connected_graphs}

Every 2-connected graph can be constructed inductively by adding [paths](wikipedia://en/638889) to a [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) (Diestel 2016, 59).
