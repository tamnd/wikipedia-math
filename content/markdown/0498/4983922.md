# J. Hyam Rubinstein

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="J. Hyam Rubinstein"}

**Joachim Hyam Rubinstein** [FAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Australian_Academy_of_Science) (born 7 March 1948, in [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne,_Australia)) an [Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australians) top [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specialising in [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774);[^o3] he is currently serving as an honorary [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in the Department of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne), having retired in 2019.

:::

He has spoken and written widely on the state of the mathematical sciences in Australia, with particular focus on the impacts of reduced Government spending for university mathematics departments.[^o4][^o5][^o6]

## Education {#Education}

In 1965, Rubinstein matriculated (i.e. graduated) from [Melbourne High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_High_School) in [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia) winning the maximum of four [exhibitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_%28scholarship%29). In 1969, he graduated from [Monash University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monash_University) in Melbourne, with a [B.Sc.(Honours)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honours_degree) degree in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).

In 1974, Rubinstein received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) under the advisership of [John Stallings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stallings). His dissertation was on the topic of *Isotopies of Incompressible Surfaces in Three Dimensional Manifolds*.

## Research interests {#Research_interests}

His major contributions include results involving almost normal [Heegaard splittings](wikipedia://en/864418) and the closely related joint work with Jon T. Pitts relating strongly irreducible [Heegaard splittings](wikipedia://en/864418) to [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734), joint work with [William Jaco](wikipedia://en/8017927) on special [triangulations](wikipedia://en/207174) of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) (namely 0-efficient and 1-efficient triangulations), and joint work with [Martin Scharlemann](wikipedia://en/49932035) on the Rubinstein–Scharlemann graphic. He is a key figure in the algorithmic theory of 3-manifolds, and one of the initial developers of the [Regina](wikipedia://en/4781110) program, which implements his [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) recognition algorithm.

His research interests also include: [shortest networks](wikipedia://en/41985) applied to [underground mine design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_mining_%28hard_rock%29), [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), [learning theory](wikipedia://en/387537), [financial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics), and [stock market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market) trading systems.

## Honours {#Honours}

- Past President of the [Australian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/4579582).
- Chair of the [Australian Committee for the Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Committee_for_the_Mathematical_Sciences).
- Elected Fellow of the [Australian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Academy_of_Science) in 2003.
- Recipient of the [Hannan Medal](wikipedia://en/23265859) in 2004.
- Recipient of the [George Szekeres Medal](wikipedia://en/23270711) in 2008.
- Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), 2012.[^o11]
- *From July 11 to 22, 2011, a workshop and conference in his honour, jointly titled “Hyamfest: Geometry & Topology Down Under”, were held at the University of Melbourne.*[^o12]
