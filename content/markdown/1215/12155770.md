# Green measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Green measure"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) — specifically, in [stochastic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_analysis) — the **Green measure** is a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) associated to an [Itō diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō_diffusion). There is an associated **Green formula** representing suitably [smooth functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) in terms of the Green measure and [first exit times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_exit_time) of the diffusion. The concepts are named after the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [George Green](wikipedia://en/243343) and are generalizations of the classical [Green's function](wikipedia://en/311001) and Green formula to the stochastic case using [Dynkin's formula](wikipedia://en/12130843).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

Let *X* be an **R**<sup>*n*</sup>-valued Itō diffusion satisfying an Itō [stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/1361454) of the form

$$\mathrm{d} X_{t} = b(X_{t}) \, \mathrm{d} t + \sigma (X_{t}) \, \mathrm{d} B_{t}.$$

Let **P**<sup>*x*</sup> denote the [law](wikipedia://en/45437) of *X* given the initial condition *X*<sub>0</sub> = *x*, and let **E**<sup>*x*</sup> denote [expectation](wikipedia://en/9653) with respect to **P**<sup>*x*</sup>. Let *L*<sub>*X*</sub> be the [infinitesimal generator](wikipedia://en/12193437) of *X*, i.e.

$$L_{X} = \sum_{i} b_{i} \frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}} + \frac1{2} \sum_{i, j} \big( \sigma \sigma^{\top} \big)_{i, j} \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i} \, \partial x_{j}}.$$

Let *D* ⊆ **R**<sup>*n*</sup> be an [open](wikipedia://en/39358), [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258) domain; let *τ*<sub>*D*</sub> be the [first exit time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_exit_time) of *X* from *D*:

$$\tau_{D} := \inf \{ t \geq 0 | X_{t} \not \in D \}.$$

:::

## The Green measure {#The_Green_measure}

Intuitively, the Green measure of a Borel set *H* (with respect to a point *x* and domain *D*) is the expected length of time that *X*, having started at *x*, stays in *H* before it leaves the domain *D*. That is, the **Green measure** of *X* with respect to *D* at *x*, denoted *G*(*x*, ⋅), is defined for Borel sets *H* ⊆ **R**<sup>*n*</sup> by

$$G(x, H) = \mathbf{E}^{x} \left[ \int_{0}^{\tau_{D}} \chi_{H} (X_{s}) \, \mathrm{d} s \right],$$

or for bounded, continuous functions *f* : *D* → **R** by

$$\int_{D} f(y) \, G(x, \mathrm{d} y) = \mathbf{E}^{x} \left[ \int_{0}^{\tau_{D}} f(X_{s}) \, \mathrm{d} s \right]$$

The name "Green measure" comes from the fact that if *X* is [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436), then

$$G(x, H) = \int_{H} G(x, y) \, \mathrm{d} y,$$

where *G*(*x*, *y*) is Green's function for the operator *L*<sub>*X*</sub> (which, in the case of Brownian motion, is $\tfrac{1}{2}$Δ, where Δ is the [Laplace operator](wikipedia://en/174706)) on the domain *D*.

::: {#o6 .statement cue=named-heading name="The Green formula"}

## The Green formula {#The_Green_formula}

Suppose that **E**<sup>*x*</sup>\[*τ*<sub>*D*</sub>\] \< +∞ for all *x* ∈ *D*, and let *f* : **R**<sup>*n*</sup> → **R** be of [smoothness class *C*<sup>2</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) with [compact support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support). Then

$$f(x) = \mathbf{E}^{x} \big[ f \big( X_{\tau_{D}} \big) \big] - \int_{D} L_{X} f (y) \, G(x, \mathrm{d} y).$$

In particular, for *C*<sup>2</sup> functions *f* with support [compactly embedded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactly_embedded) in *D*,

$$f(x) = - \int_{D} L_{X} f (y) \, G(x, \mathrm{d} y).$$

The proof of Green's formula is an easy application of Dynkin's formula and the definition of the Green measure:

$$\mathbf{E}^{x} \big[ f \big( X_{\tau_{D}} \big) \big]$$

$$= f(x) + \mathbf{E}^{x} \left[ \int_{0}^{\tau_{D}} L_{X} f (X_{s}) \, \mathrm{d} s \right]$$

$$= f(x) + \int_{D} L_{X} f (y) \, G(x, \mathrm{d} y).$$

:::
