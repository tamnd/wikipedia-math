# Susanne Brenner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Susanne Brenner"}

*Image: Susanne C. Brenner Oberwolfach 2018.jpg.* Brenner at [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematisches_Forschungsinstitut_Oberwolfach) in 2018

**Susanne Cecelia Brenner** is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), whose research concerns the [finite element method](wikipedia://en/18233581) and related techniques for the [numerical solution](wikipedia://en/21506) of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). She is a Boyd Professor[^o2] at Louisiana State University. Previously, she held the Nicholson Professorship of Mathematics and the Michael F. and Roberta Nesbit McDonald [Professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University),[^o3] She currently chairs the editorial committee of the journal *[Mathematics of Computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_Computation)*.[^o4] During 2021-2022 she is serving as President of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Brenner did her undergraduate studies in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and German at [West Chester State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Chester_University) and received a master's degree in mathematics from [SUNY Stony Brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University).[^o6] She obtained her [Ph.D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy). from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1988 under the joint supervision of [Jeffrey Rauch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Rauch) and L. Ridgway Scott; her thesis was entitled "Multigrid Methods for Nonconforming Finite Elements".

She held faculty positions at [Clarkson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarkson_University) and the [University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina) before moving to Louisiana State University in 2006.[^o6][^o8]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- $C^0$interior penalty methods for fourth order elliptic boundary value problems on polygonal domains. *J. Sci. Comput.* 22/23 (2005), 83–118.
- Korn's inequalities for piecewise vector fields. *Math. Comp.* 73 (2004), no. 247, 1067–1087.
- Poincaré-Friedrichs inequalities for piecewise $H^1$functions. *SIAM J. Numer. Anal.* 41 (2003), no. 1, 306–324.
- with L. R. Scott, *The Mathematical Theory of Finite Element Methods* ([Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Science+Business_Media), 1994; 3rd edition, 2008).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

She is a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376),[^o11] the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822),[^o12] and the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science).[^o13] The [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) has included her in the 2020 class of AWM Fellows for "being a role model nationally and internationally due to her widely-known work in finite element methods; for her promotion of women in mathematics via the Women in Numerical Analysis and Scientific Computing network, as mentor of Ph.D.s, and as advisor of graduate and undergraduate students".[^o14] Brenner was also awarded a Humboldt Forschungspreis ([Humboldt Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Research_Award)) from the [Alexander von Humboldt Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation) in 2005.[^o15]

She is included in a deck of playing cards featuring notable women mathematicians published by the [Association of Women in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Women_in_Mathematics).[^o16]
