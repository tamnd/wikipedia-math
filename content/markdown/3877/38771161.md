# Onsager–Machlup function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Onsager–Machlup function"}

The **Onsager–Machlup function** is a function that summarizes the dynamics of a [continuous stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895#Continuous_time_and_continuous_state_space). It is used to define a probability density for a stochastic process, and it is similar to the [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/23371726) of a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087). It is named after [Lars Onsager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Onsager) and Stefan Machlup who were the first to consider such probability densities.[^o2]

:::

The dynamics of a continuous stochastic process $X$ from time $t = 0$ to $t = T$ in one dimension, satisfying a [stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/1361454)

$$dX_t = b(X_t)\,dt + \sigma(X_t)\,dW_t$$

where $W$ is a [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984), can in approximation be described by the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) of its value $x_{i}$ at a finite number of points in time $t_{i}$:

$$p(x_1,\ldots,x_n) = \left( \prod^{n-1}_{i=1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma(x_i)^2\Delta t_i}} \right) \exp\left(-\sum^{n-1}_{i=1} L\left(x_i,\frac{x_{i+1}-x_i}{\Delta t_i}\right) \, \Delta t_i \right)$$

where

$$L(x,v) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{v - b(x)}{\sigma(x)}\right)^2$$

and $\Delta t_{i} = t_{i+1} - t_{i} > 0$, $t_{1} = 0$ and $t_{n} = T$. A similar approximation is possible for processes in higher dimensions. The approximation is more accurate for smaller time step sizes $\Delta t_{i}$, but in the limit $\Delta t_{i} \to 0$ the probability density function becomes ill-defined, one reason being that the product of terms

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma(x_i)^2\Delta t_i}}$$

[diverges to infinity](wikipedia://en/285773#Infinite_limits). In order to nevertheless define a density for the continuous stochastic process $X$, [ratios](wikipedia://en/87837) of probabilities of $X$ lying within a small distance $\varepsilon$ from [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) curves $\varphi_{1}$ and $\varphi_{2}$ are considered:[^o3]

$$\frac{P\left( \left |X_t - \varphi_1(t) \right| \leq \varepsilon \text{ for every }t\in[0,T] \right)}{P\left( \left |X_t - \varphi_2(t) \right | \leq \varepsilon \text{ for every }t\in[0,T] \right)} \to \exp\left(-\int^T_0 L \left (\varphi_1(t),\dot{\varphi}_1(t) \right ) \, dt + \int^T_0 L \left (\varphi_2(t),\dot{\varphi}_2(t) \right) \, dt \right)$$

as $\varepsilon \to 0$, where $L$ is the **Onsager–Machlup function**.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Consider a $d$-dimensional [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $M$ and a [diffusion process](wikipedia://en/1667059) $X = \{X_{t} : 0 \le t \le T\}$ on $M$ with [infinitesimal generator](wikipedia://en/12193437) $\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{M} + b$, where $\Delta_{M}$ is the [Laplace–Beltrami operator](wikipedia://en/1763356) and $b$ is a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641). For any two [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) curves $\varphi_{1}, \varphi_{2} : [0, T] \to M$,

$$\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0} \frac{P\left( \rho(X_t,\varphi_1(t)) \leq \varepsilon \text{ for every }t\in[0,T] \right)}{P\left( \rho(X_t,\varphi_2(t)) \leq \varepsilon \text{ for every }t\in[0,T] \right)} = \exp\left( -\int^T_0 L \left (\varphi_1(t),\dot{\varphi}_1(t) \right ) \, dt +\int^T_0 L \left (\varphi_2(t),\dot{\varphi}_2(t) \right ) \, dt \right)$$

where $\rho$ is the [Riemannian distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29), $\scriptstyle \dot{\varphi}_1, \dot{\varphi}_2$ denote the first [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921) of $\varphi_{1}, \varphi_{2}$, and $L$ is called the **Onsager–Machlup function**.

The Onsager–Machlup function is given by[^o6][^o7][^o8]

$$L(x,v) = \tfrac{1}{2}\|v-b(x)\|_x^2 +\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{div}\, b(x) - \tfrac{1}{12}R(x),$$

where $|| \cdot ||_{x}$ is the Riemannian norm in the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) $T_{x}(M)$ at $x$, $\operatorname{div} b(x)$ is the [divergence](wikipedia://en/8328) of $b$ at $x$, and $R(x)$ is the [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) at $x$.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

The following examples give explicit expressions for the Onsager–Machlup function of a continuous stochastic processes.

### Wiener process on the real line {#Wiener_process_on_the_real_line}

The Onsager–Machlup function of a [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984) on the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) $\mathbf{R}$ is given by[^o12]

$$L(x,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^2.$$

Proof: Let $X = \{X_{t} : 0 \le t \le T\}$ be a Wiener process on $\mathbf{R}$ and let $\varphi : [0, T] \to \mathbf{R}$ be a twice [differentiable curve](wikipedia://en/493403) such that $\varphi(0) = X_{0}$. Define another process $X^{\varphi} = \{X_{t}^{\varphi} : 0 \le t \le T\}$ by $X_{t}^{\varphi} = X_{t} - \varphi(t)$ and a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) $P^{\varphi}$ by

$$P^\varphi = \exp\left( \int^T_0\dot{\varphi}(t) \, dX^\varphi_t + \int^T_0\tfrac{1}{2} \left |\dot{\varphi}(t) \right |^2 \, dt \right) \, dP.$$

For every $\varepsilon > 0$, the probability that $|X_{t} - \varphi(t)| \le \varepsilon$ for every $t \in [0, T]$ satisfies

$$\begin{aligned} P \left ( \left |X_t-\varphi(t) \right |\leq\varepsilon \text{ for every }t\in[0,T] \right ) &=P\left ( \left |X^\varphi_t \right|\leq\varepsilon \text{ for every }t\in[0,T] \right) \\ &=\int_{\left \{ \left |X^\varphi_t \right |\leq\varepsilon\text{ for every }t\in[0,T] \right\}} \exp\left( -\int^T_0\dot{\varphi}(t) \, dX^\varphi_t -\int^T_0\tfrac{1}{2}|\dot{\varphi}(t)|^2 \, dt \right) \, dP^\varphi. \end{aligned}$$

By [Girsanov's theorem](wikipedia://en/336940), the distribution of $X^{\varphi}$ under $P^{\varphi}$ equals the distribution of $X$ under $P$, hence the latter can be substituted by the former:

$$P(|X_t-\varphi(t)|\leq\varepsilon \text{ for every }t\in[0,T])=\int_{\left \{ \left |X^\varphi_t \right |\leq\varepsilon\text{ for every }t\in[0,T] \right\}} \exp\left( -\int^T_0\dot{\varphi}(t) \, dX_t -\int^T_0\tfrac{1}{2}|\dot{\varphi}(t)|^2 \, dt \right) \, dP.$$

By [Itō's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō's_lemma) it holds that

$$\int^T_0\dot{\varphi}(t) \, dX_t = \dot{\varphi}(T)X_T - \int^T_0\ddot{\varphi}(t)X_t \, dt,$$

where $\scriptstyle \ddot{\varphi}$ is the second derivative of $\varphi$, and so this term is of order $\varepsilon$ on the event where $|X_{t}| \le \varepsilon$ for every $t \in [0, T]$ and will disappear in the limit $\varepsilon \to 0$, hence

$$\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0} \frac{P(|X_t-\varphi(t)|\leq\varepsilon \text{ for every }t\in[0,T])}{P(|X_t|\leq\varepsilon\text{ for every } t \in [0,T])} =\exp\left( -\int^T_0\tfrac{1}{2}|\dot{\varphi}(t)|^2 \, dt \right).$$

### Diffusion processes with constant diffusion coefficient on Euclidean space {#Diffusion_processes_with_constant_diffusion_coefficient_on_Euclidean_space}

The Onsager–Machlup function in the one-dimensional case with constant [diffusion coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō_diffusion) $\sigma$ is given by[^o14]

$$L(x,v)=\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{v-b(x)}{\sigma}\right|^2 + \frac{1}{2}\frac{db}{dx}(x).$$

In the $d$-dimensional case, with $\sigma$ equal to the [unit matrix](wikipedia://en/59718), it is given by[^o15]

$$L(x,v)=\frac{1}{2}\|v-b(x)\|^2 + \frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{div}\, b)(x),$$

where $|| \cdot ||$ is the [Euclidean norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm) and

$$(\operatorname{div}\, b)(x) = \sum_{i=1}^d \frac{\partial}{\partial x_i} b_i(x).$$

:::

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Generalizations have been obtained by weakening the differentiability condition on the curve $\varphi$.[^o18] Rather than taking the maximum distance between the stochastic process and the curve over a time interval, other conditions have been considered such as distances based on completely convex norms[^o19] and Hölder, Besov and Sobolev type norms.[^o20]

:::

::: {#o22 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Onsager–Machlup function can be used for purposes of reweighting and [sampling](wikipedia://en/160361) trajectories,[^o23] as well as for determining the most probable trajectory of a diffusion process.[^o24][^o25]
