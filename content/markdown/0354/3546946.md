# Giulio Pace

*Image: Pacius.png.* Giulio Pace

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Giulio Pace"}

**Giulio Pace de Beriga**, also known as **Giulio Pacio**, or by his Latin name **Julius Pacius of Beriga** (9 April 1550 – 1635) was a well-known Italian [Aristotelian](wikipedia://en/308) scholar and jurist.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Vicenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicenza), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy), and studied [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law) and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) in [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua).

He was deeply moved by the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation). The [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) considered him immoral and a lover of [heretical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heresy) writings and he found himself put on [trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_%28law%29) by the [Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inquisition). Fleeing first to [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva) to escape their wrath, he soon converted to [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) while in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg).

His academic career was wide and varied, as he became an itinerant figure. In Geneva, he was elected as a public professor, and taught from 1575 to 1585. Studious and having a deep knowledge of [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), he translated Aristotle. He taught law at the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg), from 1585 to 1594. Incidentally, while he was at Heidelberg, his quarrels with compatriot [Scipione Gentili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipione_Gentili) ultimately compelled the latter to leave for [Altdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Altdorf). Also while there he showed his [Ramist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramist) sympathies, but came into conflict with the philosophical faculty for trying to give private tuition in Ramist logic.[^o3] After spending 1595 teaching logic at the [Academy of Sedan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sedan), he was [provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provost_%28education%29) and prefect of studies at the [University of Nimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nimes), from 1597 to 1600. He also taught philosophy in [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary), [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden) in the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands), [Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenoble) in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), and in other places. At the [University of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montpellier), where he was from 1600 to 1616, [Nicolas Claude Fabri de Peiresc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Claude_Fabri_de_Peiresc) was his pupil.[^o4] He also taught at the [University of Valence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Valence), 1620-1, before taking a position at Padua where he taught from 1621 to 1635.[^o5]

## Works {#Works}

*Image: Pace, Giulio – De dominio maris Hadriatici, 1619 – BEIC 13725163.jpg.* *De dominio maris Hadriatici*, 1619

Pace's edition of the *[Organon](wikipedia://en/499019)* became standard during its life of 11 editions between 1584 and 1623, and even longer in the north of Europe. He also wrote a great many legal titles.

Pace wrote a pocket summary of [Ramon Llull](wikipedia://en/69677)'s art, first in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) in 1618 and then in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) in 1619. This work is seen as exceptional among its contemporaries as it does not digress into [alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy), [cabalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Kabbalah), or [magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_%28paranormal%29), and in fact stays true to Llull's traditional interests. Pace wrote a similar work for [Ramus](wikipedia://en/311650) as well.

Despite being a Protestant, in 1619, he published "De dominio maris Hadriatici dissertatio", defending the claims of the [Venetian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Republic) to dominion over the [Adriatic Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic_Sea), against the opposite claims of the Empire and the [Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples). This controversy in early international law lined him up with [Paolo Sarpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Sarpi), against [Juan Bautista Valenzuela Velázquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Bautista_Valenzuela_Velázquez) and Lorenzo Motino.[^o7][^o8]

A book was written on the life of Giulio Pace in the early 20th century, focusing on his work as a jurist.[^o9]
