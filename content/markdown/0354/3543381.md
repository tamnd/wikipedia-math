# Kato's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kato's conjecture"}

**Kato's conjecture** is a [mathematical problem](wikipedia://en/256700) named after [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Tosio Kato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tosio_Kato), of the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). Kato initially posed the problem in 1953.[^o2]

:::

Kato asked whether the square roots of certain [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137), defined via [functional calculus](wikipedia://en/965348), are [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478). The full statement of the conjecture as given by Auscher *et al.* is: "the domain of the square root of a uniformly complex elliptic operator $L =-\mathrm{div} (A\nabla)$ with bounded measurable coefficients in **R**<sup>n</sup> is the Sobolev space *H*<sup>1</sup>(**R**<sup>n</sup>) in any dimension with the estimate $||\sqrt{L}f||_{2} \sim ||\nabla f||_{2}$".[^o3]

The problem remained unresolved for nearly a half-century, until in 2001 it was jointly solved in the affirmative by [Pascal Auscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_Auscher), [Steve Hofmann](wikipedia://en/7717092), [Michael Lacey](wikipedia://en/6508239), [Alan McIntosh](wikipedia://en/57297191), and [Philippe Tchamitchian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Tchamitchian).[^o3]
