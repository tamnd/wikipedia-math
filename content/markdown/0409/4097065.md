# Adrian Smith (statistician)

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Adrian Smith"}

**Sir Adrian Frederick Melhuish Smith**, [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) (born 9 September 1946) is a British statistician who was chief executive of the [Alan Turing Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing_Institute) from 2018 to 2023 and president of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) from 2020 to 2025.[^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Smith was born on 9 September 1946 in [Dawlish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawlish) in Devon. He was educated at [Selwyn College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selwyn_College,_Cambridge), and [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), where his PhD supervisor was [Dennis Lindley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Lindley).

## Career {#Career}

From 1977 until 1990, he was professor of statistics and head of department of mathematics at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham). He was subsequently at [Imperial College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College,_London), London, where he was head of the mathematics department. Smith is a former deputy vice-chancellor of the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) and became vice-chancellor of the university on 1 September 2012.[^o8] He stood down from the role in August 2018 to become the director of the [Alan Turing Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing_Institute).[^o9][^o10]

Smith is a member of the governing body of the [London Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Business_School). He served on the Advisory Council for the [Office for National Statistics](wikipedia://en/324029) from 1996 to 1998, was statistical advisor to the Nuclear Waste Inspectorate from 1991 to 1998 and was advisor on [Operational Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_research) to the Ministry of Defence from 1982 to 1987.

He is a former president of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664). He was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 2001. His FRS citation included "his diverse contributions to [Bayesian statistics](wikipedia://en/404412). His monographs are the most comprehensive available and his work has had a major impact on the development of monitoring tools for clinicians."

In [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579), Smith is a proponent of [Bayesian statistics](wikipedia://en/404412) and [evidence-based practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence-based_practice)—a general extension of [evidence-based medicine](wikipedia://en/10013) into all areas of public policy. With Antonio Machi, he translated [Bruno de Finetti](wikipedia://en/180825)'s Theory of Probability into English. He wrote an influential paper in 1990 along with [Alan E. Gelfand](wikipedia://en/53438362), which drew attention to the significance of the [Gibbs sampler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbs_sampler) technique for Bayesian [numerical integration](wikipedia://en/170089) problems. He was also co-author of the seminal paper on the [particle filter](wikipedia://en/1396948) (Gordon, Salmond and Smith, 1993).

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/326471) and [statistics education](wikipedia://en/24985094), Smith led the team which produced the [Smith Report](wikipedia://en/27354075) on secondary mathematics education in the United Kingdom.

In April 2008, Smith was appointed as director general of science and research at the [Department for Innovation, Universities and Skills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Innovation,_Universities_and_Skills) (since merged with other departments to form the UK's [BEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Business,_Energy_and_Industrial_Strategy)). He took up his post in September 2008. His annual remuneration for this role was £160,000.[^o11]

Smith was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor) in the [2011 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_New_Year_Honours). In 2023, he was a guest on *[The Life Scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_Scientific)* on [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4).[^o13]

## Honorary doctorates {#Honorary_doctorates}

In 2011, Smith was awarded an honorary doctorate of science from the [University of Plymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Plymouth) in 2015, an honorary doctorate of science from [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University),[^o15] and in 2020, an honorary doctorate from [Federal University of Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_University_of_Rio_de_Janeiro). He also was awarded honorary doctorates from City University, the [University of Loughborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Loughborough), Queen Mary and the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London).
