# R. H. Bruck

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="R. H. Bruck"}

**Richard Hubert Bruck** (December 26, 1914 – December 18, 1991) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best known for his work in the field of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), especially in its relation to [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Bruck studied at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto), where he received his doctorate in 1940 under the supervision of [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134). He spent most his career as a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison), advising at least 31 doctoral students.

He is best known for his 1949 paper coauthored with [H. J. Ryser](wikipedia://en/20825197), the results of which became known as the [Bruck–Ryser theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruck–Ryser_theorem) (now known in a generalized form as the [Bruck-Ryser-Chowla theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruck-Ryser-Chowla_theorem)), concerning the possible [orders](wikipedia://en/267061#Order_of_planes) of [finite projective planes](wikipedia://en/24350#Finite_projective_planes).

In 1946, he was awarded a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship). In 1956, he was awarded the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) for his article [*Recent Advances in the Foundations of Euclidean Plane Geometry*](http://www.maa.org/programs/maa-awards/writing-awards/recent-advances-in-the-foundations-of-euclidean-plane-geometry).[^o5] In 1962, he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm). In 1963, he was a [Fulbright Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Lecturer) at the [University of Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Canberra). In 1965 a *Groups and Geometry* conference was held at the University of Wisconsin in honor of Bruck's retirement.

Dick Bruck and his wife Helen were supporters of the fine arts. They were patrons of the regional *American Players Theatre* in Wisconsin.[^o6]
