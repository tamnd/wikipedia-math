# Kumaraswamy distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kumaraswamy distribution"}

In [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Kumaraswamy's double bounded distribution** is a family of [continuous probability distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_probability_distribution) defined on the interval (0,1). It is similar to the [beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074), but much simpler to use especially in simulation studies since its [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487), [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) and quantile functions can be expressed in [closed form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed-form_solution). This distribution was originally proposed by [Poondi Kumaraswamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poondi_Kumaraswamy)[^o2] for variables that are lower and upper bounded with a zero-inflation. In this first article of the distribution, the natural lower bound of zero for rainfall was modelled using a discrete probability, as rainfall in many places, especially in tropics, has significant nonzero probability. This discrete probability is now called zero-inflation. This was extended to inflations at both extremes \[0,1\] in the work of Fletcher and Ponnambalam.[^o3] A good example for inflations at extremes are the probabilities of full and empty reservoirs and are important for reservoir design.

:::

## Characterization {#Characterization}

### Probability density function {#Probability_density_function}

The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) of the Kumaraswamy distribution without considering any inflation is

$$f(x; a,b) = a b x^{a-1}{ (1-x^a)}^{b-1}, \ \ \mbox{where} \ \ x \in (0,1),$$

and where *a* and *b* are non-negative [shape parameters](wikipedia://en/9210865).

### Cumulative distribution function {#Cumulative_distribution_function}

The [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) is

$$F(x; a,b)=\int_{0}^{x}f(\xi; a,b) d\xi = 1-(1-x^a)^b.\$$

### Quantile function {#Quantile_function}

The inverse cumulative distribution function (quantile function) is

$$F^{-1}(y; a,b)=(1-(1-y)^\frac{1}{b})^\frac{1}{a}. \$$

### Generalizing to arbitrary interval support {#Generalizing_to_arbitrary_interval_support}

In its simplest form, the distribution has a support of (0,1). In a more general form, the normalized variable *x* is replaced with the unshifted and unscaled variable *z* where:

$$x = \frac{z-z_{\text{min}}}{z_{\text{max}}-z_{\text{min}}} , \qquad z_{\text{min}} \le z \le z_{\text{max}}. \,\!$$

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The raw [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of the Kumaraswamy distribution are given by:[^o12][^o13]

$$m_n = \frac{b\Gamma(1+n/a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(1+b+n/a)} = bB(1+n/a,b)\,$$

where *B* is the [Beta function](wikipedia://en/245660) and Γ(.) denotes the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316). The variance, [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212), and [excess kurtosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excess_kurtosis) can be calculated from these raw moments. For example, the variance is:

$$\sigma^2=m_2-m_1^2.$$

The [Shannon entropy](wikipedia://en/15445) (in nats) of the distribution is:[^o14]

$$H=\left(1\!-\!\tfrac{1}{b}\right)+\left(1\!-\!\tfrac{1}{a}\right)H_b-\ln(ab)$$

where $H_i$ is the [harmonic number](wikipedia://en/214729) function.

:::

## Relation to the Beta distribution {#Relation_to_the_Beta_distribution}

The Kumaraswamy distribution is closely related to Beta distribution.[^o16] Assume that *X*<sub>a,b</sub> is a Kumaraswamy distributed [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with parameters *a* and *b*. Then *X*<sub>a,b</sub> is the *a*-th root of a suitably defined Beta distributed random variable. More formally, Let *Y*<sub>1,b</sub> denote a [Beta distributed](wikipedia://en/207074) random variable with parameters $\alpha=1$ and $\beta=b$. One has the following relation between *X*<sub>a,b</sub> and *Y*<sub>1,b</sub>.

$$X_{a,b}=Y^{1/a}_{1,b},$$

with equality in distribution.

$$\operatorname{P}\{X_{a,b}\le x\}=\int_0^x ab t^{a-1}(1-t^a)^{b-1}dt= \int_0^{x^a} b(1-t)^{b-1}dt=\operatorname{P}\{Y_{1,b}\le x^a\} =\operatorname{P}\{Y^{1/a}_{1,b}\le x\} .$$

One may introduce generalised Kumaraswamy distributions by considering random variables of the form $Y^{1/\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}$, with $\gamma>0$ and where $Y_{\alpha,\beta}$ denotes a Beta distributed random variable with parameters $\alpha$ and $\beta$. The raw [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of this generalized Kumaraswamy distribution are given by:

$$m_n = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)\Gamma(\alpha+n/\gamma)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+n/\gamma)}.$$

Note that we can re-obtain the original moments setting $\alpha=1$, $\beta=b$ and $\gamma=a$. However, in general, the cumulative distribution function does not have a closed form solution.

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

- If $X \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(1,1)\,$ then $X \sim U(0,1)\,$ ([Uniform distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29))
- If $X \sim U(0,1) \,$ then ${\left( 1-X^{\tfrac{1}{b}} \right)}^{ \tfrac{1}{a}} \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(a,b) \,$[^o16]
- If $X \sim \textrm{Beta}(1,b) \,$ ([Beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074)) then $X \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(1,b)\,$
- If $X \sim \textrm{Beta}(a,1) \,$ ([Beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074)) then $X \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(a,1)\,$
- If $X \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(a,1)\,$ then $(1-X) \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(1, a)\,$
- If $X \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(1,a)\,$ then $(1-X) \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(a, 1)\,$
- If $X \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(a,1)\,$ then $-\log(X) \sim \textrm{Exponential}(a)\,$
- If $X \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(1,b)\,$ then $-\log(1-X) \sim \textrm{Exponential}(b)\,$
- If $X \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(a,b)\,$ then $X \sim \textrm{GB1}(a, 1, 1, b)\,$, the [generalized beta distribution of the first kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Beta_distribution#Related_distributions)
- If $X \sim \textrm{Kumaraswamy}(a,b)\,$ then $\left\{\frac{1}{1-X} \right\} \sim \textrm{MK}(a, b)\,$, the [Modified Kumaraswamy distribution](wikipedia://en/77810504#Related_distributions).

::: {#o19 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

An example of the use of the Kumaraswamy distribution is the storage volume of a reservoir of capacity *z* whose upper bound is *z*<sub>max</sub> and lower bound is 0, which is also a natural example for having two inflations as many reservoirs have nonzero probabilities for both empty and full reservoir states.[^o3]
