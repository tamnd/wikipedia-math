# Krzysztof Gawedzki

::: {#o7 .subject cue=lead name="Krzysztof Gawedzki"}

**Krzysztof Gawędzki** (Polish pronunciation: ';[^o8] born 2 July 1947 – died 21 January 2022) was a Polish[^o9][^o10] [mathematical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physicist), a graduate of the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) and professor at the [École normale supérieure de Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_Lyon) (ENS de Lyon). He was primarily known for his research on [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) and [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics).[^o11] In 2022, he shared the [Dannie Heineman Prize for Mathematical Physics](wikipedia://en/4854281) with [Antti Kupiainen](wikipedia://en/48334698).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Born in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Republic), Gawędzki received in 1971 his doctorate from the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw).[^o13] His doctoral dissertation *Functional theory of geodesic fields* was supervised by Krzysztof Maurin (1923–2017).

In the 1980s Gawędzki did research at [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS) at the [IHES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHES) near Paris. Since the 1990s, he was a professor at the [École normale supérieure de Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_Lyon) (ENS de Lyon), and later an emeritus researcher there.[^o15]

He was known for his research in the mathematics of [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) (QFT), especially [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/364774).[^o16] In the 1980s he collaborated with [Antti Kupiainen](wikipedia://en/48334698) on the application of the [renormalization group](wikipedia://en/291462) method in the rigorous mathematical treatment of various model systems of quantum field theory.[^o17][^o18] Much of their research deals with [conformal field theories](wikipedia://en/364774), which serve as two-dimensional [toy models](wikipedia://en/701142) of non-perturbative aspects of QFT (with applications to [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) and [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481)). Gawędzki and collaborators studied the geometry of [WZW models](wikipedia://en/1593924) (also called WZNW models, Wess-Zumino-Novikov-Witten models), prototypes for rational conformal field theories.[^o19][^o20][^o21][^o22]

With Kupiainen he succeeded in the 1980s in the rigorous construction of the massless lattice $\phi^4_4$ model in four dimensions and the [Gross-Neveu model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross-Neveu_model) in two space-time dimensions.[^o23][^o24] At about the same time, this was achieved by Roland Sénéor, Jacques Magnen, [Joel Feldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Feldman) and [Vincent Rivasseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Rivasseau). This was considered an outstanding achievement in constructive quantum field theory.

In 1986 Gawędzki[^o25] identified the [Kalb–Ramond field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalb–Ramond_field) (B field), which generalizes the electromagnetic field from point particles to [strings](wikipedia://en/28305), as a degree-3 cocycle in the Deligne cohomology model.[^o26]

In the 2000s he did research on [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664),[^o27] partly in collaboration with Kupiainen. In 1995 Gawędzki and Kupiainen demonstrated anomalous scaling behavior of scalar advection in random vector field models of homogeneous turbulence.[^o28]

From January to June 2003 he was at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o13] In 1986 he was invited speaker with talk *Renormalization: from magic to mathematics* at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California).[^o29] In 2007 at ENS de Lyon a conference on mathematical physics was held in honor of his 60th birthday.[^o30] In 2017 at the [University of Nice Sophia Antipolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nice_Sophia_Antipolis) a conference on mathematical physics was held in honor of his 70th birthday.[^o31]

On 24 November 2021, the American Institute of Physics and the American Physical Society announced Krzysztof Gawędzki and Antti Kupiainen as the recipients of the 2022 [Dannie Heineman Prize for Mathematical Physics](wikipedia://en/4854281).[^o32] They were recognized for their "fundamental contributions to quantum field theory, statistical mechanics, and fluid dynamics using geometric, probabilistic, and renormalization group ideas."[^o33]

He died in [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon), France on 21 January 2022, at the age of 74.[^o34]
