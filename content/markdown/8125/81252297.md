# Miklós Csörgő

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Miklós Csörgő"}

**Miklós Csörgő** (born 12 March 1932 in [Egerfarmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egerfarmos)) is a Hungarian [probabilist](wikipedia://en/23542) and statistician known for his contributions to the theory of [empirical processes](wikipedia://en/3690433) and several other fields.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Csörgő obtained a Bachelor's degree in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) in Budapest in 1955. He arrived in Canada in January 1957 as a refugee of the suppressed [Hungarian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956). After a variety of odd jobs, in 1959 he enrolled at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University) in Montreal where he earned M.A. and Ph.D. degrees in 1961 and 1963 respectively.[^o5]

After graduating, he held positions as postdoctoral fellow and lecturer at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) for two years. In 1965 he returned to McGill University to take up a position as assistant professor; he was promoted to associate professor there in 1968. In 1972, he moved to [Carleton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_University) in Ottawa as a professor of mathematics and statistics, where he became Distinguished Research Professor after his retirement.[^o6]

One of his students was his brother, [Sándor Csörgő](wikipedia://en/1988234), who was fifteen years his junior.[^o7]
