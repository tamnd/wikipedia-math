# Tilting theory

It turns out that there are applications of our functors which make use of the analogous transformations which we like to think of as a change of basis for a fixed root-system — a tilting of the axes relative to the roots which results in a different subset of roots lying in the positive cone. ... For this reason, and because the word 'tilt' inflects easily, we call our functors tilting functors or simply tilts.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tilting theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), **tilting theory** describes a way to relate the [module categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_category) of two algebras using so-called **tilting modules** and associated **tilting functors**. Here, the second algebra is the [endomorphism algebra](wikipedia://en/59623) of a tilting module over the first algebra.

:::

Tilting theory was motivated by the introduction of reflection [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) by (txt); these functors were used to relate [representations](wikipedia://en/355100#Representations_of_quivers) of two [quivers](wikipedia://en/355100). These functors were reformulated by (txt), and generalized by (txt) who introduced tilting functors. (txt) defined tilted algebras and tilting modules as further generalizations of this.

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Suppose that *A* is a finite-dimensional [unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra) [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) over some [field](wikipedia://en/10603). A [finitely-generated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely-generated_module) right *A*-[module](wikipedia://en/276410) *T* is called a **tilting module** if it has the following three properties:

- *T* has [projective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_dimension) at most 1, in other words it is a [quotient](wikipedia://en/364820) of a [projective module](wikipedia://en/364488) by a projective [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule).
- [Ext](wikipedia://en/840758)${}^{1}_{A}$(*T*,*T* ) = 0.
- The right *A*-module *A* is the [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) of a [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) morphism between finite direct sums of direct summands of *T*.

Given such a tilting module, we define the [endomorphism algebra](wikipedia://en/59623) *B* = End<sub>*A*</sub>(*T* ). This is another finite-dimensional algebra, and *T* is a finitely-generated left *B*-module. The **tilting functors** Hom<sub>*A*</sub>(*T*,−), Ext${}^{1}_{A}$(*T*,−), −⊗<sub>*B*</sub>*T* and [Tor](wikipedia://en/856347)${}^{B}_{1}$(−,*T*) relate the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) mod-*A* of finitely-generated right *A*-modules to the category mod-*B* of finitely-generated right *B*-modules.

In practice one often considers [hereditary](wikipedia://en/5123176) finite-dimensional algebras *A* because the module categories over such algebras are fairly well understood. The endomorphism algebra of a tilting module over a hereditary finite-dimensional algebra is called a **tilted algebra**.

:::

## Facts {#Facts}

Suppose *A* is a finite-dimensional algebra, *T* is a tilting module over *A*, and *B* = End<sub>*A*</sub>(*T* ). Write *F* = Hom<sub>*A*</sub>(*T*,−), *F′* = Ext${}^{1}_{A}$(*T*,−), *G* = −⊗<sub>*B*</sub>*T*, and *G′* = Tor${}^{B}_{1}$(−,*T*). *F* is [right adjoint](wikipedia://en/53991) to *G* and *F′* is right adjoint to *G′*.

(Brenner Butler 1980) showed that tilting functors give [equivalences](wikipedia://en/379051) between certain [subcategories](wikipedia://en/310959) of mod-*A* and mod-*B*. Specifically, if we define the two subcategories $\mathcal{F}=\ker(F)$ and $\mathcal{T}=\ker(F')$ of *A*-mod, and the two subcategories $\mathcal{X}=\ker(G)$ and $\mathcal{Y}=\ker(G')$ of *B*-mod, then $(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ is a [torsion pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_pair) in *A*-mod (i.e. $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ are maximal subcategories with the property $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})=0$; this implies that every *M* in *A*-mod admits a natural [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) $0 \to U \to M \to V \to 0$ with *U* in $\mathcal{T}$ and *V* in $\mathcal{F}$) and $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})$ is a torsion pair in *B*-mod. Further, the restrictions of the functors *F* and *G* yield inverse equivalences between $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{Y}$, while the restrictions of *F′* and *G′* yield inverse equivalences between $\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathcal{X}$. (Note that these equivalences switch the order of the torsion pairs $(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})$ and $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})$.)

Tilting theory may be seen as a generalization of [Morita equivalence](wikipedia://en/3296134) which is recovered if *T* is a [projective generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_cogenerator); in that case $\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{mod}-A$ and $\mathcal{Y}=\operatorname{mod}-B$.

If *A* has finite [global dimension](wikipedia://en/2291739), then *B* also has finite global dimension, and the difference of *F* and *F**induces an isometry between the [Grothendieck groups](wikipedia://en/1634790) K<sub>0</sub>(*****A*) and K<sub>0</sub>(*B*).***

In case *A* is hereditary (i.e. *B* is a tilted algebra), the global dimension of *B* is at most 2, and the torsion pair $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})$ splits, i.e. every indecomposable object of *B*-mod is either in $\mathcal{X}$ or in $\mathcal{Y}$.

(Happel 1988) and (Cline Parshall Scott 1986) showed that in general *A* and *B* are derived equivalent (i.e. the [derived categories](wikipedia://en/985897) D<sup>b</sup>(*A*-mod) and D<sup>b</sup>(*B*-mod) are equivalent as [triangulated categories](wikipedia://en/1679700)).

:::

## Generalizations and extensions {#Generalizations_and_extensions}

A **generalized tilting module** over the finite-dimensional algebra *A* is a right *A*-module *T* with the following three properties:

- *T* has finite projective dimension.
- [Ext](wikipedia://en/840758)${}^{i}_{A}$(*T*,*T*) = 0 for all *i* > 0.
- There is an [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) $0 \to A \to T_1 \to\dots\to T_n \to 0$ where the *T<sub>i</sub>* are finite direct sums of direct summands of *T*.

These generalized tilting modules also yield derived equivalences between *A* and *B*, where *B* = End<sub>*A*</sub>(*T* ).

(Rickard 1989) extended the results on derived equivalence by [proving](wikipedia://en/82285) that two finite-dimensional algebras *R* and *S* are derived equivalent if and only if *S* is the endomorphism algebra of a "tilting complex" over *R*. Tilting complexes are generalizations of generalized tilting modules. A version of this theorem is valid for arbitrary [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* and *S*.

(Happel Reiten Smalø 1996) defined tilting objects in hereditary [abelian categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_categories) in which all Hom- and Ext-spaces are finite-dimensional over some [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) *k*. The endomorphism algebras of these tilting objects are the **quasi-tilted algebras**, a generalization of tilted algebras. The quasi-tilted algebras over *k* are precisely the finite-dimensional algebras over *k* of global dimension ≤ 2 such that every indecomposable module either has projective dimension ≤ 1 or [injective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_dimension) ≤ 1. (Happel 2001) classified the hereditary abelian categories that can appear in the above construction.

(Colpi Fuller 2007) defined tilting objects *T* in an arbitrary abelian category *C*; their definition requires that *C* contain the direct sums of arbitrary (possibly infinite) numbers of copies of *T*, so this is not a direct generalization of the finite-dimensional situation considered above. Given such a tilting object with endomorphism ring *R*, they establish tilting functors that provide equivalences between a torsion pair in *C* and a torsion pair in *R*-Mod, the category of *all* *R*-modules.

From the theory of [cluster algebras](wikipedia://en/27038423) came the definition of **cluster category** (from (Buan Marsh Reineke Reiten 2006)) and **cluster tilted algebra** ((Buan Marsh Reiten 2007)) associated to a hereditary algebra *A*. A cluster tilted algebra arises from a tilted algebra as a certain [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246), and the cluster category of *A* summarizes all the module categories of cluster tilted algebras arising from *A*.
