# Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence"}

In mathematics, the **Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence** (named in honor of [W. V. D. Hodge](wikipedia://en/384430) and [Georges de Rham](wikipedia://en/398699)) or **Frölicher spectral sequence** (named after [Alfred Frölicher](wikipedia://en/35026800), who actually discovered it; (txt)) is a [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894) that describes the precise relationship between the [Dolbeault cohomology](wikipedia://en/3189601) and the [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) of a general [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742). On a compact [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538), the sequence degenerates, thereby leading to the [Hodge decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_decomposition) of the de Rham cohomology.

:::

It is a tool in the theory of [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742) for expressing the potential failure of the results of [cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory) that are valid in general only for [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538). A spectral sequence is set up, the [degeneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degeneration_of_a_spectral_sequence) of which would give the results of [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590) and [Dolbeault's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolbeault's_theorem).

## Description of the spectral sequence {#Description_of_the_spectral_sequence}

The [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894) is as follows:

$$H^q(X, \Omega^p) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X, \mathbf C)$$

where *X* is a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742), $H^{p+q}(X, \mathbf C)$ is its cohomology with complex coefficients and the left hand term, which is the $E_1$-page of the spectral sequence, is the cohomology with values in the [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519). The existence of the spectral sequence as stated above follows from the [Poincaré lemma](wikipedia://en/600030), which gives a [quasi-isomorphism](wikipedia://en/3653039) of complexes of sheaves

$$\mathbf C \rightarrow \Omega^* := [\Omega^0 \stackrel d \to \Omega^1 \stackrel d \to \cdots \to \Omega^{\dim X}],$$

together with the usual spectral sequence resulting from a filtered object, in this case the *Hodge filtration*

$$F^p \Omega^* := [\cdots \to 0 \to \Omega^p \to \Omega^{p+1} \to \cdots ]$$

of $\Omega^*$.

## Degeneration {#Degeneration}

The central theorem related to this spectral sequence is that for a compact [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) *X*, for example a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469), the above spectral sequence degenerates at the $E_1$-page. In particular, it gives an isomorphism referred to as the [Hodge decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_decomposition)

$$\bigoplus_{p+q=n} H^p(X, \Omega^q) = H^{n}(X, \mathbf C).$$

The degeneration of the spectral sequence can be shown using [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590).[^o5][^o6] An extension of this degeneration in a relative situation, for a proper smooth map $f: X \to S$, was also shown by Deligne.[^o7]

## Purely algebraic proof {#Purely_algebraic_proof}

For [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety) [proper](wikipedia://en/603782) varieties over a field of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) 0, the spectral sequence can also be written as

$$H^q(X, \Omega^p) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X, \Omega^*),$$

where $\Omega^p$ denotes the sheaf of algebraic differential forms (also known as [Kähler differentials](wikipedia://en/603916)) on *X*, $\Omega^*$ is the (algebraic) [de Rham complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Rham_complex), consisting of the $\Omega^q$ with the differential being the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782). In this guise, all terms in the spectral sequence are of purely algebraic (as opposed to analytic) nature. In particular, the question of the degeneration of this spectral sequence makes sense for varieties over a field of characteristic *p*>0.

(Deligne Illusie 1987) showed that for a smooth proper [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X* over a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088) *k* of positive characteristic *p*, the spectral sequence degenerates, provided that dim(*X*)\<*p* and *X* admits a smooth proper lift over the ring of [Witt vectors](wikipedia://en/5573710) W<sub>2</sub>(*k*) of length two (for example, for *k*=**F**<sub>*p*</sub>, this ring would be **Z**/*p*<sup>2</sup>). Their proof uses the [Cartier isomorphism](wikipedia://en/63077136), which only exists in positive characteristic. This degeneration result in characteristic *p*>0 can then be used to also prove the degeneration for the spectral sequence for *X* over a field of characteristic 0.

## Non-commutative version {#Non-commutative_version}

The de Rham complex and also the de Rham cohomology of a variety admit generalizations to non-commutative geometry. This more general setup studies [dg categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dg_category). To a dg category, one can associate its [Hochschild homology](wikipedia://en/10499629), and also its [periodic cyclic homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_cyclic_homology). When applied to the category of [perfect complexes](wikipedia://en/46630707) on a smooth proper variety *X*, these invariants give back differential forms, respectively, de Rham cohomology of *X*. Kontsevich and Soibelman conjectured in 2009 that for any smooth and proper dg category *C* over a field of characteristic 0, the Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence starting with Hochschild homology and abutting to periodic cyclic homology, degenerates:

$$HH_*(C / k)[u^{\pm 1}] \Rightarrow HP_*(C / k).$$

This conjecture was proved by (Kaledin 2008) and (Kaledin 2016) by adapting the above idea of Deligne and Illusie to the generality of smooth and proper dg-categories. (Mathew 2020) has given a proof of this degeneration using [topological Hochschild homology](wikipedia://en/66192010).
