# Something (concept)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Something"}

**Something** and **anything** are [concepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept) of [existence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existence) in [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology), contrasting with the concept of [nothing](wikipedia://en/72197). Both are used to describe the understanding that what exists is not [nothing](wikipedia://en/72197) without needing to address the existence of [everything](wikipedia://en/17181902). The [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), [David Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lewis_%28philosopher%29), has pointed out that these are *necessarily* vague terms, asserting that "ontological assertions of common sense are correct if the quantifiers—such words as "something" and "anything"—are restricted roughly to ordinary or familiar things."[^o2]

:::

The idea that "something" is the opposite of "nothing" has existed at least since it was proposed by the [Neoplatonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplatonism) philosopher [Porphyry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_%28philosopher%29) in the 3rd century.[^o3] One of the most basic questions of both science and philosophy is: [why is there something rather than nothing at all?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_is_there_anything_at_all)[^o4] A question that follows from this is whether it is ever actually possible for there to be nothing at all, or whether there must always be something.[^o5]

Grammatically, "*something* and *anything* are commonly classified as [pronouns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pronoun), although they do not stand for another noun so clearly as does *[thing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28philosophy%29)* itself, a word always classified as a noun".[^o6]

## In predicate logic {#In_predicate_logic}

In [predicate logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_logic), what is described in layman's terms as "something" can more specifically be regarded as [existential quantification](wikipedia://en/91420), that is, the predication of a property or relation to at least one member of the domain. It is a type of [quantifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantification_%28logic%29), a [logical constant](wikipedia://en/4089175) which is [interpreted](wikipedia://en/14511671) as "there exists," "there is at least one," or "for some." It expresses that a [propositional function](wikipedia://en/229146) can be [satisfied](wikipedia://en/23401166) by at least one [member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Element_%28mathematics%29) of a [domain of discourse](wikipedia://en/229180). In other terms, it is the [predication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_%28mathematical_logic%29) of a [property](wikipedia://en/508057) or [relation](wikipedia://en/3931) to at least one member of the domain. It [asserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_assertion) that a predicate within the [scope](wikipedia://en/147460) of an existential quantifier is true of at least one [value](wikipedia://en/17456938) of a [predicate variable](wikipedia://en/1011332).
