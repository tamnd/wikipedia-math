# Nataša Pavlović

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nataša Pavlović"}

**Nataša Pavlović** is a Serbian mathematician who works as a professor of mathematics at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin). Her research concerns [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034) and nonlinear [dispersive partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/8477417).[^o2] She is known for her work with [Nets Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nets_Katz) pioneering an approach to constructing singularities in equations resembling the [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395), by transferring a finite amount of energy through an infinitely decreasing sequence of time and length scales.[^o3]

:::

Pavlović earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics from the [University of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Belgrade) in 1996, and completed her doctorate from the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago) in 2002 under the joint supervision of [Susan Friedlander](wikipedia://en/44774993) and Nets Katz.[^o2] After temporary positions at the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655), [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), and [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute), she joined the Princeton faculty in 2005, and moved to the University of Texas in 2007.[^o2]

She was a [Sloan Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) from 2008 to 2012. In 2015 she was elected as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o5] From 2013-2015, Pavlović served as a Council Member at Large for the American Mathematical Society.[^o6] She was elected a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2026.[^o7]
