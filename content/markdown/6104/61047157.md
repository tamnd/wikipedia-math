# Kimberly Sellers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kimberly Sellers"}

**Kimberly Flagg Sellers** is an American statistician. She has been the head of the statistics department at [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University) since 2023, where she is the first Black woman in the university's history to lead a science department.[^o3] Previously, Dr. Sellers was a full professor of statistics at [Georgetown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University) and a principal researcher in the Center for Statistical Research and Methodology of the [United States Census Bureau](wikipedia://en/57070), the former chair of the Committee on Women in Statistics of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), a Fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), and an elected member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute). She specializes in [count data](wikipedia://en/15690807) and [statistical dispersion](wikipedia://en/23773247), and is "the leading expert" on the [Conway–Maxwell–Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/15845763) for count data. She has also worked in the medical applications of statistics, and in [image analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_analysis) for [proteomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteomics).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Sellers is [African-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American), and was born in [Washington, DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_DC). Both her parents had advanced degrees; her father, pianist Thomas Flagg, became dean of fine arts at [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University). She set herself the goal of getting a doctorate in the mathematical sciences by the time she was in elementary school, and would read *Black Issues in Higher Education* with her father and look up the number of African-Americans to receive the Ph.D. in the mathematical sciences.

She was a Benjamin Banneker scholar at the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park), with [Raymond L. Johnson](wikipedia://en/53709624) as a mentor, and earned bachelor's and master's degrees in mathematics at Maryland in 1994 and 1998, respectively. As a master's student at Maryland, she became interested in statistics after taking a class on the subject from Piotr Mikulski. Her master's thesis was *Iterative methods for computing mean first passage times of Markov chains*.

She completed her Ph.D. in statistics at [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University) in 2001, supported by the [Gates Millennium Scholars Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gates_Millennium_Scholars_Program) as one of their first cohort of students. Her dissertation was *Vague coherent systems*, with Nozer D. Singpurwalla as her [doctoral advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor).

## Career {#Career}

From 2001 to 2004, Sellers held a visiting assistant professorship and [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) supported postdoctoral scholarship at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), where she worked with [Bill Eddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Eddy) and [Stephen Fienberg](wikipedia://en/2660488). In 2004, she became an assistant professor of [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) and [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997) at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania). She moved to Georgetown in 2006, at a time when the university was beginning a program in statistics, and took a second affiliation with the Census Bureau in 2015.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Sellers became an elected member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute) in 2018. She was named a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 2021.
