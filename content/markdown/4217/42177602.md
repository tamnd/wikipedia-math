# K-groups of a field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="K-groups of a field"}

In mathematics, especially in [algebraic *K*-theory](wikipedia://en/598500), the **algebraic *K*-group of a field** is important to compute. For a finite field, the complete calculation was given by [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519).

:::

## Low degrees {#Low_degrees}

The map sending a finite-dimensional *F*-vector space to its dimension induces an isomorphism

$$K_0(F) \cong \mathbf Z$$

for any field *F*. Next,

$$K_1(F) = F^\times,$$

the [multiplicative group](wikipedia://en/1110742) of *F*.[^o3] The second K-group of a field is described in terms of generators and relations by [Matsumoto's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsumoto's_theorem_%28K-theory%29).

## Finite fields {#Finite_fields}

The K-groups of finite fields are one of the few cases where the K-theory is known completely:[^o5] for $n \ge 1$,

$$K_n(\mathbb{F}_q) = \pi_n(BGL(\mathbb{F}_q)^+) \simeq \begin{cases} \mathbb{Z}/{(q^i - 1)}, & \text{if }n = 2i - 1 \\ 0, & \text{if }n\text{ is even} \end{cases}$$

For *n*=2, this can be seen from Matsumoto's theorem, in higher degrees it was computed by Quillen in conjunction with his work on the [Adams conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams_conjecture). A different proof was given by (Jardine 1993).

## Local and global fields {#Local_and_global_fields}

(Weibel 2005) surveys the computations of K-theory of [global fields](wikipedia://en/288358) (such as [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) and [function fields](wikipedia://en/447645)), as well as [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375) (such as [p-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423)).

## Algebraically closed fields {#Algebraically_closed_fields}

(Suslin 1983) showed that the torsion in K-theory is insensitive to extensions of algebraically closed fields. This statement is known as [Suslin rigidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_rigidity).
