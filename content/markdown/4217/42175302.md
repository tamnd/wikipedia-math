# Ruth Gregory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ruth Gregory"}

**Ruth Ann Watson Gregory** is a British mathematician and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), currently Head of Department of Physics and Professor of Theoretical Physics at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London).[^o3] Her fields of specialisation are [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) and [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology).[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Gregory earned her PhD from the [Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics](wikipedia://en/3254271) at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in 1988, writing a thesis on "[topological defects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_defect) in cosmology" supervised by John M. Stewart.

## Career {#Career}

Gregory held postdoctoral appointments at the [Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi_National_Accelerator_Laboratory) and [Fermi Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Fermi_Institute) in the University of Chicago, before returning to Cambridge for a five-year research fellowship. She was appointed Professor of Mathematics and Physics at the University of Durham in 2005.[^o4] Gregory held this post until her appointment as Head of Department of Physics and Professor of Theoretical Physics at King's College London in 2021.[^o3]

She is a visiting fellow at the [Perimeter Institute for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perimeter_Institute_for_Theoretical_Physics) where she lectures as part of the PSI's master's programme.[^o7][^o8]

She serves as a managing editor of [International Journal of Modern Physics D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Journal_of_Modern_Physics_D).[^o9]

## Research {#Research}

Her research centres on the intersection of fundamental [high energy physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_energy_physics) and [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology). She is best known for the [Gregory–Laflamme instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory–Laflamme_instability), describing an instability of [black strings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_string) in higher dimensions.[^o11]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Gregory was given the 2006 [Maxwell Medal and Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_Medal_and_Prize) by the [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics) *for her contributions to physics at the interface of general relativity and string theory, in particular for her work on the physics of cosmic strings and black holes*.[^o13]

In 2011 she received the [Royal Society Wolfson Research Merit Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_Wolfson_Research_Merit_Award) to study *Time and Extra Dimensions in Space*.[^o14]
