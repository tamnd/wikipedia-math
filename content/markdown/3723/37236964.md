# Casas-Alvero conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Casas-Alvero conjecture"}

*Image: Dr. Casas-Alvero talking about Casas-Alvero Conjecture.jpg.* Casas-Alvero talking about the conjecture in a conference in the [University of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Barcelona) on March 16, 2016.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Casas-Alvero conjecture** is a problem about [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) which have factors in common with their [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921), proposed by [Eduardo Casas-Alvero](wikipedia://en/68212985) in 2001.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Formal statement"}

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

Let *f* be a polynomial of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) *d* defined over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero. If *f* has a factor in common with each of its derivatives *f*<sup>(*i*)</sup>, *i* = 1, ..., *d* − 1, then the [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) predicts that *f* must be a power of a [linear polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_polynomial).

:::

## Analogue in non-zero characteristic {#Analogue_in_non-zero_characteristic}

The conjecture is false over a field of positive characteristic *p*: any [inseparable polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inseparable_polynomial) *f*(*X*<sup>*p*</sup>) without constant term satisfies the condition since all derivatives are zero. Another [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) (which is separable) is *X*<sup>*p*+1</sup> − *X*<sup>*p*</sup>.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

The conjecture is known to hold in characteristic zero for degrees of the form *p*<sup>*k*</sup> or 2*p*<sup>*k*</sup> where *p* is [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) and *k* is a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). Similarly, it is known for degrees of the form 3*p*<sup>*k*</sup> where *p* ≠ 2, for degrees of the form 4*p*<sup>*k*</sup> where *p* ≠ 3, 5, 7, and for degrees of the form 5*p*<sup>*k*</sup> where *p* ≠ 2, 3, 7, 11, 131, 193, 599, 3541, 8009. Similar results are available for degrees of the form 6*p*<sup>*k*</sup> and 7*p*<sup>*k*</sup>. It is later established for *d* = 12, making *d* = 20 the smallest open degree.

:::
