# Director of GCHQ

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Director of GCHQ"}

The **Director of the Government Communications Headquarters** is the highest-ranking official in the [Government Communications Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Communications_Headquarters) (GCHQ), a British [intelligence agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_agency) that specialises in [signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence), [information assurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_assurance) and [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432). The director is a [Permanent Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Secretary), and appointed by and reports to the [Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Affairs).[^o2][^o3]

:::

Though the [prime minister of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_the_United_Kingdom) has ultimate responsibility within the British government for security matters and the intelligence agencies, the Foreign Secretary has day to day ministerial responsibility for GCHQ.[^o3] The Director of GCHQ is also a permanent member of the United Kingdom's [National Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Council_%28United_Kingdom%29) and the [Cabinet Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Office)'s [Joint Intelligence Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Intelligence_Committee_%28United_Kingdom%29).[^o3]

The role of the Director of GCHQ was outlined by the [Intelligence Services Act 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Services_Act_1994), in which the director is described as "...responsible for the efficiency of GCHQ".[^o4] The director's role is to ensure:

(a) That there are arrangements for securing that no information is obtained by GCHQ except so far as necessary for the proper discharge of its functions and that no information is disclosed by it except so far as necessary for that purpose or for the purpose of any criminal proceedings; and (b) that GCHQ does not take any action to further the interests of any United Kingdom political party

The GCHQ director has become more publicly visible in the wake of the [2013 global surveillance disclosures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_surveillance_disclosures_%282013–present%29). Sir [Arthur Bonsall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Bonsall), director from 1973 to 1978, was the first director to speak publicly about his career at GCHQ when he was interviewed by the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC) in September 2013,[^o5] and Sir [Iain Lobban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iain_Lobban) testified before parliament's [Intelligence and Security Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_and_Security_Committee) in the wake of the disclosures in November 2013.[^o6] The director from 1989 to 1996, Sir [John Adye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adye), had previously spoken as a witness at the inquest into the death of [Diana, Princess of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana,_Princess_of_Wales) in February 2008 to deny that GCHQ had any involvement in the tape recordings that led to the "[Camillagate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squidgygate#"Camillagate")" or "[Squidgygate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squidgygate)" scandals.[^o7]

Soon after taking on the role in 2014, [Robert Hannigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hannigan) authored under his own name an article in the *[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times)* on the topic of [internet surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_surveillance).[^o8][^o9]
