# Lehmer mean

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lehmer mean"}

In mathematics, the **Lehmer mean** of a [tuple](wikipedia://en/132729) $x$ of positive [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), named after [Derrick Henry Lehmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Henry_Lehmer),[^o2] is defined as:

:::

$$L_p(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^n x_k^p}{\sum_{k=1}^n x_k^{p-1}}.$$

The **weighted Lehmer mean** with respect to a tuple $w$ of positive weights is defined as:

$$L_{p,w}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^n w_k\cdot x_k^p}{\sum_{k=1}^n w_k\cdot x_k^{p-1}}.$$

The Lehmer mean is an alternative to [power means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_mean) for [interpolating](wikipedia://en/14569) between [minimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum) and [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum) via [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) and [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The derivative of $p \mapsto L_p(\mathbf{x})$ is non-negative

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial p} L_p(\mathbf{x}) = \frac {\left(\sum_{j=1}^n \sum_{k=j+1}^n \left[x_j - x_k\right] \cdot \left[\ln(x_j) - \ln(x_k)\right] \cdot \left[x_j \cdot x_k\right]^{p-1}\right)} {\left(\sum_{k=1}^n x_k^{p-1}\right)^2},$$

thus this function is monotonic and the inequality

$$p \le q \Longrightarrow L_p(\mathbf{x}) \le L_q(\mathbf{x})$$

holds.

The derivative of the weighted Lehmer mean is:

$$\frac{\partial L_{p,w}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial p} = \frac{(\sum w x^{p-1})(\sum wx^p\ln{x}) - (\sum wx^p)(\sum wx^{p-1}\ln{x})}{(\sum wx^{p-1})^2}$$

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

- $\lim_{p \to -\infty} L_p(\mathbf{x})$ is the [minimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum) of the elements of $\mathbf{x}$.
- $L_0(\mathbf{x})$ is the [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463).
- $L_\frac{1}{2}\left((x_1, x_2)\right)$ is the [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) of the two values $x_1$ and $x_2$.
- $L_1(\mathbf{x})$ is the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612).
- $L_2(\mathbf{x})$ is the [contraharmonic mean](wikipedia://en/8302382).
- $\lim_{p \to \infty} L_p(\mathbf{x})$ is the [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum) of the elements of $\mathbf{x}$. Sketch of a proof: [Without loss of generality](wikipedia://en/296000) let $x_1,\dots,x_k$ be the values which equal the maximum. Then $L_p(\mathbf{x}) = x_1\cdot\frac{k + \left(\frac{x_{k+1}}{x_1}\right)^p + \cdots + \left(\frac{x_n}{x_1}\right)^p}{k + \left(\frac{x_{k+1}}{x_1}\right)^{p-1} + \cdots + \left(\frac{x_n}{x_1}\right)^{p-1}}$

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

### Signal processing {#Signal_processing}

Like a [power mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_mean), a Lehmer mean serves a non-linear [moving average](wikipedia://en/990809) which is shifted towards small signal values for small $p$ and emphasizes big signal values for big $p$. Given an efficient implementation of a [moving arithmetic mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowpass) called smooth you can implement a moving Lehmer mean according to the following [Haskell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskell_%28programming_language%29) code.

```haskell
lehmerSmooth :: Floating a => ([a] -> [a]) -> a -> [a] -> [a]
lehmerSmooth smooth p xs =
    zipWith (/)
            (smooth (map (**p) xs))
            (smooth (map (**(p-1)) xs))
```

- For big $p$ it can serve an [envelope detector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envelope_detector) on a [rectified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifier) signal.
- For small $p$ it can serve an [baseline detector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseline_%28spectrometry%29) on a [mass spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrum).

Gonzalez and Woods call this a "contraharmonic mean [filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filter_%28signal_processing%29)" described for varying values of *p* (however, as above, the [contraharmonic mean](wikipedia://en/8302382) can refer to the specific case $p = 2$). Their convention is to substitute *p* with the order of the filter *Q*:

$$f(x) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^n x_k^{Q+1}}{\sum_{k=1}^n x_k^Q}.$$

*Q*=0 is the arithmetic mean. Positive *Q* can reduce [pepper noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt-and-pepper_noise) and negative *Q* can reduce [salt noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt-and-pepper_noise).[^o10]
