# Stolarsky mean

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stolarsky mean"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Stolarsky mean** is a generalization of the [logarithmic mean](wikipedia://en/4490764). It was introduced by [Kenneth B. Stolarsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_B._Stolarsky) in 1975.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For two positive [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) $x$ and $y$, the Stolarsky mean is defined as:

$$S_p(x,y) = \left \{ \begin{array}{l l} x, & \text{if }x=y, \\ \left({\frac{x^p-y^p}{p (x-y)}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}, & \text{otherwise}. \end{array} \right .$$

:::

## Derivation {#Derivation}

It is derived from the [mean value theorem](wikipedia://en/19662), which states that a [secant line](wikipedia://en/241886), cutting the graph of a [differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206) function $f$ at $( x, f(x) )$ and $( y, f(y) )$, has the same [slope](wikipedia://en/29368) as a line [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to the graph at some point $\xi$ in the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) $[x,y]$.

$$\exists \xi\in[x,y]\ f'(\xi) = \frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}$$

The Stolarsky mean is obtained by

$$\xi = \left[f'\right]^{-1}\left(\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}\right)$$

when choosing $f(x) = x^p$.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

- $\lim_{p\to -\infty} S_p(x,y)$ is the [minimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum).
- $S_{-1}(x,y)$ is the [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046).
- $\lim_{p\to 0} S_p(x,y)$ is the [logarithmic mean](wikipedia://en/4490764). It can be obtained from the mean value theorem by choosing $f(x) = \ln x$.
- $S_{\frac{1}{2}}(x,y)$ is the [power mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_mean) with exponent $\frac{1}{2}$.
- $\lim_{p\to 1} S_p(x,y)$ is the [identric mean](wikipedia://en/7423424). It can be obtained from the mean value theorem by choosing $f(x) = x\cdot \ln x$.
- $S_2(x,y)$ is the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612).
- $S_3(x,y) = QM(x,y,GM(x,y))$ is a connection to the [quadratic mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_mean) and the [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046).
- $\lim_{p\to\infty} S_p(x,y)$ is the [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum).

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

One can generalize the mean to *n* + 1 variables by considering the [mean value theorem for divided differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_value_theorem_for_divided_differences) for the *n*th [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921). One obtains

$S_p(x_0,\dots,x_n) = {f^{(n)}}^{-1}(n!\cdot f[x_0,\dots,x_n])$ for $f(x)=x^p$.

:::
