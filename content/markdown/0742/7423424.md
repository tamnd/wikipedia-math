# Identric mean

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Identric mean"}

The **identric mean** of two positive [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) *x*, *y* is defined as:[^o2]

:::

$$\begin{aligned} I(x,y) &= \frac{1}{e}\cdot \lim_{(\xi,\eta)\to(x,y)} \sqrt[\xi-\eta]{\frac{\xi^\xi}{\eta^\eta}} \\[8pt] &= \lim_{(\xi,\eta)\to(x,y)} \exp\left(\frac{\xi\cdot\ln\xi-\eta\cdot\ln\eta}{\xi-\eta}-1\right) \\[8pt] &= \begin{cases} x & \text{if }x=y \\[8pt] \frac{1}{e} \sqrt[x-y]{\frac{x^x}{y^y}} & \text{else} \end{cases} \end{aligned}$$

It can be derived from the [mean value theorem](wikipedia://en/19662) by considering the [secant](wikipedia://en/241886) of the graph of the function $x \mapsto x\cdot \ln x$. It can be generalized to more variables according by the [mean value theorem for divided differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_value_theorem_for_divided_differences). The identric mean is a special case of the [Stolarsky mean](wikipedia://en/7423338).

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="AM-IM-GM inequality"}

## AM-IM-GM inequality {#AM-IM-GM_inequality}

Recall that $\int\log x\,dx=x(\log x-1)$. Since the integrand is strictly concave, using two variations of the [Riemann integral](wikipedia://en/26390) to approximate it (with only one summand),

\1. the [trapezoidal rule](wikipedia://en/573452) gives a lower bound, since all secant lines interpolating two points on the curve lie beneath it.

\2. the [midpoint rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midpoint_rule) gives an upper bound, since its tangent lines intersect it only at the point they're drawn from, and lie above it elsewhere.

These give the inequalities

$$\begin{aligned} &\frac{\log x+\log y}2\le\overbrace{y(\log y-1)-x(\log x-1)\over y-x}^{\frac1{y-x}\int_x^y\log t\,dt}\le\log\frac{x+y}2\\ \overset{\exp}\implies&\underset\text{GM}{\sqrt{xy}}\le\underset\text{IM}{\frac1ey^{y\over y-x}x^{x\over x-y}}\le\underset\text{AM}{x+y\over2} \end{aligned}$$

By setting \$y=1\$ and using this as the ratio between successive terms, the IM-GM inequality states that $\sqrt{2\pi n}(\frac ne)^n\over n!$ is strictly increasing while the AM-IM states that $\sqrt{2\pi}(\frac{n+1/2}e)^{n+1/2}\over n!$ is strictly decreasing; together with their limits at $n=\infty$ being $1$, this establishes that the former variant of [Stirling's approximation](wikipedia://en/151783) is a lower bound on $n!$ while the latter (which appeared first in [James Stirling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Stirling_%28mathematician%29)'s notes) is an upper bound.[^o5]
