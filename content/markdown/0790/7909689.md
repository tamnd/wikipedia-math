# Sharp EL-8

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Sharp EL-8"}

*Image: Sharp EL-8 The First Portable Electronic Calculator.jpg.* Sharp EL-8 of December 1970

The **Sharp EL-8**, also known as the **ELSI-8**,[^o8] was one of the earliest mass-produced hand-held electronic [calculators](wikipedia://en/7593)[^o8] and the first hand-held calculator to be made by [Sharp Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_Corporation). Introduced around the start of 1971, it was based on Sharp's preceding [QT-8D](wikipedia://en/28941615) and [QT-8B](wikipedia://en/29028763) compact desktop calculators and used the same logic circuits, but it was redesigned to fit in a much smaller case.

:::

Most electronic calculators before the EL-8 were intended for desktop use.[^o9] Sharp's predecessor to the EL-8, the battery-powered QT-8B, was just a portable version of a compact desk calculator.[^o8] The EL-8 was much smaller, small enough to be used in one's hand: \6.46 in long, \4.02 in wide, and \2.76 in thick, and weighing \1.59 lb with batteries.[^o13] Although it was still too bulky to easily fit in a pocket,[^o8] it was an important step toward the development of the pocket calculator.[^o9]

*Image: Sharp ELSI-8 commercial.jpg.* Ad showing the calculator's original price.

The EL-8's original price in Japan was 84,800 [Japanese yen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_yen).[^o10] The retail price in 1971 was US.[^o8][^o14]

## Operation and design {#Operation_and_design}

The operation and performance of the EL-8 are identical to its predecessor calculators, the QT-8D and QT-8B, because its logic circuits use the same set of four [Rockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwell_International)-manufactured [large-scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large-scale_integration) [integrated circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit).[^o8][^o9][^o19] Likewise, its keyboard has the same layout, including the combined ×÷ key,[^o8][^o9] and it also uses magnetic [reed switches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_switch).[^o15]

The display, like that of the QT-8D, is an 8-digit [vacuum fluorescent display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_fluorescent_display) with nine individual tubes: eight digit tubes and a single extra tube for the minus sign and overflow indicator. As with the QT-8D, the tubes are Iseden "itron" tubes[^o4] with the same distinctive "handwritten" digit style and the same half-height "0". However, unlike the QT-8D, the EL-8 digit tubes only have [eight segments to form digits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-segment_display), and the EL-8's extra tube is positioned at the top right corner of the display instead of at its right end. A separate lamp serves as a power indicator.[^o8][^o9][^o15]

*Image: Sharp EL-8 Itron vacuum-fluorescent Display Tube.jpg.* Itron DG10L Vacuum-Fluorescent Display Tube

Power is supplied by an integrated [rechargeable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rechargeable_battery) [battery pack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_pack) or by an external [battery charger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_charger), which also serves as an [AC adapter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_adapter) to allow the calculator to operate from [AC power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mains_electricity).[^o9] The battery pack (model EL-84)[^o15] contains six [nickel-cadmium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel-cadmium_battery) [AA batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AA_battery) connected in [series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_and_parallel_circuits), giving a total [voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage) of \7.2 volts[^o8] and, with the original batteries, a total capacity of 450 mAh.[^o13] (Present-day nickel-cadmium AA batteries have much greater capacity.) Battery life with the original batteries is about three hours,[^o8] which gives an average power usage of about one [watt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watt). The battery charger (model EL-81) supplies power to the calculator's charging input at voltages of \8.7 +and \9.6 volts;[^o15] the combined input power rating is about \3.2 watts.[^o16]

*Image: Sharp NR-AA Re-chargeable Battery.jpg.* Sharp NR-AA Rechargeable Battery

The EL-8's battery pack is much smaller than that of the QT-8B, and this was critical in reducing the EL-8's size and weight. However, these smaller batteries have much less capacity, and in order to maintain reasonable battery life, power usage had to be drastically cut. This was done by various improvements in the electronics, especially in the display. The QT-8B's display tubes are driven by discrete [transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor); to reduce the number of transistors needed, only one tube is lit at a time, and the circuitry cycles through the tubes in rapid sequence. The rapid switching from tube to tube requires load [resistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistor) which absorb a lot of power. In the EL-8, the display tubes are driven by integrated circuits containing multiple transistors, and all of the tubes are kept lit constantly. No switching is required and no load resistors are needed, so much less power is used. Furthermore, because each tube in the QT-8B is only lit for a fraction of the time, the tubes need a high momentary brightness in order to get a reasonable average brightness, so they must be driven with a high voltage, requiring a separate high-voltage power supply. Since the EL-8 tubes are kept constantly lit, they don't have to be as bright, so the driving voltage can be lower and a separate high-voltage supply is not needed. In addition to the display improvements, the EL-8 also has a more efficient [clock generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock_generator) circuit for its logic than the QT-8B. Together, these changes reduced the EL-8's power usage to less than a third of that of the QT-8B.[^o13]

## Related models {#Related_models}

Sharp also produced a version of the EL-8 without batteries called the **EL-8A**. This was offered at a lower price, about US.[^o14] Since this version lacks batteries, it is somewhat lighter and requires an AC adapter for power. The power connector is also slightly different, having an extra contact.[^o17]

In addition to its own EL-8 and EL-8A, Sharp also built [OEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OEM) versions of the EL-8 for the Swedish office machine company [Facit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facit) and its subsidiary [Addo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AB_Addo), which sold them as the **Facit 1111** and the **Addo-X 9364**. These have the same internals as the EL-8, but the case and keycaps are different.[^o8][^o18]
