# First-order reduction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="First-order reduction"}

In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), a **first-order reduction** is a very strong type of [reduction](wikipedia://en/848067) between two [computational problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_problem) in [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543). A first-order reduction is a reduction where each component is restricted to be in the class [FO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FO_%28complexity%29) of problems calculable in [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983).

:::

Since we have $\mbox{FO} \subsetneq \mbox{L}$, the first-order reductions are stronger reductions than the [logspace reductions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logspace_reduction).

Many important complexity classes are closed under first-order reductions, and many of the traditional [complete](wikipedia://en/1176530) problems are first-order complete as well (Immerman 1999 p. 49-50). For example, [ST-connectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ST-connectivity) is FO-complete for [NL](wikipedia://en/1145955), and [NL](wikipedia://en/1145955) is closed under FO reductions (Immerman 1999, p. 51) (as are [P](wikipedia://en/658550), [NP](wikipedia://en/21562), and most other "well-behaved" classes).
