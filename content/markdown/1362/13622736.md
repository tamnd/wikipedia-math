# First Hurwitz triplet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="First Hurwitz triplet"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) theory of [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181), the **first Hurwitz triplet** is a triple of distinct [Hurwitz surfaces](wikipedia://en/12807841) with the identical [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the lowest possible genus, namely 14 (genera 3 and 7 each admit a unique Hurwitz surface, respectively the [Klein quartic](wikipedia://en/390976) and the [Macbeath surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macbeath_surface)). The explanation for this phenomenon is arithmetic. Namely, in the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of the appropriate [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field), the rational prime 13 splits as a product of three distinct prime ideals. The principal [congruence subgroups](wikipedia://en/382694) defined by the triplet of primes produce [Fuchsian groups](wikipedia://en/471365) corresponding to the triplet of Riemann surfaces.

:::

## Arithmetic construction {#Arithmetic_construction}

Let $K$ be the real subfield of $\mathbb{Q}[\rho]$ where $\rho$ is a 7th-primitive [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950). The [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of *K* is $\mathbb{Z}[\eta]$, where $\eta=2\cos(\tfrac{2\pi}{7})$. Let $D$ be the [quaternion algebra](wikipedia://en/2776890), or symbol algebra $(\eta,\eta)_{K}$. Also Let $\tau=1+\eta+\eta^2$ and $j'=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\eta i + \tau j)$. Let $\mathcal{Q}_\mathrm{Hur}=\mathbb{Z}[\eta][i,j,j']$. Then $\mathcal{Q}_\mathrm{Hur}$ is a maximal [order](wikipedia://en/1114171) of $D$ (see [Hurwitz quaternion order](wikipedia://en/19436533)), described explicitly by [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999) \[1\].

In order to construct the first Hurwitz triplet, consider the prime decomposition of 13 in $\mathbb{Z}[\eta]$, namely

$$13=\eta (\eta +2)(2\eta-1)(3-2\eta)(\eta+3),$$

where $\eta (\eta+2)$ is invertible. Also consider the prime ideals generated by the non-invertible factors. The principal congruence subgroup defined by such a prime ideal *I* is by definition the group

$$\mathcal{Q}^1_\mathrm{Hur}(I) = \{x \in \mathcal{Q}_\mathrm{Hur}^1 : x \equiv 1 \pmod{I\mathcal{Q}_\mathrm{Hur}}\},$$

namely, the group of elements of [reduced norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_norm) 1 in $\mathcal{Q}_\mathrm{Hur}$ equivalent to 1 modulo the ideal $I\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm Hur}$. The corresponding Fuchsian group is obtained as the image of the principal congruence subgroup under a representation to P[SL(2,R)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SL%282,R%29).

Each of the three Riemann surfaces in the first Hurwitz triplet can be formed as a [Fuchsian model](wikipedia://en/1417252), the quotient of the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/1589135) by one of these three Fuchsian groups.

## Bound for systolic length and the systolic ratio {#Bound_for_systolic_length_and_the_systolic_ratio}

The [Gauss–Bonnet theorem](wikipedia://en/139229) states that

$$\chi(\Sigma)=\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{\Sigma} K(u)\,dA,$$

where $\chi(\Sigma)$ is the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of the surface and $K(u)$ is the [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) . In the case $g=14$ we have

$\chi(\Sigma)=-26$ and $K(u)=-1,$

thus we obtain that the area of these surfaces is

$$52\pi.$$

The lower bound on the [systole](wikipedia://en/2545455) as specified in \[2\], namely

$$\frac{4}{3} \log(g(\Sigma)),$$

is 3.5187.

Some specific details about each of the surfaces are presented in the following tables (the number of systolic loops is taken from \[3\]). The term Systolic Trace refers to the least reduced trace of an element in the corresponding subgroup $\mathcal{Q}^1_{Hur}(I)$. The systolic ratio is the ratio of the square of the systole to the area.
