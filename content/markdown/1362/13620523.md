# Cauchy–Hadamard theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cauchy–Hadamard theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Cauchy–Hadamard theorem** is a result in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) named after the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) [Augustin Louis Cauchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_Louis_Cauchy) and [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675), describing the [radius of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476) of a [power series](wikipedia://en/59958). It was published in 1821 by Cauchy,[^o2] but remained relatively unknown until Hadamard rediscovered it.[^o3] Hadamard's first publication of this result was in 1888;[^o4] he also included it as part of his 1892 Ph.D. thesis.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Theorem for one complex variable"}

## Theorem for one complex variable {#Theorem_for_one_complex_variable}

Consider the formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) in one complex variable *z* of the form

$$f(z) = \sum_{n = 0}^{\infty} c_{n} (z-a)^{n}$$

where $a, c_n \in \mathbb{C}.$

Then the [radius of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476) $R$ of *f* at the point *a* is given by

$$\frac{1}{R} = \limsup_{n \to \infty} \left( | c_{n} |^{1/n} \right)$$

where $\lim \sup$ denotes the [limit superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_superior), the limit as $n$ approaches infinity of the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) of the sequence values after the *n*th position. If the sequence values is unbounded so that the $\lim \sup$ is ∞, then the power series does not converge near $a$, while if the $\lim \sup$ is 0 then the radius of convergence is ∞, meaning that the series converges on the entire plane.

::: {#o9 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

[Without loss of generality](wikipedia://en/296000) assume that $a=0$. We will show first that the power series $\sum_n c_n z^n$ converges for $|z|<R$, and then that it diverges for $|z|>R$.

First suppose $|z|<R$. Let $t=1/R$ not be $0$ or $\pm\infty.$ For any $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists only a finite number of $n$ such that $\sqrt[n]{|c_n|} \geq t+\varepsilon$. Now $|c_n| < (t+\varepsilon)^n$ for all but a finite number of $c_n$, so the series $\sum_n c_n z^n$ converges if $|z| < 1/(t+\varepsilon)$. This proves the first part.

Conversely, for $\varepsilon > 0$, $|c_n|\geq (t-\varepsilon)^n$ for infinitely many $c_n$, so if $|z|=1/(t-\varepsilon) > R$, we see that the series cannot converge because its *n*th term does not tend to 0.[^o10]

:::

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Theorem for several complex variables"}

## Theorem for several complex variables {#Theorem_for_several_complex_variables}

Let $\alpha$ be an *n*-dimensional vector of natural numbers ($\alpha = (\alpha_1, \cdots, \alpha_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n$) with $\|\alpha\| := \alpha_1 + \cdots + \alpha_n$, then $f(z)$ converges with radius of convergence $\rho = (\rho_1, \cdots, \rho_n) \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\rho^\alpha = \rho_1^{\alpha_1} \cdots \rho_n^{\alpha_n}$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922)

$$\limsup_{\|\alpha\|\to\infty} \sqrt[\|\alpha\|]{|c_\alpha|\rho^\alpha}=1$$

of the multidimensional power series

$$f(z) = \sum_{\alpha\geq0}c_\alpha(z-a)^\alpha := \sum_{\alpha_1\geq0,\ldots,\alpha_n\geq0}c_{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n}(z_1-a_1)^{\alpha_1}\cdots(z_n-a_n)^{\alpha_n}.$$

::: {#o14 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

From [^o15]

Set $z = a + t\rho$ $(z_i = a_i + t\rho_i).$ Then

$$\sum_{\alpha \geq 0} c_\alpha (z - a)^\alpha = \sum_{\alpha \geq 0} c_\alpha \rho^\alpha t^{\|\alpha\|} = \sum_{\mu \geq 0} \left( \sum_{\|\alpha\| = \mu} |c_\alpha| \rho^\alpha \right) t^\mu.$$

This is a power series in one variable $t$ which converges for $|t| < 1$ and diverges for $|t| > 1$. Therefore, by the Cauchy–Hadamard theorem for one variable

$$\limsup_{\mu \to \infty} \sqrt[\mu]{\sum_{\|\alpha\| = \mu} |c_\alpha| \rho^\alpha} = 1.$$

Setting $|c_m| \rho^m = \max_{\|\alpha\| = \mu} |c_\alpha| \rho^\alpha$ gives us an estimate

$$|c_m| \rho^m \leq \sum_{\|\alpha\| = \mu} |c_\alpha| \rho^\alpha \leq (\mu + 1)^n |c_m| \rho^m.$$

Because $\sqrt[\mu]{(\mu + 1)^n} \to 1$ as $\mu \to \infty$

$$\sqrt[\mu]{|c_m| \rho^m} \leq \sqrt[\mu]{\sum_{\|\alpha\| = \mu} |c_\alpha| \rho^\alpha} \leq \sqrt[\mu]{|c_m| \rho^m} \implies \sqrt[\mu]{\sum_{\|\alpha\| = \mu} |c_\alpha| \rho^\alpha} = \sqrt[\mu]{|c_m| \rho^m} \qquad (\mu \to \infty).$$

Therefore

$$\limsup_{\|\alpha\|\to\infty} \sqrt[\|\alpha\|]{|c_\alpha|\rho^\alpha} = \limsup_{\mu \to \infty} \sqrt[\mu]{|c_m| \rho^m} = 1.$$

:::
