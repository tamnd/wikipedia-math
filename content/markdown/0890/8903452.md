# National Statistics Socio-economic Classification

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="National Statistics Socio-economic Classification"}

The **National Statistics Socio-economic Classification** (often abbreviated to **NS-SEC**) is the official socio-economic classification in the United Kingdom. It is an adaptation of the [Goldthorpe schema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_stratification) which was first known as the Nuffield Class Schema developed in the 1970s.[^o2][^o3] It was developed using the Standard Occupational Classification 1990 (SOC90) and rebased on the Standard Occupational Classification 2000 (SOC2000) before its first major use on the [2001 UK census.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_United_Kingdom_census)[^o3][^o4][^o5] The NS-SEC replaced two previous social classifications: Socio-economic Groups (SEG) and Social Class based on Occupation (SC, formerly known as Registrar General's Social Class, RGSC). The NS-SEC was [rebased on the Standard Occupational Classification 2010](https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/classificationsandstandards/standardoccupationalclassificationsoc/soc2010/soc2010volume3thenationalstatisticssocioeconomicclassificationnssecrebasedonsoc2010) prior to the [2011 UK census](wikipedia://en/21958604) and it will be further rebased on the new Standard Occupational Classification 2020 for use on the 2021 UK census.

:::

The NS-SEC is a nested classification. It has 14 operational categories, with some sub-categories, and is commonly used in eight-class, five-class, and three-class versions.[^o6] Only the three-category version is intended to represent any form of [hierarchy](wikipedia://en/13998). The version intended for most users (the analytic version) has eight classes:

1. Higher [managerial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management) and [professional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional) occupations
1. Lower managerial and professional occupations
1. Intermediate occupations (clerical, sales, service)
1. Small [employers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Employer) and own account workers
1. Lower supervisory and [technical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology) occupations
1. Semi-routine occupations
1. Routine occupations
1. Never worked or long-term unemployed

The three-class version is reduced to following:

1. Higher occupations
1. Intermediate occupations
1. Lower occupations
