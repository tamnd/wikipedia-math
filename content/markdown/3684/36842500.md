# Brauer–Wall group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brauer–Wall group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Brauer–Wall group** or **super Brauer group** or **graded Brauer group** for a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F* is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) BW(*F*) classifying [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) graded central [division algebras](wikipedia://en/26549625) over the field. It was first defined by (txt) as a generalization of the [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078).

:::

The Brauer group of a field *F* is the set of the similarity classes of finite-dimensional [central simple algebras](wikipedia://en/404082) over *F* under the operation of [tensor product](wikipedia://en/372395), where two algebras are called similar if the commutants of their [simple modules](wikipedia://en/29430) are [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic). Every similarity class contains a unique division algebra, so the elements of the Brauer group can also be identified with [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of finite-dimensional central division algebras. The analogous construction for **Z**/2**Z**-[graded algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) defines the Brauer–Wall group BW(*F*).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The Brauer group B(*F*) injects into BW(*F*) by mapping a CSA *A* to the graded algebra which is *A* in grade zero.
- (Wall 1964, theorem 3) showed that there is an [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215)

0 → B(*F*) → BW(*F*) → Q(*F*) → 0

where Q(*F*) is the group of graded [quadratic extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_extension) of *F*, defined as an extension of **Z**/2 by *F*<sup>\*</sup>/*F*<sup>\*2</sup> with multiplication (*e*,*x*)(*f*,*y*) = (*e* + *f*, (−1)<sup>*ef*</sup>*xy*). The map from BW(*F*) to Q(*F*) is the **Clifford invariant** defined by mapping an algebra to the pair consisting of its grade and [determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Determinant_of_a_quadratic_form).

- There is a map from the additive group of the [Witt–Grothendieck ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witt–Grothendieck_ring) to the Brauer–Wall group obtained by sending a [quadratic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_space) to its [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305). The map factors through the [Witt group](wikipedia://en/5578523),[^o7] which has kernel **I**<sup>3</sup>, where **I** is the fundamental ideal of W(*F*).[^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- BW(**C**) is isomorphic to **Z**/2**Z**. This is an algebraic aspect of [Bott periodicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_periodicity) of period 2 for the unitary group. The 2 super division algebras are **C**, **C**\[γ\] where γ is an odd element of square 1 commuting with **C**.
- BW(**R**) is isomorphic to **Z**/8**Z**. This is an algebraic aspect of [Bott periodicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_periodicity) of period 8 for the orthogonal group. The 8 super division algebras are **R**, **R**\[ε\], **C**\[ε\], **H**\[δ\], **H**, **H**\[ε\], **C**\[δ\], **R**\[δ\] where δ and ε are odd elements of square −1 and 1, such that conjugation by them on [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) is [complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation).

:::
