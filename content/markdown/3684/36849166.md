# Generalized multivariate log-gamma distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized multivariate log-gamma distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **generalized multivariate log-gamma (G-MVLG) distribution** is a [multivariate distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_distribution) introduced by Demirhan and Hamurkaroglu[^o2] in 2011. The G-MVLG is a flexible distribution. [Skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) and [kurtosis](wikipedia://en/16848) are well controlled by the parameters of the distribution. This enables one to control [dispersion](wikipedia://en/23773247) of the distribution. Because of this property, the distribution is effectively used as a joint [prior distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prior_distribution) in [Bayesian analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_analysis), especially when the [likelihood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likelihood) is not from the [location-scale family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location-scale_family) of distributions such as [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462).

:::

## Joint probability density function {#Joint_probability_density_function}

If $\boldsymbol{Y} \sim \mathrm{G}\text{-}\mathrm{MVLG}(\delta,\nu,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})$, the joint [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) (pdf) of $\boldsymbol{Y}=(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k})$ is given as the following:

$$f(y_1,\dots,y_k)= \delta^{\nu}\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(1-\delta)^{n} \prod_{i=1}^k \mu_i \lambda_i^{-\nu-n}}{[\Gamma(\nu+n)]^{k-1}\Gamma(\nu)n!} \exp\bigg\{(\nu +n)\sum_{i=1}^k \mu_i y_i - \sum_{i=1}^k \frac{1}{\lambda_i}\exp\{\mu_i y_i\}\bigg\},$$

where $\boldsymbol{y}\in \mathbb{R}^{k}, \nu>0, \lambda_{j}>0, \mu_{j}>0$ for $j=1,\dots,k, \delta=\det(\boldsymbol{\Omega})^{\frac{1}{k-1}},$ and

$$\boldsymbol{\Omega}=\left( \begin{array}{cccc} 1 & \sqrt{\mathrm{abs}(\rho_{12})} & \cdots & \sqrt{\mathrm{abs}(\rho_{1k})} \\ \sqrt{\mathrm{abs}(\rho_{12})} & 1 & \cdots & \sqrt{\mathrm{abs}(\rho_{2k})} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \sqrt{\mathrm{abs}(\rho_{1k})} & \sqrt{\mathrm{abs}(\rho_{2k})} & \cdots & 1 \end{array} \right),$$

$\rho_{ij}$ is the [correlation](wikipedia://en/157057) between $Y_i$ and $Y_j$, $\det(\cdot)$ and $\mathrm{abs}(\cdot)$ denote [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) and [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) of inner expression, respectively, and $\boldsymbol{g}=(\delta,\nu,\boldsymbol{\lambda}^T,\boldsymbol{\mu}^T)$ includes parameters of the distribution.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Joint moment generating function {#Joint_moment_generating_function}

The joint [moment generating function](wikipedia://en/194634) of G-MVLG distribution is as the following:

$$M_{\boldsymbol{Y}}(\boldsymbol{t}) =\delta^\nu \bigg(\prod_{i=1}^k \lambda_i^{t_i/\mu_i}\bigg)\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{\Gamma(\nu +n)}{\Gamma(\nu)n!} (1-\delta)^n \prod_{i=1}^k \frac{\Gamma(\nu+n+t_i/\mu_i)}{\Gamma(\nu+n)}.$$

### Marginal central moments {#Marginal_central_moments}

$r^\text{th}$ marginal central moment of $Y_i$ is as the following:

$${\mu_i}'_r=\left[\frac{(\lambda_i/\delta)^{t_i/\mu_i}}{\Gamma(\nu)}\sum_{k=0}^r \binom{r}{k}\left[\frac{\ln(\lambda_i/\delta)}{\mu_i}\right]^{r-k} \frac{\partial^k\Gamma(\nu+t_i/\mu_i)}{\partial t_i^k}\right]_{t_i=0}.$$

### Marginal expected value and variance {#Marginal_expected_value_and_variance}

Marginal expected value $Y_i$ is as the following:

$$\operatorname{E}(Y_{i})=\frac{1}{\mu_i}\big[\ln(\lambda_i/\delta)+\digamma(\nu)\big],$$

$$\operatorname{var}(Z_i)=\digamma^{[1]}(\nu)/(\mu_i)^2$$

where $\digamma(\nu)$ and $\digamma^{[1]}(\nu)$ are values of [digamma](wikipedia://en/406935) and [trigamma functions](wikipedia://en/2036750) at $\nu$, respectively.

:::

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

Demirhan and Hamurkaroglu establish a relation between the G-MVLG distribution and the [Gumbel distribution](wikipedia://en/270827) ([type I extreme value distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_I_extreme_value_distribution)) and gives a multivariate form of the Gumbel distribution, namely the generalized multivariate Gumbel (G-MVGB) distribution. The joint probability density function of $\boldsymbol{T}\sim \mathrm{G}\text{-}\mathrm{MVGB}(\delta,\nu,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu})$ is the following:

$$f(t_1,\dots,t_k; \delta,\nu,\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}))= \delta^\nu \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(1-\delta)^n \prod_{i=1}^k \mu_i \lambda_i^{-\nu-n}}{[\Gamma(\nu+n)]^{k-1}\Gamma(\nu)n!}\exp\bigg\{-(\nu +n)\sum_{i=1}^k \mu_i t_i - \sum_{i=1}^k \frac{1}{\lambda_i} \exp\{-\mu_i t_i\}\bigg\},\quad t_i\in \mathbb{R}.$$

The Gumbel distribution has a broad range of applications in the field of [risk analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_analysis). Therefore, the G-MVGB distribution should be beneficial when it is applied to these types of problems.
