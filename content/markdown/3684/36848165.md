# Binary matroid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Binary matroid"}

In [matroid theory](wikipedia://en/244321), a **binary matroid** is a matroid that can be [represented](wikipedia://en/37165960) over the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) [GF(2)](wikipedia://en/11137263).[^o2] That is, up to isomorphism, they are the matroids whose elements are the columns of a [(0,1)-matrix](wikipedia://en/4109196) and whose sets of elements are independent if and only if the corresponding columns are [linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent) in GF(2).

:::

## Alternative characterizations {#Alternative_characterizations}

A matroid $M$ is binary if and only if

- It is the matroid defined from a [symmetric](wikipedia://en/126474) (0,1)-matrix.[^o4]
- For every set $\mathcal{S}$ of circuits of the matroid, the [symmetric difference](wikipedia://en/262288) of the circuits in $\mathcal{S}$ can be represented as a [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of circuits.[^o5][^o6]
- For every pair of circuits of the matroid, their symmetric difference contains another circuit.[^o6]
- For every pair $C,D$ where $C$ is a circuit of $M$ and $D$ is a circuit of the [dual matroid](wikipedia://en/23515837) of $M$, $|C\cap D|$ is an even number.[^o6][^o7]
- For every pair $B,C$ where $B$ is a basis of $M$ and $C$ is a circuit of $M$, $C$ is the symmetric difference of the fundamental circuits induced in $B$ by the elements of $C\setminus B$.[^o6][^o7]
- No [matroid minor](wikipedia://en/36749660) of $M$ is the [uniform matroid](wikipedia://en/27967069) $U{}^2_4$, the four-point line.[^o8][^o9][^o10]
- In the [geometric lattice](wikipedia://en/17243191) associated to the matroid, every interval of height two has at most five elements.[^o10]

## Related matroids {#Related_matroids}

Every [regular matroid](wikipedia://en/13415343), and every [graphic matroid](wikipedia://en/745714), is binary.[^o7] A binary matroid is regular if and only if it does not contain the [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404) (a seven-element non-regular binary matroid) or its dual as a [minor](wikipedia://en/36749660).[^o12] A binary matroid is graphic if and only if its minors do not include the dual of the graphic matroid of $K_5$ nor of $K_{3,3}$.[^o13] If every circuit of a binary matroid has odd cardinality, then its circuits must all be disjoint from each other; in this case, it may be represented as the graphic matroid of a [cactus graph](wikipedia://en/9944209).[^o7]

## Additional properties {#Additional_properties}

If $M$ is a binary matroid, then so is its dual, and so is every [minor](wikipedia://en/36749660) of $M$.[^o7] Additionally, the direct sum of binary matroids is binary.

(Harary Welsh 1969) define a [bipartite matroid](wikipedia://en/36886954) to be a matroid in which every circuit has even cardinality, and an [Eulerian matroid](wikipedia://en/36854652) to be a matroid in which the elements can be partitioned into disjoint circuits. Within the class of graphic matroids, these two properties describe the matroids of [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431) and [Eulerian graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eulerian_graph) (not-necessarily-connected graphs in which all vertices have even degree), respectively. For [planar graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_graphs) (and therefore also for the graphic matroids of planar graphs) these two properties are dual: a planar graph or its matroid is bipartite if and only if its dual is Eulerian. The same is true for binary matroids. However, there exist non-binary matroids for which this duality breaks down.[^o7][^o16]

Any algorithm that tests whether a given matroid is binary, given access to the matroid via an [independence oracle](wikipedia://en/36878315), must perform an exponential number of oracle queries, and therefore cannot take polynomial time.[^o17]
