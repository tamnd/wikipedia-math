# Carl Wolfgang Benjamin Goldschmidt

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carl Wolfgang Benjamin Goldschmidt"}

**Carl Wolfgang Benjamin Goldschmidt** (4 August 1807 – 15 February 1851)[^o2][^o3][^o4] was a German [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) of [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) descent[^o5] who was a professor of [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen).[^o2] He is also known as **Benjamin Goldschmidt**,[^o3] **C. W. B. Goldschmidt**,[^o6][^o7] **Carl Goldschmidt**, and **Karl Goldschmidt**.[^o4]

:::

## Mathematical works {#Mathematical_works}

A student of [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125)[^o5] and an assistant to Gauss at the university [observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatory),[^o2][^o3] Goldschmidt frequently collaborated with Gauss on various mathematical and scientific works. Goldschmidt was in turn a professor of Gauss's protégé [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980).[^o4] Data gathered by Gauss and Goldschmidt on the growth of the [logarithmic integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_integral) compared to the [distribution of prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_of_prime_numbers) was cited by Riemann in "[On the Number of Primes Less Than a Given Magnitude](wikipedia://en/536817)", Riemann's seminal paper on the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826).[^o9]

In 1831, Goldschmidt wrote a mathematical treatise in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language), "Determinatio superficiei minimae rotatione curvae data duo puncta jungentis circa datum axem ortae" ("Determination of the surface-minimal rotation curve given two joined points about a given axis of origin").[^o10] The paper dealt with the problem in the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) of determining the [minimal surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/36847455), the [surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549) of the [planar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) between two given [points](wikipedia://en/593693) which [minimizes](wikipedia://en/52033) [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725).[^o11] [Solutions](wikipedia://en/537048) to the problem exist which are not [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122); such [discontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discontinuous) solutions are known as [Goldschmidt solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldschmidt_solutions) in honor of Goldschmidt's discovery of them.[^o7][^o12]

## Physics works {#Physics_works}

In 1834, Goldschmidt co-authored, in German, the textbook *Lehrbuch der analytischen Optik* (*Textbook of Analytical [Optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics)*) with J. C. Eduard Schmidt.[^o6] Together with Gauss and [Wilhelm Eduard Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Eduard_Weber), Goldschmidt published in 1840 *Atlas des Erdmagnetismus: nach den Elementen der Theorie entworfen* (*[Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas) of [Geomagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetism): According to the Elements of the Theory of Design*), a series of [magnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetism) [maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map).[^o14][^o15] In 1845, Goldschmidt published, also in German, a book on [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism), *Untersuchungen über die magnetische Declination in Göttingen* (*Studies of the [Magnetic Declination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_declination) in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen)*).[^o16]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Goldschmidt, who suffered from an [enlargement of the heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_the_heart), died in his sleep and was found on the morning of 15 February 1851.[^o2][^o3]
