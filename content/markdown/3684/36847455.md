# Minimal surface of revolution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minimal surface of revolution"}

*Image: Bulle caténoïde.png.* Stretching a soap film between two parallel circular wire loops generates a [catenoidal](wikipedia://en/273329) minimal surface of revolution

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **minimal surface of revolution** or **minimum surface of revolution** is a [surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549) defined from two [points](wikipedia://en/593693) in a [half-plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-plane), whose boundary is the axis of revolution of the surface. It is generated by a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) that lies in the half-plane and connects the two points; among all the surfaces that can be generated in this way, it is the one that [minimizes](wikipedia://en/52033) the [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725).[^o2] A basic problem in the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) is finding the curve between two points that produces this minimal surface of revolution.[^o2]

:::

## Relation to minimal surfaces {#Relation_to_minimal_surfaces}

A minimal surface of revolution is a subtype of [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734).[^o2] A minimal surface is defined not as a surface of minimal area, but as a surface with a [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210) of 0.[^o4] Since a mean curvature of 0 is a [necessary condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_condition) of a surface of minimal area, all minimal surfaces of revolution are minimal surfaces, but not all minimal surfaces are minimal surfaces of revolution. As a point forms a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) when [rotated about an axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_around_a_fixed_axis), finding the minimal surface of revolution is equivalent to finding the minimal surface passing through two circular [wireframes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireframe_model).[^o2] A physical realization of a minimal surface of revolution is [soap film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap_film) stretched between two parallel circular [wires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire): the soap film naturally takes on the shape with least surface area.[^o5][^o6]

## Catenoid solution {#Catenoid_solution}

*Image: Catenoid.svg.* A [catenoid](wikipedia://en/273329)

If the half-plane containing the two points and the axis of revolution is given [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate), making the axis of revolution into the *x*-axis of the coordinate system, then the curve connecting the points may be interpreted as the [graph of a function](wikipedia://en/87352). If the Cartesian coordinates of the two given points are $(x_1,y_1)$, $(x_2,y_2)$, then the area of the surface generated by a nonnegative [differentiable function](wikipedia://en/330206) $f$ may be expressed mathematically as

$$2\pi\int_{x_1}^{x_2} f(x) \sqrt{1+f'(x)^2} dx$$

and the problem of finding the minimal surface of revolution becomes one of finding the function that minimizes this integral, subject to the [boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_conditions) that $f(x_1)=y_1$ and $f(x_2)=y_2$.[^o11] In this case, the optimal curve will necessarily be a [catenary](wikipedia://en/7163).[^o2][^o11] The axis of revolution is the directrix of the catenary, and the minimal surface of revolution will thus be a [catenoid](wikipedia://en/273329).[^o2][^o8][^o9]

## Goldschmidt solution {#Goldschmidt_solution}

Solutions based on discontinuous functions may also be defined. In particular, for some placements of the two points the optimal solution is generated by a discontinuous function that is nonzero at the two points and zero everywhere else. This function leads to a surface of revolution consisting of two circular disks, one for each point, connected by a degenerate line segment along the axis of revolution. This is known as a **Goldschmidt solution**[^o11][^o12] after [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people) mathematician [Carl Wolfgang Benjamin Goldschmidt](wikipedia://en/36846598),[^o6] who announced his discovery of it in his 1831 paper "Determinatio superficiei minimae rotatione curvae data duo puncta jungentis circa datum axem ortae" ("Determination of the surface-minimal rotation curve given two joined points about a given axis of origin").[^o13]

To continue the physical analogy of soap film given above, these Goldschmidt solutions can be visualized as instances in which the soap film breaks as the circular wires are stretched apart.[^o6] However, in a physical soap film, the connecting line segment would not be present. Additionally, if a soap film is stretched in this way, there is a range of distances within which the catenoid solution is still feasible but has greater area than the Goldschmidt solution, so the soap film may stretch into a configuration in which the area is a [local minimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_minimum) but not a global minimum. For distances greater than this range, the catenary that defines the catenoid crosses the *x*-axis and leads to a self-intersecting surface, so only the Goldschmidt solution is feasible.[^o14]
