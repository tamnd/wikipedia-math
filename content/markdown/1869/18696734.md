# Stellation diagram

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stellation diagram"}

*Image: Zeroth stellation of dodecahedron facets.svg.* The stellation diagram for the regular [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) with the central pentagon highlighted. This diagram represents the dodecahedron face itself.

*Image: Brückner Vielflache Tafel 02.jpg.* Drawings by [Max Brückner](wikipedia://en/48703370) (1900). The stellation diagram of the dodecahedron (including unbounded regions) is shown in the top right. On the left page are stellation diagrams related to the [compound of five cubes](wikipedia://en/8010502) and [its dual](wikipedia://en/8010546).

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **stellation diagram** or **stellation pattern** is a two-dimensional diagram in the plane of some face of a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470), showing lines where other face planes [intersect](wikipedia://en/41780044) with this one. The lines cause 2D space to be divided up into regions. Regions not intersected by any further lines are called elementary regions. Usually [unbounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unbounded_set) regions are excluded from the diagram, along with any portions of the lines extending to [infinity](wikipedia://en/403139). Each elementary region represents a top face of one [cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29), and a bottom face of another.

:::

A collection of these diagrams, one for each face type, can be used to represent any [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of the polyhedron, by shading the regions which should appear in that stellation.

A stellation diagram exists for every face of a given polyhedron. In [face transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_transitive) polyhedra, symmetry can be used to require all faces have the same diagram shading. Semiregular polyhedra like the [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847) will have different stellation diagrams for different kinds of faces.
