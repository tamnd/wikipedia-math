# Kurosh subgroup theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kurosh subgroup theorem"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Kurosh subgroup theorem** describes the algebraic structure of [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of [free products](wikipedia://en/360036) of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447). The theorem was obtained by [Alexander Kurosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Gennadievich_Kurosh), a Russian mathematician, in 1934.[^o2] Informally, the theorem says that every subgroup of a free product is itself a free product of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) and of its intersections with the [conjugates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_%28group_theory%29) of the factors of the original free product.

:::

## History and generalizations {#History_and_generalizations}

After the original 1934 proof of Kurosh, there were many subsequent proofs of the Kurosh subgroup theorem, including proofs of Harold W. Kuhn (1952),[^o4] [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574) (1958)[^o5] and others. The theorem was also generalized for describing subgroups of [amalgamated free products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_product_with_amalgamation) and [HNN extensions](wikipedia://en/2823319).[^o6][^o7] Other generalizations include considering subgroups of free [pro-finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-finite_group) products[^o8] and a version of the Kurosh subgroup theorem for [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315).[^o9]

In modern terms, the Kurosh subgroup theorem is a straightforward corollary of the basic structural results of [Bass–Serre theory](wikipedia://en/18673708) about groups [acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on [trees](wikipedia://en/48560).[^o10]

::: {#o12 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $G = A*B$ be the [free product](wikipedia://en/360036) of groups *A* and *B* and let $H \le G$ be a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of *G*. Then there exist a family $(A_i)_{i\in I}$ of subgroups $A_i \le A$, a family $(B_j)_{j\in J}$ of subgroups $B_j \le B$, families $g_i, i\in I$ and $f_j, j\in J$ of elements of *G*, and a subset $X\subseteq G$ such that

$$H=F(X)*(*_{i\in I} g_i A_ig_i^{-1})* (*_{j\in J} f_jB_jf_j^{-1}).$$

This means that *X* *freely [generates](wikipedia://en/99945)* a subgroup of *G* isomorphic to the [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) *F*(*X*) with free basis *X* and that, moreover, *g*<sub>*i*</sub>*A*<sub>*i*</sub>*g*<sub>*i*</sub><sup>−1</sup>, *f*<sub>*j*</sub>*B*<sub>*j*</sub>*f*<sub>*j*</sub><sup>−1</sup> and *X* [generate](wikipedia://en/99945) *H* in *G* as a free product of the above form.

There is a generalization of this to the case of free products with arbitrarily many factors.[^o13] Its formulation is:

If *H* is a subgroup of ∗<sub>i∈I</sub>*G*<sub>i</sub> = *G*, then

$$H=F(X)*(*_{j\in J} g_jH_jg_j^{-1}),$$

where *X* ⊆ *G* and *J* is some index set and *g*<sub>j</sub> ∈ *G* and each *H*<sub>j</sub> is a subgroup of some *G*<sub>i</sub>.

:::

::: {#o15 .proof cue=heading name="Proof using Bass–Serre theory"}

## Proof using Bass–Serre theory {#Proof_using_Bass–Serre_theory}

The Kurosh subgroup theorem easily follows from the basic structural results in [Bass–Serre theory](wikipedia://en/18673708), as explained, for example in the book of Cohen (1987):[^o10]

Let *G* = *A*∗*B* and consider *G* as the fundamental group of a [graph of groups](wikipedia://en/12281773) **Y** consisting of a single non-loop edge with the vertex groups *A* and *B* and with the trivial edge group. Let *X* be the Bass–Serre universal covering tree for the graph of groups **Y**. Since *H* ≤ *G* also acts on *X*, consider the quotient graph of groups **Z** for the action of *H* on *X*. The vertex groups of **Z** are subgroups of *G*-stabilizers of vertices of *X*, that is, they are conjugate in *G* to subgroups of *A* and *B*. The edge groups of **Z** are trivial since the *G*-stabilizers of edges of *X* were trivial. By the fundamental theorem of Bass–Serre theory, *H* is canonically [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to the fundamental group of the [graph of groups](wikipedia://en/12281773) **Z**. Since the edge groups of **Z** are trivial, it follows that *H* is equal to the free product of the vertex groups of **Z** and the free group *F*(*X*) which is the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) (in the standard topological sense) of the underlying graph *Z* of **Z**. This implies the conclusion of the Kurosh subgroup theorem.

:::

::: {#o17 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extension"}

## Extension {#Extension}

The result extends to the case that *G* is the [amalgamated product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalgamated_product) along a common subgroup *C*, under the condition that *H* meets every conjugate of *C* only in the identity element.[^o18]

:::
