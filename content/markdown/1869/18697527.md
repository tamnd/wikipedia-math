# Rostislav Grigorchuk

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rostislav Grigorchuk"}

**Rostislav Ivanovich Grigorchuk** (Ростислав Иванович Григорчук; Ростисла́в Iва́нович Григорчу́к; b. February 23, 1953) is a mathematician working in different areas of mathematics including [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). He holds the rank of [Distinguished Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professors_in_the_United_States) in the Mathematics Department of [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University). Grigorchuk is particularly well known for having constructed, in a 1984 paper,[^o38] the first example of a [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042) of intermediate [growth](wikipedia://en/498727), thus answering an important problem posed by [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632) in 1968. This group is now known as the [Grigorchuk group](wikipedia://en/18707371)[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7] and it is one of the important objects studied in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), particularly in the study of branch groups, automaton groups and [iterated monodromy groups](wikipedia://en/4649761). Grigorchuk is one of the pioneers of asymptotic group theory as well as of the theory of dynamically defined groups. He introduced the notion of branch groups[^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11] and developed the foundations of the related theory. Grigorchuk, together with his collaborators and students, initiated the theory of groups generated by finite Mealy type automata,[^o12][^o13][^o14] interpreted them as groups of [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) type,[^o15][^o16] developed the theory of groups acting on rooted trees,[^o17] and found numerous applications[^o18][^o19][^o20] of these groups in various fields of mathematics including [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [spectral graph theory](wikipedia://en/534914), [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) and [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986).

:::

## Biographical data {#Biographical_data}

Grigorchuk was born on February 23, 1953, in [Ternopil Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternopil_Oblast), now [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine) (in 1953 part of the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR)).[^o52] He received his undergraduate degree in 1975 from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University). He obtained a PhD (Candidate of Science) in Mathematics in 1978, also from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), where his thesis advisor was [Anatoly M. Stepin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Mikhailovich_Stepin). Grigorchuk received a habilitation (Doctor of Science) degree in Mathematics in 1985 at the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494) in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow).[^o52] During the 1980s and 1990s, Rostislav Grigorchuk held positions at the [Moscow State University of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University_of_Railway_Engineering), and subsequently at the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494) and [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University).[^o52] In 2002 Grigorchuk joined the faculty of [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University) as a Professor of Mathematics, and he was promoted to the rank of Distinguished Professor in 2008.[^o22]

Rostislav Grigorchuk gave an invited address at the 1990 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto)[^o23] an AMS Invited Address at the March 2004 meeting of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in Athens, Ohio[^o24] and a plenary talk at the 2004 Winter Meeting of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055).[^o25]

Grigorchuk is the Editor-in-Chief of the journal *"Groups, Geometry and Dynamics"*,[^o26] published by the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470), and is or was a member of the editorial boards of the journals *"Mathematical Notes"*,[^o27] *"International Journal of Algebra and Computation"*,[^o28] *"Journal of Modern Dynamics"*,[^o29] *"Geometriae Dedicata"*,[^o30] *"Ukrainian Mathematical Journal"*,[^o31] *"Algebra and Discrete Mathematics"*,[^o32] *"Carpathian Mathematical Publications"*,[^o33] *"Bukovinian Mathematical Journal"*,[^o34] and *"Matematychni Studii"*.[^o35]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Grigorchuk is most well known for having constructed the first example of a finitely generated group of intermediate growth which now bears his name and is called the **[Grigorchuk group](wikipedia://en/18707371)** (sometimes it is also called the **first Grigorchuk group** since Grigorchuk constructed several other groups that are also commonly studied). This group has [growth](wikipedia://en/498727) that is faster than polynomial but slower than exponential. Grigorchuk constructed this group in a 1980 paper[^o37] and proved that it has intermediate growth in a 1984 article.[^o38] This result answered a long-standing open problem posed by [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632) in 1968 about the existence of finitely generated groups of intermediate growth. Grigorchuk's group has a number of other remarkable mathematical properties. It is a [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) infinite [residually finite](wikipedia://en/5780875) [2-group](wikipedia://en/24664) (that is, every element of the group has a finite order which is a power of 2). It is also the first example of a finitely generated group that is [amenable](wikipedia://en/657430) but not [elementary amenable](wikipedia://en/5393997), thus providing an answer to another long-standing problem, posed by [Mahlon Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahlon_Day) in 1957.[^o39] Also Grigorchuk's group is *"just infinite"*: that is, it is infinite but every proper [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) of this group is finite.[^o3]

Grigorchuk's group is a central object in the study of the so-called branch groups and automata groups. These are finitely generated groups of automorphisms of rooted trees that are given by particularly nice recursive descriptions and that have remarkable self-similar properties. The study of branch, automata and self-similar groups has been particularly active in the 1990s and 2000s and a number of unexpected connections with other areas of mathematics have been discovered there, including [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316), [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), [random walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_walks), [fractals](wikipedia://en/10913), [Hecke algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecke_algebra_of_a_locally_compact_group), bounded cohomology, [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), and others. In particular, many of these self-similar groups arise as [iterated monodromy groups](wikipedia://en/4649761) of complex polynomials. Important connections have been discovered between the algebraic structure of self-similar groups and the dynamical properties of the polynomials in question, including encoding their [Julia sets](wikipedia://en/56434).[^o40]

Much of Grigorchuk's work in the 1990s and 2000s has been on developing the theory of branch, automata and self-similar groups and on exploring these connections. For example, Grigorchuk, with co-authors, obtained a counter-example to the conjecture of [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) about *L<sup>2</sup>*-betti numbers of closed manifolds.[^o41][^o42]

Grigorchuk is also known for his contributions to the general theory of [random walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_walks) on groups and the theory of [amenable groups](wikipedia://en/657430), particularly for obtaining in 1980[^o43] what is commonly known (see for example[^o44][^o45][^o46]) as **Grigorchuk's co-growth criterion of amenability** for [finitely generated groups](wikipedia://en/963042).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1979 Rostislav Grigorchuk was awarded the [Moscow Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/9098251).[^o48]

In 1991 he obtained [Fulbright Senior Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Senior_Scholarship),[^o49] [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), New York.

In 2003 an international [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) conference in honor of Grigorchuk's 50th birthday was held in [Gaeta, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaeta,_Italy).[^o50] Special anniversary issues of the *"International Journal of Algebra and Computation"*,[^o51] the journal *"Algebra and Discrete Mathematics"*[^o52] and the book *"Infinite Groups: Geometric, Combinatorial and Dynamical Aspects"*[^o53] were dedicated to Grigorchuk's 50th birthday.

In 2009 Grigorchuk R.I. was awarded the Association of Former Students Distinguished Achievement in Research,[^o54] [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University).

In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o55]

In 2015 Rostislav Grigorchuk was awarded the AMS [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Seminal Contribution to Research.[^o56]

In 2020 Grigorchuk R.I. received the [Humboldt Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Research_Award) by Germany’s [Alexander von Humboldt Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt_Foundation).[^o57]
