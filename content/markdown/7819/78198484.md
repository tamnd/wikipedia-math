# Yilin Wang

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yilin Wang"}

Wang Yilin [Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_%28surname%29) (王)

**Yilin Wang** (; born 1991) is a mathematician whose research has involved [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), including [Teichmüller theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teichmüller_theory), the [Schramm–Loewner evolution](wikipedia://en/6706108), and [Loewner energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loewner_energy). Originally from China, and educated in France and Switzerland, she is a junior professor at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) in France and has accepted a position at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) in Switzerland starting in July 2025.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Wang was born in [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai) in 1991 and attended [Shanghai Foreign Language School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Foreign_Language_School) where she elected to learn French as a foreign language. In Wang's third year of high school, the [French Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Education_%28France%29) held a recruiting campaign in China for students who excelled in mathematics. While Wang did not score particularly well on the recruitment exam, she was the only student to answer her questionnaire in French rather than English.This caught the attention of the interviewers and she was eventually accepted into the programme.[^o4]

After [classes prépas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classes_prépas) at the [Lycée du Parc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_du_Parc) in [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon), Wang entered the [École normale supérieure (Paris)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29) in 2011. She earned a master's degree in fundamental mathematics at [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University) in Paris in 2014 and a second master's degree in probability and statistics at [Paris-Sud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_University) in 2015. She completed a Ph.D. at ETH Zurich in 2019 with the dissertation *On the Loewner energy of simple planar curves* supervised by [Wendelin Werner](wikipedia://en/6607797).

She spent three years as a C.L.E. Moore Instructor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), including a one-semester leave as Strauch Postdoctoral fellow at the [Simons Laufer Mathematical Sciences Institute](wikipedia://en/401367) in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California). Next, she became a junior professor at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) in 2022, the institute's first junior professor. In 2024, she was appointed to a position as associate professor at ETH Zurich.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Wang was a 2022 recipient of the [Maryam Mirzakhani New Frontiers Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryam_Mirzakhani_New_Frontiers_Prize), given "for innovative and far-reaching work on the Loewner energy of planar curves".

She is one of two 2024 recipients of the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize), given "for developing deep novel connections between complex analysis, probability, and mathematical physics, particularly with regards to Teichmuller theory and the theory of the Schramm–Loewner evolution".
