# Consensus dynamics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Consensus dynamics"}

**Consensus dynamics**, also known as **agreement dynamics**, is an area of research at the intersection of [systems theory](wikipedia://en/29238) and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). It studies how a group of agents—such as robots, sensors, or decision-makers—interacting over a network can reach a common decision or estimate through local rules and information exchange. This is known as the consensus problem in [multi-agent systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-agent_system), where the goal is to ensure that all agents eventually agree on a certain quantity, despite starting with potentially different initial values.

:::

Consensus dynamics has applications in areas such as [physiological systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiological_system), [gene regulatory networks](wikipedia://en/356382), [large-scale energy systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_grid), and coordinated control of autonomous vehicle fleets on land, in the air, or in space. The behavior of these systems is typically modeled using an unforced [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) governed by the network's interconnection topology and the agents' initial conditions. The **consensus protocol**, or **agreement protocol**, defines the rules of interaction that lead to convergence.

Related coordination problems include the [rendezvous problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendezvous_problem), [synchronization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronization), [flocking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flocking_%28behavior%29), and [formation control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_control). These often rely on similar mathematical foundations and are studied under the broader umbrella of **cooperative control**. One computational approach to solving these problems is [distributed constraint reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_constraint_reasoning). Consensus models are also used in social and philosophical domains to analyze how groups reach agreement through debate or deliberation. For example, simulations can evaluate whether introducing a new argument shifts the outcome of a debate by providing an additional truth value.[^o2]
