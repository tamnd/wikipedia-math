# Susan Montgomery

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Susan Montgomery"}

**M. Susan Montgomery** (born 2 April 1943 in Lansing, MI) is a distinguished American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) whose current research interests concern [noncommutative algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_algebra): in particular, [Hopf algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_algebras), their structure and representations, and their actions on other algebras. Her early research was on [group actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on [rings](wikipedia://en/48404).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Montgomery received her B.A. in 1965 from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) and her Ph.D. in Mathematics from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1969 under the supervision of [I. N. Herstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Nathan_Herstein).

## Career {#Career}

Upon receiving her Ph.D. from Chicago, Montgomery spent one year on the faculty at [DePaul University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DePaul_University). Montgomery joined the faculty of the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) (USC) in 1970 and was promoted to the rank of Professor in 1982. She was chair of the Department of Mathematics at USC from 1996 to 1999.[^o6] Montgomery has spent sabbaticals at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds), the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin), [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich), the [University of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_South_Wales), the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452), and the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute).

Montgomery wrote about a hundred research articles and several books, of which *Hopf algebras and their actions on rings* is her most cited work. This book includes a discussion of Hopf-Galois theory, an area to which Montgomery has significantly contributed, and an introduction to quantum group theory.

## Honors {#Honors}

Montgomery was awarded a [Guggenheim Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Frank_Guggenheim_Foundation)[^o8] Fellowship in 1984 and a Raubenheimer Outstanding Faculty Award by USC in 1987.

She gave an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) Invited Address at the [Joint Mathematics Meetings](wikipedia://en/14985143) in 1984. In 1995 she gave an Invited Address at the Joint AMS-Israel Math Union Meeting in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem).[^o9] In 2009, she gave a plenary lecture at the summer meeting of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055).[^o10] She has also given numerous lectures at meetings and universities around the world.

Montgomery was the Principal Lecturer at the [Conference Board of the Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/13673454) (CBMS) 1992 Conference on Hopf Algebras. Her CBMS monograph *Hopf Algebras and their Actions on Rings*[^o11] is highly cited. She has written one other book and has edited five collections of research articles.

She served as an editor for the *[Journal of Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Algebra)* for over 20 years. She was also an editor for the *[AMS Proceedings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society)*, *AMS Mathematical Surveys and Monographs*, and *[Advances in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advances_in_Mathematics)*, and currently is on the editorial boards of *Algebras and Representation Theory*[^o12] and of *[Algebra and Number Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_and_Number_Theory)*.[^o13]

Montgomery has been very active in the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), serving on the Board of Trustees from 1986–1996.[^o14] She has also served on the Council, the Policy Committee on Publications,[^o15] and on the Nominating Committee.[^o16]

In 2013 she was elected to a 3-year term as a Vice-President of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o17] She was also a member of the [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council)'s Board on Mathematical Sciences and Their Applications (BMSA), serving one year on the Executive Committee.

In 2012 she was selected a Fellow of the American Mathematical Society[^o18][^o19] and a Fellow of the [AAAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science).[^o20][^o21]
