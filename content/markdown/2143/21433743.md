# Walter Warner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Walter Warner"}

**Walter Warner** (1563–1643) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Leicestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicestershire) and educated at [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford), graduating B.A. in 1578.[^o3]

At the end of the sixteenth century he belonged to the circle round [Henry Percy, 9th Earl of Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Percy,_9th_Earl_of_Northumberland), the 'Wizard Earl'. The Earl's ‘three magi’ were Warner, [Thomas Harriot](wikipedia://en/780445) and [Robert Hues](wikipedia://en/19785484).[^o4] Percy paid Warner a retainer to help him with alchemical experiments (£20 per annum in 1595, rising to £40 in 1607).[^o5] He also belonged to the overlapping group around [Sir Walter Raleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Walter_Raleigh). At this time he was mainly known for chemical and medical interests.[^o6] It has been argued by Jean Jacquot that this group of experimental researchers, sponsored by Percy and Raleigh, represents the transitional moment from the still-magical theories of [Giordano Bruno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giordano_Bruno) to real science.[^o7]

He may have been associated with [Christopher Marlowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Marlowe)'s study group on religion, branded [atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist), but confusion is possible here with [William Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Warner_%28poet%29).[^o8]

After Henry Percy's death, he was supported by [Algernon Percy, 10th Earl of Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algernon_Percy,_10th_Earl_of_Northumberland), and then [Sir Thomas Aylesbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Thomas_Aylesbury,_1st_Baronet).[^o3] Warner edited Harriot's *Artis Analyticae Praxis* in 1631.[^o9] He met [Thomas Hobbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hobbes) through [Sir Charles Cavendish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Charles_Cavendish), who circulated Warner's works.[^o10]

Warner was a friend of [Robert Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Payne_%28clergyman%29), chaplain to Cavendish;[^o11] and this connection is frequently used to associate Warner with the [Welbeck Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welbeck_Academy).[^o12] In 1634 Warner and Hobbes discussed [refraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refraction).[^o13] This acquaintance was later brought up against Hobbes in the [Hobbes-Wallis controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobbes-Wallis_controversy).[^o14]

With [John Pell](wikipedia://en/491570) he computed the first table of [antilogarithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilogarithm) in the 1630s.[^o15] [John Aubrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Aubrey), relying on Pell's testimony, states that Warner had claimed to have anticipated [William Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Harvey)'s discovery of the [circulation of the blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulation_of_the_blood), and that Harvey must have heard of it through a Mr Prothero. Pell also mentioned that Warner had been born without a left hand.[^o16]

## Scientific work and legacy {#Scientific_work_and_legacy}

Warner was unpublished in his lifetime, but well known, in particular to [Marin Mersenne](wikipedia://en/72947) who published some of his optical work in *Universae geometriae* (1646). He was an [atomist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomist), and a believer in an [infinite universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steady_State_theory). He was both a theoretical and practical chemist, and wrote psychological works based on Bruno and [Lullism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullism). Many manuscripts of his survive, and show eclectic interests; they include works related to the circulation of the blood.[^o3] Some of Warner's papers ended up in the Pell manuscripts collected by [Richard Busby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Busby);[^o18] after his death the bulk of his papers were seized in 1644 by superstitious sequestrators.[^o19] George John Gray, writing in the *[Dictionary of National Biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography)*, states that the table of 11-figure antilogarithms later published by [James Dodson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dodson_%28mathematician%29) was believed to have passed to [Herbert Thorndike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Thorndike), and then to Busby; Pell's account in 1644 was that Warner had been bankrupt, and the creditors were likely to destroy the work.
