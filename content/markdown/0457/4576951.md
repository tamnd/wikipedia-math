# Convergence tests

Series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Convergence tests"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **convergence tests** are methods of testing for the [convergence](wikipedia://en/1743431), [conditional convergence](wikipedia://en/1198956), [absolute convergence](wikipedia://en/61532), [interval of convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_of_convergence) or divergence of an [infinite series](wikipedia://en/15287) $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$.

:::

## List of tests {#List_of_tests}

### [Limit of the summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_test) {#Limit_of_the_summand}

If the limit of the summand is undefined or nonzero, that is $\lim_{n \to \infty}a_n \ne 0$, then the series must diverge. In this sense, the partial sums are [Cauchy](wikipedia://en/6085) [only if](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Only_if) this limit exists and is equal to zero. The test is inconclusive if the limit of the summand is zero. This is also known as the **nth-term test**, **test for divergence**, or **the divergence test**.

### [Ratio test](wikipedia://en/421341) {#Ratio_test}

This is also known as **d'Alembert's criterion**.

Consider two limits $\ell=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|$ and $L=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|$. If $\ell>1$, the series diverges. If $L<1$ then the series converges absolutely. If $\ell\le1\le L$ then the test is inconclusive, and the series may converge absolutely or conditionally or diverge.

### [Root test](wikipedia://en/1470603) {#Root_test}

This is also known as the ***n*th root test** or **Cauchy's criterion**.

Let

$$r=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{|a_n|},$$

where $\limsup$ denotes the [limit superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_superior) (possibly $\infty$; if the limit exists it is the same value).

If *r* \< 1, then the series converges absolutely. If *r* > 1, then the series diverges. If *r* = 1, the root test is inconclusive, and the series may converge or diverge.

The root test is stronger than the ratio test: whenever the ratio test determines the convergence or divergence of an infinite series, the root test does too, but not conversely.[^o6]

### [Integral test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_test) {#Integral_test}

The series can be compared to an integral to establish convergence or divergence. Let $f:[1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}_+$ be a non-negative and [monotonically decreasing function](wikipedia://en/48260) such that $f(n) = a_n$. If

$$\int_1^\infty f(x) \, dx=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_1^t f(x) \, dx<\infty,$$

then the series converges. But if the integral diverges, then the series does so as well. In other words, the series ${a_n}$ converges [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the integral converges.

#### $p$-series test {#-series_test}

A commonly used corollary of the integral test is the p-series test. Let $k > 0$. Then $\sum_{n=k}^{\infty} \bigg(\frac{1}{n^p}\bigg)$ converges if $p > 1$.

The case of $p = 1, k = 1$ yields the harmonic series, which diverges. The case of $p = 2, k = 1$ is the [Basel problem](wikipedia://en/490303) and the series converges to $\frac{\pi^2}{6}$. In general, for $p > 1, k = 1$, the series is equal to the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) applied to $p$, that is $\zeta(p)$.

### [Direct comparison test](wikipedia://en/1601407) {#Direct_comparison_test}

If the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n$ is an [absolutely convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_convergent) series and $|a_n|\le |b_n|$ for sufficiently large *n* , then the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges absolutely.

### [Limit comparison test](wikipedia://en/11030945) {#Limit_comparison_test}

If $\{a_n\},\{b_n\}>0$, (that is, each element of the two sequences is positive) and the limit $\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n}$ exists, is finite and non-zero, then either both series converge or both series diverge.

### [Cauchy condensation test](wikipedia://en/3497105) {#Cauchy_condensation_test}

Let $\left \{ a_n \right \}$ be a non-negative non-increasing sequence. Then the sum $A = \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the sum $A^* = \sum_{n=0}^\infty 2^n a_{2^n}$ converges. Moreover, if they converge, then $A \leq A^* \leq 2A$ holds.

### [Abel's test](wikipedia://en/7088921) {#Abel's_test}

Suppose the following statements are true:

1. $\sum a_n$ is a convergent series,
1. $\left\{b_n\right\}$ is a monotonic sequence, and
1. $\left\{b_n\right\}$ is bounded.

Then $\sum a_nb_n$ is also convergent.

### [Absolute convergence test](wikipedia://en/61532) {#Absolute_convergence_test}

Every [absolutely convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_convergent) series converges.

### [Alternating series test](wikipedia://en/1598759) {#Alternating_series_test}

Suppose the following statements are true:

- $(a_n)_{n=1}^\infty$ is monotonic,
- $$\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$$

Then $\sum_{n = 1}^\infty (-1)^{n} a_n$ and $\sum_{n = 1}^\infty (-1)^{n+1} a_n$ are convergent series. This test is also known as the **Leibniz criterion**.

### [Dirichlet's test](wikipedia://en/3141677) {#Dirichlet's_test}

If $\{a_n\}$ is a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) and $\{b_n\}$ a sequence of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) satisfying

- $$a_n \geq a_{n+1}$$
- $$\lim_{n \rightarrow \infty}a_n = 0$$
- $\left|\sum^{N}_{n=1}b_n\right|\leq M$ for every positive integer *N*

where *M* is some constant, then the series

$$\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}a_n b_n$$

converges.

### [Cauchy's convergence test](wikipedia://en/3738733) {#Cauchy's_convergence_test}

A series $\sum_{i=0}^\infty a_i$ is convergent if and only if for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a natural number *N* such that

$$|a_{n+1}+a_{n+2}+\cdots+a_{n+p}|<\varepsilon$$

holds for all *n* > *N* and all *p* ≥ 1.

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Stolz–Cesàro theorem"}

### [Stolz–Cesàro theorem](wikipedia://en/2874706) {#Stolz–Cesàro_theorem}

Let $(a_n)_{n \geq 1}$ and $(b_n)_{n \geq 1}$ be two sequences of real numbers. Assume that $(b_n)_{n \geq 1}$ is a [strictly monotone](wikipedia://en/48260) and divergent sequence and the following limit exists:

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_{n+1}-a_n}{b_{n+1}-b_n}=l.\$$

Then, the limit

$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n}=l.\$$

:::

### [Weierstrass M-test](wikipedia://en/444456) {#Weierstrass_M-test}

Suppose that (*f*<sub>*n*</sub>) is a sequence of real- or complex-valued functions defined on a set *A*, and that there is a sequence of non-negative numbers (*M*<sub>*n*</sub>) satisfying the conditions

- $|f_n(x)|\leq M_n$ for all $n \geq 1$ and all $x \in A$, and
- $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} M_n$ converges.

Then the series

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f_n (x)$$

converges absolutely and [uniformly](wikipedia://en/50652) on *A*.

### Extensions to the ratio test {#Extensions_to_the_ratio_test}

The ratio test may be inconclusive when the limit of the ratio is 1. Extensions to the ratio test, however, sometimes allows one to deal with this case.

#### [Raabe–Duhamel's test](wikipedia://en/421341#2._Raabe's_test) {#Raabe–Duhamel's_test}

Let { *a*<sub>*n*</sub> } be a sequence of positive numbers.

Define

$$b_n=n\left(\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}-1 \right).$$

If

$$L=\lim_{n\to\infty}b_n$$

exists there are three possibilities:

- if *L* > 1 the series converges (this includes the case *L* = ∞)
- if *L* \< 1 the series diverges
- and if *L* = 1 the test is inconclusive.

An alternative formulation of this test is as follows. Let { *a*<sub>*n*</sub> } be a series of real numbers. Then if *b* > 1 and *K* (a natural number) exist such that

$$\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|\le 1-\frac{b}{n}$$

for all *n* > *K* then the series {*a*<sub>*n*</sub>} is convergent.

#### [Bertrand's test](wikipedia://en/421341#3._Bertrand's_test) {#Bertrand's_test}

Let { *a*<sub>*n*</sub> } be a sequence of positive numbers.

Define

$$b_n=\ln n\left(n\left(\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}-1 \right)-1\right).$$

If

$$L=\lim_{n\to\infty}b_n$$

exists, there are three possibilities:[^o23][^o24]

- if *L* > 1 the series converges (this includes the case *L* = ∞)
- if *L* \< 1 the series diverges
- and if *L* = 1 the test is inconclusive.

::: {#o26 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Gauss's test"}

#### [Gauss's test](wikipedia://en/421341#5._Gauss's_test) {#Gauss's_test}

Let { *a*<sub>*n*</sub> } be a sequence of positive numbers. If $\frac{a_n}{a_{n + 1}} = 1+ \frac{\alpha}{n} + O(1/n^\beta)$ for some β > 1, then $\sum a_n$ converges if $\alpha > 1$ and diverges if $\alpha \le 1$.[^o27]

:::

#### [Kummer's test](wikipedia://en/421341#6._Kummer's_test) {#Kummer's_test}

Let { *a*<sub>*n*</sub> } be a sequence of positive numbers. Then:[^o29][^o30][^o31]

(1) $\sum a_n$ converges if and only if there is a sequence $b_{n}$ of positive numbers and a real number *c* > 0 such that $b_k (a_{k}/a_{k+1}) - b_{k+1} \ge c$.

(2) $\sum a_n$ diverges if and only if there is a sequence $b_{n}$ of positive numbers such that $b_k (a_{k}/a_{k+1}) - b_{k+1} \le 0$

and $\sum 1/b_{n}$ diverges.

### Abu-Mostafa's test {#Abu-Mostafa's_test}

Let $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ be an infinite series with real terms and let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be any real function such that $f(1/n)=a_n$ for all positive integers *n* and the second derivative $f''$ exists at $x=0$. Then $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges absolutely if $f(0)=f'(0)=0$ and diverges otherwise.[^o33]

### Notes {#Notes}

- For some specific types of series there are more specialized convergence tests, for instance for [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) there is the [Dini test](wikipedia://en/954539).

## Examples {#Examples}

Consider the series

::: {#o37 .equation cue=numblk name="i"}

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^\alpha}. \tag{i}$$

:::

[Cauchy condensation test](wikipedia://en/3497105) implies that ((i)) is finitely convergent if

::: {#o38 .equation cue=numblk name="ii"}

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty 2^n \left( \frac 1 {2^n}\right)^\alpha \tag{ii}$$

:::

is finitely convergent. Since

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty 2^n \left( \frac 1 {2^n} \right)^\alpha = \sum_{n=1}^\infty 2^{n-n\alpha} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty 2^{(1-\alpha) n}$$

((ii)) is a geometric series with ratio $2^{(1-\alpha)}$. ((ii)) is finitely convergent if its ratio is less than one (namely $\alpha > 1$). Thus, ((i)) is finitely convergent [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $\alpha > 1$.

:::

## Convergence of products {#Convergence_of_products}

While most of the tests deal with the convergence of infinite series, they can also be used to show the convergence or divergence of [infinite products](wikipedia://en/26382006). This can be achieved using following theorem: Let $\left \{ a_n \right \}_{n=1}^\infty$ be a sequence of positive numbers. Then the infinite product $\prod_{n=1}^\infty (1 + a_n)$ converges [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges. Also similarly, if $0 \leq a_n < 1$ holds, then $\prod_{n=1}^\infty (1 - a_n)$ approaches a non-zero limit if and only if the series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ converges .

This can be proved by taking the logarithm of the product and using limit comparison test.[^o40]
