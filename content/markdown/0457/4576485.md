# Brauer's theorem on forms

*There also is [Brauer's theorem on induced characters](wikipedia://en/3053436).*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brauer's theorem on forms"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Brauer's theorem**, named for [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134), is a result on the representability of 0 by forms over certain [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) in sufficiently many variables.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Statement of Brauer's theorem"}

## Statement of Brauer's theorem {#Statement_of_Brauer's_theorem}

Let *K* be a field such that for every integer *r* > 0 there exists an integer ψ(*r*) such that for *n* ≥ ψ(r) every equation

$$(*)\qquad a_1x_1^r+\cdots+a_nx_n^r=0,\quad a_i\in K,\quad i=1,\ldots,n$$

has a non-trivial (i.e. not all *x*<sub>*i*</sub> are equal to 0) solution in *K*. Then, given [homogeneous polynomials](wikipedia://en/1698977) *f*<sub>1</sub>,...,*f*<sub>*k*</sub> of degrees *r*<sub>1</sub>,...,*r*<sub>*k*</sub> respectively with coefficients in *K*, for every set of positive integers *r*<sub>1</sub>,...,*r*<sub>*k*</sub> and every non-negative integer *l*, there exists a number ω(*r*<sub>1</sub>,...,*r*<sub>*k*</sub>,*l*) such that for *n* ≥ ω(*r*<sub>1</sub>,...,*r*<sub>*k*</sub>,*l*) there exists an *l*-dimensional [affine subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_subspace) *M* of *K<sup>n</sup>* (regarded as a vector space over *K*) satisfying

$$f_1(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\cdots=f_k(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=0,\quad\forall(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in M.$$

:::

## An application to the field of p-adic numbers {#An_application_to_the_field_of_p-adic_numbers}

Letting *K* be the field of [p-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423) in the theorem, the equation (\*) is satisfied, since $\mathbb{Q}_p^*/\left(\mathbb{Q}_p^*\right)^b$, *b* a natural number, is finite. Choosing *k* = 1, one obtains the following corollary:

A homogeneous equation *f*(*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>) = 0 of degree *r* in the field of p-adic numbers has a non-trivial solution if *n* is sufficiently large.

One can show that if *n* is sufficiently large according to the above corollary, then *n* is greater than *r*<sup>2</sup>. Indeed, [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) conjectured[^o6] that every homogeneous polynomial of degree *r* over **Q**<sub>*p*</sub> in more than *r*<sup>2</sup> variables represents 0. This is obviously true for *r* = 1, and it is well known that the conjecture is true for *r* = 2 (see, for example, J.-P. Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, Chapter IV, Theorem 6). See [quasi-algebraic closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-algebraic_closure) for further context.

In 1950 Demyanov[^o7] verified the conjecture for *r* = 3 and *p* ≠ 3, and in 1952 [D. J. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._J._Lewis)[^o8] independently proved the case *r* = 3 for all primes *p*. But in 1966 [Guy Terjanian](wikipedia://en/7354718) constructed a homogeneous polynomial of degree 4 over **Q**<sub>2</sub> in 18 variables that has no non-trivial zero.[^o9] On the other hand, the [Ax–Kochen theorem](wikipedia://en/5911859) shows that for any fixed degree Artin's conjecture is true for all but finitely many **Q**<sub>*p*</sub>.
