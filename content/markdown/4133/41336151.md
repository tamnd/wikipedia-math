# Michael Struwe

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Struwe"}

**Michael Struwe** (born 6 October 1955 in [Wuppertal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuppertal)) is a German mathematician who specializes in [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) and [nonlinear partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/16678376). He won the 2012 [Cantor medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor_medal) from the [Deutsche Mathematiker-Vereinigung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mathematiker-Vereinigung) for "outstanding achievements in the field of geometric analysis, calculus of variations and nonlinear partial differential equations".[^o3]

:::

He studied mathematics at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), gaining his PhD in 1980 with the title *Infinitely Many Solutions for Superlinear, Anticoercive Elliptic Boundary Value Problems without Oddness*.[^o4] He took research positions in Paris and at [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich) before gaining his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in Bonn in 1984. Since 1986, he has been working at ETH Zürich, initially as an assistant professor, becoming a full professor in 1993.[^o3] His specialisms included nonlinear partial differential equations and calculus of variations.

He is joint editor of the journals *Calculus of Variations*, *[Commentarii Mathematici Helvetici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commentarii_Mathematici_Helvetici)*, *International Mathematical Research Notices* and *Mathematische Zeitschrift*.

His publications include the book *Variational methods (Applications to nonlinear PDE and Hamiltonian systems)* (Springer-Verlag, 1990), which was praised by [Jürgen Jost](wikipedia://en/41172240) as "very useful" with an "impressive range of often difficult examples".[^o5]

Struwe was awarded a [Gauss Lecture](wikipedia://en/48440954) by the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973) in 2011. In 2012, Struwe was selected as one of the inaugural fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o6]
