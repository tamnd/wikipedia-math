# William Dunham (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Dunham"}

**William Wade Dunham** (born 1947) is an American [writer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_writing) who was originally trained in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) but became interested in the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) and specializes in [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902). He has received several awards for writing and teaching on this subject.[^o2]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Dunham received his bachelor's degree from the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) in 1969, his master's degree from [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) in 1970, and his PhD from the same institution in 1974.[^o4]

## Writings {#Writings}

Dunham won the American Association of Publishers' award for writing the Best Mathematics Book of 1994 for his book *The Mathematical Universe*.[^o2] In his book *Euler: The Master of Us All*, he examines Leonhard Euler's impressive mathematical work.[^o6][^o7] He received a [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) in 2006 for his expository article *Touring the Calculus*,[^o8] and the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) in 2022 for his article on the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961).[^o9]

In 2007, Dunham gave a lecture about Euler's product-sum formula and its relationship to [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), as well as discussed Euler's evaluation of a non-trivial integral at the celebration of "Year of Euler" by the [Euler Society](wikipedia://en/30025659).[^o10] He published a chapter "Euler and the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra" in the book *The Genius of Euler* published in 2007 to commemorate the 300th birthday of Euler.[^o11][^o12]
