# Fejér County

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fejér County"}

Fejér vármegye

**Fejér** (Fejér vármegye, ) is an [administrative county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Hungary) in central [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary). It lies on the west bank of the river [Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube) and nearly touches the eastern shore of [Lake Balaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Balaton). It shares borders with the Hungarian counties [Veszprém](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veszprém_County), [Komárom-Esztergom County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komárom-Esztergom_County), [Pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest_County), [Bács-Kiskun County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bács-Kiskun_County), [Tolna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolna_County) and [Somogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somogy_County). The capital of Fejér county is [Székesfehérvár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Székesfehérvár).

:::

## Geography {#Geography}

Geographically, Fejér County is very diverse; its southern part is similar (and adjacent) to the [Great Hungarian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hungarian_Plain), and other parts are hilly ([Bakony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakony), [Vértes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vértes_Mountains), [Gerecse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerecse) mountains). [Lake Velence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Velence), a popular resort, is also located within the county.

## History {#History}

### Early history {#Early_history}

The area was already inhabited 2,000 years ago. When this part of Hungary formed a [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) province called *[Pannonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonia)*, several settlements stood here: the capital was Gorsium, but there were other significant towns too, including those located where present-day Baracs and Dunaújváros are (the towns were called Annamatia and Intercisa, respectively). In the early medieval period Huns and Avars lived in the area. After 586 several nomadic people inhabited the area, until Hungarians conquered it in the late 9th century.

### From the Roman conquest until the Ottoman occupation {#From_the_Roman_conquest_until_the_Ottoman_occupation}

Hungarians arrived in the area between 895 and 900. The high prince and his tribe settled down in this area. The town of *Fehérvár* (modern-day Székesfehérvár) became significant as the seat of Prince [Géza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Géza). Under the reign of his son, [King Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Hungary), the town became the county seat of the newly formed county. Kings of Hungary were crowned and buried in the town until the 16th century.

#### Fejér under Ottoman rule {#Fejér_under_Ottoman_rule}

Fejér county was occupied by Ottomans between 1543 and 1688. Several of the villages were destroyed, the population dramatically decreased. After being freed from Ottoman rule, local administration was reorganized in 1692. Székesfehérvár got back its town status only in 1703.

### Ethnicity {#Ethnicity}

Besides the Hungarian majority, the main minorities are the Roma (approx. 6,500), Germans (5,500).

Total population (2011 census): 425,847 Ethnic groups (2011 census):[^o16] Identified themselves: 372 538 persons:

- [Hungarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarians): 356 982 (95,82%)
- [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Hungary): 6 277 (1,68%)
- [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans_of_Hungary): 5 419 (1,45%)
- Others and indefinable: 3 860 (1,04%)

Approx. 65,000 persons in Fejér County did not declare their ethnic group at the 2011 census.
