# Triacontagon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Triacontagon"}

Regular polygon stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **triacontagon** or 30-gon is a thirty-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621). The sum of any triacontagon's interior angles is [5040](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5040_%28number%29) degrees.

:::

## Regular triacontagon {#Regular_triacontagon}

The *[regular](wikipedia://en/333306) triacontagon* is a [constructible polygon](wikipedia://en/523430), by an edge-[bisection](wikipedia://en/152547) of a regular [pentadecagon](wikipedia://en/1496229), and can also be constructed as a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [pentadecagon](wikipedia://en/1496229), t{15}. A [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) triacontagon, t{30}, is a [hexacontagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexacontagon), {60}.

One interior angle in a [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) triacontagon is 168 degrees, meaning that one exterior angle would be 12°. The triacontagon is the largest regular polygon whose interior angle is the sum of the [interior angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_angles) of smaller [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621): 168° is the sum of the interior angles of the [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) (60°) and the [regular pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon) (108°).

The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a regular triacontagon is (with *t* = edge length)

$$A = \frac{15}{2} t^2 \cot \frac{\pi}{30} = \frac{15}{4} t^2 \left(\sqrt{15} + 3\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}\sqrt{25+11\sqrt{5}}\right)$$

The [inradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inradius) of a regular triacontagon is

$$r = \frac{1}{2} t \cot \frac{\pi}{30} = \frac{1}{4} t \left(\sqrt{15} + 3\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}\sqrt{25+11\sqrt{5}}\right)$$

The [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) of a regular triacontagon is

$$R = \frac{1}{2} t \csc \frac{\pi}{30} = \frac{1}{2} t \left(2 + \sqrt{5} + \sqrt{15+6\sqrt{5}}\right)$$

### Construction {#Construction}

*Image: 01-Dreißigeck.svg.* Regular triacontagon with given circumcircle. D is the midpoint of AM, DC = DF, and CF, which is the side length of the regular [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014), is E<sub>25</sub>E<sub>1</sub>. Since 1/30 = 1/5 - 1/6, the difference between the arcs subtended by the sides of a regular pentagon and hexagon (E<sub>25</sub>E<sub>1</sub> and E<sub>25</sub>A) is that of the regular triacontagon, AE<sub>1</sub>.

As 30 = 2 × 3 × 5 , a regular triacontagon is [constructible](wikipedia://en/523430) using a [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge).[^o6]

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Symmetries of triacontagon.png.* The symmetries of a regular triacontagon as shown with colors on edges and vertices. Lines of reflections are blue through vertices, and purple through edges. Gyrations are given as numbers in the center. Vertices are colored by their symmetry positions. Subgroup symmetries are connected by colored lines, index 2, 3, and 5.

The *regular triacontagon* has Dih<sub>30</sub> [dihedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry), order 60, represented by 30 lines of reflection. Dih<sub>30</sub> has 7 dihedral subgroups: Dih<sub>15</sub>, (Dih<sub>10</sub>, Dih<sub>5</sub>), (Dih<sub>6</sub>, Dih<sub>3</sub>), and (Dih<sub>2</sub>, Dih<sub>1</sub>). It also has eight more [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) symmetries as subgroups: (Z<sub>30</sub>, Z<sub>15</sub>), (Z<sub>10</sub>, Z<sub>5</sub>), (Z<sub>6</sub>, Z<sub>3</sub>), and (Z<sub>2</sub>, Z<sub>1</sub>), with Z<sub>n</sub> representing π/*n* radian rotational symmetry.

[John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) labels these lower symmetries with a letter and order of the symmetry follows the letter.[^o9] He gives **d** (diagonal) with mirror lines through vertices, **p** with mirror lines through edges (perpendicular), **i** with mirror lines through both vertices and edges, and **g** for rotational symmetry. **a1** labels no symmetry.

These lower symmetries allows degrees of freedoms in defining irregular triacontagons. Only the **g30** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

:::

## Dissection {#Dissection}

*Image: 30-gon rhombic dissection-size2.svg.* 30-gon with 420 rhombs

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) states that every [zonogon](wikipedia://en/55312103) (a 2*m*-gon whose opposite sides are parallel and of equal length) can be dissected into *m*(*m*-1)/2 parallelograms.[^o11] In particular this is true for regular polygons with evenly many sides, in which case the parallelograms are all rhombi. For the *regular triacontagon*, *m*=15, it can be divided into 105: 7 sets of 15 rhombs. This decomposition is based on a [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) projection of a [15-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15-cube).

## Triacontagram {#Triacontagram}

## Petrie polygons {#Petrie_polygons}

The regular triacontagon is the [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) for three 8-dimensional polytopes with E<sub>8</sub> symmetry, shown in [orthogonal projections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) in the E<sub>8</sub> [Coxeter plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane). It is also the Petrie polygon for two 4-dimensional polytopes, shown in the H<sub>4</sub> [Coxeter plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane).

The regular triacontagram {30/7} is also the Petrie polygon for the [great grand stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604513) and [grand 600-cell](wikipedia://en/8604538).
