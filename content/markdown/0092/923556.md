# Classifying space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Classifying space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), a **classifying space** *BG* of a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) *G* is the quotient of a [weakly contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_contractible) space *EG* (i.e., a topological space all of whose [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) are trivial) by a proper [free action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_action) of *G*. It has the property that any *G* [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) over a [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact) manifold is isomorphic to a [pullback](wikipedia://en/1665392) of the principal bundle $EG \to BG$.[^o2] As explained later, this means that classifying spaces [represent](wikipedia://en/844461) a set-valued [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) on the [homotopy category](wikipedia://en/2040756) of topological spaces. The term classifying space can also be used for spaces that represent a set-valued functor on the category of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450), such as [Sierpiński space](wikipedia://en/387651). This notion is generalized by the notion of [classifying topos](wikipedia://en/39651142). However, the rest of this article discusses the more commonly used notion of classifying space up to homotopy.

:::

For a [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536) *G*, *BG* is a [path-connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* such that the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of *X* is isomorphic to *G* and the higher [homotopy groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_groups) of *X* are [trivial](wikipedia://en/193059); that is, *BG* is an [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230), specifically a *K*(*G*, 1).

## Motivation {#Motivation}

An example of a classifying space for the [infinite cyclic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_cyclic_group) *G* is the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) as *X*. When *G* is a [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536), another way to specify the condition on *X* is that the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) *Y* of *X* is [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible). In that case the projection map

$$\pi\colon Y\longrightarrow X\$$

becomes a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) with structure group *G*, in fact a [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) for *G*. The interest in the classifying space concept really arises from the fact that in this case *Y* has a [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) with respect to principal *G*-bundles, in the [homotopy category](wikipedia://en/2040756). This is actually more basic than the condition that the higher homotopy groups vanish: the fundamental idea is, given *G*, to find such a contractible space *Y* on which *G* acts *[freely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29#Types_of_actions)*. (The [weak equivalence](wikipedia://en/34615176) idea of homotopy theory relates the two versions.) In the case of the circle example, what is being said is that we remark that an infinite cyclic group *C* acts freely on the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) *R*, which is contractible. Taking *X* as the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) circle, we can regard the projection π from *R* = *Y* to *X* as a [helix](wikipedia://en/179924) in geometrical terms, undergoing projection from three dimensions to the plane. What is being claimed is that π has a universal property amongst principal *C*-bundles; that any principal *C*-bundle in a definite way 'comes from' π.

## Formalism {#Formalism}

A more formal statement takes into account that *G* may be a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) (not simply a *discrete group*), and that [group actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of *G* are taken to be continuous; in the absence of continuous actions the classifying space concept can be dealt with, in homotopy terms, via the [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) construction. In homotopy theory the definition of a topological space *BG*, the **classifying space** for principal *G*-bundles, is given, together with the space *EG* which is the **total space** of the [universal bundle](wikipedia://en/2179570) over *BG*. That is, what is provided is in fact a [continuous mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_mapping)

$$\pi\colon EG\longrightarrow BG.$$

Assume that the homotopy category of [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022) is the underlying category, from now on. The *classifying* property required of *BG* in fact relates to π. We must be able to say that given any principal *G*-bundle

$$\gamma\colon Y\longrightarrow Z\$$

over a space *Z*, there is a **classifying map** φ from *Z* to *BG*, such that $\gamma$ is the [pullback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullback_of_a_bundle) of π along φ. In less abstract terms, the construction of $\gamma$ by 'twisting' should be reducible via φ to the twisting already expressed by the construction of π.

For this to be a useful concept, there evidently must be some reason to believe such spaces *BG* exist. The early work on classifying spaces introduced constructions (for example, the [bar construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_construction)), that gave concrete descriptions of *BG* as a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686) for an arbitrary discrete group. Such constructions make evident the connection with [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635).

Specifically, let *EG* be the [weak simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/20403801) whose *n-* simplices are the ordered (*n*+1)-tuples $[g_0,\ldots,g_n]$ of elements of *G*. Such an *n-*simplex attaches to the (n−1) simplices $[g_0,\ldots,\hat g_i,\ldots,g_n]$ in the same way a standard simplex attaches to its faces, where $\hat g_i$ means this vertex is deleted. The complex EG is contractible. The group *G* acts on *EG* by left multiplication,

$$g\cdot[g_0,\ldots,g_n ]=[gg_0,\ldots,gg_n],$$

and only the identity *e* takes any simplex to itself. Thus the action of *G* on *EG* is a covering space action and the quotient map $EG\to EG/G$ is the universal cover of the orbit space $BG = EG/G$, and *BG* is a $K(G,1)$.[^o5]

In abstract terms (which are not those originally used around 1950 when the idea was first introduced) this is a question of whether a certain functor is [representable](wikipedia://en/844461): the [contravariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) from the homotopy category to the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), defined by

*h*(*Z*) = set of isomorphism classes of principal *G*-bundles on *Z.*

The abstract conditions being known for this ([Brown's representability theorem](wikipedia://en/1073746)) ensure that the result, as an [existence theorem](wikipedia://en/251177), is affirmative and not too difficult.

## Examples {#Examples}

1. The [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) $S^1$ is a classifying space for the [infinite cyclic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_cyclic_group) $\mathbb{Z}.$ The total space is $E\mathbb{Z} =\mathbb{R}.$
1. The [*n*-torus](wikipedia://en/74800) $\mathbb T^n$ is a classifying space for $\mathbb{Z}^n$, the [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) of rank *n*. The total space is $E\mathbb{Z}^n=\mathbb{R}^n.$
1. The [wedge](wikipedia://en/684143) of *n* circles is a classifying space for the [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) of rank *n*.
1. A [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) (that is, [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) and without boundary) connected [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) *S* of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) at least 1 is a classifying space for its [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) $\pi_1(S).$
1. A [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) (that is, [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) and without boundary) connected [hyperbolic manifold](wikipedia://en/1589135) *M* is a classifying space for its [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) $\pi_1(M)$.
1. A finite locally connected [CAT(0)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_space) [cubical complex](wikipedia://en/54829672) is a classifying space of its [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004).
1. The [infinite-dimensional projective space](wikipedia://en/1014694#Infinite_real_projective_space) $\mathbb{RP}^\infty$ (the direct limit of finite-dimensional projective spaces) is a classifying space for the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}_2 = \mathbb{Z} /2\mathbb{Z}.$ The total space is $E\mathbb{Z}_2 = S^\infty$ (the direct limit of spheres $S^n.$ Alternatively, one may use Hilbert space with the origin removed; it is contractible).
1. The space $B\mathbb{Z}_n = S^\infty / \mathbb{Z}_n$ is the classifying space for the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) $\mathbb{Z}_n.$ Here, $S^\infty$ is understood to be a certain subset of the infinite dimensional Hilbert space $\mathbb{C}^\infty$ with the origin removed; the cyclic group is considered to act on it by multiplication with roots of unity.
1. The unordered [configuration space](wikipedia://en/54981574) $\operatorname{UConf}_n(\mathbb{R}^2)$ is the classifying space of the [Artin braid group](wikipedia://en/550138) $B_n$,[^o8] and the ordered configuration space $\operatorname{Conf}_n(\mathbb{R}^2)$ is the classifying space for the pure Artin braid group $P_n.$
1. The (unordered) [configuration space](wikipedia://en/54981574) $\operatorname{UConf}_n(\mathbb{R}^\infty)$ is a classifying space for the symmetric group $S_n.$[^o9]
1. The infinite dimensional complex [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) $\mathbb{CP}^\infty$ is the classifying space $BS^{1}$ for the circle $S^{1}$ thought of as a compact topological group.
1. The [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) $Gr(n, \mathbb{R}^\infty)$ of *n*-planes in $\mathbb{R}^\infty$ is the classifying space of the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) $O(n)$. The total space is $EO(n) = V(n, \mathbb{R}^\infty)$, the [Stiefel manifold](wikipedia://en/1392495) of *n*-dimensional orthonormal frames in $\mathbb{R}^\infty.$

## Applications {#Applications}

This still leaves the question of doing effective calculations with *BG*; for example, the theory of [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530) is essentially the same as computing the [cohomology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group) of *BG*, at least within the restrictive terms of homotopy theory, for interesting groups *G* such as [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) ([H. Cartan's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Cartan's_theorem)). As was shown by the [Bott periodicity theorem](wikipedia://en/782099), the [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) of *BG* are also of fundamental interest.

An example of a classifying space is that when *G* is cyclic of order two; then *BG* is [real projective space](wikipedia://en/1014694) of infinite dimension, corresponding to the observation that *EG* can be taken as the contractible space resulting from removing the origin in an infinite-dimensional [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932), with *G* acting via *v* going to −*v*, and allowing for [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence) in choosing *BG*. This example shows that classifying spaces may be complicated.

In relation with [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) ([Chern–Weil theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern–Weil_theory)) and the theory of [Grassmannians](wikipedia://en/373810), a much more hands-on approach to the theory is possible for cases such as the [unitary groups](wikipedia://en/173993) that are of greatest interest. The construction of the [Thom complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_complex) *MG* showed that the spaces *BG* were also implicated in [cobordism theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobordism_theory), so that they assumed a central place in geometric considerations coming out of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). Since [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) can (in many cases) be defined by the use of classifying spaces, they can also be seen as foundational in much [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410).

Generalizations include those for classifying [foliations](wikipedia://en/371227), and the [classifying toposes](wikipedia://en/39651142) for logical theories of the predicate calculus in [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262) that take the place of a 'space of models'.
