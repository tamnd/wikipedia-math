# Silver ratio

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Silver ratio"}

In mathematics, the **silver ratio** is a geometrical [proportion](wikipedia://en/1021) with exact value $1 + \surd2,$ the positive [solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_root) of the equation $x^{2} = 2x + 1.$

:::

The name *silver ratio* is by analogy with the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386), the positive solution of the equation $x^{2} = x + 1.$

Although its name is recent, the silver ratio (or silver mean) has been studied since ancient times because of its connections to the [square root of 2](wikipedia://en/341682), almost-isosceles [Pythagorean triples](wikipedia://en/24172#Special_cases_and_related_equations), [square triangular numbers](wikipedia://en/416823), [Pell numbers](wikipedia://en/1355482), the [octagon](wikipedia://en/314575), and six [polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470) with [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805).

*Image: silver\_rectangle\_in\_octagon.svg.* Silver rectangle in a regular octagon.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If the ratio of two quantities $a > b > 0$ is proportionate to the sum of two and their reciprocal ratio, they are in the silver ratio:

$$\frac{a}{b} =\frac{2a+b}{a}$$

The ratio $\frac{a}{b}$ is here denoted $\sigma.$

Substituting $a=\sigma b \,$ in the second fraction,

$$\sigma =\frac{b(2\sigma +1)}{\sigma b}.$$

It follows that the silver ratio is the positive solution of [quadratic equation](wikipedia://en/25175) $\sigma^2 -2\sigma -1 =0.$ The [quadratic formula](wikipedia://en/59217) gives the two solutions $1 \pm \sqrt{2},$ the decimal expansion of the positive [root](wikipedia://en/264210) begins with $2.414213562373095$ .

Using the [tangent function](wikipedia://en/17699968)

$$\sigma =\tan \left( \frac{3\pi}{8} \right) =\cot \left( \frac{\pi}{8} \right),$$

or the [hyperbolic sine](wikipedia://en/56567)

$$\sigma =\exp( \operatorname{arsinh}(1) ).$$

$\sigma$ and its [algebraic conjugate](wikipedia://en/458467) can be written as sums of eighth [roots of unity](wikipedia://en/171950):

$$\begin{aligned} \text{with } \omega =&\ \exp(2\pi i/8) =\sqrt{i}, \\ \sigma &=\omega -\omega^4 +\omega^{-1} \\ -\sigma^{-1} &=\omega^3 -\omega^4 +\omega^{-3} ,\end{aligned}$$

which is guaranteed by the [Kronecker–Weber theorem](wikipedia://en/1082550).

$\sigma$ is the superstable [fixed point](wikipedia://en/7344320) of the [Newton iteration](wikipedia://en/22145) $x \gets \tfrac12 (x^2 +1) /(x -1), \text{ with } x_0 \in [2,3]$

The iteration $x \gets \sqrt{1 +2x \vphantom{/} }$ results in the [continued radical](wikipedia://en/2503693#Infinitely_nested_radicals)

$$\sigma =\sqrt{1 +2\sqrt{1 +2\sqrt{1 +\cdots}}}$$

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: SilverSquare\_6.svg.* Rectangles with aspect ratios related to $\sigma$ tile the square.

The defining equation can be written

$$\begin{aligned} 1 &=\frac{1}{\sigma -1} + \frac{1}{\sigma +1} \\ &=\frac{2}{\sigma +1} + \frac{1}{\sigma}.\end{aligned}$$

The silver ratio can be expressed in terms of itself as fractions

$$\begin{aligned} \sigma &=\frac{1}{\sigma -2} \\ \sigma^2 &=\frac{\sigma -1}{\sigma -2} +\frac{\sigma +1}{\sigma -1}.\end{aligned}$$

Similarly as the infinite [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630)

$$\begin{aligned} \sigma &=2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \sigma^{-2n} \\ \sigma^2 &=-1 +2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (\sigma -1)^{-n}.\end{aligned}$$

For every integer $n$ one has

$$\begin{aligned} \sigma^n &=2\sigma^{n-1} +\sigma^{n-2} \\ &=\sigma^{n-1} +3\sigma^{n-2} +\sigma^{n-3} \\ &=2\sigma^{n-1} +2\sigma^{n-3} +\sigma^{n-4} \end{aligned}$$

from this an infinite number of further relations can be found.

[Continued fraction](wikipedia://en/46802) pattern of a few low powers

$$\begin{aligned} \sigma^{-1} &=[0;2,2,2,2,...] \approx 0.4142 \;(17/41) \\ \sigma^0 &=[1] \\ \sigma^1 &=[2;2,2,2,2,...] \approx 2.4142 \;(70/29) \\ \sigma^2 &=[5;1,4,1,4,...] \approx 5.8284 \;(5 + 29/35) \\ \sigma^3 &=[14;14,14,14,...] \approx 14.0711 \;(14 + 1/14) \\ \sigma^4 &=[33;1,32,1,32,...] \approx 33.9706 \;(33 + 33/34) \\ \sigma^5 &=[82;82,82,82,...] \approx 82.0122 \;(82 + 1/82) \end{aligned}$$

$$\sigma^{-n} \equiv (-1)^{n-1} \sigma^n \bmod 1.$$

The silver ratio is a [Pisot number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot_number), the next quadratic Pisot number after the golden ratio.[^o8] By definition of these numbers, the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) $\sqrt{2} -1$ of the [algebraic conjugate](wikipedia://en/458467) is smaller than $1,$ thus powers of $\sigma$ generate [almost integers](wikipedia://en/14041933) and the sequence $\sigma^n \bmod 1$ is dense at the borders of the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324).

### Quadratic field Q(√2) {#Quadratic_field_Q(√2)}

*Image: SilverLattice.svg.* The mapping $\iota\,(\xi) = (\overline{\xi}, \xi)$ embeds both con­jugates of integer $\xi = a + b\sigma$ into a [point-lattice](wikipedia://en/36565179) $\Lambda$ spanned by unit vectors $\iota\,(1)$ and $\iota\,(\sigma).$ The [fundamental parallelogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_cell#Primitive_cell) with area $\delta(\Lambda) = \surd8$ in silver; the [Minkowski diamond](wikipedia://en/53455) has area $4\delta(\Lambda).$

$\sigma$ is the [fundamental unit](wikipedia://en/3995927) of real [quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781) $K =\mathbb{Q}\left( \sqrt{2} \right)$ with discriminant $\Delta_k =8.$ The [integers](wikipedia://en/21834785) $\mathbb{Z}[\sigma] \text{ of } K$ are the numbers $\xi =a +b\sigma \text{ } (a,b \in \mathbb{Z}),$ with conjugate $\overline{\xi} =(a +2b) -b\sigma,$ norm $\xi \overline{\xi} =(a +b)^2 -2b^2$ and trace $\xi +\overline{\xi} =2(a +b).$[^o11] The first few positive numbers occurring as norm are 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 9, 14, 16, 17, 18, 23, 25. Arithmetic in the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $O_k =\mathbb{Z}[\sigma]$ resembles that of the rational integers, *i.e.* the elements of $\mathbb{Z}.$ Prime factorization is [unique](wikipedia://en/60546) up to order and unit factors $\pm \sigma^{\pm n} (n = 0,1,2,\ldots),$ and there is a [Euclidean function](wikipedia://en/10376#Norm-Euclidean_fields) on the absolute value of the norm.[^o13] The primes of $O_k$ are of three types:

- $1 +\sigma$ with norm $2,$ the single rational prime that [divides $\Delta_{k} ,$](wikipedia://en/384644#In_algebraic_number_theory)
- the factors $a + b\sigma$ of rational primes $p =8n \pm 1$ with norm $p,$
- the rational primes $p =8n \pm 3$ with norm $p^2,$

and any one of these numbers multiplied by a unit.[^o16]

The silver ratio can be used as [base](wikipedia://en/701207) of a [numeral system](wikipedia://en/6832938), here called the *sigmary scale*. Every [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) $x$ in $[0,1]$ can be represented as a [convergent series](wikipedia://en/1743431)

$$x =\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{a_n}{\sigma^n},$$

with [weights](wikipedia://en/245523#Discrete_weights) $a_n \in [0,1,2].$

*Image: Sigmary\_scale.svg.* The steps in the sigmary scale resemble the intervals of the [mixolydian mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixolydian_mode#Modern_Mixolydian) in log scale. Progression to the next octave is paralleled by the carry in 21 and 22.

Sigmary expansions are not unique. Due to the identities

$$\begin{aligned} \sigma^{n+1} &=2\sigma^n +\sigma^{n-1} \\ \sigma^{n+1} +\sigma^{n-1} &=2\sigma^n +2\sigma^{n-1},\end{aligned}$$

digit blocks $21_\sigma \text{ and } 22_\sigma$ [carry](wikipedia://en/1629687) to the next power of $\sigma,$ resulting in $100_\sigma \text{ and } 101_\sigma.$ The number one has finite and infinite representations $1.0_\sigma, 0.21_\sigma$ and $0.\overline{20}_\sigma, 0.1\overline{2}_\sigma,$ where the first of each pair is in [canonical form](wikipedia://en/515096). The [algebraic number](wikipedia://en/1158) $2(3\sigma -7)$ can be written $0.101_\sigma,$ or non-canonically as $0.022_\sigma.$ The [decimal number](wikipedia://en/8214) $10 =111.12_\sigma,$ $7\sigma +3 =1100_\sigma \,$ and $\tfrac{1}{\sigma -1} =0.\overline{1}_\sigma.$

Properties of canonical sigmary expansions, with coefficients $a,b,c \in \mathbb{Z}:$

- Every [algebraic integer](wikipedia://en/98981) $\xi =a +b\sigma \text{ in } K$ has a finite expansion.[^o17]
- Every [rational number](wikipedia://en/25271) $\rho =\tfrac{a +b\sigma}{c} \text{ in } K$ has a purely periodic expansion.[^o18]
- All numbers that do not lie in $K$ have chaotic expansions.

Remarkably, the same holds *[mutatis mutandis](wikipedia://en/742665)* for all quadratic Pisot numbers that satisfy the general equation $x^2=nx +1,$ with integer $n > 0.$[^o19] It follows by repeated substitution of $x=n +\frac{1}{x}$ that all positive solutions $\tfrac12 \left(n +\sqrt{n^2 + 4 \vphantom{/} } \right)$ have a purely periodic continued fraction expansion

$$\sigma_n =n +\cfrac{1}{n +\cfrac{1}{n +\cfrac{1}{\ddots}}}$$

[Vera de Spinadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_W._de_Spinadel) described the properties of these irrationals and introduced the moniker [metallic means](wikipedia://en/44108116).[^o20]

The silver ratio is related to the [central Delannoy numbers](wikipedia://en/14736250#Central_Delannoy_numbers) $D_n$ = 1, 3, 13, 63, 321, 1683, 8989,... that count the number of "king walks" between one pair of opposite corners of a square $n \times n$ lattice. The sequence has [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993)

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 -6x +x^2}} =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} D_n x^n \text{ for } \vert x\vert <\tfrac{1}{\sigma^2},$$

from which are obtained the [integral representation](wikipedia://en/15532) [^o22]

$$D_n =\frac{1}{\pi} \int_{\sigma^{-2}}^{\sigma^2} \frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\sqrt{(t -\sigma^{-2})(\sigma^2 -t)} \;t^{n+1}}$$

and [asymptotic formula](wikipedia://en/642090) [^o23]

$$D_n \sim \frac{\sigma^{2n+1}}{2\sqrt{\pi (\sigma-1) \,n}} \left( 1 -\frac{11 -3\sigma}{32 \,n} +\frac{221 -36\sigma^2}{(32 \,n)^2} +\mathcal{O}\bigl(n^{-3}\bigr) \right).$$

For an application of the sigmary scale, consider the problem of writing a possible third-order coefficient $c$ in terms of the silver ratio. The decimal value of $c$ is approximately $0.006865233,$ which can be found with the [method of dominant balance](wikipedia://en/19912352) using the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806) for the central Delannoy numbers, $n \,D_n = (6n -3)D_{n-1} -(n-1)D_{n-2},$ with $D_{-1} =D_0 =1, n_{max} =10^5.$ "The coefficients all lie in $\mathbb{Q}\left( \sqrt{2} \right)$ and have denominators equal to some power of the prime $\sqrt{2} \,\mathbb{Z}[\sigma].$"[^o25] Choosing denominator $d = 32768,$ the approximate numerator $\operatorname{dc}$ has sigmary expansion $1001201.010201012000000110..._\sigma$ and is truncated to a [quadratic integer](wikipedia://en/21834785) by dropping all digits of order $k <-9.$ Write the remaining powers $\sigma^k$ in linear form with [Pell numbers](wikipedia://en/1355482) as coefficients (see the following section), take the weighted sum and simplify, giving term $-\frac{4123 -309\sigma^3}{(32 \,n)^3}.$ A certified value for $c$ is however as yet unknown.

:::

## Pell sequences {#Pell_sequences}

*Image: SilverWord\_im.png.* Silver harmonics: the rectangle and its coloured subzones have areas in ratios $7\sigma + 3 : \sigma^{3} : \sigma^{2} : \sigma : 1.$

These numbers are related to the silver ratio as the [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number) and [Lucas numbers](wikipedia://en/398404) are to the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).

The fundamental sequence is defined by the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)

$$P_n =2P_{n-1} +P_{n-2} \text{ for } n > 1,$$

with initial values

$$P_0 =0, P_1 =1.$$

The first few terms are 0, 1, 2, 5, 12, 29, 70, 169,... . The limit ratio of consecutive terms is the silver mean.

Fractions of Pell numbers provide [rational approximations](wikipedia://en/251558#Upper_bounds_for_Diophantine_approximations) of $\sigma$ with error

$$\left\vert \sigma - \frac{P_{n+1}}{P_n} \right\vert < \frac{1}{\sqrt{8} P_n^2}$$

The sequence is extended to negative indices using

$$P_{-n} =(-1)^{n-1} P_n.$$

Powers of $\sigma$ can be written with Pell numbers as linear coefficients

$$\sigma^n =\sigma P_n +P_{n-1},$$

which is proved by [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881) on $n.$ The relation also holds for $n < 0.$

The [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) of the sequence is given by [^o27]

$$\frac{x}{1 -2x -x^2} =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} P_n x^n \text{ for } \vert x\vert <\tfrac{1}{\sigma}.$$

*Image: Silver Newton map.svg.* [Newton's method](wikipedia://en/22145) for $p(z) =$ $(z2 - 2z - 1)(z2 - 2z + \sigma) / \sigma:$ the silver ratio (right) and its conjugate with [perturbing](wikipedia://en/1490148) complex roots $1 \pm i\surd\overline{\sigma - 1}$ at the nuclei of their [basins of attraction](wikipedia://en/191094#Basins_of_attraction). [Julia set](wikipedia://en/56434) of the Newton map in orange, with unit circle and real curve for reference.

The [characteristic equation](wikipedia://en/31050469) of the recurrence is $x^2 -2x -1 =0$ with [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) $D=8.$ If the two solutions are silver ratio $\sigma$ and conjugate $\bar{\sigma},$ so that $\sigma +\bar{\sigma} =2 \;\text{ and } \;\sigma \cdot \bar{\sigma} =-1,$ the Pell numbers are computed with the [Binet formula](wikipedia://en/10918#Binet's_formula)

$$P_n =a( \sigma^n -\bar{\sigma}^n ),$$

with $a$ the positive root of $8x^2 -1 =0.$

Since $\left\vert a\,\bar{\sigma}^n \right\vert < 1 /\sigma^{2n},$ the number $P_n$ is the nearest integer to $a\,\sigma^n ,$ with $a =1 /\sqrt{8}$ and $n \ge 0.$

The Binet formula $\sigma^n +\bar{\sigma}^n$ defines the companion sequence $Q_n =P_{n+1} +P_{n-1}.$

The first few terms are 2, 2, 6, 14, 34, 82, 198,... .

This [Pell-Lucas](wikipedia://en/1355482#Pell–Lucas_numbers) sequence has the [Fermat property](wikipedia://en/53453): if p is prime, $Q_{p} \equiv Q_1 \bmod p.$ The converse does not hold, the least odd [pseudoprimes](wikipedia://en/28787420) $\,n \mid (Q_n -2)$ are 13${}^{2}$, 385, 31${}^{2}$, 1105, 1121, 3827, 4901.

Pell numbers are obtained as integral powers $n > 2$ of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) with positive [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429#Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors_of_matrices) $\sigma$

$$M = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,$$

$$M^n = \begin{pmatrix} P_{n+1} & P_n \\ P_n & P_{n-1} \end{pmatrix}$$

The [trace](wikipedia://en/43270#Relationship_to_eigenvalues) of $M^n$ gives the above $Q_n.$

## Geometry {#Geometry}

### Silver rectangle and regular octagon {#Silver_rectangle_and_regular_octagon}

*Image: Silver\_rectangle\_construction.svg.* Origami construction of a silver rectangle, with creases in green.

An [ISO 216](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_standard_paper_sizes) rectangle has edges with lengths in [Lichtenberg](wikipedia://en/341682#Applications) ratio $\surd2 \text{∶} 1.$ It can be created from a square piece of paper with an [origami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origami) folding sequence. Considered a proportion of great harmony in [Japanese aesthetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_aesthetics) — *Yamato-hi* (大和比) — [the ratio is retained](wikipedia://en/341682#Applications) if the $\surd2$ rectangle is folded in half, parallel to the short edges. [Rabatment](wikipedia://en/31010487) produces a rectangle with edges in the silver ratio (according to $\tfrac{1}{\sigma} = \surd2 - 1$).

- Fold a square sheet of paper in half, creating a falling diagonal crease (bisect 90° angle), then unfold.
- Fold the right hand edge onto the diagonal crease (bisect 45° angle).
- Fold the top edge in half, to the back side (reduce width by $\tfrac{1}{\sigma + 1}$), and open out the triangle. The result is a $\surd2$ rectangle.
- Fold the bottom edge onto the left hand edge (reduce height by $\tfrac{1}{\sigma - 1}$). The horizontal part on top is a silver rectangle.

If the folding paper is opened out, the creases coincide with diagonal sections of a regular [octagon](wikipedia://en/314575). The first two creases divide the square into a silver gnomon with angles in the ratios $5 : 2 : 1,$ between two right triangles with angles in ratios $4 : 2 : 2$ (left) and $4 : 3 : 1$ (right). The unit angle is equal to $22\tfrac{1}{2}$ degrees.

If the octagon has edge length $1,$ its area is $2\sigma$ and the diagonals have lengths $\sqrt{\sigma +1 \vphantom{/} }, \;\sigma$ and $\sqrt{2(\sigma +1) \vphantom{/} }.$ The coordinates of the vertices are given by the $8$ [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) of $\left( \pm \tfrac12, \pm \tfrac{\sigma}{2} \right).$[^o31] The paper square has edge length $\sigma -1$ and area $2.$ The triangles have areas $1, \frac{\sigma -1}{\sigma}$ and $\frac{1}{\sigma} ;$ the rectangles have areas $\sigma -1 \text{ and } \frac{1}{\sigma}.$

### Silver whirl {#Silver_whirl}

*Image: Silver\_rectangle\_whirl.svg.* A whirl of silver rectangles.

Divide a rectangle with sides in ratio $1 : 2$ into four congruent [right triangles](wikipedia://en/39420) with legs of equal length and arrange these in the shape of a silver rectangle, enclosing a [similar](wikipedia://en/39377) rectangle that is scaled by factor $\tfrac{1}{\sigma}$ and rotated about the centre by $\tfrac{\pi}{4}.$ Repeating the construction at successively smaller scales results in four infinite sequences of adjoining right triangles, tracing a [whirl](wikipedia://en/8532654) of converging silver rectangles.[^o34]

The logarithmic spiral through the vertices of adjacent triangles has [polar slope](wikipedia://en/75407072) $k =\frac{4}{\pi} \ln( \sigma).$ The [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) between the pair of grey triangles on the sides has perpendicular diagonals in ratio $\sigma$, hence is a *silver [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884)*.

If the triangles have legs of length $1$ then each discrete spiral has length $\frac{\sigma}{\sigma -1} =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \sigma^{-n} .$ The areas of the triangles in each spiral region sum to $\frac{\sigma}{4} =\tfrac12 \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \sigma^{-2n} ;$ the perimeters are equal to $\sigma +2$ (light grey) and $2\sigma -1$ (silver regions).

Arranging the tiles with the four [hypotenuses](wikipedia://en/251366) facing inward results in the diamond-in-a-square shape.

*Image: Terme Taurine Opus Sectile.jpg.* Ancient Roman tile work.

Roman architect [Vitruvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitruvius) recommended the implied *ad quadratura* ratio as one of three for proportioning a town house [*atrium*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavaedium). The scaling factor is $\tfrac{1}{\sigma -1},$ and iteration on edge length $2$ gives an angular spiral of length $\sigma +1.$

:::

### Polyhedra {#Polyhedra}

*Image: Rhombicuboctahedron\_by\_Cutting\_Rhombic\_Dodecahedron.svg.* Dimensions of the rhombi­cuboctahedron are linked to $\sigma.$

The silver mean has connections to the following [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847) with [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805); all values are based on edge length $= 2.$

- [Rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213)

The coordinates of the vertices are given by 24 distinct permutations of $( \pm \sigma, \pm 1, \pm 1),$ thus three mutually-perpendicular silver rectangles touch six of its square faces. The [midradius](wikipedia://en/4709281) is $\sqrt{2(\sigma +1) \vphantom{/} },$ the centre radius for the square faces is $\sigma.$[^o36]

- [Truncated cube](wikipedia://en/280529)

Coordinates: 24 permutations of $( \pm \sigma, \pm \sigma, \pm 1).$ Midradius: $\sigma +1,$ centre radius for the octagon faces: $\sigma.$[^o37]

- [Truncated cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/285879)

Coordinates: 48 permutations of $( \pm (2\sigma -1), \pm \sigma, \pm 1).$ Midradius: $\sqrt{6(\sigma +1) \vphantom{/} },$ centre radius for the square faces: $\sigma +2,$ for the octagon faces: $2\sigma -1.$[^o38]

- [Tetragonal trisoctahedron](wikipedia://en/723339#Cartesian_coordinates)
- [Trisoctahedron](wikipedia://en/722549#Cartesian_coordinates)

### Silver triangle {#Silver_triangle}

*Image: Silver triangle spiral.svg.* Silver triangle and whirling gnomons.

The [acute](wikipedia://en/44076423) [isosceles triangle](wikipedia://en/315428) formed by connecting two adjacent vertices of a [regular octagon](wikipedia://en/314575#Regularity) to its centre point, is here called the *silver triangle*. It is uniquely identified by its angles in ratios $2 :3 :3.$ The [apex](wikipedia://en/6022079) angle measures $360 /8=45,$ each [base](wikipedia://en/9579379) angle $67 \tfrac12$ degrees. It follows that the [height](wikipedia://en/161241) to base ratio is $\tfrac12 \tan(67 \tfrac12) =\tfrac{\sigma}{2}.$

By [trisecting](wikipedia://en/91111) one of its base angles, the silver triangle is partitioned into a similar triangle and an [obtuse](wikipedia://en/44076423) *silver [gnomon](wikipedia://en/6673051#Isosceles_triangles)*. The trisector is collinear with a [medium diagonal](wikipedia://en/314575#Diagonality) of the octagon. Sharing the apex of the parent triangle, the gnomon has angles of $67 \tfrac12 /3 =22 \tfrac12, 45 \text{ and } 112 \tfrac12$ degrees in the ratios $1 :2 :5.$ From the [law of sines](wikipedia://en/113222), its edges are in ratios $1 :\sqrt{\sigma +1} :\sigma.$

The similar silver triangle is likewise obtained by scaling the parent triangle in base to leg ratio $2\cos(67 \tfrac12)$, accompanied with an $112 \tfrac12$ degree rotation. Repeating the process at decreasing scales results in an infinite sequence of silver triangles, which converges at the [centre of rotation](wikipedia://en/410009#Related_definitions_and_terminology). It is assumed without proof that the centre of rotation is the intersection point of sequential [median lines](wikipedia://en/1072006) that join corresponding legs and base vertices.[^o40] The assumption is verified by construction, as demonstrated in the vector image.

The centre of rotation is the first [Brocard point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocard_point) of the triangle, with angle $\omega =\arctan (\tfrac{\sigma}{\sigma +2})$ and [barycentric coordinates](wikipedia://en/762954)

$$\left( \tfrac{\sigma +1}{\sigma +5} :\tfrac{2}{\sigma +5} :\tfrac{2}{\sigma +5} \right) \sim \left( \tfrac{\sigma +1}{2} :1 :1 \right).$$

The three whorls of stacked gnomons have areas in ratios

$$\left( \tfrac{\sigma +1}{2} \right)^2 :\tfrac{\sigma +1}{2} :1.$$

The [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373) through the vertices of all nested triangles has [polar slope](wikipedia://en/75407072)

$$k =\frac{4}{5\pi} \ln \left( \tfrac{\sigma}{\sigma-1} \right),$$

or an expansion rate of $\tfrac{\sigma +1}{2}$ for every $225$ degrees of rotation.

### Silver rectangle and silver triangle {#Silver_rectangle_and_silver_triangle}

*Image: Silver\_rectangle\_segmented.svg.* Powers of $\sigma$ within a silver rectangle.

Assume a silver rectangle has been constructed as indicated above, with height $1$, length $\sigma$ and [diagonal](wikipedia://en/349251) length $\sqrt{\sigma^2 +1}$. The triangles on the diagonal have [altitudes](wikipedia://en/161241) $1 /\sqrt{1 +\sigma^{-2}}\,;$ each perpendicular foot divides the diagonal in ratio $\sigma^2.$

If an horizontal line is drawn through the intersection point of the diagonal and the internal [edge](wikipedia://en/12158034) of a [rabatment square](wikipedia://en/31010487), the parent silver rectangle and the two scaled copies along the diagonal have areas in the ratios $\sigma^2 :2 :1\,,$ the rectangles opposite the diagonal both have areas equal to $\tfrac{2}{\sigma +1}.$[^o49]

Relative to [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) $A$, the coordinates of feet of altitudes $U$ and $V$ are

$$\left( \tfrac{\sigma}{\sigma^2 +1}, \tfrac{1}{\sigma^2 +1} \right) \text{ and } \left( \tfrac{\sigma}{1 +\sigma^{-2}}, \tfrac{1}{1 +\sigma^{-2}} \right).$$

If the diagram is further subdivided by perpendicular lines through $U$ and $V$, the lengths of the diagonal and its subsections can be expressed as [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367) of argument $\alpha =67 \tfrac12$ degrees, the base angle of the silver triangle:

*Image: Silver\_triangle.svg.* Diagonal segments of the silver rectangle measure the silver triangle. The ratio $\operatorname{AB}:\operatorname{AS}$ is $\sigma.$

$$\begin{aligned} \overline{A B} =\sqrt{\sigma^2 +1} &=\sec(\alpha) \\ \overline{A V} =\sigma^2 /\overline{A B} &=\sigma\sin(\alpha) \\ \overline{U V} =2 /\overline{A S} &=2\sin(\alpha) \\ \overline{S B} =4 /\overline{A B} &=4\cos(\alpha) \\ \overline{S V} =3 /\overline{A B} &=3\cos(\alpha) \\ \overline{A S} =\sqrt{1 +\sigma^{-2}} &=\csc(\alpha) \\ \overline{h} =1 /\overline{A S} &=\sin(\alpha) \\ \overline{U S} =\overline{A V} -\overline{S B} &=(2\sigma -3)\cos(\alpha) \\ \overline{A U} =1 /\overline{A B} &=\cos(\alpha),\end{aligned}$$

with $\sigma =\tan(\alpha).$

Both the lengths of the diagonal sections and the trigonometric values are elements of [biquadratic](wikipedia://en/251591#Biquadratic_equations) [number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) $K =\mathbb{Q}\left( \sqrt{2 +\sqrt{2}} \right).$

The silver rhombus with edge $1$ has diagonal lengths equal to $\overline{U V}$ and $2\overline{A U}.$ The regular [octagon](wikipedia://en/314575) with edge $2$ has long diagonals of length $2\overline{A B}$ that divide it into eight silver triangles. Since the regular octagon is defined by its side length and the angles of the silver triangle, it follows that all measures can be expressed in powers of $\sigma$ and the diagonal segments of the silver rectangle, as illustrated above, *pars pro toto* on a single triangle.

The leg to base ratio $\overline{A B} /2 \approx 1.306563$ has been dubbed the *Cordovan proportion* by Spanish architect Rafael de la Hoz Arderius. According to his observations, it is a notable measure in the [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorish_architecture) and [intricate decorations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zellij) of the [mediæval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) [Mosque of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque–Cathedral_of_Córdoba), [Andalusia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusia).[^o50]

::: {#o52 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Silver spiral"}

### Silver spiral {#Silver_spiral}

*Image: Silver\_spiral.svg.* Silver spirals with different initial angles on a $\sigma$− rectangle.

A silver spiral is a [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373) that gets wider by a factor of $\sigma$ for every quarter turn. It is described by the [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation) $r( \theta) =a \exp(k \theta),$ with initial radius $a$ and parameter $k =\frac{2}{\pi} \ln( \sigma).$ If drawn on a silver rectangle, the spiral has its pole at the foot of altitude of a triangle on the diagonal and passes through vertices of paired squares which are perpendicularly aligned and successively scaled by a factor $\sigma^{-1}.$

:::

### Ammann–Beenker tiling {#Ammann–Beenker_tiling}

*Image: AmmanSubstRules.png.* Patch inflation of Ammann A5-tiles with factor $\sigma^{2}.$

The silver ratio appears prominently in the [Ammann–Beenker tiling](wikipedia://en/19292825), a [non-periodic tiling](wikipedia://en/868145#Constructions) of the plane with octagonal symmetry, build from a square and silver rhombus with equal side lengths. Discovered by [Robert Ammann](wikipedia://en/1505379) in 1977, its algebraic properties were described by Frans Beenker five years later.[^o54] If the squares are cut into two triangles, the inflation factor for [Ammann A5-tiles](wikipedia://en/19292825#Description_of_the_tiles) is $\sigma^2,$ the dominant [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429#Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors_of_matrices) of substitution [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859)

$$M =\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}.$$
