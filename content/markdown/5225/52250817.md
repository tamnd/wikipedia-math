# Gc (engineering)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gc"}

In [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), ***g*<sub>c</sub>** is a unit [conversion factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_of_units#Conversion_factors) used to convert [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass) to [force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force) or vice versa.[^o2] It is defined as

:::

$$g_\text{c} = \frac{ma}{F}$$

In unit systems where force is a [derived unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_unit), like in [SI units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI_unit), *g*<sub>c</sub> is equal to 1. In unit systems where force is a primary unit, like in [imperial and US customary measurement systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_and_US_customary_measurement_systems), *g*<sub>c</sub> may or may not equal 1 depending on the units used, and value other than 1 may be required to obtain correct results.[^o3] For example, in the [kinetic energy](wikipedia://en/17327) (KE) formula, if *g*<sub>c</sub> = 1 is used, then KE is expressed in [foot-poundals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot-poundal); but if *g*<sub>c</sub> = 32.174 is used, then KE is expressed in [foot-pounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot-pound).

## Motivations {#Motivations}

According to [Newton's second law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton's_second_law), the [force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force) *F* is proportional to the product of mass *m* and acceleration *a*:

$$F \propto ma$$

or

$$F = K ma$$

If *F* = 1 [lbf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound-force), *m* = 1 [lb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_mass), and *a* = \32.174 ft/s2, then

$$1~\text{lbf} = K \cdot 1~\text{lb} \cdot 32.174~\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{s}^2}$$

Leading to

$$K = \frac{1~\text{lbf}}{1~\text{lb} \cdot 32.174~\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{s}^2}} = 0.03108~\frac{\text{lbf} \cdot \text{s}^2}{\text{lb} \cdot \text{ft}}$$

*g*<sub>c</sub> is defined as the [reciprocal](wikipedia://en/229940) of the constant *K*

$$g_\text{c} = \frac{1}{K} = 32.174~\frac{\text{lb} \cdot\text{ft}}{\text{lbf} \cdot \text{s}^2}$$

or equivalently, as

$$g_\text{c} = \frac{ma}{F}$$
