# Nick Katz

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nick Katz"}

**Nicholas Michael Katz** (; born December 7, 1943) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), working in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), particularly on [*p*-adic methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_methods), [monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250) and [moduli problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_problem), and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He is currently a professor of Mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and an editor of the journal *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)*.[^o3]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Katz graduated from [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) (BA 1964) and from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where in 1965 he received his master's degree and in 1966 he received his doctorate under supervision of [Bernard Dwork](wikipedia://en/1678313) with thesis *On the Differential Equations Satisfied by Period Matrices*. After that, at Princeton, he was an instructor, an assistant professor in 1968, associate professor in 1971 and professor in 1974. From 2002 to 2005 he was the chairman of faculty there. He was also a visiting scholar at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), the [University of Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kyoto), [Paris VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pierre_and_Marie_Curie), [Orsay Faculty of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orsay_Faculty_of_Sciences), the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) and the [IHES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques). While in France, he adapted methods of [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory) and [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) to the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000). Subsequently, he has applied geometric methods to various [exponential sums](wikipedia://en/468937).

From 1968 to 1969, he was a NATO Postdoctoral Fellow, from 1975 to 1976 and from 1987–1988 [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow) and from 1971 to 1972 [Sloan Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellow). In 1970 he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in Nice (*The regularity theorem in algebraic geometry*)[^o5] and in 1978 in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) (*p-adic L functions, Serre-Tate local moduli and ratios of solutions of differential equations*).[^o6]

Since 2003 he is a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and since 2004 the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences). In 2003 he was awarded with [Peter Sarnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sarnak) the [Levi L. Conant Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_L._Conant_Prize) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) for the essay "Zeroes of Zeta Functions and Symmetry"[^o7] in the *Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society*. Since 2004 he is an editor of the *Annals of Mathematics*. In 2023 he received from the AMS the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Lifetime Achievement.[^o8]

He played a significant role as a sounding-board for [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027) when Wiles was developing in secret his proof [^o9] of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953). Mathematician and cryptographer [Neal Koblitz](wikipedia://en/1587628) was one of Katz's students.

Katz studied, with Sarnak among others, the connection of the eigenvalue distribution of large random matrices of classical groups to the distribution of the distances of the zeros of various *L* and zeta functions in algebraic geometry. He also studied trigonometric sums ([Gauss sums](wikipedia://en/6378204)) with algebro-geometric methods.

He introduced the [Katz–Lang finiteness theorem](wikipedia://en/37661448).
