# Guido Torrigiani

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Guido Torrigiani"}

**Guido Torrigiani** (13 January 1920 – 7 June 2000) was an Italian mathematician, computer scientist and politician. A member of the [Italian Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Socialist_Party), he served as president of the [Province of Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Livorno) from 1956 to 1964 and later played a significant role in the development of academic computing at the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Born in [Porto Santo Stefano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Santo_Stefano), Torrigiani studied at the [Scuola Normale Superiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore) in [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa), graduating in mathematics in 1942.[^o6][^o7] After initially joining the [Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_Party_%28Italy%29), he became a member of the [Italian Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Socialist_Party) following the former's dissolution.[^o6][^o7]

Torrigiani settled in [Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livorno) and taught [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) at the [Italian Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Naval_Academy).[^o6][^o7] In 1956 he was elected [president of the Province of Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Province_of_Livorno) and was subsequently re-elected for a second term.[^o3][^o8]

During the 1960s, he was invited by his friend [Alessandro Faedo](wikipedia://en/41893091) to help establish the [Centro Nazionale Universitario di Calcolo Elettronico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_Nazionale_Universitario_di_Calcolo_Elettronico) (CNUCE), one of Italy's pioneering computing research centres. Torrigiani later served as its director until 1979.[^o6][^o7] He also taught mathematical analysis in the engineering faculty of the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa) and conducted research at the Department of Applied Mathematics "Ulisse Dini".[^o3][^o6][^o9]

From 1975 to 1990 he served as president of the Pietro Mascagni Institute of Musical Studies in Livorno, promoting musical and concert activities in the city.[^o6][^o7][^o10]

He died in Pisa on 7 June 2000 following a cardiac arrest.[^o3][^o7] Among the tributes paid after his death was a message from Italian president [Carlo Azeglio Ciampi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Azeglio_Ciampi), a personal friend.[^o11][^o12]
