# Elizabeth Wilmer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elizabeth Wilmer"}

**Elizabeth Lee Wilmer** is an American mathematician known for her work on [Markov chain mixing times](wikipedia://en/6101938). She is a professor, and former department head, of mathematics at [Oberlin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberlin_College).

:::

As a 16-year-old high school student at [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School) and captain of the school mathematics team, Wilmer won second place in the [Westinghouse Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regeneron_Science_Talent_Search) in 1987, for a project involving [3-coloring of graphs](wikipedia://en/426743). The first-place winner that year was also female, marking the first year that the top two prizes both went to women. As an undergraduate at [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College), she led the university's team that won the first [Mathematical Contest in Modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Contest_in_Modeling) of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376), and she was one of the two inaugural winners of the [Alice T. Schafer Prize](wikipedia://en/57181252) of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) for excellence by a woman in undergraduate mathematics. She graduated from Harvard in 1991, and completed her Ph.D. at Harvard in 1999. She worked with [Persi Diaconis](wikipedia://en/65467) for her doctoral dissertation, *Exact Rates of Convergence for Some Simple Non-Reversible Markov Chains*, but after Diaconis moved from Harvard to Stanford in 1997 her official [doctoral advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor) became [Joe Harris](wikipedia://en/3610251).

With David A. Levin and [Yuval Peres](wikipedia://en/36477942), Wilmer is the author of the textbook *[Markov Chains and Mixing Times](wikipedia://en/63691657)* (American Mathematical Society, 2009; 2nd ed., 2017).

As of September 2022, Wilmer is a rotating program officer at the National Science Foundation in the Probability program.[^o2]
