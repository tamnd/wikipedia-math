# Hinge theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hinge theorem"}

*Image: Hinge theorem.svg.*

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **hinge theorem** (sometimes called the **open mouth theorem**) states that if two sides of one [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) to two sides of another triangle, and the [included angle](wikipedia://en/39330#Congruence_of_triangles) of the first is larger than the included angle of the second, then the third side of the first triangle is longer than the third side of the second triangle.[^o2] This theorem is given as Proposition 24 in Book I of [Euclid's *Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107).

:::

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proof from the law of cosines"}

## Proof from the law of cosines {#Proof_from_the_law_of_cosines}

The theorem is an immediate corollary of the [law of cosines](wikipedia://en/19480890).[^o5] For two triangles with sides $\{a, b, c\}$ and $\{a, b, \hat c\}$ with angles $\gamma$ and $\hat \gamma$ opposite the respective sides $c$ and $\hat c$, the law of cosines states:

$$\begin{aligned} c^2 &= a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos \gamma, \\ \hat{c}^2 &= a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos \hat \gamma. \end{aligned}$$

The [cosine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine) function is [monotonically decreasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonically_decreasing) for angles between $0$ and $\pi$ [radians](wikipedia://en/26003), so $\hat\gamma > \gamma$ implies $\hat c > c$ (and the converse as well).

:::

## Scope and generalizations {#Scope_and_generalizations}

The hinge theorem holds in [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697) and more generally in simply connected non-positively curved [space forms](wikipedia://en/1087009).

It can be also extended from plane Euclidean geometry to higher dimension Euclidean spaces (e.g., to [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) and more generally to [simplices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplices)), as has been done for [orthocentric tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/34286384) (i.e., tetrahedra in which altitudes are concurrent) and more generally for orthocentric simplices (i.e., simplices in which altitudes are concurrent).[^o7]

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Converse"}

## Converse {#Converse}

The [converse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_%28logic%29) of the hinge theorem is also true: If the two sides of one triangle are congruent to two sides of another triangle, and the third side of the first triangle is greater than the third side of the second triangle, then the included angle of the first triangle is larger than the included angle of the second triangle.

In some textbooks, the theorem and its converse are written as the [SAS](wikipedia://en/22228915) Inequality Theorem and the [SSS](wikipedia://en/22228915) Inequality Theorem respectively.

:::
