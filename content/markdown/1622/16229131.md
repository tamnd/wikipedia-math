# Richard Davis Anderson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Davis Anderson"}

**Richard Davis Anderson Sr.** (February 17, 1922 – March 4, 2008) was an American mathematician known internationally for his work in infinite-dimensional [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). Much of his early work focused on proofs surrounding [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) and [Hilbert cubes](wikipedia://en/216389).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Richard Anderson and his twin brother, John, were born February 17, 1922, in [Hamden, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamden,_Connecticut). He received a bachelor's degree in mathematics from the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) in 1941, after just two years of study. He went on to graduate school at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin), where he studied under [R. L. Moore](wikipedia://en/1077133). His graduate work was interrupted by [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). Two days after the [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan) attack on [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Harbor), he enlisted in the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy). During his term in the U. S. Navy, he served on the [USS *Rocky Mount*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Rocky_Mount). After returning from the war, he finished his doctoral work at the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas) and went on to teach mathematics at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), where he went through the ranks of instructor, assistant professor, and associate professor (from 1951 to 1956). During this time he also spent two years (the academic years 1951–1952 and 1955–1956) at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). He then accepted a post at [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University), where he became the university's first Boyd Professor of mathematics. Boyd Professor is Louisiana State University's highest professor rank.[^o5][^o6]

## Accomplishments {#Accomplishments}

- Served as vice president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 1972 and 1973.
- Served as president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) in 1981 and 1982.[^o8]
- Served as chair of the [Council of Scientific Society Presidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Scientific_Society_Presidents) in 1984.
- Received the Award for Distinguished Service to Mathematics from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) in 1978.
- Received the [Bolzano Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano_Medal) from the [Czechoslovak Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1981.
- Invited lectures at conferences and colloquia in many places in the US and in cities of 21 other countries.
  - Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1970 in Nice[^o9]
