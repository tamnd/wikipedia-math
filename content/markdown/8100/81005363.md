# Leonard Soicher

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Leonard Soicher"}

**Leonard Hyman Soicher** is a British-Canadian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18831) who worked in [algebraic graph theory](wikipedia://en/1620216), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), [combinatorial design theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_design_theory), and [discrete computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492#Theoretical_computer_science). He is an [Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) Professor of Mathematics at [Queen Mary University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_University_of_London).[^o3]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Soicher earned a [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSc) in mathematics in 1978 and a [Master of Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Computer_Science) in 1981,[^o5] both from [Concordia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_University) in Montreal, Canada. His master's [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis), titled “The Computation of Galois groups”, was supervised by [John McKay](wikipedia://en/4583624).[^o5] He won a [Commonwealth Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Commonwealth_Trust)[^o6] to pursue a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), with [John H. Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_H._Conway) as his supervisor.[^o7] Soicher was awarded his PhD in 1985, with a PhD [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) titled “Presentations of Some Finite Groups”[^o6] that focused on sporadic simple groups.

## Career {#Career}

Soicher has worked across a range of fields as a professional pure mathematician. After his doctorate, he worked again with [John McKay](wikipedia://en/4583624) as a [postdoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) at Concordia University in Canada.[^o9] He then joined [Queen Mary University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_University_of_London) in 1987, where he remained until retirement in 2018, becoming a [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in 2007.[^o10]

Soicher is most known for his early work on presentations of sporadic simple groups; his contribution to the [ATLAS of Finite Groups](wikipedia://en/22659407); the [Bimonster group](wikipedia://en/324828); work with [Charles Leedham-Green](wikipedia://en/9742981) developing “collection from the left”[^o11] and the product replacement algorithm.[^o12][^o13] in [computational group theory](wikipedia://en/618584); his research on distance-regular graphs (especially the three so-called Soicher graphs[^o14][^o15] and the Moscow-Soicher graph[^o16][^o17]); and his long-term and ongoing authorship and development of the GRAPE package[^o18] for [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_%28computer_algebra_system%29). Soicher is also a co-creator and maintainer of the website [*designtheory.org*](https://www.designtheory.org).

Soicher has co-published with notable mathematicians, including [Peter Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Cameron_%28mathematician%29), [John Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Conway), [Simon P. Norton](wikipedia://en/1641166), [Cheryl Praeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheryl_Praeger), and [Sarah Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Rees_%28professor%29)[^o19]

## GAP {#GAP}

Soicher has been a member of the council of the computer algebra system [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_%28computer_algebra_system%29) since June 1997, and was its chair from March 2009 until September 2021.[^o21] He is the author of the GRAPE[^o22] and DESIGN[^o23] packages for GAP. A special session in honour of Soicher's 70th birthday was held at the GAP Days Summer 2025 workshop in Koper, Slovenia.[^o24]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- with John McKay: [*Computing Galois groups over the rationals*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X%2885%2990022-8), Journal of Number Theory (1985).
- with John H. Conway, Simon P. Norton: *The Bimonster, the group*Y*<sub>555</sub>, and the projective plane of order 3*, Computers in Algebra (1988).
- with Rosemary A. Bailey, Peter J. Cameron, Peter Dobcsányi, John P. Morgan: [*Designs on the web*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2004.10.027), Discrete Mathematics (2006).
- with Cheryl E. Praeger: [*Low rank representations and graphs for sporadic groups*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511526039), Cambridge University Press (1996).
