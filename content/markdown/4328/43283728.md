# The Tor Project

::: {#o6 .subject cue=lead name="The Tor Project"}

**The Tor Project, Inc.** is a [501(c)(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/501%28c%29%283%29_organization) research-education[^o7] [nonprofit organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonprofit_organization) based in [Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester,_Massachusetts), [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts).[^o8] It is founded by computer scientists [Roger Dingledine](wikipedia://en/48434704), [Nick Mathewson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Mathewson), and five others. The Tor Project is primarily responsible for maintaining software for the [Tor](wikipedia://en/20556944) anonymity network.[^o9]

:::

## History {#History}

The Tor Project, Inc. was founded on December 22, 2006[^o8] by computer scientists [Roger Dingledine](wikipedia://en/48434704), [Nick Mathewson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Mathewson) and five others. The [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation) (EFF) acted as the Tor Project's [fiscal sponsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_sponsorship) in its early years, and early financial supporters of the Tor Project included the U.S. [International Broadcasting Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Broadcasting_Bureau), [Internews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internews), [Human Rights Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Watch), the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google), and Netherlands-based [Stichting NLnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NLnet_Foundation).[^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16]

In October 2014, the Tor Project hired the public relations firm Thomson Communications in order to improve its public image (particularly regarding the terms "Dark Net" and "hidden services") and to educate journalists about the technical aspects of Tor.[^o17]

In May 2015, the Tor Project ended the Tor Cloud Service.[^o18][^o19]

In December 2015, the Tor Project announced that it had hired [Shari Steele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shari_Steele), former executive director of the Electronic Frontier Foundation, as its new executive director. Roger Dingledine, who had been acting as interim executive director since May 2015, remained at the Tor Project as a director and board member.[^o20][^o21][^o22] Later that month, the Tor Project announced that the [Open Technology Fund](wikipedia://en/49417443) would be sponsoring a [bug bounty program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bug_bounty_program) that was coordinated by [HackerOne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HackerOne).[^o23][^o24] The program was initially invite-only and focuses on finding vulnerabilities that are specific to the Tor Project's applications.[^o23]

On May 25, 2016, Tor Project employee [Jacob Appelbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Appelbaum) stepped down from his position;[^o25][^o26][^o27] this was announced on June 2 in a two-line statement by Tor.[^o28] Over the following days, allegations of sexual mistreatment were made public by several people.[^o27]

On July 13, 2016, the complete board of the Tor Project – Meredith Hoban Dunn, [Ian Goldberg](wikipedia://en/829135), Julius Mittenzwei, Rabbi Rob Thomas, [Wendy Seltzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendy_Seltzer), [Roger Dingledine](wikipedia://en/48434704) and [Nick Mathewson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Mathewson) – was replaced with [Matt Blaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Blaze), [Cindy Cohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cindy_Cohn), [Gabriella Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriella_Coleman), Linus Nordberg, [Megan Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megan_Price) and [Bruce Schneier](wikipedia://en/36732).[^o29][^o30][^o31][^o32] A new anti-harassment policy has been approved by the new board, as well as a conflicts of interest policy, procedures for submitting complaints, and an internal complaint review process.[^o33][^o34] The affair continues to be controversial, with considerable dissent within the Tor community.[^o35]

In 2020, due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), the Tor project's core team let go of 13 employees, leaving a working staff of 22 people.[^o36]

In 2023, the [Tails Project](wikipedia://en/31672755) approached the Tor Project to merge operations. The merger was completed on September 26, 2024, stating that, "By joining forces, the Tails team can now focus on their core mission of maintaining and improving Tails OS, exploring more and complementary use cases while benefiting from the larger organizational structure of The Tor Project."[^o37][^o38]

Historically, the Tor Project was largely funded by the [United States government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_government), making up 80% of its \$2 million budget in 2012.[^o46] In more recent years, this share has declined, with 35% of its \$7.3 million budget coming from U.S. government sources .[^o47][^o41] Funding from the U.S. government has come from various departments and agencies, including the [U.S. State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._State_Department) (often via the [DRL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Democracy,_Human_Rights,_and_Labor)), the [USAGM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAGM) and its predecessor the Broadcasting Board of Governors (often via the [Open Technology Fund](wikipedia://en/49417443)), the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation), and the [U.S. Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Defense) (often via [DARPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA)).[^o14] According to Tor Project representatives, the project's funding from the Department of Defense is akin to [research grants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_grant), rather than [procurement contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procurement), and no collaboration has ever occurred to reveal the identities of users.[^o48] Another source of government funding is the [Swedish government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_government), via the [Swedish International Development Cooperation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_International_Development_Cooperation_Agency) (SIDA).[^o41][^o14]

The Tor Project has also been the recipient of donations and grants from [private foundations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_foundation_%28United_States%29).[^o14] Notable contributions have come from the private foundations of technology entrepreneurs [Jack Dorsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Dorsey) and [Craig Newmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Newmark). Other prominent private foundations to make contributions include the [Ford Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Foundation), [Open Society Foundations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Society_Foundations), the [Alfred P. Sloan Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan_Foundation), and the [Heising-Simons Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heising-Simons_Foundation). Contributions have been made by technology non-profits, foundations, and companies as well, such as financial contributions from the [Zcash](wikipedia://en/51849458) Foundation, [Mozilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozilla), and [NLnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NLnet_Foundation), as well as resource contributions, such as the [Google Summer of Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Summer_of_Code) or file hosting services donated by [Fastly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fastly).

In April 2023, the Tor Project announced a partnership where Tor Browser developers would create the [Mullvad Browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullvad_Browser).[^o49] While all corporate contributions combined made up 3.4% of the Tor Project's 2021–2022 budget,[^o50] , Mullvad contributes 19% of the project's budget, making it the second largest single contributor (after the [DRL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Democracy,_Human_Rights,_and_Labor)).[^o41]

A significant portion of the Tor Project's funding is from individual donors.[^o41] Despite making up less than government and corporate funds, Tor Project places a high value on individual donations, as they are made without restrictions on what the funding may be used for, granting greater flexibility for project priorities. The project often frames donations in terms of a "Bug Smash Fund", as the donations are used to fix [software bugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_bugs) left outside the scope of other sources of funding.[^o51]

## Tools {#Tools}

- Metrics Portal

Analytics for the Tor network, including graphs of its available bandwidth and estimated user-base. This is a great resource for researchers interested in detailed statistics about Tor.

- Nyx

a terminal (command line) application for monitoring and configuring Tor, intended for command-line enthusiasts and ssh connections. This functions much like top does for system usage, providing real time information on Tor's resource utilization and state.

- Onionoo

Web-based protocol to learn about currently running Tor relays and bridges.

- [OnionShare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OnionShare)

An open source tool that allows users to securely and anonymously share a file of any size.

- [Open Observatory of Network Interference (OONI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OONI)

a global observation network, monitoring network censorship, which aims to collect high-quality data using open methodologies, using Free and Open Source Software (FL/OSS) to share observations and data about the various types, methods, and amounts of network tampering in the world.

- [Orbot](wikipedia://en/34650947)

Tor for Android and [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS) devices, developed and maintained in collaboration with the [Guardian Project](wikipedia://en/39399882).

- Orlib

a library for use by any Android application to route Internet traffic through Orbot/Tor.

- Pluggable Transports (PT)

helps circumvent censorship. Transforms the Tor traffic flow between the client and the bridge. This way, censors who monitor traffic between the client and the bridge will see innocent-looking transformed traffic instead of the actual Tor traffic.

- Relay Search

Site providing an overview of the Tor network.

- Shadow

a discrete-event network simulator that runs the real Tor software as a plug-in. Shadow is open-source software that enables accurate, efficient, controlled, and repeatable Tor experimentation.

- Stem

Python Library for writing scripts and applications that interact with Tor.

- [Tails](wikipedia://en/31672755) (The Amnesic Incognito Live System)

a live CD/USB distribution pre-configured so that everything is safely routed through Tor and leaves no trace on the local system.

- [Tor](wikipedia://en/20556944)

free software and an open network that helps a user defend against [traffic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_analysis), a form of network surveillance that threatens personal freedom and privacy, confidential business activities and relationships, and state security. The organization has also implemented the software in [Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_%28programming_language%29) named Arti.[^o54]

- [Tor Browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_Browser)

a customization of [Mozilla Firefox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firefox) which uses a Tor circuit for browsing anonymously and with other features consistent with the Tor mission.

- Tor Phone

A phone that routes its network traffic through the Tor network.[^o55] Now defunct.

- TorBirdy

Extension for [Thunderbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozilla_Thunderbird) and related \*bird [forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fork_%28software_development%29) to route connections through the Tor network.

- txtorcon

[Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) and Twisted event-based implementation of the Tor control protocol. Unit-tests, state and configuration abstractions, documentation. It is available on [PyPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_Package_Index) and in [Debian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debian).[^o56]

:::

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In March 2011, the Tor Project received the [Free Software Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Software_Foundation)'s 2010 Award for Projects of Social Benefit. The citation read, "Using free software, Tor has enabled roughly 36 million people around the world to experience freedom of access and expression on the Internet while keeping them in control of their privacy and anonymity. Its network has proved pivotal in dissident movements in both [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran) and more recently [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt)."[^o58]

In September 2012, the Tor Project received the 2012 [EFF Pioneer Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFF_Pioneer_Award), along with [Jérémie Zimmermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jérémie_Zimmermann) and [Andrew Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Huang_%28hacker%29).[^o59]

In November 2012, *[Foreign Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Policy)* magazine named Dingledine, Mathewson, and Syverson among its Top 100 Global Thinkers "for making the web safe for whistleblowers".[^o60]

In 2014, Roger Dingledine, Nick Mathewson and Paul Syverson received the [USENIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USENIX) Test of Time Award for their paper titled "Tor: The Second-Generation Onion Router", which was published in the *Proceedings of the 13th USENIX Security Symposium*, August 2004.[^o61]

In 2021, the Tor Project was awarded the [Levchin Prize for real-world cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levchin_Prize_for_real-world_cryptography).[^o62]
