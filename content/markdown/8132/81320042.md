# Peter Hintz

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Hintz"}

**Peter Hintz** (born 1991 in [Kassel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassel), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany)) is a German mathematician working in the areas of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), [microlocal analysis](wikipedia://en/6108841), [scattering theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_theory) and [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). He is currently a professor of mathematics at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University).[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hintz graduated with a BSc in mathematics and a BSc in physics from the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in 2011. He obtained his PhD in 2015 at Stanford University under the supervision of [András Vasy](wikipedia://en/52321031).[^o6]

After his postdoctoral appointments at the [Miller Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Institute) and [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655), he joined the faculty of the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) as an assistant professor in 2019, later becoming an associate professor. In 2021, he moved to [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich) as an associate professor of mathematics and physics.[^o7] As of Fall 2025, he is a full professor of mathematics at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Hintz was [Miller Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Institute) from 2015 to 2017, where he was mentored by [Maciej Zworski](wikipedia://en/34110829).[^o9] He subsequently was a [Clay Research Fellow](wikipedia://en/7655) from 2017 to 2020.[^o10] During his time as a faculty member of the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), he was an [Alfred P. Sloan Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) from 2020 to 2022.[^o11] In 2021, he delivered a plenary lecture at the 20th [International Congress on Mathematical Physics](wikipedia://en/23897905).[^o12] The following year, Hintz gave a special invited lecture at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) together with Gustav Holzegel (de).[^o13][^o14] In 2022, he was awarded the [Golden Owl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich) for excellence in teaching at ETH Zürich.[^o15] He received a [Frontiers of Science Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences_and_Applications) in 2023[^o16] and the [IAMP Early Career Award](wikipedia://en/23897340) in 2024.[^o17]

## Research {#Research}

Hintz's work applies methods from microlocal analysis as well as scattering and spectral theory to hyperbolic partial differential equations arising in Einstein's theory of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). In 2026, he proved the [black hole stability conjecture](wikipedia://en/71459264) for the family of [Kerr black holes](wikipedia://en/456715) for all subextremal angular momenta.[^o19] Previously, in collaboration with [András Vasy](wikipedia://en/52321031), he proved the nonlinear stability of the [Kerr-de Sitter](wikipedia://en/74620623) family of black holes.[^o20] He also co-authored a paper on possible violations of [Sir Roger Penrose's](wikipedia://en/26193) [strong cosmic censorship](wikipedia://en/7609) conjecture.[^o21] Among his other contributions is a proof of Price's law on the rate of decay of waves on [Kerr](wikipedia://en/456715) black hole spacetimes.[^o22]
