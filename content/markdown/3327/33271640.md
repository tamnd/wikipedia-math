# Jacob Lüroth

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacob Lüroth"}

*Image: Jacob Lueroth.jpeg.* Jacob Lüroth

**Jacob Lüroth** (18 February 1844, [Mannheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannheim), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Confederation) – 14 September 1910, [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire)) was a German mathematician who proved [Lüroth's theorem](wikipedia://en/16440164) and introduced [Lüroth quartics](wikipedia://en/33272014). His name is sometimes written **Lueroth**, following the common [printing convention for umlauted characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umlaut_%28diacritic%29#Printing_conventions_in_German). He began his studies in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), but switched to mathematics when his poor eyesight made taking astronomical observations impossible. He received his doctorate in 1865 from [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University), for a thesis on [Pascal's theorem](wikipedia://en/699966).

:::

From 1868 he was at the [Karlsruhe Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Institute_of_Technology), where he became a professor in 1869, and from 1880 he was a professor at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich), succeeding [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887). In 1883, he became a professor at the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg), where he remained until his retirement.[^o3]

Following up on [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125)'s work on [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), Lüroth discovered the [*t*-distribution](wikipedia://en/105375) usually credited to [William Sealy Gosset](wikipedia://en/105651). In modern terms, the calculation that led Lüroth to the *t*-distribution was the computation of a [Bayesian interval](wikipedia://en/3408427).[^o4]

In honor of his mathematical achievements, Lüroth was elected to the [Royal Bavarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Bavarian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1882, to the [Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina) the following year, and in 1909 to the [Heidelberg Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o2]
