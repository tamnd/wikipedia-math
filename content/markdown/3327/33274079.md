# Mahan Mj

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mahan Mj"}

**Mahan Maharaj** (born Mahan Mitra ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language): মহান মিত্র), 5 April 1968[^o3]), also known as **Mahan Mj** and **Swami Vidyanathananda**, is an Indian mathematician and monk of the [Ramakrishna Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramakrishna_Order). He is currently Professor of Mathematics at the [Tata Institute of Fundamental Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Institute_of_Fundamental_Research) in [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai). He is a recipient of the [2025 Vigyan Shree Award](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/alumni-association-iit-kanpur-2a20121b6_prof-mahan-maharaj-msc5mth1992also-activity-7409814523995447296-gcof) 2011 [Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanti_Swarup_Bhatnagar_Award) in [mathematical sciences](wikipedia://en/25750436)[^o4] and the [Infosys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys_Prize) 2015 for Mathematical Sciences.[^o5] He is best known for his work in [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774) and [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101).

:::

## Early education {#Early_education}

Mahan Mitra studied at [St. Xavier's Collegiate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Xavier's_Collegiate_School), [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta), till Class XII. He then entered the [Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Technology_Kanpur), with an All India Rank (AIR) of 67 in the [Joint Entrance Examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Entrance_Examination), where he initially chose to study [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) but later switched to mathematics.[^o9] He graduated with a Masters in mathematics from IIT Kanpur in 1992.[^o17]

## Career {#Career}

Mahan Mitra joined the PhD program in mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), with [Andrew Casson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Casson) as his advisor. He received the [Earle C. Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earle_C._Anthony) Fellowship, U.C. Berkeley in 1992–1993 and the prestigious [Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) for 1996–1997.[^o9] After earning a doctorate from U.C. Berkeley in 1997, he worked briefly at the [Institute of Mathematical Sciences, Chennai](wikipedia://en/2718996) in 1998.

He was Professor of Mathematics and Dean of Research at the [Ramakrishna Mission Vivekananda University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramakrishna_Mission_Vivekananda_University) till 2015.[^o10] He is currently Professor of Mathematics at the [Tata Institute of Fundamental Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Institute_of_Fundamental_Research), [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai).[^o11]

He has widely published and presented his research in the area of [hyperbolic manifolds](wikipedia://en/1589135) and [ending lamination](wikipedia://en/30997240) spaces. His most notable work is the proof of existence of [Cannon–Thurston maps](wikipedia://en/63553560).[^o12][^o13] This led to the resolution of the conjecture that connected limit sets of finitely generated [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652) are locally connected.[^o9] He is also the author of a book titled *Maps on boundaries of hyperbolic metric spaces*.[^o14]

In 2017, he became a laureate of the [Asian Scientist 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Scientist_100) by the *[Asian Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Scientist)*.[^o15] Mj was an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065#2018,_Rio_de_Janeiro) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 2018 in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Mj became a monk of the Ramakrishna order in 1998.[^o17] Mahan Maharaj, as he is known to his students and colleagues, is fluent in English, Hindi and Bengali. He also knows a bit of Tamil, learnt from his stay in southern part of India at [IMSc](wikipedia://en/2718996). He has been quoted as saying, "I am enjoying being a monk as much as I enjoy my mathematics".[^o18]
