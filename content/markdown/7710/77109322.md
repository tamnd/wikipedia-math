# Michael Hartl

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Hartl"}

**Michael Hartl** is an American physicist, author, and entrepreneur.[^o3][^o4] He is best known as the creator of the *Ruby on Rails Tutorial*,[^o5][^o6][^o7] founder of Tau Day,[^o3][^o8][^o9][^o10] and author of *The Tau Manifesto*,[^o3][^o11][^o12] in which he proposes replacing [pi](wikipedia://en/23601) ($\pi$) with [tau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tau_%28number%29) ($\tau$).[^o9][^o13]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Hartl graduated from [El Modena High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Modena_High_School)[^o15] in [Orange, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange,_California). He then attended [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) for his undergraduate studies, graduating with a bachelor's degree in physics.[^o16] Hartl obtained his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in Physics from the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) (Caltech) in 2003, where he researched [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650) dynamics. His dissertation was titled *Dynamics of Spinning Compact Binaries in General Relativity*.[^o17]

## Career {#Career}

*Image: Michael\_Hartl\_with\_Kip\_Thorne\_and\_Stephen\_Hawking.jpg.* Michael Hartl with [Kip Thorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kip_Thorne) and [Stephen Hawking](wikipedia://en/19376148) in 2012

After finishing his PhD, Hartl served as Caltech's editor on a corrected and expanded version of *[The Feynman Lectures on Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Feynman_Lectures_on_Physics)* at the request of [Kip Thorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kip_Thorne).[^o19][^o20] Explaining in the preface why he chose Hartl for the task, Thorne noted that "Hartl understand physics deeply, he is among the most meticulous physicists I have known, and like [Feynman](wikipedia://en/25523) he is an outstanding pedagogue."[^o19][^o20] Thorne also noted that Hartl is the only Caltech graduate student to be granted a "lifetime achievement award for excellence in teaching" by Caltech's undergraduates.[^o19][^o20][^o21]

As an entrepreneur, Hartl participated in the [Y Combinator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y_Combinator) program in 2008.[^o22] In 2010, he published the first edition of the *Ruby on Rails Tutorial* book[^o23][^o24] and screencasts,[^o25] which teach [web development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_development) using the [Ruby on Rails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_on_Rails) web application framework. The *Ruby on Rails Tutorial* quickly became both a critical and commercial success,[^o5][^o26] eventually going through seven editions.[^o27] In 2011, Hartl received a Ruby Hero Award for his service to the [Ruby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_%28programming_language%29) community, with the citation mentioning both the *Ruby on Rails Tutorial* and his first book, *RailsSpace*.[^o28][^o29] In 2013, Wikipedia co-founder [Jimmy Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Wales) described the *Ruby on Rails Tutorial* as his "favorite book".[^o30]

Hartl later co-founded the online education company Learn Enough to expand on the *Ruby on Rails Tutorial*, adding tutorials on other computer technologies and including online courses.[^o31][^o32][^o33] In partnership with [Pearson Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_Education), Hartl authored or co-authored five books published under the brand Learn Enough to Be Dangerous.[^o34][^o35][^o36][^o37][^o38] Learn Enough was acquired by a tech private equity group in 2022.[^o39]

### *The Tau Manifesto* {#The_Tau_Manifesto}

*Image: Michael\_Hartl\_talks\_about\_tau\_at\_BIL.jpg.* Michael Hartl speaking about [tau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tau_%28number%29) at the [BIL Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIL_Conference) in 2015

In 2010, Hartl published *The Tau Manifesto*, in which he proposed using the Greek letter [tau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tau) to represent the circle constant $\tau = C/r = 2\pi$,[^o41] the first time tau was publicly proposed for this purpose.[^o3][^o8] *The Tau Manifesto* proved popular,[^o42] and a revised edition was published in 2019,[^o43] followed by a print edition in 2021.[^o44][^o45] As of 2022, *The Tau Manifesto* had been translated into seven different languages.[^o46]

With the initial publication of *The Tau Manifesto* in 2010, Hartl founded Tau Day as a mathematical celebration and to promote adoption of the new constant.[^o10] Observed annually on [June 28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_28), or 6/28 (in analogy with the celebration of [Pi Day](wikipedia://en/25013) on 3/14),[^o8][^o10] Tau Day has become a widely celebrated mathematical holiday.[^o3][^o8][^o47][^o48] Hartl's constant has also seen significant adoption, including support for tau in the official Google calculator[^o49] and inclusion in programming languages such as [Microsoft.NET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.NET),[^o50] [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_%28programming_language%29),[^o51] [Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_%28programming_language%29),[^o52] and [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29).[^o53][^o54] Notable supporters of tau and Tau Day include [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology),[^o47][^o55][^o56] [SLMath](wikipedia://en/401367),[^o48] [Vi Hart](wikipedia://en/31441421),[^o57][^o58] [Vitalik Buterin](wikipedia://en/41729424),[^o59][^o60] and [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk)[^o61] (who named one of his children after the constant[^o62]).
