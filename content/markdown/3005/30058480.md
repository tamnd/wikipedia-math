# Paraproduct

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paraproduct"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **paraproduct** is a [non-commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative) [bilinear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilinear_operator) acting on [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) that in some sense is like the [product](wikipedia://en/24977) of the two functions it acts on. According to [Svante Janson](wikipedia://en/27867508) and Jaak Peetre, in an article from 1988,[^o2] "the name 'paraproduct' denotes an idea rather than a unique definition; several versions exist and can be used for the same purposes." The concept emerged in [J.-M. Bony](wikipedia://en/50769397)’s theory of paradifferential operators.[^o3]

:::

This said, for a given operator $\Lambda$ to be defined as a paraproduct, it is normally required to satisfy the following properties:

- It should "reconstruct the product" in the sense that for any pair of functions $(f, g)$ in its domain,

$$fg = \Lambda(f, g) + \Lambda(g, f).$$

- For any appropriate functions $f$ and $h$ with $h(0)=0$, it is the case that $h(f) = \Lambda(f, h'(f))$.
- It should satisfy some form of the [Leibniz rule](wikipedia://en/248988).

A paraproduct may also be required to satisfy some form of [Hölder's inequality](wikipedia://en/191538).
