# Earl Taft

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Earl Taft"}

**Earl Jay Taft** (1931 – August 9, 2021) was an American mathematician specializing in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384). He is the namesake of the [Taft Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/42285891) which he introduced in a 1971 publication, and he was the founding editor of the journal *[Communications in Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_in_Algebra)*. He was Distinguished Professor Emeritus of Mathematics at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Taft graduated from [Amherst College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst_College) in 1952. He completed his doctorate at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1956. His dissertation, *Invariant Wedderburn Factors*, was supervised by [Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson). After working as [Ritt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Ritt) Instructor of mathematics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) from 1956 to 1959, he moved to [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), where he remained for many years. He was also a regular visitor to the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Taft's wife, [Hessy Levinsons Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessy_Levinsons_Taft), had been publicized as "the most beautiful Aryan baby" in Nazi propaganda despite being Jewish. Her family escaped [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) Germany for [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), Cuba, and later the US, and she met Taft as a graduate student in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), while he was an instructor there.

After Taft retired from Rutgers, he and his wife moved to New York City. Taft died in San Francisco at the age of 89.[^o5]
