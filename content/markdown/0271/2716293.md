# Primefree sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Primefree sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **primefree sequence** is a [sequence of integers](wikipedia://en/51421) that does not contain any [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666). More specifically, it usually means a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) defined by the same [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806) as the [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number), but with different [initial conditions](wikipedia://en/1186804) causing all members of the sequence to be [composite numbers](wikipedia://en/82289) that do not all have a common [divisor](wikipedia://en/49492). To put it algebraically, a sequence of this type is defined by an appropriate choice of two composite numbers *a*<sub>1</sub> and *a*<sub>2</sub>, such that the [greatest common divisor](wikipedia://en/12354) $\mathrm{gcd}(a_1,a_2)$ is equal to 1, and such that for $n>2$ there are no primes in the sequence of numbers calculated from the formula

:::

$$a_n=a_{n-1}+a_{n-2}.$$

The first primefree sequence of this type was published by [Ronald Graham](wikipedia://en/240694) in 1964.

## Wilf's sequence {#Wilf's_sequence}

A primefree sequence found by [Herbert Wilf](wikipedia://en/3782688) has initial terms

$$a_1 = 20615674205555510, a_2 = 3794765361567513$$

The [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) that every term of this sequence is composite relies on the periodicity of Fibonacci-like number sequences [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) the members of a finite set of primes. For each prime $p$, the positions in the sequence where the numbers are divisible by $p$ repeat in a periodic pattern, and different primes in the set have overlapping patterns that result in a [covering set](wikipedia://en/2034219) for the whole sequence.

## Nontriviality {#Nontriviality}

The requirement that the initial terms of a primefree sequence be [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) is necessary for the question to be non-trivial. If the initial terms share a prime factor $p$ (e.g., set $a_1=xp$ and $a_2=yp$ for some $x$ and $y$ both greater than 1), due to the [distributive property](wikipedia://en/103118) of [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) $a_3=(x+y)p$ and more generally all subsequent values in the sequence will be multiples of $p$. In this case, all the numbers in the sequence will be composite, but for a trivial reason.

The order of the initial terms is also important. In [Paul Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Hoffman_%28science_writer%29)'s biography of [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), *[The man who loved only numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_man_who_loved_only_numbers)*, the Wilf sequence is cited but with the initial terms switched. The resulting sequence appears primefree for the first hundred terms or so, but term 138 is the 45-digit prime $439351292910452432574786963588089477522344721$.

## Other sequences {#Other_sequences}

Several other primefree sequences are known:

$a_1 = 331635635998274737472200656430763, a_2 = 1510028911088401971189590305498785$ (sequence [A083104](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OEIS:A083104) in the [OEIS](wikipedia://en/500004); Graham 1964),

$a_1 = 62638280004239857, a_2 = 49463435743205655$ (sequence [A083105](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OEIS:A083105) in the OEIS; [Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095) 1990), and

$a_1 = 407389224418, a_2 = 76343678551$ (sequence [A082411](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OEIS:A082411) in the OEIS; Nicol 1999).

The sequence of this type with the smallest known initial terms has

$a_1 = 106276436867, a_2 = 35256392432$ (sequence [A221286](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OEIS:A221286) in the OEIS; Vsemirnov 2004).
