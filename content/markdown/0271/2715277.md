# Robin Wilson (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Robin Wilson"}

**Robin James Wilson** (born 5 December 1943) is an English mathematician. He is an emeritus professor in the Department of Mathematics at the [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University), having previously been head of the Pure Mathematics Department and dean of the Faculty.[^o3] He was a stipendiary lecturer at [Pembroke College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembroke_College,_Oxford)[^o4] and, from 2004 to 2008, [Gresham Professor of Geometry](wikipedia://en/4470052) at [Gresham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham_College), London.[^o5] On occasion, he teaches at [Colorado College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_College) in the United States.[^o6] He is also a long standing fellow of [Keble College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keble_College,_Oxford).

:::

Professor Wilson is a son of former British Prime Minister [Harold Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Wilson) and his wife, [Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Wilson,_Lady_Wilson_of_Rievaulx).

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Wilson was born in 1943 to the politician [Harold Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Wilson), who later became [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom), and his wife the poet [Mary Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Wilson,_Lady_Wilson_of_Rievaulx) ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Given_name#Name_at_birth) Baldwin). He has a younger brother, Giles, who in his 50s gave up a career as a teacher to be a train driver.[^o8] Wilson attended [University College School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_School) in [Hampstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead), North London. He achieved a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) first class honours in Mathematics from [Balliol College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College), [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University), an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), and a PhD from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) (1965–1968). In a [*Guardian*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian) interview in 2008, Wilson spoke of the fact he grew up known to everyone primarily as a son of the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_%28UK%29) [leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Labour_Party_%28UK%29) and Prime Minister Harold Wilson: "I hated the attention and I still dislike being introduced as Harold Wilson's son. I feel uncomfortable talking about it to strangers even now."[^o9]

## Mathematics career {#Mathematics_career}

Wilson's academic interests lie in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), particularly in [colouring](wikipedia://en/426743) problems, e.g. the [four colour problem](wikipedia://en/10949), and algebraic properties of graphs. He also researches the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), particularly British mathematics and mathematics in the 17th century and the period 1860 to 1940, and the history of graph theory and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

In 1974, he won the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for his expository article *An introduction to [matroid](wikipedia://en/244321) theory*.[^o11][^o12] Due to his collaboration on a 1977 paper[^o13] with the [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_people) mathematician [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), Wilson has an [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) of 1.

In July 2008, Wilson published a study of the mathematical work of [Lewis Carroll](wikipedia://en/26586083), the creator of *[Alice's Adventures in Wonderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice's_Adventures_in_Wonderland)* and *[Through the Looking-Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Through_the_Looking-Glass)* — *Lewis Carroll in Numberland: His Fantastical Mathematical Logical Life* (Allen Lane, 2008. ). From January 1999 to September 2003, he was editor-in-chief of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470) Newsletter and in 2003–2008 an associate editor.[^o14] He is past president of the [British Society for the History of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/7795793).[^o15]

Since 1985, Wilson has edited the [mathematics on stamps](wikipedia://en/77446410) "Stamp Corner" column for *[The Mathematical Intelligencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Intelligencer)*.[^o16]

## Other interests {#Other_interests}

He has strong interests in music, including the operas of [Gilbert and Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_and_Sullivan), and is the co-author (with [Frederic Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_Lloyd)) of *Gilbert and Sullivan: The Official D'Oyly Carte Picture History*.[^o18] In 2007, he was a guest on *[Private Passions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_Passions)*, the biographical music discussion programme on [BBC Radio 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_3).[^o19]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Wilson is married and has twin daughters.[^o21]
