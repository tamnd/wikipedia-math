# Prime signature

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prime signature"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **prime signature** of a number is the [multiset](wikipedia://en/305303) of (nonzero) exponents of its [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorization). The prime signature of a number having prime factorization $p_1^{m_1}p_2^{m_2} \dots p_n^{m_n}$ is the multiset $\left \{m_1, m_2, \dots, m_n \right \}$.

:::

For example, all [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) have a prime signature of {1}, the [squares](wikipedia://en/213919) of primes have a prime signature of {2}, the products of 2 distinct primes have a prime signature of {1, 1} and the products of a square of a prime and a different prime (e.g. 12, 18, 20, ...) have a prime signature of {2, 1}.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The [divisor function](wikipedia://en/407249) τ(*n*), the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961) *μ*(*n*), the [number of distinct prime divisors](wikipedia://en/57197043) ω(*n*) of *n*, the number of prime divisors Ω(*n*) of *n*, the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) of the [squarefree integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squarefree_integer), and many other important functions in number theory, are functions of the prime signature of *n*.

In particular, τ(*n*) equals the product of the incremented by 1 exponents from the prime signature of *n*. For example, 20 has prime signature {2,1} and so the number of divisors is (2+1) × (1+1) = 6. Indeed, there are six divisors: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10 and 20.

The smallest number of each prime signature is a product of [primorials](wikipedia://en/422786). The first few are:

1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 30, 32, 36, 48, 60, 64, 72, 96, 120, 128, 144, 180, 192, 210, 216, ... .

A number cannot divide another unless its prime signature is included in the other numbers prime signature in the [Young's lattice](wikipedia://en/14836297).

This classification is often used in the definition of [multiplicative functions](wikipedia://en/20059): the multiset of the prime exponents of an integer is mapped to another multiset, and the multiplicative function is defined by using that multiset image as exponents with the (usually original) set of prime bases.

:::

## Sequences defined by their prime signature {#Sequences_defined_by_their_prime_signature}

Given a number with prime signature *S*, it is

- A [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) if *S* = {1},
- A [square](wikipedia://en/659942) if [gcd](wikipedia://en/12354)(*S*) is [even](wikipedia://en/143135),
- A [cube](wikipedia://en/451445) if gcd(*S*) is divisible by 3,
- A [square-free integer](wikipedia://en/29525) if max(*S*) = 1,
- A [cube-free integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube-free_integer) if max(*S*) ≤ 2,
- A [powerful number](wikipedia://en/462499) if min(*S*) ≥ 2,
- A [perfect power](wikipedia://en/4023591) if gcd(*S*) > 1,
- A *k*-[almost prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_prime) if sum(*S*) = *k*, or
- An [Achilles number](wikipedia://en/7894744) if min(*S*) ≥ 2 and gcd(*S*) = 1.
