# Sampling fraction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sampling fraction"}

In [sampling theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_sampling), the **sampling fraction** is the ratio of [sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_%28statistics%29) size to [population](wikipedia://en/27585) size or, in the context of [stratified sampling](wikipedia://en/27596), the ratio of the sample size to the size of the stratum.[^o2] The formula for the sampling fraction is

:::

$$f=\frac{n}{N},$$

where *n* is the sample size and *N* is the population size. A sampling fraction value close to 1 will occur if the sample size is relatively close to the population size. When sampling from a [finite population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_population) [without replacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_with_replacement), this may cause [dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_and_dependence) between individual samples. To correct for this dependence when calculating the sample [variance](wikipedia://en/32344), a [finite population correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_population_correction) (or finite population multiplier) of $\frac{N-n}{N-1}$ may be used. If the sampling fraction is small, less than 0.05, then the sample variance is not appreciably affected by dependence, and the finite population correction may be ignored. [^o3][^o4]
