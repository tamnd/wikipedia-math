# Complement (group theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complement"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the area of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **complement** of a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) *H* in a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* is a subgroup *K* of *G* such that

:::

$$G = HK = \{ hk : h\in H, k\in K\} \text{ and } H\cap K = \{e\}.$$

Equivalently, every element of *G* has a unique expression as a product *hk* where *h* ∈ *H* and *k* ∈ *K*. This relation is symmetrical: if *K* is a complement of *H*, then *H* is a complement of *K*. Neither *H* nor *K* need be a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of *G*.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Complements need not exist, and if they do they need not be unique. That is, *H* could have two distinct complements *K*<sub>1</sub> and *K*<sub>2</sub> in *G*.
- If there are several complements of a normal subgroup, then they are necessarily [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to each other and to the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526).
- If *K* is a complement of *H* in *G* then *K* forms both a left and right [transversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transversal_%28group_theory%29) of *H*. That is, the elements of *K* form a complete set of representatives of both the left and right [cosets](wikipedia://en/98759) of *H*.
- The [Schur–Zassenhaus theorem](wikipedia://en/8113769) guarantees the existence of complements of normal [Hall subgroups](wikipedia://en/1735335) of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707).

:::

## Relation to other products {#Relation_to_other_products}

Complements generalize both the [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) (where the subgroups *H* and *K* are normal in *G*), and the [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) (where one of *H* or *K* is normal in *G*). The product corresponding to a general complement is called the [internal Zappa–Szép product](wikipedia://en/7094111#Internal_Zappa–Szép_products). When *H* and *K* are [nontrivial](wikipedia://en/193059), complement subgroups factor a group into smaller pieces.

## Existence {#Existence}

As previously mentioned, complements need not exist.

A ***p*-complement** is a complement to a [Sylow *p*-subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_subgroup). Theorems of [Frobenius](wikipedia://en/341048) and [Thompson](wikipedia://en/862189) describe when a group has a [normal *p*-complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_p-complement). [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033) characterized finite [soluble](wikipedia://en/105012) groups amongst finite groups as those with *p*-complements for every [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*; these *p*-complements are used to form what is called a [Sylow system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_system).

A **Frobenius complement** is a special type of complement in a [Frobenius group](wikipedia://en/1716123).

A [complemented group](wikipedia://en/5780991) is one where every subgroup has a complement.
