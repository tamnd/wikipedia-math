# Development (differential geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Development"}

In classical [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), **development** is the rolling of one smooth [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_%28differential_geometry%29) over another in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). For example, the [tangent plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_plane) to a surface (such as the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) or the [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29)) at a [point](wikipedia://en/593693) can be rolled around the surface to obtain the tangent plane at other points.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The tangential contact between the surfaces being rolled over one another provides a relation between points on the two surfaces. If this relation is (perhaps only in a [local](wikipedia://en/1462457) sense) a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between the surfaces, then the two surfaces are said to be ***developable*** on each other or *developments* of each other. Differently put, the correspondence provides an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777), locally, between the two surfaces.

In particular, if one of the surfaces is a plane, then the other is called a *[developable surface](wikipedia://en/2878111)*: thus a developable surface is one which is locally isometric to a plane. The cylinder is developable, but the sphere is not.

:::

## Flat connections {#Flat_connections}

Development can be generalized further using flat connections. From this point of view, rolling the tangent plane over a surface defines an [affine connection](wikipedia://en/607690) on the surface (it provides an example of [parallel transport](wikipedia://en/285595) along a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246)), and a developable surface is one for which this connection is flat.

More generally any flat [Cartan connection](wikipedia://en/412272) on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) defines a development of that manifold onto the [model space](wikipedia://en/2908224). Perhaps the most famous example is the development of [conformally flat](wikipedia://en/295907) *n*-manifolds, in which the model-space is the *n*-sphere. The development of a conformally flat manifold is a [conformal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_mapping) [local diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/1728483) from the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of the manifold to the *n*-sphere.

## Undevelopable surfaces {#Undevelopable_surfaces}

The class of double-curved surfaces (undevelopable surfaces) contains objects that cannot be simply unfolded (developed). Such surfaces can be developed only approximately with some distortions of linear surface elements (see the [Stretched grid method](wikipedia://en/33932698))
