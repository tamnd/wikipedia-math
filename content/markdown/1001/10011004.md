# Nicholas Higham

the BBC News correspondent

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Nicholas Higham"}

**Nicholas John Higham** (25 December 1961 – 20 January 2024) was a British [numerical analyst](wikipedia://en/21506).[^o4] He was Royal Society Research Professor and [Richardson Professor of Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/10020397) in the [Department of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/10387568) at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester).[^o5][^o6][^o2][^o8]

:::

## Early life and career {#Early_life_and_career}

Nicholas John Higham was born in [Salford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salford) on 25 December 1961.[^o10] He was educated at Eccles Grammar School, Eccles College, and the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester), from which he gained his B.Sc. in mathematics (1982), M.Sc. in Numerical Analysis and Computing (1983), and PhD in Numerical Analysis (1985).[^o4][^o11] His PhD thesis was supervised by George Hall.[^o2] He was appointed lecturer in mathematics at the University of Manchester in 1985, where he has been [Richardson Professor of Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/10020397) since 1998.[^o5]

In 1988–1989 he was Visiting Assistant Professor of Computer Science at Cornell University, Ithaca, New York.[^o12]

## Research {#Research}

Higham is best known for his work on the accuracy and stability of numerical algorithms.[^o14] He has more than 140 refereed publications[^o6][^o7] on topics such as rounding error analysis, linear systems, least squares problems, matrix functions and nonlinear matrix equations, matrix nearness problems, condition number estimation, and generalized eigenvalue problems. He has contributed software to [LAPACK](wikipedia://en/1013768) and the [NAG library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_Algorithms_Group), and has contributed code included in the [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412) distribution.

Higham's books include *Functions of Matrices: Theory and Computation* (2008),[^o15]*Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*,[^o14] *Handbook of Writing for the Mathematical Sciences*,[^o16] and *MATLAB Guide*, co-authored with his brother [Desmond Higham](wikipedia://en/36854723).[^o17] He was Editor of the *Princeton Companion to Applied Mathematics*[^o18] and a contributor to the *Penguin Dictionary of Mathematics*.[^o19] His books have been translated into Chinese, Japanese and Korean.[^o20][^o21][^o22]

## Professional service {#Professional_service}

Higham served as president of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) 2017–2018.[^o24]

## Death {#Death}

Higham died on 20 January 2024 at the age of 62, after an 18 month struggle with a form of blood cancer. [^o26][^o27]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Higham's honours include the [Alston S. Householder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alston_Scott_Householder) Award VI, 1987 (for the best PhD thesis in numerical algebra 1984–1987), the 1988 [Leslie Fox Prize for Numerical Analysis](wikipedia://en/15366214), a 1999 [Junior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Whitehead_Prize) from the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097), a 2020 [IMA Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMA_Gold_Medal),[^o29] the [2019 Naylor Prize and Lectureship](https://www.lms.ac.uk/sites/lms.ac.uk/files/files/n_higham.pdf) by the London Mathematical Society, the [2021 George Pólya Prize for Mathematical Exposition](https://sinews.siam.org/Details-Page/an21-prize-spotlight#5) by the Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics (SIAM), and the [2022 Hans Schneider Prize in Linear Algebra](wikipedia://en/58527033). Higham held a prestigious [Royal Society Wolfson Research Merit Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_Wolfson_Research_Merit_Award) (2003–2008). He was elected as a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 2007[^o30] and as a [ACM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Fellow) in 2020.[^o31] In 2008 he was awarded the [Fröhlich Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fröhlich_Prize) in recognition of 'his leading contributions to numerical linear algebra and numerical stability analysis'.[^o32] He was elected a Member of [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) in 2016.[^o33] In 2022 he became [Fellow of the Royal Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Academy_of_Engineering).[^o34]

Higham was a Fellow of the Institute of Mathematics and Its Applications, a Fellow of the Institute of Engineering and Technology, and a Fellow of the Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics. He was also a Fellow of the [Alan Turing Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing_Institute).[^o35]
