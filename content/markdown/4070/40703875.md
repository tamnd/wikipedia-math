# Estimation statistics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Estimation statistics"}

**Estimation statistics**, or simply **estimation**, is a data analysis framework that uses a combination of [effect sizes](wikipedia://en/437276), [confidence intervals](wikipedia://en/280911), precision planning, and [meta-analysis](wikipedia://en/62329) to plan experiments, analyze data and interpret results.[^o2] It complements hypothesis testing approaches such as [null hypothesis significance testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing) (NHST), by going beyond the question is an effect present or not, and provides information about how large an effect is.[^o3][^o36] Estimation statistics is sometimes referred to as *the new statistics*.[^o36][^o4][^o5]

:::

The primary aim of estimation methods is to report an [effect size](wikipedia://en/437276) (a [point estimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_estimate)) along with its [confidence interval](wikipedia://en/280911), the latter of which is related to the precision of the estimate.[^o37] The confidence interval summarizes a range of likely values of the underlying population effect. Proponents of estimation see reporting a [*P* value](wikipedia://en/554994) as an unhelpful distraction from the important business of reporting an effect size with its confidence intervals,[^o6] and believe that estimation should replace significance testing for data analysis.[^o7][^o8]

## History {#History}

Starting in 1929, physicist [Raymond Thayer Birge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Thayer_Birge) published review papers[^o10] in which he used weighted-averages methods to calculate estimates of physical constants, a procedure that can be seen as the precursor to modern [meta-analysis](wikipedia://en/62329).[^o11]

In the 1930s [Jerzy Neyman](wikipedia://en/2386106) published a series of papers on statistical estimation where he defined the mathematics and terminology of [confidence intervals](wikipedia://en/280911). [^o12][^o13][^o14]

In the 1960s, estimation statistics was adopted by the non-physical sciences with the development of the [standardized effect size](wikipedia://en/437276) by [Jacob Cohen](wikipedia://en/10856724).

In the 1970s, modern research synthesis was pioneered by [Gene V. Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_V._Glass) with the first systematic review and [meta-analysis](wikipedia://en/62329) for psychotherapy.[^o15] This pioneering work subsequently influenced the adoption of meta-analyses for medical treatments more generally.

In the 1980s and 1990s, estimation methods were extended and refined for practical application by biostatisticians including [Larry Hedges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Hedges), Michael Borenstein, [Doug Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Altman), Martin Gardner, and many others, with the development of the modern (medical) [meta-analysis](https://www.wiley.com/en-sg/Introduction+to+Meta+Analysis%2C+2nd+Edition-p-9781119558354).

Starting in the 1980s, the [systematic review](wikipedia://en/2994579), used in conjunction with meta-analysis, became a technique widely used in medical research. There are over 200,000 [citations](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=meta-analysis&sort=date) to "meta-analysis" in [PubMed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PubMed).

In the 1990s, editor [Kenneth Rothman](http://www.bu.edu/sph/profile/kenneth-rothman/) banned the use of p-values from the journal [*Epidemiology*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiology_%28journal%29); compliance was high among authors but this did not substantially change their analytical thinking.[^o16]

In the 2010s, Geoff Cumming published a [textbook](https://thenewstatistics.com/itns/other-books/introduction-to-the-new-statistics/) dedicated to estimation statistics, along with software in Excel designed to teach effect-size thinking, primarily to psychologists.[^o17] Also in the 2010s, estimation methods were increasingly adopted in neuroscience.[^o18][^o19]

In 2013, the Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association recommended to use estimation in addition to hypothesis testing.[^o20] Also in 2013, the Uniform Requirements for Manuscripts Submitted to Biomedical Journals document made a similar recommendation: "Avoid relying solely on statistical hypothesis testing, such as P values, which fail to convey important information about effect size."[^o21]

In 2019, over 800 scientists signed an open comment calling for the entire concept of statistical significance to be abandoned.[^o22]

In 2019, the [Society for Neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Neuroscience) journal [eNeuro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENeuro) instituted a policy recommending the use of estimation graphics as the preferred method for data presentation.[^o23] And in 2022, the International Society of Physiotherapy Journal Editors recommended the use of estimation methods instead of null hypothesis statistical tests.[^o24]

Despite the widespread adoption of meta-analysis for clinical research, and recommendations by several major publishing institutions, the estimation framework is not routinely used in primary biomedical research.[^o25]

## Methodology {#Methodology}

Many significance tests have an estimation counterpart;[^o27] in almost every case, the test result (or its [p-value](wikipedia://en/554994)) can be simply substituted with the effect size and a precision estimate. For example, instead of using [Student's t-test](wikipedia://en/536080), the analyst can compare two independent groups by calculating the mean difference and its 95% [confidence interval](wikipedia://en/280911). Corresponding methods can be used for a [paired t-test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paired_t-test) and multiple comparisons. Similarly, for a regression analysis, an analyst would report the [coefficient of determination](wikipedia://en/1500869) (R<sup>2</sup>) and the model equation instead of the model's p-value.

However, proponents of estimation statistics warn against reporting only a few numbers. Rather, it is advised to analyze and present data using data visualization.[^o3][^o5][^o37] Examples of appropriate visualizations include the [scatter plot](wikipedia://en/412544) for regression, and Gardner–Altman plots for two independent groups.[^o28] While historical data-group plots (bar charts, box plots, and violin plots) do not display the comparison, estimation plots add a second axis to explicitly visualize the effect size.[^o29]

*Image: 20171231-wiki-figure-png.png.* **The Gardner–Altman plot. Left:** A conventional bar chart, using asterisks to show that the difference is 'statistically significant.' **Right:** A Gardner–Altman plot that shows all data points, along with the mean difference and its confidence intervals.

::: {#o31 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Gardner-Altman plot"}

### Gardner–Altman plot {#Gardner–Altman_plot}

The Gardner–Altman mean difference plot was first described by [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) and [Doug Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Altman) in 1986;[^o28] it is a statistical graph designed to display data from two independent groups.[^o5] There is also a version suitable for [paired data](http://www.estimationstats.com/#/analyze/paired). The key instructions to make this chart are as follows: (1) display all observed values for both groups side-by-side; (2) place a second axis on the right, shifted to show the mean difference scale; and (3) plot the mean difference with its confidence interval as a marker with error bars.[^o36] Gardner-Altman plots can be generated with [DABEST-Python](https://github.com/ACCLAB/DABEST-python), or [dabestr](https://github.com/ACCLAB/dabestr); alternatively, the analyst can use GUI software like the [Estimation Stats](http://www.estimationstats.com/#/) app.

*Image: Cumming\_Estimation\_Plot.png.* **The Cumming plot.** A Cumming plot as rendered by the [EstimationStats web application](http://www.estimationstats.com/#/). In the top panel, all observed values are shown. The effect sizes, sampling distribution, and 95% confidence intervals are plotted on a separate axes beneath the raw data. For each group, summary measurements (mean ± standard deviation) are drawn as gapped lines.

:::

### Cumming plot {#Cumming_plot}

For multiple groups, [Geoff Cumming](http://www.latrobe.edu.au/psychology/staff/profile?uname=GDCumming) introduced the use of a secondary panel to plot two or more mean differences and their confidence intervals, placed below the observed values panel;[^o36] this arrangement enables [easy comparison](http://www.estimationstats.com/#/analyze/multi) of mean differences ('deltas') over several data groupings. Cumming plots can be generated with the [ESCI package](https://thenewstatistics.com/itns/esci/), [DABEST](https://github.com/ACCLAB/DABEST-python), or the [Estimation Stats app](http://www.estimationstats.com/#/).

### Other methodologies {#Other_methodologies}

In addition to the mean difference, there are numerous other [effect size](wikipedia://en/437276) types, all with relative benefits. Major types include effect sizes in the [Cohen's *d*](wikipedia://en/437276#Cohen's_d) class of standardized metrics, and the [coefficient of determination](wikipedia://en/1500869) (R<sup>2</sup>) for [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997). For non-normal distributions, there are a number of more [robust effect sizes](https://garstats.wordpress.com/2016/05/02/robust-effect-sizes-for-2-independent-groups/), including [Cliff's delta](wikipedia://en/437276#Effect_size_for_ordinal_data) and the [Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov-Smirnov_statistic).

## Flaws in hypothesis testing {#Flaws_in_hypothesis_testing}

In [hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing), the primary objective of statistical calculations is to obtain a [p-value](wikipedia://en/554994), the probability of seeing an obtained result, or a more extreme result, when assuming the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) is true. If the p-value is low (usually \< 0.05), the statistical practitioner is then encouraged to reject the null hypothesis. Proponents of **estimation** reject the validity of hypothesis testing[^o36][^o37] for the following reasons, among others:

- P-values are easily and commonly misinterpreted. For example, the p-value is often mistakenly thought of as 'the probability that the null hypothesis is true.'
- The null hypothesis is always wrong for every set of observations: there is always some effect, even if it is minuscule.[^o38]
- Hypothesis testing produces dichotomous yes-no answers, while discarding important information about magnitude.[^o39]
- Any particular p-value arises through the interaction of the [effect size](wikipedia://en/437276), the [sample size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_size) (all things being equal a larger sample size produces a smaller p-value) and sampling error.[^o40]
- At low [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_of_a_test), simulation reveals that sampling error makes p-values extremely volatile.[^o41]

:::

## Benefits of estimation statistics {#Benefits_of_estimation_statistics}

### Quantification {#Quantification}

While p-values focus on yes/no answers, estimation directs the analyst's attention to quantification.

### Advantages of confidence intervals {#Advantages_of_confidence_intervals}

Confidence intervals behave in a predictable way. By definition, 95% confidence intervals have a 95% chance of covering the underlying population mean (μ). This feature remains constant with increasing sample size; what changes is that the interval becomes smaller. In addition, 95% confidence intervals are also 83% prediction intervals: one (pre experimental) confidence interval has an 83% chance of covering any future experiment's mean.[^o36] As such, knowing a single experiment's 95% confidence intervals gives the analyst a reasonable range for the population mean. Nevertheless, confidence distributions and posterior distributions provide a whole lot more information than a single point estimate or intervals,[^o45] that can exacerbate dichotomous thinking according to the interval covering or not covering a "null" value of interest (i.e. the Inductive behavior of Neyman as opposed to that of Fisher[^o46]).

### Evidence-based statistics {#Evidence-based_statistics}

Psychological studies of the perception of statistics reveal that reporting interval estimates leaves a more accurate perception of the data than reporting p-values.[^o48]

::: {#o50 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Precision planning"}

### Precision planning {#Precision_planning}

The precision of an estimate is formally defined as 1/[variance](wikipedia://en/32344), and like power, increases (improves) with increasing sample size. Like [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_power), a high level of precision is expensive; research grant applications would ideally include precision/cost analyses. Proponents of estimation believe precision planning should replace [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_power) since statistical power itself is conceptually linked to significance testing.[^o36] Precision planning can be done with the [ESCI web app](https://www.esci.thenewstatistics.com/esci-precision.html#tab-1).

:::
