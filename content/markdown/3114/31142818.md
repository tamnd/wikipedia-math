# Henricus Reneri

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henricus Reneri"}

*Image: Handschrift Henricus Reneri.jpg.* Writing by Henricus Reneri

**Henricus Reneri** or **Renerius** (1593, [Huy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huy) – 20 March 1639, [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht)) was a Dutch[^o2] philosopher.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Reneri was born at Huy in the [Prince-Bishopric of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Liège) in 1593. He studied [liberal arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts) at the [University of Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_University_of_Leuven) and attended the Grand [Séminaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary) of [Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liège). After his conversion to [Calvinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism) in 1616 he went to the [Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic). He studied theology at the [Collège Wallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_Wallon) at Leiden, but he broke off his studies in 1621. The following ten years Reneri worked as a private [tutor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutor) to the children of several Amsterdam merchant-regents, including [Adriaan Pauw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriaan_Pauw). In the meantime he studied medicine at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University). In 1631 he found a position as professor of philosophy at the illustrious school of [Deventer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deventer), the Illustre Gymnasium. From 1634 he held the same chair at the newly founded illustrious school of Utrecht, which was raised to the status of [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University) in 1636. He died only three years later, at the age of 46.

Reneri mainly taught [scholastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_scholasticism) logic and natural philosophy. Apart from his professorship, he worked on a method of his own in the [Ramist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramist) tradition. This method met especially with much enthusiasm within the Hartlib circle, the European network around the English [pansophist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pansophist) [Samuel Hartlib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Hartlib). Furthermore, Reneri was engaged in experiments and inventions in the fields of [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_optics#Renaissance_and_Early_Modern), [thermometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermometer#Development), [water clocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_clocks), [alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy) and [natural magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_magic).

Reneri was one of the best friends of [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525) and an admirer of his philosophy. They met during the winter of 1628-1629 and Descartes followed Reneri to Deventer and Utrecht. Reneri is reported to have read the [*Discours de la Méthode*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discourse_on_the_Method) and the accompanying *Essais* (including [La Géométrie](wikipedia://en/239981)) publicly in his classes.[^o4] Nevertheless, Reneri is not to be regarded as a [Cartesian philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesianism), since his works show little Cartesian influences.[^o5]

What survives are an inaugural address, several [disputations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disputations) which were presided by him, and a correspondence of more than sixty letters with leading scholars, philosophers, theologians, diplomats and poets from the Republic and abroad, such as [André Rivet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Rivet), [Constantijn Huygens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantijn_Huygens), [Pierre Gassendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Gassendi) and [Pieter Corneliszoon Hooft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_Corneliszoon_Hooft).
