# Sidney Morris

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sidney Morris"}

**Sidney "Sid" Morris** (born 1947) is an Emeritus Professor at [Federation University Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_University_Australia) who worked in the fields of mathematics and information technology. Before his retirement in 2010, he was a professor at several universities and received the 1987 [Lester R. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford) award from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772). Morris is a fellow and former vice-president of the [Australian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/4579582) (AMS).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Morris earned a [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in 1969 from the [University of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Queensland), Australia and one year later completed a Ph.D. at [Flinders University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flinders_University). His dissertation, completed under the supervision of [Igor Kluvanek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Kluvanek), defined the concept of "variety" among [topological groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_groups) and proved a result similar to the [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897).[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

During his first senior position at [La Trobe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Trobe_University), Morris began as an editor for the *Bulletin of the [Australian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/4579582)*. He then became [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) of the *Australian Mathematical Society Lecture Series*, a series published by [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press).[^o6]

In 1987, Morris received the [Lester R. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford) award from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for the article *Numerical Geometry – Numbers for Shapes*.[^o7][^o8]

Morris published his first book *The Structure of Compact Groups* in 1998 with Karl Heinrich Hofmann.[^o9] He, along with Arthur Jones and Kenneth R. Pearson, published *Abstract Algebra and Famous Impossibilities* in 2022 as part of [Springer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Publishing)'s [Undergraduate Texts in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/8871375) series.[^o10]

Although he is retired, Morris remains an editor for the AMS's *Gazette* and [MDPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MDPI)'s *Internation Journal of Topology*.[^o11][^o12] He continues to maintain his [online book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_book) *Topology Without Tears*, which was initially published in 1985.[^o13]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Sidney Morris received his [rabbinical ordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbinical_ordination) in 2016 at the age of 68.[^o15] He has been married to his wife, Elizabeth, for over fifty years.[^o16]
