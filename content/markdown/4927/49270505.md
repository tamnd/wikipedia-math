# Hafnian

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hafnian"}

Hafnium In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **hafnian** is a [scalar](wikipedia://en/3588331) function of a [symmetric matrix](wikipedia://en/126474) that generalizes the [permanent](wikipedia://en/215889).

:::

The hafnian was named by [Eduardo R. Caianiello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_R._Caianiello) "to mark the fruitful period of stay in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen) (Hafnia in Latin)."[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The hafnian of a $2n\times 2n$ symmetric matrix $A$ is defined as

$$\operatorname{haf}(A) = \sum_{\rho \in P_{2n}^2} \prod_{\{i,j\} \in \rho} A_{i,j},$$

where $P_{2n}^2$ is the set of all [partitions](wikipedia://en/340240) of the set $\{1, 2, \dots, 2n\}$ into subsets of size $2$.[^o5][^o6]

This definition is similar to that of the [Pfaffian](wikipedia://en/530060#Formal_definition), but differs in that the [signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_%28permutation%29) of the permutations are not taken into account. Thus the relationship of the hafnian to the Pfaffian is the same as relationship of the [permanent](wikipedia://en/215889) to the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468).[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Basic properties"}

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

Besides its definition as a sum over perfect pairings, the hafnian of a $2n\times 2n$ symmetric matrix $A$ can equivalently be written as

$$\operatorname{haf}(A)=\frac{1}{n!2^n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}A_{\sigma(2i-1),\sigma(2i)},$$

where $S_{2n}$ is the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) on $\{1,2,\dots,2n\}$.[^o10]

An equivalent Levi-Civita representation holds for any even-dimensional symmetric matrix $V=(V_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{N}$:

$$\operatorname{haf}(V)=\frac{1}{2^{N/2}(N/2)!}\sum_{i_1,\dots,i_N=1}^{N}|\epsilon^{i_1\cdots i_N}|V_{i_1 i_2}\cdots V_{i_{N-1}i_N},$$

where $N$ is even.[^o11]

The hafnian also admits a fermionic ([Berezin integral](wikipedia://en/20041308)) representation. If $V$ is a symmetric $2L\times 2L$ matrix and $\chi_i,\bar{\chi}_i$ are Grassmann variables, then

$$\int D(\chi,\bar{\chi})\,\exp\!\left[\frac{1}{2}(\chi\bar{\chi})^{\mathsf T}V(\chi\bar{\chi})\right]=\operatorname{haf}(V),$$

where

$$(\chi\bar{\chi})^{\mathsf T}=\bigl(\bar{\chi}_1\chi_1,\dots,\bar{\chi}_{2L}\chi_{2L}\bigr)$$

and

$D(\chi,\bar{\chi})=d\chi_1\,d\bar{\chi}_1\cdots d\chi_{2L}\,d\bar{\chi}_{2L}.$[^o11]

For low-dimensional examples,

$$\operatorname{haf}\!\begin{pmatrix}V_{11}&V_{12}\\V_{12}&V_{22}\end{pmatrix}=V_{12},$$

and for a $4\times 4$ symmetric matrix,

$\operatorname{haf}\!\begin{pmatrix} V_{11}&V_{12}&V_{13}&V_{14}\\ V_{12}&V_{22}&V_{23}&V_{24}\\ V_{13}&V_{23}&V_{33}&V_{34}\\ V_{14}&V_{24}&V_{34}&V_{44} \end{pmatrix} = V_{12}V_{34}+V_{13}V_{24}+V_{14}V_{23}.$[^o11]

A useful scaling property is the following. If $V=(V_{ij})$ and $S=(S_{ij})$ are symmetric matrices such that

$$V_{ij}=g_i g_j S_{ij},$$

then, for even $N$,

$$\operatorname{haf}(V)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N} g_i\right)\operatorname{haf}(S).$$

In particular, if $V=gS$ for a scalar $g$, then

$\operatorname{haf}(gS)=g^{N/2}\operatorname{haf}(S).$[^o11]

The hafnian of the [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of a graph counts its [perfect matchings](wikipedia://en/44031) (also known as 1-factors). Indeed, a partition of $\{1,2,\dots,2n\}$ into subsets of size 2 is equivalent to a perfect matching of the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_{2n}$.

The hafnian is closely related to the [permanent](wikipedia://en/215889). In particular,

$\operatorname{per}(C)=\operatorname{haf}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & C \\ C^\mathsf{T} & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.[^o5]

Thus, just as the hafnian counts perfect matchings in a graph from its adjacency matrix, the permanent counts matchings in a [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) from its [biadjacency matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biadjacency_matrix).

The hafnian is also related to moments of [multivariate Gaussian distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_Gaussian_distribution). By [Wick's probability theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isserlis'_theorem), for a real $2n\times 2n$ symmetric matrix $A$,

$$\operatorname{haf}(A)=\mathbb E_{\left(X_1,\dots,X_{2n}\right)\sim\mathcal N\left(0,A+\lambda I\right)}\left[X_1\cdots X_{2n}\right],$$

where $\lambda$ is any number large enough that $A+\lambda I$ is [positive semi-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_semi-definite_matrix). Since the hafnian does not depend on the diagonal entries of the matrix, the expectation on the right-hand side is independent of the choice of $\lambda$.

:::

## Generating function {#Generating_function}

Let $S = \begin{pmatrix} A & C \\ C^\mathsf{T} & B \end{pmatrix} = S^\mathsf{T}$ be an arbitrary [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) symmetric $2m \times 2m$ matrix composed of four $m \times m$ blocks $A = A^\mathsf{T}$, $B = B^\mathsf{T}$, $C$ and $C^\mathsf{T}$. Let $z_1, \ldots, z_m$ be a set of $m$ independent variables, and let $Z = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \textrm{diag}(z_1,z_2,\ldots,z_m) \\ \textrm{diag}(z_1,z_2,\ldots,z_m) & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ be an antidiagonal [block matrix](wikipedia://en/457464) composed of entries $z_j$ (each one is presented twice, one time per nonzero block). Let $I$ denote the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718). Then the following identity holds:[^o7]

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det \big(I - Z S\big) }} = \sum_{\{n_k\}} \operatorname{haf} \tilde{S}(\{n_k\}) \prod_{k=1}^m \frac{z_k^{n_k}}{n_k!}$$

where the right-hand side involves hafnians of $\Big(2\sum_k n_k\Big)\times\Big(2\sum_k n_k\Big)$ matrices $\tilde{S}(\{n_k\}) = \begin{pmatrix} \tilde{A}(\{n_k\}) & \tilde{C}(\{n_k\}) \\ \tilde{C}^\mathsf{T}(\{n_k\}) & \tilde{B}(\{n_k\}) \\ \end{pmatrix}$, whose blocks $\tilde{A}(\{n_k\})$, $\tilde{B}(\{n_k\})$, $\tilde{C}(\{n_k\})$ and $\tilde{C}^\mathsf{T}(\{n_k\})$ are built from the blocks $A$, $B$, $C$ and $C^\mathsf{T}$ respectively in the way introduced in [MacMahon's Master theorem](wikipedia://en/25567675). In particular, $\tilde{A}(\{n_k\})$ is a matrix built by replacing each entry $A_{k,t}$ in the matrix $A$ with a $n_k \times n_t$ block filled with $A_{k,t}$; the same scheme is applied to $B$, $C$ and $C^\mathsf{T}$. The sum $\sum_{\{n_k\}}$ runs over all $k$-tuples of non-negative [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), and it is assumed that $\operatorname{haf} \tilde{S}(\{n_k=0|k=1\ldots m\}) = 1$.

The identity can be [proved](wikipedia://en/82285)[^o7] by means of multivariate [Gaussian integrals](wikipedia://en/567580) and [Wick's probability theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isserlis'_theorem).

The expression in the left-hand side, $1 \Big/ \sqrt{\det \big(I - Z S\big)} \Big.$, is in fact a multivariate [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) for a series of hafnians, and the right-hand side constitutes its multivariable [Taylor expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_expansion) in the vicinity of the point $z_1 = \ldots = z_m = 0.$ As a consequence of the given relation, the hafnian of a symmetric $2m \times 2m$ matrix $S$ can be represented as the following mixed [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of the order $m$:

$$\operatorname{haf}S = \bigg(\prod_{k=1}^m \frac{\partial}{\partial z_k} \bigg) \Bigg.\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det \big(I - Z S\big)}} \Bigg\vert_{z_j=0}.$$

The hafnian generating function identity written above can be considered as a hafnian generalization of [MacMahon's Master theorem](wikipedia://en/25567675), which introduces the generating function for [matrix permanents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_permanent) and has the following form in terms of the introduced notation:

$$\frac{1}{\det \big(I - \textrm{diag} (z_1,z_2,\ldots,z_m) C\big)} = \sum_{\{n_k\}} \operatorname{per} \tilde{C}(\{n_k\}) \prod_{k=1}^m \frac{z_k^{n_k}}{n_k!}$$

Note that MacMahon's Master theorem comes as a simple [corollary](wikipedia://en/168865) from the hafnian generating function identity due to the relation $\operatorname{per} (C) = \operatorname{haf} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & C \\ C^\mathsf{T} & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

## Non-negativity {#Non-negativity}

If $C$ is a Hermitian [positive semi-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_semi-definite_matrix) $n\times n$ matrix and $B$ is a complex symmetric $n\times n$ matrix, then

$$\operatorname{haf}\begin{pmatrix} B & C \\ \overline{C} & \overline{B} \end{pmatrix}\geq 0,$$

where $\overline{C}$ denotes the [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) of $C$.[^o14]

A simple way to see this when $\begin{pmatrix} C & B \\ \overline{B} & \overline{C} \\ \end{pmatrix}$ is positive semi-definite is to observe that, by [Wick's probability theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isserlis'_theorem), $\operatorname{haf}\begin{pmatrix} B & C \\ \overline{C} & \overline{B} \\ \end{pmatrix}=\mathbb E\left[\left|X_1\dots X_n\right|^2\right]$ when $\left(X_1,\dots,X_n\right)$ is a [complex normal random vector](wikipedia://en/24666969) with mean $0$, [covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/191752) $C$ and relation matrix $B$.

This result is a generalization of the fact that the permanent of a Hermitian positive semi-definite matrix is non-negative. This corresponds to the special case $B=0$ using the relation $\operatorname{per} (C) = \operatorname{haf} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & C \\ C^\mathsf{T} & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

## Loop hafnian {#Loop_hafnian}

The loop hafnian of an $m\times m$ symmetric matrix is defined as

$$\operatorname{lhaf}(A) = \sum_{\rho \in \mathcal{M}} \prod_{(i,j) \in \rho} A_{i,j}$$

where $\mathcal{M}$ is the set of all perfect matchings of the complete graph on $m$ vertices *with [loops](wikipedia://en/1449083)*, i.e., the set of all ways to partition the set $\{1, 2, \dots, m\}$ into pairs or singletons (treating a singleton $(i)$ as the pair $(i, i)$).[^n1] Thus the loop hafnian depends on the diagonal entries of the matrix, unlike the hafnian.[^n1] Furthermore, the loop hafnian can be non-zero when $m$ is odd.

The loop hafnian can be used to count the total number of matchings in a graph (perfect or non-perfect), also known as its [Hosoya index](wikipedia://en/14221581). Specifically, if one takes the [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of a graph and sets the diagonal elements to 1, then the loop hafnian of the resulting matrix is equal to the total number of matchings in the graph.[^o16]

The loop hafnian can also be thought of as incorporating a mean into the interpretation of the hafnian as a multivariate Gaussian moment. Specifically, by [Wick's probability theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isserlis'_theorem) again, the loop hafnian of a real $m\times m$ symmetric matrix can be expressed as

$$\operatorname{lhaf}(A) = \mathbb E_{\left(X_1,\dots,X_m\right)\sim\mathcal N\left(\operatorname{diag}\left(A\right),A+\lambda I\right)}\left[X_1\dots X_m\right],$$

where $\lambda$ is any number large enough to make $A+\lambda I$ positive semi-definite.

::: {#o18 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Computation"}

## Computation {#Computation}

Computing the hafnian of a (0,1)-matrix is [\#P-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp-P-complete), because [computing the permanent of a (0,1)-matrix is #P-complete](wikipedia://en/20768719).[^o7][^o16]

The hafnian of a $2n\times 2n$ matrix can be computed in $O(n^3 2^n)$ time.[^o16]

If the entries of a matrix are non-negative, then its hafnian can be approximated to within an exponential factor in polynomial time.[^o19]
