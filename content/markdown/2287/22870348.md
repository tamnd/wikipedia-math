# Jacob Tamarkin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jacob Tamarkin"}

**Jacob David Tamarkin** (Я́ков Дави́дович Тама́ркин, Яків Давидович Тамаркін; 11 July 1888 – 18 November 1945) was a Russian-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), best known for his work in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Tamarkin was born in [Chernigov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernigov), Russian Empire (now Chernihiv, Ukraine), to a wealthy Jewish family. His father, David Tamarkin, was a [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician) and his mother, Sophie Krassilschikov, was from a family of a landowner. He shares a common ancestor with the [Van Leer family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardhus_Van_Leer), sometimes spelled Von Löhr or Valar.[^o4] He moved to [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg) as a child and grew up there. In high school, he befriended [Alexander Friedmann](wikipedia://en/728892), a future [cosmologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmologist), with whom he wrote his first mathematics paper in 1906, and remained friends and colleagues until Friedmann's sudden death in 1925. [Vladimir Smirnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Ivanovich_Smirnov_%28mathematician%29) was his other friend from the same gymnasium. Many years later, they coauthored a popular textbook titled "A course in higher mathematics".

Tamarkin studied in [St. Petersburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_University) where he defended his dissertation in 1917. His advisor was [Andrei Markov](wikipedia://en/915). After the graduation, Tamarkin worked at the [Communication Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petersburg_state_university_of_means_of_communication) and [Electrotechnical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_Electrotechnical_University). In 1919 he temporarily became a professor and a dean at [Perm State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perm_State_University), but a year later returned to St. Petersburg where he received a professorship at [St. Petersburg Polytechnical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Polytechnical_University).

In 1925 he became worried about Russia's stability and decided to [immigrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration) to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). His favorite memory was the examination in [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202) he had to take with an American consul in [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), when he tried to prove his identity.[^o5] In the U.S., he became a lecturer at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College). In 1927, Tamarkin received a professorship at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) where he remained until his retirement in 1945, after suffering a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack). He died later that year in [Bethesda, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethesda,_Maryland), a suburb of [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.)[^o6]

Tamarkin's work spanned a number of areas, including [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234), [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038), [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), [moment problem](wikipedia://en/1244992), [boundary value problem](wikipedia://en/436779) and [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). He was a proponent and a founding co-editor of the *[Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646)* (which was based at Brown at that time), together with [Otto Neugebauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Neugebauer) and [William Feller](wikipedia://en/206644).[^o7] He was also an active supporter of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), a member of the council starting 1931, and a vice-president in 1942–43. He had over twenty doctoral students at Brown, including [Dorothy Lewis Bernstein](wikipedia://en/7097637), [Nelson Dunford](wikipedia://en/21022291), [George Forsythe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Forsythe), [Margaret Gurney](wikipedia://en/59567236) and [Derrick Lehmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Henry_Lehmer).

Tamarkin was married to Helene Weichardt (1888–1934) who came from a wealthy family [of German ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Germans_in_Russia_and_the_Soviet_Union). Their son, Paul Tamarkin (1922–1977), was a [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) for [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation).[^o8]
