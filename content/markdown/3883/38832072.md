# Arthur Stanley Ramsey

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur Stanley Ramsey"}

**Arthur Stanley Ramsey** (9 September 1867 – 31 December 1954) was a British mathematician and author of mathematics and physics textbooks.[^o2] He was Fellow of [Magdalene College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalene_College,_Cambridge), and its President from 1915–52.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

The son of Rev. Adam Averell Ramsey of [Dewsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewsbury),[^o5] a [Congregational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregational) [minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_%28Christianity%29),[^o6] and his wife Hephzibah, Ramsey was educated at [Batley Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batley_Grammar_School) and [Magdalene College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalene_College,_Cambridge) where he read Mathematics (B.A. (6th [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29)) 1889; [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Oxbridge_and_Dublin%29) 1893).[^o7]

He was Assistant Master at [Fettes College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fettes_College) from 1890 to 1897, moving into academia as Fellow of Magdalene in 1897. He was [Bursar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursar) of the college, 1904–13 and [University Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Lecturer) in Mathematics, 1926-32. As a tutor, he supervised the maths work of [William Empson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Empson), who would go on to apply path-breaking tools of analytical logic to the criticism of literature.

In 1902 Ramsey married (Mary) Agnes (1875-1927),[^o8] daughter of Rev. Plumpton Stravenson Wilson,[^o9] vicar of [Horbling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horbling), Lincs. and sister to the cricketer and footballer [Geoffrey Plumpton Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Plumpton_Wilson). Mary herself was academically accomplished, having earned a Class II Honours Certificate in Modern History from [St Hugh's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Hugh's_College,_Oxford).[^o10] In April 1913, Mary stood for election to the Cambridge [Board of Guardians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Guardians) in Bridge Ward, and was elected with 321 votes.[^o11]

Ramsey and his wife had two daughters, Bridget and Margaret, and two sons, philosopher and mathematician [Frank Plumpton Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Plumpton_Ramsey) (1903–1930) and [Michael Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ramsey) (1904–1988) who was the [Archbishop of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury) for thirteen years.[^o12][^o13] Mary Agnes was killed in 1927 in a road traffic accident.[^o14]

He is buried in the [Ascension Parish Burial Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Parish_Burial_Ground) in Cambridge; his son Frank and wife Mary are buried in the same plot.[^o15] His home, Howfield, Buckingham Road, is now part of [Cambridge Blackfriars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Blackfriars).

## Publications {#Publications}
