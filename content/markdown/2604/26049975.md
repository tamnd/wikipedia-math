# Kontsevich quantization formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kontsevich quantization formula"}

article In mathematics, the **Kontsevich quantization formula** describes how to construct a generalized [★-product](wikipedia://en/1946702) [operator algebra](wikipedia://en/455987) from a given arbitrary finite-dimensional [Poisson manifold](wikipedia://en/882902). This operator algebra amounts to the [deformation quantization](wikipedia://en/4278245) of the corresponding Poisson algebra. It is due to [Maxim Kontsevich](wikipedia://en/104509).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Deformation quantization of a Poisson algebra {#Deformation_quantization_of_a_Poisson_algebra}

Given a [Poisson algebra](wikipedia://en/345704) $(A, \{\cdot, \cdot\})$, a [deformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory) quantization is an **associative** unital product $\star$ on the algebra of formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) in $\text{ħ}, A[[\text{ħ}]]$, subject to the following two axioms,

$$\begin{aligned} f\star g &=fg+\mathcal{O}(\hbar)\\ {}[f,g] &=f\star g-g\star f=i\hbar\{f,g\}+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^2) \end{aligned}$$

If one were given a Poisson manifold $(M, \{\cdot, \cdot\})$, one could ask, in addition, that

$$f\star g=fg+\sum_{k=1}^\infty \hbar^kB_k(f\otimes g),$$

where the $B_{k}$ are linear bi[differential operators](wikipedia://en/263886) of degree at most $k$.

Two deformations are said to be equivalent iff they are related by a gauge transformation of the type,

$$\begin{cases} D: A[[\hbar]]\to A[[\hbar]] \\ \sum_{k=0}^\infty \hbar^k f_k \mapsto \sum_{k=0}^\infty \hbar^k f_k +\sum_{n\ge1, k\ge0} D_n(f_k)\hbar^{n+k} \end{cases}$$

where $D_{n}$ are differential operators of order at most $n$. The corresponding induced $\star$-product, $\star'$, is then

$$f\,{\star}'\,g = D \left ( \left (D^{-1}f \right )\star \left (D^{-1}g \right ) \right ).$$

For the archetypal example, one may well consider [Groenewold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbrand_J._Groenewold)'s original ["Moyal–Weyl" $\star$-product](wikipedia://en/4277086).

## Kontsevich graphs {#Kontsevich_graphs}

A Kontsevich graph is a simple [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986) without loops on 2 external vertices, labeled *f* and *g*; and $n$ internal vertices, labeled $\Pi$. From each internal vertex originate two edges. All (equivalence classes of) graphs with $n$ internal vertices are accumulated in the set $G_{n}(2)$.

### Associated bidifferential operator {#Associated_bidifferential_operator}

Associated to each graph $\Gamma$, there is a bidifferential operator $B_{\Gamma}( f, g)$ defined as follows. For each edge there is a [partial derivative](wikipedia://en/52565) on the symbol of the target vertex. It is contracted with the corresponding index from the source symbol. The term for the graph $\Gamma$ is the product of all its symbols together with their partial derivatives. Here *f* and *g* stand for smooth functions on the manifold, and $\Pi$ is the [Poisson bivector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_bivector) of the Poisson manifold.

The term for the example graph is

$$\Pi^{i_2j_2}\partial_{i_2}\Pi^{i_1j_1}\partial_{i_1}f\,\partial_{j_1}\partial_{j_2}g.$$

### Associated weight {#Associated_weight}

For adding up these bidifferential operators there are the weights $w_{\Gamma}$ of the graph $\Gamma$. First of all, to each graph there is a multiplicity $m(\Gamma)$ which counts how many equivalent configurations there are for one graph. The rule is that the sum of the multiplicities for all graphs with $n$ internal vertices is $(n(n + 1))^{n}$. The sample graph above has the multiplicity $m(\Gamma) = 8$. For this, it is helpful to enumerate the internal vertices from 1 to $n$.

In order to compute the weight we have to integrate products of the angle in the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826), *H*, as follows. The upper half-plane is $H \subset \mathbb{C}$, endowed with the [Poincaré metric](wikipedia://en/1487868)

$$ds^2=\frac{dx^2+dy^2}{y^2};$$

and, for two points $z, w \in H$ with $z \ne w$, we measure the angle $\varphi$ between the geodesic from $z$ to $i\infty$ and from $z$ to $w$ counterclockwise. This is

$$\phi(z,w)=\frac{1}{2i}\log\frac{(z-w)(z-\bar{w})}{(\bar{z}-w)(\bar{z}-\bar{w})}.$$

The integration domain is *C*<sub>*n*</sub>(*H*) the space

$$C_n(H):=\{(u_1,\dots,u_n)\in H^n: u_i\ne u_j\forall i\ne j\}.$$

The formula amounts

$$w_\Gamma:= \frac{m(\Gamma)}{(2\pi)^{2n}n!}\int_{C_n(H)} \bigwedge_{j=1}^n\mathrm{d}\phi(u_j,u_{t1(j)})\wedge\mathrm{d}\phi(u_j,u_{t2(j)}),$$

where *t*1(*j*) and *t*2(*j*) are the first and second target vertex of the internal vertex $j$. The vertices *f* and *g* are at the fixed positions 0 and 1 in $H$.

::: {#o9 .statement cue=named-heading name="The formula"}

## The formula {#The_formula}

Given the above three definitions, the Kontsevich formula for a star product is now

$$f\star g = fg+\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left(\frac{i\hbar}{2}\right)^n \sum_{\Gamma \in G_n(2)} w_\Gamma B_\Gamma(f\otimes g).$$

::: {#o11 .statement cue=named-heading name="Explicit formula up to second order"}

### Explicit formula up to second order {#Explicit_formula_up_to_second_order}

Enforcing associativity of the $\star$-product, it is straightforward to check directly that the Kontsevich formula must reduce, to second order in $\text{ħ}$, to just

$$\begin{aligned} f\star g &= fg +\tfrac{i\hbar}{2}\Pi^{ij}\partial_i f\,\partial_j g -\tfrac{\hbar^2}{8}\Pi^{i_1j_1}\Pi^{i_2j_2}\partial_{i_1}\,\partial_{i_2}f \partial_{j_1}\,\partial_{j_2}g\\ & - \tfrac{\hbar^2}{12}\Pi^{i_1j_1}\partial_{j_1}\Pi^{i_2j_2}(\partial_{i_1}\partial_{i_2}f \,\partial_{j_2}g -\partial_{i_2}f\,\partial_{i_1}\partial_{j_2}g) +\mathcal{O}(\hbar^3) \end{aligned}$$
