# Spherical conic

*Image: Spherical-conic-chalkboard.jpg.* Spherical conics drawn on a spherical chalkboard. Two confocal conics in blue and yellow share foci F<sub>1</sub> and F<sub>2</sub>. Angles formed with red great-circle arcs from the foci through one of the conics' intersections demonstrate the reflection property of spherical conics. Three mutually perpendicular conic centers and three lines of symmetry in green define a spherical octahedron aligned with the [principal axes](wikipedia://en/11376126) of the conic.

*Image: Peirce projection spherical conics.png.* A grid on the square [dihedron](wikipedia://en/6109457) under inverse [Peirce quincuncial projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peirce_quincuncial_projection) is conformal except at four singularities around the equator, which become the foci of a grid of spherical conics.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spherical conic"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **spherical conic** or **sphero-conic** is a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) on the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859), the intersection of the sphere with a concentric [elliptic cone](wikipedia://en/66221593). It is the spherical analog of a [conic section](wikipedia://en/19008673) ([ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277), [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231), or [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052)) in the plane, and as in the planar case, a spherical conic can be defined as the [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of points the sum or difference of whose [great-circle distances](wikipedia://en/379733) to two [foci](wikipedia://en/1032607) is constant.[^o6] By taking the antipodal point to one focus, every **spherical ellipse** is also a **spherical hyperbola**, and vice versa. As a space curve, a spherical conic is a [quartic](wikipedia://en/308152), though its orthogonal projections in three [principal axes](wikipedia://en/11376126) are planar conics. Like planar conics, spherical conics also satisfy a "reflection property": the great-circle arcs from the two foci to any point on the conic have the tangent and normal to the conic at that point as their angle bisectors.

:::

Many theorems about conics in the plane extend to spherical conics. For example, Graves's theorem and Ivory's theorem about confocal conics can also be proven on the sphere; see [confocal conic sections](wikipedia://en/54142755) about the planar versions.[^o11]

Just as the [arc length of an ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277#Arc_length) is given by an incomplete [elliptic integral](wikipedia://en/9960) of the second kind, the arc length of a spherical conic is given by an incomplete elliptic integral of the third kind.[^o7]

An [orthogonal coordinate system](wikipedia://en/5006088) in Euclidean space based on concentric spheres and quadratic cones is called a [conical](wikipedia://en/5007616) or sphero-conical coordinate system. When restricted to the surface of a sphere, the remaining coordinates are confocal spherical conics. Sometimes this is called an elliptic coordinate system on the sphere, by analogy to a planar [elliptic coordinate system](wikipedia://en/5006562). Such coordinates can be used in the computation of conformal maps from the sphere to the plane.[^o5]

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The solution of the [Kepler problem](wikipedia://en/2410571) in a space of uniform positive curvature is a spherical conic, with a potential proportional to the cotangent of geodesic distance.[^o8]

Because it preserves distances to a pair of specified points, the [two-point equidistant projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-point_equidistant_projection) maps the family of confocal conics on the sphere onto two families of confocal ellipses and hyperbolae in the plane.[^o9]

If a portion of the Earth is modeled as spherical, e.g. using the [osculating sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osculating_sphere) at a point on an ellipsoid of revolution, the hyperbolae used in [hyperbolic navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_navigation) (which determines position based on the difference in received signal timing from fixed radio transmitters) are spherical conics.[^o10]
