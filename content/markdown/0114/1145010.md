# Jonathan Borwein

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jonathan Borwein"}

**Jonathan Michael Borwein** (20 May 1951 – 2 August 2016)[^o4][^o5] was a Scottish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who held an appointment as Laureate Professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Newcastle, Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Newcastle,_Australia). He was a close associate of [David H. Bailey](wikipedia://en/184462), and they have been prominent public advocates of [experimental mathematics](wikipedia://en/256363).

:::

Borwein's interests spanned [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) (analysis), [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) (optimization), [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833) (numerical and computational analysis), and high performance computing. He authored ten books, including several on experimental mathematics,[^o6] a [monograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph) on [convex functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_functions), and over 400 refereed articles.[^o7] He was a co-founder in 1995 of software company MathResources,[^o8] consulting and producing interactive software primarily for school and university mathematics. He was not associated with MathResources at the time of his death.

Borwein was also an expert on the number [pi](wikipedia://en/23601) and especially its computation.[^o9][^o10]

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Borwein was born in [St. Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Andrews), Scotland, in 1951 into a Jewish family. His father was mathematician [David Borwein](wikipedia://en/4860688), with whom he collaborated. His brother [Peter Borwein](wikipedia://en/1339736) was also a mathematician.

Borwein was married to Judith (née Roots), and had three daughters.[^o5]

He received his B.A. (Honours Math) from [University of Western Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Ontario) in 1971, and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) from [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) in 1974 as a [Rhodes Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Scholarship) at [Jesus College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Oxford).

## Career {#Career}

Prior to joining [Simon Fraser University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fraser_University) in 1993, he worked at [Dalhousie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalhousie_University) (1974–91), [Carnegie-Mellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie-Mellon) (1980–82) and the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) (1991–93). He was Shrum Professor of Science (1993–2003) and a [Canada Research Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Research_Chair) in Information Technology (2001–08) at [Simon Fraser University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fraser_University), where he was founding Director of the Centre for Experimental and Constructive Mathematics and developed the [Inverse Symbolic Calculator](wikipedia://en/9087019) together with his brother and [Simon Plouffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Plouffe). In 2004, he rejoined the Faculty of Computer Science at Dalhousie University as a Canada Research Chair in Distributed and Collaborative Research, cross-appointed in Mathematics, while preserving an adjunct appointment at Simon Fraser.

Borwein was Governor at large of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (2004–07), was president of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) (2000–02) and chair of (the Canadian National Science Library) NRC-[CISTI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CISTI) Advisory Board (2000–2003).[^o13] He served as chair of various [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO) scientific programs. He was also Chair of the Scientific Advisory Committee of the [Australian Mathematical Sciences Institute](wikipedia://en/14344543) (AMSI). He chaired the Canadian HPC consortium, later Compute Canada, and the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868)'s Committee on Electronic Information and Communications (2002–2008).[^o14]

## Awards {#Awards}

Borwein received various awards including the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) (1993),[^o16] Fellowship in the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) (1994), Fellowship in the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) (2002), an honorary degree from [Limoges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limoges) (1999), and foreign membership in the [Bulgarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Academy_of_Sciences) (2003). He was elected as a Fellow to the [Australian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Academy_of_Science) (2010). In 2014, he became a Fellow of the American Mathematical Society.[^o17] He was an ISI [highly cited mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Scientific_Information_highly_cited_researcher) for the period 1981–1999.
