# Whitney immersion theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Whitney immersion theorem"}

In [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), the **Whitney immersion theorem** (named after [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794)) states that for $m>1$, any smooth $m$-dimensional [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) (required also to be [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) and [second-countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-countable)) has a one-to-one [immersion](wikipedia://en/6993953) in [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9697) $2m$-space, and a (not necessarily one-to-one) immersion in $(2m-1)$-space. Similarly, every smooth $m$-dimensional manifold can be immersed in the $2m-1$-dimensional sphere (this removes the $m>1$ constraint).

:::

The weak version, for $2m+1$, is due to [transversality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transversality_%28mathematics%29) ([general position](wikipedia://en/383736), [dimension counting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_counting)): two *m*-dimensional manifolds in $\mathbf{R}^{2m}$ intersect generically in a 0-dimensional space.

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Immersion conjecture"}

## Further results {#Further_results}

[William S. Massey](wikipedia://en/4967089) (Massey 1960) went on to prove that every *n*-dimensional manifold is [cobordant](wikipedia://en/391832) to a manifold that immerses in $S^{2n-a(n)}$ where $a(n)$ is the number of 1's that appear in the binary expansion of $n$. (The corresponding dimensions are listed in ). In the same paper, Massey proved that for every *n* there is manifold (which happens to be a product of real projective spaces) that does not immerse in $S^{2n-1-a(n)}$.

The conjecture that every *n*-manifold immerses in $S^{2n-a(n)}$ became known as the **immersion conjecture**. This conjecture was eventually solved in the affirmative by (txt).
