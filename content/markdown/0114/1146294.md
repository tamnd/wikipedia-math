# Current algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Current algebra"}

Certain [commutation relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutation_relation) among the current density operators in [quantum field theories](wikipedia://en/25267) define an infinite-dimensional [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) called a **current algebra**.[^o2] Mathematically these are Lie algebras consisting of smooth maps from a manifold into a finite dimensional Lie algebra.[^o3]

:::

## History {#History}

The original current algebra, proposed in 1964 by [Murray Gell-Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Gell-Mann), described weak and electromagnetic currents of the strongly interacting particles, [hadrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrons), leading to the **Adler–Weisberger formula** and other important physical results. The basic concept, in the era just preceding [quantum chromodynamics](wikipedia://en/25264), was that even without knowing the Lagrangian governing hadron dynamics in detail, exact kinematical information – the local symmetry – could still be encoded in an algebra of currents.[^o5]

The commutators involved in current algebra amount to an infinite-dimensional extension of the [Jordan map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_map), where the quantum fields represent infinite arrays of oscillators.

Current algebraic techniques are still part of the shared background of [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics) when analyzing symmetries and indispensable in discussions of the [Goldstone theorem](wikipedia://en/628183).

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

In a [non-Abelian](wikipedia://en/4791400) [Yang–Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang–Mills) symmetry, where $V$ and $A$ are flavor-current and axial-current 0th components (charge densities), respectively, the paradigm of a current algebra is[^o8][^o9]

$\bigl[\ V^a(\vec{x}),\ V^b(\vec{y})\ \bigr] = i\ f^{ab}_c\ \delta(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\ V^c(\vec{x})\ ,$ and

$$\bigl[\ V^a(\vec{x}),\ A^b(\vec{y})\ \bigr] = i\ f^{ab}_c\ \delta(\vec{x} - \vec{y})\ A^c(\vec{x})\ ,\qquad \bigl[\ A^a(\vec{x}),\ A^b(\vec{y})\ \bigr] = i\ f^{ab}_c\ \delta(\vec{x} - \vec{y})\ V^c(\vec{x}) ~,$$

where $f$ are the structure constants of the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944). To get meaningful expressions, these must be [normal ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_order).

The algebra resolves to a direct sum of two algebras, $L$ and $R$, upon defining

$$L^a(\vec{x})\equiv \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\ V^a(\vec{x}) - A^a(\vec{x})\ \bigr)\ , \qquad R^a(\vec{x}) \equiv \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\ V^a(\vec{x}) + A^a(\vec{x})\ \bigr)\ ,$$

whereupon $\bigl[\ L^a(\vec{x}),\ L^b(\vec{y})\ \bigr]= i\ f^{ab}_c\ \delta(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\ L^c(\vec{x})\ ,\quad \bigl[\ L^a(\vec{x}),\ R^b(\vec{y})\ \bigr] = 0, \quad \bigl[\ R^a(\vec{x}),\ R^b(\vec{y})\ \bigr] = i\ f^{ab}_c\ \delta(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\ R^c(\vec{x})~.$

:::

## Conformal field theory {#Conformal_field_theory}

For the case where space is a one-dimensional circle, current algebras arise naturally as a [central extension](wikipedia://en/45603435#Central) of the [loop algebra](wikipedia://en/1336457), known as [Kac–Moody algebras](wikipedia://en/1146292) or, more specifically, [affine Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1350865). In this case, the commutator and normal ordering can be given a very precise mathematical definition in terms of integration contours on the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628), thus avoiding some of the formal divergence difficulties commonly encountered in quantum field theory.

When the [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386) of the Lie algebra is contracted with the current commutator, one obtains the [energy–momentum tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy–momentum_tensor) of a [two-dimensional conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/52183913). When this tensor is expanded as a [Laurent series](wikipedia://en/61213), the resulting algebra is called the [Virasoro algebra](wikipedia://en/348029).[^o11] This calculation is known as the [Sugawara construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugawara_construction).

The general case is formalized as the [vertex operator algebra](wikipedia://en/1287577).
