# BIBO stability

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="BIBO stability"}

In [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), specifically [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039), **bounded-input, bounded-output** (**BIBO**) **stability** is a form of [stability](wikipedia://en/7039#Stability) for [signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_%28information_theory%29) and [systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_system) that take inputs. If a system is BIBO stable, then the output will be [bounded](wikipedia://en/311509) for every input to the system that is bounded.

:::

A signal is bounded if there is a finite value $B > 0$ such that the signal magnitude never exceeds $B$, that is

For [discrete-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete-time) signals: $\exists B \forall n(\ |y[n]| \leq B) \quad n \in \mathbb{Z}$

For [continuous-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous-time) signals: $\exists B \forall t(\ |y(t)| \leq B) \quad t \in \mathbb{R}$

## Time-domain condition for linear time-invariant systems {#Time-domain_condition_for_linear_time-invariant_systems}

### Continuous-time necessary and sufficient condition {#Continuous-time_necessary_and_sufficient_condition}

For a [continuous time](wikipedia://en/6122) [linear time-invariant (LTI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTI_system_theory) system, the condition for BIBO stability is that the [impulse response](wikipedia://en/545825), $h(t)$ , be [absolutely integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-integrable_function), i.e., its [L<sup>1</sup> norm](wikipedia://en/45194) exists.

$$\int_{-\infty}^\infty \left|h(t)\right|\,\mathord{\operatorname{d}}t = \| h \|_1 \in \mathbb{R}$$

### Discrete-time sufficient condition {#Discrete-time_sufficient_condition}

For a [discrete time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_time) LTI system, the condition for BIBO stability is that the [impulse response](wikipedia://en/545825) be [absolutely summable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-integrable_function), i.e., its $\ell^1$ [norm](wikipedia://en/45194) exists.

$$\ \sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty |h[n]| = \| h \|_1 \in \mathbb{R}$$

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of sufficiency"}

#### Proof of sufficiency {#Proof_of_sufficiency}

Given a [discrete](wikipedia://en/8492) time LTI system with [impulse response](wikipedia://en/545825) $\ h[n]$ the relationship between the input $\ x[n]$ and the output $\ y[n]$ is

$$\ y[n] = h[n] * x[n]$$

where $*$ denotes [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519). Then it follows by the definition of convolution

$$\ y[n] = \sum_{k=-\infty}^\infty h[k] x[n-k]$$

Let $\| x \|_{\infty}$ be the maximum value of $\ |x[n]|$, i.e., the [$L_{\infty}$-norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum_norm).

$$\left|y[n]\right| = \left|\sum_{k=-\infty}^\infty h[n-k] x[k]\right|$$

$\le \sum_{k=-\infty}^\infty \left|h[n-k]\right| \left|x[k]\right|$ (by the [triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941))

$$\begin{aligned} & \le \sum_{k=-\infty}^\infty \left|h[n-k]\right| \| x \|_\infty \\ & = \| x \|_{\infty} \sum_{k=-\infty}^\infty \left|h[n-k]\right| \\ & = \| x \|_{\infty} \sum_{k=-\infty}^\infty \left|h[k]\right| \end{aligned}$$

If $h[n]$ is absolutely summable, then $\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}{\left|h[k]\right|} = \| h \|_1 \in \mathbb{R}$ and

$$\| x \|_\infty \sum_{k=-\infty}^\infty \left|h[k]\right| = \| x \|_\infty \| h \|_1$$

So if $h[n]$ is absolutely summable and $\left|x[n]\right|$ is bounded, then $\left|y[n]\right|$ is bounded as well because $\| x \|_{\infty} \| h \|_1 \in \mathbb{R}$.

The proof for continuous-time follows the same arguments.

:::

## Frequency-domain condition for linear time-invariant systems {#Frequency-domain_condition_for_linear_time-invariant_systems}

### Continuous-time signals {#Continuous-time_signals}

For a [rational](wikipedia://en/361210) and [continuous-time system](wikipedia://en/6122), the condition for stability is that the [region of convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_of_convergence) (ROC) of the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) includes the [imaginary axis](wikipedia://en/217628). When the system is [causal](wikipedia://en/554087), the ROC is the [open region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_region) to the right of a vertical line whose [abscissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abscissa) is the [real part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_part) of the "largest pole", or the [pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_%28complex_analysis%29) that has the greatest real part of any pole in the system. The real part of the largest pole defining the ROC is called the [abscissa of convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abscissa_of_convergence). Therefore, all poles of the system must be in the strict left half of the [s-plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-plane) for BIBO stability.

This stability condition can be derived from the above time-domain condition as follows:

$$\begin{aligned} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left|h(t)\right| \, dt & = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left|h(t)\right| \left| e^{-j \omega t }\right| \, dt \\ & = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left|h(t) (1 \cdot e)^{-j \omega t} \right| \, dt \\ & = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left|h(t) (e^{\sigma + j \omega})^{- t} \right| \, dt \\ & = \int_{-\infty}^\infty \left|h(t) e^{-s t} \right| \, dt \end{aligned}$$

where $s = \sigma + j \omega$ and $\operatorname{Re}(s) = \sigma = 0.$

The [region of convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_of_convergence) must therefore include the [imaginary axis](wikipedia://en/217628).

### Discrete-time signals {#Discrete-time_signals}

For a [rational](wikipedia://en/361210) and [discrete time system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_signal), the condition for stability is that the [region of convergence](wikipedia://en/18610#Region_of_convergence) (ROC) of the [z-transform](wikipedia://en/171589) includes the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071). When the system is [causal](wikipedia://en/554087), the ROC is the [open region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_region) outside a circle whose radius is the magnitude of the [pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_%28complex_analysis%29) with largest magnitude. Therefore, all poles of the system must be inside the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) in the [z-plane](wikipedia://en/171589) for BIBO stability.

This stability condition can be derived in a similar fashion to the continuous-time derivation:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{n = -\infty}^\infty \left|h[n]\right| & = \sum_{n = -\infty}^\infty \left|h[n]\right| \left| e^{-j \omega n} \right| \\ & = \sum_{n = -\infty}^\infty \left|h[n] (1 \cdot e)^{-j \omega n} \right| \\ & =\sum_{n = -\infty}^\infty \left|h[n] (r e^{j \omega})^{-n} \right| \\ & = \sum_{n = -\infty}^\infty \left|h[n] z^{- n} \right| \end{aligned}$$

where $z = r e^{j \omega}$ and $r = |z| = 1$.

The [region of convergence](wikipedia://en/18610#Region_of_convergence) must therefore include the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071).
