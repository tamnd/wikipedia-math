# List of Chinese provincial-level divisions by GDP per capita

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of Chinese provincial-level divisions by GDP per capita"}

The article is about [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China)'s [first-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province-level_divisions_of_China) [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_China) by their [gross domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product) per capita for selected years. All figures are given in the national currency, [renminbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renminbi) (CNY) and in [USD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar), at nominal values. [Purchasing power parity](wikipedia://en/64489) (PPP) indicators, which are measured in [international dollars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_dollar) and fluctuate frequently, and PPP data is not included in the main table, only included at the end of the entry. Purchasing power parity reference comes from the [World Economic Outlook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Outlook) published by the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund) (IMF). The [average CNY exchange rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_renminbi_exchange_rates) used here is from [China NBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Statistics_of_China), and [CNY PPP exchange rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_renminbi_exchange_rates#List_of_purchasing_power_parity_exchange_rates) are estimated according to the IMF.

:::

Unless otherwise specified, GDP per capita is based on the annual average population. The annual average population, also referred to as the mid-year population, is calculated as the average of the resident populations at the end of two consecutive years.

Since the implementation of reform and opening-up in mainland China in 1978, its economy has developed rapidly. In 1995, the GDP per capita of Beijing, Tianjin, and Shanghai exceeded US\$1,000; by 2000, 10 provinces had GDP per capita above US\$1,000, of which Beijing, Tianjin, and Shanghai exceeded US\$2,000. Per capita GDP surpassed US\$10,000 for the first time in Beijing (US\$10,402) and Shanghai (US\$10,593) in 2009. Mainland China's per capita GDP (US\$10,083) exceeded US\$10,000 for the first time in 2018, and 11 provinces—including Beijing, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Tianjin, Fujian, Zhejiang, Guangdong, Hubei, Chongqing, Shandong and Inner Mongolia—had per capita GDP above US\$10,000, with Beijing and Shanghai exceeding US\$20,000 per capita in the same year. As of 2025, 22 provincial-level divisions in mainland China have a nominal GDP per capita above US\$10,000, while 9 — including Yunnan, Qinghai, and Gansu — remain below this threshold. Beijing and Shanghai exceed US\$30,000, and Jiangsu and Fujian have surpassed US\$20,000.

Apart from the national capital, Beijing, and the financial capital, Shanghai, coastal provinces lead China's per capita GDP rankings, as they are most heavily exposed to [export-oriented industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export-oriented_industrialization). The [seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) in these provinces provide greater exposure to [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade) and allow both local and foreign companies, which receive [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment_in_China) (FDI) in China, to [export goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export) and access [global markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_market) more efficiently.[^o2]

For comparison purposes, this article also presents the GDP of the [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong) and [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau) [Special Administrative Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_regions_of_China) (SARs), which maintain [separate economic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_territory) and currencies. Likewise, GDP indicators for the two SARs are presented in local currency, U.S. dollars, and at [purchasing power parity](wikipedia://en/64489), with PPP data sourced from the IMF.
