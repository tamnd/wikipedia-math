# András Gyárfás

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="András Gyárfás"}

**András Gyárfás** (born 1945) is a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specializes in the study of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). He is famous for two conjectures:

:::

- Together with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) he conjectured what is now called the [Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture](wikipedia://en/336838) which states that any [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) with minimum [degree](wikipedia://en/325806#Definitions) 3 contains a [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) whose length is a [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948).
- He and [David Sumner](wikipedia://en/47676305) independently formulated the [Gyárfás–Sumner conjecture](wikipedia://en/56030165)[^o2] according to which, for every [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) *T*, the *T*-free graphs are [χ-bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Χ-bounded).

Gyárfás began working as a researcher for the Computer and Automation Research Institute of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1968. He earned a [candidate degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_degree) in 1980, and a doctorate (Dr. Math. Sci.) in 1992. He won the Géza Grünwald Commemorative Prize for young researchers of the [János Bolyai Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/22777615) in 1978.[^o3][^o4] He was co-author with Paul Erdős on 15 papers, and thus has [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) one.[^o5]
