# Thompson groups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thompson groups"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Thompson groups** (also called **Thompson's groups**, **vagabond groups** or **chameleon groups**) are three [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), commonly denoted $F \subseteq T \subseteq V$, that were introduced by Richard Thompson in some unpublished handwritten notes in 1965 as a possible counterexample to the [von Neumann conjecture](wikipedia://en/662256). Of the three, *F* is the most widely studied, and is sometimes referred to as **the Thompson group** or **Thompson's group**.

:::

The Thompson groups, and *F* in particular, have a collection of unusual properties that have made them counterexamples to many general conjectures in group theory. All three Thompson groups are infinite but [finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group). The groups *T* and *V* are (rare) examples of infinite but finitely-presented [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600). The group *F* is not simple but its [derived subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_subgroup) \[*F*,*F*\] is and the quotient of *F* by its derived subgroup is the [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) of rank 2. *F* is [totally ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered_group), has [exponential growth](wikipedia://en/498727), and does not contain a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) isomorphic to the [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) of rank 2.

It is conjectured that *F* is not [amenable](wikipedia://en/657430) and hence a further counterexample to the long-standing but recently disproved [von Neumann conjecture](wikipedia://en/662256) for finitely-presented groups: it is known that *F* is not [elementary amenable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_amenable).

(Higman 1974) introduced an infinite family of finitely presented simple groups, including Thompson's group *V* as a special case.

## Presentations {#Presentations}

A finite [presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) of *F* is given by the following expression:

$$\langle A,B \mid\ [AB^{-1},A^{-1}BA] = [AB^{-1},A^{-2}BA^{2}] = \mathrm{id} \rangle$$

where \[*x*,*y*\] is the usual group theory [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193#Group_theory), *xyx*<sup>−1</sup>*y*<sup>−1</sup>.

Although *F* has a finite presentation with 2 generators and 2 relations, it is most easily and intuitively described by the infinite presentation:

$$\langle x_0, x_1, x_2, \dots\ \mid\ x_k^{-1} x_n x_k = x_{n+1}\ \mathrm{for}\ k<n \rangle.$$

The two presentations are related by *x*<sub>0</sub>=*A*, *x*<sub>*n*</sub> = *A*<sup>1−*n*</sup>*BA*<sup>*n*−1</sup> for *n*>0.

## Other representations {#Other_representations}

*Image: AA Tree Skew2.svg.* The Thompson group *F* is generated by operations like this on binary trees. Here *L* and *T* are nodes, but *A* *B* and *R* can be replaced by more general trees.

The group *F* also has realizations in terms of operations on ordered rooted [binary trees](wikipedia://en/4321), and as a subgroup of the piecewise linear [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) of the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) that preserve orientation and whose non-differentiable points are [dyadic rationals](wikipedia://en/56263) and whose slopes are all powers of 2.

The group *F* can also be considered as acting on the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) by identifying the two endpoints of the unit interval, and the group *T* is then the group of automorphisms of the unit circle obtained by adding the homeomorphism *x*→*x*+1/2 mod 1 to *F*. On binary trees this corresponds to exchanging the two trees below the root. The group *V* is obtained from *T* by adding the discontinuous map that fixes the points of the half-open interval \[0,1/2) and exchanges \[1/2,3/4) and \[3/4,1) in the obvious way. On binary trees this corresponds to exchanging the two trees below the right-hand descendant of the root (if it exists).

The Thompson group *F* is the group of order-preserving automorphisms of the free [Jónsson–Tarski algebra](wikipedia://en/43193940) on one generator.

## Amenability {#Amenability}

The conjecture of Thompson that *F* is not [amenable](wikipedia://en/657430) was further popularized by R. Geoghegan—see also the Cannon–Floyd–Parry article cited in the references below. Its current status is open: E. Shavgulidze[^o6] published a paper in 2009 in which he claimed to prove that *F* is amenable, but an error was found, as is explained in the [MR](wikipedia://en/1485646) review.

It is known that *F* is not [elementary amenable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_amenable), see Theorem 4.10 in Cannon–Floyd–Parry.

If *F* is **not** amenable, then it would be another counterexample to the now disproved [von Neumann conjecture](wikipedia://en/662256) for finitely-presented groups, which states that a finitely-presented group is amenable [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it does not contain a copy of the free group of rank 2.

## Connections with topology {#Connections_with_topology}

The group *F* was rediscovered at least twice by topologists during the 1970s. In a paper that was only published much later but was in circulation as a preprint at that time, [P. Freyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Freyd) and A. Heller [^o8] showed that the *shift map* on *F* induces an unsplittable [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) idempotent on the [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) *K(F,1)* and that this is universal in an interesting sense. This is explained in detail in Geoghegan's book (see references below). Independently, J. Dydak and P. Minc [^o9] created a less well-known model of *F* in connection with a problem in shape theory.

In 1979, R. Geoghegan made four conjectures about *F*: (1) *F* has [type **FP**<sub>∞</sub>](wikipedia://en/60316348); (2) All homotopy groups of *F* at infinity are trivial; (3) *F* has no non-abelian free subgroups; (4) *F* is non-amenable. (1) was proved by K. S. Brown and R. Geoghegan in a strong form: there is a K(F,1) with two cells in each positive dimension.[^o10] (2) was also proved by Brown and Geoghegan [^o11] in the sense that the cohomology H\*(F,ZF) was shown to be trivial; since a previous theorem of M. Mihalik [^o12] implies that *F* is simply connected at infinity, and the stated result implies that all homology at infinity vanishes, the claim about homotopy groups follows. (3) was proved by M. Brin and C. Squier.[^o13] The status of (4) is discussed above.

It is unknown if *F* satisfies the [Farrell–Jones conjecture](wikipedia://en/20643804). It is even unknown if the Whitehead group of *F* (see [Whitehead torsion](wikipedia://en/681186)) or the projective class group of *F* (see [Wall's finiteness obstruction](wikipedia://en/36722383)) is trivial, though it easily shown that *F* satisfies the strong Bass conjecture.

D. Farley [^o14] has shown that *F* acts as [deck transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deck_transformation) on a locally finite CAT(0) [cubical complex](wikipedia://en/54829672) (necessarily of infinite dimension). A consequence is that *F* satisfies the [Baum–Connes conjecture](wikipedia://en/11774498).
