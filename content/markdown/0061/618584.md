# Computational group theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Computational group theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **computational group theory** is the study of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) by means of computers. It is concerned with designing and analysing [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) and [data structures](wikipedia://en/8519) to compute information about groups. The subject has attracted interest because for many interesting groups (including most of the [sporadic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_groups)) it is impractical to perform calculations by hand.

:::

Important algorithms in computational group theory include:

- the [Schreier–Sims algorithm](wikipedia://en/893640) for finding the [order](wikipedia://en/493527) of a [permutation group](wikipedia://en/24634)
- the [Todd–Coxeter algorithm](wikipedia://en/894779) and [Knuth–Bendix algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knuth–Bendix_algorithm) for [coset enumeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coset_enumeration)
- the [product-replacement algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product-replacement_algorithm) for finding random elements of a group

Two important [computer algebra systems](wikipedia://en/54427) (CAS) used for group theory are [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_computer_algebra_system) and [Magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma_computer_algebra_system). Historically, other systems such as CAS (for [character theory](wikipedia://en/691741)) and [Cayley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley_computer_algebra_system) (a predecessor of Magma) were important.

Some achievements of the field include:

- complete enumeration of [all finite groups of order less than 2000](wikipedia://en/47756)
- computation of [representations](wikipedia://en/12695) for all the [sporadic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_groups)
