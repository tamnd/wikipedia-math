# Semicircle

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Semicircle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) (and more specifically [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446)), a **semicircle** is a one-dimensional [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of points that forms half of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220). It is a [circular arc](wikipedia://en/5578920) that measures 180° (equivalently, $\pi$ [radians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radians), or a [half-turn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_%28geometry%29#Subdivision_of_turns)). It only has one line of symmetry ([reflection symmetry](wikipedia://en/1390873)).

:::

In non-technical usage, the term "semicircle" is sometimes used to refer to either a [closed curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_curve) that also includes the diameter segment from one end of the arc to the other or to the half-[disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disc_%28geometry%29), which is a two-dimensional [geometric region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_%28mathematics%29) that further includes all the interior points.

By [Thales' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales'_theorem), any [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) [inscribed](wikipedia://en/4470203) in a semicircle with a [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) at each of the endpoints of the semicircle and the third vertex elsewhere on the semicircle is a [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420), with a [right angle](wikipedia://en/76956) at the third vertex.

All lines intersecting the semicircle [perpendicularly](wikipedia://en/76944) are [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157) at the center of the circle containing the given semicircle.

## Arithmetic and geometric means {#Arithmetic_and_geometric_means}

*Image: quadrature\_of\_rectangle.svg.* Construction of a square with the same area as a given oblong

A semicircle can be used to [construct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge_constructions) the [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/612) and [geometric](wikipedia://en/13046) means of two lengths using straight-edge and compass. For a semicircle with a diameter of *a* + *b*, the length of its [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) is the arithmetic mean of *a* and *b* (since the radius is half of the diameter).

The [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) can be found by dividing the diameter into two segments of lengths *a* and *b*, and then connecting their common endpoint to the semicircle with a segment perpendicular to the diameter. The length of the resulting segment is the geometric mean. This can be proven by applying the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034) to three similar right triangles, each having as vertices the point where the perpendicular touches the semicircle and two of the three endpoints of the segments of lengths *a* and *b*.[^o4]

The construction of the geometric mean can be used to transform any rectangle into a square of the same area, a problem called the [quadrature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrature_%28geometry%29) of a rectangle. The side length of the square is the geometric mean of the side lengths of the rectangle. More generally, it is used as a [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) in a general method for transforming any polygonal shape into a similar copy of itself with the area of any other given polygonal shape.[^o5]

## Farey diagram {#Farey_diagram}

*Image: Comparison\_Ford\_circles\_Farey\_diagram.svg.* Comparison of Ford circles and a Farey diagram with semicircles for *n* from 1 to 9. Each semicircle intersects its corresponding circles at right angles. In \[ the SVG image,\] hover over a circle or curve to highlight it and its terms.

The [Farey sequence](wikipedia://en/350164) of order *n* is the [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of completely reduced [fractions](wikipedia://en/1704824) which when [in lowest terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_lowest_terms) have [denominators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominator) less than or equal to *n*, arranged in order of increasing size. With a restricted definition, each Farey sequence starts with the value 0, denoted by the fraction $\tfrac{0}{1}$, and ends with the fraction $\tfrac{1}{1}$. [Ford circles](wikipedia://en/365503) can be constructed [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to their neighbours, and to the x-axis at these points. Semicircles joining adjacent points on the x-axis pass through the points of contact at right angles.[^o7]

## Equation {#Equation}

The equation of a semicircle with radius $r$ and midpoint $(x_0, y_0)$ on the diameter between its endpoints and which is entirely concave from below is

$$y = y_0 + \sqrt{r^2 - (x - x_0)^2}.$$

If it is entirely concave from above, the equation is

$$y = y_0 - \sqrt{r^2 - (x - x_0)^2}.$$

## Arbelos {#Arbelos}

*Image: Arbelos.svg.* An **arbelos** (grey region)

An [arbelos](wikipedia://en/887197) is a region in the [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) bounded by three semicircles connected at their endpoints, all on the same side of a [straight line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_line) (the *baseline*) that contains their [diameters](wikipedia://en/8007).
