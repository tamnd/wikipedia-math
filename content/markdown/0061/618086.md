# Interpretability logic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Interpretability logic"}

**Interpretability logics** comprise a family of [modal logics](wikipedia://en/333365) that extend [provability logic](wikipedia://en/618119) to describe [interpretability](wikipedia://en/621176) or various related metamathematical properties and relations such as [weak interpretability](wikipedia://en/655334), Π<sub>1</sub>-conservativity, [cointerpretability](wikipedia://en/621215), [tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolerance_%28in_logic%29), [cotolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotolerance), and arithmetic complexities.

:::

Main contributors to the field are Alessandro Berarducci, [Petr Hájek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Hájek), Konstantin Ignatiev, [Giorgi Japaridze](wikipedia://en/1866981), Franco Montagna, Vladimir Shavrukov, [Rineke Verbrugge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rineke_Verbrugge), Albert Visser, and Domenico Zambella.

## Examples {#Examples}

### Logic ILM {#Logic_ILM}

The language of ILM extends that of [classical propositional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_propositional_logic) by adding the unary modal operator $\Box$ and the binary modal operator $\triangleright$ (as usual, $\Diamond p$ is defined as $\neg \Box\neg p$). The arithmetical interpretation of $\Box p$ is “$p$ is provable in [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) (PA)”, and $p \triangleright q$ is understood as “$PA+q$ is interpretable in $PA+p$”.

[Axiom schemata](wikipedia://en/764613):

1. All classical tautologies
1. $$\Box(p \rightarrow q) \rightarrow (\Box p \rightarrow \Box q)$$
1. $$\Box(\Box p \rightarrow p) \rightarrow \Box p$$
1. $$\Box (p \rightarrow q) \rightarrow (p \triangleright q)$$
1. $$(p \triangleright q)\rightarrow (\Diamond p \rightarrow \Diamond q)$$
1. $$(p \triangleright q)\wedge (q \triangleright r)\rightarrow (p\triangleright r)$$
1. $$(p \triangleright r)\wedge (q \triangleright r)\rightarrow ((p\vee q)\triangleright r)$$
1. $$\Diamond p \triangleright p$$
1. $$(p \triangleright q)\rightarrow((p\wedge\Box r)\triangleright (q\wedge\Box r))$$

[Rules of inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules_of_inference):

1. “From $p$ and $p\rightarrow q$ conclude $q$”
1. “From $p$ conclude $\Box p$”.

The completeness of ILM with respect to its arithmetical interpretation was independently proven by Alessandro Berarducci and Vladimir Shavrukov.

### Logic TOL {#Logic_TOL}

The language of TOL extends that of classical propositional logic by adding the modal operator $\Diamond$ which is allowed to take any nonempty sequence of arguments. The arithmetical interpretation of $\Diamond( p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ is “$(PA+p_1,\ldots,PA+p_n)$ is a [tolerant sequence](wikipedia://en/621230) of theories”.

Axioms (with $p,q$ standing for any formulas, $\vec{r},\vec{s}$ for any sequences of formulas, and $\Diamond()$ identified with ⊤):

1. All classical tautologies
1. $$\Diamond (\vec{r},p,\vec{s})\rightarrow \Diamond (\vec{r}, p\wedge\neg q,\vec{s})\vee \Diamond (\vec{r}, q,\vec{s})$$
1. $$\Diamond (p)\rightarrow \Diamond (p\wedge \neg\Diamond (p))$$
1. $$\Diamond (\vec{r},p,\vec{s})\rightarrow \Diamond (\vec{r},\vec{s})$$
1. $$\Diamond (\vec{r},p,\vec{s})\rightarrow \Diamond (\vec{r},p,p,\vec{s})$$
1. $$\Diamond (p,\Diamond(\vec{r}))\rightarrow \Diamond (p\wedge\Diamond(\vec{r}))$$
1. $$\Diamond (\vec{r},\Diamond(\vec{s}))\rightarrow \Diamond (\vec{r},\vec{s})$$

Rules of inference:

1. “From $p$ and $p\rightarrow q$ conclude $q$”
1. “From $\neg p$ conclude $\neg \Diamond( p)$”.

The completeness of TOL with respect to its arithmetical interpretation was proven by [Giorgi Japaridze](wikipedia://en/1866981).
