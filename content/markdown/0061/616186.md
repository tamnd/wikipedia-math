# Ludwig Bieberbach

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ludwig Bieberbach"}

**Ludwig Georg Elias Moses Bieberbach** (; 4 December 1886 – 1 September 1982) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who achieved early success solving the first part of [Hilbert's eighteenth problem](wikipedia://en/2336224); he is best known for formulating the [Bieberbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieberbach_conjecture). He was also a leading representative of [National Socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) German mathematics ("[Deutsche Mathematik](wikipedia://en/31018614)").

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Ludwig Bieberbach was born 4 December 1886 in [Goddelau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddelau), near [Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt). His mother, Karoline Ludwig, was the daughter of Georg Ludwig, the director of the Heppenheim State Asylum. His father, Eberhard Sebastian Bieberbach (1848-1893), was a physician and later director of the same asylum.[^o3]

Bieberbach studied at [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg) and under [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), receiving his doctorate in 1910. His dissertation was titled *On the theory of automorphic functions* (Theorie der automorphen Funktionen). He began working as a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) in 1910, at first with [Ernst Zermelo](wikipedia://en/178655) at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich), and soon after with [Arthur Schoenflies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Moritz_Schoenflies) at [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg).

Bieberbach wrote a habilitation thesis in 1911 about [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) of Euclidean motions – identifying [conditions under which the group must have a translational subgroup whose vectors span the Euclidean space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_Graph_%28Geometry%29#Obtaining_periodicity) – that helped solve [Hilbert's 18th problem](wikipedia://en/2336224).[^o6]

Bieberbach was appointed Professor ordinarius at the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel) in 1913. He moved to the [University of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt) in 1915. In 1916 he formulated the [Bieberbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieberbach_conjecture), stating a necessary condition for a [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110) to map the open unit disc injectively into the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) in terms of the function's [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448). In 1984 [Louis de Branges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Branges) proved the conjecture (for this reason, the [Bieberbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieberbach_conjecture) is sometimes called [de Branges's theorem](wikipedia://en/252075)).[^o7]

He taught at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) from 1921–45. He worked on [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and its applications to other areas in mathematics. He is known for his work on dynamics in several complex variables, where he obtained results similar to [Fatou](wikipedia://en/629100)'s. There is also a Bieberbach theorem on [space groups](wikipedia://en/463721). In 1928 Bieberbach wrote a book with [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522) titled *Über die Minkowskische Reduktiontheorie der positiven quadratischen Formen*.

Bieberbach was a speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) held at Zurich in 1932.

## Politics {#Politics}

Bieberbach joined the [Sturmabteilung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturmabteilung) in 1933 and the [NSDAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSDAP) in 1937. He was enthusiastically involved in the efforts to dismiss his Jewish colleagues, including [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230) and his former coauthor [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522), from their posts. He also facilitated the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo) arrests of some close colleagues, such as [Juliusz Schauder](wikipedia://en/685314). Bieberbach was heavily influenced by [Theodore Vahlen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Vahlen), another German mathematician and anti-Semite, who along with Bieberbach founded the "[Deutsche Mathematik](wikipedia://en/31018614)" ("German mathematics") movement and journal of the same name. The purpose of the movement was to encourage and promote a "German" (in this case meaning [intuitionistic](wikipedia://en/19513)) style in mathematics. For example, Bieberbach claimed that "the [Cauchy–Goursat theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy–Goursat_theorem) arouses intolerable displeasure" in Germans, and was representative of an abstract style of reasoning and "pronounced shrewdness" characteristic of "Jewish mathematics".[^o9] Bieberbach's and Vahlen's idea of German mathematics was part of a wider trend in the scientific community in Nazi Germany towards giving the sciences racial character; there were also pseudoscientific movements for "[Deutsche Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Physik)", "[German chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_chemistry)", and "[German biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_biology)".

In 1945, Bieberbach was dismissed from all his academic positions because of his support of Nazism, but in 1949 was invited to lecture at the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel) by [Ostrowski](wikipedia://en/5827091), who considered Bieberbach's political views irrelevant to his contributions to mathematics.[^o10]
