# Athanasios Papoulis

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Athanasios Papoulis"}

**Athanasios Papoulis**[^o3] (Αθανάσιος Παπούλης; 1921 – April 25, 2002) was a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_American)-[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer) and applied [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Papoulis was born in modern day [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey) in 1921, and his family was moved to [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece) in 1922 as a consequence of the [Population exchange between Greece and Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_exchange_between_Greece_and_Turkey). He earned his undergraduate degree from [National Technical University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Technical_University_of_Athens). In 1945, he stowed away on a boat to escape the impending [Greek Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Civil_War) and settled in the United States. He studied under the supervision of [John Robert Kline](wikipedia://en/21005350) at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) and earned his Ph.D. in Mathematics in 1950. His dissertation was titled *On the Strong Differentiation of the Indefinite Integral.*

He married Caryl Engwall in New York, New York in 1953, and had five children: Irene, Helen, James, Ann, and Mary. In 1952, after teaching briefly at [Union College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_College), he became a faculty member at the Polytechnic Institute of Brooklyn (now [Polytechnic Institute of New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_Institute_of_New_York_University)), where he earned the distinction of University Professor.[^o6]

### Studies {#Studies}

Papoulis contributed in the areas of [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), [communications](wikipedia://en/14773), and [signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_theory) and [system theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_theory). His classic book *Probability, Random Variables, and Stochastic Processes*[^o8] is used as a textbook in many graduate-level probability courses in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) departments all over the world.

Two classic texts aimed at \[engineering\] practitioners were \[first\] published in 1965... \[One was\] Athanasios Papoulis' *Probability, Random Variables, and Stochastic Processes*... These books popularized a pedagogy that balanced rigor and intuition.[^o9] By staying away from complete mathematical rigor while emphasizing the physical and engineering interpretations of [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), Papoulis's book gained wide popularity.

### Theory {#Theory}

Athanasios Papoulis specialized in engineering mathematics, his work covers probability, statistics, and estimation in the application of these fields to modern engineering problems. Papoulis also taught and developed subjects such as [stochastic simulation](wikipedia://en/7210212), [mean square estimation](wikipedia://en/27590), likelihood tests, [maximum entropy methods](wikipedia://en/201718), [Monte Carlo method](wikipedia://en/56098), [spectral representations](wikipedia://en/8603) and estimation, [sampling theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_sampling), [bispectrum](wikipedia://en/2496000) and system identification, [cyclostationary processes](wikipedia://en/3283910), deterministic signals in noise (part of [deterministic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic_system_%28mathematics%29) and [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) studies), [wave optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_optics) and the [Wiener](wikipedia://en/1216721) and [Kalman filters](wikipedia://en/180855).

#### Contributions {#Contributions}

- Papoulis's generalization of the sampling theorem[^o12] unified many variations of the [Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem](wikipedia://en/37864) into one theorem.[^o13][^o14]
- The [Papoulis–Gerchberg algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papoulis–Gerchberg_algorithm)[^o15][^o16][^o17] is an iterative signal restoration algorithm that has found widespread use in signal and image processing.[^o18][^o19]
- "Papoulis's eloquent proof"[^o20] of the conventional [sampling theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_theorem)[^o21] requires only two equations.
