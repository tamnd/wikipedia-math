# Alfréd Rényi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alfréd Rényi"}

Rényi Alfréd

**Alfréd Rényi** (20 March 1921 – 1 February 1970) was a Hungarian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his work in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), though he also made contributions in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Rényi was born in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest) to Artúr Rényi and Borbála Alexander; his father was a mechanical engineer, while his mother was the daughter of philosopher and literary critic [Bernhard Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhard_Alexander); his uncle was [Franz Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Alexander), a Hungarian-American psychoanalyst and physician.[^n1]

He was prevented from enrolling in university in 1939 due to the anti-Jewish laws then in force, but enrolled at the [University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) in 1940 and finished his studies in 1944. At this point, he was drafted to [forced labour service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_service_%28Hungary%29), from which he managed to escape during transportation of his company. He was in hiding with false documents for six months. Biographers tell an incredible story about Rényi: after half of a year in hiding, he managed to get hold of a soldier's uniform and march his parents out of the [Budapest Ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Ghetto), where they were captive. That mission required enormous courage and planning skills.[^n1]

Rényi then completed his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in 1947 at the [University of Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged), under the advisement of [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126). He did his postgraduate in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) and [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Petersburg), where he collaborated with a prominent Soviet mathematician [Yuri Linnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Linnik).[^n1]

Rényi married Katalin Schulhof (who used Kató Rényi as her married name), herself a mathematician, in 1946; their daughter Zsuzsanna was born in 1948. After a brief assistant professorship at Budapest, he was appointed Professor Extraordinary at the [University of Debrecen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Debrecen) in 1949. In 1950, he founded the [Mathematics Research Institute](wikipedia://en/31714670) of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences), now bearing his name, and directed it until his early death. He also headed the Department of Probability and Mathematical Statistics of the [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University), from 1952. He was elected a corresponding member (1949), then full member (1956), of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^n1]

## Work {#Work}

Rényi proved, using the [large sieve](wikipedia://en/1967859), that there is a number $K$ such that every even number is the sum of a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) and a number that can be written as the product of at most $K$ primes.[^o12] [Chen's theorem](wikipedia://en/1400840), a strengthening of this result, shows that the theorem is true for *K* = 2, for all sufficiently large even numbers. The case *K* = 1 is the still-unproven [Goldbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldbach_conjecture).

In [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), he introduced the spectrum of [Rényi entropies](wikipedia://en/1731689) of order *α*, giving an important generalisation of the [Shannon entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_entropy) and the [Kullback–Leibler divergence](wikipedia://en/1115052). The Rényi entropies give a spectrum of useful [diversity indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversity_indices), and lead to a spectrum of [fractal dimensions](wikipedia://en/285907). The [Rényi–Ulam game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rényi–Ulam_game) is a guessing game where some of the answers may be wrong.

In probability theory, he is also known for his [parking constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rényi's_parking_constants), which characterize the solution to the following problem: given a street of some length and cars of unit length parking on a random free position on the street, what is the mean density of cars when there are no more free positions? The solution to that problem is asymptotically equal to 0.7475979 .[^o13] Thus, random parking is 25.2% less efficient than optimal packing.

He wrote 32 joint papers with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284),[^o14] the most well-known of which are his papers introducing the [Erdős–Rényi model](wikipedia://en/10477221) of [random graphs](wikipedia://en/358477).[^o15]

The corpus of his bibliography was compiled by the mathematician [Pál Medgyessy](wikipedia://en/69590748).[^o16]

## Quotations {#Quotations}

Rényi, who was addicted to coffee, is the source of the quote:[^o18][^o19] "A mathematician is a device for turning coffee into theorems", which is often ascribed to Erdős. It has been suggested that this sentence was originally formulated in German, where it can be interpreted as a [double entendre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_entendre) on the meaning of the word *Satz* (theorem or coffee residue), but it is more likely that the original formulation was in Hungarian.[^o20]

He is also famous for having said, "If I feel unhappy, I do mathematics to become happy. If I am happy, I do mathematics to keep happy."[^o21]

## Remembrance {#Remembrance}

The [Alfréd Rényi Prize](wikipedia://en/26466805), awarded by the [Hungarian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Science), was established in his honor.[^o23]

In 1950 Rényi founded the [Mathematics Research Institute](wikipedia://en/31714670) of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences). It was renamed the [Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31714670) in July 1999.
