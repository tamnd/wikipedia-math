# DES-X

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="DES-X"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **DES-X** (or **DESX**) is a variant on the [DES](wikipedia://en/7978) (Data Encryption Standard) [symmetric-key](wikipedia://en/53042) [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) intended to increase the complexity of a [brute-force attack](wikipedia://en/53784). The technique used to increase the complexity is called *[key whitening](wikipedia://en/2990178)*.

:::

The original DES algorithm was specified in 1976 with a 56-bit [key size](wikipedia://en/5749): 2<sup>56</sup> possibilities for the [key](wikipedia://en/53039). There was criticism that an exhaustive search might be within the capabilities of large governments, particularly the United States' [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) (NSA). One scheme to increase the key size of DES without substantially altering the algorithm was DES-X, proposed by [Ron Rivest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Rivest) in May 1984.

The algorithm has been included in [RSA Security](wikipedia://en/301240)'s [BSAFE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_BSAFE) cryptographic library since the late 1980s.

DES-X augments DES by [XORing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XOR) an extra 64 bits of key (K<sub>1</sub>) to the [plaintext](wikipedia://en/157935) *before* applying DES, and then XORing another 64 bits of key (K<sub>2</sub>) *after* the encryption:

$$\mbox{DES-X}(M) = K_2 \oplus \mbox{DES}_K(M \oplus K_1)$$

*Image: Xor Encrypt Xor.svg.*

The key size is thereby increased to 56 + (2 × 64) = 184 bits.

However, the effective key size (security) is only increased to 56+64−1−*lb(M)* = 119 − *lb(M)* = ~119 bits, where *M* is the number of [chosen plaintext/ciphertext pairs](wikipedia://en/63852) the adversary can obtain, and *lb* denotes the [binary logarithm](wikipedia://en/249992). Moreover, effective key size drops to 88 bits given 2<sup>\32.5</sup> known plaintext and using advanced slide attack.

DES-X also increases the strength of DES against [differential cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/8536) and [linear cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/59469), although the improvement is much smaller than in the case of brute force attacks. It is estimated that differential [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715) would require 2<sup>61</sup> chosen plaintexts (vs. 2<sup>47</sup> for DES), while linear cryptanalysis would require 2<sup>60</sup> known plaintexts (vs. 2<sup>43</sup> for DES or 2<sup>61</sup> for DES with independent subkeys.[^o2]) Note that with 2<sup>64</sup> plaintexts (known or chosen being the same in this case), DES (or indeed any other [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) with a 64 bit [block size](wikipedia://en/294065)) is totally broken as the whole cipher's codebook becomes available.

Although the differential and linear attacks, currently best attack on DES-X is a known-plaintext slide attack discovered by Biryukov-Wagner [^o3] which has complexity of 2<sup>\32.5</sup> known plaintexts and 2<sup>\87.5</sup> time of analysis. Moreover the attack is easily converted into a ciphertext-only attack with the same data complexity and 2<sup>95</sup> offline time complexity.
