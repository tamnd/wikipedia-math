# Hendecagon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hendecagon"}

Regular polygon stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **hendecagon** (also **undecagon**[^o2][^o7] or **endecagon**[^o3]) or 11-gon is an eleven-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621). (The name *hendecagon*, from Greek *hendeka* "eleven" and *–gon* "corner", is often preferred to the hybrid *undecagon*, whose first part is formed from Latin *undecim* "eleven".[^o4])

:::

## Regular hendecagon {#Regular_hendecagon}

A *[regular](wikipedia://en/333306) hendecagon* is represented by [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {11}.

A regular hendecagon has [internal angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) of 147.27 [degrees](wikipedia://en/1195294) (=147 $\tfrac{3}{11}$ degrees).[^o6] The area of a regular hendecagon with side length *a* is given by[^o7]

$$A = \frac{11}{4}a^2 \cot \frac{\pi}{11} \simeq 9.36564\,a^2.$$

As 11 is not a [Fermat prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime), the regular hendecagon is not [constructible](wikipedia://en/523430) with [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge).[^o8] Because 11 is not a [Pierpont prime](wikipedia://en/2595060), construction of a regular hendecagon is still impossible [even with the usage of an angle trisector](wikipedia://en/91111).

Close approximations to the regular hendecagon can be constructed. For instance, the ancient Greek mathematicians approximated the side length of a hendecagon inscribed in a [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) as being 14/25 units long.[^o9]

The hendecagon can be constructed exactly via [neusis construction](wikipedia://en/3752339)[^o10] and also via two-fold origami.[^o11]

## Approximate construction {#Approximate_construction}

The following construction description is given by T. Drummond from 1800:[^o13]

Draw the radius **A B**, bisect it in **C**—with an opening of the compasses equal to half the radius, upon **A** and **C** as centres describe the arcs **C D I** and **A D**—with the distance **I D** upon **I** describe the arc **D O** and draw the line **C O**, which will be the extent of one side of a hendecagon sufficiently exact for practice.

On a unit circle:

- Constructed hendecagon side length $b=0.563692\ldots$
- Theoretical hendecagon side length $a=2\sin(\frac{\pi}{11})=0.563465\ldots$
- Absolute error $\delta=b-a=2.27\ldots\cdot10^{-4}$ – if $\overline{\operatorname{AB}}$ is 10 m then this error is approximately 2.3 mm.

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Symmetries of hendecagon.png.* Symmetries of a regular hendecagon. Vertices are colored by their symmetry positions. Blue mirror lines are drawn through vertices and edge. Gyration orders are given in the center.

The *regular hendecagon* has [Dih<sub>11</sub> symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry), order 22. Since 11 is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) there is one subgroup with dihedral symmetry: Dih<sub>1</sub>, and 2 [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) symmetries: Z<sub>11</sub>, and Z<sub>1</sub>.

These 4 symmetries can be seen in 4 distinct symmetries on the hendecagon. [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) labels these by a letter and group order.[^o16] Full symmetry of the regular form is **r22** and no symmetry is labeled **a1**. The dihedral symmetries are divided depending on whether they pass through vertices (**d** for diagonal) or edges (**p** for perpendiculars), and **i** when reflection lines path through both edges and vertices. Cyclic symmetries in the middle column are labeled as **g** for their central gyration orders.

Each subgroup symmetry allows one or more degrees of freedom for irregular forms. Only the **g11** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

:::

## Use in coinage {#Use_in_coinage}

The [Canadian dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_dollar) coin, the [loonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loonie), is similar to, but not exactly, a regular [hendecagonal prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendecagonal_prism),[^o18] as are the Indian 2-[rupee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rupee) coin[^o19] and several other lesser-used coins of other nations.[^o20] The cross-section of a loonie is actually a [Reuleaux hendecagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuleaux_hendecagon). The United States [Susan B. Anthony dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_B._Anthony_dollar) has a hendecagonal outline along the inside of its edges.[^n1]
