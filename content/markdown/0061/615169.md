# TI-84 Plus series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="TI-84 Plus series"}

The **TI-84 Plus series** is a line of [graphing calculators](wikipedia://en/335004) produced by [Texas Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Instruments). They are ubiquitous in the [American education system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_United_States), and have historically been considered the *de facto* standard in [grade school math](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_school_mathematics). The series has also amassed a significant [hobbyist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobbyist) following, along with a large library of community-developed applications, for its support of [assembly language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language) and [TI-BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TI-BASIC) programs.

:::

The family was superseded by the [TI-84 Plus CE](wikipedia://en/80950234) series in 2015; of the original series, only the TI-84 Plus is still in production, the Silver Edition and C Silver Edition having been discontinued that same year. However, they remain popular in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), with the TI-84 Plus being issued by schools more than 20 years after its introduction.

Beginning from around 2011, the series has received media attention from technology publications due to hobbyist app releases for the platform, such as an unofficial [ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT) client, a [web browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_browser), and a [neural network](wikipedia://en/21523). In recent years, however, the TI-84 Plus has been increasingly criticized for its antiquated technology and poor [value for money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_%28economics%29), with critics claiming that its continued sale is a result of Texas Instruments' calculator [monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly).

## Editions {#Editions}

### TI-84 Plus {#TI-84_Plus}

The original TI-84 Plus was released in 2004 as an upgrade to the [TI-83 Plus](wikipedia://en/439710#TI-83_Plus).[^o5] Its keyboard layout and user interface are identical to that of its predecessor, and it is [backwards compatible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backward_compatibility) with older TI-BASIC programs.[^o6] Despite this, it has significantly improved hardware; its CPU, the [Zilog Z80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zilog_Z80), is 2.5 times as fast as the one in the TI-83 Plus, its display is of a higher [contrast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrast_%28vision%29), and it has three times as much [flash memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_memory).[^o5][^n1][^o7]

The TI-84 Plus introduced a [real-time clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_clock) and a [mini-USB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB-B) data transfer port.[^o8] The USB port is [USB On-The-Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB_On-The-Go) compliant, allowing the TI-84 Plus to connect to—and exchange data with—another calculator; Texas Instruments calls this feature Communication Link.[^o9] The TI-84 Plus can also connect to a computer to transfer programs and files using Texas Instruments' proprietary program, TI-Connect.[^o10]

An all-white variant of the TI-84 Plus was introduced in 2023.[^o11] Currently, the calculator is still in production and issued by schools around the United States, required by some institutions, and seen as the standard option by many; critics argue that the enduring prevalence of the TI-84 Plus—which is considerably less powerful than a smartphone, yet still costs more than some—has hindered innovation in calculator technology and is a result of [monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly). They also claim that Texas Instruments exploits this by [keeping prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_gouging) at a level roughly twice that of competing calculators.[^o12][^o13][^o14]

::: {#o16 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="TI-84 Plus Silver Edition"}

### TI-84 Plus Silver Edition {#TI-84_Plus_Silver_Edition}

*Image: TI 84 Plus Silver Edition.svg.* TI-84 Plus Silver Edition

The TI-84 Plus Silver Edition (SE) was introduced on January 7, 2004 as an upgrade to the [TI-83 Plus Silver Edition](wikipedia://en/439710#TI-83_Plus_Silver_Edition). It features 24 KB of user-available RAM; the calculator technically has 128 KB—48 KB in newer revisions—but the operating system was never updated to utilize it. It has 1.5 MB of user-accessible ROM, more than three times the 480 KB in the TI-84 Plus. It came preloaded with 30 applications.[^o17]

The Silver Edition has interchangeable faceplates, and new, colored faceplates were available for purchase at Texas Instruments' online store.[^o18] It was discontinued in 2015, making the TI-84 Plus the only currently produced calculator in the series.[^o19]

:::

::: {#o21 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="TI-84 Plus C Silver Edition"}

### TI-84 Plus C Silver Edition {#TI-84_Plus_C_Silver_Edition}

*Image: The proposed Ti-84+ C Edition SE.jpg.* TI 84 Plus C Silver Edition

The TI-84 Plus C Silver Edition (C SE) was released in 2013 as the first Z80-based Texas Instruments graphing calculator with a color screen.[^o22]

It introduced a 320-by-240 pixel color screen, a modified version of the TI-84 Plus's 2.55MP operating system, and a removable 1200 mAh rechargeable lithium-ion battery.[^o23][^o22] It was praised for its high-resolution color screen, which added new capabilities such as the ability to graph multiple simultaneous functions in different colors.[^o24] The C Silver Edition would not be Texas Instruments' last foray into color-screen calculators for the student market; the TI-84 Plus CE was introduced—with an upgraded CPU—two years later.[^o25] The C Silver Edition was discontinued a few months after the launch of the CE.[^o19]

:::

## Software {#Software}

### Programs and applications {#Programs_and_applications}

The TI-84 Plus series supports both programs and applications. Programs are written by the user; some programs, like those written in TI-BASIC, are [interpreted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpreted_%28programming_languages%29) during execution and can thus be edited directly on the calculator.[^o28][^o29] Applications (also called *Flash applications*) are stored in the calculator's ROM; these tend to be more complex than standard programs, and are usually from Texas Instruments themselves.[^o30] Officially, two [programming languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_languages) are supported: TI-BASIC and [Z80 assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language).[^o9] However, there are unofficial, community-developed languages—such as the *Axe Parser*—made specifically for the TI-84 Plus series.[^o31]

The series has a significant [hobbyist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobbyist) following, and a wide variety of official and community-made software has been developed for it over the years.[^o32] These include video games, math programs, educational tools, and graphing software.[^o33] In 2011, an unofficial [web browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_browser), *Gossamer*, was released for the platform,[^o34] and in recent years, programmers have created internet-based programs like a [ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT) client, graphical [ray-casting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray-casting) software, and a [neural network](wikipedia://en/21523) that incorporates primitive [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence).[^o35][^o36][^o37] These developments have attracted the attention of technology publications.[^o38][^o39][^o40]

### Operating systems {#Operating_systems}

There have been eight [operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system) releases for the monochrome-display calculators in the series, which include the TI-84 Plus, the Silver Edition, and their respective variants.[^o8][^o7][^o17] There have been two releases for the C Silver Edition.[^o42][^o22] TI-84 Plus series calculators can run custom [user interface shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_%28computing%29), such as *MirageOS* and *Doors CS*.[^o43] In addition, calculators with an older [bootloader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bootloader) can run custom operating systems like *KnightOS*.[^o44][^o45]

### Linking software {#Linking_software}

Texas Instruments develops the TI-Connect linking software, which hosts a number of sub-programs (called *software tools*) to manage TI-84 Plus series calculators.[^o10][^o55]

::: {#o56 .table cue=table}

| Tool | Function |
|---|---|
| ScreenCapture | Capture and save a still of a calculator's screen. |
| DeviceInformation | Generate an information report of a calculator, which includes details such as its model, available storage, and installed applications. |
| DataEditor | Modify variables on a calculator, such as numbers, lists, and [matrixes](wikipedia://en/20556859). |
| DeviceExplorer | Copy files to and from a calculator, install programs and applications, and create file groups. |
| OS Downloader | Upgrade or downgrade the operating system on a calculator. |
| Backup/Restore | Create and restore complete calculator backups, including RAM, applications, and flash memory. |

:::

Texas Instruments' newer program for the [TI-84 Plus CE series](wikipedia://en/80950234) calculators, TI-Connect CE, is [backwards-compatible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backward_compatibility) with the TI-84 Plus series; it can be used in place of TI-Connect.[^o57] TI-Connect is compatible with [Windows XP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_XP) and above,[^o10] while TI-Connect CE is compatible with [Windows 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_10) and above.[^o57]

There are multiple third-party linking programs compatible with the TI-84 Plus series; most were developed for [archaic platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsolescence#Technical_obsolescence) like the [Commodore Amiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiga), the [Atari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_8-bit_computers), and [MS-DOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS-DOS), and cannot be run on modern computers.[^o58][^o59] However, one of these programs—known as *TiLP*—is more recent and supports [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows), [macOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS), and [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux).[^o60]

## Equipment {#Equipment}

Texas Instruments has released a variety of equipment for its graphing calculators. Even though only the Calculator-Based Ranger 2 features a USB port, the TI-84 Plus series can connect to older Texas Instruments equipment using its serial link port.[^o7] Since the newer [TI-84 Plus CE series](wikipedia://en/80950234) does not have this port, it is not compatible with most of this equipment.[^o25]

### ViewScreen {#ViewScreen}

The ViewScreen is an LCD panel and [projection system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projector) that mirrors the display of a calculator; it was first introduced in 1993, and there have been several models since. Unlike most other equipment, it does not use the link port. However, there is a dongle—the *Presentation Link Adapter*—that allows a standard TI-84 Plus to connect to a ViewScreen panel.[^o67]

### Presenter {#Presenter}

The Presenter, introduced in 2003, is an alternative piece of calculator screen display equipment; instead of using a proprietary display panel and overhead projection system like the ViewScreen, the Presenter connects to an existing display device—such as a television or [interactive whiteboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interactive_whiteboard)—using either an [RF modulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RF_modulator) or [composite video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_video) input. Since it uses the same [communication protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_protocol) as the ViewScreen, it is compatible with both the Presentation Link Adapter and ViewScreen variants of the TI-84 Plus.[^o69]

### Calculator-Based Laboratory {#Calculator-Based_Laboratory}

The Calculator-Based Laboratory (CBL), a general purpose [data acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_acquisition) unit, was introduced in 1994. It collects information from several types of [modular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_design) sensors by [Vernier, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernier_Science_Education)—at a speed of up to 10,000 data points per second—and sends it to a calculator via link cable, which records and graphs the data. It also features a display and basic firmware of its own for standalone data processing.[^o71] In 1999, the CBL was superseded by the CBL 2, which lacked a display for standalone use but introduced flash memory and increased the single-channel sample rate to 50,000 data points per second.[^o72]

### Calculator-Based Ranger {#Calculator-Based_Ranger}

The Calculator-Based Ranger (CBR), introduced in 1997, is, like the CBL, a data acquisition unit, but features a single built-in sonic sensor instead of multiple unique modular ones. It measures distances by emitting and receiving an [ultrasonic pulse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasound), and then calculating the distance traveled by performing a [speed-of-sound](wikipedia://en/147853) calculation using its [microprocessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microprocessor). It then stores this information in a list—which can contain up to 512 data points—and transfers that to a calculator via link cable.[^o74] The CBR was replaced by the CBR 2 in 2004, which added a USB port for faster data transfer with the TI-84 Plus series.[^o75]

### Orion Talking Graphing Calculator {#Orion_Talking_Graphing_Calculator}

The Orion TI-84 Plus Talking Graphing Calculator is a detachable accessory for the TI-84 Plus produced by Texas Instruments in collaboration with [Orbit Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_Research) and the [American Printing House for the Blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Printing_House_for_the_Blind). It adds general accessibility features—such as speech navigation—to the TI-84 Plus, and is able to read out the contents of the calculator's display using [speech synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_synthesis). It uses its own power source, a rechargeable battery, instead of drawing power from the calculator itself.[^o5][^o77]

## Variants {#Variants}

::: {#o81 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="EZ-Spot"}

### TI-84 Plus EZ-Spot {#TI-84_Plus_EZ-Spot}

The TI-84 Plus EZ-Spot series, also known as the School editions and the School Property editions, are variants of TI-84 Plus series calculators produced by Texas Instruments and only made available for schools to purchase.[^o82] They have the words "School Property" emblazoned on their faceplate and are accented yellow; this easily distinguishable design was chosen in an effort to prevent theft.[^o83] The TI-84 Plus Silver Edition and C Silver Edition also have EZ-Spot variants.[^o84]

:::

::: {#o86 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="TI-84 Plus Silver Edition VSC"}

### TI-84 Plus Silver Edition VSC {#TI-84_Plus_Silver_Edition_VSC}

The TI-84 Plus Silver Edition ViewScreen Calculator, also known as the TI-84 Plus Silver Edition VSC, is a variant of the TI-84 Plus Silver Edition intended for educational use.[^o87] It has a display output that allows it to connect to a [ViewScreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ViewScreen).[^o88]

:::

::: {#o90 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="TI-84 Plus T"}

### TI-84 Plus T {#TI-84_Plus_T}

The TI-84 Plus T is a variant of the TI-84 Plus; it was introduced in 2015 by Texas Instruments, exclusive to the Netherlands. This variant is similar to the rest of the series, but features an LED to indicate whether or not the calculator is in [press-to-test mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exam_mode). The hardware of the TI-84 Plus T is similar to the TI-84 Plus Silver Edition, but the TI-84 Plus T is no longer able to run the [assembler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language).[^o91]

:::

### TI-84 Pocket.fr and Plus Pocket SE {#TI-84_Pocket.fr_and_Plus_Pocket_SE}

The TI-84 Pocket.fr was introduced in 2011 as a miniaturized variant of the TI-84 Plus for the French market.[^o93] It was the first region-specific calculator in the TI-84 Plus series.[^n2] A year later, the TI-84 Plus Pocket SE was introduced as a miniaturized variant of the TI-84 Plus Silver Edition for the Asian market.[^o94]

:::
