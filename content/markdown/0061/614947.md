# Yousef Alavi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Yousef Alavi"}

**Yousef Alavi** (March 19, 1928 – May 21, 2013)[^o3] was an Iranian-American mathematician, based at [Western Michigan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Michigan_University) (WMU), who specialized in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) from [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University) in 1958, and his career at WMU lasted until 1996. Alavi was a frequent collaborator with [Don R. Lick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_R._Lick) and [Jiuqiang Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuqiang_Liu) (both of [Eastern Michigan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Michigan_University)) as well as with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), and in 1987, the Michigan Section of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA) awarded him its first annual Distinguished Service Award.

:::

## Early life and education (1928–1958) {#Early_life_and_education_(1928–1958)}

Alavi was born in the city of [Ahvaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahvaz) in [Pahlavi Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_Iran) in 1928.[^o3] He moved to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in 1948.[^o6] There, he earned a bachelor's degree in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) from [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University) in 1953, a master's in math from the same school in 1955, and a PhD in 1958.[^o6]

## Career (1958–1996) {#Career_(1958–1996)}

He was a professor of mathematics at WMU from 1958 until his retirement in 1996;[^o8] he chaired the department from 1989 to 1992.[^o9]

Alavi was known for complaining that "this is highly irregular!" He was also a frequent host for Erdős on his visits to Western Michigan. On one of these visits, these two things came together: he made his usual complaint at a time when Erdős and [Ronald Graham](wikipedia://en/240694) were present. This sparked a discussion on what it might mean for a graph to be highly irregular, kicking off a line of joint research on [highly irregular graphs](wikipedia://en/42651881) through which Alavi obtained [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) one.[^o10]

In 1987, he received the first Distinguished Service Award of the Michigan Section of the MAA due to his 30 years of service to the section; at that time, the Michigan House and Senate issued a special resolution honoring him.[^o11]

### Research {#Research}

In 1987, Alavi, Erdős, [Paresh J. Malde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paresh_J._Malde), and [Allen J. Schwenk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_J._Schwenk) studied the [vertex independence sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_independence_sequence) $a_1, \dots, a_m$ of an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) where *m* is the maximal order of any [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501), where $a_i$ denotes the number of independent sets on exactly *i* vertices.[^o13] By taking the [join](wikipedia://en/81278692) of several [complete graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_graphs), they were able to show that this sequence can realize any possible [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027).[^o13]

That same year, Alavi, Erdős, [A. J. Boals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._J._Boals), [Gary Chartrand](wikipedia://en/22782409), and [Ortrud Oellermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortrud_Oellermann) also introduced the notion of an [ascending subgraph decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascending_subgraph_decomposition), where a graph is decomposed into a sequence of [subgraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgraph_%28graph_theory%29) such that each of those graphs is isomorphic to a subgraph of the next graph. They conjectured that every finite graph admits an ascending subgraph decomposition, and verified it for the cases of all [paths](wikipedia://en/638889), all [cycles](wikipedia://en/168609), and all graphs with at most 6 vertices.[^o14]

In 1991, Alavi, Erdős, [Mehdi Behzad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Behzad), and [Don R. Lick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_R._Lick) introduced the notion of the [double vertex graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_vertex_graph) of any graph.[^o15] Further research involving Alavi, Lick, and [Jiuqiang Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuqiang_Liu) in 1993 and 1994 eventually led to a classification of which [Hamiltonian graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graphs) also have Hamiltonian double vertex graphs.[^o15] In particular, the double vertex graph of the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) *K(m, n)* is not Hamiltonian unless the equation $(m - n)^2 = m + n$ holds.[^o15]

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

Before his death, Alavi had been dedicated to his wife and son, both of whom ended up surviving him.[^o17] He was remembered for possessing "great energy", "kindness", and "good humor".[^o17]

Alavi died on May 21, 2013;[^o18] his health had been worsening for several months prior.[^o17] A private funeral was held for him at the [Riverside Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_Cemetery_%28Michigan%29) at noon on May 23,[^o3] and a memorial service was held for him on June 29 in the [Kanley Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanley_Chapel).[^o6]

In remembrance of Alavi, the "Yousef Alavi Memorial Symposium" was held from October 24 to 25, 2013, on the sixth floor of the [Everett Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everett_Tower) at WMU, as well as the [Fetzer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetzer_Center). It was free and open to the public.[^o19] Speakers included [Fan Chung](wikipedia://en/246562) and [Ronald Graham](wikipedia://en/240694), both of the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego).[^o19]
