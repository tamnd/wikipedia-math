# Geometric Folding Algorithms

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Geometric Folding Algorithms"}

***Geometric Folding Algorithms: Linkages, Origami, Polyhedra*** is a [monograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph) on the mathematics and [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927) of [mechanical linkages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linkage_%28mechanical%29), [paper folding](wikipedia://en/232840), and [polyhedral nets](wikipedia://en/1148129), by [Erik Demaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Demaine) and [Joseph O'Rourke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_O'Rourke_%28professor%29). It was published in 2007 by [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press) (). A Japanese-language translation by Ryuhei Uehara was published in 2009 by the Modern Science Company ().

:::

## Audience {#Audience}

Although aimed at computer science and mathematics students, much of the book is accessible to a broader audience of mathematically-sophisticated readers with some background in high-school level geometry. Mathematical origami expert [Tom Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hull_%28mathematician%29) has called it "a must-read for anyone interested in the field of computational origami". It is a monograph rather than a textbook, and in particular does not include sets of exercises.

The Basic Library List Committee of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) has recommended this book for inclusion in undergraduate mathematics libraries.

## Topics and organization {#Topics_and_organization}

The book is organized into three sections, on linkages, origami, and polyhedra.

Topics in the section on linkages include the [Peaucellier–Lipkin linkage](wikipedia://en/2163562) for converting rotary motion into linear motion, [Kempe's universality theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kempe's_universality_theorem) that any [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) can be traced out by a linkage, the existence of linkages for [angle trisection](wikipedia://en/91111), and the [carpenter's rule problem](wikipedia://en/3269639) on straightening two-dimensional [polygonal chains](wikipedia://en/12056032). This part of the book also includes applications to [motion planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_planning) for [robotic arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotic_arm), and to [protein folding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_folding).

The second section of the book concerns the [mathematics of paper folding](wikipedia://en/232840), and mathematical [origami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origami). It includes the [NP-completeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) of testing flat foldability, the problem of [map folding](wikipedia://en/26185707) (determining whether a pattern of mountain and valley folds forming a square grid can be folded flat), the work of [Robert J. Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_J._Lang) using tree structures and [circle packing](wikipedia://en/16879665) to automate the design of origami folding patterns, the [fold-and-cut theorem](wikipedia://en/27455341) according to which any polygon can be constructed by folding a piece of paper and then making a single straight cut, origami-based angle trisection, [rigid origami](wikipedia://en/20332029), and the work of [David A. Huffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_A._Huffman) on curved folds.

In the third section, on [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra), the topics include [polyhedral nets](wikipedia://en/1148129) and Dürer's conjecture on their existence for convex polyhedra, the sets of polyhedra that have a given polygon as their net, [Steinitz's theorem](wikipedia://en/19762817) characterizing the graphs of polyhedra, [Cauchy's theorem](wikipedia://en/8317687) that every polyhedron, considered as a linkage of flat polygons, is rigid, and [Alexandrov's uniqueness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandrov's_uniqueness_theorem) stating that the three-dimensional shape of a convex polyhedron is uniquely determined by the [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) of [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) on its surface.

The book concludes with a more speculative chapter on higher-dimensional generalizations of the problems it discusses.
