# Reeb graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reeb graph"}

*Image: 3D-Leveltorus-Reebgraph.png.* Reeb graph of the height function on the torus.

A **Reeb graph**[^o2] (named after [Georges Reeb](wikipedia://en/20126484) by [René Thom](wikipedia://en/61633)) is a [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) object reflecting the evolution of the [level sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_sets) of a real-valued [function](wikipedia://en/185427) on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219).[^o3] A similar concept was introduced by [G.M. Adelson-Velskii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgy_Adelson-Velsky) and [A.S. Kronrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Kronrod) and applied to analysis of [Hilbert's thirteenth problem](wikipedia://en/2336190).[^o4][^o5] Proposed by G. Reeb as a tool in [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560),[^o6] Reeb graphs are the natural tool to study multivalued functional relationships between 2D scalar fields $\psi$, $\lambda$, and $\phi$ arising from the conditions $\nabla \psi = \lambda \nabla \phi$ and $\lambda \neq 0$, because these relationships are single-valued when restricted to a region associated with an individual edge of the Reeb graph. This general principle was first used to study [neutral surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_density#Spatial_dependence) in [oceanography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanography).[^o7]

:::

Reeb graphs have also found a wide variety of applications in [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927) and [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics),[^o2][^o8] including [computer aided geometric design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_aided_geometric_design), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954)-based [shape matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_matching),[^o9][^o10][^o11] [topological data analysis](wikipedia://en/17740009),[^o12] topological simplification and cleaning, surface segmentation [^o13] and parametrization, efficient computation of level sets, [neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience),[^o14] and geometrical [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/29952).[^o4] In a special case of a function on a flat space (technically a simply connected domain), the Reeb graph forms a [polytree](wikipedia://en/1731488) and is also called a **[contour tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contour_tree)**.[^o15]

Level set graphs help [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577) related to estimating [probability density functions](wikipedia://en/43487) and [regression](wikipedia://en/826997) functions, and they can be used in [cluster analysis](wikipedia://en/669675) and function [optimization](wikipedia://en/1126536), among other things. [^o16]

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Given a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* and a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) *f*: *X* → **R**, define an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) ~ on *X* where *p*~*q* whenever *p* and *q* belong to the same [connected component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28topology%29) of a single [level set](wikipedia://en/559622) *f*<sup>−1</sup>(*c*) for some real *c*. The **Reeb graph** is the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) *X* /~ endowed with the [quotient topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_topology).

Generally, this quotient space does not have the structure of a finite graph. Even for a smooth function on a smooth manifold, the Reeb graph can be not one-dimensional and even non-[Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637).[^o19]

In fact, the [compactness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactness) of the manifold is crucial: The Reeb graph of a smooth function on a closed manifold is a one-dimensional [Peano continuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_continuum) that is [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence) to a finite graph.[^o20] In particular, the Reeb graph of a smooth function on a closed manifold with a finite number of critical values –which is the case of [Morse functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function), [Morse–Bott functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse–Bott_function) or functions with isolated critical points – has the structure of a finite graph.[^o21]

:::

## Structure of the Reeb graph defined by a smooth function {#Structure_of_the_Reeb_graph_defined_by_a_smooth_function}

Let $f:M\to {\mathbb R}$ be a [smooth function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) on a [closed manifold](wikipedia://en/669475) $M$. The structure of the Reeb graph $R_f$ depends both on the manifold $M$ and on the class of the function $f$.

### The first Betti number of the Reeb graph {#The_first_Betti_number_of_the_Reeb_graph}

Since for a smooth function on a closed manifold, the Reeb graph $R_f$ is one-dimensional,[^o24] we consider only its first [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) $b_1(R_f)$; if $R_f$ has the structure of a finite graph, then $b_1(R_f)$ is the [cycle rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_rank) of this graph. An upper bound holds[^o25][^o26]

$$b_1(R_f)\le corank(\pi_1(M)),$$

where $corank(\pi_1(M))$ is the [co-rank](wikipedia://en/18720088#Generalizations_and_related_notions) of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of the manifold. If $\dim M\ge3$, this bound is tight even in the class of simple [Morse functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function).[^o27]

If $\dim M=2$, for [smooth functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) this bound is also tight, and in terms of the [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus) $g$ of the surface $M^2$ the bound can be rewritten as

$$b_1(R_f)\le \begin{cases} g, & \text{if } M^2 \text{ is orientable }\\ g/2, & \text{if } M^2 \text{ is non-orientable }. \end{cases}$$

If $\dim M=2$, for [Morse functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function), there is a better bound for the cycle rank. Since for [Morse functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function), the Reeb graph $R_f$ is a finite graph,[^o28] we denote by $N_2$ the number of [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) with [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) 2 in $R_f$. Then[^o29]

$$b_1(R_f)\le \begin{cases} g-N_2, & \text{if } M^2 \text{ is orientable }\\ (g-N_2)/2, & \text{if } M^2 \text{ is non-orientable }. \end{cases}$$

### Leaf blocks of the Reeb graph {#Leaf_blocks_of_the_Reeb_graph}

If $f:M\to R$ is a [Morse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function) or [Morse–Bott function](wikipedia://en/348560#Morse–Bott_theory) on a [closed manifold](wikipedia://en/669475), then its Reeb graph $R_f$ has the structure of a finite graph.[^o31] This finite graph has a specific structure, namely

- If $f$ is [Morse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function), then $R_f$ has no [loops](wikipedia://en/1449083), and all its [leaf blocks](wikipedia://en/4842394) are [complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_2$, i.e., closed intervals[^o32]
- If $f$ is [Morse–Bott](wikipedia://en/348560#Morse–Bott_theory), then $R_f$ has no [loops](wikipedia://en/1449083), and each its [leaf block](wikipedia://en/4842394) contains a [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) with [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) at most 2[^o33]

## Description for Morse functions {#Description_for_Morse_functions}

If $f$ is a [Morse function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function) with distinct [critical values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_value_%28critical_point%29), the Reeb graph can be described more explicitly. Its nodes, or vertices, correspond to the critical level sets $f^{-1}(c)$. The pattern in which the arcs, or edges, meet at the nodes/vertices reflects the change in topology of the level set $f^{-1}(t)$ as $t$ passes through the critical value $c$. For example, if $c$ is a minimum or a maximum of $f$, a component is created or destroyed; consequently, an arc originates or terminates at the corresponding node, which has [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) 1. If $c$ is a [saddle point](wikipedia://en/640249) of index 1 and two components of $f^{-1}(t)$ merge at $t = c$ as $t$ increases, the corresponding vertex of the Reeb graph has degree 3 and looks like the letter "Y". The same reasoning applies if the index of $c$ is $dim X-1$ and a component of $f^{-1}(c)$ splits into two.
