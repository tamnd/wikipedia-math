# Octagon

Regular polygon stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Octagon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **octagon** (grc) is an eight-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) or 8-gon.

:::

A *[regular](wikipedia://en/333306) octagon* has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {8}[^o2] and can also be constructed as a quasiregular [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [square](wikipedia://en/659939), t{4}, which alternates two types of edges. A truncated octagon, t{8} is a [hexadecagon](wikipedia://en/6440317), {16}. A 3D analog of the octagon can be the [rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213) with the triangular faces on it like the replaced edges, if one considers the octagon to be a truncated square.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: A generalization van Aubel theorem.svg.* The diagonals of the green [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) are equal in length and at right angles to each other

Assuming [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry), the sum of all the internal angles of any octagon is 1080°. As with all polygons, the external angles total 360°.

If squares are constructed all internally or all externally on the sides of an octagon, then the midpoints of the segments connecting the centers of opposite squares form a quadrilateral that is both [equidiagonal](wikipedia://en/35012663) and [orthodiagonal](wikipedia://en/30425383) (that is, whose diagonals are equal in length and at right angles to each other).[^o5]

The [midpoint octagon](wikipedia://en/33456840) of a reference octagon has its eight vertices at the midpoints of the sides of the reference octagon. If squares are constructed all internally or all externally on the sides of the midpoint octagon, then the midpoints of the segments connecting the centers of opposite squares themselves form the vertices of a square.[^o5]

### Regularity {#Regularity}

A [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) octagon is a closed [figure](wikipedia://en/169191) with sides of the same length and internal angles of the same size. It has eight lines of [reflective symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflective_symmetry) and [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096) of order 8. A regular octagon is represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {8}. The internal [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) at each vertex of a regular octagon is 135[°](wikipedia://en/1195294) ($\scriptstyle \frac{3\pi}{4}$ [radians](wikipedia://en/26003)). The [central angle](wikipedia://en/462705) is 45° ($\scriptstyle \frac{\pi}{4}$ radians).

### Area {#Area}

The area of a regular octagon of side length *a* is given by

$$A = 2 \cot \frac{\pi}{8} a^2 = 2(1+\sqrt{2})a^2 \approx 4.828\,a^2.$$

In terms of the [circumradius](wikipedia://en/161262) *R*, the area is

$$A = 4 \sin \frac{\pi}{4} R^2 = 2\sqrt{2}R^2 \approx 2.828\,R^2.$$

In terms of the [apothem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apothem) *r* (see also [inscribed figure](wikipedia://en/4470203)), the area is

$$A = 8 \tan \frac{\pi}{8} r^2 = 8(\sqrt{2}-1)r^2 \approx 3.314\,r^2.$$

These last two [coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficients) bracket the value of [pi](wikipedia://en/23601), the area of the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071).

*Image: Octagon in square.svg.* The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) octagon can be computed as a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29).

The area can also be expressed as

$$\,\!A=S^2-a^2,$$

where *S* is the span of the octagon, or the second-shortest diagonal; and *a* is the length of one of the sides, or bases. This is easily proven if one takes an octagon, draws a square around the outside (making sure that four of the eight sides overlap with the four sides of the square) and then takes the corner triangles (these are [45–45–90 triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_right_triangles#45–45–90_triangle)) and places them with right angles pointed inward, forming a square. The edges of this square are each the length of the base.

Given the length of a side *a*, the span *S* is

$$S=\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}+a+\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}=(1+\sqrt{2})a \approx 2.414a.$$

The span, then, is equal to the *[silver ratio](wikipedia://en/920526)* times the side, a.

The area is then as above:

$$A=((1+\sqrt{2})a)^2-a^2=2(1+\sqrt{2})a^2 \approx 4.828a^2.$$

Expressed in terms of the span, the area is

$$A=2(\sqrt{2}-1)S^2 \approx 0.828S^2.$$

Another simple formula for the area is

$$\ A=2aS.$$

More often the span *S* is known, and the length of the sides, *a*, is to be determined, as when cutting a square piece of material into a regular octagon. From the above,

$$a \approx S/2.414.$$

The two end lengths *e* on each side (the leg lengths of the triangles (green in the image) truncated from the square), as well as being $e=a/\sqrt{2},$ may be calculated as

$$\,\!e=(S-a)/2.$$

### Circumradius and inradius {#Circumradius_and_inradius}

The [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) of the regular octagon in terms of the side length *a* is[^o9]

$$R=\left(\frac{\sqrt{4+2\sqrt{2}}}{2}\right)a \approx 1.307 a,$$

and the [inradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inradius) is

$$r=\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)a \approx 1.207 a.$$

(that is one-half the *[silver ratio](wikipedia://en/920526)* times the side, *a*, or one-half the span, *S*)

The inradius can be calculated from the circumradius as

$$r = R \cos \frac{\pi}{8}$$

### Diagonality {#Diagonality}

The regular octagon, in terms of the side length *a*, has three different types of [diagonals](wikipedia://en/349251):

- Short diagonal;
- Medium diagonal (also called span or height), which is twice the length of the inradius;
- Long diagonal, which is twice the length of the circumradius.

The formula for each of them follows from the basic principles of geometry. Here are the formulas for their length:[^o11]

- Short diagonal: $a\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}$ ;
- Medium diagonal: $(1+\sqrt2)a$ ; (*[silver ratio](wikipedia://en/920526)* times a)
- Long diagonal: $a\sqrt{4 + 2\sqrt2}$ .

### Construction {#Construction}

*Image: 8-folding.svg.* building a regular octagon by folding a sheet of paper

A regular octagon at a given circumcircle may be constructed as follows:

1. Draw a circle and a diameter AOE, where O is the center and A, E are points on the circumcircle.
1. Draw another diameter GOC, perpendicular to AOE.
1. (Note in passing that A,C,E,G are vertices of a square).
1. Draw the bisectors of the right angles GOA and EOG, making two more diameters HOD and FOB.
1. A,B,C,D,E,F,G,H are the vertices of the octagon.

A regular octagon can be constructed using a [straightedge](wikipedia://en/147854) and a [compass](wikipedia://en/492445), as 8 = 2<sup>3</sup>, a [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948):

*Image: Regular Octagon Inscribed in a Circle.gif.*

*Image: Meccano octagon.svg.* Meccano octagon construction.

The regular octagon can be constructed with [meccano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meccano) bars. Twelve bars of size 4, three bars of size 5 and two bars of size 6 are required.

Each side of a regular octagon subtends half a right angle at the centre of the circle which connects its vertices. Its area can thus be computed as the sum of eight isosceles triangles, leading to the result:

$$\text{Area} = 2 a^2 (\sqrt{2} + 1)$$

for an octagon of side *a*.

:::

### Standard coordinates {#Standard_coordinates}

The coordinates for the vertices of a regular octagon centered at the origin and with side length 2 are:

- (±1, ±(1+$\sqrt{2}$))
- (±(1+$\sqrt{2}$), ±1).

## Skew {#Skew}

*Image: Skew polygon in square antiprism.svg.* A regular skew octagon seen as edges of a [square antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860045), symmetry D<sub>4d</sub>, \[2<sup>\+</sup>,8\], (2\*4), order 16.

A **skew octagon** is a [skew polygon](wikipedia://en/6545461) with eight vertices and edges but not existing on the same plane. The interior of such an octagon is not generally defined. A *skew zig-zag octagon* has vertices alternating between two parallel planes.

A **regular skew octagon** is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) with equal edge lengths. In three dimensions it is a zig-zag skew octagon and can be seen in the vertices and side edges of a [square antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860045) with the same D<sub>4d</sub>, \[2<sup>\+</sup>,8\] symmetry, order 16.

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Symmetry*

::: {#o26 .table cue=table name="Symmetry"}

| *Image: Regular\_octagon\_symmetries.png.* | The eleven symmetries of a regular octagon. Lines of reflections are blue through vertices, purple through edges, and gyration orders are given in the center. Vertices are colored by their symmetry position. |
|---|---|

:::

The *regular octagon* has Dih<sub>8</sub> symmetry, order 16. There are three dihedral subgroups: Dih<sub>4</sub>, Dih<sub>2</sub>, and Dih<sub>1</sub>, and four [cyclic subgroups](wikipedia://en/52327): Z<sub>8</sub>, Z<sub>4</sub>, Z<sub>2</sub>, and Z<sub>1</sub>, the last implying no symmetry.

On the regular octagon, there are eleven distinct symmetries. John Conway labels full symmetry as **r16**.[^o28] The dihedral symmetries are divided depending on whether they pass through vertices (**d** for diagonal) or edges (**p** for perpendiculars) Cyclic symmetries in the middle column are labeled as **g** for their central gyration orders. Full symmetry of the regular form is **r16** and no symmetry is labeled **a1**.

The most common high symmetry octagons are **p8**, an [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560) octagon constructed by four mirrors can alternate long and short edges, and **d8**, an [isotoxal](wikipedia://en/6289189) octagon constructed with equal edge lengths, but vertices alternating two different internal angles. These two forms are [duals](wikipedia://en/19865549) of each other and have half the symmetry order of the regular octagon.

Each subgroup symmetry allows one or more degrees of freedom for irregular forms. Only the **g8** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

## Use {#Use}

*Image: Dehio 10 Dome of the Rock Floor plan.jpg.* The octagonal floor plan, [Dome of the Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock), [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem).

The octagonal shape is used as a design element in architecture. The [Dome of the Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock) has a characteristic octagonal plan. The [Tower of the Winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_the_Winds) in Athens is another example of an octagonal structure. The octagonal plan has also been in church architecture such as [St. George's Cathedral, Addis Ababa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George's_Cathedral,_Addis_Ababa), [Basilica of San Vitale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Vitale) (in Ravenna, Italia), [Castel del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_del_Monte,_Apulia) (Apulia, Italia), [Florence Baptistery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Baptistery), [Zum Friedefürsten Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zum_Friedefürsten_Church) (Germany) and a number of [octagonal churches in Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octagonal_churches_in_Norway). The central space in the [Aachen Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aachen_Cathedral), the Carolingian [Palatine Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatine_Chapel,_Aachen), has a regular octagonal floorplan. Uses of octagons in churches also include lesser design elements, such as the octagonal [apse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apse) of [Nidaros Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidaros_Cathedral).

Architects such as [John Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Andrews_%28architect%29) have used octagonal floor layouts in buildings for functionally separating office areas from building services, such as in the [Intelsat Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelsat_Headquarters) of Washington or [Callam Offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callam_Offices) in Canberra.
