# Golden angle

*Image: Golden Angle.svg.* The golden angle is the angle subtended by the smaller (red) arc when two arcs that make up a circle are in the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Golden angle"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **golden angle** is the smaller of the two [angles](wikipedia://en/1196) created by sectioning the circumference of a circle according to the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386); that is, into two [arcs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29) such that the ratio of the length of the smaller arc to the length of the larger arc is the same as the ratio of the length of the larger arc to the full circumference of the circle.

:::

Algebraically, let *a+b* be the circumference of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220), divided into a longer arc of length *a* and a smaller arc of length *b* such that

$$\frac{a + b}{a} = \frac{a}{b}$$

The golden angle is then the angle [subtended](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtend) by the smaller arc of length *b*. It measures approximately \137.5077640500378546463487...° or in [radians](wikipedia://en/26003) \2.39996322972865332... .

The name comes from the golden angle's connection to the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) *φ*; the exact value of the golden angle is

$$360\left(1 - \frac{1}{\varphi}\right) = 360(2 - \varphi) = \frac{360}{\varphi^2} = 180(3 - \sqrt{5})\text{ degrees}$$

or

$$2\pi \left( 1 - \frac{1}{\varphi}\right) = 2\pi(2 - \varphi) = \frac{2\pi}{\varphi^2} = \pi(3 - \sqrt{5})\text{ radians},$$

where the equivalences follow from well-known algebraic properties of the golden ratio.

## Derivation {#Derivation}

The golden ratio is equal to *φ* = *a*/*b* given the conditions above.

Let *ƒ* be the fraction of the circumference subtended by the golden angle, or equivalently, the golden angle divided by the angular measurement of the circle.

$$f = \frac{b}{a+b} = \frac{1}{1+\varphi}.$$

But since

$${1+\varphi} = \varphi^2,$$

it follows that

$$f = \frac{1}{\varphi^2}$$

This is equivalent to saying that *φ*<sup>2</sup> golden angles can fit in a circle.

The fraction of a circle occupied by the golden angle is therefore

$$f \approx 0.381966. \,$$

The golden angle *g* can therefore be numerically approximated in [degrees](wikipedia://en/1195294) as:

$$g \approx 360 \times 0.381966 \approx 137.508^\circ,\,$$

or in radians as :

$$g \approx 2\pi \times 0.381966 \approx 2.39996. \,$$

## Golden angle in nature {#Golden_angle_in_nature}

*Image: phyllotaxis\_golden\_angle.svg.* The angle between successive florets in some flowers is the golden angle.

*Image: Sunflower seed pattern animation.gif.* Animation simulating the spawning of sunflower seeds from a central meristem where the next seed is oriented one golden angle away from the previous seed.

The golden angle plays a significant role in the theory of [phyllotaxis](wikipedia://en/443124); for example, the golden angle is the angle separating the [florets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floret) on a [sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower).[^o4] Analysis of the pattern shows that it is highly sensitive to the angle separating the individual [primordia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primordia), with the [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949) angle giving the [parastichy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parastichy) with optimal packing density.[^o5]

Mathematical modelling of a plausible physical mechanism for floret development has shown the pattern arising spontaneously from the solution of a nonlinear partial differential equation on a [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29).[^o6][^o7]

## Straightedge and compass construction {#Straightedge_and_compass_construction}

As its [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) and [cosine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine) are [transcendental numbers](wikipedia://en/30325), the golden angle cannot be [constructed using a straightedge and compass](wikipedia://en/61229). Artist [Almada Negreiros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almada_Negreiros) discovered an approximate construction that is accurate to 0.11°.[^o9]
