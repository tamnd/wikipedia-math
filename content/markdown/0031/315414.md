# Gelfand–Naimark theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gelfand–Naimark theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Gelfand–Naimark theorem** states that an arbitrary [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) *A* is isometrically \*-isomorphic to a C\*-subalgebra of [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932). This result was proven by [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643) and [Mark Naimark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Naimark) in 1943 and was a significant point in the development of the theory of C\*-algebras since it established the possibility of considering a C\*-algebra as an abstract algebraic entity without reference to particular realizations as an [operator algebra](wikipedia://en/455987).

:::

## Details {#Details}

The Gelfand–Naimark representation π is the Hilbert space analogue of the [direct sum of representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_representations) π<sub>*f*</sub> of *A* where *f* ranges over the set of [pure states](wikipedia://en/454959#Pure_states) of A and π<sub>*f*</sub> is the [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982) associated to *f* by the [GNS construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNS_construction). Thus the Gelfand–Naimark representation acts on the Hilbert direct sum of the Hilbert spaces *H*<sub>*f*</sub> by

$$\pi(x) [\bigoplus_{f} H_f] = \bigoplus_{f} \pi_f(x)H_f.$$

π(*x*) is a [bounded linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) since it is the direct sum of a family of operators, each one having norm ≤ ||*x*||.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**. The Gelfand–Naimark representation of a C\*-algebra is an isometric \*-representation.

It suffices to show the map π is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective), since for \*-morphisms of C\*-algebras injective implies isometric. Let *x* be a non-zero element of *A*. By the [Krein extension theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krein_extension_theorem) for positive [linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional), there is a state *f* on *A* such that *f*(*z*) ≥ 0 for all non-negative z in *A* and *f*(−*x*\* *x*) \< 0. Consider the GNS representation π<sub>*f*</sub> with [cyclic vector](wikipedia://en/58713633) ξ. Since

$$\begin{aligned} \|\pi_f(x) \xi\|^2 & = \langle \pi_f(x) \xi \mid \pi_f(x) \xi \rangle = \langle \xi \mid \pi_f(x^*) \pi_f(x) \xi \rangle \\[6pt] & = \langle \xi \mid \pi_f(x^* x) \xi \rangle= f(x^* x) > 0, \end{aligned}$$

it follows that π<sub>*f*</sub> (x) ≠ 0, so π (x) ≠ 0, so π is injective.

The construction of Gelfand–Naimark *representation* depends only on the GNS construction and therefore it is meaningful for any [Banach \*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_*-algebra) *A* having an [approximate identity](wikipedia://en/949628). In general (when *A* is not a C\*-algebra) it will not be a [faithful representation](wikipedia://en/2617872). The closure of the image of π(*A*) will be a C\*-algebra of operators called the [C\*-enveloping algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*-enveloping_algebra) of *A*. Equivalently, we can define the C\*-enveloping algebra as follows: Define a real valued function on *A* by

$$\|x\|_{\operatorname{C}^*} = \sup_f \sqrt{f(x^* x)}$$

as *f* ranges over pure states of *A*. This is a semi-norm, which we refer to as the *C\* semi-norm* of *A*. The set **I** of elements of *A* whose semi-norm is 0 forms a two sided-ideal in *A* closed under involution. Thus the [quotient vector space](wikipedia://en/898784) *A* / **I** is an involutive algebra and the norm

$$\| \cdot \|_{\operatorname{C}^*}$$

[factors](wikipedia://en/82341) through a norm on *A* / **I**, which except for completeness, is a C\* norm on *A* / **I** (these are sometimes called pre-C\*-norms). Taking the completion of *A* / **I** relative to this pre-C\*-norm produces a C\*-algebra *B*.

By the [Krein–Milman theorem](wikipedia://en/3206654) one can show without too much difficulty that for *x* an element of the [Banach \*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_*-algebra) *A* having an approximate identity:

$$\sup_{f \in \operatorname{State}(A)} f(x^*x) = \sup_{f \in \operatorname{PureState}(A)} f(x^*x).$$

It follows that an equivalent form for the C\* norm on *A* is to take the above supremum over all states.

The universal construction is also used to define [universal C\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/668934) of isometries.

:::

::: {#o4 .remark cue=inline-head name="Remark"}

**Remark**. The [Gelfand representation](wikipedia://en/364372) or [Gelfand isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfand_isomorphism) for a commutative C\*-algebra with unit $A$ is an isometric \*-isomorphism from $A$ to the algebra of continuous complex-valued functions on the space of multiplicative linear functionals, which in the commutative case are precisely the pure states, of *A* with the weak\* topology.

:::
