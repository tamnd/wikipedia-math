# Prime knot

*Image: Hopf Link.png.* [Simplest prime link](wikipedia://en/1515511)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prime knot"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), a **prime knot** or **prime link** is a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) that is, in a certain sense, indecomposable. Specifically, it is a non-[trivial](wikipedia://en/60075) knot which cannot be written as the [knot sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_sum) of two non-trivial knots. Knots that are not prime are said to be **composite knots** or **composite links**. It can be a nontrivial problem to determine whether a given knot is prime or not.

:::

A family of examples of prime knots are the [torus knots](wikipedia://en/1165182). These are formed by wrapping a circle around a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) *p* times in one direction and *q* times in the other, where *p* and *q* are [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) integers.

Knots are characterized by their [crossing numbers](wikipedia://en/18298598). The simplest prime knot is the [trefoil](wikipedia://en/404773) with three crossings. The trefoil is actually a (2, 3)-torus knot. The [figure-eight knot](wikipedia://en/59565), with four crossings, is the simplest non-torus knot. For any positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *n*, there are a finite number of prime knots with *n* [crossings](wikipedia://en/18298598). ## Schubert's theorem {#Schubert's_theorem}

A theorem due to [Horst Schubert](wikipedia://en/38487829) (1919–2001) states that every knot can be uniquely expressed as a [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) of prime knots.[^o5]
