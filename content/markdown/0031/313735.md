# Knot polynomial

*Image: Skein (HOMFLY).svg.* Many knot polynomials are computed using [skein relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skein_relations), which allow one to change the different crossings of a knot to get simpler knots.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Knot polynomial"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), a **knot polynomial** is a [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) in the form of a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) whose coefficients encode some of the properties of a given [knot](wikipedia://en/366808).

:::

## History {#History}

The first knot polynomial, the [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647), was introduced by [James Waddell Alexander II](wikipedia://en/9848523) in 1923. Other knot polynomials were not found until almost 60 years later.

In the 1960s, [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) came up with a [skein relation](wikipedia://en/314902) for a version of the Alexander polynomial, usually referred to as the [Alexander–Conway polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander–Conway_polynomial). The significance of this skein relation was not realized until the early 1980s, when [Vaughan Jones](wikipedia://en/1227628) discovered the [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642). This led to the discovery of more knot polynomials, such as the so-called [HOMFLY polynomial](wikipedia://en/1039260).

Soon after Jones' discovery, [Louis Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kauffman) noticed the Jones polynomial could be computed by means of a [partition function](wikipedia://en/312252) (state-sum model), which involved the [bracket polynomial](wikipedia://en/3774360), an invariant of [framed knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framed_knot). This opened up avenues of research linking knot theory and [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481).

In the late 1980s, two related breakthroughs were made. [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) demonstrated that the Jones polynomial, and similar Jones-type invariants, had an interpretation in [Chern–Simons theory](wikipedia://en/391908). [Viktor Vasilyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Anatolyevich_Vasilyev) and [Mikhail Goussarov](wikipedia://en/7074133) started the theory of [finite type invariants](wikipedia://en/3014395) of knots. The coefficients of the previously named polynomials are known to be of finite type (after perhaps a suitable "change of variables").

In recent years, the Alexander polynomial has been shown to be related to [Floer homology](wikipedia://en/2672856). The graded [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of the [knot Floer homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heegaard_Floer_homology) of [Peter Ozsváth](wikipedia://en/5027843) and [Zoltan Szabó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoltán_Szabó_%28mathematician%29) is the Alexander polynomial.

## Examples {#Examples}

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

| [Alexander–Briggs notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander–Briggs_notation) | [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) $\Delta(t)$ | [Conway polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647#Alexander.E2.80.93Conway_polynomial) $\nabla(z)$ | [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642) $V(q)$ | [HOMFLY polynomial](wikipedia://en/1039260) $H(a,z)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $0_1$ ([Unknot](wikipedia://en/60075)) | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| $3_1$ ([Trefoil Knot](wikipedia://en/404773)) | $t - 1 + t^{-1}$ | $z^2 + 1$ | $q^{-1} + q^{-3} - q^{-4}$ | $-a^{4}+a^{2}z^{2}+2a^{2}$ |
| $4_1$ ([Figure-eight Knot](wikipedia://en/59565)) | $-t + 3 - t^{-1}$ | $-z^2+1$ | $q^2 - q + 1 - q^{-1} + q^{-2}$ | $a^{2}+a^{-2}-z^{2}-1$ |
| $5_1$ ([Cinquefoil Knot](wikipedia://en/16856240)) | $t^2 - t + 1 - t^{-1} + t^{-2}$ | $z^4 + 3z^2 + 1$ | $q^{-2} + q^{-4} - q^{-5} + q^{-6} - q^{-7}$ | $-a^{6}z^{2}-2a^{6}+a^{4}z^{4}+4a^{4}z^{2}+3a^{4}$ |
| $3_1 \# 3_1$ ([Granny Knot](wikipedia://en/26666182)) | $\left(t - 1 + t^{-1}\right)^2$ | $\left(z^2 + 1\right)^2$ | $\left(q^{-1} + q^{-3} - q^{-4}\right)^2$ | $\left(-a^{4}+a^{2}z^{2}+2a^{2}\right)^2$ |
| $3_1 \# 3^*_1$ ([Square Knot](wikipedia://en/26666011)) | $\left(t - 1 + t^{-1}\right)^2$ | $\left(z^2 + 1\right)^2$ | $\left(q^{-1} + q^{-3} - q^{-4}\right)\left(q + q^{3} - q^{4}\right)$ | $\left(-a^{4}+a^{2}z^{2}+2a^{2}\right) \times$ $\left(-a^{-4}+a^{-2}z^{-2}+2a^{-2}\right)$ |

:::

[Alexander–Briggs notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander–Briggs_notation) organizes knots by their crossing number.

[Alexander polynomials](wikipedia://en/1107647) and [Conway polynomials](wikipedia://en/1107647#Alexander.E2.80.93Conway_polynomial) can *not* recognize the difference of left-trefoil knot and right-trefoil knot, while the [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642) can.

So we have the same situation as the granny knot and square knot since the [addition](wikipedia://en/153008#Adding_knots) of knots in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is the product of knots in [knot polynomials](wikipedia://en/153008#Knot_polynomials).

:::
