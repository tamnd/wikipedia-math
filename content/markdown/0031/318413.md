# French Institute for Research in Computer Science and Automation

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="French Institute for Research in Computer Science and Automation"}

The **National Institute for Research in Digital Science and Technology** (**Inria**; Institut national de recherche en sciences et technologies du numérique) is a French national research institution focusing on [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969). It was created under the name **French Institute for Research in Computer Science and Automation** (**IRIA**; Institut de recherche en informatique et en automatique) in 1967 at [Rocquencourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocquencourt,_Yvelines) near [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), part of [Plan Calcul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_Calcul). Its first site was the historical premises of [SHAPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Headquarters_Allied_Powers_Europe) (central command of [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO) military forces), which is still used as Inria's main headquarters. In 1980, IRIA became INRIA.[^o3] Since 2011, it has been styled *Inria*.

:::

Inria is a [Public Scientific and Technical Research Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Scientific_and_Technical_Research_Establishment) (EPST) under the double supervision of the French [Ministry of National Education, Advanced Instruction and Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_National_Education_%28France%29) and the [Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy,_Finance_and_Industry).

## Administrative status {#Administrative_status}

*Image: INRIA Alpes'13 - 377 Grenoble.jpg.* Inria Grenoble building, 2013

Inria has nine research centers distributed across France (in [Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordeaux), [Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenoble)-[Inovallée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inovallée), [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille), [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon), [Nancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy,_France), [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989)-[Rocquencourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocquencourt,_Yvelines), [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennes), [Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau_de_Saclay), and [Sophia Antipolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophia_Antipolis)) and one center abroad in [Santiago de Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Chile), Chile. It also contributes to academic research teams outside of those centers.

Inria Rennes is part of the joint [Institut de recherche en informatique et systèmes aléatoires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_de_recherche_en_informatique_et_systèmes_aléatoires) (IRISA) with several other entities. Inria Nancy is part of the joint [LORIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LORIA) (Laboratoire Lorrain de Recherche en Informatique et ses Applications) with [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS) and the [University of Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lorraine).

Before December 2007, the three centers of Bordeaux, Lille and Saclay formed a single research center called INRIA Futurs.

In October 2010, Inria, with [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University) (now [Sorbonne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University)) and [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) started [IRILL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRILL), a center for innovation and research initiative for free software.

Inria employs 3800 people. Among them are 1300 researchers, 1000 Ph.D. students and 500 postdoctorates.

Inria does both [theoretical](wikipedia://en/323392) and applied research in computer science. In the process, it has produced many widely used programs, such as:

- [Bigloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bigloo), a [Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_%28programming_language%29) implementation
- [CADP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CADP), a tool box for the verification of asynchronous [concurrent systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concurrent_systems)
- [Caml](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caml), a language from the [ML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ML_programming_language) family
  - [Caml Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caml_Light) and [OCaml](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OCaml) implementations
- [CGAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CGAL), A C++ library for solving problems in computational geometry
- [Chorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChorusOS), microkernel-based distributed operating system
- [CompCert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CompCert), verified C compiler for PowerPC, ARM and x86\_32
- [Contrail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrail_%28software%29)[^o6]
- [CYCLADES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CYCLADES), pioneered the use of [datagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datagram), [functional layering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_layering), and the [end-to-end strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End-to-end_principle), concepts adopted in [TCP/IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCP/IP) and the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet).[^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10]
- [Eigen (C++ library)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigen_%28C++_library%29)
- [Esterel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esterel), a [programming language](wikipedia://en/23015) for State Automata
- Geneauto — code-generation from model[^o11]
- Graphite, a research platform for computer graphics, 3D modeling and numerical geometry
- Gudhi — A [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++) library with [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) interface for [computational topology](wikipedia://en/1248833) and [topological data analysis](wikipedia://en/17740009)[^o12]
- [Le Lisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Lisp), a portable Lisp implementation
- medInria, a medical image processing software, popularly used for MRI images.[^o13]
- [GNU MPFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_MPFR), an arbitrary-precision floating-point library
- [OpenViBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenVibe), a [software platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_platform) dedicated to designing, testing and using [brain–computer interfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain–computer_interface).
- [Pharo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharo), an open-source Smalltalk derived from [Squeak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squeak_%28programming_language%29).[^o14]
- [Rocq](wikipedia://en/581974), a [proof assistant](wikipedia://en/1258607)
- [scikit-learn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scikit-learn), a machine learning software package
- [Scilab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scilab), a numerical computation software package
- [SimGrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimGrid)
- [SmartEiffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SmartEiffel), a free Eiffel compiler
- [SOFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation_Open_Framework_Architecture), an open source framework for multi-physics simulation with an emphasis on medical simulation.
- [TOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_%28pattern_matching_language%29), a pattern matching language
- ViSP, an open source visual servoing platform library
- [XtreemFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XtreemFS)[^o6]
- XtreemOS, a grid distributed operating system
- Zenon, an extensible automated theorem prover producing checkable proofs

Inria furthermore leads French AI Research, ranking 12th worldwide in 2019, based on accepted publications at the [Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_on_Neural_Information_Processing_Systems).[^o15]

## History {#History}

During the summer of 1988, the INRIA connected its Sophia-Antipolis unit to the [NSFNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSFNet) via [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) using a satellite link leased to France Telecom and MCI. The link became operational on 8 August 1988, and allowed INRIA researchers to access the US network and allowed [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA) researchers access to an astronomical database based in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg). This was the first international connection to NSFNET and the first time that French networks were connected directly to a network using [TCP/IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TCP/IP). The [Internet in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_in_France) was limited to research and education for some years to come.[^o17][^o18][^o19]
