# Gaussian rational

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gaussian rational"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Gaussian rational** number is a [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) of the form *p* + *qi*, where *p* and *q* are both [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024). The set of all Gaussian rationals forms the Gaussian rational [field](wikipedia://en/10603), denoted **Q**(*i*), obtained by adjoining the [imaginary number](wikipedia://en/14554) *i* to the field of rationals **Q**.

:::

## Properties of the field {#Properties_of_the_field}

The field of Gaussian rationals provides an example of an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) that is both a [quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781) and a [cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462) (since *i* is a 4th [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950)). Like all quadratic fields it is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of **Q** with [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) of order two, in this case generated by [complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation), and is thus an [abelian extension](wikipedia://en/174448) of **Q**, with [conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductor_%28algebraic_number_theory%29) 4.[^o3]

As with cyclotomic fields more generally, the field of Gaussian rationals is neither [ordered](wikipedia://en/22430) nor [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) (as a metric space). The [Gaussian integers](wikipedia://en/48628) **Z**\[*i*\] form the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of **Q**(*i*). The set of all Gaussian rationals is [countably infinite](wikipedia://en/6026).

The field of Gaussian rationals is also a two-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over **Q** with natural [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) $\{1, i\}$.

## Ford spheres {#Ford_spheres}

*Image: Ford-Kugeln.png.* Subset of the Ford spheres

The concept of [Ford circles](wikipedia://en/365503) can be generalized from the rational numbers to the Gaussian rationals, giving Ford spheres. In this construction, the complex numbers are embedded as a plane in a three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), and for each Gaussian rational point in this plane one constructs a sphere tangent to the plane at that point. For a Gaussian rational represented in lowest terms as $p/q$ (i.e. $p$ and $q$ are relatively prime), the radius of this sphere should be $1/2|q|^2$ where $|q|^2 = q \bar q$ is the squared modulus, and $\bar q$ is the [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918). The resulting spheres are [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) for pairs of Gaussian rationals $P/Q$ and $p/q$ with $|Pq-pQ|=1$, and otherwise they do not intersect each other.[^o5][^o6]
