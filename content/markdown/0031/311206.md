# Minds, Machines and Gödel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minds, Machines and Gödel"}

"**Minds, Machines and Gödel**" is [J. R. Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lucas_%28philosopher%29)'s 1959 philosophical paper in which he argues that a human [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) cannot be accurately represented by an [algorithmic automaton](wikipedia://en/30403). Appealing to [Gödel's incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_incompleteness_theorem), he argues that for any such automaton, there would be some mathematical formula which it could not prove, but which the human mathematician could both see, and show, to be true.

:::

The paper is a [Gödelian argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödelian_argument) against [mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanism_%28philosophy%29).

Lucas presented the paper in 1959 to the [Oxford Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Philosophical_Society). It was first printed in *Philosophy*, XXXVI, 1961,[^o2] then reprinted in *The Modeling of Mind*, [Kenneth M. Sayre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_M._Sayre) and Frederick J. Crosson, eds., Notre Dame Press, 1963, and in *Minds and Machines*, ed. Alan Ross Anderson, Prentice-Hall, 1964, .
