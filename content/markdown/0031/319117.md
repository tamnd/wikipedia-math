# Ernst Kummer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ernst Kummer"}

**Ernst Eduard Kummer** (; 29 January 1810 – 14 May 1893) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Skilled in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), Kummer trained German army officers in [ballistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistics); afterwards, he taught for 10 years in a *[gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29)*, the German equivalent of high school, where he inspired the mathematical career of [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Kummer was born in [Sorau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorau), [Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brandenburg) (then part of [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia)). His father, a physician, died in 1813.[^o4]

He initially pursued Protestant theology but later studied mathematics[^o4] and was awarded a PhD from the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle) in 1831 for writing a prize-winning mathematical essay (*De cosinuum et sinuum potestatibus secundum cosinus et sinus arcuum multiplicium evolvendis*), which was published a year later.

In 1840, Kummer married Ottilie Mendelssohn, daughter of Nathan Mendelssohn and Henriette Itzig. Ottilie was a cousin of [Felix Mendelssohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Mendelssohn) and his sister Rebecca Mendelssohn Bartholdy, the wife of the mathematician [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002). His second wife (whom he married soon after the death of Ottilie in 1848), Bertha Cauer, was a maternal cousin of Ottilie. Overall, he had 13 children. His daughter Marie married the mathematician [Hermann Schwarz](wikipedia://en/3165834). Kummer retired from teaching and from mathematics in 1890 and died three years later in Berlin.

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

Kummer made several contributions to mathematics in different areas; he codified some of the relations between different [hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_series), known as contiguity relations. The [Kummer surface](wikipedia://en/647758) results from taking the quotient of a two-dimensional [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) by the cyclic group {1, −1} (an early [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504): it has 16 singular points, and its geometry was intensively studied in the nineteenth century).

Kummer also proved [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) for a considerable class of prime exponents (see [regular prime](wikipedia://en/323689), [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907)). His methods were closer, perhaps, to [*p*-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) ones than to [ideal theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_ideal) as understood later, though the term 'ideal' was invented by Kummer. He studied what were later called [Kummer extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kummer_extension) of [fields](wikipedia://en/10603): that is, extensions generated by adjoining an *n*th root to a field already containing a primitive *n*th [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950). This is a significant extension of the theory of quadratic extensions, and the genus theory of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) (linked to the 2-torsion of the class group). As such, it is still foundational for [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695).

Kummer further conducted research in [ballistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistics) and, jointly with [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817) he investigated [ray systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_system).[^o6]
