# Law of tangents

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Law of tangents"}

*Image: Triangle with notations 2.svg.* Figure 1 – A triangle. The angles $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$ are respectively opposite the sides $a$, $b$, and $c$.

In [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261), the **law of tangents** or **tangent rule**[^o2] is a statement about the relationship between the tangents of two angles of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) and the lengths of the opposing sides.

:::

In Figure 1, $a$, $b$, and $c$ are the lengths of the three sides of the triangle, and $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$ are the angles *opposite* those three respective sides. The law of [tangents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_function) states that

$$\frac{a-b}{a+b} = \frac{\tan\tfrac12(\alpha-\beta)} {\tan\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta)}.$$

The law of tangents, although not as commonly known as the [law of sines](wikipedia://en/113222) or the [law of cosines](wikipedia://en/19480890), is equivalent to the law of sines, and can be used in any case where two sides and the included angle, or two angles and a side, are known.

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

To prove the law of tangents one can start with the [law of sines](wikipedia://en/113222):

$$\frac{a}{\sin\alpha} = \frac{b}{\sin\beta} = d,$$

where $d$ is the [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) of the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432), so that $a = d \sin\alpha$ and $b = d \sin\beta$.

It follows that

$$\frac{a-b}{a+b} = \frac{d \sin \alpha - d\sin\beta} {d \sin\alpha + d\sin\beta} = \frac{\sin \alpha - \sin\beta} {\sin\alpha + \sin\beta}.$$

Using the [trigonometric identity](wikipedia://en/25804104#Product-to-sum_and_sum-to-product_identities), the factor formula for sines specifically

$$\sin\alpha \pm \sin\beta = 2 \sin\tfrac12(\alpha \pm \beta) \, \cos\tfrac12( \alpha \mp \beta),$$

we get

$$\frac{a-b}{a+b} = \frac{2\sin\tfrac12(\alpha-\beta) \, \cos\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta)} {2\sin\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta) \, \cos\tfrac12(\alpha-\beta)} = \frac{\sin\tfrac12(\alpha-\beta)} {\cos\tfrac12(\alpha-\beta)} \Bigg/ \frac{\sin\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta)} {\cos\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta)} = \frac{\tan\tfrac12(\alpha-\beta)} {\tan\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta)}.$$

As an alternative to using the identity for the sum or difference of two sines, one may cite the trigonometric identity

$$\tan \tfrac12 (\alpha \pm \beta) = \frac{\sin\alpha \pm \sin\beta} {\cos\alpha + \cos\beta}$$

(see [tangent half-angle formula](wikipedia://en/1039889)).

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

The law of tangents can be used to compute the angles of a triangle in which two sides $a$ and $b$ and the enclosed angle $\gamma$ are given.

From

$$\tan\tfrac12(\alpha-\beta) = \frac{a-b}{a+b} \tan\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta) = \frac{a-b}{a+b} \cot\tfrac12\gamma$$

compute the angle difference $\alpha - \beta = \Delta$; use that to calculate $\beta = (180^{\circ} - \gamma - \Delta)/2$ and then $\alpha = \beta + \Delta$.

Once an angle opposite a known side is computed, the remaining side $c$ can be computed using the [law of sines](wikipedia://en/113222).

In the time before electronic calculators were available, this method was preferable to an application of the [law of cosines](wikipedia://en/19480890) $c = \sqrt{a^{2} + b^{2} - 2ab \cos \gamma}$, as this latter law necessitated an additional lookup in a [logarithm table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithm_table), in order to compute the square root. In modern times the law of tangents may have better [numerical](wikipedia://en/21506) properties than the law of cosines: If $\gamma$ is small, and $a$ and $b$ are almost equal, then an application of the law of cosines leads to a subtraction of almost equal values, incurring [catastrophic cancellation](wikipedia://en/19985542).

:::

::: {#o8 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Spherical law of tangents"}

## Spherical version {#Spherical_version}

On a sphere of unit radius, the sides of the triangle are arcs of [great circles](wikipedia://en/48082). Accordingly, their lengths can be expressed in radians or any other units of angular measure. Let $A$, $B$, $C$ be the angles at the three vertices of the triangle and let $a$, $b$, $c$ be the respective lengths of the opposite sides. The spherical law of tangents says[^o9]

$$\frac{\tan\tfrac12(A-B)}{\tan\tfrac12(A+B)} = \frac{\tan\tfrac12(a-b)} {\tan\tfrac12(a+b)}.$$

:::

## History {#History}

The law of tangents was discovered by the Persian mathematician [Abu al-Wafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_al-Wafa) in the 10th century.[^o11]

[Ibn Muʿādh al-Jayyānī](wikipedia://en/6328738) also described the law of tangents for planar triangles in the 11th century.[^o12]

The law of tangents for spherical triangles was described in the 13th century by [Persian mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_in_medieval_Islam) [Nasir al-Din al-Tusi](wikipedia://en/33642424) (1201–1274), who also presented the law of sines for plane triangles in his five-volume work *Treatise on the Quadrilateral*.[^o12][^o13]

## Cyclic quadrilateral {#Cyclic_quadrilateral}

A generalization of the law of tangents holds for a [cyclic quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/194131) $\square ABCD.$ Denote the lengths of sides $|AB| = a,$ $|BC| = b,$ $|CD| = c,$ and $|DA| = d$ and angle measures $\angle{DAB} = \alpha,$ $\angle{ABC} = \beta$ .Then:[^o15]

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{(a - c)(b - d)}{(a + c)(b + d)} = \frac {\tan\tfrac12(\alpha - \beta)} {\tan\tfrac12(\alpha + \beta)}. \end{aligned}$$

This formula reduces to the law of tangents for a triangle when $c=0$.
