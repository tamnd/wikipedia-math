# Wolfgang Haken

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wolfgang Haken"}

**Wolfgang Haken** (; June 21, 1928 – October 2, 2022) was a [German American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specialized in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), in particular [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Haken was born on June 21, 1928, in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), Germany. His father was Werner Haken, a physicist who had [Max Planck](wikipedia://en/19848) as a doctoral thesis advisor.[^o4] In 1953, Haken earned a Ph.D. degree in mathematics from [Christian-Albrechts-Universität zu Kiel (Kiel University)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel) and married Anna-Irmgard von Bredow, who earned a Ph.D. degree in mathematics from the same university in 1959. In 1962, they left Germany so he could accept a position as visiting professor at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign). He became a full professor in 1965, retiring in 1998.

In 1976, together with colleague [Kenneth Appel](wikipedia://en/311544) at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign), Haken proved the [four-color theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-color_theorem): they proved that any planar graph can be [properly colored](wikipedia://en/426743) using at most four colors. Haken has introduced several ideas, including [Haken manifolds](wikipedia://en/236567), [Kneser-Haken finiteness](wikipedia://en/1018257#Foundational_results), and an expansion of the work of [Kneser](wikipedia://en/1368525) into a theory of [normal surfaces](wikipedia://en/3141418). Much of his work has an algorithmic aspect, and he is a figure in [algorithmic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_topology). One of his key contributions to this field is an algorithm to detect whether a knot is unknotted.

In 1978, Haken delivered an [invited address at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_address_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in Helsinki.[^o5] He was a recipient of the 1979 [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for his proof with Appel of the [four-color theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-color_theorem).[^o6]

Haken died in [Champaign, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign,_Illinois), on October 2, 2022, aged 94.[^o7]

## Family {#Family}

Haken's eldest son, Armin, proved that there exist [propositional](wikipedia://en/1557634) [tautologies](wikipedia://en/4495335) that require [resolution](wikipedia://en/2724082) proofs of exponential size.[^o9] Haken's eldest daughter, [Dorothea Blostein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothea_Blostein), is a professor of computer science, known for her discovery of the [master theorem for divide-and-conquer recurrences](wikipedia://en/561585). Haken’s second son, Lippold, is the inventor of the [Continuum Fingerboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuum_Fingerboard). Haken’s youngest son, Rudolf, is a professor of music, who established the world's first [Electric Strings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_Violin) university degree program at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign).[^o10] Wolfgang is the cousin of [Hermann Haken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Haken), a physicist known for laser theory and synergetics.
