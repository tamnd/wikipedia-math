# Walter Purkert

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Walter Purkert"}

**Walter Purkert** (born 22 January 1944 in [Trautenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trutnov)) is a German mathematician and historian of mathematics.[^o2]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Purkert received his doctorate ([*Promotion*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate#Germany)) in 1972 from [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University). His thesis *Die Entwicklung des abstrakten Körperbegriffs* (The development of the abstract concept of the [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) called a [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28algebra%29)) was supervised by [Hans Wußing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Wußing).[^o4] From 1972 to 1979 Purkert was employed in the mathematics section of Leipzig University's mathematics/natural sciences department. In the mathematics section of Leipzig University's department of the history of medicine and the natural sciences, he was from 1979 to 1987 a lecturer (*Dozent*), from 1987 to 1991 a professor extraordinarius, and from 1988 to 1990 the director of the mathematics section.[^o5]

Purkert was a visiting professor in the summer of 1988 at [Pace University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pace_University) and for the academic year 1992–1993 at the [University of Wuppertal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wuppertal). He has been a research assistant and professor at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) since the mid-1990s. There he was coordinating editor of the *Hausdorff Edition*.[^o5] This annotated edition of the collected works of [Felix Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/10989) includes the philosophical and literary writings published under Hausdorff's pseudonym Paul Mongré. The leading editorial committee had five members including, besides Purkert, [Egbert Brieskorn](wikipedia://en/34748690) (the project initiator),[^o6] [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495), [Reinhold Remmert](wikipedia://en/856331), and [Erhard Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erhard_Scholz). There is a first edition done by editors from Germany and four other countries. There is also a supplemented edition. More than twenty mathematicians, historians, philosophers and literary scholars worked together. The edition was carried out as a long-term project by the *Nordrhein-Westfälische Akademie der Wissenschaften und der Künste* (North Rhine-Westphalia Academy for Sciences and Arts). The edition comprises 10 separate volumes (IA, IB, and II through IX). The volumes were published between 2001 and 2020 by [Springer Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Verlag).[^o7] Purkert completed (during 2013–2018) the biography of Hausdorff (Volume IB of the edition) begun by Egbert Brieskorn (1936–2013) and also edited Hausdorff's correspondence (Volume IX of the edition).[^o2]

Purkert was the co-author, with Hans-Joachim Ilgauds, of a well-regarded biography of [Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216). Purkert also edited [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887)'s 1891–1892 lectures in Göttingen on Riemann surfaces.[^o5] In addition to his historical and editorial research, Purkert published mathematical research on [stochastic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_analysis).[^o2]

## Honors {#Honors}

In 2005 Purkert gave the historical lecture *Felix Hausdorff - Mathematiker, Philosoph und Literat*, which was part of the events accompanying the [Euler Lecture](wikipedia://en/75774672).[^o9] He was elected in 2007 a corresponding member of the [*Académie Internationale d’Histoire des Sciences*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_the_History_of_Science), Paris and in 2015 a member of the Bernoulli-Euler-Gesellschaft, Basel.[^o5]
