# Squircle

*Image: Superellipse chamfered square.svg.* Squircle centred on the origin ($a = b = 0$) with minor radius $r =\,1$: $x^{4} + y^{4} = 1$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Squircle"}

A **squircle** is a [shape](wikipedia://en/169191) intermediate between a [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) and a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220). There are at least two definitions of "squircle" in use, one based on the [superellipse](wikipedia://en/52211), the other arising from work in [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics). The word "squircle" is a [portmanteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau) of the words "square" and "circle". Squircles have been applied in [design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design) and [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics).

:::

## Superellipse-based squircle {#Superellipse-based_squircle}

In a [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706), the [superellipse](wikipedia://en/52211) is defined by the equation

$$\left|\frac{x - a}{r_a}\right|^n + \left|\frac{y - b}{r_b}\right|^n = 1,$$

where $r_{a}$ and $r_{b}$ are the [semi-major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-major_axis) and [semi-minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-minor_axis) axes, $a$ and $b$ are the $x$ and $y$ coordinates of the centre of the ellipse, and $n$ is a positive number. The prototypical squircle is then defined as the superellipse where $r_{a} = r_{b}$ and $n = 4$. Its equation is:

$$\left|x - a\right|^4 + \left|y - b\right|^4 = r^4$$

where $r$ is the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of the squircle. Compare this to the [equation of a circle](wikipedia://en/6220#Equations). When the squircle is centred at the origin, then $a = b = 0$, and it is called [Lamé's special quartic](wikipedia://en/959465).

The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) inside this squircle can be expressed in terms of the [beta function](wikipedia://en/245660) $B$ or the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) $\Gamma$ as[^o3]

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Area} &= 4 \int_0^r \sqrt[4]{r^4-x^4} \, dx \\ &= 4r\int_0^r \sqrt[4]{1-\frac{x^4}{r^4}} \, dx \\ &= 4r^2 \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt[4]{1-u^4} \, du \\ &= \sqrt[4]{4}r^2 \int_0^\frac14 v^{-\frac34}\sqrt[4]{1-4v} \, dv \\ &= r^2 \int_0^1 (1-w)^\frac14 w^{-\frac34} \, dw \\ &= r^2 \int_0^1 (1-w)^{\frac54-1} w^{\frac14-1} \, dw \\ &= r^2 \cdot \operatorname{\mathrm{B}}\left(\tfrac14,\tfrac54\right) \\ &= 4r^2 \frac{\left(\operatorname{\Gamma} \left(1+\frac14\right)\right)^2}{\operatorname{\Gamma} \left(1+\frac24\right)} \\ &= \frac{8r^2 \left(\operatorname{\Gamma} \left(\frac54\right)\right)^2 }{\sqrt\pi} \\ &= \varpi \sqrt{2} r^2 \approx 3.708149 r^2, \end{aligned}$$

where $r$ is the radius of the squircle, and $\varpi$ is the [lemniscate constant](wikipedia://en/2079065).

### $p$-norm notation {#-norm_notation}

In terms of the [$p$-norm](wikipedia://en/45194#The_p-norm_in_finite_dimensions) $\| \cdot \|_{p}$ on $\mathbb{R}^{2}$, the squircle can be expressed as:

$$\left\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_c\right\|_p = r$$

where $p = 4$, $\mathbf{x}_{c} = (a, b)$ is the vector denoting the centre of the squircle, and $\mathbf{x} = (x, y)$. Effectively, this is still a "circle" of points at a distance $r$ from the centre, but distance is defined differently. For comparison, the usual circle is the case $p = 2$, whereas the square is given by the $p \to \infty$ case (the [supremum norm](wikipedia://en/349755)), and a rotated square is given by $p = 1$ (the [taxicab norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab_norm)). This allows a straightforward generalization to a **spherical cube** (**sphube**), in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$, or **hypersphube** in higher dimensions.[^o5] Different values of $p$ may be used for a more general squircle, from which an analog to trigonometry ("[squigonometry](wikipedia://en/77345250)") has been developed.

## Fernández-Guasti squircle {#Fernández-Guasti_squircle}

Another squircle comes from work in optics.[^o8][^o9] It may be called the Fernández-Guasti squircle or FG squircle, after one of its authors, to distinguish it from the superellipse-related squircle above.[^o5] This kind of squircle, centered at the origin, is defined by the equation

$$x^2 + y^2 - \frac{s^2}{r^2} x^2 y^2 = r^2$$

where $r$ is the radius of the squircle, $s$ is the squareness parameter, and $x$ and $y$ are in the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) −*r*, *r*. If $s = 0$, the equation is a circle; if $s = 1$, it is a square. This equation allows a smooth [parametrization](wikipedia://en/453198) of the transition to a square from a circle, without invoking [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824).

### Polar form {#Polar_form}

The FG squircle's radial distance $\rho$ from center to edge can be described parametrically in terms of the circle radius and rotation angle:[^o11]

$$\rho = \frac{r\sqrt{2}}{s|\sin{2\theta}|} \sqrt{1-\sqrt{1-s^2 \sin^2{2\theta}}}$$

In practice, when plotting on a computer, a small value like 0.001 can be added to the angle argument $2\theta$ to avoid the [indeterminate form](wikipedia://en/366723) $\tfrac{0}{0}$ when $\theta = \tfrac{n\pi}{2}$ for any integer $n$, or one can set $\rho = r$ for these cases.

### Linearizing squareness {#Linearizing_squareness}

The squareness parameter $s$ in the FG squircle, while bounded between 0 and 1, results in a nonlinear interpolation of the squircle "corner" between the inner circle and the square corner. If $s_{L}$ is the intended linearly-interpolated position of the corner, the following relationship converts $s_{L}$ to $s$ for use in the squircle formula to obtain correctly interpolated squircles:[^o11]

$$s = 2 \frac{\sqrt{\left(3-2\sqrt{2}\right)s_\mathrm{L}^2-2\left(1-\sqrt{2}\right)s_\mathrm{L}}}{(1-\left(1-\sqrt{2}\right)s_\mathrm{L})^2}$$

:::

## Periodic squircle {#Periodic_squircle}

Another type of squircle arises from [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261).[^o14] This type of squircle is periodic in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ and has the equation

$$\cos\left(\frac{s \pi x}{2 r}\right) \cos\left(\frac{s \pi y}{2 r}\right) = \cos\left(\frac{s \pi }{2 }\right)$$

where $r$ is the minor radius of the squircle, $s$ is the squareness parameter, and $x$ and $y$ are in the interval −*r*, *r*. As $s$ approaches 0 in the [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602), the equation becomes a circle. When $s = 1$, the equation is a square.[^o15]

## Similar shapes {#Similar_shapes}

*Image: squircle rounded square.svg.* A squircle (blue) compared with a rounded square (red).

### Rounded square {#Rounded_square}

A shape similar to a squircle, called a *rounded square*, may be generated by separating four quarters of a circle and connecting their loose ends with straight [lines](wikipedia://en/946975), or by separating the four sides of a square and connecting them with quarter-circles. Such a shape is very similar but not identical to the squircle. Although constructing a rounded square may be conceptually and physically simpler, the squircle has a simpler equation and can be generalised much more easily. One consequence of this is that the squircle and other superellipses can be scaled up or down quite easily. This is useful where, for example, one wishes to create nested squircles.

### Truncated circle {#Truncated_circle}

*Image: Truncated circles.svg.* Various forms of a truncated circle

Another similar shape is a *[truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) circle*, the boundary of the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of the regions enclosed by a square and by a concentric circle whose [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) is both greater than the length of the side of the square and less than the length of the diagonal of the square (so that each figure has interior points that are not in the interior of the other). Such shapes lack the tangent continuity possessed by both superellipses and rounded squares.

### Rounded cube {#Rounded_cube}

A *rounded cube* can be defined in terms of [superellipsoids](wikipedia://en/19681409).

::: {#o22 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Sphube"}

### Sphube {#Sphube}

Similar to the name *squircle*, a *sphube* is a portmanteau of 'sphere' and 'cube'. It is the three-dimensional counterpart to the squircle. The equation for the FG-squircle in three dimensions is:[^o11]

$$x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - \frac{s^2}{r^2}\left(x^2 y^2 + y^2 z^2 + x^2 z^2 - \frac{s^2}{r^2}x^2 y^2 z^2\right) = r^2$$

In polar coordinates, the sphube is expressed parametrically as

$$\begin{aligned} x &= \frac{r \cos\theta\ \cos\phi}{\sqrt{1-s\cos^2\theta\sin^2\phi - s\sin^2\theta}}\\ y &= \frac{r \cos\theta\ \sin\phi}{\sqrt{1-s\cos^2\theta\cos^2\phi - s\sin^2\theta}}\\ z &= \frac{r \sin\theta}{\sqrt{1 - s\cos^2\theta}} \end{aligned}$$

While the squareness parameter $s$ in this case does not behave identically to its squircle counterpart, nevertheless the surface is a sphere when $s$ equals 0, and approaches a cube with sharp corners as $s$ approaches 1.[^o11]

## Uses {#Uses}

*Image: Squircle dishes.jpg.* Squircle-shaped porcelain dishes

Squircles are useful in [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics). If light is passed through a two-dimensional square aperture, the central spot in the [diffraction](wikipedia://en/8603) pattern can be closely modelled by a squircle or supercircle. If a rectangular aperture is used, the spot can be approximated by a [superellipse](wikipedia://en/52211).[^o9]

Squircles have also been used to construct [dinner plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_%28dishware%29). A squircular plate has a larger area (and can thus hold more food) than a circular one with the same radius, but still occupies the same amount of space in a rectangular or square cupboard.[^o25]

Many [Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia) phone models have been designed with a squircle-shaped touchpad button,[^o26][^o27] as was the second generation [Microsoft Zune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zune_Pad).[^o28] [Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc) uses an approximation of a squircle (actually a quintic superellipse) for icons in [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS), [iPadOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPadOS), [macOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS), and the home buttons of some Apple hardware.[^o29] One of the shapes for adaptive icons introduced in the [Android "Oreo"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_Oreo) operating system is a squircle.[^o30] [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung) uses squircle-shaped icons in their Android software overlay [One UI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_UI), and in [Samsung Experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_Experience) and [TouchWiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TouchWiz).[^o31]

Italian car manufacturer [Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat) used numerous squircles in the interior and exterior design of the third generation [Panda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_Panda).[^o32]
