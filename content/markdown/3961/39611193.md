# Simplicial presheaf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simplicial presheaf"}

In mathematics, more specifically in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), a **simplicial presheaf** is a [presheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presheaf) on a [site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site_%28mathematics%29) (e.g., the [category](wikipedia://en/5869) of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450)) taking values in [simplicial sets](wikipedia://en/1027784) (i.e., a [contravariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) from the site to the category of simplicial sets). Equivalently, a simplicial presheaf is a [simplicial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_object) in the category of presheaves on a site. The notion was introduced by [A. Joyal](wikipedia://en/9157736) in the 1970s.[^o2] Similarly, a **simplicial sheaf** on a site is a simplicial object in the category of [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) on the site.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Example: Consider the [étale site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_site) of a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *S*. Each *U* in the site represents the presheaf $\operatorname{Hom}(-, U)$. Thus, a **simplicial scheme**, a simplicial object in the site, represents a simplicial presheaf (in fact, often a simplicial sheaf).

Example: Let *G* be a presheaf of [groupoids](wikipedia://en/12543). Then taking [nerves](wikipedia://en/5127933) section-wise, one obtains a simplicial presheaf $BG$. For example, one might set $B\operatorname{GL} = \varinjlim B\operatorname{GL_n}$. These types of examples appear in [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748).

If $f: X \to Y$ is a [local weak equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_weak_equivalence) of simplicial presheaves, then the induced map $\mathbb{Z} f: \mathbb{Z} X \to \mathbb{Z} Y$ is also a local weak equivalence.

:::

## Homotopy sheaves of a simplicial presheaf {#Homotopy_sheaves_of_a_simplicial_presheaf}

Let *F* be a simplicial presheaf on a site. The **homotopy sheaves** $\pi_* F$ of *F* are defined as follows. For any $f:X \to Y$ in the site and a 0-simplex *s* in *F*(*X*), set $(\pi_0^\text{pr} F)(X) = \pi_0 (F(X))$ and $(\pi_i^\text{pr} (F, s))(f) = \pi_i (F(Y), f^*(s))$. We then set $\pi_i F$ to be the sheaf associated with the pre-sheaf $\pi_i^\text{pr} F$.

## Model structures {#Model_structures}

The category of simplicial presheaves on a site admits several different [model structures](wikipedia://en/2895304).

Some of them are obtained by viewing simplicial presheaves as functors

$$S^{op} \to \Delta^{op} Sets$$

The category of such functors is endowed with (at least) three model structures, namely the [projective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_and_projective_model_structure), the [Reedy](wikipedia://en/79565824), and the [injective model structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_and_projective_model_structure). The weak equivalences / fibrations in the first are maps

$$\mathcal F \to \mathcal G$$

such that

$$\mathcal F(U) \to \mathcal G(U)$$

is a weak equivalence / fibration of simplicial sets, for all *U* in the site *S*. The injective model structure is similar, but with weak equivalences and cofibrations instead.

## Stack {#Stack}

A simplicial presheaf *F* on a site is called a stack if, for any *X* and any [hypercovering](wikipedia://en/54077383) *H* →*X*, the canonical map

$$F(X) \to \operatorname{holim} F(H_n)$$

is a [weak equivalence](wikipedia://en/34615176) as simplicial sets, where the right is the [homotopy limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_limit) of

$$[n] = \{ 0, 1, \dots, n \} \mapsto F(H_n).$$

Any sheaf *F* on the site can be considered as a stack by viewing $F(X)$ as a constant simplicial set; this way, the category of sheaves on the site is included as a subcategory to the homotopy category of simplicial presheaves on the site. The inclusion functor has a left adjoint and that is exactly $F \mapsto \pi_0 F$.

If *A* is a sheaf of abelian group (on the same site), then we define $K(A, 1)$ by doing classifying space construction levelwise (the notion comes from the [obstruction theory](wikipedia://en/2179639)) and set $K(A, i) = K(K(A, i-1), 1)$. One can show (by induction): for any *X* in the site,

$$\operatorname{H}^i(X; A) = [X, K(A, i)]$$

where the left denotes a sheaf cohomology and the right the homotopy class of maps.
