# Genetic algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Genetic algebra"}

In mathematical genetics, a **genetic algebra** is a (possibly [non-associative](wikipedia://en/6533841)) algebra used to model inheritance in genetics. Some variations of these algebras are called **train algebras**, **special train algebras**, **gametic algebras**, **Bernstein algebras**, **copular algebras**, **[zygotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygote) algebras**, and **baric algebras** (also called **weighted algebra**). The study of these algebras was started by (txt).

:::

In applications to genetics, these algebras often have a basis corresponding to the genetically different [gametes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete), and the [structure constants](wikipedia://en/20285142) of the algebra encode the probabilities of producing offspring of various types. The laws of inheritance are then encoded as algebraic properties of the algebra.

For surveys of genetic algebras see (Bertrand 1966), (Wörz-Busekros 1980) and (Reed 1997).

## Baric algebras {#Baric_algebras}

Baric algebras (or weighted algebras) were introduced by (Etherington 1939). A baric algebra over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* is a possibly non-associative algebra over *K* together with a homomorphism *w*, called the weight, from the algebra to *K*.[^o8]

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Bernstein algebra"}

## Bernstein algebras {#Bernstein_algebras}

A Bernstein algebra, based on the work of (txt) on the [Hardy–Weinberg law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Weinberg_law) in genetics, is a (possibly non-associative) baric algebra *B* over a field *K* with a weight homomorphism *w* from *B* to *K* satisfying $(x^2)^2 = w(x)^2 x^2$. Every such algebra has idempotents *e* of the form $e = a^2$ with $w(a)=1$. The [Peirce decomposition](wikipedia://en/30189901) of *B* corresponding to *e* is

$$B = Ke \oplus U_e \oplus Z_e$$

where $U_e = \{ a \in \ker w : ea = a/2 \}$ and $Z_e = \{ a \in \ker w : ea = 0 \}$. Although these subspaces depend on *e*, their dimensions are invariant and constitute the *type* of *B*. An *exceptional* Bernstein algebra is one with $U_e^2 = 0$.[^o12]

:::

## Evolution algebras {#Evolution_algebras}

An *evolution algebra* over a field is an algebra with a basis on which multiplication is defined by the product of distinct basis terms being zero and the square of each basis element being a [linear form](wikipedia://en/214137) in basis elements. A *real* evolution algebra is one defined over the reals: it is *non-negative* if the structure constants in the linear form are all non-negative.[^o17] An evolution algebra is necessarily commutative and [flexible](wikipedia://en/36035264) but not necessarily associative or [power-associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-associative).[^o18]

:::

## Gametic algebras {#Gametic_algebras}

A *gametic algebra* is a finite-dimensional real algebra for which all structure constants lie between 0 and 1.[^o20]

## Genetic algebras {#Genetic_algebras}

Genetic algebras were introduced by (Schafer 1949) who showed that special train algebras are genetic algebras and genetic algebras are train algebras.

## Special train algebras {#Special_train_algebras}

Special train algebras were introduced by (Etherington 1939, section 4) as special cases of baric algebras.

A special train algebra is a baric algebra in which the kernel *N* of the [weight function](wikipedia://en/245523) is nilpotent and the principal powers of *N* are ideals.[^o8]

(Etherington 1941) showed that special train algebras are train algebras.

## Train algebras {#Train_algebras}

Train algebras were introduced by (Etherington 1939, section 4) as special cases of baric algebras.

Let $c_1, \ldots, c_n$ be elements of the field *K* with $1 + c_1 + \cdots + c_n = 0$. The formal polynomial

$$x^n + c_1 w(x)x^{n-1} + \cdots + c_n w(x)^n$$

is a *train polynomial*. The baric algebra *B* with weight *w* is a train algebra if

$$a^n + c_1 w(a)a^{n-1} + \cdots + c_n w(a)^n = 0$$

for all elements $a \in B$, with $a^k$ defined as principal powers, $(a^{k-1})a$.[^o8][^o28]
