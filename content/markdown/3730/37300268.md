# Epigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Epigroup"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), an **epigroup** is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) in which every element has a power that belongs to a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011). Formally, for all *x* in a semigroup *S*, there exists a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *n* and a subgroup *G* of *S* such that *x*<sup>*n*</sup> belongs to *G*.

:::

Epigroups are known by wide variety of other names, including **quasi-periodic semigroup**, **group-bound semigroup**, completely π-regular semigroup, **strongly π-regular semigroup** (**sπr**[^o2]),[^o3] or just **π-regular semigroup**[^o4] (although the latter is ambiguous).

More generally, in an arbitrary semigroup an element is called *group-bound* if it has a power that belongs to a subgroup.

Epigroups have applications to [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424). Many of their properties are studied in this context.[^o5]

Epigroups were first studied by [Douglas Munn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Douglas_Munn) in 1961, who called them *pseudoinvertible*.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Epigroups are a generalization of [periodic semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_semigroup),[^o9] thus all [finite semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_semigroup) are also epigroups.
- The [class](wikipedia://en/20696) of epigroups also contains all [completely regular semigroups](wikipedia://en/22665441) and all [completely 0-simple semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_0-simple_semigroup).[^o6]
- All epigroups are also [eventually regular semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eventually_regular_semigroup).[^o10] (also known as π-regular semigroups)
- A [cancellative](wikipedia://en/22772051) epigroup is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447).[^o11]
- [Green's relations](wikipedia://en/1192012) *D* and *J* coincide for any epigroup.[^o12]
- If *S* is an epigroup, any [regular](wikipedia://en/6797677) [subsemigroup](wikipedia://en/27799#Subsemigroups_and_ideals) of *S* is also an epigroup.[^o2]
- In an epigroup the [Nambooripad order](wikipedia://en/31464616) (as extended by P.R. Jones) and the [natural partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_partial_order) (of Mitsch) coincide.[^o13]

:::

::: {#o15 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The semigroup of all [square matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_matrices) of a given size over a [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067) is an epigroup.[^o6]
- The multiplicative semigroup of every [semisimple Artinian ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Artinian_ring) is an epigroup.[^o5]
- Any [algebraic semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_semigroup) is an epigroup.

:::

## Structure {#Structure}

By analogy with periodic semigroups, an epigroup *S* is [partitioned](wikipedia://en/340240) in classes given by its [idempotents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent), which act as identities for each subgroup. For each idempotent *e* of *S*, the set: $K_e = \{ x \in S \mid \exists n>0 : x^n \in G_e \}$ is called a *unipotency class* (whereas for periodic semigroups the usual name is torsion class.)[^o6]

Subsemigroups of an epigroup need not be epigroups, but if they are, then they are called subepigroups. If an epigroup *S* has a partition in unipotent subepigroups (i.e. each containing a single idempotent), then this partition is unique, and its components are precisely the unipotency classes defined above; such an epigroup is called *unipotently partitionable*. However, not every epigroup has this property. A simple counterexample is the [Brandt semigroup](wikipedia://en/32042560) with five elements *B<sub>2</sub>* because the unipotency class of its zero element is not a subsemigroup. *B<sub>2</sub>* is actually the quintessential epigroup that is not unipotently partitionable. An epigroup is unipotently partitionable [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it contains no subsemigroup that is an [ideal extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_extension) of a unipotent epigroup by *B<sub>2</sub>*.[^o6]
