# Agrawal's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Agrawal's conjecture"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Agrawal's conjecture**, due to [Manindra Agrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manindra_Agrawal) in 2002,[^o2] forms the basis for the [cyclotomic AKS test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotomic_AKS_test). Agrawal's conjecture states formally:

:::

Let $n$ and $r$ be two [coprime positive integers](wikipedia://en/6556). If

$$(X - 1)^n \equiv X^n - 1 \pmod{n,\, X^r - 1} \,$$

then either $n$ is prime or $n^2 \equiv 1 \pmod r$

## Ramifications {#Ramifications}

If Agrawal's conjecture were true, it would decrease the runtime complexity of the [AKS primality test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AKS_primality_test) from $\tilde O\mathord\left(\log^{6} n\right)$ to $\tilde O\mathord\left(\log^3 n\right)$.

## Truth or falsehood {#Truth_or_falsehood}

The conjecture was formulated by Rajat Bhattacharjee and Prashant Pandey in their 2001 thesis.[^o5] It has been computationally verified for $r < 100$ and $n < 10^{10}$,[^o6] and for $r = 5, n < 10^{11}$.[^o7]

However, a heuristic argument by [Carl Pomerance](wikipedia://en/2134159) and [Hendrik W. Lenstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_W._Lenstra) suggests there are infinitely many counterexamples.[^o8] In particular, the heuristic shows that such counterexamples have asymptotic density greater than $\tfrac{1}{n^{\varepsilon}}$ for any $\varepsilon > 0$.

Assuming Agrawal's conjecture is false by the above argument, Roman B. Popovych conjectures a modified version may still be true:

Let $n$ and $r$ be two coprime positive integers. If

$$(X - 1)^n \equiv X^n - 1 \pmod{n,\, X^r - 1}$$

and

$$(X + 2)^n \equiv X^n + 2 \pmod{n,\, X^r - 1}$$

then either $n$ is prime or $n^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{r}$.[^o9]

## Distributed computing {#Distributed_computing}

Both Agrawal's conjecture and Popovych's conjecture were tested by [distributed computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_computing) project Primaboinca which ran from 2010 to 2020, based on [BOINC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BOINC). The project found no counterexample, searching in $10^{10} < n < 10^{17}$.
