# Wilhelm Windelband

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Wilhelm Windelband"}

**Wilhelm Windelband** (; ; 11 May 1848 – 22 October 1915) was a German [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) of the [Baden school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden_school).

*Image: Windelband grab.JPG.* His grave in Heidelberg

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Windelband was born the son of a [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) state secretary for the [Province of Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brandenburg) in [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam).[^n1] He studied at the [University of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena) in which he attended lectures by [Kuno Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuno_Fischer).[^n1] He later studied in the university of [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) and of [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen).[^n2][^n1] In 1870 he presented his [Dr. phil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) dissertation under the direction of [Hermann Lotze](wikipedia://en/191379), which was entitled *Die Lehren vom Zufall* (*The Theories of Chance*).[^n3] In the following year Windelband served as a [soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldier) in the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War).[^n1] In 1873 he returned to [academia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy) and obtained his [Dr. phil. habil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University), which was entitled *Die Gewissheit der Erkenntnis: eine psychologisch-erkenntnisstheoretische Studie* (*On the Certainty of Knowledge: A Psychological-Epistemological Study*). In 1874 he married Martha Wichgraf, with whom he had four children.[^n1]

In 1876, Windelband became Professor of Inductive Philosophy at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich) in [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland).[^n1] In 1877, he returned to Germany, where he became Professor of Philosophy at the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg). In 1882 he accepted an offer of a post in the then-German [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg), where in 1894/5 and 1897/98 he became its [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29).

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

Windelband is now mainly remembered for the terms [*nomothetic* and *idiographic*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomothetic_and_idiographic), which he introduced during an address which he gave in 1894 upon his installation as the Rector of the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg), the Third Edition of which was subsequently published as a thirty-six page booklet.[^o14][^o15] The terms *nomothetic* and *idiographic* are used in [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) and elsewhere. However, as three authors, Smith (1995), Lamiell (1998) and Robinson (2012), have explained, they are used differently to the ways that Windelband meant.[^o16]

Windelband was a [neo-Kantian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Kantianism) who argued against other contemporary neo-Kantians, maintaining that "to understand [Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant) rightly means to go beyond him". Against his [positivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positivist) contemporaries, Windelband argued that philosophy should engage in humanistic dialogue with the natural sciences rather than uncritically appropriating its methodologies. His interests in [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) and cultural sciences represented an opposition to [psychologism](wikipedia://en/2598499) and [historicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historicism) schools by a critical philosophic system.

Windelband relied in his effort to reach beyond Kant on such philosophers as [Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel](wikipedia://en/12598), [Johann Friedrich Herbart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Friedrich_Herbart), and [Hermann Lotze](wikipedia://en/191379).[^o17] [Heinrich Rickert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Rickert) was closely associated with Windelband. Windelband's disciples were not only noted philosophers, but also [sociologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociologists) like [Max Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Weber) and [theologians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theologians) like [Ernst Troeltsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Troeltsch) and [Albert Schweitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Schweitzer).
