# Strong RSA assumption

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Strong RSA assumption"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), the **strong [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29) assumption** states that the [RSA problem](wikipedia://en/1544741) is intractable even when the solver is allowed to choose the public exponent *e* (for *e* ≥ 3). More specifically, given a modulus *N* of unknown factorization, and a ciphertext *C*, it is infeasible to find any pair (*M*, *e*) such that *C* ≡ *M*<sup>*e*</sup> mod *N*.

:::

The strong RSA assumption was first used for constructing [signature](wikipedia://en/59644) schemes [provably secure](wikipedia://en/1182348) against [existential forgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_forgery) without resorting to the [random oracle model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_oracle_model).
