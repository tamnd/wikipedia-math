# Computational science

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Computational science"}

**Computational science**, also known as **scientific computing**, **technical computing** or **scientific computation** (**SC**), is a division of science, and more specifically the [computer sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), which uses advanced [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing) capabilities to understand and solve complex physical problems in science. While this typically extends into computational specializations, this field of study includes:

:::

- [Algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) ([numerical](wikipedia://en/21506) and non-numerical): [mathematical models](wikipedia://en/20590), [computational models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_model), and [computer simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_simulation) developed to solve [sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciences) (e.g, [physical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_physics), [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_biology), and [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_sociology)), [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), and [humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanities) problems
- [Computer hardware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_hardware) that develops and optimizes the advanced system [hardware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_hardware), [firmware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firmware), [networking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network), and [data management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_management) components needed to solve computationally demanding problems
- The computing infrastructure that supports both the science and engineering problem solving and the developmental computer and [information science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_science)

In practical use, it is typically the application of [computer simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_simulation) and other forms of [computation](wikipedia://en/5926) from [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) and [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392) to solve problems in various scientific disciplines. The field is different from theory and laboratory experiments, which are the traditional forms of science and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering). The scientific computing approach is to gain understanding through the analysis of mathematical models implemented on [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer). Scientists and engineers develop [computer programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programs) and [application software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application_software) that model systems being studied and run these programs with various sets of input parameters. The essence of computational science is the application of numerical algorithms[^o2] and [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833). In some cases, these models require massive amounts of calculations (usually [floating-point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating-point)) and are often executed on [supercomputers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputer) or [distributed computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_computing) platforms.

## The computational scientist {#The_computational_scientist}

*Image: Ways to study a system.png.* Ways to study a system

The term [computational scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_scientist) is used to describe someone skilled in scientific computing. Such a person is usually a scientist, an engineer, or an applied mathematician who applies [high-performance computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_computing) in different ways to advance the state-of-the-art in their respective applied disciplines.

Computational science is now commonly considered a third mode of [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science), complementing and adding to [experimentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimentation)/[observation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation) and [theory](wikipedia://en/30746) (see image).[^o4] Here, one defines a [system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System) as a potential source of data,[^o5] an [experiment](wikipedia://en/59861) as a process of extracting data from a system by exerting it through its inputs[^o6] and a [model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_model) (*M*) for a system (*S*) and an experiment (*E*) as anything to which *E* can be applied in order to answer questions about *S*.[^o7] A computational scientist should be capable of:

- recognizing **complex problems**
- adequately **conceptualizing** the system containing these problems
- designing a framework of algorithms suitable for studying this system: the **simulation**
- choosing a suitable **computing infrastructure** ([parallel computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing)/[grid computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_computing)/[supercomputers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputer))
- hereby, maximizing the **computational power** of the simulation
- assessing to what level the output of the simulation resembles the systems: the model is **validated**
- adjusting the conceptualization of the system accordingly
- repeat the cycle until a suitable level of validation is obtained: the computational scientist trusts that the simulation generates adequately realistic results for the system under the studied conditions

Substantial effort in computational sciences has been devoted to developing algorithms, efficient implementation in programming languages, and validating computational results. A collection of problems and solutions in computational science can be found in Steeb, Hardy, Hardy, and Stoop (2004).[^o8]

Philosophers of science, among them Humphreys[^o9] and Gelfert,[^o10] addressed the question: "to what degree does computational science qualify as science?" They address the general question of epistemology: how does gain insight from such computational science approaches? Tolk[^o11] uses these insights to show the epistemological constraints of computer-based simulation research. As computational science uses mathematical models representing the underlying theory in executable form, in essence, they apply modeling (theory building) and simulation (implementation and execution). While simulation and computational science are our most sophisticated way to express our knowledge and understanding, they also come with all constraints and limits already known for computational solutions.

## Applications of computational science {#Applications_of_computational_science}

Problem domains for computational science/scientific computing include:

### Predictive computational science {#Predictive_computational_science}

Predictive computational science is a scientific discipline concerned with the formulation, calibration, numerical solution, and validation of mathematical models designed to predict specific aspects of physical events, given initial and boundary conditions, and a set of characterizing parameters and associated uncertainties.[^o15] In typical cases, the predictive statement is formulated in terms of probabilities. For example, given a mechanical component and a periodic loading condition, "the probability is (say) 90% that the number of cycles at failure (Nf) will be in the interval N1\<Nf\<N2".[^o16]

### Urban complex systems {#Urban_complex_systems}

Cities are massively [complex systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_system) created by humans, made up of humans, and governed by humans.[^o18] Computational models and simulations are used to study urban development and city dynamics.[^o19][^o20]

### Computational finance {#Computational_finance}

In [financial markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_market), huge volumes of interdependent assets are traded by a large number of interacting market participants in different locations and time zones. Their behavior is complex and the characterization and measurement of the risk inherent to this diverse set of instruments is typically based on complicated [mathematical](wikipedia://en/20590) and [computational models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_model). Solving these models exactly, even at a single instrument level, is typically not possible, making the use of [numerical algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_algorithm) necessary. This has become even more urgent and complex recently, as the credit crisis has clearly demonstrated the role of cascading effects going from single instruments through portfolios of single institutions to even the interconnected trading network. Understanding this requires a multi-scale and holistic approach where interdependent risk factors such as market, credit, and liquidity risk are modeled simultaneously and at different interconnected scales.

### Computational biology {#Computational_biology}

New developments in [biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology) are now revolutionizing biology and [biomedical research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_research). Examples of these techniques are [high-throughput sequencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_sequencing), high-throughput [quantitative PCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_polymerase_chain_reaction), intra-cellular imaging, [in-situ hybridization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_situ_hybridization) of gene expression, three-dimensional imaging techniques like [Light Sheet Fluorescence Microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_sheet_fluorescence_microscopy), and [Optical Projection (micro)-Computer Tomography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_projection_tomography). Given the massive amounts of complicated data that is generated by these techniques, their meaningful interpretation, and even their storage, form major challenges calling for new approaches. Going beyond current bioinformatics approaches, [computational biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_biology) needs to develop new methods to discover meaningful patterns in these large data sets. Model-based reconstruction of [gene networks](wikipedia://en/356382) can be used to organize the gene expression data in a systematic way and to guide further data collection. A major challenge here is to understand how gene regulation is controlling fundamental biological processes like [biomineralization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomineralization) and [embryogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryogenesis). The sub-processes like [gene regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_regulation), [organic molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_compound) interacting with the mineral deposition process, [cellular processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28biology%29), [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology), and other processes at the tissue and environmental levels are linked. Rather than being directed by a central control mechanism, biomineralization and embryogenesis can be viewed as an emergent behavior resulting from a complex system, in which several sub-processes on very different [temporal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporal_scales) and [spatial scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_scale) (ranging from nanometer and nanoseconds to meters and years) are connected into a multi-scale system. One of the few available options to understand such systems is by developing a [multi-scale model](wikipedia://en/4003614) of the system.

### Complex systems theory {#Complex_systems_theory}

Using [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), [non-equilibrium dynamics](wikipedia://en/472429), and explicit simulations, computational systems theory tries to uncover the true nature of [complex adaptive systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_adaptive_system).

### Computational science and engineering {#Computational_science_and_engineering}

Computational science and engineering (CSE) is a relatively new discipline that deals with the development and application of computational models and simulations, often coupled with [high-performance computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_computing), to solve complex physical problems arising in engineering analysis and design (computational engineering) as well as natural phenomena (computational science). CSE has become accepted amongst scientists, engineers and academics as the "third mode of discovery" (next to theory and experimentation).[^o25] In many fields, computer simulation is integral and therefore essential to business and research. Computer simulation provides the capability to enter fields that are either inaccessible to traditional experimentation or where carrying out traditional empirical inquiries is prohibitively expensive. CSE should neither be confused with pure [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), nor with [computer engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_engineering), although a wide domain in the former is used in CSE (e.g., certain algorithms, data structures, parallel programming, high-performance computing), and some problems in the latter can be modeled and solved with CSE methods (as an application area).

## Methods and algorithms {#Methods_and_algorithms}

Algorithms and mathematical methods used in computational science are varied. They are usually categorized as [Computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805),[^o27][^o28][^o29][^o30] including [symbolic computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_computation) in fields such as statistics, [equation solving](wikipedia://en/537048), algebra, calculus, geometry, linear algebra, [tensor analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_analysis) (multilinear algebra), [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization); or [Numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506),[^o31][^o32][^o33][^o34] including:

- [Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing) derivatives by [finite differences](wikipedia://en/39147)
- Application of [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) as convergent and asymptotic series
- [Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing) derivatives by [Automatic differentiation](wikipedia://en/734787) (AD)
- [Finite element method](wikipedia://en/18233581) for solving PDEs[^o35][^o36]
- High order difference approximations via [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) and [Richardson extrapolation](wikipedia://en/650022)
- [Methods of integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methods_of_integration)[^o37] on a uniform [mesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesh_%28mathematics%29): [rectangle rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectangle_rule) (also called *midpoint rule*), [trapezoid rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapezoid_rule), [Simpson's rule](wikipedia://en/219962)
- [Runge–Kutta methods](wikipedia://en/213671) for solving ordinary differential equations
- [Newton's method](wikipedia://en/22145)[^o38]
- [Discrete Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/8811)
- [Monte Carlo methods](wikipedia://en/56098)[^o39][^o40]
- [Numerical linear algebra](wikipedia://en/7330660),[^o41][^o42][^o43] including decompositions and [eigenvalue algorithms](wikipedia://en/516560)
- [Linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730)[^o44][^o45]
- [Branch and cut](wikipedia://en/3237914)
- [Branch and bound](wikipedia://en/456580)
- [Molecular dynamics](wikipedia://en/198608), [Car–Parrinello molecular dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car–Parrinello_molecular_dynamics)
- [Space mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_mapping)
- [Time stepping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_stepping) methods for dynamical systems

Historically and today, Fortran remains popular for most applications of scientific computing.[^o46][^o47] Other [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015) and [computer algebra systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_algebra_systems) commonly used for the more mathematical aspects of scientific computing applications include [GNU Octave](wikipedia://en/48707), [Haskell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskell_%28programming_language%29),[^o46] [Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_%28programming_language%29),[^o46] [Maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_%28software%29),[^o47] [Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica),[^o48][^o49][^o50][^o51][^o52] [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412),[^o53][^o54][^o55] [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) (with third-party [SciPy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SciPy) library[^o56][^o57][^o58]), [Perl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perl) (with third-party [PDL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perl_Data_Language) library), [R](wikipedia://en/376707),[^o59] [Scilab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scilab),[^o60][^o61] and [TK Solver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TK_Solver). The more computationally intensive aspects of scientific computing will often use some variation of [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29) or [Fortran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran) and optimized algebra libraries such as [BLAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLAS) or [LAPACK](wikipedia://en/1013768). In addition, [parallel computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computing) is heavily used in scientific computing to find solutions of large problems in a reasonable amount of time. In this framework, the problem is either divided over many cores on a single CPU node (such as with [OpenMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenMP)), divided over many CPU nodes networked together (such as with [MPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Message_Passing_Interface)), or is run on one or more [GPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphics_processing_unit) (typically using either [CUDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUDA) or [OpenCL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenCL)).

Computational science application programs often model real-world changing conditions, such as weather, airflow around a plane, automobile body distortions in a crash, the motion of stars in a galaxy, an explosive device, etc. Such programs might create a 'logical mesh' in computer memory where each item corresponds to an area in space and contains information about that space relevant to the model. For example, in [weather models](wikipedia://en/1505381), each item might be a square kilometer; with land elevation, current wind direction, humidity, temperature, pressure, etc. The program would calculate the likely next state based on the current state, in simulated time steps, solving differential equations that describe how the system operates, and then repeat the process to calculate the next state.

## Conferences and journals {#Conferences_and_journals}

In 2001, the *International Conference on Computational Science (ICCS)* was first organized. Since then, it has been organized yearly. ICCS is an A-rank conference in the [CORE ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CORE_ranking).[^o64]

The *Journal of Computational Science* published its first issue in May 2010.[^o65][^o66][^o67] The *Journal of Open Research Software* was launched in 2012*.*[^o68] The [ReScience C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ReScience_C) initiative, which is dedicated to replicating computational results, was started on [GitHub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GitHub) in 2015.[^o69]

## Education {#Education}

At some institutions, a specialization in scientific computation can be earned as a "minor" within another program (which may be at varying levels). However, there are increasingly many [bachelor's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree), [master's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree), and [doctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_degree) programs in computational science. The joint degree program master program computational science at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) and the [Vrije Universiteit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrije_Universiteit_Amsterdam) in computational science was first offered in 2004. In this program, students:

- learn to build computational models from real-life observations;
- develop skills in turning these models into computational structures and in performing large-scale simulations;
- learn theories that will give a firm basis for the analysis of complex systems;
- learn to analyze the results of simulations in a virtual laboratory using advanced numerical algorithms.

[ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) offers a bachelor's and master's degree in Computational Science and Engineering. The degree equips students with the ability to understand scientific problem and apply numerical methods to solve such problems. The directions of specializations include Physics, Chemistry, Biology and other Scientific and Engineering disciplines.

The Ph.D. in Scientific Computing at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) was established in 1988.[^o71] [George Mason University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_University) has offered a multidisciplinary doctorate Ph.D. program in Computational Sciences and Informatics starting from 1992.[^o72]

The School of Computational and Integrative Sciences, [Jawaharlal Nehru University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru_University) (erstwhile School of Information Technology[^o73]) also offers a master's science program for computational science with two specialties: [Computational Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_biology) and [Complex Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_Systems).[^o74]
