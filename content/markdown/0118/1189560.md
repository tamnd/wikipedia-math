# Loop (topology)

*Image: Fundamental group torus2.png.* Two loops $a$, $b$ in a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Loop"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **loop** in a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) $f$ from the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) $I = [0,1]$ to $X$ such that $f(0) = f(1)$. In other words, it is a [path](wikipedia://en/1189553) whose initial point is equal to its terminal point.[^o2]

:::

A loop may also be seen as a continuous map $f$ from the [pointed](wikipedia://en/1236666) [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) $S^{1}$ into $X$, because $S^{1}$ may be regarded as a [quotient](wikipedia://en/237213) of $I$ under the identification of 0 with 1.

The set of all loops in $X$ forms a space called the [loop space](wikipedia://en/2174332) of $X$.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $X$ be a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). A loop is a continuous function $f : [0,1] \to X$ such that $f(0) = f(1)$. If $f$ begins and ends at $x_0 \in X$ the loop is said to be *based at $x_0$*. A loop is then a [path](wikipedia://en/1189553) that begins and ends at the same point $x_0$.[^o5]

The set of homotopy classes of loops based at $x_0$ together with the operation of [path composition](wikipedia://en/1189553#Path_composition), forms the *[fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of $X$ relative to $x_0$*, usually denoted by $\pi_1(X,x_0)$.[^o5]

:::
