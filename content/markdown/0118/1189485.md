# Abu al-Wafa' al-Buzjani

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Abu al-Wafa' al-Buzjani"}

**Abū al-Wafāʾ Muḥammad ibn Muḥammad ibn Yaḥyā ibn Ismāʿīl ibn al-ʿAbbās al-Būzjānī** (ابو الوفاء بوژگانی, ابو الوفاء بوزجانی;[^o3] 10 June 940 – 15 July 998)[^o8] was a [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians)[^o4][^o5][^o6] [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematician) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_astronomy) who worked in [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad). He made important innovations in [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405), and his work on [arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetics) for businessmen contains the first instance of using [negative numbers](wikipedia://en/154616) in a [medieval Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age) text.

:::

He is also credited with compiling the tables of [sines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) and [tangents](wikipedia://en/31482) at 15['](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minute_of_angle) intervals. He also introduced the [secant and cosecant](wikipedia://en/30367#Reciprocal_functions) functions, as well studied the interrelations between the six [trigonometric](wikipedia://en/18717261) lines associated with an arc.[^o8] His *Almagest* was widely read by medieval Arabic astronomers in the centuries after his death. He is known to have written several other books that have not survived.

## Life {#Life}

Abū al-Wafāʾ was born in [Buzhgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzhgan), (now [Torbat-e Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torbat-e_Jam)) in [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan) (in today's Iran). At age 19, in 959, he moved to [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad) and remained there until his death in 998. He was a contemporary of the distinguished scientists [Abū Sahl al-Qūhī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abū_Sahl_al-Qūhī) and [al-Sijzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Sijzi) who were in Baghdad at the time and others such as [Abu Nasr Mansur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Nasr_Mansur), [Abu-Mahmud Khojandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu-Mahmud_Khojandi), [Kushyar Gilani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushyar_Gilani) and [al-Biruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Biruni).[^o9] In Baghdad, he received patronage from members of the [Buyid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyid) court.[^n1]

## Astronomy {#Astronomy}

Abū al-Wafāʾ was the first to build a wall [quadrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrant_%28instrument%29) to observe the sky.[^o9] It has been suggested that he was influenced by the works of [al-Battani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Battani) as the latter described a quadrant instrument in his *Kitāb az-Zīj*.[^o9] His use of the concept of the [tangent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_%28trigonometry%29) helped solve problems involving right-angled [spherical triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_triangle). He developed a new technique to calculate [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) tables, allowing him to construct more accurate tables than his predecessors.[^n1]

In 997, he participated in an experiment to determine the difference in local time between his location, Baghdad, and that of al-Biruni (who was living in Kath, now a part of [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan)).[^o13] The result was very close to present-day calculations, showing a difference of approximately 1 hour between the two longitudes. Abū al-Wafāʾ is also known to have worked with [Abū Sahl al-Qūhī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abū_Sahl_al-Qūhī), who was a famous maker of astronomical instruments.[^n1] While what is extant from his works lacks theoretical innovation, his observational data were used by many later astronomers, including al-Biruni.[^n1]

### *Almagest* {#Almagest}

Among his works on astronomy, only the first seven treatises of his *Almagest* (*Kitāb al-Majisṭī*) are now extant.[^o17] The work covers numerous topics in the fields of plane and [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405), planetary theory, and solutions to determine the direction of [Qibla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qibla).[^o9][^n1]

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

He defined the tangent function, and he established several [trigonometric identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_identity) in their modern form, where the [ancient Greek mathematicians](wikipedia://en/1503369) had expressed the equivalent identities in terms of chords.[^o22] The trigonometric identities he introduced were:

$$\sin(a \pm b) = \sin(a) \cos(b) \pm \cos(a) \sin(b)$$

$$\cos(2 a) = 1 - 2\sin^2(a)$$

$$\sin(2 a) = 2\sin(a) \cos(a)$$

He has discovered the [law of tangents](wikipedia://en/319484)[^o20] and may have discovered the [law of sines](wikipedia://en/113222) for [spherical triangles](wikipedia://en/650405), however, other scholars like [Abu-Mahmud Khojandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu-Mahmud_Khojandi) have been credited with the latter achievement:[^o21]

$$\frac{\sin A}{\sin a} = \frac{\sin B}{\sin b} = \frac{\sin C}{\sin c}$$

where $A, B, C$ are the sides of the triangle (measured in radians on the unit sphere) and $a, b, c$ are the opposing angles.[^o22]

Some sources suggest that he introduced the [tangent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_%28trigonometry%29) function, although other sources give the credit for this innovation to [al-Marwazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habash_al-Hasib_al-Marwazi).[^o22]

## Works {#Works}

- *Almagest* (كتاب المجسطي *Kitāb al-Majisṭī*).
- A book of [zij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zij) called *Zīj al-wāḍiḥ* (زيج الواضح), no longer extant.[^n1]
- "A Book on Those Geometric Constructions Which Are Necessary for a Craftsman", (كتاب في ما یحتاج إليه الصانع من الأعمال الهندسية *Kitāb fī mā yaḥtāj ilayh al-ṣāniʿ min al-aʿmāl al-handasiyya*).[^n2] This text contains over one hundred geometric constructions, including for a regular [heptagon](wikipedia://en/365349), which have been reviewed and compared with other mathematical treatises. The legacy of this text in Latin Europe is still debated.[^n3][^o27]
- "A Book on What Is Necessary from the Science of Arithmetic for Scribes and Businessmen", (كتاب في ما يحتاج إليه الكتاب والعمال من علم الحساب *Kitāb fī mā yaḥtāj ilayh al-kuttāb wa’l-ʿummāl min ʾilm al-ḥisāb*).[^n2] This is the first book where [negative numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_numbers) have been used in the medieval Islamic texts.[^n1]

He also wrote translations and commentaries on the algebraic works of [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110), [al-Khwārizmī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khwārizmī), and [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s *Elements*.[^n1]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

- The crater [Abul Wáfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abul_Wáfa_%28crater%29) on the Moon is named after him.[^o32][^o33]
- On 10 June 2015, [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google) changed its logo in memory of Abū al-Wafāʾ al-Būzjānī.[^o34]
