# William Esson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William Esson"}

**William Esson**, FRS (17 May 1838 – 28 August 1916) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in [Carnoustie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnoustie), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland).[^o4]

Esson attended [St John's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Oxford), [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University).

## Career {#Career}

He then became a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_%28college%29) of [Merton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College).[^o6] In 1892, he became the [Savilian Professor of Geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savilian_Professor_of_Geometry) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), based at [New College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College,_Oxford). He worked on problems in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) with [Augustus George Vernon Harcourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_George_Vernon_Harcourt).

In 1869 he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) and in 1895 delivered, jointly with Harcourt, their Bakerian Lecture *on the Laws of Connexion between the Conditions of a Chemical Change and its Amount. III. Further Researches on the Reaction of Hydrogen Dioxide and Hydrogen Iodide*.[^o7]

He was on the governing body of [Abingdon School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abingdon_School) until 1900.[^o8]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1874, Esson leased 13 [Bradmore Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradmore_Road) in [North Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Oxford).[^o10] He died in [Abingdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abingdon,_Oxfordshire), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England).[^o11]
