# Parallelogon

zonogon

*Image: Parallelogons as 2 or 3 vectors.png.* A parallelogon is constructed by two or three pairs of parallel line segments. The vertices and edges on the interior of the hexagon are suppressed.

*Image: 2d-bravais.svg.* There are five [Bravais lattices](wikipedia://en/661808#Bravais_lattices_in_at_most_two_dimensions) in two dimensions, related to the parallelogon tessellations by their five symmetry variations.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Parallelogon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **parallelogon** is a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) with [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497) opposite sides (hence the name) that can [tile](wikipedia://en/321671) a [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) by [translation](wikipedia://en/204682) ([rotation](wikipedia://en/410009) is not permitted).[^o11][^o2]

:::

Parallelogons have four or six sides, opposite sides that are equal in length, and 180-degree [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096) around the center.[^o11] A four-sided parallelogon is a [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496).

The three-dimensional analogue of a parallelogon is a [parallelohedron](wikipedia://en/25207334). All faces of a parallelohedron are parallelogons.[^o2]

## Two polygonal types {#Two_polygonal_types}

Quadrilateral and hexagonal parallelogons each have varied geometric symmetric forms. They all have [central inversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_inversion) symmetry, order 2. Every convex parallelogon is a [zonogon](wikipedia://en/55312103), but hexagonal parallelogons enable the possibility of nonconvex polygons.
