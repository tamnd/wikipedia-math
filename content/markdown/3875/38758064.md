# Order-3 apeirogonal tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Order-3 apeirogonal tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **order-3 apeirogonal tiling** is a [regular tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_hyperbolic_tiling) of the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/241291). It is represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {∞,3}, having three regular [apeirogons](wikipedia://en/5658261#Hyperbolic_geometry) around each vertex. Each apeirogon is [inscribed](wikipedia://en/161262) in a [horocycle](wikipedia://en/11084460).

:::

The [order-2 apeirogonal tiling](wikipedia://en/35870958) represents an infinite [dihedron](wikipedia://en/6109457) in the Euclidean plane as {∞,2}.

## Images {#Images}

Each [apeirogon](wikipedia://en/5658261) face is [circumscribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumscribe) by a [horocycle](wikipedia://en/11084460), which looks like a circle in a [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836), internally tangent to the projective circle boundary.

*Image: Order-3 apeirogonal tiling one cell horocycle.png.*

The edges of the tiling, shown in blue, form an order-3 [Cayley tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley_tree).

### Symmetry {#Symmetry}

The dual to this tiling represents the fundamental domains of \[(∞,∞,∞)\] (\*∞∞∞) symmetry. There are 15 small index subgroups (7 unique) constructed from \[(∞,∞,∞)\] by mirror removal and alternation. Mirrors can be removed if its branch orders are all even, and cuts neighboring branch orders in half. Removing two mirrors leaves a half-order gyration point where the removed mirrors met. In these images fundamental domains are alternately colored black and white, and mirrors exist on the boundaries between colors. The symmetry can be doubled as [∞∞2 symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ii2_symmetry) by adding a mirror bisecting the fundamental domain. Dividing a fundamental domain by 3 mirrors creates a [∞32 symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I32_symmetry).

A larger subgroup is constructed \[(∞,∞,∞<sup>\*</sup>)\], index 8, as (∞\*∞<sup>∞</sup>) with gyration points removed, becomes (\*∞<sup>∞</sup>).

## Related polyhedra and tilings {#Related_polyhedra_and_tilings}

This tiling is topologically related as a part of sequence of regular polyhedra with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {n,3}.

## Functions on tilings {#Functions_on_tilings}

Functions on this tiling that have symmetry properties tied to it are called [modular functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_function); the [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) are a special case. This is visually evident in the visualizations of Klein's [j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988) on the Poincaré disk, as well as the [Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/1270458).
