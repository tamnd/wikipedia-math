# Golden rectangle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Golden rectangle"}

*Image: SimilarGoldenRectangles.svg.* $\tfrac{a}{b}$ = $\tfrac{a+b}{a}$ = φ.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **golden rectangle** is a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) with side lengths in [aspect ratio](wikipedia://en/1021) $\varphi :1$, the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386), where $\varphi = \tfrac12\bigl(1 + \sqrt5\bigr) \approx {}$$1.618$. That is, one side of the rectangle is $\varphi$ times longer than another.

:::

Golden rectangles exhibit a special form of [self-similarity](wikipedia://en/28782): if a square is added to the long side, or removed from the short side, the result is a golden rectangle as well.

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Owing to the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034), the diagonal dividing one half of a square equals the radius of a circle whose outermost point is the corner of a golden rectangle added to the square.[^o4] Thus, a golden rectangle can be [constructed with only a straightedge and compass](wikipedia://en/61229) in four steps:

1. Draw a square
1. Draw a line from the midpoint of one side of the square to an opposite corner
1. Use that line as the radius to draw an arc that defines the height of the rectangle
1. Complete the golden rectangle

A distinctive feature of this shape is that when a [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) section is added—or removed—the product is another golden rectangle, having the same [aspect ratio](wikipedia://en/1021) as the first. Square addition or removal can be repeated infinitely, in which case corresponding corners of the squares form an infinite sequence of points on the [golden spiral](wikipedia://en/219370), the unique [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373) with this property. Diagonal lines drawn between the first two orders of embedded golden rectangles will define the intersection point of the diagonals of all the embedded golden rectangles; [Clifford A. Pickover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_A._Pickover) referred to this point as "the Eye of God".[^o5]

### Golden whirl {#Golden_whirl}

*Image: Golden\_rectangle\_whirl.svg.* A whirl of golden rectangles.

Divide a square into four [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [right triangles](wikipedia://en/39420) with legs in ratio $1 : 2$ and arrange these in the shape of a golden rectangle, enclosing a [similar](wikipedia://en/39377) rectangle that is scaled by factor $\tfrac{1}{\varphi}$ and rotated about the centre by $\arctan(\tfrac{1}{2}).$ Repeating the construction at successively smaller scales results in four infinite sequences of adjoining right triangles, tracing a [whirl](wikipedia://en/8532654) of converging golden rectangles.[^o7]

The logarithmic spiral through the vertices of adjacent triangles has [polar slope](wikipedia://en/75407072) $k =\frac{\ln( \varphi)}{\arctan( \tfrac{1}{2})} .$ The [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) between the pair of upright grey triangles has perpendicular diagonals in ratio $\varphi$, hence is a [golden rhombus](wikipedia://en/20825292).

If the triangle has legs of lengths $1$ and $2$ then each discrete spiral has length $\varphi^2 =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \varphi^{-n} .$ The areas of the triangles in each spiral region sum to $\varphi =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \varphi^{-2n} ;$ the perimeters are equal to $5 +\sqrt{5}$ (grey) and $4\varphi$ (yellow regions).

:::

## History {#History}

The proportions of the golden rectangle have been claimed for the [Babylonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia) *[Tablet of Shamash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablet_of_Shamash)* (c. 888–855 BC).[^o9] However, [Mario Livio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Livio) calls any knowledge of the golden ratio in ancient Babylon or ancient Egypt "highly unlikely".[^n1]

According to Livio, since the publication of [Luca Pacioli](wikipedia://en/18473)'s *[Divina proportione](wikipedia://en/16175342)* in 1509, "the Golden Ratio started to become available to artists in theoretical treatises that were not overly mathematical, that they could actually use."[^o11]

The 1927 [Villa Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Stein) designed by [Le Corbusier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Corbusier), some of whose [architecture utilizes the golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386#Architecture), features dimensions that closely approximate golden rectangles.[^o12]

## Relation to regular polygons and polyhedra {#Relation_to_regular_polygons_and_polyhedra}

[Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331) gives an alternative construction of the golden rectangle using three polygons [circumscribed](wikipedia://en/161262) by congruent circles: a regular [decagon](wikipedia://en/327393), [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733), and [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014). The respective lengths *a*, *b*, and *c* of the sides of these three polygons satisfy the equation *a*<sup>2</sup> + *b*<sup>2</sup> = *c*<sup>2</sup>, so line segments with these lengths form a [right triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_right_triangles#Sides_of_regular_polygons) (by the converse of the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034)). The ratio of the side length of the hexagon to the decagon is the golden ratio, so this triangle forms half of a golden rectangle.[^o14]

The [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of two opposite edges of a regular [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) forms a golden rectangle. The twelve vertices of the icosahedron can be decomposed in this way into three mutually-perpendicular golden rectangles, whose boundaries are linked in the pattern of the [Borromean rings](wikipedia://en/873118).[^o15]

## Relation to angles of the golden triangle {#Relation_to_angles_of_the_golden_triangle}

*Image: Golden\_rectangle\_segmented.svg.* Powers of $\varphi$ within a golden rectangle.

Assume a golden rectangle has been constructed as indicated above, with height $1$, length $\varphi$ and [diagonal](wikipedia://en/349251) length $\textstyle \sqrt{\varphi^2 + 1}$. The triangles on the diagonal have [altitudes](wikipedia://en/161241) $\textstyle 1 \big/\sqrt{1 + \varphi^{-2}}\,;$ each perpendicular foot divides the diagonal in ratio $\varphi^2.$

If a horizontal line is drawn through the intersection point of the diagonal and the internal [edge](wikipedia://en/12158034) of the square, the original golden rectangle and the two scaled copies along the diagonal have linear sizes in the ratios $\varphi^2 :\varphi :1\,,$ the square and rectangle opposite the diagonal both have areas equal to $\varphi^{-2}.$[^o17] The foot of altitude $V$ coincides with the pole of the [golden spiral](wikipedia://en/219370) and divides the diagonal of the lower right rectangle in ratio $\varphi^2$.

Relative to [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) $A$, the coordinates of feet of altitudes $U$ and $V$ are $\left( \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}, \tfrac{1}{\varphi \sqrt{5}} \right)$ and $\left( \tfrac{\varphi^2}{\sqrt{5}}, \tfrac{\varphi}{\sqrt{5}} \right)$; the length of line segment $\overline{U V}$ is equal to altitude $h.$

If the diagram is further subdivided by perpendicular lines through $U$ and $V$, the lengths of the diagonal and its subsections can be expressed as [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367) of arguments 72 and 36 degrees, the angles of the [golden triangle](wikipedia://en/4525417):

*Image: Pentagon\_segments.svg.* Diagonal segments of the golden rectangle measure nested pentagons. The ratio $\operatorname{AU}:\operatorname{SV}$ is $\varphi^{2}.$

$$\begin{aligned} \overline{A B} + \overline{A S} &=\tan(72^\circ)\\ \overline{A B} = {\textstyle \sqrt{\varphi^2 +1}} &=2\sin(72^\circ)\\ \overline{A V} =\varphi \big/\overline{A S} &=\cot(36^\circ)\\ \overline{A S} ={\textstyle \sqrt{1 +\varphi^{-2}}} &=2\sin(36^\circ)\\ \overline{U V} =1 \big/ \overline{A S} &=\cot(36^\circ) \varphi^{-1}\\ \overline{S B} =\overline{A S} \big/\varphi &=\tan(36^\circ)\\ \overline{U S} =2 \big/(\varphi\overline{A B}) &=2\cot(72^\circ)\\ \overline{A U} =1 \big/\overline{A B} &=\varphi\cot(72^\circ)\\ \overline{U V} - \overline{A U} &=\cot(72^\circ)\\ \overline{S V} =(2 -\varphi) \big/\overline{A B} &=\cot(72^\circ) \varphi^{-1}, \end{aligned}$$

with $\varphi =2\cos(36^\circ).$

Both the lengths of the diagonal sections and the trigonometric values are elements of [biquadratic](wikipedia://en/251591#Biquadratic_equations) [number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) $\textstyle K =\mathbb{Q}\Bigl(\sqrt{\varphi\sqrt{5}}\, \Bigr)$.

The [golden rhombus](wikipedia://en/20825292) with edge $\tfrac{1}{2}$ has diagonal lengths equal to $\overline{U V}$ and $\overline{A U}$. The [regular pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014#Regular_pentagons) with side length $2\varphi^{-1} =\sec(36^\circ)$ has area $5\overline{A U}.$ Its [five diagonals](wikipedia://en/197544#Geometry) divide the pentagon into golden triangles and gnomons, and an upturned, scaled copy at the centre. Since the regular pentagon is defined by its side length and the angles of the golden triangle, it follows that all measures can be expressed in powers of $\varphi$ and the diagonal segments of the golden rectangle, as illustrated above.

*Image: Golden\_pentatonic\_scales.svg.* Intervals on the diagonal of the golden rectangle.

Interpreting the diagonal sections as [musical string lengths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monochord) results in a set of ten corres­ponding [pitches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_%28music%29), one of which doubles at the [octave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave). Mapping the [intervals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_%28music%29) in [logarithmic scale](wikipedia://en/164055) — with the "golden octave" equal to $\varphi^4$ — shows [equally tempered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_temperament) [semitones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitone), [minor thirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_third) and one [major second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_second) in the span of an [eleventh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_%28interval%29). An analysis in musical terms is substantiated by the single exceptional pitch proportional to $\overline{U S}$, that approximates the [harmonic seventh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_seventh) within remarkable one [cent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cent_%28music%29) accuracy.

This set of ten tones can be partitioned into two [modes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mode_%28music%29) of the [pentatonic scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentatonic_scale): the palindromic "Egyptian" mode (red dots, Chinese Ruibin\_diao.ogg [guqin tuning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guqin_tunings)) and the stately "blues minor" mode (blue dots, Chinese Mangong\_diao.ogg tuning).
