# Appeal to fear

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Appeal to fear"}

An **appeal to fear** (also called ***argumentum ad metum*** or ***argumentum in terrorem***) is a [fallacy](wikipedia://en/53986) in which a person attempts to create support for an idea by attempting to increase fear towards an alternative. An appeal to fear is related to the broader strategy of [fear appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear_appeal) and is a common tactic in [marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing), [politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics), and [media (communication)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_%28communication%29).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Logic {#Logic}

This fallacy has the following [argument form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_form):

Either P or Q is true.

Q is frightening.

Therefore, P is true.

The argument is invalid. The [appeal to emotion](wikipedia://en/219718) is used in exploiting existing fears to create support for the speaker's proposal, namely P. Also, often the [false dilemma](wikipedia://en/17687) fallacy is involved, suggesting Q is the proposed idea's sole alternative.[^o5]

## Fear, uncertainty and doubt {#Fear,_uncertainty_and_doubt}

[Fear, uncertainty and doubt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear,_uncertainty_and_doubt) (FUD) is the appeal to fear in [sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales) or [marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing); in which a company disseminates negative (and vague) information on a competitor's product. The term originated to describe misinformation tactics in the [computer hardware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_hardware) industry and has since been used more broadly. FUD is "implicit coercion" by "any kind of [disinformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disinformation) used as a competitive weapon."[^o7] FUD creates a situation in which buyers are encouraged to purchase by [brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_recognition), regardless of the relative technical merits. Opponents of certain large computer corporations state that the spreading of fear, uncertainty, and doubt is an [unethical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unethical) marketing technique that these corporations consciously employ.

## Fear appeal in politics {#Fear_appeal_in_politics}

Fear appeal is a subject studied within political science. Fear surveys indicate that, in specific contexts, messages from pro-authoritarian actors in the media are perceived with fear. Consequently, systems of oppression rely on the cultivation of fear appeals. For instance, the specific case of Belarus illustrates how communication strategies, the institutional framework, and repressive means are closely related and intertwined. At the same time, survey results show that respondents are indeed affected by these fear appeals.[^o9]

## As persuasion {#As_persuasion}

[Fear appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear_appeal) are often used in [marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing) and [social policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_policy), as a method of [persuasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persuasion). Fear is an effective tool to change attitudes,[^o11] which are moderated by the motivation and ability to process the fear message. Examples of fear appeal include reference to [social exclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_exclusion), and getting laid-off from one's job,[^o12] getting cancer from smoking or involvement in car accidents and driving.

Fear appeals are *[nonmonotonic](wikipedia://en/48260)*, meaning that the level of persuasion does not always increase when the claimed danger is increased. A study of public service messages on AIDS found that if the messages were too aggressive or fearful, they were rejected by the subject; a moderate amount of fear is the most effective [attitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attitude_%28psychology%29) changer.[^o12]

Others argue that it is not the level of fear that is decisive changing attitudes via the persuasion process. Rather, as long as a scare-tactics message includes a recommendation to cope with the fear, it can work.[^o13]
