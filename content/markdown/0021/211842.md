# Erdős–Borwein constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erdős–Borwein constant"}

The **Erdős–Borwein constant**, named after [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Peter Borwein](wikipedia://en/1339736), is the sum of the [reciprocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_%28mathematics%29) of the [Mersenne numbers](wikipedia://en/18908).

:::

By definition it is:

$$E=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^n-1}\approx1.606695152415291763\dots$$

## Equivalent forms {#Equivalent_forms}

It can be proven that the following forms all sum to the same constant:

$$E=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n^2}}\frac{2^n+1}{2^n-1}$$

$$E=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^{mn}}$$

$$E=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^n(2^n-1)}$$

$$E=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_0(n)}{2^n}$$

where σ<sub>0</sub>(*n*) = *d*(*n*) is the [divisor function](wikipedia://en/407249), a [multiplicative function](wikipedia://en/20059) that equals the number of positive [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) of the number *n*. To prove the equivalence of these sums, note that they all take the form of [Lambert series](wikipedia://en/1367040) and can thus be resummed as such.[^o4]

## Irrationality {#Irrationality}

In 1948, Erdős showed that the [constant](wikipedia://en/48779551) *E* is an [irrational number](wikipedia://en/20647689).[^o6] Later, Borwein provided an alternative proof.[^o7]

Despite its irrationality, the [binary representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system) of the Erdős–Borwein constant may be calculated efficiently.[^o8][^o9]

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Erdős–Borwein constant comes up in the [average case analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_case_analysis) of the [heapsort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heapsort) algorithm, where it controls the constant factor in the running time for converting an unsorted array of items into a heap.[^o12]

:::
