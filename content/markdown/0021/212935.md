# Measure of non-compactness

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Measure of non-compactness"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), two **measures of non-compactness** are commonly used; these associate numbers to sets in such a way that [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) sets all get the measure 0, and other sets get measures that are bigger according to "how far" they are removed from compactness.

:::

The underlying idea is the following: a bounded set can be covered by a single ball of some radius. Sometimes several balls of a smaller radius can also cover the set. A compact set in fact can be covered by finitely many balls of arbitrary small radius, because it is [totally bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_bounded). So one could ask: what is the smallest radius that allows to cover the set with finitely many balls?

Formally, we start with a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) *M* and a subset *X*. The **ball measure of non-compactness** is defined as

α(*X*) = [inf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum) {*r* > 0 : there exist finitely many balls of radius *r* which cover *X*}

and the **Kuratowski measure of non-compactness** is defined as

β(*X*) = inf {*d* > 0 : there exist finitely many sets of diameter at most *d* which cover *X*}

Since a ball of radius *r* has diameter at most 2*r*, we have α(*X*) ≤ β(*X*) ≤ 2α(*X*).

The two measures α and β share many properties, and we will use γ in the sequel to denote either one of them. Here is a collection of facts:

- *X* is bounded if and only if γ(*X*) \< ∞.
- γ(*X*) = γ(*X*<sup>cl</sup>), where *X*<sup>cl</sup> denotes the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of *X*.
- If *X* is compact, then γ(*X*) = 0. Conversely, if γ(*X*) = 0 and *X* is [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space), then *X* is compact.
- γ(*X* ∪ *Y*) = max(γ(*X*), γ(*Y*)) for any two subsets *X* and *Y*.
- γ is continuous with respect to the [Hausdorff distance](wikipedia://en/429296) of sets.

Measures of non-compactness are most commonly used if *M* is a [normed vector space](wikipedia://en/21538). In this case, we have in addition:

- γ(*aX*) = |*a*| γ(*X*) for any [scalar](wikipedia://en/3588331) *a*
- γ(*X* + *Y*) ≤ γ(*X*) + γ(*Y*)
- γ(conv(*X*)) = γ(*X*), where conv(*X*) denotes the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of *X*

Note that these measures of non-compactness are useless for subsets of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>*n*</sup>: by the [Heine–Borel theorem](wikipedia://en/59595), every bounded closed set is compact there, which means that γ(*X*) = 0 or ∞ according to whether *X* is bounded or not.

Measures of non-compactness are however useful in the study of infinite-dimensional [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), for example. In this context, one can prove that any ball *B* of radius *r* has α(*B*) = *r* and β(*B*) = 2*r*.
