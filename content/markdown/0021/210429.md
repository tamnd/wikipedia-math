# Brook Taylor

the comedy writer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Brook Taylor"}

**Brook Taylor** (18 August 1685 – 29 December 1731) was an English mathematician and barrister best known for several results in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). Taylor's most famous developments are [Taylor's theorem](wikipedia://en/51714) and the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448), essential in the infinitesimal approach of functions in specific points.

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

*Image: Taylor - Methodus incrementorum directa et inversa, 1715 - 811460.tif.* *Methodus incrementorum directa et inversa*, 1715

Brook Taylor was born in [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton,_London) (former [Middlesex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex)). Taylor was the son of John Taylor, MP of [Patrixbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrixbourne), Kent[^o4] and Olivia Tempest, the daughter of John Tempest of Edmonton,[^o5][^o6] and not [Sir Nicholas Tempest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Nicolas_Tempest,_1st_Baronet), Baronet of Durham.[^o7][^o8]

He entered [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), as a [fellow-commoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_commoner) in 1701, and took degrees in [LL.B.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Law) in 1709 and [LL.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LL.D.) in 1714. Taylor studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) under [John Machin](wikipedia://en/274756) and [John Keill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Keill), leading to Taylor obtaining a solution to the problem of "[center of oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_oscillation)". Taylor's solution remained unpublished until May 1714,[^o10] when his claim to [priority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_priority) was disputed by [Johann Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/268886).

Taylor's *Methodus Incrementorum Directa et Inversa* (1715) ("Direct and Indirect Methods of Incrementation") added a new branch to higher mathematics, called "[calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) of [finite differences](wikipedia://en/39147)". Taylor used this development to determine the form of movement in vibrating strings. Taylor also wrote the first satisfactory investigation of [astronomical refraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_refraction).[^o11][^o12] The same work contains the well-known [Taylor's theorem](wikipedia://en/51714), the importance of which remained unrecognized until 1772, when [Joseph-Louis Lagrange](wikipedia://en/87793) realized its usefulness and termed it "the main foundation of differential calculus".[^o13][^o14]

In Taylor's 1715 essay *Linear Perspective*, Taylor set forth the principles of perspective in a more understandable form, but the work suffered from brevity and obscurity problems which plagued most of his writings, meaning the essay required further explanation in the treatises of [Joshua Kirby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Kirby) (1754) and Daniel Fournier (1761).[^o14][^o15]

Taylor was elected as a fellow in the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1712. In the same year, Taylor sat on the committee for adjudicating the claims of Sir [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) and [Gottfried Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Leibniz). He acted as secretary to the society from 13 January 1714 to 21 October 1718.

From 1715 onward, Taylor's studies took a philosophical and religious bent. He corresponded with the [Comte de Montmort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comte_de_Montmorency) on the subject of [Nicolas Malebranche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Malebranche)'s tenets. Unfinished treatises written on his return from [Aix-la-Chapelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-la-Chapelle) in 1719, *On the Jewish Sacrifices* and *On the Lawfulness of Eating Blood*, were afterwards found among his papers.[^o14]

Taylor was one of few English mathematicians, along with Isaac Newton and [Roger Cotes](wikipedia://en/236798), who was capable of holding his own with the [Bernoullis](wikipedia://en/199226), but a lack of clarity affected a great part of his demonstrations and Taylor lost brevity through his failure to express his ideas fully and clearly.[^o14]

His health began to fail in 1717 after years of intense work.[^o16]

Taylor married Miss Brydges of [Wallington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallington,_London), Surrey in 1721 without his father's approval. The marriage led to an estrangement with his father, which improved in 1723 after Taylor's wife died in childbirth while giving birth to a son. Taylor's son did not survive.

He spent the next two years with his family at Bifrons, and in 1725 he married with his father's approval, Sabetta Sawbridge of [Olantigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olantigh), [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent). She died in childbirth in 1730, though his only[^o17] daughter, Elizabeth, survived.

Taylor's father died in 1729, leaving Taylor to inherit the Bifrons estate.

Taylor died at the age of 46, on 29 December 1731, at [Somerset House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerset_House), London.

## Selected writings {#Selected_writings}

*Image: Taylor Brook Goupy NPG.jpg.* Brook Taylor

Taylor's grandson, Sir William Young, printed a [posthumous work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posthumous_work) entitled *Contemplatio Philosophica* for private circulation in 1793, (2nd Bart., 10 January 1815). The work was prefaced by a biography,[^o16] and had an appendix containing letters addressed to him by [Bolingbroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_St_John,_1st_Viscount_Bolingbroke), [Bossuet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques-Bénigne_Bossuet), and others.

Several short papers by Taylor were published in *Phil. Trans.,* vols. xxvii to xxxii, which including accounts of experiments in [magnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetism) and [capillary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary) attraction. In 1719, Brook issued an improved version of his work on perspective, *New Principles of Linear Perspective*, which was revised by [John Colson](wikipedia://en/2285149) in 1749. A French translation was published in 1757.[^o19] It was reprinted, with a portrait and short biography, in 1811.

## Tribute {#Tribute}

[Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_%28crater%29) is an [impact crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_crater) located on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon), named in honor of Brook Taylor in 1935.[^o23]
