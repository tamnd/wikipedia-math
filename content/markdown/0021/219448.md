# Appeal to flattery

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Appeal to flattery"}

**Appeal to flattery**[^o2] is a [fallacy](wikipedia://en/53986) in which a person uses [flattery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flattery), excessive compliments, in an attempt to appeal to their audience's [vanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity) to win support for their side.[^o3] It is also known as **apple polishing**, **wheel greasing**, **brown nosing**, **appeal to pride**, **appeal to vanity** or **argumentum ad superbiam**.[^o4] The appeal to flattery is a specific kind of [appeal to emotion](wikipedia://en/219718).[^o5]

:::

Flattery is often used to hide the true intent of an idea or proposal. [Praise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praise) offers a momentary personal distraction that can often weaken judgment. Moreover, it is usually a cunning form of [appeal to consequences](wikipedia://en/224012), since the audience is subject to be flattered *as long as they comply* with the flatterer.[^o4]

Examples:

"Surely a man as smart as you can see this is a brilliant proposal." (failing to accept the proposal is a tacit admission of stupidity)

"Is there a strong man here who could carry this for me?" (a failure to demonstrate physical strength implies weakness)

A refusal which does not deny the compliment could be formulated thus: "I may be \[positive attribute\], but that doesn't mean that I will \[perform action\] for you."

It is not necessarily a logical fallacy, however, when the compliment is sincere, and directly related to the argument. Example:[^o4]

"You are a stunningly beautiful girl – you should become a model."
