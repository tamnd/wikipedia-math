# Jacques Tits

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacques Tits"}

**Jacques Tits** (; 12 August 1930 – 5 December 2021) was a Belgian-born French mathematician who worked on [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [incidence geometry](wikipedia://en/1576323). He introduced [Tits buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tits_building), the [Tits alternative](wikipedia://en/12704641), the [Tits group](wikipedia://en/606719), and the [Tits metric](wikipedia://en/57665268).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Tits was born in [Uccle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uccle), Belgium to Léon Tits, a professor, and Louisa André.[^o4] Jacques attended the Athénée of Uccle and the [Free University of Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Brussels_%281834–1969%29). His thesis advisor was Paul Libois, and Tits graduated with his doctorate in 1950 with the dissertation *Généralisation des groupes projectifs basés sur la notion de transitivité*.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Tits held professorships at the Free University of Brussels (now split into the [Université libre de Bruxelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_libre_de_Bruxelles) and the [Vrije Universiteit Brussel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrije_Universiteit_Brussel)) (1962–1964), the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) (1964–1974) and the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) in Paris, until becoming [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) in 2000. He changed his citizenship to French in 1974 in order to teach at the Collège de France, which at that point required French citizenship. Because [Belgian nationality law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_nationality_law) did not allow [dual nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_nationality) at the time, he [renounced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renunciation_of_citizenship) his Belgian citizenship.[^o6]

Tits was an "honorary" member of the [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394) group; as such, he helped popularize [H.S.M. Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter)'s work, introducing terms such as [Coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_number), [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004), and [Coxeter graph](wikipedia://en/5358570).[^o7]

## Death {#Death}

Tits died on 5 December 2021, at the age of 91[^o6] in the [13th arrondissement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_arrondissement_of_Paris), Paris.[^o9]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Tits received the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) in 1993, the [Cantor Medal](wikipedia://en/1691369) from the [Deutsche Mathematiker-Vereinigung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mathematiker-Vereinigung) (German Mathematical Society) in 1996, and the German distinction "[Pour le Mérite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pour_le_Mérite)". In 2008 he was awarded the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285), along with [John Griggs Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Griggs_Thompson), "for their profound achievements in algebra and in particular for shaping modern group theory".[^o11]

Tits became a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1979.[^o6] He was a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o12] He became a foreign member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1988.[^o13]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

He introduced the theory of *buildings* (sometimes known as *[Tits buildings](wikipedia://en/275015)*), which are combinatorial structures on which groups act, particularly in [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) theory (including [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707), and groups defined over the [p-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423)). The related theory of [(B, N) pairs](wikipedia://en/1772133) is a basic tool in the theory of [groups of Lie type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groups_of_Lie_type). Of particular importance is his classification of all irreducible buildings of spherical type and rank at least three, which involved classifying all [polar spaces](wikipedia://en/3948557) of rank at least three. The existence of these buildings initially depended on the existence of a group of Lie type in each case, but in joint work with [Mark Ronan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ronan) he constructed those of rank at least four independently, yielding the groups directly. In the rank-2 case spherical building are [generalized n-gons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_n-gons), and in joint work with Richard Weiss he classified these when they admit a suitable group of symmetries (the so-called [Moufang polygons](wikipedia://en/20674820)). In collaboration with [François Bruhat](wikipedia://en/9169813) he developed the theory of affine buildings, and later he classified all irreducible buildings of affine type and rank at least four.[^o15]

Another of his well-known theorems is the "[Tits alternative](wikipedia://en/12704641)": if *G* is a [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of a [linear group](wikipedia://en/3983656), then either *G* has a [solvable subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvable_subgroup) of [finite index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_index) or it has a [free subgroup](wikipedia://en/59735) of rank 2.[^o16]

The [Tits group](wikipedia://en/606719) and the [Kantor–Koecher–Tits construction](wikipedia://en/30230650) are named after him. He introduced the [Kneser–Tits conjecture](wikipedia://en/28219167).[^o17][^o18]
