# Eternity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eternity"}

**Eternity**, also **sempiternity**[^o2] or **forever**, or **perpetuity**, is [time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time) with no end[^o4] i.e. [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824).[^o5]

:::

In the context of human life, eternity and [death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death) are co-existing realities.[^o6][^o7][^o8]

## Etymology {#Etymology}

[Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero) used the word aeternitatis, in the work: [De Inventione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Inventione) 1, 27, 39.,[^o10] written at some time between the years 88 - 81 BC[^o11] which is an early or the earliest extant written form from which the English word is derived;[^o12] first shown in history in a *circa* 1374 translation by [Chaucer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaucer).[^o13] The first usage in French is 1175: eternitez: B. de Ste-Maure, within *['ducs de Normandie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benoît_de_Sainte-Maure#Chronique_des_ducs_de_Normandie)*.[^o14]

## Philosophy {#Philosophy}

### Eastern tradition {#Eastern_tradition}

In [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese) 永 (*yǒng* 'eternity') exists in the 論語 (*[Analects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analects)*). [^o17]

### Western tradition {#Western_tradition}

#### Classical {#Classical}

The concept of eternity formed in ancient philosophy was specifically from within the [Platonic tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_tradition). [^o20] During the [Classical period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_antiquity) (8th-7th century BC - 473/529 AD) [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954) (c. 428–423 BC - 348/347 BC) described time as *a moving image of eternity*[^o21] (in [Timaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timaeus_%28dialogue%29) 37[^o22] D[^o22][^o23]) using the word: αἰών.[^o24] Plato stated the [kosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmos) was ἁγἡρων (ageless) in Timaeus 33a, [^o25] in 37e6–38a6 the [eternal forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_forms) are contrasted with the [temporality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporality) of the world. [^n1] [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308) (384–322 BC) stated οὐρανοῦ was eternal (in Book I of [Περὶ οὐρανοῦ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_caelo))[^o27][^o28] and an [eternal world](wikipedia://en/25979603) (in *[Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_%28Aristotle%29)*).[^o29]

The ancient Greek word for everlastingness was ἀίδιος (*aidios*)[^o30] as exists via [Plotinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plotinus), who also used the word *aoin* [^o31] (eternity), in [Ennead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enneads) III.7. [^o32]

#### 17th century {#17th_century}

[Thomas Hobbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hobbes) was re-stating the sense of the definition of [Boethius](wikipedia://en/195684) when [^o34] published 1662, as a response to [John Wallis](wikipedia://en/239290),[^o35] he wrote that he considered that "eternity is a permanent now".[^o36]

#### 19th century {#19th_century}

In post [revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) France, [^o38] [revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary) [^o39] [Louis Auguste Blanqui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Auguste_Blanqui) wrote his 1872 cosmological [prison literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prison_literature) [^o40] hypothesis *L'Éternité par les astres*, [^o41] while imprisoned in Fort du Taureau [sea fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_fortress). [^o42]

## Physics {#Physics}

[Camille Flammarion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Flammarion) (1893) described eternalness as being the absolute. [^o44]

In 1983; [Steinhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Steinhardt) proposed [eternal inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_inflation), the first of two models based on [Guth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Guth)'s [inflationary model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflationary_model) [^o45] of 1981 (originating with Староби́нский (*[Starobinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Starobinsky)*) 1979)[^o46] of the so-called [big bang theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_bang_theory) for the explanation of the orign of the universe; [^o47] the second model [chaotic inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaotic_inflation) [^o45] (1983) - *eternal chaotic inflation* (1986), was proposed by Линде (*[Linde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Linde)*) [^o48]

The possibility of eternal universes with reference to [General Relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Relativity) was a subject of physics since the 21st century.[^o49]

## Religion {#Religion}

Eternity as infinite duration is an important concept in many lives and [religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion). [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God) or gods are often said to endure eternally, or exist for all time, forever, without beginning or end. Religious views of an [afterlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterlife) may speak of it in terms of eternity or [eternal life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortality).

### Abrahamic religions {#Abrahamic_religions}

In [Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis) 21:33 of the [Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament)[^o65] El-Olam[^o66] is God-Eternal.[^o65][^o66][^o67]

[Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus) In [the Gospels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gospels) explicitly threatens *the wicked* with an eternal punishment.[^o68] In the context of [Revelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Revelation) 14: 9-11 of [the Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bible), the book Revelation describes how smoke from application of [burning sulphur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur#Toxicity_of_sulfur_compounds) onto those who *worship [the beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beast_%28Revelation%29)* will transit upwardly forever. [^o69] The position onto which sinners are condemned for all time into [hell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell), if the souls are punished in a conscious state of existence, is known as [traditionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalism), and [annihilationism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annihilationism) if the punishment on arrival in the infernal dominion is that their consciousness is then annulled-extincted. [^o70] [Thomas Aquinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas) (c. 1225 – 1274) believed in an eternal God, without either a beginning or end; the concept of eternity is of [divine simplicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_simplicity), thus incapable of being defined or fully understood by humankind.[^n2] Christian theologians may regard [immutability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immutability_%28theology%29), like the eternal [Platonic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_realism), as essential to eternity.[^n3]

In the [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam) context, while *azal* indicates eternity at the beginning of known time and *abad* at the end; *[sarmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmad)* indicates all (both) aspects,[^o73] this latter as an [adjectival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjective) form is in *[surah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surah)* 28 parts 71 and 72 of the [Qur'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur'an).[^o74][^o75] In Islamic culture the eternity of the universe was a subject in the writings of *[Abu Ali ibn Sīna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Ali_ibn_Sīna)*[^o76] (died 1037), [^o77] *[Abu Hamid al-Ghazali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Hamid_al-Ghazali)*, *[Abu Walid Muhammad Ibn Rushd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Walid_Muhammad_Ibn_Rushd)*.[^o76]

### Egyptian {#Egyptian}

Ancient Egyptian eternity terms were neheh, for cyclical time, and djet, for linear.[^o79][^o80][^o81][^o82][^o83][^o84] [Rameses III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rameses_III)[^o85] (c.1187-1156 B.C.E.) [^o86] [funerary temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funerary_temple)[^o85] was: 'United-with -Eternity'.[^o87]

### Greco-Roman {#Greco-Roman}

[Mythic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology#Sources)[^o89] [Iliadical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad)[^o90][^o91] ἀθάνατος (athanatos) is the [immortal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortality).[^o89]

The ancient greek word for everlasting, and/or eternal, exists in the [Orphica Hymni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orphica_Hymni).[^o92]

The timelessness in [divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divinity) of classical period [Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine) (354–430 AD), as exists in Book XI of [the Confessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Confessions), and [Boethius](wikipedia://en/195684) (c. 480–524), in Book V of [the Consolation of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Consolation_of_Philosophy) were adopted as the reality of the subject for later thinkers in the [western tradition of philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_philosophy).[^o93] Boethius stated eternity was: *interminabilis vitae tota simul et perfecta possessio*,[^o94] which is translated as "simultaneously full and perfect possession of interminable life".[^n4] and *nunc permanens*, which in English is a: permanent now.[^o94]

### Vedic {#Vedic}

Within the [Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_Vedic_religion) [^o97] [Rigveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda), [Aditi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aditi), as a goddess, is eternal. [^o98]

### Symbolism {#Symbolism}

Eternity is often symbolized by the endless snake, swallowing its own tail, the [ouroboros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouroboros).[^o106] The circle, band, or ring is also commonly used as a symbol for eternity, as is the mathematical [symbol of infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol_of_infinity), $\infty$. Symbolically, these are reminders that eternity has no beginning or end.
