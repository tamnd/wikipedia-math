# Equilateral polygon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Equilateral polygon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **equilateral polygon** is a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) which has all sides of the same length. Except in the [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) case, an equilateral polygon does not need to also be [equiangular](wikipedia://en/2476494) (have all angles equal), but if it does then it is a [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306). If the number of sides is at least four, an equilateral polygon does not need to be a [convex polygon](wikipedia://en/660019): it could be [concave](wikipedia://en/605156) or even [self-intersecting](wikipedia://en/50055439).

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

All [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) and [edge-transitive polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotoxal_polygon) are equilateral. When an equilateral polygon is non-crossing and [cyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_polygon) (its vertices are on a circle) it must be regular. An equilateral [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) must be convex; this polygon is a [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) (possibly a [square](wikipedia://en/659939)).

A convex [equilateral pentagon](wikipedia://en/19922843) can be described by two consecutive angles, which together determine the other angles. However, equilateral pentagons, and equilateral polygons with more than five sides, can also be concave, and if concave pentagons are allowed then two angles are no longer sufficient to determine the shape of the pentagon.

A [tangential polygon](wikipedia://en/35520654) (one that has an [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle) tangent to all its sides) is equilateral if and only if the alternate angles are equal (that is, angles 1, 3, 5, ... are equal and angles 2, 4, ... are equal). Thus if the number of sides *n* is odd, a tangential polygon is equilateral if and only if it is regular.[^o4]

:::

## Measurement {#Measurement}

[Viviani's theorem](wikipedia://en/10460511) generalizes to equilateral polygons:[^o6] The sum of the perpendicular distances from an interior point to the sides of an equilateral polygon is independent of the location of the interior point.

The *principal diagonals* of a [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) each divide the hexagon into quadrilaterals. In any convex equilateral hexagon with common side *a*, there exists a principal diagonal *d*<sub>1</sub> such that[^o7]

$$\frac{d_1}{a} \leq 2$$

and a principal diagonal *d*<sub>2</sub> such that

$$\frac{d_2}{a} > \sqrt{3}.$$

## Optimality {#Optimality}

*Image: Reinhardt 15-gons.svg.* Four Reinhardt pentadecagons

When an equilateral polygon is inscribed in a [Reuleaux polygon](wikipedia://en/1356536), it forms a [Reinhardt polygon](wikipedia://en/66207727). Among all convex polygons with the same number of sides, these polygons have the largest possible [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) for their [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007), the largest possible [width](wikipedia://en/580238) for their diameter, and the largest possible width for their perimeter.[^o9]
