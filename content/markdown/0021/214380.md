# Hate Crime Statistics Act

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hate Crime Statistics Act"}

The **Hate Crime Statistics Act**, [28 U.S.C. § 534](https://www.fbi.gov/about-us/cjis/ucr/hate-crime/2010/resources/hate-crime-2010-hate-crime-statistics-act) (HCSA), passed in 1990 and modified in 2009 by the [Matthew Shepard and James Byrd, Jr. Hate Crimes Prevention Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Shepard_and_James_Byrd,_Jr._Hate_Crimes_Prevention_Act),[^o3] requires the [Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Attorney_General) to collect data on crimes committed because of the victim's race, religion, disability, sexual orientation, or ethnicity. The bill was signed into law by [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush), and was the first federal statute to "recognize and name gay, lesbian and bisexual people."[^o4] Since 1992, the [Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice) through one of its agencies, the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI), has jointly published an annual report on hate crime statistics.[^o5][^o6]

:::

On November 16, 2020, the FBI released its 2019 Hate Crime Statistics Act (HCSA) report with the total number of reported hate crime incidents rising 2.7% to 7,317 (2019) from 7,120 (2018).[^o7]
