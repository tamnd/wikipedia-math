# Julia Wolf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Julia Wolf"}

**Julia Wolf** is a British mathematician specialising in [arithmetic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243381) who was the 2016 winner of the Anne Bennett Prize of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097). She is currently a professor in the Department of Pure Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Wolf writes that her childhood ambition was to become a [carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpenter), and that she became attracted to science only after subscribing to *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* as a teenager.

She read mathematics at [Clare College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge), completing the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) in 2003. She remained at Cambridge for graduate study, and completed her PhD there in 2008. Her dissertation, *Arithmetic Structure in Sets of Integers*, was supervised by [Timothy Gowers](wikipedia://en/365715). She was also mentored in her doctoral studies by [Ben Green](wikipedia://en/1927952), whom she met when he was a postdoctoral researcher at Cambridge from 2001 to 2005.

Since earning her doctorate she has been a postdoctoral fellow at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California), Triennial assistant professor at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) in [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey), Hadamard associate professor at the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) (earning a [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at the [University of Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris-Sud) in 2012), and Heilbronn reader in combinatorics and number theory at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol). She returned to Cambridge as a university lecturer in 2018, and was a Fellow of Clare College from 2018 to 2022.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2016 the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) gave Wolf their [Anne Bennett Prize](wikipedia://en/61347778) "in recognition of her outstanding contributions to [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333), combinatorics and harmonic analysis and to the mathematical community." The award citation particularly cited her work with Gowers on counting solutions to [systems of linear equations](wikipedia://en/113087) over [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974), and her work on quadratic analogues of the [Goldreich–Levin theorem](wikipedia://en/1182871).
