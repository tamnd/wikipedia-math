# Cartesian monoidal category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cartesian monoidal category"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in the field known as [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) where the monoidal ("tensor") product is the [categorical product](wikipedia://en/237868) is called a **cartesian monoidal category**. Any [category](wikipedia://en/53759) with finite products (a "finite product category") can be thought of as a cartesian monoidal category. In any cartesian monoidal category, the [terminal object](wikipedia://en/59405) is the monoidal unit. [Dually](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duality_%28category_theory%29), a monoidal finite coproduct category with the monoidal structure given by the [coproduct](wikipedia://en/310889) and unit the [initial object](wikipedia://en/59405) is called a **cocartesian monoidal category**, and any finite coproduct category can be thought of as a cocartesian monoidal category.

:::

Cartesian categories with an internal [Hom functor](wikipedia://en/2741037) that is an [adjoint functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor) to the product are called [Cartesian closed categories](wikipedia://en/237037).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Cartesian monoidal categories have a number of special and important properties, such as the existence of [diagonal maps](wikipedia://en/4606682) Δ<sub>*x*</sub> : *x* → *x* ⊗ *x* and augmentations *e*<sub>*x*</sub> : *x* → *I* for any [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) *x*. In applications to [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) we can think of Δ as "duplicating data" and *e* as "deleting data". These maps make any object into a [comonoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comonoid). In fact, any object in a cartesian monoidal category becomes a comonoid in a unique way.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

Cartesian monoidal categories:

- **Set**, the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) with the [singleton set](wikipedia://en/381798) serving as the unit.
- **Cat**, the [bicategory of small categories](wikipedia://en/2072741) with the [product category](wikipedia://en/4587226), where the category with one object and only its identity map is the unit.

Cocartesian monoidal categories:

- **Vect**, the [category of vector spaces](wikipedia://en/11231812#Category_of_vector_spaces) over a given [field](wikipedia://en/10603), can be made cocartesian monoidal with the monoidal product given by the [direct sum of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_vector_spaces) and the [trivial vector space](wikipedia://en/1463006#Trivial_or_zero_vector_space) as unit.
- **Ab**, the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748), with the [direct sum of abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_abelian_groups) as monoidal product and the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059) as unit.
- More generally, the category *R*-**Mod** of (left) [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* ([commutative](wikipedia://en/61346) or not) becomes a cocartesian monoidal category with the [direct sum of modules](wikipedia://en/58899) as tensor product and the [trivial module](wikipedia://en/7455889) as unit.

In each of these categories of modules equipped with a cocartesian monoidal structure, finite products and coproducts coincide (in the sense that the product and coproduct of finitely many objects are isomorphic). Or more formally, if *f* : *X*<sub>1</sub> ∐ ... ∐ *X*<sub>*n*</sub> → *X*<sub>1</sub> × ... × *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is the "canonical" map from the *n*-ary coproduct of objects *X*<sub>*j*</sub> to their product, for a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *n*, in the event that the map *f* is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828), we say that a [biproduct](wikipedia://en/59574) for the objects *X*<sub>*j*</sub> is an object $X = \bigoplus_{j \in {1,\ldots,n}} X_j$ isomorphic to $\coprod_{j\in {1,\ldots,n}}X_j$ and $\prod_{j\in {1,\ldots,n}}X_j$ together with maps *i*<sub>*j*</sub> : *X*<sub>*j*</sub> → *X* and *p*<sub>*j*</sub> : *X* → *X*<sub>*j*</sub> such that the pair (*X*, {*i*<sub>*j*</sub>}) is a coproduct diagram for the objects *X*<sub>*j*</sub> and the pair (*X*, {*p*<sub>*j*</sub>}) is a product diagram for the objects *X*<sub>*j*</sub> , and where *p*<sub>*j*</sub> ∘ *i*<sub>*j*</sub> = id<sub>*X*<sub>*j*</sub></sub>. If, in addition, the category in question has a [zero object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object), so that for any objects *A* and *B* there is a unique map 0<sub>*A*,*B*</sub> : *A* → 0 → *B*, it often follows that *p*<sub>*k*</sub> ∘ *i*<sub>*j*</sub> = : *δ*<sub>*ij*</sub>, the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890), where we interpret 0 and 1 as the 0 maps and identity maps of the objects *X*<sub>*j*</sub> and *X*<sub>*k*</sub>, respectively. See [pre-additive category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-additive_category) for more.
