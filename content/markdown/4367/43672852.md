# Normal form (dynamical systems)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Normal form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **normal form** of a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) is a simplified form that can be useful in determining the system's behavior.

:::

Normal forms are often used for determining local [bifurcations](wikipedia://en/2326042) in a system. All systems exhibiting a certain type of bifurcation are locally (around the equilibrium) [topologically equivalent](wikipedia://en/5506149) to the normal form of the bifurcation. For example, the normal form of a [saddle-node bifurcation](wikipedia://en/3201787) is

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} = \mu + x^2$$

where $\mu$ is the bifurcation parameter. The transcritical bifurcation

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} = r \ln x + x - 1$$

near $x=1$ can be converted to the normal form

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}t} = R u - u^2 + O(u^3)$$

with the transformation $u = \frac{r}{2}(x -1), R = r + 1$.[^o2]

See also [canonical form](wikipedia://en/515096) for use of the terms **canonical form**, **normal form**, or **standard form** more generally in mathematics.
