# Descending wedge

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Descending wedge"}

The **descending wedge** symbol **∨** may represent:

:::

- [Logical disjunction](wikipedia://en/7962) in [propositional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus)
- [Join](wikipedia://en/3956618) in [lattice theory](wikipedia://en/469304)
- The [wedge sum](wikipedia://en/684143) in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954)
- The [V sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_sign), a [symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_symbols) representing [peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace) among other things

The vertically reflected symbol, ∧, is a [wedge](wikipedia://en/37702563), and often denotes a related or [dual](wikipedia://en/609737) operator.

The ∨ symbol was introduced by [Russell](wikipedia://en/4163) and [Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395) in *[Principia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/24133)*, where they called it the *Logical Sum* or *Disjunctive Function*.[^o2]

In [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) the symbol is encoded 2228. In [TeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeX), it is `\vee` or `\lor`.

One motivation and the most probable explanation for the choice of the symbol ∨ is the latin word "vel" meaning "or" in the inclusive sense. Several authors use "vel" as name of the "or" function.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10]
