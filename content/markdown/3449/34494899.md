# Piecewise-deterministic Markov process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Piecewise-deterministic Markov process"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **piecewise-deterministic Markov process** (**PDMP**) is a process whose behaviour is governed by random jumps at points in time, but whose evolution is deterministically governed by an [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753) between those times. The class of models is "wide enough to include as special cases virtually all the non-diffusion models of [applied probability](wikipedia://en/366719)."[^o3] The process is defined by three quantities: the flow, the jump rate, and the transition measure.[^o2]

:::

The model was first introduced in a paper by [Mark H. A. Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_H._A._Davis) in 1984.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Piecewise linear models such as [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876), [continuous-time Markov chains](wikipedia://en/1125342), the [M/G/1 queue](wikipedia://en/33378064), the [GI/G/1 queue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GI/G/1_queue) and the [fluid queue](wikipedia://en/33791981) can be encapsulated as PDMPs with simple differential equations.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

PDMPs have been shown useful in [ruin theory](wikipedia://en/14993253),[^o8] [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578),[^o9][^o10] for modelling [biochemical processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry) such as DNA replication in [eukaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryotes) and subtilin production by the organism [B. subtilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._subtilis),[^o11] and for modelling [earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake).[^o12] Moreover, this class of processes has been shown to be appropriate for biophysical neuron models with stochastic ion channels.[^o13]

:::

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Löpker and Palmowski have shown conditions under which a [time reversed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reversed_process) PDMP is a PDMP.[^o16] General conditions are known for PDMPs to be stable.[^o17]

Galtier et al.[^o18] studied the law of the trajectories of PDMP and provided a reference measure in order to express a **density of a trajectory** of the PDMP. Their work opens the way to any application using densities of trajectory. (For instance, they used the density of a trajectories to perform [importance sampling](wikipedia://en/867671), this work was further developed by Chennetier and Al.[^o19] to estimate the reliability of industrial systems.)

:::
