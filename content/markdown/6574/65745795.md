# Jennifer Morse (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jennifer Morse"}

*Image: Jennifer Morse.jpg.*

**Jennifer Leigh Morse** is a mathematician specializing in [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582). She is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia).

:::

## Research {#Research}

Morse's interests in algebraic combinatorics include [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) and applications to [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics), [symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/22510359), [Young tableaux](wikipedia://en/683368), and $k$-Schur functions, which are a generalization of [Schur polynomials](wikipedia://en/3147062).

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Morse earned her Ph.D. in 1999 from the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego). Her dissertation, *Explicit Expansions for Knop-Sahi and Macdonald Polynomials*, was supervised by [Adriano Garsia](wikipedia://en/18857510).

She has been a faculty member at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), at the [University of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Miami), and at [Drexel University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drexel_University) before moving to the University of Virginia in 2017.

## Book {#Book}

Morse is one of six coauthors of the book *$k$-Schur Functions and Affine Schubert Calculus* (Fields Institute Monographs 33, Springer, 2014).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Morse was named a [Simons Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Fellow) in Mathematics in 2012 and again in 2021. She was elected as a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in the 2021 class of fellows, "for contributions to algebraic combinatorics and representation theory and service to the mathematical community". She is an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the 2026 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842).
