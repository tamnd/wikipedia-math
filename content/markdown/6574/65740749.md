# Sinai–Ruelle–Bowen measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sinai–Ruelle–Bowen measure"}

In the mathematical discipline of [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), a **Sinai–Ruelle–Bowen (SRB) measure** is an [invariant measure](wikipedia://en/7635201) that behaves similarly to, but is not an [ergodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic) measure. In order to be ergodic, the time average would need to equal the space average for almost all initial states $x \in X$, with $X$ being the [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101).[^o2] For an SRB measure $\mu$, it suffices that the ergodicity condition be valid for initial states in a set $B(\mu)$ of positive [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198).[^o8]

:::

The initial ideas pertaining to SRB measures were introduced by [Yakov Sinai](wikipedia://en/1751626), [David Ruelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ruelle) and [Rufus Bowen](wikipedia://en/24717250) in the less general area of [Anosov diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/2320078) and [axiom A attractors](wikipedia://en/5471083).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $T:X \rightarrow X$ be a [map](wikipedia://en/516931). Then a measure $\mu$ defined on $X$ is an **SRB measure** if there exist $U \subset X$ of positive Lebesgue measure, and $V \subset U$ with same Lebesgue measure, such that:[^o8][^o9]

$$\lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i = 0}^n \varphi(T^i x) = \int_U \varphi \, d\mu$$

for every $x \in V$ and every continuous function $\varphi: U \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$.

One can see the SRB measure $\mu$ as one that satisfies the conclusions of [Birkhoff's ergodic theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic_theorems) on a smaller set contained in $X$.

:::

## Existence of SRB measures {#Existence_of_SRB_measures}

The following theorem establishes sufficient conditions for the existence of SRB measures. It considers the case of Axiom A attractors, which is simpler, but it has been extended times to more general scenarios.[^o11]

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem 1"}

**Theorem 1:**[^o11] Let $T: X \rightarrow X$ be a $C^2$ [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) with an [Axiom A attractor](wikipedia://en/5471083) $\mathcal{A} \subset X$. Assume that this attractor is *irreducible*, that is, it is not the union of two other sets that are also invariant under $T$. Then there is a unique [Borelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_algebra) measure $\mu$, with $\mu(X) = 1$, characterized by the following equivalent statements:

1. $\mu$ is an SRB measure;
1. $\mu$ has absolutely continuous measures conditioned on the [unstable manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unstable_manifold) and submanifolds thereof;
1. $h(T) = \int \log {\bigl| \det(D T)|_{E^u} \bigr|} \, d\mu$, where $h$ is the [Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov–Sinai_entropy), $E^u$ is the unstable manifold and $D$ is the [differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886).

Also, in these conditions $\left(T, X, \mathcal{B}(X), \mu \right)$ is a [measure-preserving dynamical system](wikipedia://en/398931).

It has also been proved that the above are equivalent to stating that $\mu$ equals the zero-noise limit [stationary distribution](wikipedia://en/2524727) of a [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) with states $T^i(x)$.[^o13] That is, consider that to each point $x \in X$ is associated a transition probability $P_\varepsilon(\cdot \mid x)$ with noise level $\varepsilon$ that measures the amount of uncertainty of the next state, in a way such that:

$$\lim_{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0} P_{\varepsilon}(\cdot \mid x) = \delta_{Tx}(\cdot),$$

where $\delta$ is the [Dirac measure](wikipedia://en/1019002). The zero-noise limit is the stationary distribution of this Markov chain when the noise level approaches zero. The importance of this is that it states mathematically that the SRB measure is a "good" approximation to practical cases where small amounts of noise exist,[^o13] though nothing can be said about the amount of noise that is tolerable.

:::
