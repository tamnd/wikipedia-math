# Erdős–Tetali theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erdős–Tetali theorem"}

In [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333), an area of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Erdős–Tetali theorem** is an [existence theorem](wikipedia://en/251177) concerning economical [additive bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additive_basis) of every order. More specifically, it states that for every fixed integer $h \geq 2$, there exists a subset of the natural numbers $\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ satisfying

$$r_{\mathcal{B},h}(n) \asymp \log n,$$

where $r_{\mathcal{B},h}(n)$ denotes the number of ways that a natural number *n* can be expressed as the sum of *h* elements of *B*.[^o2]

:::

The theorem is named after [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Prasad V. Tetali](wikipedia://en/45055276), who published it in 1990.

## Motivation {#Motivation}

The original motivation for this result is attributed to a problem posed by S. Sidon in 1932 on *economical bases*. An additive basis $\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ is called *economical*[^o4] (or sometimes *thin*[^o5]) when it is an additive basis of order *h* and

$$r_{\mathcal{B},h}(n) \ll_{\varepsilon} n^\varepsilon$$

for every $\varepsilon > 0$. In other words, these are additive bases that use as few numbers as possible to represent a given *n*, and yet represent every natural number. Related concepts include [$B_h[g]$-sequences](wikipedia://en/20823677)[^o6] and the [Erdős–Turán conjecture on additive bases](wikipedia://en/31244492).

Sidon's question was whether an economical basis of order 2 exists. A positive answer was given by P. Erdős in 1956,[^o7] settling the case $h = 2$ of the theorem. Although the general version was believed to be true, no complete proof appeared in the literature before the paper by Erdős and Tetali.[^o8]

## Ideas in the proof {#Ideas_in_the_proof}

The proof is an instance of the [probabilistic method](wikipedia://en/173525), and can be divided into three main steps. First, one starts by defining a *random sequence* $\omega \subseteq \mathbb{N}$ by

$$\Pr(n\in \omega) = C\cdot n^{\frac{1}{h} - 1} (\log n)^{\frac{1}{h}},$$

where $C>0$ is some large real constant, $h\geq 2$ is a fixed integer and *n* is sufficiently large so that the above formula is well-defined. A detailed discussion on the [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) associated with this type of construction may be found on Halberstam & Roth (1983).[^o10] Secondly, one then shows that the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of the [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) $r_{\omega,h}(n)$ has the order of [log](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_function). That is,

$$\mathbb{E}(r_{\omega,h}(n)) \asymp \log n.$$

Finally, one shows that $r_{\omega,h}(n)$ [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) concentrates around its mean. More explicitly:

$$\Pr\big(\exists c_1,c_2>0 ~|~ \text{for all large } n\in\mathbb{N} ,~ c_1\mathbb{E}(r_{\omega,h}(n)) \leq r_{\omega,h}(n) \leq c_2\mathbb{E}(r_{\omega,h}(n))\big) = 1$$

This is the critical step of the proof. Originally it was dealt with by means of [Janson's inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janson's_inequality), a type of [concentration inequality](wikipedia://en/32023963) for multivariate polynomials. Tao & Vu (2006)[^o11] present this proof with a more sophisticated two-sided concentration inequality by V. Vu (2000),[^o12] thus relatively simplifying this step. Alon & Spencer (2016) classify this proof as an instance of the [Poisson paradigm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_paradigm).[^o13]

## Relation to the Erdős–Turán conjecture on additive bases {#Relation_to_the_Erdős–Turán_conjecture_on_additive_bases}

::: {#o16 .problem cue=unsolved}

Let $h\geq 2$ be an integer. If $\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ is an infinite set such that $r_{\mathcal{B},h}(n)>0$ for every *n*, does this imply that:

$\limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac{r_{\mathcal{B},h}(n)}{\log n} > 0$?

:::

The original [Erdős–Turán conjecture on additive bases](wikipedia://en/31244492) states, in its most general form, that if $\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ is an additive basis of order *h* then

$$\limsup_{n\to \infty} r_{\mathcal{B},h}(n) = \infty;$$

that is, $r_{\mathcal{B},h}(n)$ cannot be bounded. In his 1956 paper, P. Erdős[^o7] asked whether it could be the case that

$$\limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac{r_{\mathcal{B},2}(n)}{\log n} > 0$$

whenever $\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ is an additive basis of order 2. In other words, this is saying that $r_{\mathcal{B},2}(n)$ is not only unbounded, but that no function smaller than log can dominate $r_{\mathcal{B},2}(n)$. The question naturally extends to $h\geq 3$, making it a stronger form of the Erdős–Turán conjecture on additive bases. In a sense, what is being conjectured is that there are no additive bases substantially more economical than those guaranteed to exist by the Erdős–Tetali theorem.

:::

## Further developments {#Further_developments}

### Computable economical bases {#Computable_economical_bases}

All the known proofs of Erdős–Tetali theorem are, by the nature of the infinite probability space used, [non-constructive proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-constructive). However, Kolountzakis (1995)[^o19] showed the existence of a [recursive set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursive_set) $\mathcal{R}\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ satisfying $r_{\mathcal{R},2}(n) \asymp \log n$ such that $\mathcal{R}\cap\{0,1,\ldots ,n\}$ takes polynomial time in *n* to be computed. The question for $h\geq 3$ remains open.

### Economical subbases {#Economical_subbases}

Given an arbitrary additive basis $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{N}$, one can ask whether there exists $\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}$ such that $\mathcal{B}$ is an economical basis. V. Vu (2000)[^o21] showed that this is the case for [Waring bases](wikipedia://en/33569) $\mathbb{N}^{\wedge} k := \{0^k, 1^k, 2^k,\ldots \}$, where for every fixed *k* there are economical subbases of $\mathbb{N}^{\wedge}k$ of order $s$ for every $s \geq s_k$, for some large computable constant $s_k$.

### Growth rates other than log {#Growth_rates_other_than_log}

Another possible question is whether similar results apply for functions other than log. That is, fixing an integer $h\geq 2$, for which functions *f* can we find a subset of the natural numbers $\mathcal{B}\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ satisfying $r_{\mathcal{B},h}(n) \asymp f(n)$? It follows from a result of C. Táfula (2019)[^o23] that if *f* is a [locally integrable](wikipedia://en/4258398), [positive](wikipedia://en/22284647) [real function](wikipedia://en/1268958) satisfying

- $\frac{1}{x}\int_{1}^{x} f(t) \,\mathrm{d}t \asymp f(x)$, and
- $\log x \ll f(x) \ll x^{\frac{1}{h-1}}(\log x)^{-\varepsilon}$ for some $\varepsilon > 0$,

then there exists an additive basis $\mathcal{B}\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ of order *h* which satisfies $r_{\mathcal{B},h}(n) \asymp f(n)$. The minimal case $f(x) = \log x$ recovers Erdős–Tetali's theorem.

## See also {#See_also}

- [Erdős–Fuchs theorem](wikipedia://en/18543655): For any non-zero $C\in \mathbb{R}$, there is **no** set $\mathcal{B}\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ which satisfies $\textstyle{\sum_{n\leq x} r_{\mathcal{B},2}(n) = Cx + o\left(x^{1/4} \log(x)^{-1/2}\right)}$.
- [Erdős–Turán conjecture on additive bases](wikipedia://en/31244492): If $\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ is an additive basis of order 2, then $r_{\mathcal{B},2}(n) \neq O(1)$.
- [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569), the problem of representing numbers as sums of *k*-powers, for fixed $k\geq 2$.
