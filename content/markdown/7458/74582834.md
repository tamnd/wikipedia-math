# Mathematics for social justice

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematics for social justice"}

**Mathematics for social justice** is a [pedagogical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogy) approach to [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471) that seeks to incorporate lessons from [critical mathematics pedagogy](wikipedia://en/52098442) and similar educational philosophies into the teaching of mathematics at schools and colleges. The approach tries to empower students on their way to developing a positive mathematics identity and becoming active, numerically literate citizens who can navigate and participate in society.[^o2][^o3] Mathematics for [social justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_justice) puts particular emphasis on overcoming social inequalities.[^o4] Its proponents, for example, [Bob Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Moses_%28activist%29),[^o5] may understand numerical literacy as a civil right. Many of the founders of the movement, e.g. Eric Gutstein, were initially mathematics teachers,[^o6] but the movement has since expanded to include the teaching of mathematics at colleges and universities.[^o7] Their educational approach is influenced by earlier [critical pedagogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_pedagogy) advocates such as [Paulo Freire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Freire) and others.[^o8] Mathematics for social justice has been criticised, however, its proponents argue that it both fits into existing teaching frameworks and promotes students' success in mathematics.[^o9]

:::

Mathematics for social justice often overlaps with other approaches to mathematics education, the practice and research of mathematics, including [ethics in mathematics](wikipedia://en/54956115)[^o10] and [ethnomathematics](wikipedia://en/324872).[^o11] Common to these approaches is that they can be understood as a sociopolitical turn in mathematics.[^o12]

## Criticism {#Criticism}

Pedagogical approaches incorporating issues of social justice into mathematics classrooms have been heavily criticised by some mathematicians and educators.[^o14] They argue that mathematics is neutral and that its education and research should be separated from issues of social justice.[^o15][^o16] Balancing the requirements of a mathematical education that teaches students mathematical skills and social justice can be difficult.[^o17] Some of its opponents use this to argue against mathematics for social justice because it would necessarily come at the expense of teaching mathematical knowledge.[^o18]

Eric Gutstein's book [Rethinking Mathematics](wikipedia://en/12180915) has been said to calculate controversy[^o19] and to unnecessarily bring partisan politics into mathematics. The criticism is shared with other works in this area.
