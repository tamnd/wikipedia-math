# Sylvanus A. Tyler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Sylvanus A. Tyler"}

**Sylvanus A. Tyler Sr.** (August 21, 1914 – July 23, 1986) was an American mathematician and biostatistician.[^o3][^o4] Tyler worked on the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project) at the [Argonne National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratory), where he was one of the few African American mathematicians assigned to the project.[^o5]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Tyler was born in Chicago in 1914 and attended city public schools. He earned an undergraduate degree from [Fisk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisk_University) and a master's degree from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Tyler served in the [United States Army Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Signal_Corps) and was stationed in Italy.[^o7]

Tyler becan working at the Argonne National Laboratory in 1946 and worked at the lab for 34 years, retiring in 1980. Tyler also served as faculty at [Tougaloo College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tougaloo_College) and [Alabama A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama_A&M_University). Tyler was published in over 75 journal articles during his career.[^o5][^o8][^o9]

After his retirement, Tyler continued to support the Argonne lab and the [United States Department of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Interior) as a statistical consultant.[^o7][^o10]

## Death {#Death}

Tyler died in July 1986 at the age of 71. His obituary was published in the *[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune)*.[^o5]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1949, Tyler was featured in an *[Ebony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebony_%28magazine%29)* magazine article titled "Atom Scientists: Ten Negro Scientists at Argonne Lab Help in Race to Harness Atomic Materials."
