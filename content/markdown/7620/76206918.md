# Xu Ruiyun

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Xu Ruiyun"}

[Xu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_%28surname_徐%29)

**Xu Ruiyun** (; 15 June 1915 – 23 January 1969), also known as **Süe-Yung Zee-Kiang**, was a [Chinese mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_mathematician) specialising in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). A student of [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625), she was the first Chinese woman to receive a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in mathematics. In 1941, she returned to China and worked at [Zhejiang University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang_University) and later [Hangzhou University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou_University). In 1969, during the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution), she died by suicide.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Xu was born in [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai) on 15 June 1915. Her family originally came from [Cixi, Zhejiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cixi,_Zhejiang). Her father was an industrialist who owned a factory making socks. In 1927, she entered [Wu Pen Girls' School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Pen_Girls'_School).[^n1] In 1932 she entered [Zhejiang University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang_University) in [Hangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou), where she studied under [Chen Jiangong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Jiangong) and [Su Buqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su_Buqing).[^n2] She graduated in 1936 and was hired as a teaching assistant.[^n3] At Zhejiang University she also entered a relationship with biology student [Jiang Ximing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiang_Ximing). They married in February 1937.[^n3]

In 1937, Xu and Jiang received the Humboldt scholarship to study in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany). They left China in May 1937, were delayed for a month and a half in [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan), before arriving in Germany in August.[^n4] They first studied the German language in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) for two months, and then entered the [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) program at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München).[^n5] Xu was supervised by [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625) and studied [trigonometric series](wikipedia://en/4099125), in particular [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038).[^n5] At the end of 1940, she received her doctorate, becoming the first Chinese woman PhD in mathematics.[^n5][^n6][^n7]

In January 1941, Xu and Jiang left Germany. In March, they arrived in [Chongqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing), the wartime capital of China during the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War). In April, they arrived in [Meitan, Guizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitan_County), where the College of Science of Zhejiang University held its wartime campus.[^n8] There she lectured in mathematics, and her students included Cao Xihua, Ye Yanqian, and some later Chinese mathematicians.[^n9] After the end of the war, Xu was promoted to professor in 1946, when she was 31. She returned to Hangzhou with Zhejiang University in the same year.[^n10]

The [People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_China) was established in 1949. During the [1952 reorganisation of Chinese higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_reorganisation_of_Chinese_higher_education), Xu remained at Zhejiang University, where she was the office director of teaching and research in higher mathematics. Around this time she joined the [China Democratic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Democratic_League) and studied [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism).[^n11] In 1953 she was moved to Zhejiang Teachers College (浙江师范学院, later became [Hangzhou University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou_University) in 1958), where she chaired the department of mathematics.[^n12] During her time at Zhejiang Teachers College, she learned Russian and translated [Isidor Natanson](wikipedia://en/34253287)'s *Theory of Functions of a Real Variable*, which was published in Chinese in 1955.[^n13]

Around this time, Xu was elected as a representative to the [Zhejiang Provincial People's Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang_Provincial_People's_Congress) in 1954 and a member of the People's Government of Zhejiang. She was also elected as the secretary general of the Zhejiang Mathematical Society (浙江省数学会). In 1956, Xu joined the [Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party).[^n14]

In 1964, Xu and [Hua Luogeng](wikipedia://en/2337582) chaired the first national conference on functional analysis at the [Hengshan Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hengshan_Picardie_Hotel) in Shanghai. She was the CCP [party group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_group) leader at the conference. In 1965, Xu began to translate Carathéodory's *Theory of Functions of a Complex Variable*. She could not complete it due to her workload in the [Socialist Education Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Education_Movement).[^n15] After the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution) began in 1966, Xu began to undergo [struggle sessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struggle_session) within Hangzhou University. She was forced to kneel for long times and was whipped. In 1968, Xu's husband Jiang Ximing was accused of being a German spy, and Xu was detained and was [pressured to confess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_confession) her alleged spy activities. On 23 January 1969, Xu committed [suicide by hanging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_by_hanging).[^n16]

In 1978, Hangzhou University held a commemoration ceremony and [rehabilitated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_rehabilitation) Xu. She was buried in Liuxia Subdistrict, Hangzhou.[^n17] In 2009, the department of mathematics of Zhejiang University purchased a new grave for Xu and made a new tombstone.[^n18] On 13 June 2015, Zhejiang University held a conference commemorating the 100th anniversary of Xu's birth.[^n19]

## Works and influences {#Works_and_influences}

Xue et al. consider Xu to be a member of the third generation of the mathematical analysis tradition at Zhejiang University.[^n20] Xu's publications include:

- Kiang, Süe-Yung. (18 January 1941). "Über die Fouriersche Entwicklung der singulären Funktion bei einer Lebesgueschen Zerlegung". *Mathematische Zeitschrift*. 47. p. 330–342. doi:10.1007/BF01180966
- Zee-Kiang, Süe-Yung. (April 1944). "On the Variation of Increasing Functions Whose First 2n Fourier Coefficients are Given". *Journal of the London Mathematical Society*. 19 (74). p. 71–77. doi:10.1112/jlms/19.74\_Part\_2.71

Xu's translations include:

- Natanson, Isidor. isbn:9787040292213
- Hardy, G. H.; Rogosinski, W. W.

When she was teaching at Zhejiang University, Xu was the thesis advisor of the Chinese American mathematician [Yuan-Shih Chow](wikipedia://en/15359177).[^n21] Three members of the [Chinese Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Academy_of_Sciences)–[Shi Zhongci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi_Zhongci), [Wang Yuan](wikipedia://en/15303814), and [Hu Hesheng](wikipedia://en/15289611)–had taken her classes at the university.[^n22] Her adopted daughter also became a PhD in mathematics.[^n22]
