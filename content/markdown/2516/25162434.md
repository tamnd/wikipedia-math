# Tertiary ideal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tertiary ideal"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **tertiary ideal** is a two-sided [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) in a perhaps noncommutative [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) that cannot be expressed as a nontrivial intersection of a right [fractional ideal](wikipedia://en/460700) with another ideal. Tertiary ideals generalize [primary ideals](wikipedia://en/3021435) to the case of [noncommutative rings](wikipedia://en/24062516). Although [primary decompositions](wikipedia://en/1208391) do not exist in general for ideals in noncommutative rings, tertiary decompositions do, at least if the ring is [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129).

:::

Every primary ideal is tertiary. Tertiary ideals and primary ideals coincide for commutative rings. To any (two-sided) ideal, a tertiary ideal can be associated called the tertiary radical, defined as

$$t(I) = \{r \in R \mbox{ }|\mbox{ } \forall s \notin I, \mbox{ }\exists x \in (s)\mbox{ } x \notin I \text{ and } (x)(r) \subset I \}.$$

Then *t*(*I*) always contains *I*.

If *R* is a (not necessarily commutative) Noetherian ring and *I* a right ideal in *R*, then *I* has a unique irredundant decomposition into tertiary ideals

$$I = T_1 \cap \dots \cap T_n.$$
