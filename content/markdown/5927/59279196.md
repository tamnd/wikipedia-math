# Kasso Okoudjou

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kasso Okoudjou"}

**Kasso Akochayé Okoudjou** is a [Beninese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin)-American mathematician and Professor of Mathematics at [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University). His research spans [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [time–frequency analysis](wikipedia://en/857897), and [fractals](wikipedia://en/10913), with particular focus on [frame theory](wikipedia://en/4504771) and its applications to [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) and [quantum information](wikipedia://en/25274). He is a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o3] and served as the 2018 [Martin Luther King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr.) Visiting professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Okoudjou studied mathematics at the [University of Abomey-Calavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Abomey-Calavi) in [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin), earning a [maîtrise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maîtrise) in 1996.[^o6] After graduating, he worked as an instructor at the Complexe Scolaire William Ponty de Porto-Novo.[^o6] In 1998 he began graduate studies at the [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology), where he earned his PhD in 2003 with a dissertation on the characterization of function spaces and bilinear [pseudodifferential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudodifferential_operator) through [Gabor frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabor_frame), supervised by Christopher Edward Heil. His thesis received the [Sigma Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Xi) Best PhD Thesis Award.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

After completing his doctorate, Okoudjou was appointed H. C. Wang Assistant Professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 2003. He spent 2005 at the [Erwin Schrödinger International Institute for Mathematical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger_International_Institute_for_Mathematical_Physics) in Vienna, then joined the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park) in 2006.[^o6] He served as the Martin Luther King Visiting professor at MIT from 2017 to 2019, and moved to [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University) in 2020, where he is Professor of Mathematics.[^o9]

## Research {#Research}

Okoudjou's research centers on harmonic analysis, time–frequency analysis, and fractal geometry. A major strand of his work involves [frame theory](wikipedia://en/4504771), which concerns redundant representations of data. He has applied frame-theoretic methods to problems in digital signal processing and [quantum information](wikipedia://en/25274), with conditional results drawing on the [Zauner conjecture](wikipedia://en/20159695#Zauner's_conjecture) and the [Heil–Ramanathan–Topiwala conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heil–Ramanathan–Topiwala_conjecture). In 2018, he received a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) grant to study finite [Gabor systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabor_system) and related conjectures.[^o11]

## Service and advocacy {#Service_and_advocacy}

In June 2020, Okoudjou was appointed co-chair of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) task force on racial discrimination, established in the wake of [a nationwide reckoning on racial justice in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020–2021_United_States_racial_unrest). The task force published its report, "Towards a Fully Inclusive Mathematics Profession," in March 2021.[^o15]
