# Computing the Continuous Discretely

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Computing the Continuous Discretely"}

***Computing the Continuous Discretely: Integer-Point Enumeration in Polyhedra*** is an undergraduate-level textbook in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), on the interplay between the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of [convex polytopes](wikipedia://en/1817885) and the number of [integer lattice points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_lattice_point) they contain. It was written by Matthias Beck and Sinai Robins, and published in 2007 by [Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer-Verlag) in their [Undergraduate Texts in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/8871375) series (Vol. 154). A second edition was published in 2015, and a German translation of the first edition by Kord Eickmeyer, *Das Kontinuum diskret berechnen*, was published by Springer in 2008.[^o3]

:::

## Topics {#Topics}

The book begins with a motivating problem, the [coin problem](wikipedia://en/2302444) of determining which amounts of money can be represented (and what is the largest non-representable amount of money) for a given system of coin values. Other topics touched on include [face lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_lattice) of polytopes and the [Dehn–Sommerville equations](wikipedia://en/8276156) relating numbers of faces; [Pick's theorem](wikipedia://en/328252) and the [Ehrhart polynomials](wikipedia://en/316904), both of which relate lattice counting to volume; [generating functions](wikipedia://en/160993), [Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/52247), and [Dedekind sums](wikipedia://en/1122242), different ways of encoding sequences of numbers into mathematical objects; [Green's theorem](wikipedia://en/243334) and its discretization; [Bernoulli polynomials](wikipedia://en/228161); the [Euler–Maclaurin formula](wikipedia://en/9637) for the difference between a sum and the corresponding integral; special polytopes including [zonotopes](wikipedia://en/669406), the [Birkhoff polytope](wikipedia://en/7771277), and [permutohedra](wikipedia://en/12745947); and the enumeration of [magic squares](wikipedia://en/52202). In this way, the topics of the book connect together geometry, [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

## Audience and reception {#Audience_and_reception}

This book is written at an undergraduate level, and provides many exercises, making it suitable as an undergraduate textbook. Little mathematical background is assumed, except for some [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) towards the end of the book. The book also includes open problems, of more interest to researchers in these topics. As reviewer Darren Glass writes, "Even people who are familiar with the material would almost certainly learn something from the clear and engaging exposition that these two authors use."

Reviewer [Margaret Bayer](wikipedia://en/63919419) calls the book "coherent and tightly developed ... accessible and engaging", and reviewer Oleg Karpenkov calls it "outstanding".
