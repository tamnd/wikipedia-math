# Kenneth W. Wachter

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kenneth W. Wachter"}

**Kenneth W. Wachter** (born January 13, 1947) is an American mathematical [demographer](wikipedia://en/45642) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661), known for contributions to demographic microsimulation ([agent-based modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent-based_model)), [anthropometric history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropometric_history), statistical [endocrinology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrinology), and evolutionary [biodemography](wikipedia://en/7521584).[^o2] He is [Distinguished Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_professor) [Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) of [Demography](wikipedia://en/45642) and [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o2] Wachter was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1999.[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Wachter was born in [Brooklyn, New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), and grew up in [Westfield, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield,_New_Jersey). He is the son of [lawyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawyer) John H. Wachter and [columnist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columnist) Evelyn Ardelle Sidman.[^o5] He attended The [Pingry School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingry_School) in Elizabeth, [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey), and was named a Presidential Scholar by President [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) in 1964.[^o3] He earned his [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) magna cum laude in [History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History) and [Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature) from [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College) in 1968, where he was elected to [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) in his junior year[^o6] and awarded the Harvard Prize for History and Literature.[^o3]

As a Keasbey Memorial Scholar, Wachter attended graduate school in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom). In 1971, while working as a [Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_fellow) at [St Catherine's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Catherine's_College,_Oxford), he was awarded a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) by special decree in [Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o2] He completed his doctorate in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), in 1974. His [doctoral dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) was titled "Foundations of the Asymptotic Theory of Random Matrix Spectra".[^o2]

## Career {#Career}

Wachter worked at [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) as an Associate Member of technical staff in the early years of his career (1968–1969).[^o8] Wachter became a Miller Fellow in [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1977.[^o2]

In 1978, Wachter was a founding member of the newly reconstituted department of [Demography](wikipedia://en/45642) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he taught throughout his career.[^o5] Under the leadership of Eugene Hammel, Ronald D. Lee, and Wachter, the [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) [Demography](wikipedia://en/45642) Department fostered [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) and formal [demography](wikipedia://en/45642), macro-level economic and [historical demography](wikipedia://en/10817881), interdisciplinary [qualitative research](wikipedia://en/371299), [biodemography](wikipedia://en/7521584), and [aging studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageing_studies), training some of the leading demographers of the 21st Century.[^o9] From 1997 to 2008, he chaired the Department of Demography. From 2009 to 2010, he was named Miller Professor at the [Miller Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Institute) for Basic Research in Science. He has been a Professor of the Graduate School and [Distinguished Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_professor) [Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) since 2014.[^o2]

## Leadership and public advisory roles {#Leadership_and_public_advisory_roles}

Wachter was an expert witness for the U.S. Department of Justice's Civil Division and a member of the Secretary of Commerce's Special Advisory Panel on Census Adjustment from 1989 to 1991.[^o11] In addition to serving on the [Editorial Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editorial_board) of the Proceedings of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) covering [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science) from 2000 to 2006 and starting in 2010, he chaired the Scientific Advisory Committee of the [Max Planck Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute) for [Demographic](wikipedia://en/45642) Research in [Rostock, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock) from 1999 to 2004 and the Committee on Population of the National Research Council from 2003 to 2008.[^o3]

Additionally, he was a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) Committee on Publications (2015–2023), the [Board of Directors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_directors) of the [Population Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_Association_of_America) (1999–2002), and the [Board of Directors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_directors) of the [Social Science Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Science_Research_Council) (1993–2000).[^o12]

## Research {#Research}

In 1971, with [Peter Laslett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Laslett) and Eugene Hammel, Wachter developed the SOCSIM [demographic](wikipedia://en/45642) [microsimulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsimulation) program, the first to track [kinship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinship) through parents of both sexes with rates changing over time,[^o14] a [prototype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prototype) for the agent-based models that have become common in social science. Beginning with *Statistical Studies of Historical Social Structure* (1978), SOCSIM has been applied to a host of problems in [kinship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinship) forecasting, [population structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_structure_%28genetics%29), [incest taboos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incest_taboo), [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_%28human_categorization%29) and family, [orphanhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orphan), and bereavement.[^o15] It is one of the few creations from the early days of scientific computing still in use after fifty years.[^o16]

*Height, Health, and History* (1990) by [Roderick Floud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roderick_Floud), Annabel Gregory, and Wachter opened up the use of stature to estimate changes in living standards, analyzing English military and charity measurements of heights from the 1700s onward.[^o17] In the 1980s, Wachter partnered with Dr. George R. Merriam III to develop the PULSAR approach to peak detection in episodic [hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone) secretion series,[^o18] which has continued in use among [endocrinologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrinology) into the 2020s.[^o19][^o20]

The volume *Between Zeus and the Salmon* (1997), edited by Wachter and [Caleb Finch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleb_Finch), consolidated the new field of the [biodemography](wikipedia://en/7521584) of [aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageing).[^o21] David Steinsaltz, Steve Evans, and Wachter went on to formulate [mathematical models](wikipedia://en/20590) with rich demographic structure for the [evolutionary process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) of [mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation) accumulation, helping elucidate commonalities across species in processes of [senescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senescence).[^o22] Wachter's textbook *Essential Demographic Methods* (2014) brought conceptual depth to the teaching of formal [demography](wikipedia://en/45642).[^o23]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Throughout his career, Wachter has received awards for his scientific and academic contributions. He was named a [Presidential Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Scholars_Program) in 1964 and went on to be elected a Fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 1986.[^o25] In 1988, the [Population Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_Association_of_America) honored him with the Mindel C. Sheps Award for Outstanding Contributions to Mathematical [Demography](wikipedia://en/45642).[^o26] His research and leadership earned him fellowship in both the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 1997,[^o27] followed by election as a member of the [U.S. National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1999.[^o3] Beyond his research achievements, UC Berkeley's Division of [Social Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science) recognized him with the Distinguished Teaching Award in 2002 and the Distinguished Service Award in 2008, alongside the Alumni "Letter in Life" Award from [The Pingry School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingry_School) in 2007.[^o28]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He is married to Bernadette Bell and lives in [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California) and on [The Sea Ranch, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Ranch,_California).[^o5]
