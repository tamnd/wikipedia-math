# Walter Hayman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Walter Hayman"}

**Walter Kurt Hayman** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (formerly **Haymann**; 6 January 1926 – 1 January 2020) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for contributions to [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).[^o3] He was a professor at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London).[^o4]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Hayman was born in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), Germany, the son of Roman law professor Franz Haymann (1874–1947) and Ruth Therese Hensel, daughter of mathematician [Kurt Hensel](wikipedia://en/2242565). He was a great-great-grandson of acclaimed composer [Fanny Mendelssohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanny_Mendelssohn). Because of his Jewish heritage, he left Germany, then under Nazi rule, alone by train in 1938. He continued his schooling at [Gordonstoun School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordonstoun_School),[^o7] and later at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge) under [John Edensor Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371) and his doctoral advisor [Mary Cartwright](wikipedia://en/1529094). He taught at [King's College, Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Newcastle), and the [University of Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Exeter).[^o6]

In 1947, he married [Margaret Riley Crann](wikipedia://en/58711225) after they met at a Quaker meeting.[^o8] Together, they founded the [British Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/3264277).[^o9] The pair had three daughters, including the peace activist Carolyn Hayman and the filmmaker [Sheila Hayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheila_Hayman).[^o6]

He is known for his asymptotic results in [Bieberbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieberbach_conjecture) in 1955,[^o10] and for Hayman's alternatives in [Nevanlinna Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevanlinna_Theory). His work with [Wolfgang Fuchs](wikipedia://en/5397705) gave a solution to an inverse problem of the Nevanlinna theory for entire functions, predating [David Drasin](wikipedia://en/56903444)'s 1976 work.

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

Hayman was elected to the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1956 and of the [Finnish Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters) in 1978:[^o12] he was elected "Foreign member" of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) on 16 December 1985.[^o13] In 1992 he received an honorary doctorate from the Faculty of Mathematics and Science at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University), Sweden[^o14] In 1995 he was awarded the [De Morgan Medal](wikipedia://en/1096532) by the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o15] In 2008, an issue of the Journal *Computational Methods and Function Theory* was dedicated to him on the occasion of his 80th birthday.[^o16]
