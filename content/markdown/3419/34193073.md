# Marvin Knopp

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Marvin Knopp"}

**Marvin Isadore Knopp** (January 4, 1933 – December 24, 2011) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked primarily in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He made notable contributions to the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000).

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Knopp was born on January 4, 1933, in [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois). He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) under [Paul T. Bateman](wikipedia://en/26642296) from the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois) in 1958 where he became friends with fellow student [Gene Golub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Golub).[^o5] Over the course of his career, he advised twenty Ph.D. students.[^o6] He is the father of pianist Seth Knopp, and of Yehudah, Abby, and Elana.[^o7] Marvin was married to Josephine Zadovsky Knopp for 25 years. Knopp died on December 24, 2011, during a vacation in Florida with his partner of 30 years and common law wife Phyllis Zemble. Marvin found happiness from his children, old movies, great music and numbers. During the 6 years following his death, his papers and books were organized (with the help of Wladimer Pribitkin), his photographs and his mathematical correspondence, were donated to the [American Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/3248005) (AIM). On AIM's website, you can find 131 of [Knopp's reprints](https://aimath.org/cgi-bin/library.cgi?database=reprints;mode=display;BrowseTitle=Knopp,%20Marvin).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Knopp was born in [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois) in 1933. He was an [Ashkenazi Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews).

## Career {#Career}

After receiving his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in 1958, Knopp taught at the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin) and then, for a few years, at the [University of Illinois Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Chicago) before moving, in 1976, to [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University) where he stayed until his sudden death in 2011.[^o10] Knopp was a leading expert in the theory of modular forms and a pioneering figure in the theory of [Eichler cohomology](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Eichler_cohomology), modular integrals and generalized modular forms. He was closely associated with [Emil Grosswald](wikipedia://en/21442284).[^o11] In [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084)'s influential book *A Panorama of Pure Mathematics* ([Academic Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Press), 1982), he is mentioned (p. 95) as one of those who "made substantial contributions" to the theory of modular forms.[^o12]
