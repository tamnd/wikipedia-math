# Harry Pollard (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harry Pollard"}

**Harry Pollard** (28 February 1919 – November 20, 1985)[^o3] was an American mathematician. He received his PhD from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1942 under the supervision of [David Widder](wikipedia://en/14428679). He then taught at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), and was professor of mathematics at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) from 1961 until his death in 1985. He is known for his work on [celestial mechanics](wikipedia://en/102182), [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) and the [n-body problem](wikipedia://en/23859945)[^o3] as well as for the several textbooks he authored or co-authored.[^o9][^o12] In the theory of Orthogonal polynomials, Pollard solved a conjecture of [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477), establishing mean convergence of the partial sums in $L^p$ norms for the [Legendre polynomials](wikipedia://en/100349) and [Jacobi polynomials](wikipedia://en/30863587) in a series of three papers in the Transactions of the American Mathematical Society. The first of these papers deals with the fundamental case of [Legendre polynomials](wikipedia://en/100349).[^o5] The end point case in Pollard's theorem was established by [Sagun Chanillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagun_Chanillo).[^o6]

:::
