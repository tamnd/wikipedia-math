# Network flow problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Network flow problem"}

In [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555), **network flow problems** are a class of computational problems in which the input is a [flow network](wikipedia://en/645676) (a graph with numerical capacities on its edges), and the goal is to construct a [flow](wikipedia://en/645676#Flows), numerical values on each edge that respect the capacity constraints and that have incoming flow equal to outgoing flow at all vertices except for certain designated terminals.[^o2]

:::

Specific types of network flow problems include:

- The [maximum flow problem](wikipedia://en/403165), in which the goal is to maximize the total amount of flow out of the source terminals and into the sink terminals[^o2]
- The [minimum-cost flow problem](wikipedia://en/6807932), in which the edges have costs as well as capacities and the goal is to achieve a given amount of flow (or a maximum flow) that has the minimum possible cost[^o2]
- The [multi-commodity flow problem](wikipedia://en/6809151), in which one must construct multiple flows for different commodities whose total flow amounts together respect the capacities[^o2]
- [Nowhere-zero flow](wikipedia://en/9909979), a type of flow studied in combinatorics in which the flow amounts are restricted to a [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742) of nonzero values

The [max-flow min-cut theorem](wikipedia://en/78130) equates the value of a maximum flow to the value of a [minimum cut](wikipedia://en/3562453), a partition of the vertices of the flow network that minimizes the total capacity of edges crossing from one side of the partition to the other. [Approximate max-flow min-cut theorems](wikipedia://en/44528136) provide an extension of this result to multi-commodity flow problems. The [Gomory–Hu tree](wikipedia://en/24093092) of an undirected flow network provides a concise representation of all minimum cuts between different pairs of terminal vertices.

[Algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) for constructing flows include

- [Dinic's algorithm](wikipedia://en/23635821), a strongly polynomial algorithm for maximum flow[^o2]
- The [Edmonds–Karp algorithm](wikipedia://en/239230), a faster strongly polynomial algorithm for maximum flow
- The [Ford–Fulkerson algorithm](wikipedia://en/53777), a [greedy algorithm](wikipedia://en/89247) for maximum flow that is not in general strongly polynomial
- The [network simplex algorithm](wikipedia://en/46762817), a method based on linear programming but specialized for network flow[^o2]
- The [out-of-kilter algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out-of-kilter_algorithm) for minimum-cost flow[^o2]
- The [push–relabel maximum flow algorithm](wikipedia://en/3444072), one of the most efficient known techniques for maximum flow

Otherwise the problem can be formulated as a more conventional [linear program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_program) or similar and solved using a general purpose optimization solver.
