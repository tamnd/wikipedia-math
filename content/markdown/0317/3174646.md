# Free Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Free Lie algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **free Lie algebra** over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* is a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) generated by a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) *X*, without any imposed relations other than the defining relations of alternating *K*-bilinearity and the [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Free lie.png.*

The definition of the free Lie algebra generated by a set *X* is as follows:

Let *X* be a set and $i\colon X \to L$ a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) of sets ([function](wikipedia://en/185427)) from *X* into a Lie algebra *L*. The Lie algebra *L* is called **free on *X*** if $i$ is the [universal morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_morphism); that is, if for any Lie algebra *A* with a morphism of sets $f\colon X \to A$, there is a unique Lie algebra morphism $g\colon L\to A$ such that $f = g \circ i$.

Given a set *X*, one can show that there exists a unique free Lie algebra $L(X)$ generated by *X*.

In the language of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) sending a set *X* to the Lie algebra generated by *X* is the [free functor](wikipedia://en/333835#Free_functor) from the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) to the category of Lie algebras. That is, it is [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint) to the [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783).

The free Lie algebra on a set *X* is naturally [graded](wikipedia://en/5475402). The 1-graded component of the free Lie algebra is just the [free vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_vector_space) on that set.

One can alternatively define a free Lie algebra on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) *V* as left adjoint to the forgetful functor from Lie algebras over a field *K* to vector spaces over the field *K* – forgetting the Lie algebra structure, but remembering the vector space structure.

:::

## Universal enveloping algebra {#Universal_enveloping_algebra}

The [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) of a free Lie algebra on a set *X* is the [free associative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_associative_algebra) generated by *X*. By the [Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem](wikipedia://en/762696) it is the "same size" as the symmetric algebra of the free Lie algebra (meaning that if both sides are graded by giving elements of *X* degree 1 then they are [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) as graded vector spaces). This can be used to describe the dimension of the piece of the free Lie algebra of any given degree.

[Ernst Witt](wikipedia://en/2619911) showed that the number of [basic commutators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_commutator) of degree *k* in the free Lie algebra on an *m*-element set is given by the [necklace polynomial](wikipedia://en/430792):

$$M_m(k) = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{d|k}\mu(d)\cdot m^{k/d},$$

where $\mu$ is the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961).

The graded dual of the universal enveloping algebra of a free Lie algebra on a finite set is the [shuffle algebra](wikipedia://en/32234404). This essentially follows because universal enveloping algebras have the structure of a [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714), and the [shuffle product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuffle_product) describes the action of comultiplication in this algebra. See [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628) for a detailed exposition of the inter-relation between the shuffle product and comultiplication.

## Hall sets {#Hall_sets}

An explicit basis of the free Lie algebra can be given in terms of a **Hall set**, which is a particular kind of subset inside the [free magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_magma) on *X*. Elements of the free magma are [binary trees](wikipedia://en/4321), with their leaves labelled by elements of *X*. Hall sets were introduced by (txt) based on work of [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033) on groups. Subsequently, [Wilhelm Magnus](wikipedia://en/8337666) showed that they arise as the [graded Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/5475402) associated with the filtration on a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) given by the [lower central series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_central_series). This correspondence was motivated by [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193) identities in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) due to Philip Hall and Witt.

## Lyndon basis {#Lyndon_basis}

The [Lyndon words](wikipedia://en/3919355) are a special case of the [Hall words](wikipedia://en/31782613), and so in particular there is a basis of the free Lie algebra corresponding to Lyndon words. This is called the **Lyndon basis**, named after [Roger Lyndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Lyndon). (This is also called the Chen–Fox–Lyndon basis or the Lyndon–Shirshov basis, and is essentially the same as the **Shirshov basis**.) There is a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) γ from the Lyndon words in an ordered alphabet to a basis of the free Lie algebra on this alphabet defined as follows:

- If a word *w* has length 1 then $\gamma(w) = w$ (considered as a generator of the free Lie algebra).
- If *w* has length at least 2, then write $w = uv$ for Lyndon words *u*, *v* with *v* as long as possible (the "standard factorization"[^o8]). Then $\gamma(w) = [\gamma(u),\gamma(v)]$.

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Shirshov–Witt theorem"}

## Shirshov–Witt theorem {#Shirshov–Witt_theorem}

(txt) and (txt) showed that any [Lie subalgebra](wikipedia://en/17944#Subalgebras,_ideals_and_homomorphisms) of a free Lie algebra is itself a free Lie algebra.

:::

::: {#o14 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

[Serre's theorem on a semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/62858445) uses a free Lie algebra to construct a semisimple algebra out of generators and relations.

The [Milnor invariants](wikipedia://en/22722334#Milnor_invariants) of a [link group](wikipedia://en/22722334) are related to the free Lie algebra on the components of the [link](wikipedia://en/1161784), as discussed in that article.

See also [Lie operad](wikipedia://en/59501534) for the use of a free Lie algebra in the construction of the operad.

:::
