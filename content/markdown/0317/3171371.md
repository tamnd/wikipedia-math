# Generator (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generator"}

*Image: One5Root.svg.* The 5th [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity) in the complex plane under multiplication form a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of order 5. Each non-identity element by itself is a generator for the whole group.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the term **generator** or **generating set** may refer to any of a number of related concepts. The underlying concept in each case is that of a smaller [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of objects, together with a set of [operations](wikipedia://en/4140245) that can be applied to it, that result in the creation of a larger collection of objects, called the **generated set**. The larger set is then said to be **generated by** the smaller set. It is commonly the case that the generating set has a simpler set of properties than the generated set, thus making it easier to discuss and examine. It is usually the case that properties of the generating set are in some way preserved by the act of generation; likewise, the properties of the generated set are often reflected in the generating set.

:::

## List of generators {#List_of_generators}

A list of examples of generating sets follow.

- Generating set or [spanning set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanning_set) of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370): a set that spans the vector space
- [Generating set of a group](wikipedia://en/99945): a subset of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) that is not contained in any [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of the group other than the entire group
- [Generating set of a ring](wikipedia://en/24873145#Subring_generated_by_a_set): A subset *S* of a ring *A* **generates** *A* if the only [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of *A* containing *S* is *A*
- [Generating set of an ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generating_set_of_an_ideal) in a ring
- [Generating set of a module](wikipedia://en/50718738)
- A [generator](wikipedia://en/9550415), in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), is an [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) that can be used to distinguish [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891)
- In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a collection of sets that generate the topology is called a [subbase](wikipedia://en/395395)
- Generating set of a [topological algebra](wikipedia://en/2688895): *S* is a generating set of a [topological algebra](wikipedia://en/2688895) *A* if the smallest closed [subalgebra](wikipedia://en/45239) of *A* containing *S* is *A*
- Generating a [σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/29586#σ-algebra_generated_by_an_arbitrary_family) by a collection of subsets

## Differential equations {#Differential_equations}

In the study of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), and commonly those occurring in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), one has the idea of a set of infinitesimal displacements that can be extended to obtain a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470), or at least, a local part of it, by means of integration. The general concept is of using the [exponential map](wikipedia://en/276548) to take the vectors in the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) and extend them, as [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096), to an open set surrounding the tangent point. In this case, it is not unusual to call the elements of the tangent space the *generators* of the manifold. When the manifold possesses some sort of symmetry, there is also the related notion of a *charge* or *current*, which is sometimes also called the generator, although, strictly speaking, charges are not elements of the tangent space.

- Elements of the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) to a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) are sometimes referred to as "generators of the group," especially by physicists.[^o4] The [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) can be thought of as the infinitesimal vectors generating the group, at least locally, by means of the [exponential map](wikipedia://en/44612290), but the Lie algebra does not form a generating set in the strict sense.[^o5]
- In [stochastic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_processes), an [Itō diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō_diffusion) or more general [Itō process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō_process) has an [infinitesimal generator](wikipedia://en/12193437).
- The **generator** of any [continuous symmetry](wikipedia://en/3667668) implied by [Noether's theorem](wikipedia://en/150159), the generators of a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) being a special case. In this case, a generator is sometimes called a [charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_%28physics%29) or [Noether charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noether_charge), examples include:
  - [angular momentum](wikipedia://en/2839#Total_angular_momentum_as_generator_of_rotations) as the generator of [rotations](wikipedia://en/39789),[^o6]
  - [linear momentum](wikipedia://en/20431#Symmetry_and_conservation) as the generator of [translations](wikipedia://en/204682),[^o6]
  - [electric charge](wikipedia://en/9804) being the generator of the [U(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%281%29) symmetry group of [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism),
  - the [color charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge) of [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark) are the generators of the [SU(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%283%29) [color symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge) in [quantum chromodynamics](wikipedia://en/25264),
- More precisely, "charge" should apply only to the [root system](wikipedia://en/277087) of a Lie group.
