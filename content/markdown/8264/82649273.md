# David Gamarnik

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Gamarnik"}

**David Gamarnik** is an American [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician), and a Nanyang Technological University Professor in the Operations Research and Statistics Group at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology)'s [Sloan School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_School_of_Management).[^o3] His research spans discrete probability, statistics and machine learning, algorithms and optimization, quantum computing, stochastic processes and queueing theory.[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Gamarnik was born in [Tbilisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tbilisi), [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_%28country%29) and completed part of his undergraduate studies at [Tbilisi State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tbilisi_State_University) in 1990. He received a B.A. in mathematics from [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) in 1993.[^o4] He received his Ph.D. in Operations Research from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) in 1998, with a dissertation titled *Stability and Performance of Multiclass Queueing Networks*.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Gamarnik worked as a research staff member at [IBM Thomas J. Watson Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Thomas_J._Watson_Research_Center) (1997–2005).[^o4] He joined the [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) [Sloan School of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_School_of_Management) in 2005, serving as an Assistant Professor (2005–2007), Associate Professor (2007–2012), and full Professor (since 2012).[^o4]

He has written over 150 scientific publications and a textbook, and made profound contributions to numerous domains, including discrete probability, random structures, algorithms and combinatorial optimization, statistics and machine learning, quantum computing, stochastic processes, as well as queueing theory.[^o8][^o9][^o10]

Along with [Madhu Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhu_Sudan), he introduced Overlap Gap Property, a new method for understanding [average-case complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average-case_complexity), based on theory of [spin glasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_glasses).[^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15]

He has served in editorial roles (area editor/associate editor) for premier operations research and applied probability journals, including *[Mathematics of Operations Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_Operations_Research)*, the *[Annals of Applied Probability](wikipedia://en/31018340)*, *[Queueing Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queueing_Systems)*, *[Stochastic Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_Systems)*, and [*Operations Research*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_Research_%28journal%29).[^o16]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Gamarnik has received multiple honors and awards from professional societies, including:

- Erlang Prize (Applied Probability Society of [INFORMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences), 2004)[^o18]
- INFORMS Applied Probability Society Best Publication Award (2011)[^o19]
- INFORMS Franz Edelman Prize Laureate (2014)[^o20]
- Fellow of INFORMS (2021)[^o21]
- Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (Class of 2022)[^o22]
- Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) (2023)[^o23]

## Books {#Books}

- *Queueing Theory: Classical and Modern Methods*, 2022.[^o25]
