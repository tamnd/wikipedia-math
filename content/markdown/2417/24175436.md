# Ellingham–Horton graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ellingham–Horton graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Ellingham–Horton graphs** are two 3-[regular graphs](wikipedia://en/85821) on 54 and 78 vertices: the **Ellingham–Horton 54-graph** and the **Ellingham–Horton 78-graph**. They are named after Joseph D. Horton and [Mark N. Ellingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ellingham), their discoverers. These two graphs provide counterexamples to the conjecture of [W. T. Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021) that every [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) 3-connected [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph).[^o4] The [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) of the **Ellingham-Horton 54-graph** and the **Ellingham-Horton 78-graph** is 3 and the [queue numbers](wikipedia://en/46183215) 2.[^o5] The **Ellingham-Horton 54-graph** is [1-planar](wikipedia://en/38249632).[^o6]

:::

The first counterexample to the Tutte conjecture was the [Horton graph](wikipedia://en/24116567), published by (Bondy Murty 1976).[^o8] After the Horton graph, a number of smaller counterexamples to the Tutte conjecture were found. Among them are a 92-vertex graph by (Horton 1982),[^o10] a 78-vertex graph by (Owens 1983),[^o12] and the two Ellingham–Horton graphs.

The first Ellingham–Horton graph was published by (Ellingham 1981) and is of order 78.[^o14] At that time it was the smallest known counterexample to the Tutte conjecture. The second Ellingham–Horton graph was published by (Ellingham Horton 1983) and is of order 54.[^o16] In 1989, Georges' graph, the smallest currently-known Non-Hamiltonian 3-connected cubic bipartite graph was discovered, containing 50 vertices.[^o17]
