# Dan-Virgil Voiculescu

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dan-Virgil Voiculescu"}

**Dan-Virgil Voiculescu** (; born 14 June 1949) is a [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). He has worked in single [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), operator [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748) and [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185). More recently, he developed [free probability](wikipedia://en/679696) theory.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Voiculescu studied at the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest), receiving his PhD in 1977 under the direction of [Ciprian Foias](wikipedia://en/28465086). He was an assistant at the University of Bucharest (1972–1973), a researcher at the [Institute of Mathematics of the Romanian Academy](wikipedia://en/8609230) (1973–1975), and a researcher at [INCREST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INCREST) (1975–1986). He came to Berkeley in 1986 for the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), and stayed on as visiting professor. Voiculescu was appointed professor at Berkeley in 1987.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

He received the 2004 [NAS Award in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Award_in_Mathematics) from the National Academy of Sciences (NAS) for “the theory of free probability, in particular, using [random matrices](wikipedia://en/1648765) and a new concept of [entropy](wikipedia://en/9891) to solve several hitherto intractable problems in von Neumann algebras.”[^o6]

Voiculescu was elected to the National Academy of Sciences in 2006. In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7]
