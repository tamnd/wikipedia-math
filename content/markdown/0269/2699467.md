# Bidirected graph

*Image: Bidirected graph features.svg.* The different types of edge in a bidirected graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bidirected graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) domain of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **bidirected graph** (introduced by (Edmonds Johnson 1970))[^o3] is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) in which each [edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29) is given an independent [orientation](wikipedia://en/36619818) (or direction, or arrow) at each end.

:::

There are three kinds of bidirected edges: ***extraverted***, where the arrows point outward, towards the [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899), at both ends; ***introverted***, where both arrows point inward, away from the vertices; and ***directed***, in which the two arrows have the same direction (one arrow points away from its vertex and towards the opposite end, while the other arrow points in the same direction as the first, away from the opposite end and towards its own vertex). The bidirected edges of kind *directed* are the same as ordinary directed edges in a [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986); thus, a directed graph is a special kind of bidirected graph.

It is sometimes desirable to have also edges with only one end (**half-edges**); these get only one arrow. An edge with no ends (a **loose edge**) has no arrows. The edges that are neither half nor loose edges may be called **ordinary edges**.

A [skew-symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/17283742) is the [double covering graph](wikipedia://en/21241712) of a bidirected graph.

A bidirected graph may be regarded as an orientation of a [signed graph](wikipedia://en/2472618), similarly to how a directed graph may be viewed as an orientation of an ordinary [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph).

## Other meanings {#Other_meanings}

A symmetric [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986) (that is, a directed graph in which the reverse of every edge is also an edge) is sometimes also called a "bidirected graph".[^o5]
