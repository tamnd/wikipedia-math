# Thébault's theorem

*Image: Thebault 1 2 3.svg.* Thébault's 3 problems

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thébault's theorem"}

**Thébault's theorem** is the name given variously to one of the [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) problems proposed by the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Victor Thébault](wikipedia://en/2696057), individually known as Thébault's problem I, II, and III.

:::

## Thébault's problem I {#Thébault's_problem_I}

Given any [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496), construct on its sides four [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) external to the parallelogram. The [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) formed by joining the centers of those four squares is a square.[^o3]

It is a special case of [van Aubel's theorem](wikipedia://en/3021425) and a square version of [Napoleon's theorem](wikipedia://en/2728914). All three of these theorems are just a special case of [Petr–Douglas–Neumann theorem](wikipedia://en/35762367).

*Image: Thebault problem I tiling pattern.png.* Tiling pattern based on Thébault's problem I

## Thébault's problem II {#Thébault's_problem_II}

Given a square, construct [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) on two adjacent edges, either both inside or both outside the square. Then the triangle formed by joining the vertex of the square distant from both triangles and the vertices of the triangles distant from the square is equilateral.[^o5]

## Thébault's problem III {#Thébault's_problem_III}

Given any [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) ABC, and any point M on BC, construct the [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle) and [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) of the triangle. Then construct two additional circles, each [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to AM, BC, and to the circumcircle. Then their centers and the center of the incircle are collinear.[^o7][^o8]

Until 2003, academia thought this third problem of Thébault the most difficult to [prove](wikipedia://en/82285). It was published in the [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly) in 1938, and proved by [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands) mathematician H. Streefkerk in 1973. However, in 2003, Jean-Louis Ayme discovered that Sawayama Yuzaburo, an instructor at The Central Military School of Tokyo, independently proposed and solved this problem in 1905.[^o9]

An "external" version of this theorem, where the incircle is replaced by an excircle and the two additional circles are external to the circumcircle, is found in Shay Gueron (2002). [^o10] A proof based on [Casey's theorem](wikipedia://en/14599476) is in the paper.
