# Raychaudhuri equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Raychaudhuri equation"}

In [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), the **Raychaudhuri equation**, or **Landau–Raychaudhuri equation**,[^o2] is a fundamental result describing the motion of nearby bits of matter.

:::

The equation is important as a fundamental lemma for the [Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems](wikipedia://en/294408) and for the study of [exact solutions in general relativity](wikipedia://en/1686520), but has independent interest, since it offers a simple and general validation of our intuitive expectation that [gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation) should be a universal attractive force between any two bits of [mass–energy](wikipedia://en/422481) in general relativity, as it is in [Newton's theory of gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_universal_gravitation).

The equation was discovered independently by the Indian physicist [Amal Kumar Raychaudhuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amal_Kumar_Raychaudhuri)[^o3] and the Soviet physicist [Lev Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Landau).[^o4]

## Mathematical statement {#Mathematical_statement}

Given a [timelike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timelike) unit [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) $\vec{X}$ (which can be interpreted as a family or [congruence](wikipedia://en/2698313) of nonintersecting [world lines](wikipedia://en/165487) via the [integral curve](wikipedia://en/1322061), not necessarily [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096)), Raychaudhuri's equation in $D$ spacetime dimensions can be written as

$$\dot{\theta} = - \frac{\theta^2}{D-1} - 2 \sigma^2 + 2 \omega^2 - {E[\vec{X}]^a}_a + {{\dot{X}^a}}_{;a}$$

where

$$2\sigma^2 = \sigma_{mn} \, \sigma^{mn}, \; 2\omega^2 = \omega_{mn} \, \omega^{mn}$$

are (non-negative) quadratic invariants of the *shear tensor*

$$\sigma_{ab} = \theta_{ab} - \frac{1}{D-1} \, \theta \, h_{ab}$$

and the *vorticity tensor*

$$\omega_{ab} = {h^m}_a \, {h^n}_b X_{[m;n]}$$

respectively. Here,

$$\theta_{ab} = {h^m}_a \, {h^n}_b X_{(m;n)}$$

is the *expansion tensor*, $\theta$ is its [trace](wikipedia://en/43270), called the *expansion scalar*, and

$$h_{ab} = g_{ab} + X_a \, X_b$$

is the *projection tensor* onto the hyperplanes orthogonal to $\vec{X}$. Also, dot denotes differentiation with respect to [proper time](wikipedia://en/494418) counted along the world lines in the congruence. Finally, the trace of the [tidal tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrogravitic_tensor) $E[\vec{X}]_{ab}$ can also be written as

$${E[\vec{X}]^a}_{a} = R_{mn} \, X^m \, X^n$$

This quantity is sometimes called the *Raychaudhuri scalar*.

## Intuitive significance {#Intuitive_significance}

The expansion scalar measures the fractional rate at which the volume of a small ball of matter changes with respect to time as measured by a central comoving observer (and so it may take negative values). In other words, the above equation gives us the evolution equation for the expansion of the timelike congruence. If the derivative (with respect to proper time) of this quantity turns out to be *negative* along some world line (after a certain event), then any expansion of a small ball of matter (whose center of mass follows the world line in question) must be followed by recollapse. If not, continued expansion is possible.

The shear tensor measures any tendency of an initially spherical ball of matter to become distorted into an ellipsoidal shape. The vorticity tensor measures any tendency of nearby world lines to twist about one another (if this happens, our small blob of matter is rotating, as happens to fluid elements in an ordinary fluid flow which exhibits nonzero vorticity).

The right hand side of Raychaudhuri's equation consists of two types of terms:

1. terms which promote (re)-collapse
   - initially nonzero expansion scalar,
   - nonzero shearing,
   - positive trace of the tidal tensor; this is precisely the condition guaranteed by assuming the *strong energy condition*, which holds for the most important types of solutions, such as physically reasonable [fluid solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_solution),
1. terms which oppose (re)-collapse
   - nonzero vorticity, corresponding to Newtonian [centrifugal forces](wikipedia://en/19265670),
   - positive divergence of the acceleration vector (e.g., outward pointing acceleration due to a spherically symmetric explosion, or more prosaically, due to body forces on fluid elements in a ball of fluid held together by its own self-gravitation).

Usually one term will win out. However, there are situations in which a balance can be achieved. This balance may be:

- *stable*: in the case of [hydrostatic equilibrium](wikipedia://en/51776) of a ball of perfect fluid (e.g. in a model of a stellar interior), the expansion, shear, and vorticity all vanish, and a radial divergence in the acceleration vector (the necessary [body force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_force) on each blob of fluid being provided by the pressure of surrounding fluid) counteracts the Raychaudhuri scalar, which for a perfect fluid in four dimensions is $E[\vec{X}]^a{}_a = 4 \pi ( \mu + 3 p )$ in [geometrized units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrized_unit_system). In Newtonian gravitation, the trace of the tidal tensor is $4 \pi \mu$; in general relativity, the tendency of pressure to oppose gravity is partially offset by this term, which under certain circumstances can become important.
- *unstable*: for example, the world lines of the dust particles in the [Gödel solution](wikipedia://en/2085068) have vanishing shear, expansion, and acceleration, but constant vorticity just balancing a constant Raychaudhuri scalar due to nonzero vacuum energy ("cosmological constant").

## Focusing theorem {#Focusing_theorem}

Suppose the strong [energy condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conditions) holds in some region of our spacetime, and let $\vec{X}$ be a timelike *geodesic* unit vector field with *vanishing vorticity*, or equivalently, which is hypersurface orthogonal. For example, this situation can arise in studying the world lines of the dust particles in cosmological models which are exact dust solutions of the Einstein field equation (provided that these world lines are not twisting about one another, in which case the congruence would have nonzero vorticity).

Then Raychaudhuri's equation becomes

$$\dot{\theta} = - \frac{\theta^2}{D-1} - 2 \sigma^2 - {E[\vec{X}]^a}_a$$

Now the right hand side is always negative or zero, so the expansion scalar never increases in time.

Since the last two terms are non-negative, we have

$$\dot{\theta} \leq - \frac{\theta^2}{D-1}$$

Integrating this inequality with respect to proper time $\tau$ gives

$$\frac{1}{\theta} \geq \frac{1}{\theta_0} + \frac{\tau}{D-1}$$

If the initial value $\theta_0$ of the expansion scalar is negative, this means that our geodesics must converge in a [caustic](wikipedia://en/880406) ($\theta$ goes to minus infinity) within a proper time of at most $(D-1)/|\theta_0|$ after the measurement of the initial value $\theta_0$ of the expansion scalar. This need not signal an encounter with a curvature singularity, but it does signal a breakdown in our mathematical description of the motion of the dust.

:::

## Optical equations {#Optical_equations}

There is also an optical (or null) version of Raychaudhuri's equation for null geodesic congruences : $\dot{\theta} = - \frac{1}{D-2}\theta^2 - 2 \sigma^2 + 2 \omega^2 - R_{\mu\nu} U^\mu U^\nu$.

Though it closely resembles the aforementioned timelike Raychaudhuri equation, one should note that there is now a requirement of having the congruence being geodesic, which was absent in the timelike case, and, in addition to that, that since $U_a$ is lightlike the projector $h_{ab}$ is defined instead as $g_{ab} + N_a U_b + U_a N_b$, where $N_a$ is any vector such that $N_a N^a = 0$ and $U_a N^a = -1$ ($N$ is never unique, but one can show the exact choice does not matter as long as those two conditions are met).

The most notable consequence of these changes is that $\theta$ does not represent a fractional volumetric increase anymore, but rather a fractional *areal* increase, as $h_a^b$ now projects onto a 2-dimensional subspace (more accurately, into a 2-*co*dimensional subspace, which is the same if $D = 4$).

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

### Applications {#Applications}

The [event horizon](wikipedia://en/29320146) is defined as the boundary of the [causal past](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causal_past) of null infinity. Such boundaries are generated by null geodesics. The affine parameter goes to infinity as we approach null infinity, and no caustics form until then. So, the expansion of the event horizon has to be nonnegative. As the expansion gives the rate of change of the logarithm of the area density, this means the event horizon area can never go down, at least classically, assuming the null energy condition.

:::
