# Signomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Signomial"}

A **signomial** is a kind of [function](wikipedia://en/185427) of one or more independent variables. It is perhaps most easily thought of as an extension of multivariable [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000)—an extension that permits exponents to be arbitrary real numbers (rather than just non-negative integers) while requiring the independent variables to be strictly positive (so that division by zero and other inappropriate arithmetic operations are not encountered).

:::

Formally, a signomial is a function with domain $\mathbb{R}_{>0}^n$ which takes values

$$f(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) = \sum_{i=1}^M \left(c_i \prod_{j=1}^n x_j^{a_{ij}}\right)$$

where the coefficients $c_i$ and the exponents $a_{ij}$ are real numbers. Signomials are [closed](wikipedia://en/293802) under addition, subtraction, multiplication, and scaling.

If we restrict all $c_i$ to be positive, then the function f is a [posynomial](wikipedia://en/9644721). Consequently, each signomial is either a posynomial, the negative of a posynomial, or the difference of two posynomials. If, in addition, all exponents $a_{ij}$ are non-negative integers, then the signomial becomes a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) whose domain is the positive [orthant](wikipedia://en/1283725).

For example,

$$f(x_1, x_2, x_3) = 2.7 x_1^2x_2^{-1/3}x_3^{0.7} - 2x_1^{-4}x_3^{2/5}$$

is a signomial.

The term "signomial" was introduced by Richard J. Duffin and Elmor L. Peterson in their seminal joint work on general algebraic optimization—published in the late 1960s and early 1970s. A recent introductory exposition involves [optimization problems](wikipedia://en/1126536).[^o2] [Nonlinear optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_optimization) problems with [constraints](wikipedia://en/4171950) and/or [objectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objective_function) defined by signomials are harder to solve than those defined by only posynomials, because (unlike posynomials) signomials cannot necessarily be made [convex](wikipedia://en/245568) by applying a logarithmic change of variables. Nevertheless, signomial optimization problems often provide a much more accurate mathematical representation of real-world nonlinear optimization problems.
