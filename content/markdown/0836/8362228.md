# Truncated triakis tetrahedron

*Image: Truncated triakis tetrahedron (green) 01.gif.* A truncated triakis tetrahedron

*Image: Truncated triakis tetrahedron.stl.* 3D model of a truncated triakis tetrahedron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Truncated triakis tetrahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **truncated triakis tetrahedron** is a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) with 16 [faces](wikipedia://en/149926): four sets of three [pentagons](wikipedia://en/20741014) with a shared vertex, arranged in a [tetrahedral](wikipedia://en/2904472) arrangement, with four [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733) in the remaining gaps. The faces cannot all be [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306), so it is a [near-miss Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/3881930). As a [fullerene](wikipedia://en/10628), it is called **tetrahedral fullerene** or **C<sub>28</sub> fullerene**, and has been suggested as the smallest stable carbon fullerene.

:::

## Chemistry {#Chemistry}

This structure is a [fullerene](wikipedia://en/10628), one of two 28-vertex fullerenes. In this context, it is called *tetrahedral fullerene*[^o5] or C<sub>28</sub> fullerene.[^o7] It has been suggested that, as an [allotrope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allotrope) of carbon (C<sub>28</sub>), it may form the smallest stable fullerene,[^o6] and experiments have found it to be stabilized by encapsulating a metal atom.[^o7] Its tetrahedral symmetry and its four irregular vertices where three pentagons meet make it [tetravalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetravalent), likely either to form tetravalent clusters of fullerenes or to encapsulate tetravalent atoms such as [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium).[^o8]

## Geometry {#Geometry}

This polyhedron is one of the polyhedra studied in 1935 by Michael Goldberg as a possible solution to the [isoperimetric problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoperimetric_problem) of maximizing the volume for a given number of faces (16 in this case) and a given surface area.[^o10] For this optimization problem, the optimal geometric form for the polyhedron is one in which the faces are all tangent to an [inscribed sphere](wikipedia://en/991786).[^o11] Despite the connection with Goldberg, this polyhedron is not a [Goldberg polyhedron](wikipedia://en/39781140), because it has only [tetrahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2904472) rather than the [icosahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2877844) of the Goldberg polyhedra.[^o12]

The pentagons of this polyhedron cannot be [regular pentagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon), because if they were then the hexagonal faces would not be flat. As a polyhedron that has near-regular but not regular faces, it is an example of a [near-miss Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/3881930), "the closest near miss with higher than prismatic symmetry".[^o13]

A combinatorially equivalent (but not necessarily geometrically congruent) polyhedron can be constructed from a [triakis tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/722548) by [truncating](wikipedia://en/4224429) the order-6 vertices. The truncated vertices become four hexagon faces, and the obtuse triangles of the triakis tetrahedron are truncated at their acute vertices into pentagons.

## Hexakis Truncated Tetrahedron {#Hexakis_Truncated_Tetrahedron}

The dual of the *truncated triakis tetrahedron* is called a **hexakis truncated tetrahedron**. It is constructed by augmenting the hexagonal faces of a [truncated tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/279651) with pyramids.
