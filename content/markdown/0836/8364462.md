# Energetic space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Energetic space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more precisely in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), an **energetic space** is, intuitively, a subspace of a given [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) equipped with a new "energetic" [inner product](wikipedia://en/14856). The motivation for the name comes from [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), as in many physical problems the [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) of a system can be expressed in terms of the energetic inner product.

:::

## Energetic space {#Energetic_space}

Formally, consider a real Hilbert space $X$ with the [inner product](wikipedia://en/14856) $(\cdot|\cdot)$ and the [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) $\|\cdot\|$. Let $Y$ be a linear subspace of $X$ and $B:Y\to X$ be a [strongly monotone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongly_monotone) [symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_operator) [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator), that is, a linear operator satisfying

- $(Bu|v)=(u|Bv)\,$ for all $u, v$ in $Y$
- $(Bu|u) \ge c\|u\|^2$ for some constant $c>0$ and all $u$ in $Y.$

The **energetic inner product** is defined as

$(u|v)_E =(Bu|v)\,$ for all $u,v$ in $Y$

and the **energetic norm** is

$\|u\|_E=(u|u)^\frac{1}{2}_E \,$ for all $u$ in $Y.$

The set $Y$ together with the energetic inner product is a [pre-Hilbert space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Hilbert_space). The **energetic space** $X_E$ is defined as the [completion](wikipedia://en/7832) of $Y$ in the energetic norm. $X_E$ can be considered a subset of the original Hilbert space $X,$ since any [Cauchy sequence](wikipedia://en/6085) in the energetic norm is also Cauchy in the norm of $X$ (this follows from the strong monotonicity property of $B$).

The energetic inner product is extended from $Y$ to $X_E$ by

$$(u|v)_E = \lim_{n\to\infty} (u_n|v_n)_E$$

where $(u_n)$ and $(v_n)$ are sequences in *Y* that converge to points in $X_E$ in the energetic norm.

## Energetic extension {#Energetic_extension}

The operator $B$ admits an **energetic extension** $B_E$

$$B_E:X_E\to X^*_E$$

defined on $X_E$ with values in the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) $X^*_E$ that is given by the formula

$\langle B_E u | v \rangle_E = (u|v)_E$ for all $u,v$ in $X_E.$

Here, $\langle \cdot |\cdot \rangle_E$ denotes the duality bracket between $X^*_E$ and $X_E,$ so $\langle B_E u | v \rangle_E$ actually denotes $(B_E u)(v).$

If $u$ and $v$ are elements in the original subspace $Y,$ then

$$\langle B_E u | v \rangle_E = (u|v)_E = (Bu|v) = \langle u|B|v\rangle$$

by the definition of the energetic inner product. If one views $Bu,$ which is an element in $X,$ as an element in the dual $X^*$ via the [Riesz representation theorem](wikipedia://en/26452), then $Bu$ will also be in the dual $X_E^*$ (by the strong monotonicity property of $B$). Via these identifications, it follows from the above formula that $B_E u= Bu.$ In different words, the original operator $B:Y\to X$ can be viewed as an operator $B:Y\to X_E^*,$ and then $B_E:X_E\to X^*_E$ is simply the function extension of $B$ from $Y$ to $X_E.$

## An example from physics {#An_example_from_physics}

*Image: String illust.svg.* A string with fixed endpoints under the influence of a force pointing down.

Consider a [string](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rope) whose endpoints are fixed at two points $a<b$ on the real line (here viewed as a horizontal line). Let the vertical outer [force density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_density) at each point $x$ $(a\le x \le b)$ on the string be $f(x)\mathbf{e}$, where $\mathbf{e}$ is a [unit vector](wikipedia://en/167053) pointing vertically and $f:[a, b]\to \mathbb R.$ Let $u(x)$ be the [deflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflection_%28engineering%29) of the string at the point $x$ under the influence of the force. Assuming that the deflection is small, the [elastic energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastic_energy) of the string is

$$\frac{1}{2} \int_a^b\! u'(x)^2\, dx$$

and the total [potential energy](wikipedia://en/23703) of the string is

$$F(u) = \frac{1}{2} \int_a^b\! u'(x)^2\,dx - \int_a^b\! u(x)f(x)\,dx.$$

The deflection $u(x)$ minimizing the potential energy will satisfy the [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309)

$$-u''=f\,$$

with [boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_conditions)

$$u(a)=u(b)=0.\,$$

To study this equation, consider the space $X=L^2(a, b),$ that is, the [Lp space](wikipedia://en/45194) of all [square-integrable functions](wikipedia://en/28680558) $u:[a, b]\to \mathbb R$ in respect to the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198). This space is Hilbert in respect to the inner product

$$(u|v)=\int_a^b\! u(x)v(x)\,dx,$$

with the norm being given by

$$\|u\|=\sqrt{(u|u)}.$$

Let $Y$ be the set of all [Locally integrable function](wikipedia://en/4258398) ($\text{L}^1_\text{loc}$) on $[a, b]$ that are [twice continuously differentiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) from $u:[a, b]\to \mathbb R$, with the [boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_conditions) $u(a)=u(b)=0.$ Then $Y$ is a linear subspace of $X.$

Consider the operator $B:Y\to X$ given by the formula

$$Bu = -u'',\,$$

so the deflection satisfies the equation $Bu=f.$ Using [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252) and the boundary conditions, one can see that

$$(Bu|v)=-\int_a^b\! u''(x)v(x)\, dx=\int_a^b u'(x)v'(x) = (u|Bv)$$

for any $u$ and $v$ in $Y.$ Therefore, $B$ is a symmetric linear operator.

$B$ is also strongly monotone, since, by the [Friedrichs' inequality](wikipedia://en/6613227)

$$\|u\|^2 = \int_a^b u^2(x)\, dx \le C \int_a^b u'(x)^2\, dx = C\,(Bu|u)$$

for some $C>0.$

The energetic space in respect to the operator $B$ is then the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) $H^1_0(a, b).$ We see that the elastic energy of the string which motivated this study is

$$\frac{1}{2} \int_a^b\! u'(x)^2\, dx = \frac{1}{2} (u|u)_E,$$

so it is half of the energetic inner product of $u$ with itself.

To calculate the deflection $u$ minimizing the total potential energy $F(u)$ of the string, one writes this problem in the form

$(u|v)_E=(f|v)\,$ for all $v$ in $X_E$.

Next, one usually approximates $u$ by some $u_h$, a function in a finite-dimensional subspace of the true solution space. For example, one might let $u_h$ be a continuous [piecewise linear function](wikipedia://en/444224) in the energetic space, which gives the [finite element method](wikipedia://en/18233581). The approximation $u_h$ can be computed by solving a [system of linear equations](wikipedia://en/113087).

The energetic norm turns out to be the natural norm in which to measure the error between $u$ and $u_h$, see [Céa's lemma](wikipedia://en/11076807).
