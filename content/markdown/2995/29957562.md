# Infinite expression

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Infinite expression"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **infinite expression** is an [expression](wikipedia://en/609125) in which some [operators](wikipedia://en/4140245) take an infinite number of [arguments](wikipedia://en/32842052), or in which the nesting of the operators continues to an infinite depth.[^o2] A generic concept for infinite expression can lead to ill-defined or self-inconsistent constructions (much like a [set of all sets](wikipedia://en/303405)), but there are several instances of infinite expressions that are well-defined.

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of well-defined infinite expressions are[^o5]

- [infinite sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_sum), such as

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n = a_0 + a_1 + a_2 + \cdots \,$$

- [infinite products](wikipedia://en/26382006), such as

$$\prod_{n=0}^\infty b_n = b_0 \times b_1 \times b_2 \times \cdots$$

- [infinite nested radicals](wikipedia://en/2503693#Infinitely_nested_radicals), such as

$$\sqrt{1+2\sqrt{1+3 \sqrt{1+\cdots}}}$$

- [infinite power towers](wikipedia://en/482379#Extension_to_infinite_heights),[^o6] such as

$$\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}^{\sqrt{2}^{\cdot^{\cdot^{\cdot}}}}}$$

- [infinite continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199), such as

$$c_0 + \underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\mathrm K}} \frac{1}{c_n} = c_0 + \cfrac{1}{c_1 + \cfrac{1}{c_2 + \cfrac{1}{c_3 + \cfrac{1}{c_4 + \ddots}}}},$$

where the left hand side uses [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125)'s [Kettenbruch notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_continued_fraction).[^o7]

In [infinitary logic](wikipedia://en/630478), one can use infinite [conjunctions](wikipedia://en/18152) and infinite [disjunctions](wikipedia://en/7962).

Even for well-defined infinite expressions, the *value* of the infinite expression may be ambiguous or not well-defined; for instance, there are multiple summation rules available for assigning values to series, and the same series may have different values according to different summation rules if the series is not [absolutely convergent](wikipedia://en/61532).
