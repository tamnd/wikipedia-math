# Boaz Tsaban

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Boaz Tsaban"}

**Boaz Tsaban** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): בועז צבאן; born February 1973) is an [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelis) mathematician on the faculty of [Bar-Ilan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar-Ilan_University). His research interests include [selection principles](wikipedia://en/50398478) within set theory and [nonabelian cryptology](wikipedia://en/42983810), within mathematical cryptology.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Boaz Tsaban grew up in [Or Yehuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Or_Yehuda), a city near Tel Aviv. At the age of 16 he was selected with other high school students to attend the first cycle of a [special preparation program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Or_Yehuda#Education) in mathematics, at Bar-Ilan University, being admitted to regular mathematics courses at the University a year later. He completed his B.Sc., M.Sc. and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) degrees with highest distinctions. Two years as a post-doctoral fellow at [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University) were followed by a three-year Koshland Fellowship at the [Weizmann Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute_of_Science) before he joined the Department of Mathematics, Bar-Ilan University in 2007.

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

In the field of selection principles, Tsaban devised the method of omission of intervals[^o6] for establishing covering properties of sets of real numbers that have certain combinatorial structures. In nonabelian cryptology he devised the algebraic span method[^o7][^o8] that solved a number of computational problems that underlie a number of proposals for nonabelian public-key cryptographic schemes (such as the [commutator key exchange](wikipedia://en/24708948)).

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

Tsaban's doctoral dissertation, supervised by [Hillel Furstenberg](wikipedia://en/6450152), won, with [Irit Dinur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irit_Dinur), the Nessyahu prize[^o10] for the best Ph.D. in mathematics in Israel in 2003. In 2009 he won the Wolf Foundation Krill Prize[^o11] for Excellence in Scientific Research.
