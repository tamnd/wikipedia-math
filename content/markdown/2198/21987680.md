# Non-sampling error

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Non-sampling error"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **non-sampling error** is a catch-all term for the deviations of estimates from their true values that are not a function of the sample chosen, including various [systematic errors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systematic_error) and [random errors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_error) that are not due to sampling.[^o2] Non-sampling errors are much harder to quantify than [sampling errors](wikipedia://en/1955561).[^o3]

:::

Non-sampling errors in survey estimates can arise from:[^o4]

- [Coverage errors](wikipedia://en/26513269), such as failure to accurately represent all population units in the sample, or the inability to obtain information about all sample cases;
- [Response errors](wikipedia://en/36449924) by respondents due for example to definitional differences, misunderstandings, or deliberate misreporting;
- Mistakes in recording the data or coding it to standard classifications;
- [Pseudo-opinions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-opinion) given by respondents when they have no opinion, but do not wish to say so
- Other errors of collection, nonresponse, processing, or [imputation](wikipedia://en/1309220) of values for missing or inconsistent data.[^o4]

An excellent discussion of issues pertaining to non-sampling error can be found in several sources such as Kalton (1983)[^o5] and Salant and Dillman (1995),[^o6]
