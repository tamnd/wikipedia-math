# Jim Propp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jim Propp"}

*Image: Jim Propp.jpg.* Jim Propp in 1989

**James Gary Propp** is a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Massachusetts Lowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Lowell).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

In high school, Propp was one of the national winners of the [United States of America Mathematical Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America_Mathematical_Olympiad) (USAMO), and an alumnus of the [Hampshire College Summer Studies in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/242549).[^o3] Propp obtained his [AB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in mathematics in 1982 at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). After advanced study at [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), he obtained his PhD from the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley). He has held professorships at seven universities, including [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard), [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin), and the [University of Massachusetts Lowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Lowell).

## Mathematical research {#Mathematical_research}

Propp is the co-editor of the book *Microsurveys in Discrete Probability* (1998) and has written more than fifty journal articles on [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), and [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446). He lectures extensively and has served on the Mathematical Olympiad Committee of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), which sponsors the USAMO. In the early 90s Propp lived in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston) and later in [Arlington, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Massachusetts).[^o13][^o12]

In 1996, Propp and David Wilson invented [coupling from the past](wikipedia://en/17318637), a method for [sampling](wikipedia://en/160361) from the [stationary distribution](wikipedia://en/2524727) of a [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) among [Markov chain Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/236801) (MCMC) [algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithms). Contrary to many MCMC algorithms, coupling from the past gives in principle a perfect sample from the stationary distribution.[^o5][^o6] His papers have discussed the use of [surcomplex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surcomplex_number) in [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924);[^o7] the solution to the counting of [alternating sign matrices](wikipedia://en/1740155);[^o8] and [occurrences of Grandi's series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occurrences_of_Grandi's_series) as an [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of [infinite-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite-dimensional) [real projective space](wikipedia://en/1014694).[^o9][^o10]

## Other contributions {#Other_contributions}

Propp was a member of the [National Puzzlers' League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Puzzlers'_League) under the [pseudonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudonym) Aesop.[^o12] He was recruited for the organisation by colleague Henri Picciotto,[^o13] [cruciverbalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruciverbalist) and co-author of the league's first cryptic crossword collection.[^o14] Propp is the creator of the "Self-Referential Aptitude Test", a humorous multiple-choice test in which all questions except the last make [self-references](wikipedia://en/28545) to their own answers. It was created in the early 1990s for a puzzlers' party.[^o15]

Propp is the author of *Tuscanini*, a 1992 children's book about a musical elephant, illustrated by Ellen Weiss.[^o16]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In 2015 he was elected as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to combinatorics and probability, and for mentoring and exposition."[^o18]

## Personal {#Personal}

He is married to research psychologist Alexandra (Sandi) Gubin. They have a son Adam and a daughter Eliana.[^o20]
