# Synthetic control method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Synthetic control method"}

*Image: SCMGermany.png.* Comparison of per-capita GDP in West Germany before and after the 1990 German reunification and the hypothetical one if the reunification had not taken place.[^o2]

In [causal inference](wikipedia://en/37103476), **synthetic controls** are a class of methods where the [quasi-experimental](wikipedia://en/11864322) control group is synthesized from a weighted average of potential control units. The method is often used to evaluate treatment effects in scenarios where only one or a small number of units are treated.[^o3]

:::

The method was first proposed in a series of articles by [Alberto Abadie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Abadie) and coauthors.[^o4][^o5][^o6] A synthetic control is a weighted average of several units (such as regions or companies) combined to recreate the trajectory that the outcome of a treated unit would have followed in the absence of the intervention. The weights are selected in a data-driven manner to ensure that the resulting synthetic control closely resembles the treated unit in terms of key predictors of the outcome variable.[^o4] Unlike [difference in differences](wikipedia://en/3404894) approaches, this method can account for the effects of [confounders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confounder) changing over time, by weighting the control group to better match the treatment group before the intervention.[^o7] Another advantage of the synthetic control method is that it allows researchers to systematically select comparison groups. It has been applied to the fields of [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics),[^o8] [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science),[^o2] [health policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_policy),[^o7] [criminology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminology),[^o9] and others.

The synthetic control method combines elements from [matching](wikipedia://en/23389623) and [difference-in-differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference-in-differences) techniques. Difference-in-differences methods are often-used policy evaluation tools that estimate the effect of an intervention at an aggregate level (e.g. state, country, age group etc.) by averaging over a set of unaffected units. Famous examples include studies of the employment effects of a raise in the [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage_in_the_United_States) in New Jersey fast food restaurants by comparing them to fast food restaurants just across the border in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia) that were unaffected by a minimum wage raise,[^o10] and studies that look at [crime rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_rates) in southern cities to evaluate the impact of the [Mariel Boatlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariel_boat_lift) on crime.[^o11] The control group in this specific scenario can be interpreted as a [weighted average](wikipedia://en/33274), where some units effectively receive zero weight while others get an equal, non-zero weight.

The synthetic control method tries to offer a more systematic way to assign weights to the control group. It typically uses a relatively long time series of the outcome prior to the intervention and estimates weights in such a way that the control group mirrors the treatment group as closely as possible. In particular, assume we have *J* observations over *T* time periods where the relevant treatment occurs at time $T_{0}$ where $T_{0}<T.$ Let

$$\alpha_{it}=Y_{it}-Y^N_{it},$$

be the treatment effect for unit $i$ at time $t$, where $Y^N_{it}$ is the outcome in absence of the treatment. Without loss of generality, if unit 1 receives the relevant treatment, only $Y^N_{1t}$ is not observed for $t>T_{0}$. We aim to estimate $(\alpha_{1T_{0}+1}......\alpha_{1T})$.

Imposing some structure

$$Y^N_{it}=\delta_{t}+\theta_{t}Z_{i}+\lambda_{t}\mu_{i}+\varepsilon_{it}$$

and assuming there exist some optimal weights $w_2, \ldots, w_J$ such that

$$Y_{1t} = \sum^J_{j=2} w_{j}Y_{jt}$$

for $t\leqslant T_{0}$, the synthetic controls approach suggests using these weights to estimate the counterfactual

$$Y^N_{1t}=\sum^J_{j=2}w_{j}Y_{jt}$$

for $t>T_{0}$. So under some regularity conditions, such weights would provide estimators for the treatment effects of interest. In essence, the method uses the idea of matching and using the training data pre-intervention to set up the weights and hence a relevant control post-intervention.[^o4]

Synthetic controls have been used in a number of empirical applications, ranging from studies examining natural catastrophes and growth,[^o12] or civil conflicts and growth,[^o13] studies that examine the effect of vaccine mandates on childhood immunization,[^o14] and studies linking political murders to house prices.[^o15] Recently, the synthetic control method is actively used in new drug development when evaluating the causal impact of a treatment or intervention, especially in situations where randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are not feasible.[^o16]
