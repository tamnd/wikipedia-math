# Lattice plane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lattice plane"}

*Image: MillerIndices SydW.tif.* The lattice planes created from the miller indices for (100), (010), (001), (110), (101), (011), (020), (002), (222), (111), (11̅1), with origin point showed by the yellow dot.

In [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography), a **lattice plane** of a given [Bravais lattice](wikipedia://en/661808) is any plane containing at least three noncollinear Bravais lattice points. Equivalently, a lattice plane is a plane whose intersections with the lattice (or any crystalline structure of that lattice) are [periodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_functions) (i.e. are described by 2D Bravais lattices).[^o2] A **family of lattice planes** is a collection of equally spaced parallel lattice planes that, taken together, intersect all lattice points. Every family of lattice planes can be described by a set of integer [Miller indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_indices) that have no common divisors (i.e. are [relative prime](wikipedia://en/6556)). Conversely, every set of Miller indices without common divisors defines a family of lattice planes. In example figure 1 shows (100), (010), and (001) are a family of lattice planes and can be denoted by {100}. If, on the other hand, the Miller indices are not relative prime, the family of planes defined by them is not a family of lattice planes, because not every plane of the family then intersects lattice points.[^o3]

:::

Conversely, planes that are *not* lattice planes have *aperiodic* intersections with the lattice called [quasicrystals](wikipedia://en/25350); this is known as a "cut-and-project" construction of a quasicrystal (and is typically also generalized to higher dimensions).[^o4]

*Image: MillerIndices CsCl.tif.* The lattice planes for the miller indices (111), (100), (110), and (222) in CsCl cubic lattice. Where the pink atoms are Cs and the purple are Cl.

[Miller indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_indices) is a notation system for lattice planes in crystallography. It is made from three integers h, k, and l. Written as (hkl). The values of these are given by the question g<sub>hkl</sub> = hb<sub>1</sub> + kb<sub>2</sub> + lb<sub>3</sub>. Where b is the basis of the reciprocal lattice for a given Bravais lattice. Each integer is related to the reciprocal lattice axis a, b, and c respectively. To find the plane indicated by the [miller indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_indices), first take the integers (hkl) and dived 1 by them to get $\tfrac{1}{h}$,$\tfrac{1}{k}$,$\tfrac{1}{l}$ . Next take the Bravais lattice with the axis a,b,c and choose an origin. From the origin move on the axis by respectively. For example, if the [miller indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_indices) were (111) then you would get $\tfrac{1}{1}$,$\tfrac{1}{1}$,$\tfrac{1}{1}$ which equals 1,1,1 which means to move up each axis by 1 to find the plane from the origin. Figure 2[^o5][^o6] shows the miler indices in a real crystal lattice: CsCl cubic crystal lattice (Pm3̅m space group).
