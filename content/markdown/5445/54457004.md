# Polarization constants

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polarization constants"}

In [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342) and optimization, **polarization constants** (also known as **Chebyshev constants**) are solutions to a max-min problem for potentials. Originally, these problems were introduced by a Japanese mathematician [Makoto Ohtsuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makoto_Ohtsuka).[^o2] Recently these problems got some attention as they can help to generate [random](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random) points on smooth [manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifolds) (in particular, [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090)) with prescribed [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487). The problem of finding the polarization constant is connected to the problem of [energy minimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_minimization) and, in particular to the [Thomson problem](wikipedia://en/3104018).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Practical motivation {#Practical_motivation}

From the practical point of view, these problems can be used to answer the following question: if $K(x,x_j)$ denotes the amount of a substance received at $x$ due to an injector of the substance located at $x_j$, what is the smallest number of like injectors and their optimal locations on $A$ so that a prescribed minimal amount of the substance reaches every point of $A$? For example, one can relate this question to treating [tumors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumors) with [radioactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive) seeds.

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Formal Definition"}

## Formal Definition {#Formal_Definition}

More precisely, for a compact set $A$ and kernel $K:A\times A \to \mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}$, the discrete polarization problem is the following: determine $N$-point configurations $\{x_j\}_{j=1}^N$ on $A$ so that the minimum of $\sum_{j=1}^N K(x,x_j)$ for $x\in A$ is as large as possible.

:::

## Classical kernels {#Classical_kernels}

The Chebyshev nomenclature for this max-min problem emanates from the case when $K$ is the logarithmic kernel, $K(x,y)=\log |x-y|^{-1},$ for when $A$ is a subset of the complex plane, the problem is equivalent to finding the constrained $N$-th degree [Chebyshev polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chebyshev_polynomial) for $A$; that is, the [monic polynomial](wikipedia://en/314730) in the complex variable $z$ with all its zeros on $A$ having minimal uniform norm on $A$.

If $A$ is the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) in the plane and $K(x,y)=|x-y|^{-s}$, $s>0$ (i.e., kernel of a [Riesz potential](wikipedia://en/6779393)), then $N$ equally spaced points on the circle solve the $N$ point polarization problem.[^o9][^o10]
