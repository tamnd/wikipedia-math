# Twofish

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Twofish"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **Twofish** is a [symmetric key](wikipedia://en/53042) [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) with a [block size](wikipedia://en/294065) of 128 [bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit) and [key sizes](wikipedia://en/5749) up to 256 bits. It was one of the five finalists of the [Advanced Encryption Standard contest](wikipedia://en/99431), but it was not selected for standardization. Twofish is related to the earlier block cipher [Blowfish](wikipedia://en/3940).

:::

Twofish's distinctive features are the use of pre-computed key-dependent [S-boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution_box), and a relatively complex [key schedule](wikipedia://en/609603). One half of an n-bit key is used as the actual encryption key and the other half of the n-bit key is used to modify the encryption algorithm (key-dependent S-boxes). Twofish borrows some elements from other designs; for example, the [pseudo-Hadamard transform](wikipedia://en/1188115)[^o5] (PHT) from the [SAFER](wikipedia://en/630497) family of ciphers. Twofish has a [Feistel structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feistel_network) like [DES](wikipedia://en/7978). Twofish also employs a [Maximum Distance Separable](wikipedia://en/8288796) matrix.

When it was introduced in 1998, Twofish was slightly slower than [Rijndael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijndael) (the chosen algorithm for [Advanced Encryption Standard](wikipedia://en/1260)) for 128-bit [keys](wikipedia://en/53039), but somewhat faster for 256-bit keys. Since 2008, virtually all AMD and Intel processors have included hardware acceleration of the Rijndael algorithm via the [AES instruction set](wikipedia://en/23095336); Rijndael implementations that use the instruction set are now orders of magnitude faster than (software) Twofish implementations.[^o6]

Twofish was designed by [Bruce Schneier](wikipedia://en/36732), [John Kelsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kelsey_%28cryptanalyst%29), [Doug Whiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Whiting), [David Wagner](wikipedia://en/298253), [Chris Hall](wikipedia://en/13498814), and [Niels Ferguson](wikipedia://en/204933): the "extended Twofish team" met to perform further [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715) of Twofish. Other AES contest entrants included [Stefan Lucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Lucks), [Tadayoshi Kohno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadayoshi_Kohno), and [Mike Stay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Stay).

The Twofish cipher has not been [patented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent), and the [reference implementation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_implementation) has been placed in the [public domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_domain). As a result, the Twofish algorithm is free for anyone to use without any restrictions whatsoever. It is one of a few ciphers included in the [OpenPGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenPGP) standard (RFC 9580). However, Twofish has seen less widespread usage than [Blowfish](wikipedia://en/3940), which has been available longer.

## Performance {#Performance}

During the design of Twofish, performance was always an important factor. It was designed to allow for several layers of performance trade offs, depending on the importance of encryption speed, memory usage, hardware gate count, key setup and other parameters. This allows a highly flexible algorithm, which can be implemented in a variety of applications.

There are multiple space-time tradeoffs that can be made, in software as well as in hardware for Twofish. An example of such a tradeoff would be the precomputation of round subkeys or s-boxes, which can lead to speed increases of a factor of two or more. These come, however, at the cost of more [RAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random-access_memory) needed to store them.

## Cryptanalysis {#Cryptanalysis}

In 1999, [Niels Ferguson](wikipedia://en/204933) published an [impossible differential attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impossible_differential_attack) that breaks 6 rounds out of 16 of the 256-bit key version using 2<sup>256</sup> steps.[^o4]

, the best published cryptanalysis of the Twofish block cipher is a [truncated differential cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/9506236) of the full 16-round version. The paper claims that the probability of truncated differentials is 2<sup>−57.3</sup> per block and that it will take roughly 2<sup>51</sup> chosen plaintexts (32 [petabytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petabyte) worth of data) to find a good pair of truncated differentials.[^o3]

[Bruce Schneier](wikipedia://en/36732) responded in a 2005 blog entry that this paper did not present a full cryptanalytic attack, but only some hypothesized differential characteristics: "But even from a theoretical perspective, Twofish isn't even remotely broken. There have been no extensions to these results since they were published in 2000."[^o11]
