# Logit

*Image: Logit.svg.* Plot of $\operatorname{logit}(x)$ in the domain of $(0, 1)$, where the base of the logarithm is $e$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Logit"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **logit** (**log**istic un**it**) or **log-odds** function is the [quantile function](wikipedia://en/10167616) associated with the standard [logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/542666). It has many uses in [data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954) and [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), especially in [data transformations](wikipedia://en/10056274).

:::

Mathematically, the logit is the [inverse](wikipedia://en/14907) of the [standard logistic function](wikipedia://en/84563) $\textstyle \sigma(x) = 1/(1+e^{-x})$, so the logit is defined as

$$\operatorname{logit} p = \sigma^{-1}(p) = \ln \frac{p}{1-p} \quad \text{for} \quad p \in (0,1).$$

Because of this, the logit is also called the **log-odds** since it is equal to the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) of the [odds](wikipedia://en/172069) $\frac{p}{1-p}$ where $p$ is a probability. Thus, the logit is a type of function that maps probability values from $(0, 1)$ to real numbers in $(-\infty, +\infty)$,[^o2] akin to the [probit function](wikipedia://en/1689101).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If $p$ is a [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), then $\tfrac{p}{1-p}$ is the corresponding [odds](wikipedia://en/172069); the $\operatorname{logit}$ of the probability is the logarithm of the odds, i.e.:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{logit}(p) &= \hphantom{-} \ln\left( \frac{p}{1-p} \right) = \ln(p)-\ln(1-p) \\ &= -\ln\left( \frac{1}{p}-1\right) = 2\operatorname{atanh}(2p-1). \end{aligned}$$

The base of the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) function used is of little importance in the present article, as long as it is greater than 1, but the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) with base $e$ is the one most often used. The choice of base corresponds to the choice of [logarithmic unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_unit) for the value: base 2 corresponds to a [shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_%28unit%29), base $e$ to a [nat](wikipedia://en/3070481), and base 10 to a [hartley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartley_%28unit%29); these units are particularly used in information-theoretic interpretations. For each choice of base, the logit function takes values between negative and positive infinity.

The [logistic function](wikipedia://en/84563) of any number $\alpha$ is given by the inverse-$\operatorname{logit}$:

$$\operatorname{logit}^{-1}(\alpha) = \operatorname{logistic}(\alpha) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\alpha}} = \frac{e^\alpha}{ e^\alpha + 1} = \frac{\tanh(\frac{\alpha}{2})+1}{2}$$

The difference between the $\operatorname{logit}$s of two probabilities is the logarithm of the [odds ratio](wikipedia://en/406880) ($R$), thus providing a shorthand for writing the correct combination of odds ratios [only by adding and subtracting](wikipedia://en/87372):

$$\begin{aligned} \ln(R) &= \ln\left( \frac{\tfrac{p_1}{1-p_1}}{\tfrac{p_2}{1-p_2}} \right) \\ &= \ln\left( \frac{p_1}{1-p_1} \right) - \ln\left(\frac{p_2}{1-p_2}\right) \\ &= \operatorname{logit}(p_1)-\operatorname{logit}(p_2)\,. \end{aligned}$$

The [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) for the logit function is given by:

$$\operatorname{logit}(x)=2\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(2x-1)^{2n+1}}{2n+1}.$$

:::

## History {#History}

Several approaches have been explored to adapt linear regression methods to a domain where the output is a probability value $(0, 1)$, instead of any real number $(-\infty, +\infty)$. In many cases, such efforts have focused on modeling this problem by mapping the range $(0, 1)$ to $(-\infty, +\infty)$ and then running the linear regression on these transformed values.[^o6]

In 1934, [Chester Ittner Bliss](wikipedia://en/14264865) used the cumulative normal distribution function to perform this mapping and called his model *[probit](wikipedia://en/1689101)*, an abbreviation of "probability unit". This is, however, computationally more expensive.[^o6] In 1944, [Joseph Berkson](wikipedia://en/9875090) used log of odds and called this function *logit*, an abbreviation of "logistic unit", following the analogy for probit.[^o7]

::: {#o8 .definition cue=dfni name="log-odds"}

Log odds was used extensively by [Charles Sanders Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117) in the late 19th century.[^o9] [G. A. Barnard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._A._Barnard) in 1949 coined the commonly used term **log-odds**;[^o10][^n1] the log-odds of an event is the logit of the probability of the event.[^o12] Barnard also coined the term *lods* as an abstract form of "log-odds",[^n2] but suggested that "in practice the term 'odds' should normally be used, since this is more familiar in everyday life".[^n3]

:::

## Uses and properties {#Uses_and_properties}

- The logit in [logistic regression](wikipedia://en/226631) is a special case of a link function in a [generalized linear model](wikipedia://en/747122): it is the canonical [link function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_function) for the [Bernoulli distribution](wikipedia://en/199189).
- More abstractly, the logit is the [natural parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_parameter) for the [binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/3876) (see Exponential family).
- The logit function is the negative of the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of the [binary entropy function](wikipedia://en/5275277).
- The logit is also central to the probabilistic [Rasch model](wikipedia://en/2161615) for [measurement](wikipedia://en/19022), which has applications in psychological and educational assessment, among other areas.
- The inverse-logit function (i.e., the [logistic function](wikipedia://en/84563)) is also sometimes referred to as the *expit* function.[^o16]
- In plant disease epidemiology, the logistic, Gompertz, and monomolecular models are collectively known as the Richards family models.
- The log-odds function of probabilities is often used in state estimation algorithms[^o17] because of its numerical advantages in the case of small probabilities. Instead of multiplying very small floating point numbers, log-odds probabilities can just be summed up to calculate the (log-odds) joint probability.[^o18][^o19]

## Comparison with probit {#Comparison_with_probit}

*Image: Logit-probit.svg.* Comparison of the logit function with a scaled [probit](wikipedia://en/1689101) (i.e. the inverse [CDF](wikipedia://en/5793) of the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462)), comparing $\operatorname{logit}(x)$ vs. $\Phi^{-1}(x) / \sqrt{\pi/8}$, which makes the slopes the same at the $y$-origin.

Closely related to the $\operatorname{logit}$ function (and [logit model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logit_model)) are the [probit function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probit_function) and [probit model](wikipedia://en/1611247). The $\operatorname{logit}$ and $\operatorname{probit}$ are both [sigmoid functions](wikipedia://en/87210) with a domain between 0 and 1, which makes them both [quantile functions](wikipedia://en/10167616) – i.e., inverses of the [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) (CDF) of a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543). In fact, the $\operatorname{logit}$ is the [quantile function](wikipedia://en/10167616) of the [logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/542666), while the $\operatorname{probit}$ is the quantile function of the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462). The $\operatorname{probit}$ function is denoted $\operatorname{\Phi}^{-1}(x)$, where $\operatorname{\Phi}(x)$ is the CDF of the standard normal distribution, as just mentioned:

$$\operatorname{\Phi}(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^x e^{-y^2/2} dy.$$

As shown in the graph on the right, the $\operatorname{logit}$ and $\operatorname{probit}$ functions are extremely similar when the $\operatorname{probit}$ function is scaled, so that its slope at $y = 0$ matches the slope of the $\operatorname{logit}$. As a result, [probit models](wikipedia://en/1611247) are sometimes used in place of [logit models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logit_model) because for certain applications (e.g., in [item response theory](wikipedia://en/420159)) the implementation is easier.[^o21]
