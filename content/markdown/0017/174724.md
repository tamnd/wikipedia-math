# Goro Shimura

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Goro Shimura"}

Shimura

**Gorō Shimura** was a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and Michael Henry Strater [Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus) of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) who worked in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146), and [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177).[^o3] He was known for developing the theory of [complex multiplication of abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_variety_of_CM-type) and [Shimura varieties](wikipedia://en/10389861), as well as posing the [Taniyama–Shimura conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama–Shimura_conjecture) which ultimately led to the [proof](wikipedia://en/21950759) of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Gorō Shimura was born in [Hamamatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamamatsu), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan), on 23 February 1930.[^o5] Shimura graduated with a B.A. in mathematics and a D.Sc. in mathematics from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in 1952 and 1958, respectively.[^o5]

After graduating, Shimura became a lecturer at the University of Tokyo, then worked abroad — including ten months in Paris and a seven-month stint at Princeton's [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) — before returning to Tokyo, where he married Chikako Ishiguro.[^o7][^o5] He then moved from Tokyo to join the faculty of [Osaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_University), but growing unhappy with his funding situation, he decided to seek employment in the United States.[^o7][^o5] Through [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) he obtained a position at Princeton University. Shimura joined the Princeton faculty in 1964 and retired in 1999, during which time he advised over 28 doctoral students and received the [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) in 1970, the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) for number theory in 1977, the [Asahi Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahi_Prize) in 1991, and the [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize) for lifetime achievement in 1996.[^o3][^o8]

Shimura described his approach to mathematics as "phenomenological": his interest was in finding new types of interesting behavior in the theory of automorphic forms. He also argued for a "romantic" approach, something he found lacking in the younger generation of mathematicians.[^o9] Shimura used a two-part process for research, using one desk in his home dedicated to working on new research in the mornings and a second desk for perfecting papers in the afternoon.[^o5]

Shimura had two children, Tomoko and Haru, with his wife Chikako.[^o5] Shimura died on 3 May 2019 in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey) at the age of 89.[^o3][^o5]

## Research {#Research}

Shimura was a colleague and a friend of [Yutaka Taniyama](wikipedia://en/1077843), with whom he wrote the first book on the [complex multiplication of abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_variety_of_CM-type) and formulated the Taniyama–Shimura conjecture.[^o11] Shimura then wrote a long series of major papers, extending the phenomena found in the theory of [complex multiplication of elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/537183) and the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) to higher dimensions (e.g. Shimura varieties). This work provided examples for which the equivalence between [motivic](wikipedia://en/31744361) and [automorphic *L*-functions](wikipedia://en/32328300) postulated in the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905) could be tested: [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146) realized in the [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of a Shimura variety have a construction that attaches [Galois representations](wikipedia://en/486929) to them.[^o12]

In 1958, Shimura generalized the initial work of [Martin Eichler](wikipedia://en/4224478) on the [Eichler–Shimura congruence relation](wikipedia://en/28167018) between the [local *L*-function](wikipedia://en/396014) of a [modular curve](wikipedia://en/669552) and the eigenvalues of [Hecke operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecke_operators).[^o13][^o14] In 1959, Shimura extended the work of Eichler on the [Eichler–Shimura isomorphism](wikipedia://en/35173784) between Eichler cohomology groups and spaces of [cusp forms](wikipedia://en/610165) which would be used in [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544)'s proof of the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705).[^o15][^o16]

In 1971, Shimura's work on explicit [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695) in the spirit of [Kronecker's Jugendtraum](wikipedia://en/2164726) resulted in his proof of [Shimura's reciprocity law](wikipedia://en/35152264).[^o17] In 1973, Shimura established the [Shimura correspondence](wikipedia://en/28754333) between modular forms of half integral weight *k*+1/2, and modular forms of even weight 2*k*.[^o18]

Shimura's formulation of the Taniyama–Shimura conjecture (later known as the modularity theorem) in the 1950s played a key role in the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem by [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027) in 1995. In 1990, [Kenneth Ribet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Ribet) proved [Ribet's theorem](wikipedia://en/700981) which demonstrated that Fermat's Last Theorem followed from the semistable case of this conjecture.[^o19] Shimura dryly commented that his first reaction on hearing of [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027)'s proof of the semistable case was 'I told you so'.[^o20]

## Other interests {#Other_interests}

His hobbies were [shogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogi) problems of extreme length and collecting [Imari porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imari_porcelain). *The Story of Imari: The Symbols and Mysteries of Antique Japanese Porcelain* is a non-fiction work about the Imari porcelain that he collected over 30 years that was published by [Ten Speed Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Speed_Press) in 2008.[^o5][^o22]
