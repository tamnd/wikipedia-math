# Faltings' theorem

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Faltings' theorem"}

**Faltings' theorem.**

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Faltings' theorem"}

**Faltings' theorem** is a result in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), according to which a non-singular [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891#Algebraic_geometry) greater than 1 over the field $\mathbb{Q}$ of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) has only finitely many [rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200). This was conjectured in 1922 by [Louis Mordell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Mordell),[^n1] and known as the **Mordell conjecture** until its 1983 proof by [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226). The conjecture was later generalized by replacing $\mathbb{Q}$ by any [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field).

:::

## Background {#Background}

Let $C$ be a [non-singular](wikipedia://en/4230456) algebraic curve of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) $g$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then the set of rational points on $C$ may be determined as follows:

- When $g=0$, there are either no points or infinitely many. In such cases, $C$ may be handled as a [conic section](wikipedia://en/19008673).
- When $g=1$, if there are any points, then $C$ is an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) and its rational points form a [finitely generated abelian group](wikipedia://en/58527). (This is *Mordell's Theorem*, later generalized to the [Mordell–Weil theorem](wikipedia://en/23087727).) Moreover, [Mazur's torsion theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazur's_torsion_theorem) restricts the structure of the torsion subgroup.
- When $g>1$, according to Faltings' theorem, $C$ has only a finite number of rational points.

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proofs"}

## Proofs {#Proofs}

[Igor Shafarevich](wikipedia://en/1091815) conjectured that there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of [abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152) of fixed dimension and fixed [polarization](wikipedia://en/247152#Polarisations) degree over a fixed number field with [good reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_reduction) outside a fixed finite set of [places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_%28mathematics%29).[^n2] [Aleksei Parshin](wikipedia://en/50782329) showed that Shafarevich's finiteness conjecture would imply the Mordell conjecture, using what is now called Parshin's trick.[^n3]

[Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226) proved Shafarevich's finiteness conjecture using a known reduction to a case of the [Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/3124804), together with tools from [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), including the theory of [Néron models](wikipedia://en/9190726).[^n4] The main idea of Faltings' proof is the comparison of [Faltings heights](wikipedia://en/3141855#Faltings_height) and [naive heights](wikipedia://en/3141855#Naive_height) via [Siegel modular varieties](wikipedia://en/61144542).

### Later proofs {#Later_proofs}

- [Paul Vojta](wikipedia://en/1802312) gave a proof based on [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558).[^n5] [Enrico Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475) found a more elementary variant of Vojta's proof.[^n6]
- Brian Lawrence and [Akshay Venkatesh](wikipedia://en/6647964) gave a proof based on [$p$-adic Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/26086853), borrowing also some of the easier ingredients of Faltings' original proof.[^n7]

:::

## Consequences {#Consequences}

Faltings' 1983 paper had as consequences a number of statements which had previously been conjectured:

- The *Mordell conjecture* that a curve of genus greater than 1 over a number field has only finitely many rational points;
- [Tate's isogeny theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate's_isogeny_theorem) that abelian varieties with isomorphic [Tate modules](wikipedia://en/3094921) (as $\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}$-modules with Galois action) are [isogenous](wikipedia://en/1085176).

A sample application of Faltings' theorem is to a weak form of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953): for any fixed $n\ge 4$ there are at most finitely many primitive integer solutions (pairwise [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) solutions) to $a^n+b^n=c^n$, since for such $n$ the [Fermat curve](wikipedia://en/897529) $x^n+y^n=1$ has genus greater than 1.

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Because of the [Mordell–Weil theorem](wikipedia://en/23087727), Faltings' theorem can be reformulated as a statement about the intersection of a curve $C$ with a finitely generated subgroup $\Gamma$ of an abelian variety $A$. Generalizing by replacing $A$ by a [semiabelian variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiabelian_variety), $C$ by an arbitrary subvariety of $A$, and $\Gamma$ by an arbitrary finite-rank subgroup of $A$ leads to the [Mordell–Lang conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell–Lang_conjecture), which was proved in 1995 by [McQuillan](wikipedia://en/39463065)[^n8] following work of Laurent, [Raynaud](wikipedia://en/4760928), Hindry, [Vojta](wikipedia://en/1802312), and [Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226).

Another higher-dimensional generalization of Faltings' theorem is the [Bombieri–Lang conjecture](wikipedia://en/3142105) that if $X$ is a [pseudo-canonical variety](wikipedia://en/35797504) (i.e., a variety of general type) over a number field $k$, then $X(k)$ is not [Zariski](wikipedia://en/241744) [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) in $X$. Even more general conjectures have been put forth by [Paul Vojta](wikipedia://en/1802312).

The Mordell conjecture for function fields was proved by [Yuri Ivanovich Manin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Ivanovich_Manin)[^n9] and by [Hans Grauert](wikipedia://en/2979031).[^n10] In 1990, [Robert F. Coleman](wikipedia://en/26813984) found and fixed a gap in Manin's proof.[^n11]

:::
