# Frank A. Stevenson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frank A. Stevenson"}

**Frank A. Stevenson** (born 1970) is a [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway) [software developer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_developer), and part-time [cryptanalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalyst). He is primarily known for his exposition of weaknesses in the [DVD Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD_Forum)'s [Content Scramble System](wikipedia://en/18935238) (CSS).[^o2] Although the [cryptoanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptoanalysis) was done independently, he is known for his relations to [DeCSS](wikipedia://en/39675),[^o3] and appeared before the courts as a witness in the [Jon Johansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Johansen) court trial. He also gave a [deposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deposition_%28law%29) for the [DVD CCA v. McLaughlin, Bunner, et al.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD_CCA_v._McLaughlin,_Bunner,_et_al.) case.

:::

Stevenson worked for [Funcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funcom) as a [game developer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_developer) for many years, after which he moved to [Kvaleberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kvaleberg) to work on [mobile phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone) software. In July 2010, Stevenson published information about vulnerabilities in the [A5/1 encryption algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A5/1_encryption_algorithm) used by most [2G GSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2G_GSM) networks, and also showed the *Kraken* software, that demonstrates that the crypto indeed can be broken with modest hardware.[^o4]
