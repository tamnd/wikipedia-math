# Abelian extension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abelian extension"}

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), an **abelian extension** is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) whose [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) is [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974). When the Galois group is also [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327), the extension is also called a **cyclic extension**. Going in the other direction, a Galois extension is called **solvable** if its Galois group is [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012), i.e., if the group can be decomposed into a series of normal [extensions](wikipedia://en/345807) of an abelian group. Every finite extension of a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) is a cyclic extension.

:::

## Description {#Description}

[Class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695) provides detailed information about the abelian extensions of [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field), [function fields](wikipedia://en/2783843) of [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) over finite fields, and [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375).

There are two slightly different definitions of the term **cyclotomic extension.** It can mean either an extension formed by adjoining [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity) to a field, or a subextension of such an extension. The [cyclotomic fields](wikipedia://en/30872462) are examples. A cyclotomic extension, under either definition, is always abelian.

If a field *K* contains a primitive *n*-th root of unity and the *n*-th root of an element of *K* is adjoined, the resulting [Kummer extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kummer_extension) is an abelian extension (if *K* has characteristic *p* we should say that *p* doesn't divide *n*, since otherwise this can fail even to be a [separable extension](wikipedia://en/363340)). In general, however, the Galois groups of *n*-th roots of elements operate both on the *n*-th roots and on the roots of unity, giving a non-abelian Galois group as [semi-direct product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-direct_product). [Kummer theory](wikipedia://en/581610) gives a complete description of the abelian extension case, and the [Kronecker–Weber theorem](wikipedia://en/1082550) tells us that if *K* is the field of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), an extension is abelian if and only if it is a subfield of a field obtained by adjoining a root of unity.

There is an important analogy with the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), which classifies all covering spaces of a space: abelian covers are classified by its [abelianisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianisation) which relates directly to the first [homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group).
