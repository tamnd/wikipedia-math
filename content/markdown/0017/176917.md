# Mikhail Ostrogradsky

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mikhail Ostrogradsky"}

**Mikhail Vasilyevich Ostrogradsky** (Михаи́л Васи́льевич Острогра́дский; 24 September 1801 – 1 January 1862), also known as **Mykhailo Vasyliovych Ostrohradskyi** (Миха́йло Васи́льович Острогра́дський), was a [Russian Imperial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire)[^o3][^o4] [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [mechanician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics), and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) of [Ukrainian Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporozhian_Cossacks) ancestry.[^o5][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10] Ostrogradsky was a student of [Timofei Osipovsky](wikipedia://en/6096792) and is considered to be a disciple of [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902), who was known as one of the leading mathematicians of Imperial Russia.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Ostrogradsky was born on 24 September 1801 in the village of Pashennaya (at the time in the [Poltava Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Governorate), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), today in [Kremenchuk Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremenchuk_Raion), [Poltava Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Oblast), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)). From 1816 to 1820, he studied under [Timofei Osipovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timofei_Fedorovic_Osipovsky) (1765–1832) and graduated from the [Imperial University of Kharkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_University). When Osipovsky was suspended on religious grounds in 1820, Ostrogradsky refused to be examined and he never received his Ph.D. degree. From 1822 to 1826, he studied at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) and at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) in [Paris, France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris,_France). In 1828, he returned to the Russian Empire and settled in [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg), where he was elected a member of the [Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Science). He also became a professor of the [main military engineering school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Engineering-Technical_University) of the Russian Empire.

Ostrogradsky died in [Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava) in 1862, aged 60. The [Kremenchuk Mykhailo Ostrohradskyi National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremenchuk_Mykhailo_Ostrohradskyi_National_University) in [Kremenchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremenchuk), [Poltava oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_oblast), as well as *Ostrogradsky street* in [Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava), are named after him.

## Work {#Work}

*Image: Coin of Ukraine Ostrograd R.jpg.* A 2 [hryvna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hryvna) commemorative coin minted by the [National Bank of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Ukraine) in 2001.

*Image: Меморіальна дошка на будинку, де жив Остроградський.JPG.* Commemorative plaque in [Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava) on the last house where Ostrogradsky resided.

He worked mainly in the mathematical fields of [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), [integration](wikipedia://en/15532) of [algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/974169), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and in the fields of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) and [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586). In the latter, his key contributions are in the [motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_%28physics%29) of an [elastic body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29) and the development of methods for integration of the equations of [dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamics_%28mechanics%29) and [fluid power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_power), following up on the works of [Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler), [Joseph Louis Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_Lagrange), [Siméon Denis Poisson](wikipedia://en/44670) and [Augustin Louis Cauchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_Louis_Cauchy).

In Russia, his work in these fields was continued by [Nikolay Dmitrievich Brashman](wikipedia://en/1005204) (1796–1866), [August Yulevich Davidov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Davidov) (1823–1885) and especially by [Nikolai Yegorovich Zhukovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Yegorovich_Zhukovsky) (1847–1921).

*Image: Могила вченого-математика академіка М.В.Остроградського.JPG.* Ostrogradsky's grave in the village of [Pashenivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashenivka), where he was born.

Ostrogradsky did not appreciate the work on [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610) of [Nikolai Lobachevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Ivanovich_Lobachevsky) from 1823, and he rejected it, when it was submitted for publication in the Saint Petersburg Academy of Sciences.

Ostrogradsky was a teacher of the children of Emperor [Nicholas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_I_of_Russia).[^o13]

### Divergence theorem {#Divergence_theorem}

In 1826, Ostrogradsky gave the first general proof of the [divergence theorem](wikipedia://en/151864), which was discovered by [Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange) in 1762.[^o15] This theorem may be expressed using Ostrogradsky's equation:

$$\iiint_V \left( {\partial P\over \partial x} + {\partial Q\over \partial y} + {\partial R\over \partial z} \right) dx \, dy \, dz = \iint_\Sigma \left( P \cos \lambda + Q \cos \mu + R \cos \nu \right) d\Sigma;$$

where *P*, *Q*, and *R* are differentiable functions of *x*, *y*, and *z* defined on the compact region *V* bounded by a smooth closed surface *Σ*; *λ*, *μ*, and *ν* are the angles that the outward normal to *Σ* makes with the positive *x*, *y*, and *z* axes respectively; and d*Σ* is the surface area element on *Σ*.

### Ostrogradsky's integration method {#Ostrogradsky's_integration_method}

His method for integrating [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210)[^o17] is well known. First, we separate the rational part of the integral of a fractional rational function, the sum of the rational part (algebraic fraction) and the transcendental part (with the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) and the [arctangent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_trigonometric_function)). Second, we determine the rational part without integrating it, and we assign a given integral in Ostrogradsky's form:

$$\int {R(x)\over P(x)} \, dx = {T(x)\over S(x)} + \int {X(x)\over Y(x)} \, dx,$$

where $P(x),\, S(x),\, Y(x)$ are known polynomials of degrees *p*, *s*, *y* respectively; $R(x)$ is a known polynomial of degree not greater than $p - 1$; and $T(x),\, X(x)$ are unknown polynomials of degrees not greater than $s - 1$ and $y - 1$ respectively.

Third, $S(x)$ is the greatest common divisor of $P(x)$ and $P'(x)$. Fourth, the denominator of the remaining integral $Y(x)$ can be calculated from the equation $P(x) = S(x)\,Y(x)$.

When we differentiate both sides of the equation above, we get: $R(x) = T'(x)Y(x) - T(x)H(x) + X(x)S(x)$,

where $H(x) = {Y(x) S'(x) \over S(x)}$.

It can be shown that $H(x)$ is polynomial.
