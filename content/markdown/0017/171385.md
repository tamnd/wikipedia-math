# Cryptomathic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cryptomathic"}

**Cryptomathic** is a [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) company specializing in the area of [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) for e-commerce security systems. The company develops secure software for the financial and governmental industries. It focuses especially on developing back-end solutions using [hardware security modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardware_security_module).[^o3]

:::

Cryptomathic has its headquarters in [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus), Denmark. The company was founded in 1986, by three [professors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) from [University of Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aarhus), among them [Peter Landrock](wikipedia://en/46710302) and [Ivan Damgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Damgård). It now operates world-wide with offices in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London), UK; [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich), Germany and [San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California), US[^o4]

Cryptomathic has collaborated in research projects with the [Isaac Newton Institute](wikipedia://en/736837) for Mathematical Sciences to develop Cryptomathic's systems for securing messaging between [hardware security modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardware_security_module) (HSMs). With [Bristol University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_University), Cryptomathic conducted research on [authenticated encryption](wikipedia://en/2244296) between HSMs.[^o5]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

In 2002, Cryptomathic's chief cryptographer [Vincent Rijmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Rijmen)[^o7] was named one of the top 100 innovators in the world under the age of 35 by the [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) [Technology Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_Review) [TR100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TR35).[^o8]

In 2003, Cryptomathic was recognized by the [World Economic Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum) as a Technology Pioneer, based on its innovative product for mobile electronic signatures.[^o9] The term "What You See Is What You Sign" ([WYSIWYS](wikipedia://en/6187863)) was coined in 1998 by [Peter Landrock](wikipedia://en/46710302) and Torben Pedersen of Cryptomathic during their work on delivering secure and legally binding digital signatures for Pan-European projects.[^o10] In 2004, Cryptomathic received the [Visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_Inc.) Smart Star Award for it contributions to the field of [EMV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMV) and [Chip and PIN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chip_and_PIN) based on its data preparation offering.[^o11] In 2010, Cryptomathic's founder, Peter Landrock was named a Finalist for European Inventor 2010 in the "Lifetime Achievement" category by the [European Patent Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Patent_Office).[^o12]

The member of Cryptomathic's advisory board, [Whitfield Diffie](wikipedia://en/427776) is co-author of the [Diffie–Hellman key exchange](wikipedia://en/7903), a method of securely exchanging [cryptographic keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographic_keys).[^o13] Diffie and Hellman were awarded with the 2015 [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award) for "fundamental contributions to modern cryptography" including [public-key cryptography](wikipedia://en/24222) and [digital signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signatures).[^o14]
