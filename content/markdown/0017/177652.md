# List of psychological research methods

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of psychological research methods"}

A wide range of [research methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_methods) are used in psychology. These methods vary by the sources from which information is obtained, how that information is sampled, and the types of instruments that are used in data collection. Methods also vary by whether they collect [qualitative](wikipedia://en/371299) data, [quantitative](wikipedia://en/389564) data or both.

:::

[Qualitative psychological research](wikipedia://en/331913) findings are not arrived at by statistical or other quantitative procedures. [Quantitative psychological research](wikipedia://en/371317) findings result from mathematical modeling and statistical estimation or statistical inference. The two types of research differ in the methods employed, rather than the topics they focus on.

There are three main types of psychological research:

::: {#o2 .entry cue=list-entry name="Correlational research"}

- [Correlational research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_and_dependence)

:::

::: {#o3 .entry cue=list-entry name="Descriptive research"}

- [Descriptive research](wikipedia://en/2685241)

:::

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Experimental research"}

- [Experimental research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_psychology)

:::

## Common methods {#Common_methods}

Common research designs and data collection methods include:

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="Archival research"}

- [Archival research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archival_research)

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="Case study"}

- [Case study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_study) uses different research methods (e.g. interview, observation, self-report questionnaire) with a single case or small number of cases.

:::

::: {#o8 .entry cue=list-entry name="Clustered randomized controlled trial"}

- [Clustered randomized controlled trial](wikipedia://en/26286800)

:::

::: {#o9 .entry cue=list-entry name="Computer simulation"}

- [Computer simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_simulation) (modeling)

:::

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="Ethnography"}

- [Ethnography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnography)

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="Event sampling methodology"}

- [Event sampling methodology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Event_sampling_methodology), also referred to as [experience sampling methodology](wikipedia://en/19250284), diary study, or ecological momentary assessment

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=list-entry name="Experiment"}

- [Experiment](wikipedia://en/59861), often with separate [treatment and control groups](wikipedia://en/1765232) (see [scientific control](wikipedia://en/1640288) and [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541)). See [Experimental psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_psychology) for many details.

:::

::: {#o13 .entry cue=list-entry name="Field experiment"}

- [Field experiment](wikipedia://en/1305037)

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=list-entry name="Focus group"}

- [Focus group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focus_group)

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=list-entry name="Interview"}

- [Interview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview), can be [structured](wikipedia://en/2474014) or [unstructured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unstructured_interview).

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=list-entry name="Meta-analysis"}

- [Meta-analysis](wikipedia://en/62329)

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=list-entry name="Neuroimaging"}

- [Neuroimaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroimaging) and other [psychophysiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychophysiology) methods

:::

::: {#o18 .entry cue=list-entry name="Observational study"}

- [Observational study](wikipedia://en/6326483), can be naturalistic (see [natural experiment](wikipedia://en/170141)), participant or controlled.

:::

::: {#o19 .entry cue=list-entry name="Program evaluation"}

- [Program evaluation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Program_evaluation)

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=list-entry name="Quasi-experiment"}

- [Quasi-experiment](wikipedia://en/11864322)

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=list-entry name="Randomized control trial"}

- [Randomized control trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_control_trial)

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=list-entry name="Self-report inventory"}

- [Self-report inventory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-report_inventory)

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=list-entry name="Survey"}

- [Survey](wikipedia://en/272134), often with a [random sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_sample) (see [survey sampling](wikipedia://en/27578))

:::

::: {#o24 .entry cue=list-entry name="Twin study"}

- [Twin study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_study)

:::

Research designs vary according to the period(s) of time over which data are collected:

::: {#o25 .entry cue=list-entry name="Retrospective cohort study"}

- [Retrospective cohort study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrospective_cohort_study): Participants are chosen, then data are collected about their past experiences.

:::

::: {#o26 .entry cue=list-entry name="Prospective cohort study"}

- [Prospective cohort study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospective_cohort_study): Participants are recruited prior to the proposed independent effects being administered or occurring.

:::

::: {#o27 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cross-sectional study"}

- [Cross-sectional study](wikipedia://en/1557627): A population is sampled on all proposed measures at one point in time.

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="Longitudinal study"}

- [Longitudinal study](wikipedia://en/1557623): Participants are studied at multiple time points. May address the [cohort effect](wikipedia://en/5947372) and help to indicate causal directions of effects.

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cross-sequential study"}

- [Cross-sequential study](wikipedia://en/37264795): Groups of different ages are studied at multiple time points; combines cross-sectional and longitudinal designs

:::
