# Complex Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complex Lie algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **complex Lie algebra** is a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826).

:::

Given a complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, its **conjugate** $\overline{\mathfrak g}$ is a complex Lie algebra with the same underlying [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) but with $i = \sqrt{-1}$ acting as $-i$ instead.[^o2] As a real Lie algebra, a complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is trivially [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to its conjugate. A complex Lie algebra is isomorphic to its conjugate [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it admits a real form (and is said to be defined over the real numbers).

## Real form {#Real_form}

Given a complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, a real Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_0$ is said to be a [real form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_form) of $\mathfrak{g}$ if the [complexification](wikipedia://en/755604) $\mathfrak{g}_0 \otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}$ is isomorphic to $\mathfrak{g}$.

A real form $\mathfrak{g}_0$ is abelian (resp. nilpotent, solvable, semisimple) if and only if $\mathfrak{g}$ is abelian (resp. nilpotent, solvable, semisimple).[^o4] On the other hand, a real form $\mathfrak{g}_0$ is [simple](wikipedia://en/59500649) if and only if either $\mathfrak{g}$ is simple or $\mathfrak{g}$ is of the form $\mathfrak{s} \times \overline{\mathfrak{s}}$ where $\mathfrak{s}, \overline{\mathfrak{s}}$ are simple and are the conjugates of each other.[^o4]

The existence of a real form in a complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ implies that $\mathfrak g$ is isomorphic to its conjugate;[^o2] indeed, if $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{g}_0 \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C} = \mathfrak{g}_0 \oplus i\mathfrak{g}_0$, then let $\tau : \mathfrak{g} \to \overline{\mathfrak{g}}$ denote the $\mathbb{R}$-linear isomorphism induced by complex conjugate and then

$$\tau(i(x + iy)) = \tau(ix - y) = -ix- y = -i\tau(x + iy),$$

which is to say $\tau$ is in fact a $\mathbb{C}$-linear isomorphism.

Conversely, suppose there is a $\mathbb{C}$-linear isomorphism $\tau: \mathfrak{g} \overset{\sim}\to \overline{\mathfrak{g}}$; [without loss of generality](wikipedia://en/296000), we can assume it is the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069) on the underlying real vector space. Then define $\mathfrak{g}_0 = \{ z \in \mathfrak{g} | \tau(z) = z \}$, which is clearly a real Lie algebra. Each element $z$ in $\mathfrak{g}$ can be written uniquely as $z = 2^{-1}(z + \tau(z)) + i 2^{-1}(i\tau(z) - iz)$. Here, $\tau(i\tau(z) - iz) = -iz + i\tau(z)$ and similarly $\tau$ fixes $z + \tau(z)$. Hence, $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{g}_0 \oplus i \mathfrak{g}_0$; i.e., $\mathfrak{g}_0$ is a real form.

## Complex Lie algebra of a complex Lie group {#Complex_Lie_algebra_of_a_complex_Lie_group}

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a semisimple complex Lie algebra that is the Lie algebra of a [complex Lie group](wikipedia://en/2187847) $G$. Let $\mathfrak{h}$ be a [Cartan subalgebra](wikipedia://en/1336000) of $\mathfrak{g}$ and $H$ the Lie subgroup corresponding to $\mathfrak{h}$; the conjugates of $H$ are called [Cartan subgroups](wikipedia://en/4838153).

Suppose there is the decomposition $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{n}^- \oplus \mathfrak{h} \oplus \mathfrak{n}^+$ given by a choice of positive roots. Then the [exponential map](wikipedia://en/44612290) defines an isomorphism from $\mathfrak{n}^+$ to a closed subgroup $U \subset G$.[^o6] The Lie subgroup $B \subset G$ corresponding to the [Borel subalgebra](wikipedia://en/62074925) $\mathfrak{b} = \mathfrak{h} \oplus \mathfrak{n}^+$ is closed and is the [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of $H$ and $U$;[^o7] the conjugates of $B$ are called [Borel subgroups](wikipedia://en/588356).
