# Parabolic geometry (differential geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Parabolic geometry"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and the study of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), a **parabolic geometry** is a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) *G*/*P* which is the quotient of a [semisimple Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group) *G* by a [parabolic subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) *P*. More generally, the curved analogs of a parabolic geometry in this sense is also called a parabolic geometry: any geometry that is modeled on such a space by means of a [Cartan connection](wikipedia://en/412272).

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) *P*<sup>*n*</sup> is an example. It is the homogeneous space PGL(*n*+1)/*H* where *H* is the isotropy group of a line. In this geometrical space, the notion of a straight line is meaningful, but there is no preferred ("affine") parameter along the lines. The curved analog of projective space is a manifold in which the notion of a [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) makes sense, but for which there are no preferred parametrizations on those geodesics. A [projective connection](wikipedia://en/5776733) is the relevant Cartan connection that gives a means for describing a projective geometry by gluing copies of the projective space to the tangent spaces of the base manifold. Broadly speaking, [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) refers to the study of manifolds with this kind of connection.

Another example is the [conformal sphere](wikipedia://en/295907). Topologically, it is the *n*-sphere, but there is no notion of length defined on it, just of angle between curves. Equivalently, this geometry is described as an [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) of [Riemannian metrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on the sphere (called a conformal class). The group of transformations that preserve angles on the sphere is the [Lorentz group](wikipedia://en/230489) O(*n*+1,1), and so *S*<sup>*n*</sup> = O(*n*+1,1)/*P*. [Conformal geometry](wikipedia://en/295907) is, more broadly, the study of manifolds with a conformal equivalence class of Riemannian metrics, i.e., manifolds modeled on the conformal sphere. Here the associated Cartan connection is the [conformal connection](wikipedia://en/5804750).

Other examples include:

- [CR geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CR_geometry), the study of manifolds modeled on a real hyperquadric $Q^{2(p+q)-1} = SU(p,q)/P \subseteq \mathbb{C}^{p+q}$, where $P$ is the stabilizer of an [isotropic line](wikipedia://en/8088814) (see [CR manifold](wikipedia://en/3131794))
- contact projective geometry, the study of manifolds modeled on $SP(n)/P$ where $P$ is that subgroup of the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) stabilizing the line generated by the first [standard basis](wikipedia://en/462005) vector in $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$
