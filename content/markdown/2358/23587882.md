# Valentina Harizanov

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Valentina Harizanov"}

**Valentina Harizanov** is a [Serbian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian-American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and professor of mathematics at [The George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_George_Washington_University). Her main research contributions are in [computable structure theory](wikipedia://en/11574361) (roughly at the intersection of [computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414) and [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858)), where she introduced the notion of degree spectra of relations on computable structures and obtained the first significant results concerning uncountable, countable, and finite [Turing degree](wikipedia://en/764405) spectra.[^o3] Her recent interests include [algorithmic learning theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_learning_theory) and spaces of [orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) on [groups](wikipedia://en/19447).

:::

## Education {#Education}

She obtained her Bachelor of Science in mathematics in 1978 at the [University of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Belgrade) and her Ph.D. in mathematics in 1987 at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) under the direction of [Terry Millar](wikipedia://en/67211693).[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

At The George Washington University, Harizanov was an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) of mathematics from 1987 to 1993, an [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor) of mathematics from 1994 to 2002, and a professor of mathematics from 2003 to the present. She has held two [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_professor) positions, one in 1994 at the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park) and one in 2014 at the Kurt Gödel Research Center at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna).[^o6]

Harizanov has co-directed the Center for Quantum Computing, Information, Logic, and Topology at The George Washington University since 2011.[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

In 2009, Harizanov received a grant from the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) to research how algebraic, topological, and algorithmic properties of mathematical structures relate.[^o9]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Harizanov won the Oscar and Shoshana Trachtenberg Prize for Faculty Scholarship from The George Washington University (GWU) in 2016.[^o11] This award is presented each year to a [tenured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenure) GWU faculty member to recognize outstanding research accomplishments.[^o12] She was named [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) Eisenbud Professor for Fall 2020.[^o13]

## Publications {#Publications}

Harizanov has over 40 publications in [peer-reviewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-review) journals, including

- V.S. Harizanov, "Some effects of Ash-Nerode and other decidability conditions on degree spectra " Annals of Pure and Applied Logic 55 (1), pp. 51–65 (1991), cited 21 times according to [Web of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_of_Science)

In addition, she has published the following book-length survey paper and co-edited, co-authored book:

- V.S. Harizanov, “Pure computable model theory,” in the volume: Handbook of Recursive Mathematics, vol. 1, Yu.L. Ershov, S.S. Goncharov, A. Nerode, and J.B. Remmel, editors (North-Holland, Amsterdam, 1998), pp. 3–114.
- M. Friend, N.B. Goethe, and V.S. Harizanov, Induction, Algorithmic Learning Theory, and Philosophy, Series: Logic, Epistemology, and the Unity of Science, vol. 9, Springer, Dordrecht, 304 pp., 2007.

Degree spectra of relations are introduced and first studied in Harizanov's dissertation: *Degree Spectrum of a Recursive Relation on a Recursive Structure*(1987).[^o3]
