# Differential game

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differential game"}

In [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), **differential games** are dynamic games that unfold in [continuous time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_time), meaning players’ actions and outcomes evolve smoothly rather than in discrete steps,[^o2] and for which the rate of change of each [state variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_variable)—like position, speed, or resource level—is governed by a [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309). This distinguishes them from turn-based games ([sequential games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequential_game)) like chess, focusing instead on real-time strategic conflicts.

:::

Differential games are sometimes called **continuous-time games**, a broader term that includes them.[^o3] While the two overlap significantly, continuous-time games also encompass models not governed by differential equations, such as those with stochastic [jump processes](wikipedia://en/2180750), where abrupt, unpredictable events introduce discontinuities.

Early differential games, often inspired by military scenarios, modeled situations like a pursuer chasing an evader, such as a missile targeting an aircraft.[^o4] Today, they also apply to fields like economics and engineering, analyzing competition over resources or the control of moving systems.[^o5][^o6]

## Connection to optimal control {#Connection_to_optimal_control}

Differential games are related closely to [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565) problems. In an optimal control problem there is single control $u(t)$ and a single criterion to be optimized; differential game theory generalizes this to two controls $u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t)$ and two criteria, one for each player.[^o8] Each player attempts to control the state of the system so as to achieve its goal; the system responds to the inputs of all players.

## History {#History}

In the study of [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_%28economics%29), differential games have been employed since a 1925 article by [Charles F. Roos](wikipedia://en/52206911).[^o10] The first to study the formal theory of differential games was [Rufus Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Isaacs_%28game_theorist%29), publishing a text-book treatment in 1965.[^o11] One of the first games analyzed was the ['homicidal chauffeur game'](wikipedia://en/15007740).

::: {#o13 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Differential games with random time horizon"}

## Random time horizon {#Random_time_horizon}

Games with a random time horizon are a particular case of differential games.[^o14] In such games, the terminal time is a random variable with a given [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) function. Therefore, the players maximize the [mathematical expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_expectancy) of the cost function. It was shown that the modified optimization problem can be reformulated as a discounted differential game over an infinite time interval[^o15][^o16]

:::

::: {#o18 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Differential games have been applied to economics. Recent developments include adding [stochasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochasticity) to differential games and the derivation of the [stochastic feedback Nash equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_feedback_Nash_equilibrium) (SFNE). A recent example is the stochastic differential game of capitalism by Leong and Huang (2010).[^o19] In 2016 [Yuliy Sannikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuliy_Sannikov) received the [John Bates Clark Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bates_Clark_Medal) from the *[American Economic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Economic_Association)* for his contributions to the analysis of continuous-time dynamic games using [stochastic calculus](wikipedia://en/228001) methods.[^o20][^o21]

Additionally, differential games have applications in [missile guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_guidance)[^o22][^o23] and [autonomous systems](wikipedia://en/312398).[^o24]

For a survey of [pursuit–evasion](wikipedia://en/3857398) differential games see Pachter.[^o25]

:::
