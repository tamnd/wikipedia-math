# Interclass correlation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Interclass correlation"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **interclass correlation** (or *interclass correlation coefficient*) measures the relationship between two variables of different classes (types), such as the weights of 10-year-old sons and their 40-year-old fathers. Deviations for each variable are calculated from the [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) of their respective classes – specifically, a son's weight minus the mean weight of all sons, or a father's weight minus the mean weight of all fathers.

:::

The [Pearson correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/221708) is the most commonly used measure of interclass correlation.

The interclass correlation differs from [*intra*class correlation](wikipedia://en/11391832), which involves variables of the same class, such as the weights of women and their identical twins. In this case, deviations are measured from the mean of all members of the single class, such as all women within the set of identical twins.
