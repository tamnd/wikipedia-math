# Harold Grad

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harold Grad"}

**Harold Grad** (January 23, 1923, in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) – November 17, 1986) was an American applied mathematician. His work specialized in the application of [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) to [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics) and [magnetohydrodynamics](wikipedia://en/40310).

:::

## Work {#Work}

In statistical mechanics he had developed in his thesis new methods for the solution of the [Boltzmann equation](wikipedia://en/1026522). He derived the [Boltzmann equation](wikipedia://en/1026522) from [Liouville equation](wikipedia://en/312301) using [BBGKY hierarchy](wikipedia://en/5703638) under certain limits, known as [Boltzmann–Grad limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann–Grad_limit).[^o4] Harold Grad was the founder of the Magneto-fluid Dynamics Division of the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) and served as its head until shortly before his death[^o5] From 1964 to 1967 and 1974 to 1977 he was a member of the Advisory Committee for Fusion Energy at [Oak Ridge National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Ridge_National_Laboratory).[^o6]

Grad was a critic and supporter of many early fusion schemes including picket fences, [magnetic mirrors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_mirror) and [biconic cusps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biconic_cusp).[^o7][^o8][^o9]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1970, Grad became a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences). He was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in Nice in 1970[^o11] and in Stockholm in 1962.

In 1981, Grad was awarded a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship).[^o12] In 1982, he was awarded the [Eringen Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eringen_Medal) from the Society of Engineering Science,[^o13] and in 1986, he received the [James Clerk Maxwell Prize for Plasma Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clerk_Maxwell_Prize_for_Plasma_Physics) from the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society).

The Courant Institute offers the Harold Grad Memorial Prize to outstanding performance and promise as a graduate student.[^o5]

## Life {#Life}

Grad received a bachelor's degree in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) from the [Cooper Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Union) in 1943 and his masters at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) in 1945. Grad did his doctoral work under [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238) and graduated in 1948. His thesis was on the approximation of the Boltzmann Equation by torque. He was then at the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) of Mathematical Sciences of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) from 1948 as associate professor and from 1957 until his death. Grad conducted research in [magnetohydrodynamics](wikipedia://en/40310), the mathematical formulation of plasma physics and applications of plasma physics to nuclear fusion. He led the [magnetohydrodynamics](wikipedia://en/40310) department from 1956 until 1980.
