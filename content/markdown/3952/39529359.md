# Centered octahedral number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Centered octahedral number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **centered octahedral number** or **Haüy octahedral number** is a [figurate number](wikipedia://en/325260) that counts the points of a three-dimensional [integer lattice](wikipedia://en/3333402) that lie inside an [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) centered at the origin.[^o13] The same numbers are special cases of the [Delannoy numbers](wikipedia://en/14736250), which count certain two-dimensional lattice paths.[^o14] The Haüy octahedral numbers are named after [René Just Haüy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Just_Haüy).

:::

## History {#History}

The name "Haüy octahedral number" comes from the work of [René Just Haüy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Just_Haüy), a French [mineralogist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineralogist) active in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. His "Haüy construction" approximates an octahedron as a [polycube](wikipedia://en/710426), formed by accreting concentric layers of cubes onto a central cube. The centered octahedral numbers count the cubes used by this construction.[^o4] Haüy proposed this construction, and several related constructions of other polyhedra, as a model for the structure of [crystalline minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal).[^o5][^o6]

## Formula {#Formula}

*Image: centered\_octahedral\_number\_lattice.svg.* Number of points on an integer cubic lattice of taxicab distance of up to 0 (black), 1 (red and black) and 2 (red, black and blue) from the origin

The number of three-dimensional lattice points within *n* steps of the origin is given by the formula

$$\frac{(2n+1)\left(2n^2+2n+3\right)}{3}$$

The first few of these numbers (for *n* = 0, 1, 2, ...) are

1, 7, 25, 63, 129, 231, 377, 575, 833, 1159, ...

The [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) of the centered octahedral numbers is[^o9][^o10]

$$\frac{(1+x)^3}{(1-x)^4}.$$

The centered octahedral numbers obey the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)[^o13]

$$C(n)=C(n-1)+4n^2+2.$$

They may also be computed as the sums of pairs of consecutive [octahedral numbers](wikipedia://en/584310).

:::

## Alternative interpretations {#Alternative_interpretations}

*Image: Delannoy3x3.svg.* 63 Delannoy paths through a 3 × 3 grid

The octahedron in the three-dimensional integer lattice, whose number of lattice points is counted by the centered octahedral number, is a [metric ball](wikipedia://en/160556) for three-dimensional [taxicab geometry](wikipedia://en/408354), a geometry in which distance is measured by the sum of the coordinatewise distances rather than by [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932). For this reason, (Luther Mertens 2011) call the centered octahedral numbers "the volume of the crystal ball".[^o10]

The same numbers can be viewed as figurate numbers in a different way, as the centered figurate numbers generated by a [pentagonal pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128461). That is, if one forms a sequence of concentric shells in three dimensions, where the first shell consists of a single point, the second shell consists of the six vertices of a pentagonal pyramid, and each successive shell forms a larger pentagonal pyramid with a [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915) of points on each triangular face and a [pentagonal number](wikipedia://en/504786) of points on the pentagonal face, then the total number of points in this configuration is a centered octahedral number.[^o13]

The centered octahedral numbers are also the [Delannoy numbers](wikipedia://en/14736250) of the form *D*(3,*n*). As for Delannoy numbers more generally, these numbers count the paths from the southwest corner of a 3 × *n* grid to the northeast corner, using steps that go one unit east, north, or northeast.[^o14]
