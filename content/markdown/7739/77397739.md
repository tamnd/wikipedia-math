# Jakub Nakcjanowicz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jakub Nakcjanowicz"}

**Jakub Nakcjanowicz** or **Nakcyanowicz** ([Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language): *Jokūbas Nakcijonavičius*; 1 May 1725 – 1777) was a [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) priest, mathematician, and astronomer of the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish–Lithuanian_Commonwealth). He was the second director of the [Vilnius University Astronomical Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_University_Astronomical_Observatory) (1758–1764).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Jakob Nakcyanowicz was born on 1 May 1725 in [Grodno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grodno), present-day [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus). He joined the [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) in 1742. From 1744 to 1746 he taught in present-day [Ilūkste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilūkste). From 1746 to 1749 Nakcyanowicz studied philosophy at the [Jesuit College in Polotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit_College_in_Polotsk). He returned to lecture in Ilūkste from 1749 to 1750. From 1750 to 1751 he lectured [syntax](wikipedia://en/26860) and grammar in [Pašiaušė](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pašiaušė). From 1751 to 1755 he studied theology at [Vilnius University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_University). He was ordained as a priest in 1754. Nakcyanowicz was prefect of the Diocesan Seminary in Vilnius.[^o3] Nakcyanowicz once again returned to Ilūkste to lecture on mathematics from 1754 to 1758. In 1758 he became the director of the [Vilnius University Astronomical Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_University_Astronomical_Observatory), a position preceded by the observatory's founder [Thomas Zebrowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Zebrowski), holding that title until 1764. Nakcyanowicz was Zebrowski's student.[^o4] In the university itself, Nakcyanowicz lectured on mathematics, geodesics, philosophy, and experimental physics. From 1764 he taught at various schools at Grodno.[^o5][^o6] From 1766 to 1768 he taught mathematics at [Navahrudak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navahrudak). In 1773 he was dean of the faculty of philosophy of Vilnius University.[^o7]

As a scientist, Nakcyanowicz was mostly interested in the works of [Christian Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Wolff_%28philosopher%29). Nakcyanowicz developed a mathematics textbook entitled *Exercitationes in analysi cum finitorum tum infinitorum mathematicae*, which was published in 1758. The textbook concerned the [binomial theorem](wikipedia://en/4677), progressions, [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), and [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261). He wrote another textbook entitled *Praelectiones mathematicae ex Wolfianis elementis adornatae* in 1759–1761, which concerned the basic principles of mathematics, [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118), geometry, trigonometry, and some algebra. In 1762 published a work on [conic sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic_sections) and other algebraic and transcendental curves.[^o5]
