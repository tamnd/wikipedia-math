# Loewner's torus inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Loewner's torus inequality"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), **Loewner's torus inequality** is an [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) due to [Charles Loewner](wikipedia://en/9337483). It relates the [systole](wikipedia://en/2545455) and the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of an arbitrary [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on the [2-torus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-torus).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

*Image: TorusSystoleLoop.png.* Shortest loop on a torus

In 1949 [Charles Loewner](wikipedia://en/9337483) proved that every metric on the 2-[torus](wikipedia://en/74800) $\mathbb T^2$ satisfies the optimal inequality

$$\operatorname{sys}^2 \leq \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \operatorname{area}(\mathbb T^2),$$

where "sys" is its [systole](wikipedia://en/2545455), i.e. least length of a noncontractible loop. The constant appearing on the right hand side is the [Hermite constant](wikipedia://en/12528854) $\gamma_2$ in dimension 2, so that Loewner's torus inequality can be rewritten as

$$\operatorname{sys}^2 \leq \gamma_2\;\operatorname{area}(\mathbb T^2).$$

The inequality was first mentioned in the literature in (Pu 1952).

:::

## Case of equality {#Case_of_equality}

The boundary case of equality is attained if and only if the metric is flat and homothetic to the so-called *equilateral torus*, i.e. torus whose group of deck transformations is precisely the [hexagonal lattice](wikipedia://en/2868762) spanned by the cube roots of unity in $\mathbb C$. Geometrically, this torus can be obtained by gluing opposite pairs of edges of either a [regular hexagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_hexagon), or a [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) with 60° and 120° angles.

## Alternative formulation {#Alternative_formulation}

Given a doubly periodic metric on $\mathbb R^2$ (e.g. an imbedding in $\mathbb R^3$ which is invariant by a $\mathbb Z^2$ isometric action), there is a nonzero element $g\in \mathbb Z^2$ and a point $p\in \mathbb R^2$ such that $\operatorname{dist}(p, g.p)^2 \leq \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \operatorname{area} (F)$, where $F$ is a fundamental domain for the action, while $\operatorname{dist}$ is the Riemannian distance, namely least length of a path joining $p$ and $g . p$.

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of Loewner's torus inequality"}

## Proof of Loewner's torus inequality {#Proof_of_Loewner's_torus_inequality}

Loewner's torus inequality can be proved most easily by using the computational formula for the variance,

$$\operatorname{E}(X^2)-(\operatorname{E}(X))^2=\mathrm{var}(X).$$

Namely, the formula is applied to the [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) defined by the measure of the unit area flat torus in the conformal class of the given torus. For the random variable *X*, one takes the conformal factor of the given metric with respect to the flat one. Then the expected value E(*X*<sup>2</sup>) of *X*<sup>2</sup> expresses the total area of the given metric. Meanwhile, the expected value E(*X*) of *X* can be related to the systole by using [Fubini's theorem](wikipedia://en/255245). The variance of *X* can then be thought of as the isosystolic defect, analogous to the isoperimetric defect of [Bonnesen's inequality](wikipedia://en/9719512). This approach therefore produces the following version of Loewner's torus inequality with isosystolic defect:

$$\mathrm{area}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(\mathrm{sys})^2\geq \mathrm{var}(f),$$

where *ƒ* is the conformal factor of the metric with respect to a unit area flat metric in its conformal class.

:::

## Higher genus {#Higher_genus}

Whether or not the inequality

$$(\mathrm{sys})^2 \leq \gamma_2\,\mathrm{area}$$

is satisfied by all surfaces of nonpositive [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) is unknown. For [orientable surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable_surface) of genus 2 and genus 20 and above, the answer is affirmative, see work by Katz and Sabourau below.
