# Monotone polygon

*Image: M-polygon.svg.* Lines orthogonal to $L$: solid lime 2px solid blue 2px solid red 2px The top two polygons are monotone with respect to $L$ while the bottom two are not.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monotone polygon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) $P$ in the plane is called **monotone** with respect to a straight line $L$, if every line [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/71675950) to $L$ intersects the boundary of $P$ at most twice.[^o2]

:::

Similarly, a [polygonal chain](wikipedia://en/12056032) $C$ is called **monotone** with respect to a straight line $L$, if every line orthogonal to $L$ intersects $C$ at most once.

For many practical purposes this definition may be extended to allow cases when some edges of $P$ are orthogonal to $L$, and a [simple polygon](wikipedia://en/1059530) may be called monotone if a line segment that connects two points in $P$ and is orthogonal to $L$ lies completely in $P$.

Following the terminology for [monotone functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotone_function), the former definition describes **polygons strictly monotone** with respect to $L$.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Assume that *L* coincides with the [*x*-axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-axis). Then the leftmost and rightmost vertices of a monotone polygon decompose its boundary into two monotone [polygonal chains](wikipedia://en/12056032) such that when the vertices of any chain are being traversed in their natural order, their X-coordinates are [monotonically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonic) increasing or decreasing. In fact, this property may be taken for the definition of monotone polygon and it gives the polygon its name.

A [convex polygon](wikipedia://en/660019) is monotone with respect to any straight line and a polygon which is monotone with respect to every straight line is convex.

A linear time algorithm is known to report all directions in which a given simple polygon is monotone.[^o5] It was generalized to report all ways to decompose a simple polygon into two monotone chains (possibly monotone in different directions.)[^o6]

[Point in polygon](wikipedia://en/504484) queries with respect to a monotone polygon may be answered in [logarithmic time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_time) after [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time) preprocessing (to find the leftmost and rightmost vertices).[^o2]

A monotone polygon may be easily [triangulated](wikipedia://en/679384) in linear time.[^o7]

For a given set of points in the plane, a [bitonic tour](wikipedia://en/15936136) is a monotone polygon that connects the points. The minimum [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) bitonic tour for a given point set with respect to a fixed direction may be found in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) using [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297).[^o8] It is easily shown that such a minimal bitonic tour is a simple polygon: a pair of crossing edges may be replaced with a shorter non-crossing pair while preserving the bitonicity of the new tour.

*Image: Polygon-to-monotone.png.* Breaking a polygon into monotone polygons

A simple polygon may be easily [cut into monotone polygons](wikipedia://en/679384#Using_monotone_polygons) in *O*(*n* log *n*) time. However, since a triangle is a monotone polygon, [polygon triangulation](wikipedia://en/679384) is in fact cutting a polygon into monotone ones, and it may be performed for simple polygons in *O*(*n*) time with a complex algorithm.[^o9] A simpler randomized algorithm with linear expected time is also known.[^o10]

Cutting a simple polygon into the minimal number of uniformly monotone polygons (i.e., monotone with respect to the same line) can be performed in polynomial time.[^o11]

In the context of [motion planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_planning), two nonintersecting monotone polygons are separable by a single translation (i.e., there exists a translation of one polygon such that the two become separated by a straight line into different halfplanes) and this separation may be found in linear time.[^o12]

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

### Sweepable polygons {#Sweepable_polygons}

A polygon is called **sweepable**, if a straight line may be continuously moved over the whole polygon in such a way that at any moment its intersection with the polygonal area is a convex set. A monotone polygon is sweepable by a line which does not change its orientation during the sweep. A polygon is **strictly sweepable** if no portion of its area is swept more than once. Both types of sweepability are recognized in quadratic time.[^o16]

### 3D {#3D}

There is no single straightforward generalization of polygon monotonicity to higher dimensions.

In one approach the preserved monotonicity trait is the line *L*. A three-dimensional polyhedron is called **weakly monotonic** in direction *L* if all cross-sections orthogonal to *L* are simple polygons. If the cross-sections are convex, then the polyhedron is called **weakly monotonic in convex sense**.[^o12] Both types may be recognized in polynomial time.[^o16]

In another approach the preserved one-dimensional trait is the orthogonal direction. This gives rise for the notion of [polyhedral terrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_terrain) in three dimensions: a polyhedral surface with the property that each vertical (i.e., parallel to Z axis) line intersects the surface at most by one point or segment.
