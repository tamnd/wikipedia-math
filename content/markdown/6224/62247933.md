# Lemon (geometry)

*Image: Geometric\_lemon.jpg.* A lemon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lemon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **lemon** is a [geometric shape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_shape) that is constructed as the [surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549) of a [circular arc](wikipedia://en/5578920) of [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) less than half of a full circle rotated about an axis passing through the endpoints of the lens (or arc). The surface of revolution of the complementary arc of the same circle, through the same axis, is called an **apple**.

*Image: spindle\_torus\_apple\_lemon.png.* An *apple* and a *lemon* derived from a [spindle torus](wikipedia://en/74800#Geometry) with proportions of a [vesica piscis](wikipedia://en/471830)

The apple and lemon together make up a **spindle [torus](wikipedia://en/74800)** (or *self-crossing torus* or *self-intersecting torus*). The lemon forms the boundary of a [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292), while its surrounding apple is non-convex.[^o2][^o3]

:::

*Image: CFL football.jpg.* North American football

The ball in North American [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gridiron_football) has a shape resembling a geometric lemon. However, although used with a related meaning in geometry, the term "football" is more commonly used to refer to a surface of revolution whose [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) is positive and [constant](wikipedia://en/3638803), formed from a more complicated curve than a circular arc.[^o4] Alternatively, a football may refer to a more abstract [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504), a surface modeled locally on a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) except at two points.[^o5]

## Area and volume {#Area_and_volume}

The lemon is generated by rotating an arc of radius $R$ and half-angle $\varphi_m$ less than $\pi/2$ about its chord. Note that $\varphi$ denotes latitude, as used in geophysics. The surface area is given by[^o7]

$$A = 2\pi R^2 \int_{-\varphi_m}^{\varphi_m} (\cos\varphi - \cos\varphi_m)\,d\varphi.$$

The volume is given by

$$V = \pi R^3 \int_{-\varphi_m}^{\varphi_m} (\cos\varphi - \cos\varphi_m)^2 \cos\varphi \,d\varphi.$$

These integrals can be evaluated analytically, giving

$$A = 4\pi R^2 (\sin\varphi_m - \varphi_m\cos\varphi_m),$$

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3 \left[\sin^3\varphi_m - \tfrac{3}{4} \cos\varphi_m(2\varphi_m - \sin2\varphi_m)\right].$$

The apple is generated by rotating an arc of half-angle $\varphi_m$ greater than $\pi/2$ about its chord. The above equations are valid for both the lemon and apple.
