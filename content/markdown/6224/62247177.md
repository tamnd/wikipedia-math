# Dietmar Salamon

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Dietmar Salamon"}

**Dietmar Arno Salamon** (7 March 1953 – 4 November 2025) was a German mathematician.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Salamon studied mathematics at the [Leibniz University Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_University_Hannover). In 1982, he earned his doctorate at the [University of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bremen) with dissertation *On control and observation of neutral systems*.[^o4] He subsequently spent two years as a postdoctoral fellow at the Mathematical Research Center at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison), followed by one year at the Mathematical Research Institute at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich). In 1986, he became a lecturer at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick), where he was appointed full professor in 1994. The summer semester 1988 he spent as a visiting professor at the University of Bremen and the winter semester 1991 at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. From 1998 to 2018, he was a full professor of mathematics at ETH Zurich, retiring as professor emeritus in 2018.[^o6]

Salamon's field of research is [symplectic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_topology) and related fields such as [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298). Symplectic topology is a relatively new field of mathematics that developed into an important branch of mathematics in the 1990s. Some important new techniques are [Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Leonidovich_Gromov)'s [pseudoholomorphic curves](wikipedia://en/2294542), [Floer homology](wikipedia://en/2672856), and [Seiberg-Witten invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiberg-Witten_invariants) on four-dimensional [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470).

In 1994, he was an Invited Speaker with talk *Lagrangian intersections, 3-manifolds with boundary and the Atiyah-Floer conjecture* at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in Zurich. In 2012, he was elected a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). In 2017, he received, with [Dusa McDuff](wikipedia://en/2838322), the AMS [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Mathematical Exposition for the book *J-holomorphic curves and symplectic topology*, which they co-authored.[^o7] He was a member of [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) from 2011[^o8] until his death on 4 November 2025, at the age of 72.[^o9]
