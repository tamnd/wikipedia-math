# Joseph Lee Rodgers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph Lee Rodgers"}

**Joseph Lee Rodgers III** (born February 9, 1953)[^o4] is an American psychologist who specializes in quantitative psychology and topics in developmental psychology and social biology. He is the Lois Autrey Betts Professor of Psychology and Human Development Emeritus at [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University), and he is also the [George Lynn Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lynn_Cross) Research Professor Emeritus at the [University of Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oklahoma), where he taught from 1981 to 2012.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Rodgers graduated from the [University of Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oklahoma) in 1975 with a B.S. in mathematics and a B.A. in psychology. He received his Ph.D. in [quantitative psychology](wikipedia://en/3067624) from the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) in 1981, with a minor in [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878). His dissertation was on the effects of family configuration on mental development.

He is a past president of the [Society for the Study of Social Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_the_Study_of_Social_Biology), the [Society of Multivariate Experimental Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Multivariate_Experimental_Psychology), and Divisions 5 and 34 of the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association). From 2006 to 2011, he was [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) of *[Multivariate Behavioral Research](wikipedia://en/26785977)*. He has been a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) since 2012.[^o6]

His substantive research has focused on topics such as the relationship between [birth order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_order) and [human intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_intelligence),[^o7][^o8] as well as adolescent risk behaviors, like sexual activity and drug use.[^o9][^o10][^o11] His methodological research focuses on behavior genetics, exploratory data analysis,[^o12] correlation and regression,[^o13][^o14] and best practices for research methods.[^o15]
