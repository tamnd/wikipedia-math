# Aluthge transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aluthge transform"}

In mathematics and more precisely in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **Aluthge transformation** is an operation defined on the set of [bounded operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_operators) of a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932). It was introduced by [Ariyadasa Aluthge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariyadasa_Aluthge) to study [p-hyponormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-hyponormal) [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $H$ be a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) and let $B(H)$ be the algebra of linear operators from $H$ to $H$. By the [polar decomposition](wikipedia://en/1369241) theorem, there exists a unique [partial isometry](wikipedia://en/1348079) $U$ such that $T=U|T|$ and $\ker(U)\supset\ker(T)$, where $|T|$ is the [square root of the operator](wikipedia://en/2929701) $T^*T$. If $T\in B(H)$ and $T=U|T|$ is its polar decomposition, the Aluthge transform of $T$ is the operator $\Delta(T)$ defined as:

$$\Delta(T)=|T|^{\frac12}U|T|^{\frac12}.$$

More generally, for any real number $\lambda\in [0,1]$, the $\lambda$-Aluthge transformation is defined as

$$\Delta_\lambda(T):=|T|^{\lambda}U|T|^{1-\lambda}\in B(H).$$

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

For vectors $x,y \in H$, let $x\otimes y$ denote the operator defined as

$$\forall z\in H\quad x\otimes y(z)=\langle z,y\rangle x.$$

An elementary calculation[^o7] shows that if $y\ne0$, then $\Delta_\lambda(x\otimes y)=\Delta(x\otimes y)=\frac{\langle x,y\rangle}{\lVert y \rVert^2} y\otimes y.$

:::
