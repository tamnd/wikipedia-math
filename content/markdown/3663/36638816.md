# Armenian eternity sign

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Armenian eternity sign"}

*Image: Arevakhach.svg.* Armenian Sun Cross (Arevakhach)

The **Armenian eternity sign** (⟨֎ ֍⟩, Հավերժության նշան) or **Arevakhach’** (Արեւախաչ, "Sun Cross") is an ancient [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia) [national symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_symbol) and a symbol of the [national identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_identity) of the [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians).[^o2] It is one of the most common symbols in [Armenian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_architecture),[^o3][^o4] carved on *[khachkars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khachkar)* and on walls of [churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church).

:::

## Evolution and use {#Evolution_and_use}

*Image: Armenian soldier from Letchashen, 15-14th centuries BC. Reconstructed by Prof. A. D. Tchagharian, Sardarapat museum, Armenia.jpg.* Armenian soldier from [Lchashen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lchashen–Metsamor_culture), 15-14th centuries BC. Reconstructed by Prof. A. D. Tchagharian in Sardarabat Museum

In medieval Armenian culture, the eternity sign symbolized the concept of everlasting, celestial life.[^o6] From the 1st century BC, it appeared on Armenian [steles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stele); later it became part of *[khachkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khachkar)* symbolism.[^o7] Around the 8th century the use of the Armenian symbol of eternity had become a long established national iconographical practice,[^o8] and it has kept its meaning in modern times.[^o9] Besides being one of the main components of *khachkars*,[^o10] it can be found on church walls,[^o11][^o12][^o13] tomb stones and other architectural monuments.[^o14][^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18] Notable churches with the eternity sign include the [Mashtots Hayrapet Church of Garni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashtots_Hayrapet_Church_of_Garni),[^o19] [Horomayr Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horomayr_Monastery),[^o20] [Nor Varagavank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nor_Varagavank),[^o21] [Tsitsernavank Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsitsernavank_Monastery).[^o22] An [identical symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_eternity_sign) appears in the reliefs of the [Divriği Great Mosque and Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divriği_Great_Mosque_and_Hospital),[^o23] and is likely a borrowing from earlier Armenian churches of the area. It can also be found on [Armenian manuscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_manuscript).

The eternity sign is used on the logos of government agencies and on [commemorative coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commemorative_coin),[^o24] as well as Armenian government agencies and non-government organizations and institutions in Armenia and the [Armenian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_diaspora).[^o25]

The symbol is also used by [Armenian neopagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_neopaganism) organizations and their followers. It is called by them "Arevakhach" (Արեւախաչ, "sun cross").[^o26]

## ArmSCII and Unicode {#ArmSCII_and_Unicode}

*Image: Right-Facing and Left Facing Armenian Eternity Sign.svg.* The right- and left-facing Armeternity unicode font glyphs

In [ArmSCII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArmSCII), Armenian Standard Code for Information Interchange, an Armenian eternity sign has been encoded in 7-bit and 8-bit standard and ad hoc encodings since at least 1987. In 2010 the Armenian National Institute of Standards suggested encoding an Armenian Eternity sign in the [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) character set,[^o28] and both left-facing ⟨֎⟩ and right-facing ⟨֍⟩ Armenian eternity signs were included in Unicode version 7.0 when it was released in June 2014.[^o29]
