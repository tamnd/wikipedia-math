# Spieker center

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spieker center"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Spieker center** is a special point associated with a plane [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654). It is defined as the [center of mass](wikipedia://en/173961) of the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of the triangle. The Spieker center of a triangle $\triangle ABC$ is the [center of gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_gravity) of a homogeneous wire frame in the shape of $\triangle ABC$.[^o2][^o3] The point is named in honor of the 19th-century [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) [geometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometer) [Theodor Spieker](wikipedia://en/35733447).[^o4] The Spieker center is a [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488) and it is listed as the point *X*(10) in [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421)'s [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593).

:::

## Location {#Location}

*Image: SpiekerCenter.svg.* **Construction of the Spieker center.** \#ffe8c4 \#c4a487 solid black solid red

The following result can be used to locate the Spieker center of any triangle.[^o2]

The Spieker center of triangle $\triangle ABC$ is the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) of the [medial triangle](wikipedia://en/3339743) of $\triangle ABC$.

That is, the Spieker center of $\triangle ABC$ is the center of the circle [inscribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscribed) in the [medial triangle](wikipedia://en/3339743) of $\triangle ABC$. This circle is known as the [Spieker circle](wikipedia://en/9502005).

The Spieker center is also located at the intersection of the three [cleavers](wikipedia://en/14925692) of triangle $\triangle ABC$. A cleaver of a triangle is a line segment that [bisects the perimeter](wikipedia://en/152547#Area_bisectors_and_area-perimeter_bisectors_of_a_triangle) of the triangle and has one endpoint at the midpoint of one of the three sides. Each cleaver contains the center of mass of the boundary of $\triangle ABC$, so the three cleavers meet at the Spieker center.

To see that the incenter of the medial triangle coincides with the intersection point of the cleavers, consider a homogeneous wireframe in the shape of triangle $\triangle ABC$ consisting of three wires in the form of line segments having lengths $a, b, c$. The wire frame has the same center of mass as a system of three particles of masses $a, b, c$ placed at the midpoints $D, E, F$ of the sides $\overline{BC}, \overline{CA}, \overline{AB}$. The centre of mass of the particles at $E$ and $F$ is the point $P$ which divides the segment $\overline{EF}$ in the ratio $c : b$. The line $DP$ is the internal bisector of $\angle D$. The centre of mass of the three particle system thus lies on the internal bisector of $\angle D$. Similar arguments show that the center mass of the three particle system lies on the internal bisectors of $\angle E$ and $\angle F$ also. It follows that the center of mass of the wire frame is the point of concurrence of the internal bisectors of the angles of the triangle $\triangle DEF$ , which is the incenter of the medial triangle $\triangle DEF$ .

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: CleavanceCenter.svg.* The Spieker center of a triangle is the cleavance center of the triangle. \#ffe8c4 dashed #333333 solid blue \#c4a487 solid red

Let $S$ be the Spieker center of triangle $\triangle ABC$.

- The [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) of $S$ are

$bc(b+c) : ca(c+a) : ab(a+b).$[^o8]

- The [barycentric coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barycentric_coordinate_system_%28mathematics%29) of $S$ are

$b+c : c+a : a+b.$[^o8]

- $S$ is the [radical center](wikipedia://en/326298) of the three [excircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle).[^o9]
- $S$ is the [cleavance center](wikipedia://en/14925692) of triangle $\triangle ABC$ [^o2]
- $S$ is [collinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collinear) with the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) ($I$), the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) ($G$), and the [Nagel point](wikipedia://en/9489914) ($M$) of triangle $\triangle ABC$. Moreover,[^o10]

$$IS= SM, \quad IG= 2 \cdot GS, \quad MG= 2\cdot IG.$$

Thus on a suitably scaled and positioned number line, $I = 0$, $G = 2$, $S = 3$, and $M = 6$.

- $S$ lies on the [Kiepert hyperbola](wikipedia://en/69991093). $S$ is the point of concurrence of the lines $AX, BY, CZ$ where $\triangle XBC, \triangle YCA, \triangle ZAB$ are similar, isosceles and similarly situated triangles constructed on the sides of triangle $\triangle ABC$ as bases, having the common base angle

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}\left[ \tan\left(\frac{A}{2}\right) \tan\left(\frac{B}{2}\right) \tan\left(\frac{C}{2}\right) \right].$$
