# Knot thickness

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Knot thickness"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), each [link](wikipedia://en/1161784) and [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) can have an assigned **knot thickness**. Each realization of a link or knot has a thickness assigned to it. The thickness τ of a link allows us to introduce a scale with respect to which we can then define the [ropelength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ropelength) of a link.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

There exist several possible definitions of thickness that coincide for smooth enough curves.

### Global radius of curvature {#Global_radius_of_curvature}

The thickness is defined using the simpler concept of the local thickness τ(*x*). The local thickness at a point *x* on the link is defined as

$$\tau(x)=\inf r(x,y,z),\,$$

where *x*, *y*, and *z* are points on the link, all distinct, and *r*(*x*, *y*, *z*) is the radius of the circle that passes through all three points (*x*, *y*, *z*). From this definition we can deduce that the local thickness is at most equal to the local radius of curvature.

The thickness of a link is defined as

$\tau(L) = \inf \tau(x).$[^o5]

### Injectivity radius {#Injectivity_radius}

This definition ensures that a [normal tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_tube) to the link with radius equal to τ(*L*) will not self intersect, and so we arrive at a "real world" knot made out of a thick string.[^o7]

:::
