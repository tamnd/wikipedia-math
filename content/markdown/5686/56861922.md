# White noise analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="White noise analysis"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **white noise analysis**, otherwise known as Hida calculus, is a framework for infinite-dimensional and [stochastic calculus](wikipedia://en/228001), based on the [Gaussian white noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_white_noise) [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325), to be compared with [Malliavin calculus](wikipedia://en/837875) based on the [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984).[^o2] It was initiated by Takeyuki Hida in his 1975 Carleton Mathematical Lecture Notes.[^o3]

:::

The term [white noise](wikipedia://en/46182) was first used for signals with a flat spectrum.

## White noise measure {#White_noise_measure}

The white noise [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) $\mu$ on the space $S'(\mathbb{R})$ of [tempered distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempered_distributions) has the [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401)[^o5]

$$C(f)=\int_{S'(\mathbb{R})}\exp \left( i\left\langle \omega ,f\right\rangle \right) \, d\mu (\omega )=\exp \left( -\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}} f^2(t) \, dt\right), \quad f\in S(\mathbb{R}).$$

### Brownian motion in white noise analysis {#Brownian_motion_in_white_noise_analysis}

A version of Wiener's Brownian motion $B(t)$ is obtained by the [dual pairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_pair)

$$B(t) = \langle \omega, 1\!\!1_{[0,t)}\rangle,$$

where $1\!\!1_{[0,t)}$ is the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) of the interval $[0,t)$. Informally

$$B(t)=\int_0^t \omega(t) \, dt$$

and in a generalized sense

$$\omega(t)=\frac{d B(t)}{dt}.$$

## Hilbert space {#Hilbert_space}

Fundamental to white noise analysis is the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932)

$$(L^2):=L^2\left( S'(\mathbb{R}),\mu \right),$$

generalizing the Hilbert spaces $L^2(\mathbb{R}^n,e^{-\frac{1}{2} x^2}d^n x)$ to infinite dimension.

### [Wick polynomials](wikipedia://en/11938767) {#Wick_polynomials}

An orthonormal basis in this Hilbert space, generalizing that of [Hermite polynomials](wikipedia://en/195984), is given by the so-called "Wick", or "[normal ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_order)" polynomials $\left\langle {:\omega^n:} , f_n\right\rangle$ with ${:\omega^n:} \in S'(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and $f_n \in S(\mathbb{R}^n)$

with normalization

$$\int_{S'(\mathbb{R})}\left\langle :\omega^n:,f_n \right\rangle^2 \, d\mu(\omega) = n!\int f_{n}^2(x_1,\ldots,x_n) \, d^n x,$$

entailing the Itô-Segal-Wiener isomorphism of the white noise Hilbert space $(L^2)$ with [Fock space](wikipedia://en/230491):

$$L^2\left( S'(\mathbb{R}),\mu \right) \simeq \bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^\infty \operatorname{Sym} L^2(\mathbb{R}^n,n! \, d^n x).$$

The "chaos expansion"

$$\varphi(\omega) =\sum_n \left\langle :\omega^n:, f_n\right\rangle$$

in terms of [Wick polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wick_ordered) correspond to the expansion in terms of multiple [Wiener integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_integral). Brownian [martingales](wikipedia://en/274536) $M_t(\omega)$ are characterized by kernel functions $f_n$ depending on $t$ only a "cut-off":

$$f_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n;t)= \begin{cases} f_n (x_1,\ldots,x_n) & \text{if } i x_i\leq t, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}$$

### [Gelfand triples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfand_triple) {#Gelfand_triples}

Suitable restrictions of the kernel function $\varphi _{n}$ to be smooth and rapidly decreasing in $x$ and $n$ give rise to spaces of white noise [test functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29#Definitions_of_test_functions_and_distributions) $\varphi$, and, by [duality](wikipedia://en/609737), to spaces of [generalized functions](wikipedia://en/1580246) $\Psi$ of white noise, with

$$\left\langle \! \left\langle \Psi ,\varphi \right\rangle \!\right\rangle :=\sum_n n!\left\langle \psi_n,\varphi_n \right\rangle$$

generalizing the scalar product in $(L^2)$. Examples are the Hida triple, with

$$\varphi \in (S)\subset (L^2)\subset (S)^\ast \ni \Psi$$

or the more general Kondratiev triples.[^o10]

## T- and S-transform {#T-_and_S-transform}

Using the white noise test functions

$$\varphi_f(\omega ):=\exp \left( i\left\langle \omega ,f\right\rangle \right) \in (S),\quad f \in S(\mathbb{R})$$

one introduces the "T-transform" of white noise distributions $\Psi$ by setting

$$T\Psi (f):=\left\langle \!\left\langle \Psi ,\varphi _{f}\right\rangle \!\right\rangle .$$

Likewise, using

$$\phi_f(\omega ):=\exp \left( -\frac{1}{2}\int f^2(t) \, dt\right) \exp\left( -\left\langle \omega ,f\right\rangle \right) \in (S)$$

one defines the "S-transform" of white noise distributions $\Psi$ by

$$S\Psi (f):=\left\langle \!\left\langle \Psi ,\phi_f\right\rangle\! \right\rangle,\quad f \in S(\mathbb{R}).$$

It is worth noting that for [generalized functions](wikipedia://en/1580246) $\Psi$, the S-transform is just

$$S\Psi (f)=\sum n!\left\langle \psi_n,f^{\otimes n}\right\rangle.$$

Depending on the choice of Gelfand triple, the white noise test functions and distributions are characterized by corresponding growth and analyticity properties of their S- or T-transforms.[^o5][^o10]

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Characterization theorem"}

### Characterization theorem {#Characterization_theorem}

> The function $G(f)$ is the T-transform of a (unique) Hida distribution $\Psi$ iff for all $f_1,f_2\in S(R),$ the function $z\mapsto G(zf_1+f_2)$ is analytic in the whole complex plane and of second order exponential growth, i.e.
>
> $$\left\vert G(\ f)\right\vert <ae^{bK(f,f)},$$
>
> where $K$ is some continuous quadratic form on $S'(\mathbb{R})\times S'(\mathbb{R})$.[^o5][^o14][^o15]

The same is true for S-transforms, and similar characterization theorems hold for the more general [Kondratiev](wikipedia://en/42815855) distributions.[^o10]

:::

## Calculus {#Calculus}

For test functions $\varphi \in (S)$, partial, directional derivatives exist:

$$\partial_\eta \varphi (\omega ):=\lim_{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0}\frac{\varphi (\omega +\varepsilon \eta )-F(\omega )} \varepsilon$$

where $\omega$ may be varied by any generalized function $\eta$. In particular, for the Dirac distribution $\eta =\delta _{t}$ one defines the "Hida derivative", denoting

$$\partial_t \varphi (\omega ):=\lim_{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0} \frac{\varphi(\omega +\varepsilon \delta_t)-F(\omega )} \varepsilon.$$

Gaussian [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252) yields the dual operator on distribution space

$$\partial_t^\ast =-\partial_t+\omega(t)$$

An infinite-dimensional gradient

$$\nabla :(S)\rightarrow L^2(R,dt) \otimes (S)$$

is given by

$$\nabla F(t,\omega) =\partial_t F(\omega).$$

The Laplacian $\triangle$ ("[Laplace–Beltrami operator](wikipedia://en/1763356)") with

$$-\triangle =\int dt\;\partial_t^\ast \partial_t \geq 0$$

plays an important role in infinite-dimensional analysis and is the image of the [Fock space](wikipedia://en/230491) number operator.

## Stochastic integrals {#Stochastic_integrals}

A stochastic integral, the [Hitsuda–Skorokhod integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitsuda–Skorokhod_integral), can be defined for suitable families $\Psi (t)$ of white noise distributions as a [Pettis integral](wikipedia://en/20983125)

$$\int \partial_t^\ast \Psi (t) \, dt\in (S)^\ast,$$

generalizing the [Itô integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itô_integral) beyond adapted integrands.

::: {#o19 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

In general terms, there are two features of white noise analysis that have been prominent in applications.[^o20][^o21][^o22][^o23][^o24]

First, white noise is a generalized [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) with independent values at each time.[^o25] Hence it plays the role of a generalized system of independent coordinates, in the sense that in various contexts it has been fruitful to express more general processes occurring e.g. in engineering or [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680), in terms of white noise.[^o26][^o22][^o23]

Second, the characterization theorem given above allows various heuristic expressions to be identified as generalized functions of white noise. This is particularly effective to attribute a well-defined mathematical meaning to so-called "[functional integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_integral)". [Feynman integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_integral) in particular have been given rigorous meaning for large classes of quantum dynamical models.

Noncommutative extensions of the theory have grown under the name of quantum white noise, and finally, the [rotational invariance](wikipedia://en/424202) of the white noise characteristic function provides a framework for representations of infinite-dimensional rotation groups.
