# Sophie Dabo-Niang

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Sophie Dabo-Niang"}

**Sophie Dabo-Niang** (née *Dabo*) is a [Senegalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalese) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661), and professor[^o5] who has done outreach to increase the status of African mathematicians.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life {#Early_life}

Sophie was encouraged to pursue mathematics by her parents and her teachers. She knew she wanted to study mathematics early in high school.[^o5]

### Education {#Education}

Sophie Dabo-Niang earned her PhD in 2002 from the [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).[^o5] Sophie enjoys passing on her passion for mathematics to her students.[^o5]

### Marriage and children {#Marriage_and_children}

As of 2016, Dabo-Niang is married.[^o5] She had 3 children between starting her master's degree and finishing her doctoral thesis, and has 4 children in total.[^o5][^o8] She has said that balancing parenting and her mathematics career has been a challenge, and she credits her persistence to her desire to succeed and the support of her husband.[^o5]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Dabo-Niang has published articles on [functional statistics](wikipedia://en/2141368), [nonparametric](wikipedia://en/223369) and semi-parametric estimates of weakly independent processes, [spatial statistics](wikipedia://en/9467349), and [mathematical epidemiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_epidemiology).[^o10]

Dabo-Niang serves as an editor of the journal [Revista Colombiana de Estadística](wikipedia://en/32307378)[^o11][^o12] and is on the scientific committee of the [Centre International de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées (CIMPA)](wikipedia://en/50319899).[^o13]

### Professorship and developing country outreach {#Professorship_and_developing_country_outreach}

Sophie Dabo-Niang has successfully supervised the doctoral theses of several students in Africa.[^o15] As of January 2021 she is a full professor at the [University of Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lille) and is supervising and co-supervising multiple African students.[^o5] She has taught master's-level statistics courses, including in Senegal.[^o5]

She introduced the [spatial statistics](wikipedia://en/9467349) subfields to [a university in Dakar, Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Berger_University), and supervised the first Senegalese and Mauritanian doctoral students focusing on the field. She often participates on thesis juries in Africa.[^o5]

Dabo-Niang has coordinated scientific events in Africa. In Senegal, she coordinated a [CIMPA](wikipedia://en/50319899) event and an event to encourage young girls in the mathematical sciences.[^o5] She serves as the chair of the Developing Countries Committee for the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470).[^o12][^o16]
