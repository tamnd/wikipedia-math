# Kawahara equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kawahara equation"}

The **Kawahara equation** is a [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) that arises in various fields of [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), particularly in the study of wave phenomena. Named after the Japanese mathematician T. Kawahara, who first introduced it in the context of [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034) and nonlinear wave propagation, this equation extends the well-known [Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg-de_Vries_equation) by incorporating higher-order derivatives. This inclusion allows for the modeling of more complex wave behaviors, capturing phenomena such as wave shape distortion and the emergence of solitary waves in dispersive systems.[^o2][^o3]

:::

The Kawahara equation is a fifth-order KdV equation and typically expressed as:[^o4]

$$\partial_t \phi + \alpha \phi \partial_x \phi+ \beta \partial_x^3\phi - \gamma\, \partial_x^5 \phi = 0,$$

where waves described by function $\phi(x,t)$ and $\alpha,\,\beta$, and $\gamma > 0$ are constant.
