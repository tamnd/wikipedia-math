# Metaplectic structure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Metaplectic structure"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **metaplectic structure** is the [symplectic](wikipedia://en/294298) analog of [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303) on [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable) [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652). A metaplectic structure on a [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) allows one to define the [symplectic spinor bundle](wikipedia://en/32313477), which is the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) bundle associated to the metaplectic structure via the metaplectic representation, giving rise to the notion of a **symplectic spinor field** in differential geometry.

:::

Symplectic spin structures have wide applications to [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), in particular to [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) where they are an essential ingredient in establishing the idea that symplectic spin geometry and symplectic Dirac operators may give valuable tools in symplectic geometry and symplectic topology. They are also of purely mathematical interest in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), and [K theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_theory). They form the foundation for symplectic spin geometry.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A metaplectic structure [^o4] on a [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) $(M, \omega)$ is an [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) lift of the [symplectic frame bundle](wikipedia://en/32050867) $\pi_{\mathbf R}\colon{\mathbf R}\to M\,$ with respect to the double covering $\rho\colon {\mathrm {Mp}}(n,{\mathbb R})\to {\mathrm {Sp}}(n,{\mathbb R}).\,$ In other words, a pair $({\mathbf P},F_{\mathbf P})$ is a metaplectic structure on the principal bundle $\pi_{\mathbf R}\colon{\mathbf R}\to M\,$ when

a) $\pi_{\mathbf P}\colon{\mathbf P}\to M\,$ is a principal ${\mathrm {Mp}}(n,{\mathbb R})$-bundle over $M$,

b) $F_{\mathbf P}\colon{\mathbf P}\to {\mathbf R}\,$ is an [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) $2$-fold [covering map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_map) such that

$\pi_{\mathbf R}\circ F_{\mathbf P}=\pi_{\mathbf P}$ and $F_{\mathbf P}({\mathbf p}q)=F_{\mathbf P}({\mathbf p})\rho(q)$ for all ${\mathbf p}\in {\mathbf P}$ and $q\in {\mathrm {Mp}}(n,{\mathbb R}).$

The principal bundle $\pi_{\mathbf P}\colon{\mathbf P}\to M\,$ is also called the bundle of **metaplectic frames** over $M$.

Two metaplectic structures $({\mathbf P_1},F_{\mathbf P_1})$ and $({\mathbf P_2},F_{\mathbf P_2})$ on the same [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) $(M, \omega)$ are called **equivalent** if there exists a ${\mathrm {Mp}}(n,{\mathbb R})$-equivariant map $f\colon {\mathbf P_1}\to {\mathbf P_2}$ such that

$F_{\mathbf P_2}\circ f=F_{\mathbf P_1}$ and $f({\mathbf p}q)=f({\mathbf p})q$ for all ${\mathbf p}\in {\mathbf P_1}$ and $q\in {\mathrm {Mp}}(n,{\mathbb R}).$

Of course, in this case $F_{\mathbf P_1}$ and $F_{\mathbf P_2}$ are two equivalent double coverings of the symplectic frame ${\mathrm {Sp}}(n,{\mathbb R})$-bundle $\pi_{\mathbf R}\colon{\mathbf R}\to M\,$ of the given symplectic manifold $(M, \omega)$.

### Obstruction {#Obstruction}

Since every [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) $M$ is necessarily of even dimension and [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable), one can prove that the topological [obstruction](wikipedia://en/2179639) to the existence of metaplectic structures is precisely the same as in Riemannian [spin geometry](wikipedia://en/32013990).[^o6] In other words, a symplectic manifold $(M, \omega)$ admits a **metaplectic structures** if and only if the second [Stiefel-Whitney class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stiefel-Whitney_class) $w_2(M)\in H^2(M,{\mathbb Z_2})$ of $M$ vanishes. In fact, the modulo $_2$ reduction of the first [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) $c_1(M)\in H^2(M,{\mathbb Z})$ is the second [Stiefel-Whitney class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stiefel-Whitney_class) $w_2(M)$. Hence, $(M, \omega)$ admits metaplectic structures if and only if $c_1(M)$ is even, i.e., if and only if $w_2(M)$ is zero.

If this is the case, the isomorphy classes of metaplectic structures on $(M, \omega)$ are classified by the first [cohomology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group) $H^1(M,{\mathbb Z_2})$ of $M$ with ${\mathbb Z_2}$-coefficients.

As the manifold $M$ is assumed to be oriented, the first [Stiefel-Whitney class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stiefel-Whitney_class) $w_1(M)\in H^1(M,{\mathbb Z_2})$ of $M$ vanishes too.

## Examples {#Examples}

### Manifolds admitting a metaplectic structure {#Manifolds_admitting_a_metaplectic_structure}

- Phase spaces $(T^{\ast}N,\theta)\, ,$ $N$ any orientable manifold.
- [Complex projective spaces](wikipedia://en/762977) ${\mathbb P}^{2k+1}{\mathbb C}\, ,$ $\, k\in {\mathbb N}_{0}\, .$ Since ${\mathbb P}^{2k+1}{\mathbb C}\,$ is [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected), such a structure has to be unique.
- [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) $Gr(2,4)\, ,$ etc.
