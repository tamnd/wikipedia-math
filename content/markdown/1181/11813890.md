# Supersymmetry algebras in 1 + 1 dimensions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Supersymmetry algebras in 1 + 1 dimensions"}

A two dimensional [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space), i.e. a flat space with one time and one spatial dimension, has a two-dimensional [Poincaré group](wikipedia://en/230487#Other_dimensions) IO(1,1) as its [symmetry group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacetime_symmetries). The respective [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) is called the [Poincaré algebra](wikipedia://en/230487#Poincar.C3.A9_algebra). It is possible to extend this algebra to a [supersymmetry algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersymmetry_algebra), which is a $\mathbb{Z}_2$-graded [Lie superalgebra](wikipedia://en/297501). The most common ways to do this are discussed below.

:::

## *N*=(2,2) algebra {#algebra}

Let the Lie algebra of IO(1,1) be generated by the following generators:

- $H = P_0$ is the generator of the time translation,
- $P = P_1$ is the generator of the space translation,
- $M = M_{01}$ is the generator of [Lorentz boosts](wikipedia://en/18404).

For the commutators between these generators, see [Poincaré algebra](wikipedia://en/230487#Poincar.C3.A9_algebra).

The $\mathcal{N}=(2,2)$ supersymmetry algebra over this space is a [supersymmetric extension](wikipedia://en/45603435) of this Lie algebra with the four additional generators ([supercharges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercharge)) $Q_+, \, Q_-, \, \overline{Q}_+, \, \overline{Q}_-$, which are odd elements of the Lie superalgebra. Under Lorentz transformations the generators $Q_+$ and $\overline{Q}_+$ transform as left-handed [Weyl spinors](wikipedia://en/33162584#Weyl_spinors), while $Q_-$ and $\overline{Q}_-$ transform as right-handed Weyl spinors. The algebra is given by the Poincaré algebra plus[^o3]

$$\begin{aligned} &\begin{aligned} &Q_+^2 = Q_{-}^2 = \overline{Q}_+^2 = \overline{Q}_-^2 =0, \\ &\{ Q_{\pm}, \overline{Q}_{\pm} \} = H \pm P, \\ \end{aligned} \\ &\begin{aligned} &\{\overline{Q}_+, \overline{Q}_- \} = Z, && \{Q_+, Q_- \} = Z^*, \\ &\{Q_-, \overline{Q}_+ \} =\tilde{Z}, && \{Q_+, \overline{Q}_-\} = \tilde{Z}^*,\\ &{[iM, Q_{\pm}]} = \mp Q_{\pm}, && {[iM, \overline{Q}_{\pm}]} = \mp \overline{Q}_{\pm}, \end{aligned} \end{aligned}$$

where all remaining commutators vanish, and $Z$ and $\tilde{Z}$ are complex [central charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_charge). The supercharges are related via $Q_{\pm}^\dagger = \overline{Q}_\pm$. $H$, $P$, and $M$ are [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/189682).

## Subalgebras of the *N*=(2,2) algebra {#Subalgebras_of_the_algebra}

### The *N*=(0,2) and *N*=(2,0) subalgebras {#The_and_subalgebras}

The $\mathcal{N} = (0,2)$ subalgebra is obtained from the $\mathcal{N} = (2,2)$ algebra by removing the generators $Q_-$ and $\overline{Q}_-$. Thus its anti-commutation relations are given by[^o3]

$$\begin{aligned} &Q_+^2 = \overline{Q}_+^2 = 0, \\ &\{ Q_{+}, \overline{Q}_{+} \} = H + P \\ \end{aligned}$$

plus the commutation relations above that do not involve $Q_-$ or $\overline{Q}_-$. Both generators are left-handed Weyl spinors.

Similarly, the $\mathcal{N} = (2,0)$ subalgebra is obtained by removing $Q_+$ and $\overline{Q}_+$ and fulfills

$$\begin{aligned} &Q_-^2 = \overline{Q}_-^2 = 0, \\ &\{ Q_{-}, \overline{Q}_{-} \} = H - P. \\ \end{aligned}$$

Both supercharge generators are right-handed.

### The *N*=(1,1) subalgebra {#The_subalgebra}

The $\mathcal{N} = (1,1)$ subalgebra is generated by two generators $Q_+^1$ and $Q_-^1$ given by

$\begin{aligned} Q^1_{\pm} = e^{i \nu_{\pm}} Q_{\pm} + e^{-i \nu_{\pm}} \overline{Q}_{\pm} \end{aligned}$for two real numbers $\nu_+$and $\nu_-$.

By definition, both supercharges are real, i.e. $(Q_{\pm}^1)^\dagger = Q^1_\pm$. They transform as [Majorana-Weyl spinors](wikipedia://en/29276#Spinor_fields_in_physics) under Lorentz transformations. Their anti-commutation relations are given by[^o3]

$$\begin{aligned} &\{ Q^1_{\pm}, Q^1_{\pm} \} = 2 (H \pm P), \\ &\{ Q^1_{+}, Q^1_{-} \} = Z^1, \end{aligned}$$

where $Z^1$ is a real central charge.

### The *N*=(0,1) and *N*=(1,0) subalgebras {#The_and_subalgebras}

These algebras can be obtained from the $\mathcal{N} = (1,1)$ subalgebra by removing $Q_-^1$ resp. $Q_+^1$from the generators.
