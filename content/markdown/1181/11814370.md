# Center (category theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Center"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **center** (or **Drinfeld center**, after Soviet-American mathematician [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747)) is a variant of the notion of the [center](wikipedia://en/342453) of a monoid, group, or ring to a category.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The center of a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) $\mathcal{C} = (\mathcal{C},\otimes,I)$, denoted $\mathcal{Z(C)}$, is the category whose objects are pairs $(A,u)$ consisting of an object $A$ of $\mathcal{C}$ and an isomorphism $u_X:A \otimes X \rightarrow X \otimes A$ which is [natural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) in $X$ satisfying

$$u_{X \otimes Y} = (1 \otimes u_Y)(u_X \otimes 1)$$

and

$u_I = 1_A$ (this is actually a consequence of the first axiom).[^n1]

An arrow from $(A,u)$ to $(B,v)$ in $\mathcal{Z(C)}$ consists of an arrow $f\colon A \rightarrow B$ in $\mathcal{C}$ such that

$$v_X (f \otimes 1_X) = (1_X \otimes f) u_X.$$

This definition of the center appears in (Joyal Street 1991). Equivalently, the center may be defined as

$$\mathcal Z(\mathcal C) = \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal C \otimes \mathcal C^{op}}(\mathcal C),$$

i.e., the endofunctors of $\mathcal C$ which are compatible with the left and right action of $\mathcal C$ on itself given by the tensor product.

### Braiding {#Braiding}

The category $\mathcal{Z(C)}$ becomes a [braided monoidal category](wikipedia://en/1470466) with the tensor product on objects defined as

$$(A,u) \otimes (B,v) = (A \otimes B,w)$$

where $w_X = (u_X \otimes 1)(1 \otimes v_X)$, and the obvious braiding.

### Higher categorical version {#Higher_categorical_version}

The categorical center is particularly useful in the context of higher categories. This is illustrated by the following example: the center of the ([abelian](wikipedia://en/45063)) category $\mathrm{Mod}_R$ of $R$-modules, for a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $R$, is $\mathrm{Mod}_R$ again. The center of a monoidal [∞-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_category) $\mathcal C$ can be defined, analogously to the above, as

$$Z(\mathcal C) := \mathrm{End}_{\mathcal C \otimes \mathcal C^{op}}(\mathcal C).$$

Now, in contrast to the above, the center of the derived category of $R$-modules (regarded as an ∞-category) is given by the derived category of modules over the cochain complex encoding the [Hochschild cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochschild_cohomology), a complex whose degree 0 term is $R$ (as in the abelian situation above), but includes higher terms such as $\mathrm{Hom}(R, R)$ ([derived](wikipedia://en/433875) Hom).[^o8]

The notion of a center in this generality is developed by (Lurie 2017, §5.3.1). Extending the above-mentioned braiding on the center of an ordinary monoidal category, the center of a monoidal ∞-category becomes an $E_2$-monoidal category. More generally, the center of a $E_k$-monoidal category is an algebra object in $E_k$-monoidal categories and therefore, by [Dunn additivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunn_additivity), an $E_{k+1}$-monoidal category.

:::

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

(Hinich 2007) has shown that the Drinfeld center of the category of sheaves on an [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) *X* is the category of sheaves on the [inertia orbifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertia_orbifold) of *X*. For *X* being the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) of a finite group *G*, the inertia orbifold is the stack quotient *G*/*G*, where *G* acts on itself by conjugation. For this special case, Hinich's result specializes to the assertion that the center of the category of [*G*-representations](wikipedia://en/12695) (with respect to some ground field *k*) is equivalent to the category consisting of *G*-graded *k*-vector spaces, i.e., objects of the form

$$\bigoplus_{g \in G} V_g$$

for some *k*-vector spaces, together with *G*-equivariant morphisms, where *G* acts on itself by conjugation.

In the same vein, (Ben-Zvi Francis Nadler 2010) have shown that Drinfeld center of the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves on a perfect stack *X* is the derived category of sheaves on the loop stack of *X*.

:::

## Related notions {#Related_notions}

### Centers of monoid objects {#Centers_of_monoid_objects}

The [center of a monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_a_group) and the Drinfeld center of a monoidal category are both instances of the following more general concept. Given a monoidal category $\mathcal C$ and a [monoid object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid_object) $A$ in $\mathcal C$, the center of $A$ is defined as

$$Z(A) = \mathrm{End}_{A \otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}}(A).$$

For $\mathcal C$ being the category of sets (with the usual cartesian product), a monoid object is simply a monoid, and $Z(A)$ is the center of the monoid. Similarly, if $\mathcal C$ is the category of abelian groups, monoid objects are rings, and the above recovers the [center of a ring](wikipedia://en/53796787). Finally, if $\mathcal C$ is the [category of categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_categories), with the product as the monoidal operation, monoid objects in $\mathcal C$ are monoidal categories, and the above recovers the Drinfeld center.

### Categorical trace {#Categorical_trace}

The categorical trace of a monoidal category (or monoidal ∞-category) is defined as

$$Tr(C) := C \otimes_{C \otimes C^{op}} C.$$

The concept is being widely applied, for example in (Zhu 2018).
