# Robert Creighton Buck

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Creighton Buck"}

**Robert Creighton Buck** (30 August 1920 [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati) – 1 February 1998 Wisconsin), usually cited as R. Creighton Buck, was an American mathematician who, with [Ralph Boas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_P._Boas,_Jr), introduced [Boas–Buck polynomials](wikipedia://en/32770184).[^o2] He taught at [University of Wisconsin&ndash;Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin&ndash;Madison) for 40 years. In addition, he was a writer.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Buck was born in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati).[^o5] He studied at the [University of Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cincinnati) and then earned his PhD in 1947[^o5] at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) under [David Widder](wikipedia://en/14428679) and [Ralph Boas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Boas) with dissertation *Uniqueness, Interpolation and Characterization Theorems for Functions of Exponential Type*. For three years he was an assistant professor at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), before he became in 1950 an associate professor at the [University of Wisconsin, Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin,_Madison), where he was promoted to professor in 1954. In 1973, he became the acting director of the University of Wisconsin Army Mathematics Research Center when [J. Barkley Rosser](wikipedia://en/679224) retired.[^o6] At Madison he became in 1980 "Hilldale Professor" and from 1964 to 1966 he was chair of the mathematics department. In 1990 he retired as professor emeritus but remained mathematically active.[^o5]

Buck worked on [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713), [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), topological algebra, and [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476). He worked for six years for the [Institute for Defense Analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses) in operations research. Buck wrote, in collaboration with Ellen F. Buck,[^o7] a textbook *Advanced Calculus*, commonly used in U.S. colleges and universities. He also worked on the history of mathematics. For his essay *Sherlock Holmes in Babylon*[^o8] he won the [Lester Randolph Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford). His doctoral students include [Lee Rubel](wikipedia://en/37550612) and [Thomas W. Hawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_W._Hawkins), a well-known historian of mathematics.

Buck was vice-president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA), whose "Committee on the Undergraduate Program in Mathematics“ (CUPM) he founded and from 1959 to 1963 chaired. In 1962 he was an invited speaker (*Global solutions of differential equations*) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm).

Buck was an accomplished amateur pianist and at age 18 won a prize for composition for piano. He wrote several science fiction stories.[^o5]

At age 77, he died at his home in Winconsin on February 1, 1998.
