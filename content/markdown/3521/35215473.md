# Bracket ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bracket ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762), the **bracket ring** is the [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) *k*\[*x*<sub>11</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*dn*</sub>\] generated by the *d*-by-*d* [minors](wikipedia://en/262107) of a [generic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_matrix) *d*-by-*n* [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) (*x*<sub>*ij*</sub>).

:::

The bracket ring may be regarded as the ring of polynomials on the [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of a [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) under the [Plücker embedding](wikipedia://en/1190270).[^o2]

For given *d* ≤ *n* we define as formal variables the *brackets* \[λ<sub>1</sub> λ<sub>2</sub> ... λ<sub>*d*</sub>\] with the λ taken from {1,...,*n*}, subject to \[λ<sub>1</sub> λ<sub>2</sub> ... λ<sub>*d*</sub>\] = − \[λ<sub>2</sub> λ<sub>1</sub> ... λ<sub>*d*</sub>\] and similarly for other [transpositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transposition_%28mathematics%29). The [set](wikipedia://en/26691) Λ(*n*,*d*) of size $\binom{n}{d}$ generates a polynomial ring *K*\[Λ(*n*,*d*)\] over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K*. There is a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) Φ(*n*,*d*) from *K*\[Λ(*n*,*d*)\] to the polynomial ring *K*\[*x*<sub>*i*,*j*</sub>\] in *nd* indeterminates given by mapping \[λ<sub>1</sub> λ<sub>2</sub> ... λ<sub>*d*</sub>\] to the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the *d* by *d* matrix consisting of the columns of the *x*<sub>*i*,*j*</sub> indexed by the λ. The *bracket ring* *B*(*n*,*d*) is the image of Φ. The [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) *I*(*n*,*d*) of Φ encodes the relations or *syzygies* that exist between the minors of a generic *n* by *d* matrix. The projective variety defined by the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) *I* is the (*n*−*d*)*d* dimensional Grassmann variety whose points correspond to *d*-dimensional [subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357) of an *n*-dimensional space.[^n1]

To compute with brackets it is necessary to determine when an expression lies in the ideal *I*(*n*,*d*). This is achieved by a *straightening law* due to Young (1928).[^n2]
