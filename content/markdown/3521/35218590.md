# Ludwig Adolf Sohncke

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ludwig Adolf Sohncke"}

**Ludwig Adolf Sohncke** (20 June 1807 in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg) (now Kaliningrad), [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) – 16 January 1853 in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_%28Saale%29), Kingdom of Prussia) was a German mathematician. He was father of the mathematician [Leonhard Sohncke](wikipedia://en/37572822).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Ludwig Adolf Sohncke studied at the [Albertina University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg) in his native town Königsberg, was doctoral student of [Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340).[^n1] There he began his scientific career as a private [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) in 1833. Together with Jacobi and [Franz Ernst Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Ernst_Neumann), he was co-founder and co-director of the Königsberg Seminar of Physics that aimed to install a more intensive and effective model for teaching Physics and mathematics.[^n2] In this institution, [Otto Hesse](wikipedia://en/740995) and [Theodor Schönemann](wikipedia://en/31259301) belonged to his students.[^n3]

In 1835, he was appointed — by recommendation of [Friedrich Wilhelm Bessel](wikipedia://en/11574) — to the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle) as extraordinary professor of mathematics in succession of [Julius Plücker](wikipedia://en/52146),[^n4] where he advanced to an [ordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_professor) in 1839. He worked with varied interests in analysis, mainly in the field of [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939), where he made discoveries over the [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183),[^n5] further in geometry, mechanics, and history of mathematics.[^n6] He translated the *History of Geometry* by [Michel Chasles](wikipedia://en/2252716) (1837) into the German language,[^o9] and edited some of his analytic lectures. Seen as a whole, his list of publications is small;[^n7] possible reasons could be difficult economic condition,[^n8] since the payments for professors in Halle were low and often was not enough for their living,[^n9] or a great burden of teaching, or a problem with alcohol.[^n8]

Together with the physics professor Kämtz, Sohncke made an attempt in 1837 to found a seminar for mathematics and physics similar to that he had been co-founder in Königsberg, but their initiative was opposed by the biologist [Schweigger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Schweigger). After long negotiations, a "Seminar für Mathematik und die gesammten Naturwissenschaften" (*Seminar for Mathematics and whole Sciences*) was founded with Sohnche as head of the mathematical division; later he became director of the seminar.[^n10] Although it brought out considerable success, it never got the importance of the Königsberg seminar.[^n11]

Ludwig Adolf Sohncke died in Halle on 16 January 1853; his academic successor was [Ferdinand Joachimsthal](wikipedia://en/4881398).[^n12]
