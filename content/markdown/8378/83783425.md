# Bertrand series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bertrand series"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Bertrand series** is a [series](wikipedia://en/15287) of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) of the form

:::

$$\sum_n \frac{1}{n^\alpha (\ln n)^\beta}$$

where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are two real numbers.[^o2][^o3] They are named after [Joseph Bertrand](wikipedia://en/488196).[^o4]

For $\beta=0$, this series reduces to the series $\textstyle \sum_n \frac{1}{n^\alpha}$ defining the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).

One can show a [necessary and sufficient](wikipedia://en/14922) condition of [convergence](wikipedia://en/1743431) for such series: it converges if and only if $\alpha>1$ or ($\alpha=1$ and $\beta>1$).[^o2][^o3] It can be proved by using the [integral test for convergence](wikipedia://en/454016) or the [Cauchy condensation test](wikipedia://en/3497105).

One has the following approximate values:

- $\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac1{n(\ln n)^2}\approx 2.10974$ ;
- $\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac1{n(\ln n)^3}\approx 2.06589$ ;
- $\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac1{n^2\ln n}\approx 0.60552$ .
