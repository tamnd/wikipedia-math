# Gysin homomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gysin homomorphism"}

In the field of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) known as [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), the **Gysin sequence** is a [long exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_exact_sequence) which relates the [cohomology classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_classes) of the [base space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_space), the fiber and the [total space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_space) of a [sphere bundle](wikipedia://en/276526#Sphere_bundles). The Gysin sequence is a useful tool for calculating the [cohomology rings](wikipedia://en/1069723) given the [Euler class](wikipedia://en/2932393) of the sphere bundle and vice versa. It was introduced by (txt), and is generalized by the [Serre spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/3085825).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Consider a fiber-oriented sphere bundle with total space *E*, base space *M*, fiber *S*<sup>*k*</sup> and [projection map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projection_map) $\pi$: $S^k \hookrightarrow E \stackrel{\pi}{\longrightarrow} M.$

Any such bundle defines a degree *k* + 1 cohomology class *e* called the [Euler class](wikipedia://en/2932393) of the bundle.

### De Rham cohomology {#De_Rham_cohomology}

Discussion of the sequence is clearest with [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530). There cohomology classes are represented by [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519), so that *e* can be represented by a (*k* + 1)-form.

The projection map $\pi$ induces a map in cohomology $H^\ast$ called its [pullback](wikipedia://en/9413508) $\pi^\ast$

$$\pi^*:H^*(M)\longrightarrow H^*(E). \,$$

In the case of a fiber bundle, one can also define a [pushforward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiberwise_integral) map $\pi_\ast$

$$\pi_*:H^*(E)\longrightarrow H^{*-k}(M)$$

which acts by [fiberwise integration of differential forms](wikipedia://en/40730681) on the oriented sphere – note that [this map goes "the wrong way"](wikipedia://en/22629996): it is a covariant map between objects associated with a contravariant functor.

Gysin proved that the following is a long exact sequence

$$\cdots \longrightarrow H^n(E) \stackrel{\pi_*}{\longrightarrow} H^{n-k}(M) \stackrel{e_\wedge}{\longrightarrow} H^{n+1}(M) \stackrel{\pi^*}{\longrightarrow} H^{n+1}(E) \longrightarrow \cdots$$

where $e_\wedge$ is the [wedge product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedge_product) of a differential form with the Euler class *e*.

### Integral cohomology {#Integral_cohomology}

The Gysin sequence is a long exact sequence not only for the [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) of differential forms, but also for [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) with integral coefficients. In the integral case one needs to replace the wedge product with the [Euler class](wikipedia://en/2932393) with the [cup product](wikipedia://en/848684), and the pushforward map no longer corresponds to integration.

:::

## Gysin homomorphism in algebraic geometry {#Gysin_homomorphism_in_algebraic_geometry}

Let *i*: *X* → *Y* be a (closed) [regular embedding](wikipedia://en/47089144) of codimension *d*, *Y'* → *Y* a morphism and *i'*: *X*' = *X* ×<sub>*Y*</sub> *Y*' → *Y*' the induced map. Let *N* be the pullback of the normal bundle of *i* to *X'*. Then the **refined Gysin homomorphism** *i*<sup>!</sup> refers to the composition

$$i^!: A_k(Y') \overset{\sigma}\longrightarrow A_k(N) \overset{\text{Gysin}} \longrightarrow A_{k-d}(X')$$

where

- σ is the [specialization homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialization_homomorphism); which sends a *k*-dimensional subvariety *V* to the [normal cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_cone) to the intersection of *V* and *X'* in *V*. The result lies in *N* through $C_{X'/Y'} \hookrightarrow N$.
- The second map is the (usual) Gysin homomorphism induced by the zero-section embedding $X' \hookrightarrow N$.

The homomorphism *i*<sup>!</sup> *encodes* [intersection product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_product) in [intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602) in that one either shows the intersection product of *X* and *V* to be given by the formula $X \cdot V = i^![V],$ or takes this formula as a definition.[^n1]

::: {#o9 .example cue=inline-head name="Example"}

**Example**: Given a vector bundle *E*, let *s*: *X* → *E* be a section of *E*. Then, when *s* is a [regular section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_section), $s^{!}[X]$ is the class of the zero-locus of *s*, where \[*X*\] is the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) of *X*.[^n2]

:::
