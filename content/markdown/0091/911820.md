# Normal modal logic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Normal modal logic"}

In [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), a **normal [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365)** is a set *L* of modal formulas such that *L* contains:

:::

- All propositional [tautologies](wikipedia://en/4495335);
- All instances of the [Kripke](wikipedia://en/911960) schema: $\Box(A\to B)\to(\Box A\to\Box B)$

and it is closed under:

- Detachment rule (*[modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900)*): $A\to B, A \in L$ implies $B \in L$;
- Necessitation rule: $A \in L$ implies $\Box A \in L$.

The smallest logic satisfying the above conditions is called **K**. Most modal logics commonly used nowadays (in terms of having philosophical motivations), e.g. [C. I. Lewis](wikipedia://en/433707)'s S4 and [S5](wikipedia://en/12708106), are normal (and hence are extensions of **K**). However a number of [deontic](wikipedia://en/1343980) and [epistemic logics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemic_logic), for example, are non-normal, often because they give up the Kripke schema.

Every normal modal logic is [regular](wikipedia://en/14764284) and hence [classical](wikipedia://en/14763077).

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="S4 (logic)"}
