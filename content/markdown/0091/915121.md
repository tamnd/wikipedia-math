# Appeal to probability

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Appeal to probability"}

An **appeal to probability** (or **appeal to possibility**, also known as *possibiliter ergo probabiliter*, "possibly, therefore probably") is the [logical fallacy](wikipedia://en/3637937) of taking something for granted because it is possibly the case.[^n1][^n2] The fact that an event is possible does not imply that the event is probable, nor that the event was realized.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

A fallacious appeal to possibility:

If it *can* happen *(premise)*.

It *will* happen. *(invalid conclusion)*

Something can go wrong *(premise)*.

Therefore, something will go wrong *(invalid conclusion)*.

If I do not bring my umbrella *(premise)*

It will rain. *(invalid conclusion)*.

[Murphy's law](wikipedia://en/47635) is a (typically deliberate, [tongue-in-cheek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongue-in-cheek)) invocation of the fallacy.

:::
