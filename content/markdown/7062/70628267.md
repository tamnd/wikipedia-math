# Kahn–Kalai conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kahn–Kalai conjecture"}

The **Kahn–Kalai conjecture**, also known as the **expectation threshold conjecture** or more recently the **Park-Pham Theorem**, was a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) in the field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) and [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), proposed by [Jeff Kahn](wikipedia://en/26919476) and [Gil Kalai](wikipedia://en/19709056) in 2006.[^o2][^o3] It was proven in a paper published in 2024.[^o8]

:::

## Background {#Background}

This conjecture concerns the general problem of estimating when [phase transitions](wikipedia://en/54423) occur in systems.[^o2] For example, in a [random network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_network) with $N$ nodes, where each edge is included with probability $p$, it is unlikely for the graph to contain a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle) if $p$ is less than a threshold value $(\log N)/N$, but highly likely if $p$ exceeds that threshold.[^o7]

Threshold values are often difficult to calculate, but a lower bound for the threshold, the "expectation threshold", is generally easier to calculate.[^o2] The Kahn–Kalai conjecture is that the two values are generally close together in a precisely defined way, namely that there is a [universal constant](wikipedia://en/48779551) $K$ for which the ratio between the two is less than $K \log{l(\mathcal{F})}$ where $l(\mathcal{F})$ is the size of a largest [minimal element](wikipedia://en/303398) of an increasing family $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of a power set.[^o8]

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

[Jinyoung Park](wikipedia://en/70679437) and Huy Tuan Pham announced a proof of the conjecture in 2022; it was published in 2024.[^o7][^o8]

:::
