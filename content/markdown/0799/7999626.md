# Gårding's inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gårding's inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Gårding's inequality** is a result that gives a lower bound for the [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) induced by a real [linear elliptic partial differential operator](wikipedia://en/550137). The inequality is named after [Lars Gårding](wikipedia://en/8000370).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Statement of the inequality"}

## Statement of the inequality {#Statement_of_the_inequality}

Let $\Omega$ be a [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258), [open domain](wikipedia://en/39358) in $n$-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) and let $H^k(\Omega)$ denote the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) of $k$-times weakly differentiable functions $u\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ with weak derivatives in $L^2(\Omega)$. Assume that $\Omega$ satisfies the $k$-extension property, i.e., that there exists a [bounded linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) $E\colon H^k(\Omega)\rightarrow H^k(\mathbb{R}^n)$ such that $Eu\vert_\Omega=u$ for all $u\in H^k(\Omega)$.

Let *L* be a linear partial differential operator of even order *2k*, written in divergence form

$$(L u)(x) = \sum_{0 \leq | \alpha |, | \beta | \leq k} (-1)^{| \alpha |} \mathrm{D}^{\alpha} \left( A_{\alpha \beta} (x) \mathrm{D}^{\beta} u(x) \right),$$

and suppose that *L* is uniformly elliptic, i.e., there exists a constant *θ* > 0 such that

$$\sum_{| \alpha |, | \beta | = k} \xi^{\alpha} A_{\alpha \beta} (x) \xi^{\beta} > \theta | \xi |^{2 k} \mbox{ for all } x \in \Omega, \xi \in \mathbb{R}^{n} \setminus \{ 0 \}.$$

Finally, suppose that the coefficients *A<sub>αβ</sub>* are [bounded](wikipedia://en/311509), [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) on the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of Ω for |*α*| = |*β*| = *k* and that

$$A_{\alpha \beta} \in L^{\infty} (\Omega) \mbox{ for all } | \alpha |, | \beta | \leq k.$$

Then **Gårding's inequality** holds: there exist constants *C* > 0 and *G* ≥ 0

$$B[u, u] + G \| u \|_{L^{2} (\Omega)}^{2} \geq C \| u \|_{H^{k} (\Omega)}^{2} \mbox{ for all } u \in H_{0}^{k} (\Omega),$$

where

$$B[v, u] = \sum_{0 \leq | \alpha |, | \beta | \leq k} \int_{\Omega} A_{\alpha \beta} (x) \mathrm{D}^{\alpha} u(x) \mathrm{D}^{\beta} v(x) \, \mathrm{d} x$$

is the bilinear form associated to the operator *L*.

:::

## Application: the Laplace operator and the Poisson problem {#Application:_the_Laplace_operator_and_the_Poisson_problem}

**Be careful, in this application, Garding's Inequality seems useless here as the final result is a direct consequence of Poincaré's Inequality, or Friedrich Inequality. (See talk on the article).**

As a simple example, consider the [Laplace operator](wikipedia://en/174706) Δ. More specifically, suppose that one wishes to solve, for *f* ∈ *L*<sup>2</sup>(Ω) the [Poisson equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_equation)

$$\begin{cases} - \Delta u(x) = f(x), & x \in \Omega; \\ u(x) = 0, & x \in \partial \Omega; \end{cases}$$

where Ω is a bounded [Lipschitz domain](wikipedia://en/7820612) in **R**<sup>*n*</sup>. The corresponding weak form of the problem is to find *u* in the Sobolev space *H*<sub>0</sub><sup>1</sup>(Ω) such that

$$B[u, v] = \langle f, v \rangle \mbox{ for all } v \in H_{0}^{1} (\Omega),$$

where

$$B[u, v] = \int_{\Omega} \nabla u(x) \cdot \nabla v(x) \, \mathrm{d} x,$$

$$\langle f, v \rangle = \int_{\Omega} f(x) v(x) \, \mathrm{d} x.$$

The [Lax–Milgram lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lax–Milgram_lemma) ensures that if the bilinear form *B* is both continuous and elliptic with respect to the norm on *H*<sub>0</sub><sup>1</sup>(Ω), then, for each *f* ∈ *L*<sup>2</sup>(Ω), a unique solution *u* must exist in *H*<sub>0</sub><sup>1</sup>(Ω). The hypotheses of Gårding's inequality are easy to verify for the Laplace operator Δ, so there exist constants *C* and *G* ≥ 0

$$B[u, u] \geq C \| u \|_{H^{1} (\Omega)}^{2} - G \| u \|_{L^{2} (\Omega)}^{2} \mbox{ for all } u \in H_{0}^{1} (\Omega).$$

Applying the [Poincaré inequality](wikipedia://en/4722074) allows the two terms on the right-hand side to be combined, yielding a new constant *K* > 0 with

$$B[u, u] \geq K \| u \|_{H^{1} (\Omega)}^{2} \mbox{ for all } u \in H_{0}^{1} (\Omega),$$

which is precisely the statement that *B* is elliptic. The continuity of *B* is even easier to see: simply apply the [Cauchy–Schwarz inequality](wikipedia://en/38128) and the fact that the Sobolev norm is controlled by the *L*<sup>2</sup> norm of the gradient.
