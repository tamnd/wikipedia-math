# Jacobian ideal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacobian ideal"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Jacobian ideal** or **gradient ideal** is the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) generated by the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) of a function or [function germ](wikipedia://en/352714). Let $\mathcal{O}(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ denote the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of [smooth functions](wikipedia://en/1408000) in $n$ variables and $f$ a function in the ring. The Jacobian ideal of $f$ is

:::

$$J_f := \left\langle \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}, \ldots, \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n} \right\rangle.$$

## Relation to deformation theory {#Relation_to_deformation_theory}

In [deformation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory), the deformations of a [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) given by a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) $f$ is classified by the ring

$$\frac{\mathbb{C}[x_1, \ldots, x_n]}{(f) + J_f}.$$

This is shown using the [Kodaira–Spencer map](wikipedia://en/35205007).

## Relation to Hodge theory {#Relation_to_Hodge_theory}

In [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590), there are objects called real [Hodge structures](wikipedia://en/10297305) which are the data of a real vector space $H_\mathbb{R}$ and an increasing [filtration](wikipedia://en/726014) $F^\bullet$ of $H_\mathbb{C} = H_\mathbb{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}$ satisfying a list of compatibility structures. For a smooth projective variety $X$ there is a canonical Hodge structure.

### Statement for degree d hypersurfaces {#Statement_for_degree_d_hypersurfaces}

In the special case $X$ is defined by a homogeneous degree $d$ polynomial $f \in \Gamma(\mathbb{P}^{n+1},\mathcal{O}(d))$ this Hodge structure can be understood completely from the Jacobian ideal. For its graded-pieces, this is given by the map[^o5]

$$\mathbb{C}[Z_0,\ldots, Z_n]^{(d(n-1+p) - (n+2))} \to \frac{F^pH^n(X,\mathbb{C})}{F^{p+1}H^n(X,\mathbb{C})}$$

which is surjective on the primitive cohomology, denoted $\text{Prim}^{p,n-p}(X)$ and has the kernel $J_f$. Note the primitive cohomology classes are the classes of $X$ which do not come from $\mathbb{P}^{n+1}$, which is just the Lefschetz class $[L]^n = c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))^d$.

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Sketch of proof"}

### Sketch of proof {#Sketch_of_proof}

#### Reduction to residue map {#Reduction_to_residue_map}

For $X \subset \mathbb{P}^{n+1}$ there is an associated short [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) of complexes

$$0 \to \Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}^\bullet \to \Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}^\bullet(\log X) \xrightarrow{res} \Omega_X^\bullet[-1] \to 0$$

where the middle complex is the complex of sheaves of [logarithmic forms](wikipedia://en/1052096) and the right-hand map is the [residue map](wikipedia://en/7062375). This has an associated long exact sequence in cohomology. From the [Lefschetz hyperplane theorem](wikipedia://en/3459657) there is only one interesting cohomology group of $X$, which is $H^n(X;\mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{H}^n(X;\Omega_X^\bullet)$. From the long exact sequence of this short exact sequence, there the induced residue map

$$\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{n+1}, \Omega^\bullet_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(\log X)\right) \to \mathbb{H}^{n+1}(\mathbb{P}^{n+1},\Omega^\bullet_X[-1])$$

where the right hand side is equal to $\mathbb{H}^{n}(\mathbb{P}^{n+1},\Omega^\bullet_X)$, which is isomorphic to $\mathbb{H}^n(X;\Omega_X^\bullet)$. Also, there is an isomorphism

$$H^{n+1}_{dR}(\mathbb{P}^{n+1}-X) \cong \mathbb{H}^{n+1}\left(\mathbb{P}^{n+1};\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}^\bullet(\log X)\right)$$

Through these isomorphisms there is an induced residue map

$$res: H^{n+1}_{dR}(\mathbb{P}^{n+1}-X) \to H^n(X;\mathbb{C})$$

which is injective, and surjective on primitive cohomology. Also, there is the Hodge decomposition

$$H^{n+1}_{dR}(\mathbb{P}^{n+1}-X) \cong \bigoplus_{p+q = n+1}H^q(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}}^p(\log X))$$

and $H^q(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}}^p(\log X)) \cong \text{Prim}^{p-1,q}(X)$.

#### Computation of de Rham cohomology group {#Computation_of_de_Rham_cohomology_group}

In turns out the [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) group $H^{n+1}_{dR}(\mathbb{P}^{n+1}-X)$ is much more tractable and has an explicit description in terms of polynomials. The $F^p$ part is spanned by the meromorphic forms having poles of order $\leq n - p + 1$ which surjects onto the $F^p$ part of $\text{Prim}^n(X)$. This comes from the reduction isomorphism

$$F^{p+1}H^{n+1}_{dR}(\mathbb{P}^{n+1}-X;\mathbb{C}) \cong \frac{ \Gamma(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(n-p+1)) }{ d\Gamma(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}(n-p)) }$$

Using the canonical $(n+1)$-form

$$\Omega = \sum_{j=0}^n (-1)^j Z_j dZ_0\wedge \cdots \wedge \hat{dZ_j}\wedge \cdots \wedge dZ_{n+1}$$

on $\mathbb{P}^{n+1}$ where the $\hat{dZ_j}$ denotes the deletion from the index, these meromorphic differential forms look like

$$\frac{A}{f^{n-p+1}}\Omega$$

where

$$\begin{aligned} \text{deg}(A) &= (n-p+1)\cdot\text{deg}(f) - \text{deg}(\Omega) \\ &= (n-p+1)\cdot d - (n + 2) \\ &= d(n-p+1) - (n+2) \end{aligned}$$

Finally, it turns out the kernel[^o5] <sup>Lemma 8.11</sup> is of all polynomials of the form $A' + fB$ where $A' \in J_f$. Note the Euler identity

$$f = \sum Z_j \frac{\partial f}{\partial Z_j}$$

shows $f \in J_f$.
