# Eguchi–Hanson space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eguchi–Hanson space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), the **Eguchi–Hanson space** is a non-compact, [self-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual), asymptotically locally Euclidean (ALE) metric on the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) of the 2-sphere *T*<sup>\*</sup>*S*<sup>2</sup>. The [holonomy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holonomy_group) of this 4-real-dimensional [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) is SU(2). The metric is generally attributed to the physicists [Tohru Eguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohru_Eguchi) and [Andrew J. Hanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_J._Hanson); it was discovered independently by the mathematician [Eugenio Calabi](wikipedia://en/621000) around the same time in 1979.[^o2][^o3]

:::

The Eguchi-Hanson metric has [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor) equal to zero, making it a solution to the vacuum [Einstein equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein's_equations) of general relativity, albeit with Riemannian rather than Lorentzian [metric signature](wikipedia://en/463225). It may be regarded as a [resolution](wikipedia://en/9914115) of the *A*<sub>1</sub> singularity according to the [ADE classification](wikipedia://en/648042) which is the singularity at the fixed point of the *C*<sup>2</sup>/*Z*<sub>2</sub> [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) where the *Z*<sub>2</sub> group inverts the signs of both complex coordinates in *C*<sup>2</sup>. The even dimensional space *C*<sup>d/2</sup>/*Z*<sub>d/2</sub> of (real-)dimension $d$ can be described using complex coordinates $w_i \in \mathbb C^{d/2}$ with a metric

$$g_{i \bar j} = \bigg(1+\frac{\rho^d}{r^{d}}\bigg)^{2/d}\bigg[\delta_{i\bar j}-\frac{\rho^d \bar w_i w_{\bar j}}{r^2(\rho^d+r^{d})}\bigg],$$

where $\rho$ is a scale setting constant and $r^2 = |w|^2_{\mathbb C^{d/2}}$.

Aside from its inherent importance in pure [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the space is important in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305). Certain types of [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116) can be approximated as a combination of several Eguchi–Hanson metrics since both have the same holonomy group. Similarly, the space can also be used to construct [Calabi–Yau manifolds](wikipedia://en/188932) by replacing the orbifold singularities of $T^6/\mathbb Z_3$ with Eguchi–Hanson spaces.[^o4]

The Eguchi–Hanson metric is the prototypical example of a [gravitational instanton](wikipedia://en/2618270); detailed expressions for the metric are given in that article. It is then an example of a [hyperkähler manifold](wikipedia://en/646120).[^o3]
