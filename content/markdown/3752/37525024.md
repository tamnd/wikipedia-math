# Critical exponent of a word

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Critical exponent of a word"}

In mathematics and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), the **critical exponent** of a finite or infinite sequence of symbols over a finite alphabet describes the largest number of times a contiguous [subsequence](wikipedia://en/236098) can be repeated. For example, the critical exponent of "Mississippi" is 7/3, as it contains the string "ississi", which is of length 7 and period 3.

:::

If *w* is an infinite word over the alphabet *A* and *x* is a finite word over *A*, then *x* is said to occur in *w* with exponent α, for positive real α, if there is a factor *y* of *w* with *y* = *x*<sup>*a*</sup>*x*<sub>0</sub> where *x*<sub>0</sub> is a prefix of *x*, *a* is the integer part of α, and the length |*y*| = α |*x*|: we say that *y* is an *α-power*. The word *w* is *α-power-free* if it contains no factors which are β-powers for any β ≥ α.[^o2]

The *critical exponent* for *w* is the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) of the α for which *w* has α-powers,[^o3] or equivalently the [infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum) of the α for which *w* is α-power-free.[^o13]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If $\mathbf{w}$ is a word (possibly infinite), then the *critical exponent* of $\mathbf{w}$ is defined to be

$$E(\mathbf{w}) = \sup \{ r \in \mathbb{Q}^{\geq 1} : \mathbf{w} \, \text{ contains an } \, r \text{-power}\}$$

where $\mathbb{Q}^{\geq 1} = \{ q \in \mathbb{Q} : q \geq 1 \}$.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The critical exponent of the [Fibonacci word](wikipedia://en/1702139) is (5 + $\sqrt{5}$)/2 ≈ 3.618.[^o13][^o9]
- The critical exponent of the [Thue–Morse sequence](wikipedia://en/491903) is 2.[^o13] The word contains arbitrarily long squares, but in any factor *xxb* the letter *b* is not a prefix of *x*.

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The critical exponent can take any real value greater than 1.[^o13][^n1]
- The critical exponent of a [morphic word](wikipedia://en/37432570) over a finite alphabet is either infinite or an [algebraic number](wikipedia://en/1158) of degree at most the size of the alphabet.[^o13]

:::

::: {#o15 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Repetition threshold"}

## Repetition threshold {#Repetition_threshold}

The **repetition threshold** of an alphabet *A* of *n* letters is the minimum critical exponent of infinite words over *A*: clearly this value RT(*n*) depends only on *n*. For *n*=2, any binary word of length four has a factor of exponent 2, and since the critical exponent of the Thue–Morse sequence is 2, the repetition threshold for binary alphabets is RT(2) = 2. It is known that RT(3) = 7/4, RT(4) = 7/5 and that RT(*n*) = *n*/(*n*-1) for *n* ≥ 5. [^o3][^o9]

:::
