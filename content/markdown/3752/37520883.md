# Left and right (algebra)

::: {#o1 .table cue=table}

| *s a* *s b* *s c* *s d* *s e* *s f* *s g* … | *a t* *b t* *c t* *d t* *e t* *f t* *g t* … |
|---|---|
| Left multiplication by $s$ and right multiplication by $t$. An abstract notation without any specific sense. |  |

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Left and right"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the terms **left** and **right** denote the order of a [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) (usually, but not always, called "[multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845)") in non-[commutative](wikipedia://en/294390) [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364). A binary operation ∗ is usually written [in the infix form](wikipedia://en/199014):

:::

*s* ∗ *t*

The [argument](wikipedia://en/32842052) $s$ is placed on the left side, and the argument $t$ is on the right side. Even if the symbol of the operation is omitted, the order of $s$ and $t$ does matter (unless ∗ is commutative).

A **two-sided** property is fulfilled on both sides. A **one-sided** property is related to one (unspecified) of two sides.

Although the terms are similar, left–right distinction in algebraic parlance is not related either to [left and right limits](wikipedia://en/379840) in [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), or to [left and right in geometry](wikipedia://en/2544098).

## Binary operation as an operator {#Binary_operation_as_an_operator}

A binary operation ∗ may be considered as a [family](wikipedia://en/19653337) of [unary](wikipedia://en/161006) [operators](wikipedia://en/22213) through [currying](wikipedia://en/6600):

*R*<sub>*t*</sub>(*s*) = *s* ∗ *t*,

depending on $t$ as a parameter – this is the family of *right* operations. Similarly,

*L*<sub>*s*</sub>(*t*) = *s* ∗ *t*

defines the family of *left* operations parametrized with $s$.

If for some $e$, the left operation $L_{e}$ is the [identity operation](wikipedia://en/15069), then $e$ is called a left [identity](wikipedia://en/14962). Similarly, if $R_{e} = \operatorname{id}$, then $e$ is a right identity.

In [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) which is [invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_set) under *any* left multiplication in a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) is called a left [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977). Similarly, a right multiplication-invariant subring is a right ideal.

## Left and right modules {#Left_and_right_modules}

Over [non-commutative rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative_ring), the left–right distinction is applied to [modules](wikipedia://en/276410), namely to specify the side where a scalar (module element) appears in the [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373).

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

| Left module | Right module |
|---|---|
| *s*(**x** + **y**) = *s***x** + *s***y** (*s*<sub>1</sub> + *s*<sub>2</sub>)**x** = *s*<sub>1</sub>**x** + *s*<sub>2</sub>**x** *s*(*t***x**) = (*s t*)**x** | (**x** + **y**)*t* = **x***t* + **y***t* **x**(*t*<sub>1</sub> + *t*<sub>2</sub>) = **x***t*<sub>1</sub> + **x***t*<sub>2</sub> (**x***s*)*t* = **x**(*s t*) |

:::

The distinction is not purely syntactical because one gets two different associativity rules (the lowest row in the table) which link multiplication in a module with multiplication in a ring.

A [bimodule](wikipedia://en/492612) is simultaneously a left and right module, with two *different* scalar multiplication operations, obeying an associativity condition on them.

## In category theory {#In_category_theory}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) the usage of "left" and "right" has some algebraic resemblance, but refers to left and right sides of [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891). See [adjoint functors](wikipedia://en/53991).
