::: {#o8 .subject cue=lead name="Icosahedral honeycomb"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **icosahedral honeycomb** is one of four compact, [regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regular_polytopes_and_compounds), [space-filling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-filling_geometry) [tessellations](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycombs](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in [hyperbolic 3-space](wikipedia://en/318742). With [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\{3,5,3\},$ there are three [icosahedra](wikipedia://en/14968) around each [edge](wikipedia://en/12158034), and 12 icosahedra around each [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417), in a regular [dodecahedral](wikipedia://en/7149361) [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719). It is analogous to the [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485) and the [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821).

:::

## Description {#Description}

The [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) of a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968) is around 138.2°, so it is impossible to fit three icosahedra around an edge in Euclidean 3-space. However, in hyperbolic space, properly scaled icosahedra can have dihedral angles of exactly 120 degrees, so three of those can fit around an edge.

The **partially diminished icosahedral honeycomb** or **[parabidiminished icosahedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabidiminished_icosahedron) honeycomb**, pd{3,5,3}, is a non-Wythoffian uniform honeycomb with [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361) and [pentagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860091) cells, with a tetrahedrally diminished dodecahedron vertex figure. The icosahedral cells of the {3,5,3} are [diminished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminishment_%28geometry%29) at opposite vertices (parabidiminished), leaving a [pentagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860091) ([parabidiminished icosahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabidiminished_icosahedron)) core, and creating new dodecahedron cells above and below.[^o81][^o82]
