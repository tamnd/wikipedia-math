# Fréchet manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fréchet manifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in particular in [nonlinear analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_analysis), a **Fréchet manifold** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) modeled on a [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801) in much the same way as a [manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_%28mathematics%29) is modeled on a [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697).

:::

More precisely, a Fréchet manifold consists of a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) $X$ with an atlas of coordinate charts over Fréchet spaces whose transitions are [smooth mappings](wikipedia://en/5480302). Thus $X$ has an [open cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover) $\left\{ U_{\alpha} \right\}_{\alpha \in I},$ and a collection of [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) $\phi_{\alpha} : U_{\alpha} \to F_{\alpha}$ onto their images, where $F_{\alpha}$ are [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801), such that

$$\phi_{\alpha\beta} := \phi_\alpha \circ \phi_\beta^{-1}|_{\phi_\beta\left(U_\beta\cap U_\alpha\right)}$$

is smooth for all pairs of indices $\alpha, \beta.$

## Classification up to homeomorphism {#Classification_up_to_homeomorphism}

It is by no means true that a finite-dimensional manifold of dimension $n$ is globally homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$ or even an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n.$ However, in an infinite-dimensional setting, it is possible to classify "[well-behaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-behaved)" Fréchet manifolds up to homeomorphism quite nicely. A 1969 theorem of David Henderson states that every infinite-dimensional, [separable](wikipedia://en/27855), [metric](wikipedia://en/20018) Fréchet manifold $X$ can be [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) as an open subset of the infinite-dimensional, separable [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932), $H$ (up to linear isomorphism, there is only one such space).

The embedding homeomorphism can be used as a global chart for $X.$ Thus, in the infinite-dimensional, separable, metric case, up to homeomorphism, the "only" topological Fréchet manifolds are the open subsets of the separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. But in the case of differentiable or smooth Fréchet manifolds (up to the appropriate notion of diffeomorphism) this fails.
