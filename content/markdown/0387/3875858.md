# Brauer algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brauer algebra"}

In mathematics, a **Brauer algebra** is an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) introduced by [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134)[^o19] in the context of the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954). It plays the same role that the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) does for the representation theory of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) in [Schur–Weyl duality](wikipedia://en/15291970).

:::

## Structure {#Structure}

The Brauer algebra $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$ is a $\mathbb{Z}[\delta]$-algebra depending on the choice of a positive integer $n$. Here $\delta$ is an indeterminate, but in practice $\delta$ is often specialised to the dimension of the [fundamental representation](wikipedia://en/649743) of an [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) $O(\delta)$. The Brauer algebra has the dimension

$$\dim\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta) = \frac{(2n)!}{2^n n!} = (2n-1)!! = (2n-1)(2n-3)\cdots 5\cdot 3\cdot 1$$

### Diagrammatic definition {#Diagrammatic_definition}

*Image: Brauer algebra.jpg.* The product of 2 basis elements *A* and *B* of the Brauer algebra with *n* = 12

A basis of $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$ consists of all pairings on a set of $2n$ elements $X_1, ..., X_n, Y_1, ..., Y_n$ (that is, all [perfect matchings](wikipedia://en/44031) of a [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_{2n}$: any two of the $2n$ elements may be matched to each other, regardless of their symbols). The elements $X_i$ are usually written in a row, with the elements $Y_i$ beneath them.

The product of two basis elements $A$ and $B$ is obtained by concatenation: first identifying the endpoints in the bottom row of $A$ and the top row of $B$ (Figure *AB* in the diagram), then deleting the endpoints in the middle row and joining endpoints in the remaining two rows if they are joined, directly or by a path, in *AB* (Figure *AB=nn* in the diagram). Thereby all closed loops in the middle of *AB* are removed. The product $A\cdot B$ of the basis elements is then defined to be the basis element corresponding to the new pairing multiplied by $\delta^r$ where $r$ is the number of deleted loops. In the example $A\cdot B = \delta^{2} AB$.

### Generators and relations {#Generators_and_relations}

$\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$ can also be defined as the $\mathbb{Z}[\delta]$-algebra with generators $s_1,\ldots,s_{n-1}, e_1, \ldots, e_{n-1}$ satisfying the following relations:

- Relations of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901):

$$s_i^2 = 1$$

$s_i s_j = s_j s_i$ whenever $|i-j|>1$

$$s_i s_{i+1} s_i = s_{i+1} s_i s_{i+1}$$

- Almost-[idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent) relation:

$$e_i^2 = \delta e_i$$

- Commutation:

$$e_i e_j = e_j e_i$$

$$s_i e_j = e_j s_i$$

whenever$|i-j|>1$

- Tangle relations

$$e_i e_{i\pm 1} e_i = e_i$$

$$s_i s_{i\pm 1} e_i = e_{i\pm 1} e_i$$

$$e_i s_{i\pm 1} s_i = e_i e_{i\pm 1}$$

- Untwisting:

$s_i e_i = e_i s_i = e_i$:

$$e_i s_{i\pm 1} e_i = e_i$$

In this presentation $s_i$ represents the diagram in which $X_k$ is always connected to $Y_k$ directly beneath it except for $X_i$ and $X_{i+1}$ which are connected to $Y_{i+1}$ and $Y_i$ respectively. Similarly $e_i$ represents the diagram in which $X_k$ is always connected to $Y_k$ directly beneath it except for $X_i$ being connected to $X_{i+1}$ and $Y_i$ to $Y_{i+1}$.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Basic properties"}

### Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

The Brauer algebra is a [subalgebra](wikipedia://en/45239) of the [partition algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_algebra).

The Brauer algebra $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$ is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/3876151) if $\delta\in\mathbb{C}-\{0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots,\pm n\}$.[^o16][^o20]

The subalgebra of $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$ generated by the generators $s_i$ is the [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) of the symmetric group $S_n$.

The subalgebra of $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$ generated by the generators $e_i$ is the [Temperley-Lieb algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperley-Lieb_algebra) $TL_n(\delta)$.[^o17]

The Brauer algebra is a [cellular algebra](wikipedia://en/31504702).

For a pairing $A$ let $n(A)$ be the number of closed loops formed by identifying $X_i$ with $Y_i$ for any $i=1,2,\dots,n$: then the **Jones trace** $\text{Tr}(A) = \delta^{n(A)}$ obeys $\text{Tr}(AB)=\text{Tr}(BA)$ i.e. it is indeed a [trace](wikipedia://en/43270#Generalizations).

:::

## Representations {#Representations}

### Brauer-Specht modules {#Brauer-Specht_modules}

Brauer-Specht modules are finite-dimensional modules of the Brauer algebra. If $\delta$ is such that $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$ is semisimple, they form a complete set of simple modules of $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$.[^o17] These modules are parametrized by [partitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_%28number_theory%29), because they are built from the [Specht modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specht_modules) of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901), which are themselves parametrized by partitions.

For $0\leq \ell \leq n$ with $\ell\equiv n\bmod 2$, let $B_{n,\ell}$ be the set of perfect matchings of $n+\ell$ elements $X_1,\dots ,X_n,Y_1,\dots ,Y_\ell$, such that $Y_j$ is matched with one of the $n$ elements $X_1,\dots ,X_n$. For any ring $k$, the space $kB_{n,\ell}$ is a left $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$-module, where basis elements of $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$ act by graph concatenation. (This action can produce matchings that violate the restriction that $Y_1,\dots ,Y_\ell$ cannot match with one another: such graphs must be modded out.) Moreover, the space $kB_{n,\ell}$ is a right $S_\ell$-module.[^o18]

Given a [Specht module](wikipedia://en/5122533) $V_\lambda$ of $kS_\ell$, where $\lambda$ is a partition of $\ell$ (i.e. $|\lambda|=\ell$), the corresponding **Brauer-Specht module** of $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$ is

$$W_{\lambda} = kB_{n,|\lambda|} \otimes_{kS_{|\lambda|}} V_\lambda \qquad \big(|\lambda|\leq n, |\lambda|\equiv n\bmod 2\big)$$

A basis of this module is the set of elements $b\otimes v$, where $b\in B_{n,|\lambda|}$ is such that the $|\lambda|$ lines that end on elements $Y_j$ do not cross, and $v$ belongs to a basis of $V_\lambda$.[^o18] The dimension is

$$\dim(W_\lambda) = \binom{n}{|\lambda|} (n-|\lambda|-1)!! \dim(V_\lambda)$$

i.e. the product of a [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668), a [double factorial](wikipedia://en/507208), and the dimension of the corresponding Specht module, which is given by the [hook length formula](wikipedia://en/41497810).

### Schur-Weyl duality {#Schur-Weyl_duality}

Let $V=\mathbb{R}^d$ be a Euclidean [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of dimension $d$, and $O(V)=O(d,\mathbb{R})$ the corresponding orthogonal group. Then write $B_n(d)$ for the specialisation $\mathbb{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\delta]}\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$ where $\delta$ acts on $\mathbb{R}$ by multiplication with $d$. The [tensor power](wikipedia://en/30844) $V^{\otimes n} := \underbrace{V\otimes\cdots\otimes V}_{n\text{ times}}$ is naturally a $B_n(d)$-[module](wikipedia://en/276410): $s_i$ acts by switching the $i$th and $(i+1)$th tensor factor and $e_i$ acts by contraction followed by expansion in the $i$th and $(i+1)$th tensor factor, i.e. $e_i$ acts as

$$v_1\otimes \cdots\otimes v_{i-1}\otimes\Big(v_i\otimes v_{i+1}\Big)\otimes\cdots \otimes v_n \mapsto v_1\otimes \cdots\otimes v_{i-1}\otimes\left(\langle v_i, v_{i+1}\rangle \sum_{k=1}^d (w_k\otimes w_k)\right)\otimes\cdots \otimes v_n$$

where $w_1,\ldots,w_d$ is any [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) of $V$. (The sum is in fact independent of the choice of this basis.)

This action is useful in a generalisation of the [Schur-Weyl duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur-Weyl_duality): if $d\geq n$, the image of $B_n(d)$ inside $\operatorname{End}(V^{\otimes n})$ is the centraliser of $O(V)$ inside $\operatorname{End}(V^{\otimes n})$, and conversely the image of $O(V)$ is the centraliser of $B_n(d)$.[^o16] The tensor power $V^{\otimes n}$ is therefore both an $O(V)$- and a $B_n(d)$-module and satisfies

$$V^{\otimes n} = \bigoplus_{\lambda} U_\lambda \boxtimes W_\lambda$$

where $\lambda$ runs over a subset of the [partitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_%28number_theory%29) such that $|\lambda|\leq n$ and $|\lambda|\equiv n \bmod 2$, $U_\lambda$ is an irreducible $O(V)$-module, and $W_\lambda$ is a Brauer-Specht module of $B_n(d)$.

It follows that the Brauer algebra has a natural action on the space of polynomials on $V^n$, which commutes with the action of the orthogonal group.

If $\delta$ is a negative even [integer](wikipedia://en/14563), the Brauer algebra is related by Schur-Weyl duality to the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) $\text{Sp}_{-\delta}(\mathbb{C})$, rather than the orthogonal group.

:::

## Walled Brauer algebra {#Walled_Brauer_algebra}

The walled Brauer algebra $\mathfrak{B}_{r,s}(\delta)$ is a subalgebra of $\mathfrak{B}_{r+s}(\delta)$. Diagrammatically, it consists of diagrams where the only allowed pairings are of the types $X_{i\leq r}-X_{j>r}$, $Y_{i\leq r}-Y_{j>r}$, $X_{i\leq r}-Y_{j\leq r}$, $X_{i>r} - Y_{j>r}$. This amounts to having a wall that separates $X_{i\leq r},Y_{i\leq r}$ from $X_{i>r},Y_{i>r}$, and requiring that $X-Y$ pairings cross the wall while $X-X,Y-Y$ pairings don't.[^o15]

The walled Brauer algebra is generated by $\{s_i\}_{1\leq i\leq r+s-1,i\neq r} \cup\{e_r\}$. These generators obey the basic relations of $\mathfrak{B}_{r+s}(\delta)$ that involve them, plus the two relations[^o14]

$$e_rs_{r+1}s_{r-1}e_r s_{r-1} = e_rs_{r+1}s_{r-1}e_r s_{r+1} \quad , \quad s_{r-1}e_rs_{r+1}s_{r-1}e_r = s_{r+1}e_rs_{r+1}s_{r-1}e_r$$

(In $\mathfrak{B}_{r+s}(\delta)$, these two relations follow from the basic relations.)

For $\delta$ a natural integer, let $V$ be the natural representation of the general linear group $GL_\delta(\mathbb{C})$. The walled Brauer algebra $\mathfrak{B}_{r,s}(\delta)$ has a natural action on $V^{\otimes r}\otimes (V^*)^{\otimes s}$, which is related by [Schur-Weyl duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur-Weyl_duality) to the action of $GL_\delta(\mathbb{C})$.[^o15]
