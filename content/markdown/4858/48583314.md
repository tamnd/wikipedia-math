# Dorothy Vaughan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dorothy Vaughan"}

**Dorothy Jean Johnson Vaughan** (September 20, 1910 – November 10, 2008) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [human computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_computer), [computer programmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmer) and [schoolteacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher) who worked for the [National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Advisory_Committee_for_Aeronautics) (NACA), and [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA), at [Langley Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langley_Research_Center) in [Hampton, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton,_Virginia). In 1949, she became acting supervisor of the [West Area Computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Area_Computers), the first African-American woman to receive a promotion and supervise a group of staff at the center.

:::

She later was promoted officially to the position of supervisor. During her 28-year career, Vaughan prepared for the introduction of computers in the early 1960s by teaching herself and her staff the [Fortran programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran). She later headed the programming section of the Analysis and Computation Division (ACD) at Langley.

Vaughan is one of the women featured in [Margot Lee Shetterly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Lee_Shetterly)'s history *[Hidden Figures: The Story of the African-American Women Who Helped Win the Space Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Figures_%28book%29)* (2016). It was adapted as a [biographical film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Figures) of the same name, also released in 2016.

In 2019, Vaughan was honored with the [Congressional Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Gold_Medal) posthumously.[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

Vaughan graduated from Wilberforce University in 1929. Although encouraged by professors to do graduate study at [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University),[^o7] Vaughan worked as a mathematics teacher at [Robert Russa Moton High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.R._Moton_High_School) in [Farmville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmville,_Virginia), in order to assist her family during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States).[^n1] During the 14 years of her teaching career, Virginia's public schools and other facilities were still racially segregated under [Jim Crow laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws).[^o6]

In 1935, [Langley Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langley_Research_Center) in [Hampton, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton,_Virginia), established its first female [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_%28occupation%29) pool.[^o7] In 1941, President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) issued [Executive Order 8802](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_8802), to desegregate the defense industry, and [Executive Order 9346](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Employment_Practice_Committee) to end racial segregation and discrimination in hiring and promotion among federal agencies and defense contractors.[^o6] These helped ensure the war effort drew from all of American society after the United States entered [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) in 1941. With the enactment of the two Executive Orders, and with many men being swept into service, federal agencies such as the [National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Advisory_Committee_for_Aeronautics) (NACA) also expanded their hiring and increased recruiting of women, including women of color, to support the war production of airplanes.[^o7] Two years following the issuance of Executive Orders 8802 and 9346, the Langley Memorial Aeronautical Laboratory (Langley Research Center), a facility of the NACA, began hiring more Black women to meet the drastic increase in demand for processing aeronautical research data.[^o8] The US believed that the war was going to be won in the air. It had already ramped up airplane production, creating a great demand for engineers, mathematicians, craftsmen and skilled tradesmen.

In 1943, Vaughan began a 28-year-career as a [human computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_%28occupation%29) and computer programmer at [Langley Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langley_Research_Center) where she came to perform calculations for [flight paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_path), the [Scout Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scout_%28rocket_family%29), and [computer programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programming). Her career in this field kicked off during the height of World War II. She came to the Langley Memorial Aeronautical Laboratory thinking that it would be a temporary war job. One of her children later worked at NACA.[^o9] Vaughan was assigned to the [West Area Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Area_Computers), a segregated unit, which consisted of only African Americans. This was due to prevailing [Jim Crow laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws) that required newly hired African American women to work separately from their white women counterparts.[^o8] They were also required to use separate dining and bathroom facilities.[^o8] This segregated group consisted of African-American women who made complex mathematical calculations by hand, using tools of the time.[^o7][^o10]

Their work expanded in the postwar years to support research and design for the United States' space program, which was emphasized under President [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy). In 1949, Vaughan was assigned as the acting head of the [West Area Computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Area_Computers), taking over from a woman who had died. She was the first Black supervisor at NACA and one of few female supervisors, leading a group composed entirely of African-American women.[^o11] She served for years in an acting role before being promoted officially to the position as supervisor.[^n1]

Vaughan moved into the area of electronic computing in 1961, after NACA introduced the first [digital computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_computer) to the center. Vaughan became proficient in computer programming, teaching herself [FORTRAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran) and teaching it to her coworkers to prepare them for the transition. She contributed to the space program through her work on the [Scout Launch Vehicle Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scout_%28rocket_family%29).[^o10]

Vaughan continued after [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA), the successor agency, was established in 1958. When NACA became NASA, segregated facilities, including the West Computing office, were abolished. In a 1994 interview, Vaughan recalled that working at Langley during the [Space Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Race) felt like being on "the cutting edge of something very exciting".[^n2] Regarding being an African American woman during that time in Langley, she remarked, "I changed what I could, and what I couldn't, I endured." Vaughan worked in the Numerical Techniques division through the 1960s. Dorothy Vaughan and many of the former West Computers joined the new Analysis and Computation Division (ACD), a racially and gender-integrated group on the frontier of electronic computing. She worked at NASA-Langley for 28 years.[^o11]

During her career at Langley, Vaughan was also raising her six children, one of whom later also worked at NASA-Langley. Vaughan lived in [Newport News, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News,_Virginia), and commuted to work at Hampton via public transportation.

## Later years {#Later_years}

*Image: L Dorothy Vaughan M Lessie Hunter R Vivian Adair.jpg.* Vaughan with her [human computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_computer) colleagues [Lessie Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lessie_Hunter), and [Vivian Adair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivian_Adair). [Margaret Ridenhour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Ridenhour) and [Charlotte Craidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Craidon) are standing in the back.

Vaughan wanted to continue at another management position at NASA, but never received an offer.[^o15] She retired from NASA in 1971, at the age of 61. In her final decade of her career, she alongside [Katherine G. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Johnson) and [Mary Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Jackson_%28engineer%29) helped calculate astronauts [John Glenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Glenn)'s launch into orbit,[^o15] Neil Armstrong famous first step on the moon, and even the first American to be in space, Alan Shepard.[^o16] She died on November 10, 2008, aged 98 and was buried at Hampton Memorial Gardens.[^o17] Vaughan was a member of [Alpha Kappa Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Kappa_Alpha), an African-American sorority. She was also an active member of the [African Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Methodist_Episcopal_Church) where she participated in music and missionary activities.[^o18]

At the time of her death, she was survived by four of her six children, ten grandchildren and fourteen great-grandchildren.[^o19]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Vaughan was the first Black female manager at [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA). As one of the first female [FORTRAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran) programmers, she instructed her peers and paved a way for female programmers at NASA.[^o21]

In 2005, a scholarship fund with the Salem Community Foundation was created under Dorothy Vaughan’s name to further music training by the Salem Music Study Club.[^o22]

Vaughan is one of the women featured in [Margot Lee Shetterly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Lee_Shetterly)'s 2016 non-fiction book *[Hidden Figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Figures_%28book%29)*, and the [feature film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Figures). She was portrayed by the [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award) winning actress [Octavia Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavia_Spencer).

The Dorothy J. Vaughan Academy of Technology opened in [Charlotte, NC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte,_North_Carolina), in August 2017. This school is inspired by Vaughan’s “leadership, innovation, creativity, curiosity, and love of learning.” The school is a member of the Magnet Schools of America Association.[^o23]

In 2019, Vaughan was awarded the [Congressional Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Gold_Medal).[^o3] Also in 2019, the [Vaughan crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaughan_%28crater%29) on the far side of the Moon was named in her honor.

On 6 November 2020, a satellite named after her (ÑuSat 12 or "Dorothy", COSPAR 2020-079D of the [ÑuSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ÑuSat) series) was launched into space.[^o24]

Vaughan’s personal Bible and NASA retirement identification card are displayed in the Museum of the Bible’s exhibition *Scripture and Science: Our Universe, Ourselves, Our Place.* [^o25] The [African Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Methodist_Episcopal_Church) also gave her a service award.[^o25]

[North Central University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Central_University) has a scholarship in honor of Dorothy Vaughan for [BIPOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Person_of_color) and/or female students.[^o26]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- 1925: Beechurst High School – Class Valedictorian
- 1925: West Virginia Conference of the A.M.E. Sunday School Convention – Full Tuition Scholarship
- 1929: Wilberforce University – Mathematician Graduate Cum Laude
- 1949–1958: Head of National Advisory Committee of Aeronautics' Segregated West Computing Unit (NACA)
- October 16, 2019: [a lunar crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaughan_%28crater%29) is named after her.[^o28] This name was chosen by [planetary scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_scientist) [Ryan N. Watkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_N._Watkins) and her student, and submitted on what would have been Dorothy Vaughan's 109th birthday.[^o29]
- November 8, 2019: [Congressional Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Gold_Medal)[^o3] which was accepted by family members at the 2024 ceremony[^o30]
- On November 6, 2020, a satellite named after her was launched into space[^o31]
- On July 19, 2024, NASA Johnson Space Center's central data office, known as Building 12, was renamed as the "Dorothy Vaughan Center in Honor of the Women of Apollo" as a "tribute to the people who made humanity's first steps on the Moon possible."[^o32]§
