# Michael Guy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Guy"}

**Michael J. T. Guy** (born 1 April 1943) is a British [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is known for early work on computer systems, such as the [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_%28computer%29) system at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge),[^o3] and for contributions to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805), and the theory of [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra) in higher dimensions. He worked closely with [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807), and is the son of Conway's collaborator [Richard K. Guy](wikipedia://en/816308).

:::

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

With [Conway](wikipedia://en/15807), Guy found the complete solution to the [Soma cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soma_cube) of [Piet Hein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Hein_%28scientist%29).[^o5][^o6] Also with Conway, an enumeration led to the discovery of the [grand antiprism](wikipedia://en/4058047), an unusual [uniform polychoron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polychoron) in four dimensions. The two had met at [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge), where Guy was an undergraduate student from 1960, and Conway was a graduate student. It was through Michael that Conway met Richard Guy, who would become a co-author of works in [combinatorial game theory](wikipedia://en/292231).[^o7] Michael Guy with Conway made numerous particular contributions to geometry, number and game theory, often published in problem selections by Richard Guy. Some of these are [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446), others contributions to [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492).[^o8] They also worked on the [sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806).[^o9]

Guy began work as a research student of [J. W. S. Cassels](wikipedia://en/1709206) at [Department of Pure Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics](wikipedia://en/3254271) (DPMMS), Cambridge.[^o10] He did not complete a Ph.D., but joint work with Cassels produced numerical examples on the [Hasse principle](wikipedia://en/489707) for [cubic surfaces](wikipedia://en/526126).[^o11]

## Computer science {#Computer_science}

He subsequently went into computer science. He worked on the [filing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File_system) for [Titan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_%281963_computer%29), Cambridge's [Atlas 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_%28computer%29),[^o13][^o14] being one of a team of four in one office including [Roger Needham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Needham).[^o15][^o16] In working on [ALGOL 68](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALGOL_68), he was co-author with [Stephen R. Bourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_R._Bourne) of [ALGOL 68C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALGOL_68C).[^o17][^o18]
