# Flag-waving

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Flag-waving"}

**Flag-waving** is a [fallacious argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallacious_argument) or [propaganda technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_technique) used to justify an action based on the undue connection to nationalism or patriotism or benefit for an idea, group or country.[^o2][^o3] It is a variant of [argumentum ad populum](wikipedia://en/30874633).[^o4] This fallacy [appeals to emotion](wikipedia://en/219718) instead to logic of the audience aiming to [manipulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_manipulation) them to win an argument. All ad populum fallacies are based on the presumption that the recipients already have certain beliefs, biases, and prejudices about the issue.[^o5]

:::

If flag-waving is based on connecting to some [symbol](wikipedia://en/37673) of patriotism or nationalism it is a form of appeal to stirring symbols which can be based on undue connection not only to nationalism but also to some religious or cultural symbols—for example, a politician appearing on TV with children, farmer, teacher, together with the "common" man, etc.

The act of flag-waving is a superficial display of support or loyalty to, for example, a [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation) or a [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party).[^o6]
