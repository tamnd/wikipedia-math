# Enid Charles

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Enid Charles"}

**Dorothy** **Enid Charles** (29 December 1894 – 26 March 1972) was a British [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist), [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminist) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who was a pioneer in the fields of [demography](wikipedia://en/45642) and [population statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_statistics).

:::

She was born in [Denbigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denbigh), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales). She obtained a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at [Newnham College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newnham_College,_Cambridge), where she became friends with Dorothy (Dodo) Crowther, sister of the writer [James Crowther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Crowther).[^o2] She gained a Ph.D. in [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology) from the [University of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town), South Africa.[^o3]

Charles met the [conscientious objector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objector) [Lancelot Hogben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancelot_Hogben) while at Cambridge; they married in 1918.[^o4] Out of a dozen or so socialist and feminist couples in Britain in the early 20th century, Charles was the only wife to keep her name.[^o5] The couple, who had two sons and two daughters,[^o6] separated in 1953 and divorced in 1957.[^o7]

Charles worked on [fertility rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) and marriage rates for the [Dominion Bureau of Statistics](wikipedia://en/186760) in [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada). In 1934, Charles projected drastic decline in population of the United Kingdom if fertility rates continued to fall. These results led her to speak out against the then commonly accepted principle of [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics).[^o8][^o9] She subsequently worked as a Regional Adviser in Epidemiology and Health Statistics and as a Population Statistics Consultant for the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization) in [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore) and [New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Delhi).[^o3]

She died in [Torquay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torquay), England, in 1972, aged 77.[^o4]
