# PRESS statistic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="PRESS statistic"}

*Image: LOOCV.gif.* [Leave-one-out cross-validation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leave-one-out_cross-validation): Illustration of fitting a model and finding the PRESS statistic for n=8 observations

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the**predicted residual error sum of squares** (**PRESS**) is a form of [cross-validation](wikipedia://en/416612) used in [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997) to provide a summary measure of the [fit of a model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_fitting) to a sample of observations that were not themselves used to estimate the model. It is calculated as the sum of squares of the prediction residuals for those observations.[^o2][^o3][^o4] Specifically, the PRESS statistic is an exhaustive form of [cross-validation](wikipedia://en/416612), as it tests all the possible ways that the original data can be divided into a training and a validation set.

:::

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Procedure"}

## Procedure {#Procedure}

Instead of *fitting* only one model on all data, [leave-one-out cross-validation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leave-one-out_cross-validation) is used to fit N models (on N observations) where for each model one data point is left out from the [training set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_set). The out-of-sample predicted value is calculated for the omitted observation in each case, and the PRESS statistic is calculated as the sum of the squares of all the resulting prediction errors:[^o7]

$$\operatorname{PRESS} =\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \hat{y}_{i, -i})^2$$

:::

## Usage {#Usage}

Given this procedure, the PRESS statistic can be calculated for a number of candidate model structures for the same dataset, with the lowest values of PRESS indicating the best structures. Models that are over-parameterised ([over-fitted](wikipedia://en/173332)) would tend to give small residuals for observations included in the model-fitting but large residuals for observations that are excluded. The PRESS statistic has been extensively used in [lazy learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazy_learning) and locally linear learning to speed-up the assessment and the selection of the neighbourhood size.[^o9][^o10]
