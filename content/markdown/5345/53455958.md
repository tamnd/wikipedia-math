# Robert B. Davis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert B. Davis"}

**Robert B. Davis** (June 23, 1926 – December 21, 1997) was an American mathematician and [mathematics educator](wikipedia://en/326471).[^o2]

:::

Davis was born in [Fall River, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_River,_Massachusetts).[^o3] He graduated from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) with a B.S, M.S, and Ph.D. (1951) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). He was a professor and researcher at the [University of New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Hampshire), [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University), the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign)[^o4] and [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), where he was named New Jersey Professor of Mathematics Education in 1988.[^o5][^o6] He was one of the founders of the Madison Project, a study of mathematics education which spanned 15 years. The project is named for Madison Junior High School in Syracuse, where it began.[^o7] The project moved to [Webster College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webster_College) near [St, Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis) in 1961.[^o8]

Davis was the founding editor of *The Journal of Mathematical Behavior* (originally *The Journal of Children's Mathematical Behavior*), with [Herbert Ginsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Ginsburg) in 1971.[^o2][^o7][^o5]

Davis was given the Ross Taylor/Glenn Gilbert National Leadership Award posthumously by the National Council of Supervisors of Mathematics in 1998.[^o9]
