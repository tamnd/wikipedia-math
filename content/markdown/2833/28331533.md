# Divisor topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Divisor topology"}

In mathematics, more specifically [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649), the **divisor topology** is a specific [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_%28structure%29) on the set $X = \{2, 3, 4,...\}$ of positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) greater than or equal to two. The divisor topology is the [poset topology](wikipedia://en/5909536) for the [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) relation of [divisibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisibility) of integers on $X$.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The sets $S_n = \{x \in X : x\mathop|n \}$ for $n = 2,3,...$ form a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29) for the **divisor topology**[^o4] on $X$, where the notation $x\mathop|n$ means $x$ is a divisor of $n$.

The open sets in this topology are the [lower sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_set) for the partial order defined by $x\leq y$ if $x\mathop|y$. The closed sets are the [upper sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_set) for this partial order.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

All the properties below are proved in [^o4] or follow directly from the definitions.

- The closure of a point $x\in X$ is the set of all multiples of $x$.
- Given a point $x\in X$, there is a smallest neighborhood of $x$, namely the basic open set $S_x$ of divisors of $x$. So the divisor topology is an [Alexandrov topology](wikipedia://en/542198).
- $X$ is a [T<sub>0</sub> space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T0_space). Indeed, given two points $x$ and $y$ with $x<y$, the open neighborhood $S_x$ of $x$ does not contain $y$.
- $X$ is a not a [T<sub>1</sub> space](wikipedia://en/148721), as no point is [closed](wikipedia://en/33086408). Consequently, $X$ is not [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637).
- The [isolated points](wikipedia://en/390557) of $X$ are the [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666).
- The set of prime numbers is [dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_%28topology%29) in $X$. In fact, every dense open set must include every prime, and therefore $X$ is a [Baire space](wikipedia://en/200550).
- $X$ is [second-countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-countable).
- $X$ is [ultraconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraconnected), since the closures of the singletons $\{x\}$ and $\{y\}$ contain the product $xy$ as a common element.
- Hence $X$ is a [normal space](wikipedia://en/48629). But $X$ is not [completely normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_normal). For example, the singletons $\{6\}$ and $\{4\}$ are [separated sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separated_set) (6 is not a multiple of 4 and 4 is not a multiple of 6), but have no disjoint open neighborhoods, as their smallest respective open neighborhoods meet non-trivially in $S_6\cap S_4=S_2$.
- $X$ is not a [regular space](wikipedia://en/53058), as a basic neighborhood $S_x$ is finite, but the closure of a point is infinite.
- $X$ is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233), [locally connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_connected), [path connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_connected) and [locally path connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_path_connected).
- $X$ is a [scattered space](wikipedia://en/40260318), as each nonempty subset has a first element, which is an isolated element of the set.
- The [compact subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_subset) of $X$ are the finite subsets, since any set $A\subseteq X$ is covered by the collection of all basic open sets $S_n$, which are each finite, and if $A$ is covered by only finitely many of them, it must itself be finite. In particular, $X$ is not [compact](wikipedia://en/6042).
- $X$ is [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) in the sense that each point has a compact neighborhood ($S_x$ is finite). But points don't have closed compact neighborhoods ($X$ is not [locally relatively compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_relatively_compact).)
