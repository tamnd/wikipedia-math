# Correlation sum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Correlation sum"}

In [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295), the **correlation sum** is the estimator of the [correlation integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_integral), which reflects the mean probability that the states at two different times are close:

:::

$$C(\varepsilon) = \frac{1}{N^2} \sum_{\stackrel{i,j=1}{i \neq j}}^N \Theta(\varepsilon - \| \vec{x}(i) - \vec{x}(j)\|), \quad \vec{x}(i) \in \mathbb{R}^m,$$

where $N$ is the number of considered states $\vec{x}(i)$, $\varepsilon$ is a threshold distance, $\| \cdot \|$ a norm (e.g. [Euclidean norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm)) and $\Theta( \cdot )$ the [Heaviside step function](wikipedia://en/87299). If only a [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) is available, the phase space can be reconstructed by using a time delay embedding (see [Takens' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takens'_theorem)):

$$\vec{x}(i) = (u(i), u(i+\tau), \ldots, u(i+\tau(m-1)),$$

where $u(i)$ is the time series, $m$ the embedding dimension and $\tau$ the time delay.

The correlation sum is used to estimate the [correlation dimension](wikipedia://en/2075246).
