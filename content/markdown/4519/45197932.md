# Cramer–Castillon problem

*Image: Cramer castillon problem.svg.* Two solutions whose sides pass through $A, B, C$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cramer–Castillon problem"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Cramer–Castillon problem** is a problem stated by the [Genevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Geneva) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Gabriel Cramer](wikipedia://en/499331) solved by the Italian mathematician, resident in Berlin, [Jean de Castillon](wikipedia://en/26942894) in 1776.[^n1]

:::

The problem is as follows (see the image): given a circle $Z$ and three points $A, B, C$ in the same plane and not on $Z$, to construct every possible triangle inscribed in $Z$ whose sides (or their elongations) pass through $A, B, C$ respectively.

Centuries before, [Pappus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/509300) had solved a special case: when the three points are collinear. But the general case had the reputation of being very difficult.[^n2] After the geometrical construction of Castillon, [Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_Lagrange) found an analytic solution, easier than Castillon's. In the beginning of the 19th century, [Lazare Carnot](wikipedia://en/26532764) generalized it to $n$ points.[^n3]
