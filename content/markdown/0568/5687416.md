# Henry Seely White

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henry Seely White"}

*Image: Henry Seely White.jpg.*

**Henry Seely White** (May 20, 1861 – May 20, 1943) was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was born in [Cazenovia, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cazenovia,_New_York) to parents Aaron White and Isadore Maria Haight. He matriculated at [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University) in [Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut) and graduated with honors in 1882 at the age of twenty-one. White excelled at Wesleyan in astronomy, ethics, Latin, logic, mathematics, and philosophy. At the university, [John Monroe Van Vleck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Monroe_Van_Vleck) taught White mathematics and astronomy. Later, Van Vleck persuaded White to continue to study mathematics at the graduate level. Subsequently, White studied at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) under [Klein](wikipedia://en/41887), and received his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in 1891.

:::

White was Mathematics Department Chair at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University). He left Northwestern to be near his ill mother and became Chairman of the Mathematics Department at [Vassar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_College). He "attributed his interest in geometry both to his work at Wesleyan and Goettingen and to summers spent working on his grandfather's farm."[^o4] His particular interests were in the fields of the geometry of curves and surfaces ([curves](wikipedia://en/89246), [differential geometry of surfaces](wikipedia://en/15513875)), algebraic planes and twisted curves ([algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [algebraic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_curves), [twisted curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twisted_curves)), [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) sets of lines in a plane ([line coordinates](wikipedia://en/21252759)), the [theory of invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_invariants), relativity in mechanics, and correspondences.[^o2][^o4]

White became president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for 1907–1908. In 1915 White was elected a Fellow of the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o2] Northwestern conferred upon him an [LL.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LL.D.) in the same year. At the time of its 100th anniversary in 1932, Wesleyan conferred upon him a [D.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.Sc.)[^o5]
