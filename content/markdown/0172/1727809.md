# Somer–Lucas pseudoprime

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Somer–Lucas pseudoprime"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), an [odd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_number) and [composite number](wikipedia://en/82289) *N* is a **Somer–Lucas *d*-[pseudoprime](wikipedia://en/28787420)** (with given *d* ≥ 1) if there exists a nondegenerate [Lucas sequence](wikipedia://en/734635) $U(P,Q)$ with the discriminant $D=P^2-4Q,$ such that $\gcd(N,D)=1$ and the rank appearance of *N* in the sequence *U*(*P*, *Q*) is

:::

$$\frac{1}{d}\left(N-\left(\frac{D}{N}\right)\right),$$

where $\left(\frac{D}{N}\right)$ is the [Jacobi symbol](wikipedia://en/97313).

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Unlike the standard [Lucas pseudoprimes](wikipedia://en/1055370), there is no known efficient primality test using the Lucas *d*-pseudoprimes. Hence they are not generally used for computation.

:::

## See also {#See_also}

Lawrence Somer, in his 1985 thesis, also defined the [Somer d-pseudoprimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somer_d-pseudoprime). They are described in brief on page 117 of Ribenboim 1996.
