# Doron Zeilberger

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Doron Zeilberger"}

**Doron Zeilberger** (דורון ציילברגר; born 2 July 1950) is an Israeli-American mathematician. He is known for his work in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and writings on [ultrafinitism](wikipedia://en/145018).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

He received his doctorate from the [Weizmann Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute_of_Science) in 1976, under the direction of [Harry Dym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Dym), with the thesis "New Approaches and Results in the Theory of Discrete Analytic Functions."[^o5] He is a Board of Governors Professor of Mathematics at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University).[^o6]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Zeilberger has made contributions to combinatorics, [hypergeometric identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_identities), and [q-series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-series). He gave the first proof of the [alternating sign matrix conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_sign_matrix_conjecture), noteworthy not only for its mathematical content, but also for the fact that Zeilberger recruited nearly a hundred volunteer checkers to "pre-referee" the paper. In 2011, together with [Manuel Kauers](wikipedia://en/40133395) and [Christoph Koutschan](wikipedia://en/48644256), Zeilberger proved the [*q*-TSPP conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-TSPP_conjecture), which was independently stated in 1983 by [George Andrews](wikipedia://en/3356273) and [David P. Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_P._Robbins).[^o8]

Zeilberger is an [ultrafinitist](wikipedia://en/145018).[^o9] He is also known for crediting his computer "Shalosh B. Ekhad" as a co-author[^o10] ("Shalosh" and "Ekhad" mean "Three" and "One" in [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) respectively, referring to his first computer, an [AT&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_AT&T) [3B1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3b1)[^o11]), and for his provocative opinions published on his "Dr. Z's Opinions" page.[^o12][^o13]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Zeilberger received a [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) in 1990.[^o15] Together with [Herbert Wilf](wikipedia://en/3782688), Zeilberger was awarded the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)'s [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Seminal Contributions to Research in 1998 for their development of [WZ theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WZ_theory), which has revolutionized the field of hypergeometric summation. In 2004, Zeilberger was awarded the [Euler Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_Medal); the citation refers to him as "a champion of using computers and algorithms to do mathematics quickly and efficiently".[^o16] In 2016 he received, together with [Manuel Kauers](wikipedia://en/40133395) and [Christoph Koutschan](wikipedia://en/48644256), the [David P. Robbins Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_P._Robbins_Prize) of the American Mathematical Society.

Zeilberger was a member of the inaugural 2013 class of fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o17]
