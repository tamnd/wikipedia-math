# Free-by-cyclic group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Free-by-cyclic group"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), especially, in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), the class of free-by-cyclic groups have been deeply studied as important examples. A [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$ is said to be **free-by-cyclic** if it has a [free](wikipedia://en/59735) [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) $F$ such that the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) $G/F$ is [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327). In other words, $G$ is free-by-cyclic if it can be expressed as a [group extension](wikipedia://en/345807) of a free group by a cyclic group (NB there are two conventions for 'by'). Usually, we assume $F$ is finitely generated and the quotient is an infinite cyclic group. Equivalently, we can define a free-by-cyclic group constructively: if $\varphi$ is an automorphism of $F$, the semidirect product $F \rtimes_\varphi \mathbb{Z}$ is a free-by-cyclic group.

:::

An isomorphism class of a free-by-cyclic group is determined by an [outer automorphism](wikipedia://en/12747184). If two automorphisms $\varphi, \psi$ represent the same outer automorphism, that is, $\varphi = \psi\iota$ for some [inner automorphism](wikipedia://en/61839) $\iota$, the free-by-cyclic groups $F \rtimes_\varphi \mathbb{Z}$ and $F \rtimes_\psi \mathbb{Z}$ are isomorphic.

## Examples and results {#Examples_and_results}

The study of free-by-cyclic groups is strongly related to that of the attaching outer automorphism. Among the motivating questions are those concerning their non-positive curvature properties, such as being [CAT(0)](wikipedia://en/12942017).

- A free-by-cyclic group is [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/3172817), if and only if it does not contain a subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}^2$, if and only if no nontrivial [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) is left invariant by the attaching automorphism (irreducible case: Bestvina and Feighn, 1992; general case: Brinkmann, 2000).[^o3]
- Hyperbolic free-by-cyclic groups are [fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004) of compact non-positively curved [cube complexes](wikipedia://en/54829672) (Hagen and Wise, 2015).[^o4]
- Some free-by-cyclic groups are [hyperbolic relative](wikipedia://en/21993809) to [free-abelian](wikipedia://en/247296) subgroups. More generally, all free-by-cyclic groups are hyperbolic relative to a collection of subgroups that are free-by-cyclic for an automorphism of polynomial growth.[^o5][^o6]
- Any [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) subgroup of a free-by-cyclic group is [finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group) (Feighn and Handel, 1999).[^o7]
- The [conjugacy problem](wikipedia://en/4942384) for free-by-cyclic groups is solved (Bogopolski, Martino, Maslakova and Ventura, 2006).[^o8] (Bridson and Groves 2010) [^o9]
- Free-by-cyclic groups are conjugacy separable (Dahmani, Hughes, Kudlinska, and Touikan 2026)[^o10]
- Notably, there are non-CAT(0) free-by-cyclic groups (Gersten, 1994).[^o11]
- However, all free-by-cyclic groups satisfy a quadratic [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182) (Bridson and Groves, 2010)[^o9].
- All free-by-cyclic groups where the underlying free group has [rank](wikipedia://en/18720088) $2$ are CAT(0) (Brady, 1995).[^o12]
- Many examples of free-by-cyclic groups with polynomially-growing attaching maps are known to be CAT(0).[^o13][^o14]
- Free-by-cyclic groups are [equationally noetherian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equationally_noetherian_group) and have [well-ordered](wikipedia://en/33456) [growth rates](wikipedia://en/498727) (Kudlinska and Valiunas, 2025).[^o15]
