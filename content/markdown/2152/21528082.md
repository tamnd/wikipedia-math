# Jerry L. Bona

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jerry L. Bona"}

**Jerry Lloyd Bona** (born February 5, 1945) is an American mathematician, known for his work in [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988), [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), and [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833), and active in some other branches of pure and applied mathematics.

:::

Bona received his PhD in 1971 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) under supervision of [Garrett Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/1180916) and worked from 1970 to 1972 at the Fluid Mechanics Research Institute [University of Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Essex), where along with [Brooke Benjamin](wikipedia://en/16733041) and J. J. Mahony, he published on Model Equations for Long Waves in Non-linear Dispersive Systems, known as [Benjamin–Bona–Mahony equation](wikipedia://en/16703476). He is probably best known for his statement about equivalent statements of the [Axiom of Choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_of_Choice): “The Axiom of Choice is obviously true, the [Well–ordering theorem](wikipedia://en/33458) is obviously false; and who can tell about [Zorn’s Lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorn's_Lemma)?"[^o3]

Jerry Bona has worked at [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University), [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) and is a Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago). In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o4] In 2013 he became a fellow of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376).[^o5]

## Quotes {#Quotes}

The [Axiom of Choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_of_Choice) is obviously true, the [well-ordering principle](wikipedia://en/33458) obviously false, and who can tell about [Zorn's lemma](wikipedia://en/51442)?[^o7] This is a joke: although the three are all mathematically equivalent, many mathematicians find the axiom of choice to be intuitive, the well-ordering principle to be counterintuitive, and Zorn's lemma to be too complex for any intuition.
