# Weakly holomorphic modular form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weakly holomorphic modular form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **weakly holomorphic modular form** is similar to a holomorphic modular form, except that it is allowed to have poles at cusps. Examples include [modular functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_function) and [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

To simplify notation this section does the level 1 case; the extension to higher levels is straightforward.

A level 1 weakly holomorphic modular form is a function *f* on the upper half plane with the properties:

- *f* transforms like a modular form: $f((a\tau+b)/(c\tau+d)) = (c\tau+d)^kf(\tau)$ for some integer *k* called the **weight**, for any elements of SL<sub>2</sub>(**Z**).
- As a function of *q*=e<sup>2π*i*τ</sup>, *f* is given by a [Laurent series](wikipedia://en/61213) whose [radius of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476) is *1* (so *f* is holomorphic on the upper half plane and meromorphic at the cusps).

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The ring of level 1 modular forms is generated by the Eisenstein series *E*<sub>4</sub> and *E*<sub>6</sub> (which generate the ring of holomorphic modular forms) together with the inverse 1/Δ of the [modular discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_discriminant).

Any weakly holomorphic modular form of any level can be written as a quotient of two holomorphic modular forms. However, not every quotient of two holomorphic modular forms is a weakly holomorphic modular form, as it may have poles in the upper half plane.

:::
