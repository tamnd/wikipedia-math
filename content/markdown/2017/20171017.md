# Fourier sine and cosine series

Sine and cosine series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fourier sine and cosine series"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly the field of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) and [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659), the **Fourier sine and cosine series** are two [mathematical series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_series) named after [Joseph Fourier](wikipedia://en/25767401).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

In this article, $f$ denotes a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued [function](wikipedia://en/185427) on $\mathbb{R}$ which is [periodic](wikipedia://en/76848) with period 2*L*.

:::

## Sine series {#Sine_series}

If $f$ is an [odd function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_function) with period $2L$, then the Fourier Half Range sine series of *f* is defined to be

$$f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n \sin \left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$$

which is just a form of complete [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) with the only difference that $a_0$ and $a_n$ are zero, and the series is defined for half of the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172).

In the formula we have

$$b_n = \frac{2}{L} \int_0^L f(x) \sin \left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) \, dx, \quad n \in \mathbb{N} .$$

## Cosine series {#Cosine_series}

If $f$ is an [even function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_function) with a period $2L$, then the Fourier cosine series is defined to be

$$f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \cos \left(\frac{n \pi x}{L}\right)$$

where

$$a_n = \frac{2}{L} \int_0^L f(x) \cos \left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) \, dx, \quad n \in \mathbb{N}_0 .$$

## Remarks {#Remarks}

This notion can be generalized to functions which are not even or odd, but then the above formulas will look different.
