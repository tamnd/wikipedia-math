# Proper forcing axiom

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Proper forcing axiom"}

In the mathematical field of [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), the **proper forcing axiom** (*PFA*) is a significant strengthening of [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783), where [forcings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forcing_%28set_theory%29) with the [countable chain condition](wikipedia://en/1550771) (ccc) are replaced by proper forcings.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

A [forcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forcing_%28set_theory%29) or [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) $P$ is **proper** if for all [regular](wikipedia://en/374128) uncountable [cardinals](wikipedia://en/6173) $\lambda$, [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) with P preserves [stationary subsets](wikipedia://en/1679067) of $[\lambda]^\omega$.

The **proper forcing axiom** asserts that if $P$ is proper and $D_\alpha$ is a dense subset of $P$ for each $\alpha < \omega_1$, then there is a filter $G \subseteq P$ such that $D_\alpha \cap G$ is nonempty for all $\alpha < \omega_1$.

The class of proper forcings, to which PFA can be applied, is rather large. For example, standard arguments show that if $P$ is [ccc](wikipedia://en/1550771) or [&omega;-closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/&omega;-closed), then $P$ is proper. If $P$ is a [countable support iteration](wikipedia://en/43053125) of proper forcings, then $P$ is proper. Crucially, all proper forcings preserve [$\aleph_1$](wikipedia://en/6173).

:::

## Consequences {#Consequences}

PFA directly implies its version for ccc forcings, [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783). In [cardinal arithmetic](wikipedia://en/6173), PFA implies $2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_2$. PFA implies any two $\aleph_1$-dense subsets of R are isomorphic,[^o5] any two [Aronszajn trees](wikipedia://en/12774077) are club-isomorphic,[^o6] and every automorphism of the [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/54476844) $P(\omega)\text{/fin}$ is trivial.[^o7] PFA implies that the [Singular Cardinals Hypothesis](wikipedia://en/4623947) holds. An especially notable consequence proved by [John R. Steel](wikipedia://en/2290967) is that the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300) holds in [L(R)](wikipedia://en/2250604), the smallest [inner model](wikipedia://en/1118686) containing the real numbers. Another consequence is the failure of [square principles](wikipedia://en/2292730) and hence existence of inner models with many [Woodin cardinals](wikipedia://en/248105).

## Consistency strength {#Consistency_strength}

If there is a [supercompact cardinal](wikipedia://en/248116), then there is a model of set theory in which PFA holds. The proof uses the fact that proper forcings are preserved under countable support iteration, and the fact that if $\kappa$ is supercompact, then there exists a [Laver function](wikipedia://en/19089448) for $\kappa$.

It is not yet known precisely how much large cardinal strength comes from PFA, and currently the best lower bound is a bit below the existence of a Woodin cardinal that is a limit of Woodin cardinals.

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Other forcing axioms"}

## Other forcing axioms {#Other_forcing_axioms}

The **bounded proper forcing axiom** (BPFA) is a weaker variant of PFA which instead of arbitrary dense subsets applies only to maximal [antichains](wikipedia://en/348973) of size $\omega_1$. **[Martin's maximum](wikipedia://en/15978374)** is the strongest possible version of a forcing axiom.

Forcing axioms are viable candidates for extending the axioms of set theory as an alternative to [large cardinal](wikipedia://en/383644) axioms.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="The Fundamental Theorem of Proper Forcing"}

## The Fundamental Theorem of Proper Forcing {#The_Fundamental_Theorem_of_Proper_Forcing}

The Fundamental Theorem of Proper Forcing, due to [Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211), states that any [countable support iteration](wikipedia://en/43053125) of proper forcings is itself proper. This follows from the Proper Iteration Lemma, which states that whenever $(P_\alpha)_{\alpha\leq\kappa}$ is a countable support forcing iteration based on $(Q_\alpha)_{\alpha<\kappa}$ and $N$ is a countable elementary substructure of $H_\lambda$ for a sufficiently large regular cardinal $\lambda$, and $P_\kappa\in N$ and $\alpha\in \kappa\cap N$ and $p$ is $(N,P_\alpha)$-generic and $p$ forces $q\in P_\kappa/G_{P_\alpha}\cap N[G_{P_\alpha}]$, then there exists $r\in P_\kappa$ such that $r$ is $N$-generic and the restriction of $r$ to $P_\alpha$ equals $p$ and $p$ forces the restriction of $r$ to $[\alpha,\kappa)$ to be stronger or equal to $q$.

This version of the Proper Iteration Lemma, in which the name $q$ is not assumed to be in $N$, is due to Schlindwein.[^o13]

The Proper Iteration Lemma is proved by a fairly straightforward induction on $\kappa$, and the Fundamental Theorem of Proper Forcing follows by taking $\alpha=0$.
