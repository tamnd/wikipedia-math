# Klein geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Klein geometry"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Klein geometry** is a type of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) motivated by [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) in his influential [Erlangen program](wikipedia://en/243382). More specifically, it is a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) *X* together with a [transitive action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_action) on *X* by a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) *G*, which acts as the [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) of the geometry.

:::

For background and motivation see the article on the [Erlangen program](wikipedia://en/243382).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A **Klein geometry** is a pair (*G*, *H*) where *G* is a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) and *H* is a [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [Lie subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_subgroup) of *G* such that the (left) [coset space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coset_space) *G*/*H* is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233). The group *G* is called the **principal group** of the geometry and *G*/*H* is called the **space** of the geometry (or, by an abuse of terminology, simply the *Klein geometry*). The space *X* = *G*/*H* of a Klein geometry is a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) of dimension

dim *X* = dim *G* − dim *H*.

There is a natural smooth [left action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of *G* on *X* given by

$$g \cdot (aH) = (ga)H.$$

Clearly, this action is transitive (take *a* = 1), so that one may then regard *X* as a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) for the action of *G*. The [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_%28group_theory%29) of the identity coset *H* ∈ *X* is precisely the group *H*.

Given any connected smooth manifold *X* and a smooth transitive action by a Lie group *G* on *X*, we can construct an associated Klein geometry (*G*, *H*) by fixing a basepoint *x*<sub>0</sub> in *X* and letting *H* be the stabilizer subgroup of *x*<sub>0</sub> in *G*. The group *H* is necessarily a closed subgroup of *G* and *X* is naturally [diffeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphic) to *G*/*H*.

Two Klein geometries (*G*<sub>1</sub>, *H*<sub>1</sub>) and (*G*<sub>2</sub>, *H*<sub>2</sub>) are **geometrically isomorphic** if there is a [Lie group isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_group_isomorphism) *φ* : *G*<sub>1</sub> → *G*<sub>2</sub> so that *φ*(*H*<sub>1</sub>) = *H*<sub>2</sub>. In particular, if *φ* is [conjugation](wikipedia://en/49176) by an element *g* ∈ *G*, we see that (*G*, *H*) and (*G*, *gHg*<sup>−1</sup>) are isomorphic. The Klein geometry associated to a homogeneous space *X* is then unique up to isomorphism (i.e. it is independent of the chosen basepoint *x*<sub>0</sub>).

:::

## Bundle description {#Bundle_description}

Given a Lie group *G* and closed subgroup *H*, there is natural [right action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of *H* on *G* given by right multiplication. This action is both free and [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_action). The [orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) are simply the left [cosets](wikipedia://en/98759) of *H* in *G*. One concludes that *G* has the structure of a smooth [principal *H*-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) over the left coset space *G*/*H*:

$$H\to G\to G/H .$$

## Types of Klein geometries {#Types_of_Klein_geometries}

### Effective geometries {#Effective_geometries}

The action of *G* on *X* = *G*/*H* need not be effective. The **kernel** of a Klein geometry is defined to be the kernel of the action of *G* on *X*. It is given by

$$K = \{k \in G : g^{-1}kg \in H\;\;\forall g \in G\}.$$

The kernel *K* may also be described as the [core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_%28group%29) of *H* in *G* (i.e. the largest subgroup of *H* that is [normal](wikipedia://en/21918) in *G*). It is the group generated by all the normal subgroups of *G* that lie in *H*.

A Klein geometry is said to be **effective** if *K* = 1 and **locally effective** if *K* is [discrete](wikipedia://en/468536). If (*G*, *H*) is a Klein geometry with kernel *K*, then (*G*/*K*, *H*/*K*) is an effective Klein geometry canonically associated to (*G*, *H*).

### Geometrically oriented geometries {#Geometrically_oriented_geometries}

A Klein geometry (*G*, *H*) is **geometrically oriented** if *G* is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233). (This does *not* imply that *G*/*H* is an [oriented manifold](wikipedia://en/187446)). If *H* is connected it follows that *G* is also connected (this is because *G*/*H* is assumed to be connected, and *G* → *G*/*H* is a [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826)).

Given any Klein geometry (*G*, *H*), there is a geometrically oriented geometry canonically associated to (*G*, *H*) with the same base space *G*/*H*. This is the geometry (*G*<sub>0</sub>, *G*<sub>0</sub> ∩ *H*) where *G*<sub>0</sub> is the [identity component](wikipedia://en/1088787) of *G*. Note that *G* = *G*<sub>0</sub> *H*.

::: {#o9 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Reductive homogeneous space"}

### Reductive geometries {#Reductive_geometries}

A Klein geometry (*G*, *H*) is said to be **reductive** and *G*/*H* a **reductive homogeneous space** if the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak h$ of *H* has an *H*-invariant complement in $\mathfrak g$.

:::
