# Trisectrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trisectrix"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **trisectrix** is a curve which can be used to [trisect an arbitrary angle](wikipedia://en/91111) with ruler and compass and this curve as an additional tool. Such a method falls outside those allowed by [compass and straightedge constructions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge_constructions), so they do not contradict the well known theorem which states that an arbitrary angle cannot be trisected with that type of construction. There is a variety of such curves and the methods used to construct an angle trisector differ according to the curve. Examples include:

:::

- [Limaçon trisectrix](wikipedia://en/18635261) (some sources refer to this curve as simply the trisectrix.)[^o3]
- [Trisectrix of Maclaurin](wikipedia://en/2738114)[^o4]
- [Tschirnhausen cubic](wikipedia://en/10475041) (a.k.a. Catalan's trisectrix and L'Hôpital's cubic)[^o5]
- [Deslanges trisectrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deslanges_trisectrix)[^o6]
- [Cubic parabola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_parabola) (the graph of the cube function)[^o3]
- [Hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052) with eccentricity 2[^o7][^o3]
- [Parabola](wikipedia://en/23231#As_trisectrix)[^o3]
- [Cycloid of Ceva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycloid_of_Ceva)[^o3]

A related concept is a **sectrix**, which is a curve which can be used to divide an arbitrary angle by any integer.[^o8] Examples include:

- [Archimedean spiral](wikipedia://en/61559)[^o9]
- [Quadratrix of Hippias](wikipedia://en/40429150)[^o10][^o3]
- [Sine curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine_curve)[^o11]

## See also {#See_also}

- [Sectrix of Maclaurin](wikipedia://en/18635842), a family of curves different members of which can divide angles into different numbers of parts
- [Neusis construction](wikipedia://en/3752339), the use of a marked ruler in constructions such as angle trisection
- [Quadratrix](wikipedia://en/2885016), a curve used for [squaring the circle](wikipedia://en/201359)
