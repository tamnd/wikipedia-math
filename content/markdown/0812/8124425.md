# Control point (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Control point"}

In [computer-aided geometric design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_geometric_design) a **control point** is a member of a set of [points](wikipedia://en/593693) used to determine the shape of a [spline curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spline_curve) or, more generally, a [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_representation_of_surfaces) or higher-dimensional object.[^o2]

:::

For [Bézier curves](wikipedia://en/4664), it has become customary to refer to the $d$-vectors $\mathbf p_i$ in a [parametric representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_representation) $\sum_i \mathbf p_i \phi_i$ of a curve or surface in $d$-space as **control points**, while the [scalar-valued functions](wikipedia://en/161879) $\phi_i$, defined over the relevant parameter domain, are the corresponding [*weight*](wikipedia://en/245523) or *[blending functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blending_function)*. Some would reasonably insist, in order to give intuitive geometric meaning to the word "control", that the blending functions form a [partition of unity](wikipedia://en/48636), i.e., that the $\phi_i$ are nonnegative and sum to one. This property implies that the curve lies within the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of its control points.[^o3] This is the case for Bézier's representation of a polynomial curve as well as for the [B-spline](wikipedia://en/21834) representation of a spline curve or [tensor-product spline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor-product_spline) surface.
