# List of knot theory topics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of knot theory topics"}

**[Knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008)** is the study of [mathematical knots](wikipedia://en/366808). While inspired by knots which appear in daily life in shoelaces and rope, a mathematician's knot differs in that the ends are joined so that it cannot be undone. In precise mathematical language, a knot is an [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) in 3-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), **R**<sup>3</sup>. Two mathematical knots are equivalent if one can be transformed into the other via a deformation of **R**<sup>3</sup> upon itself (known as an [ambient isotopy](wikipedia://en/7404467)); these transformations correspond to manipulations of a knotted string that do not involve cutting the string or passing the string through itself.

:::

## Knots, links, braids {#Knots,_links,_braids}

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Knot (mathematics)"}

- [Knot (mathematics)](wikipedia://en/366808) gives a general introduction to the concept of a knot.

:::

  - Two classes of knots: [torus knots](wikipedia://en/1165182) and [pretzel knots](wikipedia://en/1286130)
  - [Cinquefoil knot](wikipedia://en/16856240) also known as a (5, 2) torus knot.
  - [Figure-eight knot (mathematics)](wikipedia://en/59565) the only 4-crossing knot
  - [Granny knot (mathematics)](wikipedia://en/26666182) and [Square knot (mathematics)](wikipedia://en/26666011) are a connected sum of two [Trefoil knots](wikipedia://en/404773)
  - [Perko pair](wikipedia://en/16618607), two entries in a knot table that were later shown to be identical.
  - [Stevedore knot (mathematics)](wikipedia://en/26652059), a [prime knot](wikipedia://en/315030) with crossing number 6
  - [Three-twist knot](wikipedia://en/26643955) is the twist knot with three-half twists, also known as the 5<sub>2</sub> knot.
  - [Trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773) A knot with crossing number 3
  - [Unknot](wikipedia://en/60075)

::: {#o5 .entry cue=list-entry name="Knot complement"}

- [Knot complement](wikipedia://en/1350120), a compact 3 manifold obtained by removing an open neighborhood of a proper embedding of a tame knot from the 3-sphere.

## Invariants and properties {#Invariants_and_properties}

::: {#o68 .entry cue=list-entry name="Knot invariant"}

- [Knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) Talk:Knot invariant is an invariant defined on knots which is invariant under ambient isotopies of the knot.

:::

::: {#o69 .entry cue=list-entry name="Finite type invariant"}

- [Finite type invariant](wikipedia://en/3014395) Talk:Finite type invariant is a knot invariant that can be extended to an invariant of certain singular knots

:::

::: {#o70 .entry cue=list-entry name="Knot polynomial"}

- [Knot polynomial](wikipedia://en/313735) is a knot invariant in the form of a polynomial whose coefficients encode some of the properties of a given knot.

:::

  - [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) and the associated [Alexander matrix](wikipedia://en/12267898); The first knot polynomial (1923). Sometimes called the [Alexander–Conway polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander–Conway_polynomial)
  - [Bracket polynomial](wikipedia://en/3774360) is a polynomial invariant of framed links. Related to the Jones polynomial. Also known as the Kauffman bracket.
  - [Conway polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647#Alexander.E2.80.93Conway_polynomial) uses [Skein relations](wikipedia://en/314902).
  - [Homfly polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homfly_polynomial) or [HOMFLYPT polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOMFLYPT_polynomial).
  - [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642) assigns a [Laurent polynomial](wikipedia://en/61351) in the variable *t*<sup>1/2</sup> to the knot or link.
  - [Kauffman polynomial](wikipedia://en/9565831) is a 2-variable knot polynomial due to Louis Kauffman.
