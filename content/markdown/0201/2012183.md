# Robert Berger (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Berger"}

**Robert Berger** (born 1938) is an applied mathematician, known for discovering the first [aperiodic tiling](wikipedia://en/868145)[^o2] using a set of 20,426 distinct tile shapes.

:::

## Contributions to tiling theory {#Contributions_to_tiling_theory}

The unexpected existence of aperiodic tilings, although not Berger's explicit construction of them, follows from another result proved by Berger: that the so-called [domino problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domino_problem) is [undecidable](wikipedia://en/15631055), disproving a conjecture of [Hao Wang](wikipedia://en/1056047), Berger's advisor. The result is analogous to a 1962 construction used by [Kahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Kahr), [Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_F._Moore), and Wang, to show that a more constrained version of the domino problem was undecidable.[^o4]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Berger did his undergraduate studies at [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute), and studied [applied physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_physics) at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard), earning a master's degree, before shifting to applied mathematics for his doctorate. Along with Hao Wang, Berger's other two doctoral committee members were [Patrick Carl Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Carl_Fischer) and [Marvin Minsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Minsky). Later, he has worked in the Digital Integrated Circuits Group of the [Lincoln Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Laboratory).[^o6]

## Publications {#Publications}

Berger's work on tiling was published as "The Undecidability of the Domino Problem" in the *[Memoirs of the AMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memoirs_of_the_AMS)* in 1966.[^o8] This paper is essentially a reprint of Berger's 1964 dissertation at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o9]

In 2009, a paper by Berger and other Lincoln Laboratories researchers, "Wafer-scale 3D integration of InGaAs image sensors with Si readout circuits", won the best paper award at the IEEE International 3D System Integration Conference (3DIC).[^o10] In 2010, a [CMOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMOS) [infrared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared) imaging device with an [analog-to-digital converter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog-to-digital_converter) in each pixel, coinvented by Berger, was one of *R&D Magazine**s [R&D 100 Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R&D_100_Award) recipients.[^o11]***
