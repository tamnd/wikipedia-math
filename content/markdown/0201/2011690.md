# Surface bundle over the circle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Surface bundle over the circle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **surface bundle over the circle**[^o2] is a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) with [base space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_space) a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220), and with fiber space a [surface](wikipedia://en/27865). Therefore the [total space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_space) has dimension 2 + 1 = 3. In general, [fiber bundles](wikipedia://en/276526) over the circle are a special case of [mapping tori](wikipedia://en/21034384).

:::

Here is the construction: take the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of a surface with the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324). Glue the two copies of the surface, on the boundary, by some homeomorphism. This homeomorphism is called the [monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250) of the surface bundle. It is possible to show that the homeomorphism type of the bundle obtained depends only on the [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176), in the [mapping class group](wikipedia://en/793295), of the gluing homeomorphism chosen.

This construction is an important source of examples both in the field of [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774) as well as in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164). In the former we find that the [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of the three-manifold is determined by the dynamics of the homeomorphism. This is the fibered part of [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190)'s geometrization theorem for Haken manifolds, whose proof requires the [Nielsen–Thurston classification](wikipedia://en/1684758) for surface homeomorphisms as well as deep results in the theory of [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652). In geometric group theory the [fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004) of such bundles give an important class of [HNN-extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNN-extension): that is, [extensions](wikipedia://en/345807) of the fundamental group of the fiber (a surface) by the integers.

A simple special case of this construction (considered in [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740)'s foundational paper) is that of a [torus bundle](wikipedia://en/680735).
