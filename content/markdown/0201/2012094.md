# Schiffler point

*Image: Schiffler Point.svg.* Diagram of the Schiffler point solid black 2px dotted blue dotted lime dotted red solid black 1px

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schiffler point"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Schiffler point** of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is a [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488), a point defined from the triangle that is [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) under [Euclidean transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_transformation) of the triangle. This point was first defined and investigated by [Schiffler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Schiffler) et al. (1985).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A triangle $\triangle ABC$ with the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) $I$ has its Schiffler point at the point of concurrence of the [Euler lines](wikipedia://en/203622) of the four triangles $\triangle BCI, \triangle CAI, \triangle ABI, \triangle ABC$. **Schiffler's theorem** states that these four lines all meet at a single point.[^o6]

:::

## Coordinates {#Coordinates}

[Trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) for the Schiffler point are

$\frac{1}{\cos B + \cos C} : \frac{1}{\cos C + \cos A} : \frac{1}{\cos A + \cos B}$ [^o6]

or, equivalently,

$$\frac{b+c-a}{b+c} : \frac{c+a-b}{c+a} : \frac{a+b-c}{a+b}$$

where $a, b, c$ denote the side lengths of triangle $\triangle ABC$.
