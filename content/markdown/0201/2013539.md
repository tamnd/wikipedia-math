# Gross rating point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gross rating point"}

In [advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising), a **gross rating point** (**GRP**) measures the size of an audience that an advertisement impacts.[^o2] GRPs help answer *how often* "must someone see it before they can readily recall it" and "how many times" does it take before the desired outcome occurs.[^o3]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

Gross rating points are a measure of the impact by a campaign using a specific [medium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_%28communication%29) or schedule. It quantifies [impressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_per_impression) as a percentage of the target population, multiplied by [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_%28marketing%29). This percentage may be greater, or in fact much greater, than 100.

[Target rating points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_rating_point) express the same concept, but with regard to a more narrowly defined target audience.[^o5][^o6]

GRPs are used predominantly as a measure of media with high *potential* exposures or [impressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impression_%28online_media%29). [Nielsen Media Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research) is an example of a company which uses GRPs.[^o7]

## Purpose {#Purpose}

With "today's fragmented media world" the value of GRP is, according to the [Advertising Research Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising_Research_Foundation)'s *Journal of Advertising Research*, even greater than in the pre-Internet era.[^o9] Since "the required frequency changes with the product and the competitive climate it is in",[^o3] the purpose of the GRP metric is to measure [impressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impression_%28online_media%29) compared to the number of people in the target for an advertising campaign.[^o5] GRP values are commonly used by [media buyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_buying) to compare the advertising strength of components of a media plan.

For conventional media such as radio and TV, multi-tasking has reduced the value per GRP, and a measure named *Persuasion Rating Point (PRP)* was proposed in mid 2020.[^o10]

::: {#o12 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

"One GRP is one percent of all potential adult television viewers (or in radio, listeners) in a market." If they are exposed to the ad three times, then that is 3 GRPs.[^o3]

GRPs are simply total impressions related to the size of the target population: They are most directly calculated by summing the ratings of individual ads in a campaign.

Mathematically:

GRPs (%) = 100 \* Total Impressions (#) ÷ Defined population (#)[^o5]

GRPs (%) = 100 \* Reach (%) × Average frequency (#)

Three examples:

- If 100,000 ad impressions are displayed on multiple episodes or TV stations for a defined population of 100,000 people, the total is 100 GRPs. However, total [reach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reach_%28advertising%29) is not always 100%.
- If an average of 12% of the people view each episode of a television program, and an ad is placed on 5 episodes, then the campaign has 12 × 5 = 60 GRPs.
- If 50% view three episodes, that's 150 GRPs.

:::
