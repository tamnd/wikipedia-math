# Étale spectrum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Étale spectrum"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **étale spectrum** of a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) or of an [**E**<sub>∞</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-infinity_ring)-ring, denoted by Spec<sup>ét</sup> or Spét, is an analog of the [prime spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_spectrum) Spec of a commutative ring that is obtained by replacing the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) with the [étale topology](wikipedia://en/5707590).

:::

The usual prime spectrum Spec enjoys the following relation: for a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) (*S*, *O*<sub>*S*</sub>) and a commutative ring *A*,

$$\operatorname{Hom}(S, \operatorname{Spec}(A)) \simeq \operatorname{Hom}(A, \Gamma(S, \mathcal{O}_S)),$$

where Hom on the left is for [morphisms of schemes](wikipedia://en/7482797) and Hom on the right [ring homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/26411). That is, Spec is the [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint) to the [global section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_section) [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $(S, \mathcal{O}_S) \mapsto \Gamma(S, \mathcal{O}_S)$. So, roughly, one can (and typically does) simply define the étale spectrum Spét to be the right adjoint to the global section functor on the category of "spaces" with étale topology.[^n1][^n2]

Over a [field of characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_of_characteristic_zero), Behrend constructs the étale spectrum of a [graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) called a perfect resolving algebra.[^n3] He then defines a [differential graded scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_graded_scheme) (a type of a [derived scheme](wikipedia://en/40776948)) as one that is étale-locally such an étale spectrum.

The notion makes sense in the usual algebraic geometry but appears more frequently in the context of [derived algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/40778281).
