# Q-category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Q-category"}

In mathematics, a **Q-category** or **almost quotient category[^o3]** is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) that is a "milder version of a Grothendieck site."[^o2] A Q-category is a [coreflective subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coreflective_subcategory).[^o3] The Q stands for a quotient.

:::

The concept of Q-categories was introduced by Alexander Rosenberg in 1988.[^o2] The motivation for the notion was its use in [noncommutative algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/25493347); in this formalism, [noncommutative spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_space) are defined as [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) on Q-categories.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A Q-category is defined by the formula[^o3]

$$\mathbb{A} : (u^* \dashv u_*) : \bar A \stackrel{\overset{u^*}{\leftarrow}}{\underset{u_*}{\to}} A$$

where $u^*$ is the left adjoint in a pair of [adjoint functors](wikipedia://en/53991) and is a [full and faithful functor](wikipedia://en/876534).

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The category of [presheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presheaves) over any Q-category is itself a Q-category.[^o3]
- For any category, one can define the *Q-category of cones*.[^o3]
- There is a Q-category of sieves.[^o3]

:::
