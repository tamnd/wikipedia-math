# Somos' quadratic recurrence constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Somos' quadratic recurrence constant"}

In [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Somos' quadratic recurrence constant** or simply **Somos' constant** is a [constant](wikipedia://en/48779551) defined as an expression of infinitely many nested [square roots](wikipedia://en/29208). It arises when studying the [asymptotic behaviour](wikipedia://en/641995) of a certain [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838)[^o11] and also in connection to the [binary representations](wikipedia://en/238686) of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) between [zero](wikipedia://en/34513) and [one](wikipedia://en/77591639).[^o2] The constant named after [Michael Somos](wikipedia://en/24464863). It is defined by:

:::

$$\sigma = \sqrt {1 \sqrt {2 \sqrt{3 \sqrt{4\sqrt{5\cdots}}}}}$$

which gives a numerical value of approximately:[^o3]

$\sigma = 1.661687949633594121295\dots\;$ .

## Sums and products {#Sums_and_products}

Somos' constant can be alternatively defined via the following [infinite product](wikipedia://en/26382006):

$$\sigma=\prod_{k=1}^\infty k^{1/2^k} = 1^{1/2}\;2^{1/4}\; 3^{1/8}\; 4^{1/16} \dots$$

This can be easily rewritten into the far more quickly [converging](wikipedia://en/26551602) product representation

$$\sigma = \left(\frac{2}{1}\right)^{1/2} \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{1/4} \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{1/8} \left(\frac{5}{4}\right)^{1/16} \dots$$

which can then be compactly represented in [infinite product](wikipedia://en/26382006) form by:

$$\sigma = \prod_{k=1}^{\infty} \left(1+ \frac{1}{k}\right)^{1/2^k}$$

Another product representation is given by:

$$\sigma = \prod_{n=1}^\infty\prod_{k=0}^n (k+1)^{(-1)^{k+n} \binom{n}{k}}$$

Expressions for $\ln\sigma$ include:[^o5][^o6]

$$\ln \sigma = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{\ln k}{2^k}$$

$$\ln \sigma = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{k} \text{Li}_k\left(\tfrac12\right)$$

$$\ln \frac\sigma2 = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^k}\left(\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{k}\right)-\frac1k\right)$$

## Integrals {#Integrals}

Integrals for $\ln\sigma$ are given by:[^o5][^o8]

$$\ln \sigma = \int_0^1 \frac{1-x}{(x-2)\ln x} dx$$

$$\ln \sigma = \int_0^1 \int_0^1 \frac{-x}{(2-xy)\ln(xy)} dx dy$$

::: {#o10 .statement cue=named-heading name="Other formulas"}

## Other formulas {#Other_formulas}

The constant $\sigma$ arises when studying the asymptotic behaviour of the sequence[^o11]

$$g_0 = 1$$

$$g_n = n g_{n-1}^2, \qquad n \ge 1$$

with first few terms 1, 1, 2, 12, 576, 1658880, ... . This sequence can be shown to have asymptotic behaviour as follows:[^o5]

$$g_n \sim {\sigma^{2^n}}\left(n+2-n^{-1}+4n^{-2}-21n^{-3}+138n^{-4}+O(n^{-5})\right)^{-1}$$

[Guillera](wikipedia://en/80673725) and Sondow give a representation in terms of the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of the [Lerch transcendent](wikipedia://en/1342978) $\Phi(z, s, q)$:[^o8]

$$\ln\sigma = -\frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial s}\!\left( 1/2, 0, 1 \right)$$

If one defines the [Euler-constant function](wikipedia://en/148594#Euler-constant_function) (which gives [Euler's constant](wikipedia://en/148594) for $z=1$) as:

$$\gamma(z)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty z^{n-1}\left(\frac1n - \ln\left(\frac{n+1}{n}\right)\right)$$

one has:[^o12][^o13][^o14]

$$\gamma(\tfrac12)=2\ln\frac2 \sigma$$

:::

## Universality {#Universality}

One may define a *"continued binary expansion"* for all real numbers in the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $(0,1]$, similarly to the [decimal expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_expansion) or [simple continued fraction expansion](wikipedia://en/46802). This is done by considering the unique [base-2 representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base-2) for a number $x\in(0,1]$ which does not contain an infinite tail of 0's (for example write [one half](wikipedia://en/464963) as $0.01111..._2$ instead of $0.1_2$). Then define a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) $(a_k)\subseteq \mathbb{N}$ which gives the difference in positions of the 1's in this base-2 representation. This expansion for $x$ is now given by:[^o16]

$$x=\langle a_1, a_2, a_3, ... \rangle$$

*Image: SomosConstant.png.* The geometric means of the terms of [Pi](wikipedia://en/23601) and [e](wikipedia://en/9633) appear to tend to Somos' constant.

For example the [fractional part](wikipedia://en/501532) of [Pi](wikipedia://en/23601) we have:

$$\{\pi\} = 0.14159 \,26535 \, 89793... = 0.00100 \, 10000 \, 11111 ..._2$$

The first 1 occurs on position 3 after the [radix point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radix_point). The next 1 appears three places after the first one, the third 1 appears five places after the second one, etc. By continuing in this manner, we obtain:

$$\pi-3= \langle 3, 3, 5, 1, 1, 1, 1 ... \rangle$$

This gives a [bijective](wikipedia://en/3942) map $(0,1] \mapsto \mathbb{N} ^\mathbb{N}$, such that for every real number $x\in(0,1]$ we uniquely can give:[^o16]

$$x = \langle a_1, a_2, a_3, ... \rangle :\Leftrightarrow x= \sum _{k=1}^\infty 2^{-(a_1+...+a_k)}$$

It can now be proven that for almost all numbers $x\in(0,1]$ the limit of the [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) of the terms $a_k$ converges to Somos' constant. That is, for almost all numbers in that interval we have:[^o2]

$$\sigma = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{a_1a_2...a_n}$$

Somos' constant is universal for the "continued binary expansion" of numbers $x\in(0,1]$ in the same sense that [Khinchin's constant](wikipedia://en/435315) is universal for the simple continued fraction expansions of numbers $x\in\mathbb{R}$.

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The *generalized Somos' constants* may be given by:

$$\sigma_t=\prod_{k=1}^\infty k^{1/t^k} = 1^{1/t}\;2^{1/t^2}\; 3^{1/t^3}\; 4^{1/t^4}\dots$$

for $t>1$.

The following series holds:

$$\ln\sigma_t=\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{\ln k}{t^k}$$

We also have a connection to the [Euler-constant function](wikipedia://en/148594#Euler-constant_function):[^o13]

$$\gamma(\tfrac1t)=t\ln\left(\frac{t}{(t-1)\sigma_t^{t-1}}\right)$$

and the following limit, where $\gamma$ is [Euler's constant](wikipedia://en/148594):

$$\lim_{t\to 0^+} t\sigma_{t+1}^{t}=e^{-\gamma}$$

:::
