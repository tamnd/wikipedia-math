# William Gemmell Cochran

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="William Gemmell Cochran"}

**William Gemmell Cochran** (15 July 1909 – 29 March 1980) was a prominent [statistician](wikipedia://en/26685). He was born in [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland) but spent most of his life in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States).

:::

Cochran studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow) and the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). He worked at [Rothamsted Experimental Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Experimental_Station) from 1934 to 1939, when he moved to the United States. There he helped establish several departments of statistics. His longest spell in any one university was at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard), which he joined in 1957 and from which he retired in 1976.

## Writings {#Writings}

Cochran wrote many articles and books. His books became standard texts:

- *Experimental Designs* (with [Gertrude Mary Cox](wikipedia://en/2168672)) 1950
- Cochran, William G. (1977). "Sampling Techniques". Wiley. isbn:0-471-16240-X
- *Statistical Methods Applied to Experiments in Agriculture and Biology* by [George W. Snedecor](wikipedia://en/1134409) (Cochran contributed from the fifth (1956) edition)
- *Planning and Analysis of Observational Studies* (edited by [Lincoln E. Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_E._Moses) and [Frederick Mosteller](wikipedia://en/6125615)) 1983.
