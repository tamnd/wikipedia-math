# Thom's second isotopy lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thom's second isotopy lemma"}

In mathematics, especially in [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), **Thom's second isotopy lemma** is a family version of [Thom's first isotopy lemma](wikipedia://en/71215326); i.e., it states a family of maps between [Whitney stratified spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_stratified_spaces) is locally trivial when it is a **Thom mapping**.[^o2] Like the first isotopy lemma, the lemma was introduced by [René Thom](wikipedia://en/61633).

:::

(Mather 2012, § 11) gives a sketch of the proof. (Verona 1984) gives a simplified proof. Like the first isotopy lemma, the lemma also holds for the stratification with [Bekka's condition (C)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bekka's_condition_%28C%29), which is weaker than Whitney's condition (B).[^o5]

## Thom mapping {#Thom_mapping}

Let $f : M \to N$ be a smooth map between smooth manifolds and $X, Y \subset M$ submanifolds such that $f|_X, f|_Y$ both have differential of constant rank. Then **Thom's condition $(a_f)$** is said to hold if for each sequence $x_i$ in *X* converging to a point *y* in *Y* and such that $\operatorname{ker}(d(f|_{X})_{x_i})$ converging to a plane $\tau$ in the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810), we have $\operatorname{ker}(d(f|_Y)_y) \subset \tau.$[^o7]

Let $S \subset M, S' \subset N$ be [Whitney stratified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_stratification) closed subsets and $p : S \to Z, q : S' \to Z$ maps to some smooth manifold *Z* such that $f : S \to S'$ is a map over *Z*; i.e., $f(S) \subset S'$ and $q \circ f|_S = p$. Then $f$ is called a **Thom mapping** if the following conditions hold:[^o7]

- $f|_S, q$ are proper.
- $q$ is a submersion on each stratum of $S'$.
- For each stratum *X* of *S*, $f(X)$ lies in a stratum *Y* of $S'$ and $f : X \to Y$ is a submersion.
- Thom's condition $(a_f)$ holds for each pair of strata of $S$.

Then Thom's second isotopy lemma says that a Thom mapping is locally trivial over *Z*; i.e., each point *z* of *Z* has a neighborhood *U* with homeomorphisms $h_1 : p^{-1}(z) \times U \to p^{-1}(U), h_2 : q^{-1}(z) \times U \to q^{-1}(U)$ over *U* such that $f \circ h_1 = h_2 \circ (f|_{p^{-1}(z)} \times \operatorname{id})$.[^o7]
