# Martin Maechler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Martin Maechler"}

**Martin Maechler** (spelled Mächler in the original German) is a Swiss computational statistician and a professor emeritus at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) (Swiss Federal Institute of Technology in Zurich). He has worked as a co-developer of [R](wikipedia://en/376707), a widely used environment for statistical computing and graphics, and is a long-time member of the R Core Development Team.[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Maechler pursued his academic studies at ETH Zurich, earning a Ph.D. in mathematics in 1989 focusing on [nonparametric regression](wikipedia://en/4536068) and [curve estimation](wikipedia://en/555425). His doctoral advisors were [Frank Hampel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Hampel) and [Hans-Rudolf Künsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Rudolf_Künsch).[^o5] He conducted post-doctoral research at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington)'s Department of Statistics for a year from August 1989 and another year at Bell Communications Research, Morristown, NJ.[^o6]

He has dedicated most of his professional career to ETH Zurich's Seminar for Statistics, serving as a Senior Scientist and Lecturer before becoming an adjunct professor, and has been emeritus since March 2024. [^o3][^o7] His primary areas of research and teaching include computational statistics, robust statistical methods, clustering algorithms, and numerical computations, particularly with matrices.[^o6][^o7]

## R Project involvement {#R_Project_involvement}

Maechler is considered a pioneer in the development of the R programming language.[^o3] He joined the R Project in 1995 shortly after its inception, drawn by the open-source nature of the environment and its origin in the ACM award winning [S\_programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S_programming_language). His motivation was to "create something meaningful for mankind," particularly aiding universities in poorer countries that benefit from the open-source model.[^o3]

He is a member of the R Core Development Team and serves as the Secretary General of the [R Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_Foundation).[^o6][^o7] Maechler has authored/maintained numerous R packages, including:[^o9]

- `Matrix`: for [sparse](wikipedia://en/341015) and dense matrix classes.
- `cluster`: provides methods for cluster analysis.
- `robustbase`: offering fundamental robust statistical methods.

Maechler is also a key figure in the development of [Emacs Speaks Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emacs_Speaks_Statistics) (ESS), an add-on for the [GNU Emacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_Emacs) text editor that provides dedicated support for statistical programming languages like R. He has been an ESS Core Developer since around 1997 and served as the project leader since 2004.[^o7]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

Maechler has co-authored publications and R packages in the domain of linear and generalized linear [mixed-effects models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-effects_models). One of his highly cited works is the R package `lme4`.[^o11] He has also contributed to the field of [data visualization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_visualization) with the `scatterplot3d` package.[^o11]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Martin Maechler is married and has two grown sons. He is an active member of his local church and served as a member of Zurich's city council for the [Evangelical People's Party of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_People's_Party_of_Switzerland) (EVP/PEV) from 2008 to 2014. His hobbies include cross-country skiing, jogging, swimming, hiking, reading, and listening to music.[^o7][^o13]
