# Meusnier's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Meusnier's theorem"}

*Image: Meusnier\_theorem\_color.png.* Meusnier’s theorem, visualized

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), **Meusnier's theorem** states that all [curves](wikipedia://en/89246) on a [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_%28differential_geometry%29) passing through a given point *p* and having the same [tangent line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_line) at *p* also have the same [normal curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_curvature) at *p* and their [osculating circles](wikipedia://en/1896705) form a sphere. The theorem was first announced by [Jean Baptiste Meusnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Baptiste_Meusnier) in 1776, but not published until 1785.[^o2] At least prior to 1912, several writers in English were in the habit of calling the result *Meunier's theorem*, although there is no evidence that Meusnier himself ever spelt his name in this way.[^o3] This alternative spelling of Meusnier's name also appears on the [Arc de Triomphe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_inscribed_on_the_Arc_de_Triomphe) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).
