# Belief structure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Belief structure"}

A **belief structure** is a distributed assessment with [beliefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief).

:::

## Evidential reasoning {#Evidential_reasoning}

A belief structure is used in the [evidential reasoning (ER) approach](wikipedia://en/11303928) for [multiple-criteria decision analysis (MCDA)](wikipedia://en/1050551) to represent the performance of an alternative option on a criterion.

In the ER approach, an MCDA problem is modelled by a [belief decision matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief_decision_matrix) instead of a conventional [decision matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_Matrix). The difference between the two is that, in the former, each element is a belief structure; in the latter, conversely, each element is a single value (either numerical or textual).

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

### Application {#Application}

For example, the quality of a car engine may be assessed to be “excellent” with a high degree of belief (e.g. 0.6) due to its low [fuel consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_consumption), low vibration and high responsiveness. At the same time, the quality may be assessed to be only “good” with a lower degree of belief (e.g. 0.4 or less) because its quietness and starting can still be improved. Such an assessment can be modeled by a belief structure: *Si*(engine)={(excellent, 0.6), (good, 0.4)}, where *Si* stands for the assessment of engine on the *i*th criterion (quality). In the belief structure, “excellent” and “good” are assessment standards, whilst “0.6” and “0.4” are degrees of belief.

:::
