# Ximera

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ximera"}

**Ximera** (pronounced “[chimera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimera)”) is an open-source, interactive textbook platform, most commonly used in teaching math.[^o2] The name stands for "Ximera: Interactive, Mathematics, Education, Resources, for All."[^o3] Content is authored using [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX),[^o4] and can run on GitHub and Overleaf with no prior installation.[^o5][^o3] Currently, there are at least 13 courses hosted on Ximera, and it is used by over 45,000 students with an estimated savings of \$5.4 million.[^o6] A preprint on arXiv was also created with "the help of Ximera".[^o7]

:::

## Features {#Features}

Ximera supports embedding of GeoGebra and Desmos interactives with a LaTeX command. An interactive *answer box* gives immediate feedback to students, and the grades can be integrated with [Canvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instructure), the learning management system.[^o9][^o10] The accessibility features have also been promoted by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).[^o11]

## Development {#Development}

*Image: Massive Online Open calculus Logo(Calculus One Logo).jpg.* MOOCulus Logo

The first Ximera course was initially released on [Coursera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coursera) in the Spring Semester of 2012–13 under the name Calculus One.[^o13] **MOOCulus**, an online platform that lets you practice Calculus was developed at the Ohio State University to provide students a place to practice Calculus problems. The platform, which was built using [Ruby on Rails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_on_Rails) was built because Coursera didn't offer an engaging way to practice problems.[^o14][^o15] The whole course, which consists of 200+ videos, was typeset as a [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) on April 10, 2014. The textbook, which is licensed under *Creative Commons Attribution Non-commercial Share Alike License,* incorporated some of its example and exercise problems from [Elementary calculus: An approach using Infinitesimals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_calculus:_An_approach_using_infinitesimals).[^o16]

In 2023, Ximera received a \$2.1 million grant from the Department of Education. Students are expected to save \$4 million to \$10 a year.[^o2] As part of the funding, accessibility (defined by [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_Content_Accessibility_Guidelines) 2.1AA) is a requirement, and they partnered with "Tailor Swift Bot" for that work.[^o17] This is to comply with [United States Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice) rules for Title II of the [Americans with Disabilities Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans_with_Disabilities_Act), effective April 2026.[^o4]
