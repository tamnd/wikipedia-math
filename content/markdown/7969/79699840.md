# Triameter (graph theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Triameter"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **triameter** is a metric [invariant](wikipedia://en/1655142) that generalizes the concept of a [graph's diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341). It is defined as the maximum sum of pairwise [distances](wikipedia://en/1020021) between any three [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) in a [connected graph](wikipedia://en/1614492) $G$ and is denoted by

$$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(G) = \max\{d(u,v) + d(v,w) + d(u,w) \mid u,v,w \in V\},$$

where $V$ is the vertex set of $G$ and $d(u,v)$ is the length of the shortest [path](wikipedia://en/638889) between [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) $u$ and $v$.

:::

It extends the idea of the [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341), which captures the longest [path](wikipedia://en/638889) between any two of its [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899). A **triametral triple** is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of three [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) achieving $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(G)$.

## History {#History}

The parameter of **triameter** is related to the channel [assignment problem](wikipedia://en/140592)—the problem of assigning [frequencies](wikipedia://en/10779) to the [transmitters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmitter) in some optimal manner and with no [interferences](wikipedia://en/15112). [Chartrand](wikipedia://en/22782409) et al.. introduced the concept of [radio $k$-coloring](wikipedia://en/47160384) of a [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) [simple graph](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/SimpleGraph.html) in 2005. Then (2012, 2015) sharp lower bounds on [radio $k$-chromatic number](wikipedia://en/47160384) of [connected graphs](wikipedia://en/1614492) were provided in terms of a newly defined parameter called **triameter** of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806). Apart from this, the concept of **triameter** also finds application in metric [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471).

In 2014, Henning and Yeo proved a [Graffiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti_%28program%29) conjecture on lower bound of [total domination number](wikipedia://en/1747972#Variants) of a [connected graph](wikipedia://en/1614492) in terms of its **triameter**. Saha and Panigrahi denoted this parameter as $M$-value of a graph in their paper.

The concept of **triameter** was first formally introduced in 2021 and studied by [A. Das](https://sites.google.com/site/angsumancrypto/home). He investigated its connections to other [graph parameters](wikipedia://en/1655142) such as [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341), [radius](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/GraphRadius.html), [girth](wikipedia://en/12013), and [domination numbers](wikipedia://en/1747972). Building on this foundation, A. Hak, S. Kozerenko and B. Oliynyk extended the study in 2022 exploring an interplay between **triameter** and [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) for some and establishing a tight lower bound for **triameter** of [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) in terms of their order and number of leaves.

Recently, K. Jeya Daisy, S. Nihisha, and P. Jeyanthi, linked **triameter** to the [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), they studied **triameter** of the [zero-divisor graph](wikipedia://en/57837828) of a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) with [identity](wikipedia://en/298428).

## Metric properties {#Metric_properties}

The metric properties of **triameter** were first studied by A. Das. The **triameter** of any [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) graph $G$ is tightly bounded by its [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) and [radius](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/GraphRadius.html) in the following way:

$$\begin{aligned} 2 \mathop{\mathrm{diam}}(G) \leq &\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(G) \leq 3 \mathop{\mathrm{diam}}(G),\\ 2 \mathop{\mathrm{rad}}(G) \leq &\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(G) \leq 6 \mathop{\mathrm{rad}}(G). \end{aligned}$$

### Bounds for trees {#Bounds_for_trees}

*Image: Tree representing tight lower triameter bound for n=17, l=5.svg.* A tree *T* with tr(*T*) = 20 representing tight lower triameter bound for *n*=17, *l*=5.

For the [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) tighter bounds hold:

$$4 \mathop{\mathrm{rad}}(G) - 2 \leq \mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(G) \leq 6 \mathop{\mathrm{rad}}(G),$$

If $G$ is a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) with more than $2$ leaves, then the stronger lower bound $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(G) \geq 4\mathop{\mathrm{rad}}(G)$ holds. For any connected graph $G$ with $n \geq 3$ vertices the lower bound $\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(G) \leq 2n - 2$ takes place. Moreover, the equality holds if and only if $G$ is a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) with $2$ or $3$ leaves.

The general tight lower bound for any given pair $(n, l)$ is known. Let $T$ be a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) with $n\geq 4$ vertices and $l\geq 3$ leaves, then the following holds:

$$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(T)\geq 6 \left\lfloor{\frac{n-1}{l}}+2\min\{(n-1)\bmod{l},3\}\right\rfloor.$$

## Diameter–triameter interplay {#Diameter–triameter_interplay}

The key question is whether some relationships between [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) and **triameter** hold for various :

*Image: Triameter diameter interplay.svg.* The graph *G* has tr(*G*) = d(*u*,*v*,*w*) = 12 and diam(*G*) = d(*x*,*y*) = 5. The triametral triple *u*, *v*, *w* does not contain a diametral pair, and the diametral pair *x*, *y* cannot be extended to a triametral triple.

(TD) Any **triametral triple** in a graph contains a [diametral](wikipedia://en/2979341) pair. (DT) Any [diametral](wikipedia://en/2979341) pair in a graph can be extended to a **triametral triple**.

In fact, both (TD) and (DT) hold for [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) and [block graphs](wikipedia://en/29106883). Every pair of vertices (even not diametral) in a [symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/1577896) can be extended to a **triametral triple**, which implies (DT); however, the first property (TD) does not hold for them.

There are weaker versions of these properties:

(TD$'$) Any **triametral triple** contains a [peripheral](wikipedia://en/325802#peripheral) vertex. (D$'$T) Any [peripheral](wikipedia://en/325802#peripheral) vertex can be extended to a **triametral triple**.

Neither [modular graphs](wikipedia://en/51807881) nor [distance hereditary graphs](wikipedia://en/17315337) satisfy any of these properties, even weaker versions. For (D$'$T) counterexample see the graph on the left in the figure, in fact it is both [modular](wikipedia://en/51807881) and [distance hereditary](wikipedia://en/17315337). For (TD$'$) the [distance hereditary](wikipedia://en/17315337) counterexample is the graph on the right in the figure, and for the [modular](wikipedia://en/51807881) it is a [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{2,3}$.

*Image: A pair of distance-hereditary graphs being a counterexample to diameter--triameter interplay.svg.* A pair of [distance hereditary graphs](wikipedia://en/17315337) being a counterexample to diameter–triameter interplay. The triametral triple *u*, *v*, *w* of the graph on the left does not contain a peripheral vertex, while the peripheral vertex *x* of the graph on the right cannot be extended to a triametral triple. Both graphs are [distance hereditary](wikipedia://en/17315337), the graph on the left is also a [median graph](wikipedia://en/16970848).

Also, (TD) does not hold for the [hypercubes](wikipedia://en/6706815) and, consequently, [median graphs](wikipedia://en/16970848).

## Open problems {#Open_problems}

Several open problems regarding the triameter:

- Does (DT) hold for the [median graphs](wikipedia://en/16970848)? This is an interesting question because [median graphs](wikipedia://en/16970848) generalize [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) and [hypercubes](wikipedia://en/6706815), for which (DT) holds. A Meta-Conjecture, stated by H. Mulder , suggests that any (sensible) [property](wikipedia://en/1655142) shared by [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) and [hypercubes](wikipedia://en/6706815) is also shared by all [median graphs](wikipedia://en/16970848). Notably, none of the diameter–triameter properties (even weaker ones) holds for [modular graphs](wikipedia://en/51807881), which generalize [median graphs](wikipedia://en/16970848).

Additionally, one could investigate if (D$'$T) or (TD$'$) hold for [median graphs](wikipedia://en/16970848).

- Can better lower **triameter** bounds be established in terms of the maximum $\Delta(G)$ and minimum degree $\sigma(G)$?
