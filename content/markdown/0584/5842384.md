# Katětov–Tong insertion theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Katětov–Tong insertion theorem"}

The **Katětov–Tong insertion theorem**[^o2][^o3][^o4] is a theorem of [point-set topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-set_topology) proved independently by [Miroslav Katětov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miroslav_Katětov) and [Hing Tong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hing_Tong) in the 1950s. The theorem states the following:

:::

Let $X$ be a [normal](wikipedia://en/48629) [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) and let $g, h\colon X \to \mathbb{R}$ be functions with $g$ upper [semicontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semicontinuous), $h$ lower semicontinuous, and $g \leq h$. Then there exists a continuous function $f\colon X \to \mathbb{R}$ with $g \leq f \leq h.$

This theorem has a number of applications and is the first of many classical insertion theorems. In particular it implies the [Tietze extension theorem](wikipedia://en/31404) and consequently [Urysohn's lemma](wikipedia://en/49253), and so the conclusion of the theorem is equivalent to normality.
