# Walter Rudin

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Walter Rudin"}

**Walter Rudin** (May 2, 1921 – May 20, 2010[^o4]) was an [Austrian-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria)[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and professor of mathematics at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison).[^o5]

:::

In addition to his contributions to [complex](wikipedia://en/5759) and [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), Rudin was known for his [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) textbooks: *[Principles of Mathematical Analysis](wikipedia://en/70241079)*,[^o6] *Real and Complex Analysis*,[^o7] and *Functional Analysis.*[^o8] Rudin wrote *Principles of Mathematical Analysis* only two years after obtaining his Ph.D. from Duke University, while he was a [C. L. E. Moore Instructor](wikipedia://en/21957255) at MIT. *Principles*, acclaimed for its elegance and clarity,[^o25] has since become a standard textbook for introductory real analysis courses in the United States.[^o9]

Rudin's analysis textbooks have also been influential in mathematical education worldwide, having been translated into 13 languages, including Russian,[^o10] Chinese,[^o11] and Spanish.[^o12]

## Biography {#Biography}

Rudin was born into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews) family in [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Austria_%281919–1934%29) in 1921. He was enrolled for a period of time at a Swiss boarding school, the [Institut auf dem Rosenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_auf_dem_Rosenberg), where he was part of a small program that prepared its students for entry to British universities.[^o14] His family fled to [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic) after the [Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss) in 1938.

When France [surrendered to Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_22_June_1940) in 1940, Rudin fled to England and served in the [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy) for the rest of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), after which he left for the United States. He obtained both his B.A. in 1947 and Ph.D. in 1949 from [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University). After his Ph.D., he was a [C. L. E. Moore instructor](wikipedia://en/21957255) at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT). He briefly taught at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester) before becoming a professor at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) where he remained for 32 years.[^o4] His research interests ranged from [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) to [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).

In 1970 Rudin was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Nice.[^o15] He was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Mathematical Exposition in 1993 for authorship of the now classic analysis texts, *Principles of Mathematical Analysis* and *Real and Complex Analysis.* He received an honorary degree from the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) in 2006.

In 1953, he married fellow mathematician [Mary Ellen Estill](wikipedia://en/2976742), known for her work in [set-theoretic topology](wikipedia://en/3665974). The two resided in [Madison, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin), in the eponymous [Walter Rudin House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Rudin_House), a home designed by architect [Frank Lloyd Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lloyd_Wright). They had four children.[^o2]

Rudin died on May 20, 2010, after suffering from [Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson's_disease).[^o4]
