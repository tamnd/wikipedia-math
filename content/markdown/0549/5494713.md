# Stable manifold theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stable manifold theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the study of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) and [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), the **stable manifold theorem** is an important result about the structure of the set of [orbits](wikipedia://en/252250) approaching a given [hyperbolic fixed point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_fixed_point). It roughly states that the existence of a [local diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/1728483) near a fixed point implies the existence of a local stable [center manifold](wikipedia://en/3948656) containing that fixed point. This manifold has dimension equal to the number of [eigenvalues](wikipedia://en/2161429) of the [Jacobian matrix](wikipedia://en/195351) of the fixed point that are less than 1.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Stable manifold theorem"}

## Stable manifold theorem {#Stable_manifold_theorem}

Let

$$f: U \subset \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$$

be a [smooth map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_map) with hyperbolic fixed point at $p$. We denote by $W^{s}(p)$ the [stable set](wikipedia://en/5466649) and by $W^{u}(p)$ the [unstable set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unstable_set) of $p$.

The theorem[^o5][^o6][^o7] states that

- $W^{s}(p)$ is a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) and its [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) has the same dimension as the [stable space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_space) of the [linearization](wikipedia://en/1118789) of $f$ at $p$.
- $W^{u}(p)$ is a smooth manifold and its tangent space has the same dimension as the [unstable space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unstable_space) of the linearization of $f$ at $p$.

Accordingly $W^{s}(p)$ is a **[stable manifold](wikipedia://en/5466649)** and $W^{u}(p)$ is an **[unstable manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unstable_manifold)**.
