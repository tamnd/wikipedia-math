# Bidiagonalization

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bidiagonalization"}

**Bidiagonalization** is one of unitary (orthogonal) [matrix decompositions](wikipedia://en/253873) such that **U**\* **A** **V** = **B**, where **U** and **V** are [unitary](wikipedia://en/129222) ([orthogonal](wikipedia://en/105620)) matrices; \* denotes [Hermitian transpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_transpose); and **B** is upper [bidiagonal](wikipedia://en/1451250). **A** is allowed to be rectangular.

:::

For [dense matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_matrix), the left and right unitary matrices are obtained by a series of [Householder reflections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Householder_reflection) alternately applied from the left and right. This is known as Golub-Kahan bidiagonalization. For large matrices, they are calculated iteratively by using [Lanczos method](wikipedia://en/2593852), referred to as Golub-Kahan-Lanczos method.

Bidiagonalization has a very similar structure to the [singular value decomposition](wikipedia://en/142207) (SVD). However, it is computed within finite operations, while SVD requires iterative schemes to find singular values. The latter is because the squared singular values are the roots of [characteristic polynomials](wikipedia://en/218268) of **A**\* **A**, where **A** is assumed to be tall.
