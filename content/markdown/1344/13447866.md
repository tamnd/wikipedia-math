# EpiData

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="EpiData"}

**EpiData** is a group of applications used in combination for creating documented data structures and analysis of [quantitative data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_data).[^o3]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The EpiData Association, which created the software, was created in 1999 and is based in [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark). EpiData was developed in [Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_%28programming_language%29) and uses open standards such as [HTML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTML) where possible.

EpiData is widely used by organizations and individuals to create and analyze large amounts of data. The [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization) (WHO) uses EpiData in its STEPS method of collecting [epidemiological](wikipedia://en/66997), medical, and [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health) data, for [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878), and for other quantitative-based projects.

Epicentre, the research wing of [Médecins Sans Frontières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Médecins_Sans_Frontières), uses EpiData to manage data from its international research studies and field epidemiology studies. E.g.: Piola P, Fogg C et al.: Supervised versus unsupervised intake of six-dose artemether-lumefantrine for treatment of acute, uncomplicated Plasmodium falciparum malaria in Mbarara, Uganda: a randomised trial. Lancet. 2005 Apr 23–29;365(9469):1467-73 '15850630'. Other examples: '16765397', '15569777' or '17160135'.

EpiData has two parts:

- Epidata Entry – used for simple or programmed data entry and data documentation. It handles simple forms or related systems
- EpiData Analysis – performs basic statistical analysis, graphs, and comprehensive data management, such as recoding data, label values and variables, and basic statistics. This application can create [control charts](wikipedia://en/435754), such as [pareto charts](wikipedia://en/4060171) or [p-charts](wikipedia://en/3135066), and many other methods to visualize and describe statistical data.

The software is free; development is funded by governmental and non-governmental organizations like WHO.
