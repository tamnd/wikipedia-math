# Hexagonal bipyramid

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hexagonal bipyramid"}

A **hexagonal bipyramid** is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) formed from two hexagonal [pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045) joined at their bases. The resulting solid has 12 triangular [faces](wikipedia://en/149926), 8 [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) and 18 edges. The 12 faces are identical [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428).

:::

Although it is [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive), it is not a [Platonic solid](wikipedia://en/23905) because some vertices have four faces meeting and others have six faces, and it is not a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034) because its faces cannot be [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285); 6 equilateral triangles would make a flat vertex.

It is one of an infinite set of [bipyramids](wikipedia://en/4153). Having twelve faces, it is a type of [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407), although that name is usually associated with the [regular polyhedral](wikipedia://en/333299) form with pentagonal faces.

The hexagonal bipyramid has a [plane of symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_of_symmetry) (which is [horizontal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_plane) in the figure to the right) where the bases of the two pyramids are joined. This plane is a regular [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733). There are also six planes of symmetry crossing through the two [apices](wikipedia://en/6022079). These planes are [rhombic](wikipedia://en/183884) and lie at 30° [angles](wikipedia://en/1196) to each other, [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to the horizontal plane.

The *hexagonal bipyramid*, dt{2,6}, can be in sequence [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366), rdt{2,6}, [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429), trdt{2,6} and alternated ([snubbed](wikipedia://en/9428917)), srdt{2,6}:

*Image: Snub rectified hexagonal bipyramid sequence.png.*

It is the first polyhedra in a sequence defined by the [face configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration) *V4.6.2n*. This group is special for having all even number of edges per vertex and form bisecting planes through the polyhedra and infinite lines in the plane, and continuing into the hyperbolic plane for any $n \ge 7.$

With an even number of faces at every vertex, these polyhedra and tilings can be shown by alternating two colors so all adjacent faces have different colors.

Each face on these domains also corresponds to the fundamental domain of a [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) with order 2,3,n mirrors at each triangle face vertex.
