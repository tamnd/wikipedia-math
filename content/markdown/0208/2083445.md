# Going up and going down

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Going up and going down"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **going up** and **going down** are terms which refer to certain properties of [chains](wikipedia://en/30330#Chains) of [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) in [integral extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_extension).

:::

The phrase **going up** refers to the case when a chain can be extended by "upward [inclusion](wikipedia://en/27631)", while **going down** refers to the case when a chain can be extended by "downward inclusion".

The major results are the **Cohen–Seidenberg theorems**, which were [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by [Irvin S. Cohen](wikipedia://en/10051038) and [Abraham Seidenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Seidenberg). These are known as the **going-up** and **going-down theorems**.

## Going up and going down {#Going_up_and_going_down}

Let *A* ⊆ *B* be an [extension of commutative rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_extension).

The going-up and going-down theorems give sufficient conditions for a chain of prime ideals in *B*, each member of which lies over members of a longer chain of prime ideals in *A*, to be able to be extended to the length of the chain of prime ideals in *A*.

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Incomparability property (commutative algebra)"}

### Lying over and incomparability {#Lying_over_and_incomparability}

First, we fix some terminology. If $\mathfrak{p}$ and $\mathfrak{q}$ are [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) of *A* and *B*, respectively, such that

$$\mathfrak{q} \cap A = \mathfrak{p}$$

(note that $\mathfrak{q} \cap A$ is automatically a prime ideal of *A*) then we say that $\mathfrak{p}$ *lies under* $\mathfrak{q}$ and that $\mathfrak{q}$ *lies over* $\mathfrak{p}$. In general, a ring extension *A* ⊆ *B* of [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346) is said to satisfy the **lying over property** if every prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of *A* lies under some prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ of *B*.

The extension *A* ⊆ *B* is said to satisfy the **incomparability property** if whenever $\mathfrak{q}$ and $\mathfrak{q}'$ are distinct primes of *B* lying over a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ in *A*, then $\mathfrak{q}$ ⊈ $\mathfrak{q}'$ and $\mathfrak{q}'$ ⊈ $\mathfrak{q}$.

:::

### Going-up {#Going-up}

The ring extension *A* ⊆ *B* is said to satisfy the **going-up property** if whenever

$$\mathfrak{p}_1 \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_2 \subseteq \!\!\;\cdots\cdots\cdots\!\!\, \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_n$$

is a chain of prime ideals of *A* and

$$\mathfrak{q}_1 \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_2 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_m$$

is a chain of prime ideals of *B* with *m* \< *n* and such that $\mathfrak{q}_i$ lies over $\mathfrak{p}_i$ for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *m*, then the latter chain can be extended to a chain

$$\mathfrak{q}_1 \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_2 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_m \subseteq \cdots \subseteq \mathfrak{q}_n$$

such that $\mathfrak{q}_i$ lies over $\mathfrak{p}_i$ for each 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n*.

In (Kaplansky 1970) it is shown that if an extension *A* ⊆ *B* satisfies the going-up property, then it also satisfies the lying-over property.

### Going-down {#Going-down}

The ring extension *A* ⊆ *B* is said to satisfy the **going-down property** if whenever

$$\mathfrak{p}_1 \supseteq \mathfrak{p}_2 \supseteq \!\!\;\cdots\cdots\cdots\!\!\, \supseteq \mathfrak{p}_n$$

is a chain of prime ideals of *A* and

$$\mathfrak{q}_1 \supseteq \mathfrak{q}_2 \supseteq \cdots \supseteq \mathfrak{q}_m$$

is a chain of prime ideals of *B* with *m* \< *n* and such that $\mathfrak{q}_i$ lies over $\mathfrak{p}_i$ for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *m*, then the latter chain can be extended to a chain

$$\mathfrak{q}_1 \supseteq \mathfrak{q}_2 \supseteq \cdots \supseteq \mathfrak{q}_m \supseteq \cdots \supseteq \mathfrak{q}_n$$

such that $\mathfrak{q}_i$ lies over $\mathfrak{p}_i$ for each 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n*.

There is a generalization of the ring extension case with ring morphisms. Let *f* : *A* → *B* be a (unital) [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) so that *B* is a ring extension of *f*(*A*). Then *f* is said to satisfy the **going-up property** if the going-up property holds for *f*(*A*) in *B*.

Similarly, if *B* is a ring extension of *f*(*A*), then *f* is said to satisfy the **going-down property** if the going-down property holds for *f*(*A*) in *B*.

In the case of ordinary ring extensions such as *A* ⊆ *B*, the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516) is the pertinent map.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Going-up and going-down theorems"}

## Going-up and going-down theorems {#Going-up_and_going-down_theorems}

The usual statements of going-up and going-down theorems refer to a ring extension *A* ⊆ *B*:

1. (Going up) If *B* is an [integral extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_extension) of *A*, then the extension satisfies the going-up property (and hence the lying over property), and the incomparability property.
1. (Going down) If *B* is an integral extension of *A*, and *B* is a domain, and *A* is integrally closed in its field of fractions, then the extension (in addition to going-up, lying-over and incomparability) satisfies the going-down property.

There is another sufficient condition for the going-down property:

- If *A* ⊆ *B* is a [flat extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_extension) of commutative rings, then the going-down property holds.[^o10]

*Proof*:[^o11] Let *p*<sub>1</sub> ⊆ *p*<sub>2</sub> be prime ideals of *A* and let *q*<sub>2</sub> be a prime ideal of *B* such that *q*<sub>2</sub> ∩ *A* = *p*<sub>2</sub>. We wish to prove that there is a prime ideal *q*<sub>1</sub> of *B* contained in *q*<sub>2</sub> such that *q*<sub>1</sub> ∩ *A* = *p*<sub>1</sub>. Since *A* ⊆ *B* is a flat extension of rings, it follows that *A*<sub>*p*<sub>2</sub></sub> ⊆ *B*<sub>*q*<sub>2</sub></sub> is a flat extension of rings. In fact, *A*<sub>*p*<sub>2</sub></sub> ⊆ *B*<sub>*q*<sub>2</sub></sub> is a faithfully flat extension of rings since the inclusion map *A*<sub>*p*<sub>2</sub></sub> → *B*<sub>*q*<sub>2</sub></sub> is a local homomorphism. Therefore, the induced map on spectra Spec(*B*<sub>*q*<sub>2</sub></sub>) → Spec(*A*<sub>*p*<sub>2</sub></sub>) is [surjective](wikipedia://en/27873) and there exists a prime ideal of *B*<sub>*q*<sub>2</sub></sub> that contracts to the prime ideal *p*<sub>1</sub>*A*<sub>*p*<sub>2</sub></sub> of *A*<sub>*p*<sub>2</sub></sub>. The contraction of this prime ideal of *B*<sub>*q*<sub>2</sub></sub> to *B* is a prime ideal *q*<sub>1</sub> of *B* contained in *q*<sub>2</sub> that contracts to *p*<sub>1</sub>. The proof is complete. **Q.E.D.**

:::
