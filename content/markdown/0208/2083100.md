# Isogonal conjugate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isogonal conjugate"}

*Image: Isogonal\_Conjugate.svg.* Construction of an isogonal conjugate $P*$ for an arbitrary triangle $\triangle ABC$. solid lime solid blue solid red

*Image: Isogonal\_Conjugate\_transform.svg.* Isogonal conjugate transformation over the points inside the triangle.

:::

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **isogonal conjugate** of a [point](wikipedia://en/593693) $P$ with respect to a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) $\triangle ABC$ is constructed by [reflecting](wikipedia://en/295829) the lines $PA$, $PB$, $PC$ about the [angle bisectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_bisectors) of $A$, $B$, $C$ respectively. These three reflected lines [concur](wikipedia://en/2206157) at the isogonal conjugate of $P$. (This definition applies only to points not on a [sideline](wikipedia://en/43958459) of triangle $\triangle ABC$.) This is a direct result of the trigonometric form of [Ceva's theorem](wikipedia://en/152428).

The isogonal conjugate of a point $P$ is sometimes denoted by $P*$. The isogonal conjugate of $P*$ is $P$.

The isogonal conjugate of the [incentre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incentre) $I$ is itself. The isogonal conjugate of the [orthocentre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthocentre) $H$ is the [circumcentre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcentre) $O$. The isogonal conjugate of the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) $G$ is (by definition) the [symmedian point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmedian_point) $K$. The isogonal conjugates of the [Fermat points](wikipedia://en/2666205) are the [isodynamic points](wikipedia://en/5102885) and vice versa. The [Brocard points](wikipedia://en/7029586) are isogonal conjugates of each other.

In [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790), if $X = x : y : z$ is a point not on a sideline of triangle $\triangle ABC$, then its isogonal conjugate is

$$X^* = X^{-1} = \frac{1}{x} : \frac{1}{y} : \frac{1}{z}.$$

Because the conjugate coordinates are [reciprocals](wikipedia://en/229940), the isogonal conjugate of $X$ is sometimes denoted by $X^{-1}$. The [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $S$ of triangle centers under the trilinear product, defined by

$$(p:q:r)*(u:v:w) = pu:qv:rw,$$

is a [commutative group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_group), and the inverse of each $X$ in $S$ is $X^{-1}$.

As isogonal conjugation is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427), it makes sense to speak of the isogonal conjugate of sets of points, such as lines and circles. For example, the isogonal conjugate of a line is a [circumconic](wikipedia://en/9610491); specifically, an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277), [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231), or [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052) according as the line intersects the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) in 0, 1, or 2 points. The isogonal conjugate of the circumcircle is the [line at infinity](wikipedia://en/403142). Several well-known [cubics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_plane_curve) (e.g., [Thompson cubic](wikipedia://en/12267919), Darboux cubic, [Neuberg cubic](wikipedia://en/69415319)) are self-isogonal-conjugate, in the sense that if $X$ is on the cubic, then $X^{-1}$ is also on the cubic.

## Another construction for the isogonal conjugate of a point {#Another_construction_for_the_isogonal_conjugate_of_a_point}

*Image: A Second Definition Of Isogonal Conjugate.png.* A second definition of isogonal conjugate

For a given point $P$ in the plane of triangle $\triangle ABC$, let the reflections of $P$ in the sidelines $BC, CA, AB$ be $P_{a}, P_{b}, P_{c}$. Then the center of the circle $\text{〇}P_{a}P_{b}P_{c}$ is the isogonal conjugate of $P$.[^o3]
