# Wireworld

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wireworld"}

*Image: Wireworld two-diodes.gif.* 2 Wireworld [diodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diode), the above one in conduction direction, the lower one in reverse-biasing

**Wireworld**, alternatively **WireWorld**, is a [cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/54342) first proposed by [Brian Silverman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Silverman) in 1987, as part of his program Phantom Fish Tank. It subsequently became more widely known as a result of an article in the "Computer Recreations" column of *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)*.[^o2] Wireworld is particularly suited to simulating [transistors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor), and is [Turing-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing-complete).

:::

## Rules {#Rules}

*Image: Animated display.gif.* Example of a complicated circuit made in WireWorld: a [seven-segment display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven-segment_display) and decoder. Conductor cells are dark green to highlight signal flow and display segments.

A Wireworld cell can be in one of four different states, usually numbered 0–3 in software, modeled by colors in the examples here:

empty (black), electron head (blue), electron tail (red), conductor (yellow).

As in all cellular automata, time proceeds in discrete steps called generations (sometimes "gens" or "ticks"). Cells behave as follows:

- empty → empty,
- electron head → electron tail,
- electron tail → conductor,
- conductor → electron head if exactly one or two of the neighbouring cells are electron heads, otherwise remains conductor.

Wireworld uses what is called the [Moore neighborhood](wikipedia://en/4323874), which means that in the rules above, neighbouring means one cell away (range value of one) in any direction, both orthogonal and diagonal.

These simple rules can be used to construct [logic gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_gate) (see below).

*Image: Wireworld XOR-gate.gif.* 2 [clock generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock_generator) sending electrons into an [XOR gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XOR_gate)

## Applications {#Applications}

Entities built within Wireworld universes include [Langton's ant](wikipedia://en/105016) (allowing any Langton's ant pattern to be built within Wireworld)[^o6] and the Wireworld computer, a [Turing-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing-complete) computer implemented as a cellular automaton.[^o7]
