# Carl R. de Boor

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Carl R. de Boor"}

**Carl-Wilhelm Reinhold de Boor** (born 3 December 1937) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and professor emeritus at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison).[^o3] In 1993, he was elected as a member of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) for contributions to numerical analysis and methods in particular numerical tools used in [computer-aided design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_design).[^o4]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

**de Boor** was born in [Stolp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolp), Germany (now [Słupsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Słupsk), Poland), in 1937, as the seventh of eight children born to Werner (an anti-Nazi Lutheran minister) and Toni de Boor.[^o6] The family fled in 1945, settling in [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin), then part of [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany).[^o3] He stayed in West Germany when his application to study chemistry at [Humboldt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University) in [East Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Berlin) was turned down.[^o7] He was supported by [Otto Friedrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Friedrich), the brother of Carl's father's first wife. Two years later, he began a romantic relationship with Otto's niece, Matilda Friedrich, the daughter of political scientist [Carl Friedrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich).[^o3] With the support of the Friedrich family, Carl emigrated to the United States in 1959, learning English on his trip across the Atlantic.[^o7]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

*Image: Carl de Boor - National Medal of Science, 2005.webm.* de Boor discussing his life and career.

Having left [Hamburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_University) without a degree, de Boor enrolled as a mathematics graduate student at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). He worked briefly as a research assistant to [Garrett Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/1180916) before joining [General Motors Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors_Research_Laboratories) in [Warren, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren,_Michigan), where he first encountered [splines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splines).[^o9] He earned his Ph.D. from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1966, then joined [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) as an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor). In 1972, he moved to the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) as a professor of mathematics and computer science, based at the Army Math Research Center.[^o10]

## Retirement and personal life {#Retirement_and_personal_life}

Carl de Boor retired from the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) in 2003 and relocated to [Orcas Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orcas_Island) in [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_%28state%29) state, with his second wife, author [Helen Bee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Bee), who he married in 1991.[^o6] He is also an affiliated professor at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington).[^o12]

## Awards {#Awards}

De Boor was elected to the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1997, and received the 2003 [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).[^o14] Other honors have included election to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1987[^o15] and the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) in 1993,[^o16] honorary degrees from [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) and [Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion) (the Israel Institute of Technology), as well as membership in the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina) in Germany and the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o17] He won the [John von Neumann Lecture Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann_Prize) from the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) in 1996 and the [John A. Gregory Award of Geometric Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._Gregory_Award_of_Geometric_Design) in 2009.[^o18]
