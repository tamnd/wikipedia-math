# Steiner's conic problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Steiner's conic problem"}

In [enumerative geometry](wikipedia://en/3069092), **Steiner's conic problem** is the problem of finding the number of smooth [conics](wikipedia://en/19008673) tangent to five given conics in the plane [in general position](wikipedia://en/383736). If the problem is considered in the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155) **CP**<sup>2</sup>, the correct solution is 3264.[^o2] The problem is named after [Jakob Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445) who first posed it and who gave an incorrect solution in 1848.[^o3]

:::

## History {#History}

Steiner claimed that the number of conics tangent to 5 given conics in general position is 7776 = 6<sup>5</sup>, but later realized this was wrong.[^o5] The correct number 3264 was found in about 1859 by [Ernest de Jonquières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_de_Jonquières) who did not publish because of Steiner's reputation, and by Chasles using his theory of characteristics,[^o6] and by Berner in 1865. However these results, like many others in classical [intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602), do not seem to have been given complete proofs until the work of [Fulton](wikipedia://en/9642149) and [MacPherson](wikipedia://en/10238121) in about 1978.[^n1]

## Formulation and solution {#Formulation_and_solution}

The space of (possibly degenerate) conics in the complex projective plane **CP**<sup>2</sup> can be identified with the [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977) **CP**<sup>5</sup> (since each conic is defined by a homogeneous degree-2 polynomial in three variables, with 6 complex coefficients, and multiplying such a polynomial by a non-zero [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) does not change the conic). Steiner observed that the conics tangent to a given conic form a degree 6 [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) in **CP**<sup>5</sup>. So the conics tangent to 5 given conics correspond to the intersection points of 5 degree 6 hypersurfaces, and by [Bézout's theorem](wikipedia://en/149217) the number of intersection points of 5 generic degree 6 hypersurfaces is 6<sup>5</sup> = 7776, which was Steiner's incorrect solution. The reason this is wrong is that the five degree 6 hypersurfaces are not in general position and have a common intersection in the [Veronese surface](wikipedia://en/1569217), corresponding to the set of double lines in the plane, all of which have double intersection points with the 5 conics. In particular the intersection of these 5 hypersurfaces is not even 0-dimensional but has a 2-dimensional component. So to find the correct answer, one has to somehow eliminate the plane of spurious degenerate conics from this calculation.[^n2]

One way of eliminating the degenerate conics is to [blow up](wikipedia://en/1817790) **CP**<sup>5</sup> along the Veronese surface.[^n3] The [Chow ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_ring) of the blowup is generated by *H* and *E*, where *H* is the total transform of a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) and *E* is the [exceptional divisor](wikipedia://en/14271694). The total transform of a degree 6 hypersurface is 6*H*, and Steiner calculated (6*H*)<sup>5</sup> = 6<sup>5</sup>*P* as *H*<sup>5</sup>=*P* (where *P* is the class of a point in the Chow ring). However the number of conics is not (6*H*)<sup>5</sup> but (6*H*−2*E*)<sup>5</sup> because the strict transform of the hypersurface of conics tangent to a given conic is 6*H*−2*E*.

Suppose that *L* = 2*H*−*E* is the strict transform of the conics tangent to a given line. Then the intersection numbers of *H* and *L* are given by *H*<sup>5</sup>=1*P*, *H*<sup>4</sup>*L*=2*P*, *H*<sup>3</sup>*L*<sup>2</sup>=4*P*, *H*<sup>2</sup>*L*<sup>3</sup>=4*P*, *H*<sup>1</sup>*L*<sup>4</sup>=2*P*, *L*<sup>5</sup>=1*P*. So we have (6*H*−2*E*)<sup>5</sup> = (2*H*+2*L*)<sup>5</sup> = 3264*P*.

Fulton & MacPherson gave a precise description of exactly what "general position" means (although their two propositions about this are not quite right, and are corrected in a note on page 29 of their paper).[^o11] If the five conics have the properties that

- there is no line such that every one of the 5 conics is either tangent to it or passes through one of two fixed points on it (otherwise there is a "double line with 2 marked points" tangent to all 5 conics)
- no three of the conics pass through any point (otherwise there is a "double line with 2 marked points" tangent to all 5 conics passing through this triple intersection point)
- no two of the conics are tangent
- no three of the five conics are tangent to a line
- a pair of lines each tangent to two of the conics do not intersect on the fifth conic (otherwise this pair is a [degenerate conic](wikipedia://en/392586) tangent to all 5 conics)

then the total number of conics *C* tangent to all 5 (counted with multiplicities) is 3264. Here the multiplicity is given by the product over all 5 conics *C*<sub>*i*</sub> of (4 − number of intersection points of *C* and *C*<sub>*i*</sub>). In particular if *C* intersects each of the five conics in exactly 3 points (one double point of tangency and two others) then the multiplicity is 1, and if this condition always holds then there are exactly 3264 conics tangent to the 5 given conics.

Over other algebraically closed fields the answer is similar, unless the field has [characteristic 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_2) in which case the number of conics is 51 rather than 3264.
