# Oka–Weil theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Oka–Weil theorem"}

In mathematics, especially the theory of [several complex variables](wikipedia://en/360243), the **Oka–Weil theorem** is a result about the [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652) of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) on [Stein spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stein_space) due to [Kiyoshi Oka](wikipedia://en/381978) and [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The Oka–Weil theorem states that if *X* is a Stein space and *K* is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) $\mathcal{O}(X)$-convex subset of *X*, then every holomorphic function in an [open neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_neighborhood) of *K* can be approximated uniformly on *K* by holomorphic functions on $\mathcal{O}(X)$ (in particular, by polynomials).[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Since [Runge's theorem](wikipedia://en/3199591) may not hold for several complex variables, the Oka–Weil theorem is often used as an approximation theorem for several complex variables. The [Behnke–Stein theorem](wikipedia://en/21663799) was originally proved using the Oka–Weil theorem.

:::
