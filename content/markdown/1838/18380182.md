# Essential range

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Essential range"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), the **essential range**, or the set of **essential values**, of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) is intuitively the 'non-negligible' [range of the function](wikipedia://en/275991): It does not change between two functions that are equal [almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688). One way of thinking of the essential range of a function is the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) on which the range of the function is 'concentrated'.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $(X,{\cal A},\mu)$ be a [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270), and let $(Y,{\cal T})$ be a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). For any $({\cal A},\sigma({\cal T}))$-[measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) $f:X\to Y$, we say the **essential range** of $f$ to mean the set

$\operatorname{ess.im}(f) = \left\{y\in Y\mid0<\mu(f^{-1}(U))\text{ for all }U\in{\cal T} \text{ with } y \in U\right\}.$[^o4][^o5][^o6]

Equivalently, $\operatorname{ess.im}(f)=\operatorname{supp}(f_*\mu)$, where $f_*\mu$ is the [pushforward measure](wikipedia://en/9412979) onto $\sigma({\cal T})$ of $\mu$ under $f$ and $\operatorname{supp}(f_*\mu)$ denotes the [support](wikipedia://en/6591796) of $f_*\mu.$[^o7]

### Essential values {#Essential_values}

The phrase "**essential value** of $f$" is sometimes used to mean an element of the essential range of $f.$[^o9][^o10]

:::

## Special cases of common interest {#Special_cases_of_common_interest}

### *Y* = **C** {#Y_=_C}

Say $(Y,{\cal T})$ is $\mathbb C$ equipped with its usual topology. Then the essential range of *f* is given by

$\operatorname{ess.im}(f) = \left\{z \in \mathbb{C} \mid \text{for all}\ \varepsilon\in\mathbb R_{>0}: 0<\mu\{x\in X: |f(x) - z| < \varepsilon\}\right\}.$[^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16][^o17]

In other words: The essential range of a complex-valued function is the set of all complex numbers *z* such that the inverse image of each ε-neighbourhood of *z* under *f* has positive measure.

### (*Y*,*T*) is discrete {#(Y,T)_is_discrete}

Say $(Y,{\cal T})$ is [discrete](wikipedia://en/56061), i.e., ${\cal T}={\cal P}(Y)$ is the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of $Y,$ i.e., the [discrete topology](wikipedia://en/56061#Definition) on $Y.$ Then the essential range of *f* is the set of values *y* in *Y* with strictly positive $f_*\mu$-measure:

$\operatorname{ess.im}(f)=\{y\in Y:0<\mu(f^\text{pre}\{y\})\}=\{y\in Y:0<(f_*\mu)\{y\}\}.$[^o19][^o20][^o21]

::: {#o23 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The essential range of a measurable function, being the [support of a measure](wikipedia://en/6591796), is always closed.
- The essential range ess.im(f) of a measurable function is always a subset of $\overline{\operatorname{im}(f)}$.
- The essential image cannot be used to distinguish functions that are almost everywhere equal: If $f=g$ holds $\mu$-[almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688), then $\operatorname{ess.im}(f)=\operatorname{ess.im}(g)$.
- These two facts characterise the essential image: It is the biggest set contained in the closures of $\operatorname{im}(g)$ for all g that are a.e. equal to f:

$$\operatorname{ess.im}(f) = \bigcap_{f=g\,\text{a.e.}} \overline{\operatorname{im}(g)}.$$

- The essential range satisfies $\forall A\subseteq X: f(A) \cap \operatorname{ess.im}(f) = \emptyset \implies \mu(A) = 0$.
- This fact characterises the essential image: It is the *smallest* closed subset of $\mathbb{C}$ with this property.
- The [essential supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_supremum) of a real valued function equals the supremum of its essential image and the essential infimum equals the infimum of its essential range. Consequently, a function is essentially bounded if and only if its essential range is bounded.
- The essential range of an essentially bounded function f is equal to the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137#Spectrum_of_a_unital_Banach_algebra) $\sigma(f)$ where f is considered as an element of the [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) $L^\infty(\mu)$.

:::

::: {#o25 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If $\mu$ is the zero measure, then the essential image of all measurable functions is empty.
- This also illustrates that even though the essential range of a function is a subset of the closure of the range of that function, equality of the two sets need not hold.
- If $X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ is open, $f:X\to\mathbb{C}$ continuous and $\mu$ the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198), then $\operatorname{ess.im}(f)=\overline{\operatorname{im}(f)}$ holds. This holds more generally for all [Borel measures](wikipedia://en/4322) that assign non-zero measure to every non-empty open set.

:::

::: {#o27 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extension"}

## Extension {#Extension}

The notion of essential range can be extended to the case of $f : X \to Y$, where $Y$ is a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018). If $X$ and $Y$ are [differentiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219) of the same dimension, if $f\in$ [VMO](wikipedia://en/4694026#The_space_VMO)$(X, Y)$ and if $\operatorname{ess.im} (f) \ne Y$, then $\deg f = 0$.[^o28]

:::
