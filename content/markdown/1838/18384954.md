# Richard Swan

Richard Swan (disambiguation)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Swan"}

**Richard Gordon Swan** (; born 1933) is an American mathematician who is known for the [Serre–Swan theorem](wikipedia://en/363540) relating the geometric notion of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) to the algebraic concept of [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488),[^o3] and for the [Swan representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_representation), an [*l*-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) [projective representation](wikipedia://en/348860) of a [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673).[^o4] His work has mainly been in the area of [algebraic *K*-theory](wikipedia://en/598500).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

As an undergraduate at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), Swan was one of five winners in the [William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) in 1952.[^o6] He earned his Ph.D. in 1957 from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) under the supervision of [John Coleman Moore](wikipedia://en/13433862).

In 1969 he proved in full generality what is now known as the [Stallings–Swan theorem](wikipedia://en/9880197#Examples).[^o8][^o9] He is the Louis Block Professor Emeritus of Mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o10]

His doctoral students at Chicago include [Charles Weibel](wikipedia://en/39431320), also known for his work in *K*-theory.[^o7]

Together with [Otto Forster](wikipedia://en/68051547) he proved the [Forster–Swan theorem](wikipedia://en/78537564).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1970 Swan was awarded the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)'s [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in Algebra.
