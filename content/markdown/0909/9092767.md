# Alfred Kempe

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alfred Kempe"}

**Sir Alfred Bray Kempe** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (6 July 1849 – 21 April 1922) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best known for his work on [linkages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linkage_%28mechanical%29) and the [four colour theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_colour_theorem).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kempe was the son of the Rector of [St James's Church, Piccadilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_James's_Church,_Piccadilly), the Rev. John Edward Kempe. Among his brothers were [Sir John Arrow Kempe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Arrow_Kempe) and [Harry Robert Kempe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Robert_Kempe). He was educated at [St Paul's School, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Paul's_School,_London) and then studied at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) was one of his teachers. He graduated BA (22nd [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29)) in 1872. Despite his interest in mathematics he became a [barrister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrister), specialising in the [ecclesiastical law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_law). He was knighted in 1913, the same year he became the [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_%28ecclesiastical%29) for the [Diocese of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_London_%28England%29). He was also Chancellor of the dioceses of Newcastle, Southwell, St Albans, Peterborough, Chichester, and Chelmsford. He received the honorary degree [DCL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Canon_Law) from the [University of Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Durham) and he was elected a [Bencher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bencher) of the [Inner Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Temple) in 1909.

In 1876 he published his article *On a General Method of describing Plane Curves of the nth degree by Linkwork,*[^o5] which presented a procedure for constructing a linkage that traces an arbitrary algebraic [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691). This was a remarkable generalization of his work on the design of linkages to trace straight lines. This direct connection between [linkages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linkage_%28mechanical%29) and [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) is now called [Kempe's universality theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kempe's_universality_theorem).[^o6] While Kempe's proposed [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) was flawed, the first complete proof was provided in 2002, based on his ideas.[^o7][^o8]

*Image: Quadruplanar inversor of Sylvester and Kempe Alternate (animated).gif.* The Sylvester–Kempe Inversor draws a straight line.

In 1877 Kempe discovered a new [straight line linkage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_line_linkage) called the [Quadruplanar inversor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadruplanar_inversor) or *Sylvester–Kempe Inversor* and published his influential lectures on the subject.[^o9] In 1879 Kempe wrote his famous "proof" of the [four colour theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_colour_theorem), shown incorrect by [Percy Heawood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Heawood) in 1890. Much later, his work led to fundamental concepts such as the [Kempe chain](wikipedia://en/3621036) and unavoidable sets.

Kempe (1886) revealed a rather marked philosophical bent, and much influenced [Charles Sanders Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117). Kempe also discovered what are now called [multisets](wikipedia://en/305303), although this fact was not noted until long after his death.[^o10][^o11]

Kempe was elected a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1881. He was Treasurer and vice-president of the Royal Society 1899–1919. He was a president of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) from 1892 to 1894. He was also a [mountain climber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountaineering), mostly in Switzerland.

His first wife was Mary, daughter of [Sir William Bowman, 1st Baronet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_William_Bowman,_1st_Baronet); she died in 1893. He then married, in 1897, Ida, daughter of Judge Meadows White, QC. He had two sons and one daughter.
