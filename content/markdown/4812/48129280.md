# Hinke Osinga

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hinke Osinga"}

**Hinke Maria Osinga** (born 25 December 1969)[^o3] is a Dutch mathematician and an expert in [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087). She works as a professor of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland) in New Zealand.[^o4] As well as for her research, she is known as a creator of [mathematical art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_art).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Osinga earned a master's degree in 1991 and a Ph.D. in 1996 from the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen).[^o4] Her doctoral dissertation, jointly supervised by [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) theorist [Henk Broer](wikipedia://en/56810865) and [computational geometer](wikipedia://en/176927) Gert Vegter, was on the computation of [invariant manifolds](wikipedia://en/29763944).

After postdoctoral studies at [The Geometry Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Geometry_Center) and the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), and a short-term lecturership at the [University of Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Exeter), she became a lecturer at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol) in 2001, and was promoted to reader and professor there in 2005 and 2011, respectively. She moved to Auckland in 2011,[^o4] becoming the first female mathematics professor at Auckland and the second in New Zealand.[^o7]

## Mathematical art {#Mathematical_art}

In 2004 Osinga created a [crocheted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crochet) visualization of the Lorenz manifold, an invariant manifold for the [Lorenz system](wikipedia://en/5642583), and published the crochet pattern for her work with her husband [Bernd Krauskopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernd_Krauskopf); the resulting [mathematical textile artwork](wikipedia://en/13410380) involved over 25,000 crochet stitches, and measured nearly a meter across.[^o9][^o10] Osinga and Krauskopf later collaborated with artist Benjamin Storch on a stainless steel sculpture that provides another interpretation of the same mathematical system.[^o11]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Osinga was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2014, speaking on "Mathematics in Science and Technology".[^o13] In 2015 she was elected as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) "for contributions to theory and computational methods for dynamical systems."[^o14] In October 2016 she became the first female mathematician elected to the Royal Society of New Zealand.[^o15][^o16] She was awarded the [Aitken Lectureship](wikipedia://en/75557217) in 2017.[^o17]

In 2017 Osinga was selected as one of the Royal Society Te Apārangi's "[150 women in 150 words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/150_women_in_150_words)", celebrating the contributions of women to knowledge in New Zealand.[^o18] The same year she received the Moyal Medal from Macquarie University.[^o19]
