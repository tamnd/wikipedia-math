# Monoid factorisation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monoid factorisation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **factorisation** of a [free monoid](wikipedia://en/523166) is a sequence of [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of words with the property that every word in the free monoid can be written as a concatenation of elements drawn from the subsets. The Chen–[Fox](wikipedia://en/1343918)–[Lyndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Lyndon) theorem states that the [Lyndon words](wikipedia://en/3919355) furnish a factorisation. The [Schützenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Schützenberger) theorem relates the definition in terms of a multiplicative property to an additive property.

:::

Let $A^{\ast}$ be the [free monoid](wikipedia://en/523166) on an alphabet *A*. Let *X*<sub>*i*</sub> be a sequence of subsets of $A^{\ast}$ indexed by a [totally ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered_set) [index set](wikipedia://en/393055) *I*. A factorisation of a word *w* in $A^{\ast}$ is an expression

$$w = x_{i_1} x_{i_2} \cdots x_{i_n} \$$

with $x_{i_j} \in X_{i_j}$ and $i_1 \ge i_2 \ge \ldots \ge i_n$. Some authors reverse the order of the inequalities.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Chen–Fox–Lyndon theorem"}

## Chen–Fox–Lyndon theorem {#Chen–Fox–Lyndon_theorem}

A [Lyndon word](wikipedia://en/3919355) over a totally ordered alphabet *A* is a word that is [lexicographically](wikipedia://en/567667) less than all its rotations.[^o4] The **Chen–Fox–Lyndon theorem** states that every string may be formed in a unique way by concatenating a lexicographically non-increasing sequence of Lyndon words. Hence taking $X_{l}$ to be the [singleton set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singleton_set) $\{l\}$ for each Lyndon word $l$, with the index set *L* of Lyndon words ordered lexicographically, we obtain a factorisation of $A^{\ast}$.[^o5] Such a factorisation can be found in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time) and constant space by Duval's algorithm.[^o6] The algorithm[^o7] in [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) code is:

```python3
def chen_fox_lyndon_factorization(s: list[int]) -> list[int]:
    """Monoid factorisation using the Chen–Fox–Lyndon theorem.

    Args:
        s: a list of integers

    Returns:
        a list of integers
    """
    n = len(s)
    factorization = []
    i = 0
    while i < n:
        j, k = i + 1, i
        while j < n and s[k] <= s[j]:
            if s[k] < s[j]:
                k = i
            else:
                k += 1
            j += 1
        while i <= k:
            factorization.append(s[i:i + j - k])
            i += j - k
    return factorization
```

:::

## Hall words {#Hall_words}

The [Hall set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_set) provides a factorization.[^o9] Indeed, Lyndon words are a special case of Hall words. The article on [Hall words](wikipedia://en/31782613) provides a sketch of all of the mechanisms needed to establish a proof of this factorization.

## Bisection {#Bisection}

A **bisection** of a free monoid is a factorisation with just two classes *X*<sub>0</sub>, *X*<sub>1</sub>.[^o11]

Examples:

$$A = \{a,b\}, X_{0} = \{A^{\ast}b\}, X_{1} = \{a\}.$$

If *X*, *Y* are [disjoint sets](wikipedia://en/39359) of non-empty words, then (*X*,*Y*) is a bisection of $A^{\ast}$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922)[^o12]

$$YX \cup A = X \cup Y \ .$$

As a consequence, for any partition *P*, *Q* of *A*<sup>\+</sup> there is a unique bisection (*X*,*Y*) with *X* a subset of *P* and *Y* a subset of *Q*.[^o13]

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Schützenberger theorem"}

## Schützenberger theorem {#Schützenberger_theorem}

This theorem states that a sequence *X*<sub>*i*</sub> of subsets of $A^{\ast}$ forms a factorisation if and only if two of the following three statements hold:

- Every element of $A^{\ast}$ has at least one expression in the required form;
- Every element of $A^{\ast}$ has at most one expression in the required form;
- Each conjugate class *C* meets just one of the [monoids](wikipedia://en/19652) $M_{i} = X_{i}^{\ast}$ and the elements of *C* in *M*<sub>*i*</sub> are conjugate in *M*<sub>*i*</sub>.[^o16]

:::
