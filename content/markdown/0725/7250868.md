# Bradley Efron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bradley Efron"}

**Bradley Efron** (; born May 24, 1938)[^o3] is an American statistician. Efron has been president of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) (2004) and of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) (1987–1988).[^o4] He is a past editor (for theory and methods) of the *[Journal of the American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/16179313)*, and he is the founding editor of the *[Annals of Applied Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annals_of_Applied_Statistics)*.[^o4] Efron is also the recipient of many awards (see below).

:::

Efron is especially known for proposing the [bootstrap resampling technique](wikipedia://en/6885770),[^o5] which has had a major impact in the field of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and virtually every area of statistical application. The bootstrap was one of the first computer-intensive statistical techniques, replacing traditional [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) derivations with data-based computer simulations.[^o6]

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Efron was born in [St. Paul, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul,_Minnesota) in May 1938, the son of Russian Jewish immigrants Esther and Miles Efron.[^o8] He attended the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), graduating in mathematics in 1960. By his own admission he "had no talent for modern abstract math". His interest in statistics emerged after reading a [Harald Cramér](wikipedia://en/223129) book cover to cover.[^o9] Soon later, he arrived at [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford) in fall of 1960, earning his Ph.D., under the direction of Rupert Miller and [Herbert Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Solomon), in the Department of Statistics. While at Stanford, he was suspended for six months for his involvement with the *[Stanford Chaparral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Chaparral)**s parody of*****[Playboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playboy)*magazine.[^o10][^o11]***

He is currently a professor of Statistics and Biostatistics at Stanford. At Stanford he has been the Chair of the Department of Statistics, Associate Dean of the School of Humanities and Sciences, Chairman of the University Advisory Board, Chair of the Faculty Senate, and co-director of the undergraduate-level Mathematical & Computational Science Program.

Efron holds the Max H. Stein endowed chair as Professor of Humanities and Sciences at Stanford.

He has made many important contributions to many areas of statistics. Efron's work has spanned both theoretical and applied topics, including empirical Bayes analysis (with [Carl Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Morris_%28statistician%29)), applications of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) to [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577), the analysis of survival data, and inference for microarray gene expression data.[^o12] He is the author of a classic monograph, *The Jackknife, the Bootstrap and Other Resampling Plans* (1982) and has also co-authored (with [Robert Tibshirani](wikipedia://en/31404681)) the text *An Introduction to the Bootstrap* (1994).

He created a set of [intransitive dice](wikipedia://en/4519119) called [Efron's dice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efron's_dice).[^o13][^o14][^o15] [^o16]

## Awards {#Awards}

Efron has been given many honors, including a [MacArthur Prize Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Prize_Fellowship), membership in the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), fellowship in the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) (IMS) and the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) (ASA), the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award),[^o18] the [Wilks Medal](wikipedia://en/15966180), the [Parzen Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parzen_Prize), and the [Rao Prize](wikipedia://en/1418009#In_his_honour), [Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Fisher_Lectureship), Rietz, and Wald lecturer.[^o19]

In 2005, he was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science), the highest scientific honor by the United States, for his exceptional work in the field of Statistics (especially for his inventing of the bootstrap resampling technique).[^o20] He was presented with the award on May 29, 2007.[^o21]

In 2014, he was awarded the [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Gold.

In 2016, Efron was chosen to receive the [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Award) in the Basic Sciences category jointly with [David Cox](wikipedia://en/1990771), for the development of “pioneering and hugely influential” statistical methods, in Efron's case again especially the bootstrap resampling technique.[^o22]

He received the [International Prize in Statistics](wikipedia://en/52048719) at the 2019 [World Statistics Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Statistics_Congress).[^o23][^o24]
