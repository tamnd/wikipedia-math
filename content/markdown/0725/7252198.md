# Real algebraic geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Real algebraic geometry"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **real algebraic geometry** is the sub-branch of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) studying real [algebraic sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_set), i.e. [real-number](wikipedia://en/20646438) solutions to [algebraic equations](wikipedia://en/404001) with real-number coefficients, and [mappings](wikipedia://en/516931) between them (in particular [real polynomial mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_polynomial_mapping)).

:::

**Semialgebraic geometry** is the study of [semialgebraic sets](wikipedia://en/13329722), i.e. real-number solutions to algebraic [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489) with-real number coefficients, and mappings between them. The most natural mappings between semialgebraic sets are [semialgebraic mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semialgebraic_mapping), i.e., mappings whose graphs are semialgebraic sets.

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Terminology"}

## Terminology {#Terminology}

Nowadays the words 'semialgebraic geometry' and 'real algebraic geometry' are used as synonyms, because real algebraic sets cannot be studied seriously without the use of semialgebraic sets. For example, a projection of a real algebraic set along a coordinate axis need not be a real algebraic set, but it is always a semialgebraic set: this is the [Tarski–Seidenberg theorem](wikipedia://en/19389633).[^o4][^o5] Related fields are [o-minimal theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O-minimal) and [real analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/28156210).

Examples: [Real plane curves](wikipedia://en/28156512) are examples of real algebraic sets and [polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470) are examples of semialgebraic sets. Real [algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/974169) and [Nash functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_function) are examples of semialgebraic mappings. Piecewise polynomial mappings (see the [Pierce–Birkhoff conjecture](wikipedia://en/22052605)) are also semialgebraic mappings.

**Computational real algebraic geometry** is concerned with the algorithmic aspects of real algebraic (and semialgebraic) geometry. The main algorithm is [cylindrical algebraic decomposition](wikipedia://en/12218154). It is used to cut semialgebraic sets into nice pieces and to compute their projections.

**Real algebra** is the part of algebra which is relevant to real algebraic (and semialgebraic) geometry. It is mostly concerned with the study of [ordered fields](wikipedia://en/22430) and [ordered rings](wikipedia://en/525697) (in particular [real closed fields](wikipedia://en/1066093)) and their applications to the study of [positive polynomials](wikipedia://en/28416554) and [sums-of-squares of polynomials](wikipedia://en/17001761). (See [Hilbert's 17th problem](wikipedia://en/2336214) and the [Krivine-Stengle Positivstellensatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krivine-Stengle_Positivstellensatz).) The relation of real algebra to real algebraic geometry is similar to the relation of [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) to [complex algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_algebraic_geometry). Related fields are the theory of [moment problems](wikipedia://en/1244992), [convex optimization](wikipedia://en/1674411), the theory of [quadratic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_forms), [valuation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_theory) and [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858).

:::

## Timeline of real algebra and real algebraic geometry {#Timeline_of_real_algebra_and_real_algebraic_geometry}

- 1826 [Fourier's algorithm](wikipedia://en/9203671) for systems of linear inequalities.[^o7] Rediscovered by [Lloyd Dines](wikipedia://en/47924017) in 1919[^o8] and [Theodore Motzkin](wikipedia://en/13554055) in 1936.[^o9]
- 1835 [Sturm's theorem](wikipedia://en/1221919) on real root counting[^o10]
- 1856 Hermite's theorem on real root counting.[^o11]
- 1876 [Harnack's curve theorem](wikipedia://en/5209604).[^o12] (This bound on the number of components was later extended to all [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) of all real algebraic sets[^o13][^o14][^o15] and all semialgebraic sets.[^o16])
- 1888 Hilbert's theorem on ternary quartics.[^o17]
- 1900 [Hilbert's problems](wikipedia://en/154584) (especially the [16th](wikipedia://en/381750) and the [17th](wikipedia://en/2336214) problem)
- 1902 [Farkas' lemma](wikipedia://en/2480226)[^o18] (Can be reformulated as linear positivstellensatz.)
- 1914 [Annibale Comessatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annibale_Comessatti) showed that not every real algebraic surface is birational to **RP**<sup>*2*</sup>[^o19]
- 1916 Fejér's conjecture about nonnegative trigonometric polynomials.[^o20] (Solved by [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126).[^o21])
- 1927 [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351)'s solution of [Hilbert's 17th problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert's_17th_problem)[^o22]
- 1927 Krull–Baer Theorem[^o23][^o24] (connection between orderings and valuations)
- 1928 Pólya's Theorem on positive polynomials on a simplex[^o25]
- 1929 [B. L. van der Waerden](wikipedia://en/91390) sketches a proof that real algebraic and [semialgebraic sets](wikipedia://en/13329722) are triangularizable,[^o26] but the necessary tools have not been developed to make the argument rigorous.
- 1931 [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920)'s [real quantifier elimination](wikipedia://en/19389633).[^o27] Improved and popularized by [Abraham Seidenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Seidenberg) in 1954.[^o28] (Both use [Sturm's theorem](wikipedia://en/1221919).)
- 1936 [Herbert Seifert](wikipedia://en/637024) proved that every closed smooth submanifold of $\mathbb{R}^n$ with trivial normal bundle, can be isotoped to a component of a nonsingular real algebraic subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ which is a complete intersection[^o29] (from the conclusion of this theorem the word "component" can not be removed[^o30]).
- 1940 [Marshall Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Stone)'s representation theorem for partially ordered rings.[^o31] Improved by [Richard Kadison](wikipedia://en/25798650) in 1951[^o32] and Donald Dubois in 1967[^o33] (Kadison–Dubois representation theorem). Further improved by Mihai Putinar in 1993[^o34] and Jacobi in 2001[^o35] (Putinar–Jacobi representation theorem).
- 1952 [John Nash](wikipedia://en/102567) proved that every closed smooth manifold is diffeomorphic to a nonsingular component of a real algebraic set.[^o36]
- 1956 [Pierce–Birkhoff conjecture](wikipedia://en/22052605) formulated.[^o37] (Solved in dimensions ≤ 2.[^o38])
- 1964 [Krivine's Nullstellensatz and Positivestellensatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stengle's_Positivstellensatz).[^o39] Rediscovered and popularized by Stengle in 1974.[^o40] (Krivine uses [real quantifier elimination](wikipedia://en/19389633) while Stengle uses Lang's homomorphism theorem.[^o41])
- 1964 Lojasiewicz triangulated semi-analytic sets[^o42]
- 1964 [Heisuke Hironaka](wikipedia://en/13822) proved the resolution of singularity theorem[^o43]
- 1964 [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794) proved that every analytic variety admits a stratification satisfying the [Whitney conditions](wikipedia://en/9322242).[^o44]
- 1967 [Theodore Motzkin](wikipedia://en/13554055) finds a positive polynomial which is not a [sum of squares of polynomials](wikipedia://en/17001761).[^o45]
- 1972 [Vladimir Rokhlin](wikipedia://en/7703611) proved [Gudkov's conjecture](wikipedia://en/46586556).[^o46]
- 1973 [Alberto Tognoli](wikipedia://en/47983769) proved that every closed smooth manifold is diffeomorphic to a nonsingular real algebraic set.[^o47]
- 1975 [George E. Collins](wikipedia://en/41768958) discovers [cylindrical algebraic decomposition](wikipedia://en/12218154) algorithm, which improves Tarski's real [quantifier elimination](wikipedia://en/2150441) and allows to implement it on a computer.[^o48]
- 1973 [Jean-Louis Verdier](wikipedia://en/9168893) proved that every subanalytic set admits a stratification with condition (w).[^o49]
- 1979 Michel Coste and [Marie-Françoise Roy](wikipedia://en/39889069) discover the real spectrum of a commutative ring.[^o50]
- 1980 [Oleg Viro](wikipedia://en/14465687) introduced the "patch working" technique and used it to classify real algebraic curves of low degree.[^o51] Later Ilya Itenberg and Viro used it to produce counterexamples to the [Ragsdale conjecture](wikipedia://en/9852079),[^o52][^o53] and [Grigory Mikhalkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Mikhalkin) applied it to [tropical geometry](wikipedia://en/867041) for curve counting.[^o54]
- 1980 [Selman Akbulut](wikipedia://en/4558855) and Henry C. King gave a topological characterization of real algebraic sets with isolated singularities, and topologically characterized nonsingular real algebraic sets (not necessarily compact)[^o55]
- 1980 Akbulut and King proved that every knot in $S^n$ is the link of a real algebraic set with isolated singularity in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$[^o56]
- 1981 Akbulut and King proved that every compact PL manifold is PL homeomorphic to a real algebraic set.[^o57][^o58][^o59]
- 1983 Akbulut and King introduced "Topological Resolution Towers" as topological models of real algebraic sets, from this they obtained new topological invariants of real algebraic sets, and topologically characterized all 3-dimensional algebraic sets.[^o60] These invariants later generalized by Michel Coste and Krzysztof Kurdyka[^o61] as well as Clint McCrory and Adam Parusiński.[^o62]
- 1984 Ludwig Bröcker's theorem on minimal generation of basic open [semialgebraic sets](wikipedia://en/13329722)[^o63] (improved and extended to basic closed [semialgebraic sets](wikipedia://en/13329722) by Scheiderer.[^o64])
- 1984 Benedetti and Dedo proved that not every closed smooth manifold is diffeomorphic to a totally algebraic nonsingular real algebraic set (totally algebraic means all its Z/2Z-homology cycles are represented by real algebraic subsets).[^o65]
- 1991 Akbulut and King proved that every closed smooth manifold is homeomorphic to a totally algebraic real algebraic set.[^o66]
- 1991 Schmüdgen's solution of the multidimensional moment problem for compact semialgebraic sets and related strict positivstellensatz.[^o67] Algebraic proof found by Wörmann.[^o68] Implies Reznick's version of Artin's theorem with uniform denominators.[^o69]
- 1992 Akbulut and King proved ambient versions of the Nash-Tognoli theorem: Every closed smooth submanifold of **R**<sup>*n*</sup> is isotopic to the nonsingular points (component) of a real algebraic subset of **R**<sup>*n*</sup>, and they extended this result to immersed submanifolds of **R**<sup>*n*</sup>.[^o70][^o71]
- 1992 Benedetti and Marin proved that every compact closed smooth 3-manifold **M** can be obtained from $S^3$ by a sequence of blow ups and downs along smooth centers, and that **M** is homeomorphic to a possibly singular affine real algebraic rational threefold[^o72]
- 1997 Bierstone and Milman proved a canonical resolution of singularities theorem[^o73]
- 1997 Mikhalkin proved that every closed smooth n-manifold can be obtained from $S^n$ by a sequence of topological blow ups and downs[^o74]
- 1998 [János Kollár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/János_Kollár) showed that not every closed 3-manifold is a projective real 3-fold which is birational to **RP**<sup>*3*</sup>[^o75]
- 2000 Scheiderer's local-global principle and related non-strict extension of Schmüdgen's positivstellensatz in dimensions ≤ 2.[^o76][^o77][^o78]
- 2000 [János Kollár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/János_Kollár) proved that every closed smooth 3–manifold is the real part of a compact complex manifold which can be obtained from $\mathbb{CP}^3$ by a sequence of real blow ups and blow downs.[^o79]
- 2003 Welschinger introduces an invariant for counting real rational curves[^o80]
- 2005 Akbulut and King showed that not every nonsingular real algebraic subset of **RP**<sup>*n*</sup> is smoothly isotopic to the real part of a nonsingular complex algebraic subset of **CP**<sup>*n*</sup>[^o81][^o82]
