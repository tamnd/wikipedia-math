# Arthur's conjectures

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur's conjectures"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Arthur conjectures** refer to a set of [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) proposed by [James Arthur](wikipedia://en/9525221) in 1989.[^o2] These conjectures pertain to the properties of [automorphic representations](wikipedia://en/782146) of [reductive groups](wikipedia://en/1088386) over [adele rings](wikipedia://en/247978) and the [unitary representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_representations_of_a_star_Lie_superalgebra) of reductive groups over local fields.[^o2] Arthur’s work, which was motivated by the [Arthur–Selberg trace formula](wikipedia://en/20419621), suggests a framework for understanding complex relationships in these areas.[^o3]

:::

Arthur's conjectures have implications for other mathematical theories, notably implying the [generalized Ramanujan conjectures](wikipedia://en/782173) for [cusp forms](wikipedia://en/610165) on [general linear groups](wikipedia://en/113564). [^o3][^o4] The Ramanujan conjectures, in turn, are central to the study of automorphic forms, as they predict specific behaviors of certain classes of mathematical functions known as cusp forms.[^o4]

To better understand the Arthur conjectures, familiarity with automorphic forms and reductive groups is useful, as is knowledge of the trace formula developed by Arthur and [Atle Selberg](wikipedia://en/2021). These mathematical tools allow for analysis of representations of groups in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939).
