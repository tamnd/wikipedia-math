# Freidlin–Wentzell theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Freidlin–Wentzell theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Freidlin–Wentzell theorem** (due to [Mark Freidlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Freidlin) and [Alexander D. Wentzell](wikipedia://en/56540637)) is a result in the [large deviations theory](wikipedia://en/3772909) of [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895). Roughly speaking, the Freidlin–Wentzell theorem gives an estimate for the probability that a (scaled-down) sample path of an [Itō diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō_diffusion) will stray far from the mean path. This statement is made precise using [rate functions](wikipedia://en/3772816). The Freidlin–Wentzell theorem generalizes [Schilder's theorem](wikipedia://en/13345478) for standard [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *B* be a standard Brownian motion on **R**<sup>*d*</sup> starting at the origin, 0 ∈ **R**<sup>*d*</sup>, and let *X*<sup>*ε*</sup> be an **R**<sup>*d*</sup>-valued Itō diffusion solving an Itō [stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/1361454) of the form

$$\begin{cases} dX_t^\varepsilon = b(X_t^\varepsilon) \, dt + \sqrt{\varepsilon} \, dB_t, \\ X_0^\varepsilon = 0, \end{cases}$$

where the drift [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) *b* : **R**<sup>*d*</sup> → **R**<sup>*d*</sup> is [uniformly Lipschitz continuous](wikipedia://en/51731). Then, on the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *C*<sub>0</sub> = *C*<sub>0</sub>(\[0, *T*\]; **R**<sup>*d*</sup>) equipped with the [supremum norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum_norm) ||⋅||<sub>∞</sub>, the family of processes (*X*<sup>*ε*</sup>)<sub>*ε*>0</sub> satisfies the large deviations principle with good rate function *I* : *C*<sub>0</sub> → **R** ∪ {+∞} given by

$$I(\omega) = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^T | \dot{\omega}_t - b(\omega_t) |^2 \, dt$$

if *ω* lies in the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) *H*<sup>1</sup>(\[0, *T*\]; **R**<sup>*d*</sup>), and *I*(*ω*) = +∞ otherwise. In other words, for every [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) *G* ⊆ *C*<sub>0</sub> and every [closed set](wikipedia://en/47279) *F* ⊆ *C*<sub>0</sub>,

$$\limsup_{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \big( \varepsilon \log \mathbf{P} \big[ X^\varepsilon \in F \big]\big) \leq -\inf_{\omega \in F} I(\omega)$$

and

$$\liminf_{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \big( \varepsilon \log \mathbf{P} \big[ X^{\varepsilon} \in G \big]\big) \geq - \inf_{\omega \in G} I(\omega).$$

:::
