# Schilder's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schilder's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Schilder's theorem** is a generalization of the [Laplace method](wikipedia://en/642006) from integrals on $\mathbb{R}^n$ to functional Wiener integration. The theorem is used in the [large deviations theory](wikipedia://en/3772909) of [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895). Roughly speaking, out of Schilder's theorem one gets an estimate for the probability that a (scaled-down) sample path of [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) will stray far from the mean path (which is constant with value 0). This statement is made precise using [rate functions](wikipedia://en/3772816). Schilder's theorem is generalized by the [Freidlin–Wentzell theorem](wikipedia://en/13345788) for [Itō diffusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō_diffusion).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *C*<sub>0</sub> = *C*<sub>0</sub>(\[0, *T*\]; **R**<sup>*d*</sup>) be the Banach space of continuous functions $f : [0,T] \longrightarrow \mathbf{R}^d$ such that $f(0)=0$, equipped with the [supremum norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum_norm) ||⋅||<sub>∞</sub> and $C_0^\ast$ be the subspace of absolutely continuous functions whose derivative is in $L^2$ (the so-called [Cameron-Martin space](wikipedia://en/6573013)). Define the rate function

$$I(\omega) = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{T} \| \dot{\omega}(t) \|^{2} \, \mathrm{d} t$$

on $C_0^\ast$ and let $F:C_0\to\mathbb{R},G:C_0\to\mathbb{C}$ be two given functions, such that $S:=I+F$ (the "action") has a unique minimum $\Omega\in C_0^\ast$.

Then under some differentiability and growth assumptions on $F,G$ which are detailed in [Schilder 1966](https://doi.org/10.2307/1994588), one has

$$\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\lambda F(\lambda^{-1/2} \omega)\right)G(\lambda^{-1/2} \omega)\right]}{\exp\left(-\lambda S(\Omega)\right)} = G(\Omega)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\langle\omega, D(\Omega) \omega\rangle\right)\right]$$

where $\mathbb{E}$ denotes expectation with respect to the Wiener measure $\mathbb{P}$ on $C_0$ and $D(\Omega)$ is the Hessian of $F$ at the minimum $\Omega$; $\langle\omega, D(\Omega) \omega\rangle$ is meant in the sense of an $L^2([0,T])$ inner product.

:::

## Application to large deviations on the Wiener measure {#Application_to_large_deviations_on_the_Wiener_measure}

Let *B* be a standard Brownian motion in *d*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>*d*</sup> starting at the origin, 0 ∈ **R**<sup>*d*</sup>; let **W** denote the [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_%28stochastic_processes%29) of *B*, i.e. classical [Wiener measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_measure). For *ε* > 0, let **W**<sub>*ε*</sub> denote the law of the rescaled process $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$*B*. Then, on the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *C*<sub>0</sub> = *C*<sub>0</sub>(\[0, *T*\]; **R**<sup>*d*</sup>) of continuous functions $f : [0,T] \longrightarrow \mathbf{R}^d$ such that $f(0)=0$, equipped with the [supremum norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum_norm) ||⋅||<sub>∞</sub>, the [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) **W**<sub>*ε*</sub> satisfy the large deviations principle with good rate function *I* : *C*<sub>0</sub> → **R** ∪ {+∞} given by

$$I(\omega) = \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{T} | \dot{\omega}(t) |^{2} \, \mathrm{d} t$$

if *ω* is [absolutely continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_continuous), and *I*(*ω*) = +∞ otherwise. In other words, for every [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) *G* ⊆ *C*<sub>0</sub> and every [closed set](wikipedia://en/47279) *F* ⊆ *C*<sub>0</sub>,

$$\limsup_{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \varepsilon \log \mathbf{W}_{\varepsilon} (F) \leq - \inf_{\omega \in F} I(\omega)$$

and

$$\liminf_{\varepsilon \downarrow 0} \varepsilon \log \mathbf{W}_{\varepsilon} (G) \geq - \inf_{\omega \in G} I(\omega).$$

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Taking *ε* = 1/*c*<sup>2</sup>, one can use Schilder's theorem to obtain estimates for the probability that a standard Brownian motion *B* strays further than *c* from its starting point over the time interval \[0, *T*\], i.e. the probability

$$\mathbf{W} (C_0 \smallsetminus \mathbf{B}_c (0; \| \cdot \|_\infty)) \equiv \mathbf{P} \big[ \| B \|_\infty > c \big],$$

as *c* tends to infinity. Here **B**<sub>*c*</sub>(0; ||⋅||<sub>∞</sub>) denotes the [open ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_ball) of radius *c* about the zero function in *C*<sub>0</sub>, taken with respect to the [supremum norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum_norm). First note that

$$\| B \|_\infty > c \iff \sqrt{\varepsilon} B \in A := \left \{ \omega \in C_0 \mid |\omega(t)| > 1 \text{ for some } t \in [0, T] \right\}.$$

Since the rate function is continuous on *A*, Schilder's theorem yields

$$\begin{aligned} \lim_{c \to \infty} \frac{\log \left (\mathbf{P} \left [ \| B \|_\infty > c \right] \right )}{c^2} &= \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \varepsilon \log \left (\mathbf{P} \left[ \sqrt{\varepsilon} B \in A \right] \right ) \\[6pt] &= - \inf \left\{ \left. \frac{1}{2} \int_0^T | \dot{\omega}(t) |^2 \, \mathrm{d} t \,\right|\, \omega \in A \right\} \\[6pt] &= - \frac{1}{2} \int_0^T \frac{1}{T^2} \, \mathrm{d} t \\[6pt] &= - \frac{1}{2 T}, \end{aligned}$$

making use of the fact that the [infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum) over paths in the collection *A* is attained for $\omega(t) = \tfrac{t}{T}$ . This result can be heuristically interpreted as saying that, for large $c$ and/or large $T$

$$\frac{\log \left (\mathbf{P} \left [ \| B \|_\infty > c \right] \right )}{c^2} \approx - \frac{1}{2T} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \mathbf{P} \left[ \| B \|_\infty > c \right ] \approx \exp \left( - \frac{c^2}{2 T} \right).$$

In fact, the above probability can be estimated more precisely: for $B$ a standard Brownian motion in $\mathbf{R}^{n}$, and any $T, c$ and $\varepsilon > 0$, we have:

$$\mathbf{P} \left[ \sup_{0 \leq t \leq T} \left| \sqrt{\varepsilon} B_t \right | \geq c \right] \leq 4 n \exp \left( - \frac{c^2}{2 n T \varepsilon} \right).$$

:::
