# Norman Routledge

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Norman Routledge"}

**Norman Arthur Routledge** (7 March 1928 – 27 April 2013) was a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [schoolteacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schoolteacher).[^o5][^o6] He was a personal friend of fellow mathematician [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208) (1912–1954).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Norman Routledge was born near [Alexandra Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Park,_London), north [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London), England.[^o6] He was about to begin secondary education at [Glendale County School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glendale_County_School), [Wood Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_Green), in 1939, when the outbreak of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) intervened. He was evacuated with his mother, going to live in [Letchworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letchworth) with an aunt, and attending [Letchworth Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letchworth_Grammar_School), where he was taught mathematics by George Braithwaite.[^o5]

In 1946 Routledge matriculated with a scholarship at [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), where he read [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). He was supervised by [Albert Ingham](wikipedia://en/5380138) and [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033). He gained a [first class degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_degree) in 1949 and went on to research in [recursion theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion_theory). It resulted in the papers *Ordinal recursion* (1953) and *Concerning definable sets* (1954).[^o5]

Routledge taught as a scientific officer at the [Royal Aircraft Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Aircraft_Establishment), [RAE Farnborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAE_Farnborough). He went on to the [National Physical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Physical_Laboratory_%28United_Kingdom%29) (NPL), [Teddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddington). These were placements to fulfil the requirements for his compulsory [national service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_the_United_Kingdom#After_1945). At the NPL in 1952 he was able to become an operator of an early version of the [Automatic Computing Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_Computing_Engine): the [Pilot ACE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_ACE) project supported by [Harry Huskey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Huskey)'s prototype [assembler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language).[^o6][^o8]

Returning to academia, Routledge became a research Fellow in mathematics at King's College, Cambridge. He taught college undergraduates, and after a time was a director of studies.[^o6][^o5] In 1957, he was photographed by [Antony Barrington Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Barrington_Brown). The photograph is now in the collection of the [National Portrait Gallery, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Portrait_Gallery,_London).[^o9]

In 1959, [Robert Birley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Birley), Headmaster at [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College), asked Routledge for a recommendation of some promising student for a mathematics teaching post; and he suggested himself. He taught mathematics at the school for some years and was later a housemaster. He was considered an inspirational teacher,[^o6] teaching among other Etonians [Simon P. Norton](wikipedia://en/1641166), [Timothy Gowers](wikipedia://en/365715) and [Stephen Wolfram](wikipedia://en/52469).[^o10][^o11][^o12] Later in his life, he taught music for the [Salvation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvation_Army) community in [Bermondsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermondsey), southeast London.[^o6]

Routledge was a raconteur, including on his personal life.[^o13] In retirement towards the end of his own life, he was able to be more openly gay.[^o6]

In Turing's biography, Routledge is described as "clever, flamboyant and sometimes outrageous".[^o14]

## Association with Alan Turing {#Association_with_Alan_Turing}

Routledge was a friend of the mathematician and codebreaker [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208), whom he met after World War II, when Turing was in Cambridge to study physiology.[^o5] Turing wrote personal letters to Routledge towards the end of his life. After his arrest and before his trial, he sent the following cryptic [syllogism](wikipedia://en/48046) to Routledge in 1952:[^o16][^o17][^o18][^o19]

Turing believes that machines think Turing lies with men Therefore machines cannot think

The 1992 documentary programme *The Strange Life and Death of Dr Turing* had Routledge as one of the interviewees.[^o20]
