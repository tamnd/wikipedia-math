# John Coleman Moore

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Coleman Moore"}

**John Coleman Moore** (May 27, 1923 – January 1, 2016) was an American mathematician. The [Borel−Moore homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel−Moore_homology) and [Eilenberg–Moore spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/10220853) are named after him.[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Moore was born in 1923 in [Staten Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staten_Island), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29).[^o5] He received his [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1948 from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1952 from [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) under the supervision of [George W. Whitehead](wikipedia://en/13641389).

## Career {#Career}

Moore began his career at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) as an instructor, and was eventually promoted to full professor in 1961. He retired from Princeton in 1989, after which he took a half-time position at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester).[^o7]

His most-cited paper is on [Hopf algebras](wikipedia://en/325714), co-authored with [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632).[^o8] As a faculty member at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), he advised 24 students and is the [academic ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_genealogy) of over 1000 mathematicians. He was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1958[^o10] in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) and in 1970 in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice).

In 1983, a conference on [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748) was held at Princeton in honor of Moore's 60th birthday.[^o11] In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o12] He died in 2016 at the age of 92.[^o13]
