# Composition algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Composition algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **composition algebra** $A$ over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$ is a [not necessarily associative](wikipedia://en/6533841) [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) over $K$ together with a [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degenerate_form) [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) $N$ that satisfies

:::

$$N(xy) = N(x)N(y)$$

for all $x$ and $y$ in $A$.

A composition algebra includes an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) called a **conjugation**: $x \mapsto x^*.$ The quadratic form $N(x) = x x^*$ is called the **norm** of the algebra.

A composition algebra (*A*, ∗, *N*) is either a [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625) or a **split algebra**, depending on the existence of a non-zero *v* in *A* such that *N*(*v*) = 0, called a [null vector](wikipedia://en/1453977).[^o2] When *x* is *not* a null vector, the [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940) of *x* is $\frac{x^*}{N(x)}$. When there is a non-zero null vector, *N* is an [isotropic quadratic form](wikipedia://en/10917170), and "the algebra splits".

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Structure theorem"}

## Structure theorem {#Structure_theorem}

Every [unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra) composition algebra over a field $K$ can be obtained by repeated application of the [Cayley–Dickson construction](wikipedia://en/175609) starting from $K$ (if the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of $K$ is different from $2$) or a 2-dimensional composition subalgebra (if $\operatorname{char}(K) = 2$). The possible dimensions of a composition algebra are $1$, $2$, $4$, and $8$.[^o17][^o5][^o6]

- 1-dimensional composition algebras only exist when $\operatorname{char}(K) \ne 2$.
- Composition algebras of dimension 1 and 2 are commutative and associative.
- Composition algebras of dimension 2 are either [quadratic field extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_field_extension) of $K$ or isomorphic to $K \oplus K$.
- Composition algebras of dimension 4 are called [quaternion algebras](wikipedia://en/2776890). They are associative but not commutative.
- Composition algebras of dimension 8 are called [octonion algebras](wikipedia://en/10277277). They are neither associative nor commutative.

For consistent terminology, algebras of dimension 1 have been called *unarion*, and those of dimension 2 *binarion*.[^o13]

Every composition algebra is an [alternative algebra](wikipedia://en/3160).[^o7]

Using the doubled form ( \_ : \_ ): *A* × *A* → *K* defined by $(a:b) = N(a+b) - N(a) - N(b)=ab^*+ba^*,$ then the trace of *a* is given by $(a:1) = a + a^*$ and the conjugate by $a^* = (a:1)e - a$ where $e$ is multiplicative identity of $A$. A series of exercises proves that a composition algebra is always an alternative algebra.

:::

## Instances and usage {#Instances_and_usage}

When the field $K$ is taken to be [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $\mathbf{C}$ and the quadratic form $z^{2}$, then four composition algebras over $\mathbf{C}$ are $\mathbf{C} \operatorname{itself}$, the [bicomplex numbers](wikipedia://en/4802961), the [biquaternions](wikipedia://en/1207070) (isomorphic to the 2 complex [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541) $M(2,\,\mathbf{C})$), and the [bioctonions](wikipedia://en/51005149) $\mathbf{C} \otimes \mathbf{O}$, which are also called complex octonions.

The matrix ring $M(2,\,\mathbf{C})$ has long been an object of interest, first as [biquaternions](wikipedia://en/1207070) by [Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817) (1853), later in the isomorphic matrix form, and especially as [Pauli algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauli_algebra).

The [squaring function](wikipedia://en/659942) $N(x) = x^{2}$ on the [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) field forms the primordial composition algebra. When the field $K$ is taken to be real numbers $\mathbf{R}$, then there are just six other real composition algebras.[^o5] In two, four, and eight dimensions there are both a [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625) and a [split algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_algebra):

binarions: complex numbers with quadratic form $x^{2} + y^{2}$ and [split-complex numbers](wikipedia://en/893559) with quadratic form $x^{2} - y^{2}$,

[quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) and [split-quaternions](wikipedia://en/1461265),

[octonions](wikipedia://en/51436) and [split-octonions](wikipedia://en/3861649).

Every composition algebra has an associated [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) B(*x,y*) constructed with the norm N and a [polarization identity](wikipedia://en/2047965):

$B(x,y) \ = \ [N(x + y) - N(x) - N(y)]/2 .$[^o10]

## History {#History}

The composition of sums of squares was noted by several early authors. [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110) was aware of the identity involving the sum of two squares, now called the [Brahmagupta–Fibonacci identity](wikipedia://en/326483), which is also articulated as a property of Euclidean norms of complex numbers when multiplied. [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) discussed the [four-square identity](wikipedia://en/222004) in 1748, and it led [W. R. Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._R._Hamilton) to construct his four-dimensional algebra of [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440).[^o13] In 1848 [tessarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessarine) were described giving first light to bicomplex numbers.

About 1818 Danish scholar Ferdinand Degen displayed the [Degen's eight-square identity](wikipedia://en/4982245), which was later connected with norms of elements of the [octonion](wikipedia://en/51436) algebra:

Historically, the first non-associative algebra, the [Cayley numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley_number) ... arose in the context of the number-theoretic problem of quadratic forms permitting composition…this number-theoretic question can be transformed into one concerning certain algebraic systems, the composition algebras...[^o13]

In 1919 [Leonard Dickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Dickson) advanced the study of the [Hurwitz problem](wikipedia://en/36830673) with a survey of efforts to that date, and by exhibiting the method of doubling the quaternions to obtain [Cayley numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley_number). He introduced a new [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358) $e$, and for quaternions $q$ and $Q$ writes a Cayley number $q + Qe$. Denoting the quaternion conjugate by $q'$, the product of two Cayley numbers is[^o14]

$$(q + Qe)(r + Re) = (qr - R'Q) + (Rq + Q r')e .$$

The conjugate of a Cayley number is $q\boldsymbol{ - Qe}$, and the quadratic form is $qq' + QQ'$, obtained by multiplying the number by its conjugate. The doubling method has come to be called the [Cayley–Dickson construction](wikipedia://en/175609).

In 1923 the case of real algebras with [positive definite forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_definite_form) was delimited by the [Hurwitz's theorem (composition algebras)](wikipedia://en/25167317).

In 1931 [Max Zorn](wikipedia://en/19740) introduced a gamma (γ) into the multiplication rule in the Dickson construction to generate [split-octonions](wikipedia://en/3861649).[^o15] [Adrian Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Albert) also used the gamma in 1942 when he showed that Dickson doubling could be applied to any [field](wikipedia://en/10603) with the [squaring function](wikipedia://en/659942) to construct binarion, quaternion, and octonion algebras with their quadratic forms.[^o16] [Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson) described the [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) of composition algebras in 1958.[^o17]

The classical composition algebras over $\mathbf{R}$ and $\mathbf{C}$ are [unital algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra). Composition algebras *without* a [multiplicative identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicative_identity) were found by H.P. Petersson ([Petersson algebras](wikipedia://en/42854564)) and Susumu Okubo ([Okubo algebras](wikipedia://en/39255698)) and others.[^o18]
