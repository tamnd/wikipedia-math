# Symmetric monoidal category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Symmetric monoidal category"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **symmetric monoidal category** is a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) (i.e. a category in which a "tensor product" $\otimes$ is defined) such that the tensor product is symmetric (i.e. $A\otimes B$ is, in a certain strict sense, [naturally isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturally_isomorphic) to $B\otimes A$ for all objects $A$ and $B$ of the category). One of the prototypical examples of a symmetric monoidal category is the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces) over some fixed [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *k,* using the ordinary [tensor product of vector spaces](wikipedia://en/2993836).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A symmetric monoidal category is a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) (*C*, ⊗, *I*) such that, for every pair *A*, *B* of objects in *C*, there is an isomorphism $s_{AB}: A \otimes B \to B \otimes A$ called the *swap map*[^o4] that is [natural](wikipedia://en/45022) in both *A* and *B* and such that the following diagrams commute:

## Examples {#Examples}

Some examples and non-examples of symmetric monoidal categories:

- The [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829). The tensor product is the set theoretic [cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095), and any [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798) can be fixed as the unit object.
- The [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092). The tensor product is the [direct product of groups](wikipedia://en/3706015), and the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059) is the unit object.
- More generally, any category with finite [products](wikipedia://en/237868), that is, a [cartesian monoidal category](wikipedia://en/43676277), is symmetric monoidal. The tensor product is the direct product of objects, and any [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object) (empty product) is the unit object.
- The [category of bimodules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_bimodules) over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* is monoidal (using the ordinary [tensor product of modules](wikipedia://en/2993836)), but not necessarily symmetric. If *R* is [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346), the category of left *R*-modules is symmetric monoidal. The latter example class includes the category of all vector spaces over a given field.
- Given a field *k* and a group (or a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) over *k*), the category of all *k*-linear [representations of the group](wikipedia://en/12695) (or of the Lie algebra) is a symmetric monoidal category. Here the standard [tensor product of representations](wikipedia://en/43079145) is used.
- The categories (**Ste**,$\circledast$) and (**Ste**,$\odot$) of [stereotype spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_space) over ${\mathbb C}$ are symmetric monoidal, and moreover, (**Ste**,$\circledast$) is a [closed](wikipedia://en/1396924) symmetric monoidal category with the [internal hom-functor](wikipedia://en/2741037#Internal_Hom_functor) $\oslash$.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The [classifying space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classifying_space_%28category_theory%29) (geometric realization of the [nerve](wikipedia://en/5127933)) of a symmetric monoidal category is an $E_\infty$ space, so its [group completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_completion) is an [infinite loop space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_loop_space).[^o9]

:::

## Specializations {#Specializations}

A [dagger symmetric monoidal category](wikipedia://en/7156065) is a symmetric monoidal category with a compatible [dagger structure](wikipedia://en/7152070).

A [cosmos](wikipedia://en/46926054) is a [complete](wikipedia://en/62781) cocomplete [closed](wikipedia://en/1396924) symmetric monoidal category.

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

In a symmetric monoidal category, the natural isomorphisms $s_{AB}: A \otimes B \to B \otimes A$ are their *own* inverses in the sense that $s_{BA}\circ s_{AB}=1_{A\otimes B}$. If we abandon this requirement (but still require that $A\otimes B$ be naturally isomorphic to $B\otimes A$), we obtain the more general notion of a [braided monoidal category](wikipedia://en/1470466).

:::
