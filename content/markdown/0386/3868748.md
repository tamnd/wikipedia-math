# Table of Newtonian series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Table of Newtonian series"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [Newtonian series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtonian_series), named after [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627), is a sum over a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) $a_n$ written in the form

:::

$$f(s) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^n {s\choose n} a_n = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-s)_n}{n!} a_n$$

where

$${s \choose n}$$

is the [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668) and $(s)_n$ is the [falling factorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rising_factorial). Newtonian series often appear in relations of the form seen in [umbral calculus](wikipedia://en/214124).

## List {#List}

The generalized [binomial theorem](wikipedia://en/4677) gives

$$(1+z)^s = \sum_{n = 0}^{\infty}{s \choose n}z^n = 1+{s \choose 1}z+{s \choose 2}z^2+\cdots.$$

A proof for this identity can be obtained by showing that it satisfies the differential equation

$$(1+z) \frac{d(1+z)^s}{dz} = s (1+z)^s.$$

The $\log$ of the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316), and its derivative the [digamma function](wikipedia://en/406935), can both have Newtonian series found by taking their [binomial transform](wikipedia://en/3877474) as sequences over the integers:

$$\begin{aligned}\log(\Gamma(s+1))&=\sum_{n=1}^\infty{s\choose n}\sum_{k=1}^n(-1)^{n-k}{n-1\choose k-1}\log(k)\\ \psi(s+1)+\gamma=H_s&=\sum_{n=1}^\infty{s\choose n}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}n\end{aligned}$$

These are both valid in the right half-plane $\Re(s)>0$, as proven by [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422) in 1900[^o3] and [Moritz Abraham Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moritz_Abraham_Stern) in 1847 (see [Digamma function#Newton series](wikipedia://en/406935#Newton_series)) respectively.

The [Stirling numbers of the second kind](wikipedia://en/2229292) are given by the finite sum

$$\left\{\begin{matrix} n \\ k \end{matrix}\right\} =\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}{k \choose j} j^n.$$

This formula is a special case of the *k*th [forward difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_difference) of the [monomial](wikipedia://en/357416) *x*<sup>*n*</sup> evaluated at *x* = 0:

$$\Delta^k x^n = \sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}{k \choose j} (x+j)^n.$$

A related identity forms the basis of the [Nörlund–Rice integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nörlund–Rice_integral):

$$\sum_{k=0}^n {n \choose k}\frac {(-1)^{n-k}}{s-k} = \frac{n!}{s(s-1)(s-2)\cdots(s-n)} = \frac{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(s-n)}{\Gamma(s+1)}= B(n+1,s-n),s \notin \{0,\ldots,n\}$$

where $\Gamma(x)$ is the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) and $B(x,y)$ is the [Beta function](wikipedia://en/245660).

The [trigonometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_function) have [umbral](wikipedia://en/214124) identities:

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^n {s \choose 2n} = 2^{s/2} \cos \frac{\pi s}{4}$$

and

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^n {s \choose 2n+1} = 2^{s/2} \sin \frac{\pi s}{4}$$

The umbral nature of these identities is a bit more clear by writing them in terms of the [falling factorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falling_factorial) $(s)_n$. The first few terms of the sin series are

$$s - \frac{(s)_3}{3!} + \frac{(s)_5}{5!} - \frac{(s)_7}{7!} + \cdots$$

which can be recognized as resembling the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) for sin *x*, with (*s*)<sub>*n*</sub> standing in the place of *x*<sup>*n*</sup>.

In [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) it is of interest to sum

$$\!\sum_{k=0}B_k z^k,$$

where *B* are the [Bernoulli numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_numbers). Employing the generating function its Borel sum can be evaluated as

$$\sum_{k=0}B_k z^k= \int_0^\infty e^{-t} \frac{t z}{e^{t z}-1} \, dt= \sum_{k=1}\frac z{(k z+1)^2}.$$

The general relation gives the Newton series

$$\sum_{k=0}\frac{B_k(x)}{z^k}\frac{{1-s\choose k}}{s-1}= z^{s-1}\zeta(s,x+z),$$

where $\zeta$ is the [Hurwitz zeta function](wikipedia://en/406952) and $B_k(x)$ the [Bernoulli polynomial](wikipedia://en/228161). The series does not converge, the identity holds formally.

Another identity is $\frac 1{\Gamma(x)}= \sum_{k=0}^\infty {x-a\choose k}\sum_{j=0}^k \frac{(-1)^{k-j}}{\Gamma(a+j)}{k\choose j},$ which converges for $x>a$. This follows from the general form of a Newton series for equidistant nodes (when it exists, i.e. is convergent)

$$f(x)=\sum_{k=0}{\frac{x-a}h \choose k} \sum_{j=0}^k (-1)^{k-j}{k\choose j}f(a+j h).$$
