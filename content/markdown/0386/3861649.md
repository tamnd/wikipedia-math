# Split-octonion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Split-octonion"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **split-octonions** are an 8-dimensional [nonassociative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonassociative) algebra over the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438). Unlike the standard [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436), they contain non-zero elements which are non-invertible. Also the [signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_%28quadratic_form%29) of their [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) differ: the split-octonions have a split signature (4,4) whereas the octonions have a positive-definite signature (8,0).

:::

Up to isomorphism, the octonions and the split-octonions are the only two 8-dimensional [composition algebras](wikipedia://en/3861635) over the real numbers. They are also the only two [octonion algebras](wikipedia://en/10277277) over the real numbers. Split-octonion algebras analogous to the split-octonions can be defined over any [field](wikipedia://en/10603).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Cayley–Dickson construction {#Cayley–Dickson_construction}

The octonions and the split-octonions can be obtained from the [Cayley–Dickson construction](wikipedia://en/175609) by defining a multiplication on pairs of [quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternions). We introduce a new imaginary unit ℓ and write a pair of [quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternions) (*a*, *b*) in the form *a* + *b*ℓ. The product is defined by the rule:[^o5]

$$(a + b \ell)(c + d \ell) = (ac + \lambda \bar db) + (da + b\bar c)\ell$$

where

$$\lambda = \ell^2.$$

If *λ* is chosen to be −1, we get the octonions. If, instead, it is taken to be +1 we get the split-octonions. One can also obtain the split-octonions via a Cayley–Dickson doubling of the [split-quaternions](wikipedia://en/1461265). Here either choice of *λ* (±1) gives the split-octonions.

### Multiplication table {#Multiplication_table}

*Image: SplitFanoPlane.svg.* A mnemonic for the products of the split octonions.

A [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) for the split-octonions is given by the set $\{\ 1,\ i,\ j,\ k,\ \ell,\ \ell i,\ \ell j,\ \ell k\ \}$.

Every split-octonion $x$ can be written as a [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of the basis elements,

$$x = x_0 + x_1\,i + x_2\,j + x_3\,k + x_4\,\ell + x_5\,\ell i + x_6\,\ell j + x_7\,\ell k,$$

with real coefficients $x_a$.

By linearity, multiplication of split-octonions is completely determined by the following [multiplication table](wikipedia://en/57122):

A convenient [mnemonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mnemonic) is given by the diagram at the right, which represents the multiplication table for the split-octonions. This one is derived from its parent octonion (one of 480 possible), which is defined by:

$$e_i e_j = - \delta_{ij}e_0 + \varepsilon _{ijk} e_k,\,$$

where $\delta_{ij}$ is the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890) and $\varepsilon _{ijk}$ is the [Levi-Civita symbol](wikipedia://en/195982) with value $+1$ when $ijk = 123, 154, 176, 264, 257, 374, 365,$ and:

$$e_ie_0 = e_0e_i = e_i;\,\,\,\,e_0e_0 = e_0 ,$$

with $e_0$ the scalar element, and $i, j, k = 1 ... 7.$

The red arrows indicate possible direction reversals imposed by negating the lower right quadrant of the parent creating a split octonion with this multiplication table.

### Conjugate, norm and inverse {#Conjugate,_norm_and_inverse}

The *conjugate* of a split-octonion *x* is given by

$$\bar x = x_0 - x_1\,i - x_2\,j - x_3\,k - x_4\,\ell - x_5\,\ell i - x_6\,\ell j - x_7\,\ell k ,$$

just as for the octonions.

The [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) on *x* is given by

$$N(x) = \bar x x = (x_0^2 + x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2) - (x_4^2 + x_5^2 + x_6^2 + x_7^2) .$$

This quadratic form *N*(*x*) is an [isotropic quadratic form](wikipedia://en/10917170) since there are non-zero split-octonions *x* with *N*(*x*) = 0. With *N*, the split-octonions form a [pseudo-Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/10962546) of eight dimensions over **R**, sometimes written **R**<sup>4,4</sup> to denote the signature of the quadratic form.

If *N*(*x*) ≠ 0, then *x* has a (two-sided) [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940) *x*<sup>−1</sup> given by

$$x^{-1} = N(x)^{-1}{\bar x}.$$

## Properties {#Properties}

The split-octonions, like the octonions, are noncommutative and nonassociative. Also like the octonions, they form a [composition algebra](wikipedia://en/3861635) since the quadratic form *N* is multiplicative. That is,

$$N(xy) = N(x)N(y).$$

The split-octonions satisfy the [Moufang identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moufang_identities) and so form an [alternative algebra](wikipedia://en/3160). Therefore, by [Artin's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin's_theorem), the subalgebra generated by any two elements is associative. The set of all invertible elements (i.e. those elements for which *N*(*x*) ≠ 0) form a [Moufang loop](wikipedia://en/755329).

The automorphism group of the split-octonions is a 14-dimensional [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945), the [split real form](wikipedia://en/22385144#Split_real_form) of the exceptional [simple Lie group](wikipedia://en/292831) [G<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/292864).

:::

## Zorn's vector-matrix algebra {#Zorn's_vector-matrix_algebra}

Since the split-octonions are nonassociative they cannot be represented by ordinary [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) (matrix multiplication is always associative). [Zorn](wikipedia://en/19740) found a way to represent them as "matrices" containing both scalars and vectors using a modified version of matrix multiplication.[^o12] Specifically, define a *vector-matrix* to be a 2×2 matrix of the form[^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16]

$$\begin{bmatrix}a & \mathbf v\\ \mathbf w & b\end{bmatrix} ,$$

where *a* and *b* are real numbers and **v** and **w** are vectors in **R**<sup>3</sup>. Define multiplication of these matrices by the rule

$$\begin{bmatrix}a & \mathbf v\\ \mathbf w & b\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}a' & \mathbf v'\\ \mathbf w' & b'\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}aa' + \mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w' & a\mathbf v' + b'\mathbf v + \mathbf w \times \mathbf w'\\ a'\mathbf w + b\mathbf w' - \mathbf v\times\mathbf v' & bb' + \mathbf v'\cdot\mathbf w \end{bmatrix}$$

where · and × are the ordinary [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) and [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092) of 3-vectors. With addition and scalar multiplication defined as usual the set of all such matrices forms a nonassociative unital 8-dimensional algebra over the reals, called **Zorn's vector-matrix algebra**.

Define the "[determinant](wikipedia://en/8468)" of a vector-matrix by the rule

$$\det\begin{bmatrix}a & \mathbf v\\ \mathbf w & b\end{bmatrix} = ab - \mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w.$$

This determinant is a quadratic form on Zorn's algebra which satisfies the composition rule:

$$\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B).\,$$

Zorn's vector-matrix algebra is, in fact, isomorphic to the algebra of split-octonions. Write an octonion $x$ in the form

$$x = (a + \mathbf v) + \ell(b + \mathbf w)$$

where $a$ and $b$ are real numbers and **v** and **w** are pure imaginary quaternions regarded as vectors in **R**<sup>3</sup>. The isomorphism from the split-octonions to Zorn's algebra is given by

$$x\mapsto \phi(x) = \begin{bmatrix}a + b & \mathbf v + \mathbf w \\ -\mathbf v + \mathbf w & a - b\end{bmatrix}.$$

This isomorphism preserves the norm since $N(x) = \det(\phi(x))$.

## Applications {#Applications}

Split-octonions are used in the description of physical law. For example:

- The [Dirac equation](wikipedia://en/39407) in physics (the equation of motion of a free spin 1/2 particle, e.g. an electron or a proton) can be expressed on native split-octonion arithmetic.[^o19]
- [Supersymmetric quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersymmetric_quantum_mechanics) has an octonionic extension.[^o20]
- The Zorn-based split-octonion algebra can be used in modeling local gauge symmetric SU(3) quantum chromodynamics.[^o21]
- The problem of a ball rolling without slipping on a ball of radius 3 times as large has the split real form of the exceptional group [G<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/292864) as its symmetry group, because this problem can be described using split-octonions.[^o22]
