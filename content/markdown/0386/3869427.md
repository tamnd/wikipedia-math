# Synthetic differential geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Synthetic differential geometry"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **synthetic differential geometry** is a formalization of the theory of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) in the language of [topos theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topos_theory). There are several insights that allow for such a reformulation. The first is that most of the analytic data for describing the class of [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) can be encoded into certain [fibre bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibre_bundle) on manifolds: namely bundles of [jets](wikipedia://en/1521924) (see also [jet bundle](wikipedia://en/928060)). The second insight is that the operation of assigning a bundle of jets to a smooth manifold is [functorial](wikipedia://en/10987) in nature. The third insight is that over a certain [category](wikipedia://en/5869), these are [representable functors](wikipedia://en/844461). Furthermore, their representatives are related to the algebras of [dual numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_numbers), so that [smooth infinitesimal analysis](wikipedia://en/3869419) may be used.

:::

Synthetic differential geometry can serve as a platform for formulating certain otherwise obscure or confusing notions from differential geometry. For example, the meaning of what it means to be *natural* (or *invariant*) has a particularly simple expression, even though the formulation in classical differential geometry may be quite difficult.
