# Spherinder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spherinder"}

*Image: Couronne solide.svg.* The **spherinder** can be seen as the volume between two parallel and equal solid 2-[spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) (3-balls) in 4-dimensional space, here stereographically projected into 3D.

In [four-dimensional geometry](wikipedia://en/1364622), the **spherinder**, or **spherical cylinder** or **spherical prism**, is a geometric object, defined as the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of a 3-[ball](wikipedia://en/160556) (or solid 2-[sphere](wikipedia://en/27859)) of radius *r*<sub>1</sub> and a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) of length 2*r*<sub>2</sub>:

:::

$$D = \{ (x,y,z,w) | x^2+y^2+z^2\leq r_1^2,\ w^2\leq r_2^2 \}$$

Like the [duocylinder](wikipedia://en/2114445), it is also analogous to a [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29) in 3-space, which is the Cartesian product of a disk with a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860). It is a [rotatope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotatope) and a [toratope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toratope).

It can be seen in 3-dimensional space by [stereographic projection](wikipedia://en/143431) as two concentric spheres, in a similar way that a [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112) (cubic prism) can be projected as two concentric cubes, and how a [circular cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_cylinder) can be projected into 2-dimensional space as two concentric circles.

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Spherindrical coordinate system"}

## Spherindrical coordinate system {#Spherindrical_coordinate_system}

One can define a "spherindrical" coordinate system $(r, \theta, \varphi, w)$, consisting of [spherical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_coordinates) with an extra coordinate $w$. This is analogous to how [cylindrical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindrical_coordinates) are defined: $r$ and $\varphi$ being [polar coordinates](wikipedia://en/25120) with an elevation coordinate $z$. Spherindrical coordinates can be converted to Cartesian coordinates using the formulas

$$\begin{aligned} x &= r \cos \varphi \sin \theta \\ y &= r \sin \varphi \sin \theta \\ z &= r \cos \theta \\ w &= w \end{aligned}$$

where $r$ is the radius, $\theta$ is the zenith angle, $\varphi$ is the azimuthal angle, and $w$ is the height. Cartesian coordinates can be converted to spherindrical coordinates using the formulas

$$\begin{aligned} r &= \sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2} \\ \varphi &= \arctan{\frac{y}{x}} \\ \theta &= \operatorname{arccot}{\frac{z}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}} \\ w &= w \end{aligned}$$

The [hypervolume element](wikipedia://en/1920694) for spherindrical coordinates is $\mathrm{d}H = r^2\sin{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}w,$ which can be derived by computing the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351).

:::

## Measurements {#Measurements}

### Hypervolume {#Hypervolume}

Given a spherinder with a spherical base of radius $r$ and a height $h$, the hypervolume of the spherinder is given by

$$H=\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 h$$

### Surface volume {#Surface_volume}

The surface volume of a spherinder, like the surface area of a cylinder, is made up of three parts:

- the volume of the top base: $\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3$
- the volume of the bottom base: $\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3$
- the volume of the lateral 3D surface: $4 \pi r^2 h$, which is the surface area of the spherical base times the height

Therefore, the total surface volume is

$$SV=\frac{8}{3}\pi r^3 + 4\pi r^2 h$$

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

The above formulas for hypervolume and surface volume can be proven using integration. The hypervolume of an arbitrary 4D region is given by the quadruple integral

$$H=\iiiint\limits_{D} \mathrm{d}H$$

The hypervolume of the spherinder can be integrated over spherindrical coordinates.

$$H_\mathrm{spherinder}=\iiiint\limits_{D} \mathrm{d}H = \int_{0}^{h}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{R}r^2\sin{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}w = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 h$$

:::

## Related 4-polytopes {#Related_4-polytopes}

*Image: Truncated icosidodecahedral prism.png.* The related [truncated icosidodecahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_icosidodecahedral_prism) is constructed from two [truncated icosidodecahedra](wikipedia://en/286534) connected by [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), shown here in [stereographic projection](wikipedia://en/143431) with some prisms hidden.

The spherinder is related to the [uniform prismatic polychora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polychoron#Prismatic_uniform_polychora), which are [Cartesian products](wikipedia://en/24104095) of a regular or semiregular [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) and a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860). There are eighteen convex uniform prisms based on the [Platonic](wikipedia://en/23905) and [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847) ([tetrahedral prism](wikipedia://en/5977290), [truncated tetrahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_tetrahedral_prism), [cubic prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_prism), [cuboctahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuboctahedral_prism), [octahedral prism](wikipedia://en/8813885), [rhombicuboctahedral prism](wikipedia://en/28998802), [truncated cubic prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_cubic_prism), [truncated octahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_octahedral_prism), [truncated cuboctahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_cuboctahedral_prism), [snub cubic prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snub_cubic_prism), [dodecahedral prism](wikipedia://en/5981640), [icosidodecahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosidodecahedral_prism), [icosahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_prism), [truncated dodecahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_dodecahedral_prism), [rhombicosidodecahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombicosidodecahedral_prism), [truncated icosahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_icosahedral_prism), [truncated icosidodecahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_icosidodecahedral_prism), [snub dodecahedral prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snub_dodecahedral_prism)), plus an infinite family based on [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851), and another infinite family of uniform [duoprisms](wikipedia://en/2114155), which are products of two [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306).
