# Roman Bezrukavnikov

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Roman Bezrukavnikov"}

**Roman Bezrukavnikov** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Роман Безрукавников; born July 1, 1973) is a Russian-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) born in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow).[^o3] He is a mathematics professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and the chief research fellow at the [HSE International Laboratory of Representation Theory and Mathematical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSE_Faculty_of_Mathematics) who specializes in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

He graduated from [Moscow State School 57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_School_57) mathematical class in 1990,[^o7] and earned an [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) in 1994. He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_philosophy) in mathematics from [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University) in 1998 under the supervision of [Joseph N. Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_N._Bernstein).

Bezrukavnikov was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 1996-1998 and again in 2007–2008.[^o9] He was a Dickson Instructor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1999-2001. In 2001 he was awarded a [Clay Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_Research_Fellowship),[^o10] and in 2004, he won a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) from the [Alfred P. Sloan Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan_Foundation).[^o11][^o12] He was awarded a Simons Fellowship in Mathematics by the [Simons Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Foundation) in 2014, and again in 2020.[^o13]
