# Integral graph

*Image: Integral and Non-Integral graphs.svg.* The blue graph, [$C_{4}$](wikipedia://en/878962), is one of the only integral [cycle graphs](wikipedia://en/878962), whose [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) has [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) $0, 0, 2, -2$. The red graph is *not* integral, as its eigenvalues are $0, -1, \frac{\sqrt{17} + 1}{2}, \frac{-\sqrt{17} + 1}{2}$.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Integral graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **integral graph** is a graph whose [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463)'s [spectrum](wikipedia://en/534914) consists entirely of integers. In other words, a graph is an integral graph if all of the [roots](wikipedia://en/264210) of the [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of its adjacency matrix are integers.

:::

The notion was introduced in 1974 by [Frank Harary](wikipedia://en/1731731) and Allen Schwenk.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K<sub>n</sub>* is integral for all *n*.[^o3]
- The only [cycle graphs](wikipedia://en/878962) that are integral are $C_3$, $C_4$, and $C_6$.[^o3]
- If a graph is integral, then so is its [complement graph](wikipedia://en/1175649); for instance, the complements of complete graphs, [edgeless graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgeless_graph), are integral. If two graphs are integral, then so is their [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/2856852) and [strong product](wikipedia://en/11494014);[^o3] for instance, the Cartesian products of two complete graphs, the [rook's graphs](wikipedia://en/7324959), are integral.[^o6] Similarly, the [hypercube graphs](wikipedia://en/6706815), as Cartesian products of any number of complete graphs $K_2$, are integral.[^o3]
- The [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231) of a regular integral graph is again integral. For instance, as the line graph of $K_4$, the [octahedral graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedral_graph) is integral, and as the complement of the line graph of $K_5$, the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) is integral.[^o3]
- Among the [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) [symmetric graphs](wikipedia://en/1577896) the [utility graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_graph), the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926), the [Nauru graph](wikipedia://en/24052345) and the [Desargues graph](wikipedia://en/1578059) are integral.
- The [Higman–Sims graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higman–Sims_graph), the [Hall–Janko graph](wikipedia://en/1110667), the [Clebsch graph](wikipedia://en/23970662), the [Hoffman–Singleton graph](wikipedia://en/4112667), the [Shrikhande graph](wikipedia://en/13672415) and the [Hoffman graph](wikipedia://en/24211555) are integral.
- A [regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) is [periodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous-time_quantum_walk#Periodic_graphs) if and only if it is an integral graph.
- A [walk-regular graph](wikipedia://en/54617362) that admits [perfect state transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous-time_quantum_walk#Perfect_state_transfer) is an integral graph.
- The [Sudoku graphs](wikipedia://en/59925424), graphs whose vertices represent cells of a Sudoku board and whose edges represent cells that should not be equal, are integral.[^o7]
