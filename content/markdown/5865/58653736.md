# Unramified morphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unramified morphism"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), an **unramified morphism** is a [morphism](wikipedia://en/7482797) $f: X \to Y$ of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) such that (a) it is locally of finite presentation and (b) for each $x \in X$ and $y = f(x)$, we have that

:::

1. The [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) $k(x)$ is a [separable algebraic extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_algebraic_extension) of $k(y)$.
1. $f^{\#}(\mathfrak{m}_y) \mathcal{O}_{x, X} = \mathfrak{m}_x,$ where $f^{\#}: \mathcal{O}_{y, Y} \to \mathcal{O}_{x, X}$ and $\mathfrak{m}_y, \mathfrak{m}_x$ are [maximal ideals](wikipedia://en/48164) of the [local rings](wikipedia://en/153391).

A flat unramified morphism is called an [étale morphism](wikipedia://en/3022771). Less strongly, if $f$ satisfies the conditions when restricted to sufficiently small neighborhoods of $x$ and $y$, then $f$ is said to be unramified near $x$.

Some authors prefer to use weaker conditions, in which case they call a morphism satisfying the above a **G-unramified morphism**.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Simple example"}

## Simple example {#Simple_example}

Let $A$ be a ring and *B* the ring obtained by adjoining an [integral element](wikipedia://en/9478630) to *A*; i.e., $B = A[t]/(F)$ for some [monic polynomial](wikipedia://en/314730) *F*. Then $\operatorname{Spec}(B) \to \operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is unramified if and only if the polynomial *F* is [separable](wikipedia://en/362400) (i.e., it and its derivative generate the unit ideal of $A[t]$).

:::

## Curve case {#Curve_case}

Let $f: X \to Y$ be a [finite morphism](wikipedia://en/2139357) between [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety) connected [curves](wikipedia://en/89246) over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018), *P* a [closed point](wikipedia://en/33086408) of *X* and $Q = f(P)$. We then have the local ring homomorphism $f^{\#} : \mathcal{O}_Q \to \mathcal{O}_P$ where $(\mathcal{O}_Q, \mathfrak{m}_Q)$ and $(\mathcal{O}_P, \mathfrak{m}_P)$ are the local rings at *Q* and *P* of *Y* and *X*. Since $\mathcal{O}_P$ is a [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633), there is a unique integer $e_P > 0$ such that $f^{\#} (\mathfrak{m}_Q) \mathcal{O}_P = {\mathfrak{m}_P}^{e_P}$. The integer $e_P$ is called the **ramification index** of $P$ over $Q$.[^o5] Since $k(P) = k(Q)$ as the base field is algebraically closed, $f$ is unramified at $P$ (in fact, [étale](wikipedia://en/3022771)) if and only if $e_P = 1$. Otherwise, $f$ is said to be ramified at *P* and *Q* is called a [branch point](wikipedia://en/483173).

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterization"}

## Characterization {#Characterization}

Given a morphism $f: X \to Y$ that is locally of finite presentation, the following are equivalent:[^o8]

1. *f* is unramified.
1. The [diagonal map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_embedding) $\delta_f: X \to X \times_Y X$ is an open immersion.
1. The relative [cotangent sheaf](wikipedia://en/44901757) $\Omega_{X/Y}$ is zero.

:::
