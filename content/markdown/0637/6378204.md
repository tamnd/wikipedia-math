# Gauss sum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gauss sum"}

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), a **Gauss sum** or **Gaussian sum** is a particular kind of finite [sum](wikipedia://en/246160) of [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity), typically

:::

$$G(\chi) := G(\chi, \psi)= \sum \chi(r)\cdot \psi(r)$$

where the sum is over elements $r$ of some [finite](wikipedia://en/23706953) [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $R$, $\psi$ is a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) of the [additive group](wikipedia://en/510523) $R^{+}$ into the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071), and $\chi$ is a group homomorphism of the [unit group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_group) $R^{\times}$ into the unit circle, extended to non-unit $r$, where it takes the value 0. Gauss sums are the analogues for finite fields of the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316).[^o2]

Such sums are ubiquitous in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). They occur, for example, in the functional equations of [Dirichlet $L$-functions](wikipedia://en/400092), where for a [Dirichlet character](wikipedia://en/174515) $\chi$ the equation relating $L(s, \chi)$ and $L(1 - s, \overline{\chi}$) (where $\overline{\chi}$ is the [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) of $\chi$) involves a factor

$$\frac{ G(\chi) }{ |G(\chi)| }.$$

## History {#History}

The case originally considered by [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) was the [quadratic Gauss sum](wikipedia://en/595898), for $R$ the [field of residues](wikipedia://en/10789680) [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$, and $\chi$ the [Legendre symbol](wikipedia://en/18567). In this case Gauss proved that $G(\chi) = p^{}$ or $ip^{}$ for $p$ congruent to 1 or 3 modulo 4 respectively (the quadratic Gauss sum can also be evaluated by Fourier analysis as well as by [contour integration](wikipedia://en/704359)).

An alternate form for this Gauss sum is

$$\sum e^{2 \pi i r^2/p}.$$

Quadratic Gauss sums are closely connected with the theory of [theta functions](wikipedia://en/449745).

The general theory of Gauss sums was developed in the early 19th century, with the use of [Jacobi sums](wikipedia://en/3142215) and their [prime decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_decomposition) in [cyclotomic fields](wikipedia://en/30872462). Gauss sums over a residue ring of integers $\bmod N$ are linear combinations of closely related sums called [Gaussian periods](wikipedia://en/456085).

The absolute value of Gauss sums is usually found as an application of [Plancherel's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plancherel's_theorem) on finite groups. In the case where $R$ is a field of $p$ elements and $\chi$ is nontrivial, the absolute value is $p^{}$. The determination of the exact value of general Gauss sums, following the result of Gauss on the quadratic case, is a long-standing issue. For some cases see [Kummer sum](wikipedia://en/6006062).

## Properties of Gauss sums of Dirichlet characters {#Properties_of_Gauss_sums_of_Dirichlet_characters}

The Gauss sum of a [Dirichlet character](wikipedia://en/174515) modulo $N$ is

$$G(\chi)=\sum_{a=1}^N\chi(a)e^{2\pi ia/N}.$$

If $\chi$ is also [primitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_Dirichlet_character), then

$$|G(\chi)|=\sqrt{N},$$

in particular, it is nonzero. More generally, if $N_{0}$ is the [conductor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductor_of_a_Dirichlet_character) of $\chi$ and $\chi_{0}$ is the primitive Dirichlet character modulo $N_{0}$ that induces $\chi$, then the Gauss sum of $\chi$ is related to that of $\chi_{0}$ by

$$G(\chi)=\mu\left(\frac{N}{N_0}\right)\chi_0\left(\frac{N}{N_0}\right)G\left(\chi_0\right)$$

where $\mu$ is the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961). Consequently, $G(\chi)$ is non-zero precisely when $\tfrac{N}{N_{0}}$ is [squarefree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squarefree) and [relatively prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_prime) to $N_{0}$.[^o5]

Other relations between $G(\chi)$ and Gauss sums of other characters include

$$G(\overline{\chi})=\chi(-1)\overline{G(\chi)},$$

where $\overline{\chi}$ is the complex conjugate Dirichlet character, and if $\chi'$ is a Dirichlet character modulo $N'$ such that $N$ and $N'$ are relatively prime, then

$$G\left(\chi\chi^\prime\right) = \chi\left(N^\prime\right) \chi^\prime(N) G(\chi) G\left(\chi^\prime\right).$$

The relation among $G(\chi\chi')$, $G(\chi)$, and $G(\chi')$ when $\chi$ and $\chi'$ are of the *same* modulus (and $\chi\chi'$ is primitive) is measured by the [Jacobi sum](wikipedia://en/3142215) $J(\chi, \chi')$. Specifically,

$$G\left(\chi\chi^\prime\right)=\frac{G(\chi)G\left(\chi^\prime\right)}{J\left(\chi,\chi^\prime\right)}.$$

## Further properties {#Further_properties}

- Gauss sums can be used to prove [quadratic reciprocity](wikipedia://en/3090886), [cubic reciprocity](wikipedia://en/6299003), and [quartic reciprocity](wikipedia://en/20131133).
- Gauss sums can be used to calculate the number of solutions of polynomial equations over finite fields, and thus can be used to calculate certain zeta functions.
