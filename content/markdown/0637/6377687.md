# Arthur W. Conway

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur W. Conway"}

**Arthur William Conway** [^o3] [PRIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy#Presidents) (2 October 1875 – 11 July 1950) was a distinguished Irish mathematician and [mathematical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physicist) who wrote one of the first books on [relativity](wikipedia://en/30001) and co-edited two volumes of [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817)'s collected works. He also served as President of [University College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Dublin) between 1940 and 1947.[^o5]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

*Image: Arthur W Conway plaque.jpg.* Memorial plaque in [Wexford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wexford) town

Born in [Wexford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wexford), he received his early education in [St Peter's College, Wexford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Peter's_College,_Wexford) and proceeded to enter old University College, Dublin in 1892. He received his BA degree from the [Royal University of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_University_of_Ireland) in 1896 with honours in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), English, [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [Natural Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Philosophy). In 1897, he received his MA degree with highest honours in mathematics and proceeded to [Corpus Christi College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Oxford), becoming University Scholar there in 1898, and studying under [Augustus Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_Love).[^o5] Also in 1901, he was appointed to the professorship of [Mathematical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Physics) in the old University College Dublin and held the chair until the creation of the new college in 1909 until 1940. He also taught for a short time at [St. Patrick's College, Maynooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Patrick's_College,_Maynooth).

He married Agnes Christina Bingham on 19 August 1903; they had three daughters and one son.

One of Conway's students was [Éamon de Valera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Éamon_de_Valera), whom he introduced to Hamilton's [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440).[^o5] De Valera warmed to the subject and engaged in research of this novelty of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384). Later, when de Valera became Taoiseach (he was also subsequently [President of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ireland)), he called upon Conway while forming the [Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies).[^o6]

Conway's earliest publications, dating back to 1903, were on the electromagnetic theory. He is remembered for his application of [biquaternion](wikipedia://en/1207070) algebra to the [special theory of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_theory_of_relativity), and in 1915 published a 43-page tract "Relativity" in Edinburgh. He published an article[^o7] in 1911, and in 1912 asserted priority over [Ludwik Silberstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwik_Silberstein), who also applied biquaternions to relativity.[^o8] This claim was backed up by [George Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Temple_%28mathematician%29) in his book *100 Years of Mathematics*.[^o9] In 1947 Conway put quaternions to use with rotations in hyperbolic space.[^o10] The next year he published [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) applications[^o11] which were referred to in a PhD thesis[^o12] by [J. Lambek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Lambek) in 1950.

In 1918, he was the [Irish Parliamentary Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Parliamentary_Party) candidate in [South Londonderry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Londonderry_%28UK_Parliament_constituency%29) and in the [National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Ireland_%28constituency%29), coming second in both.[^o13]

Arthur Conway continued his scholarship in the fields of mathematics and theoretical physics, and made a special study of [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817). With [J. L. Synge](wikipedia://en/1252509), he edited the first volume of Hamilton's mathematical papers[^o14] and with A. J. McConnell he edited the second volume of Hamilton's mathematical papers.[^o15] Conway was also active in college life, being appointed [Registrar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Registrar_%28education%29), a position he occupied until his election as president in 1940.[^o16] He retired in 1947 from the presidency of UCD. In 1953, some of his writings were edited by J. McConnell for publication by the Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies.[^o17]

He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in Toronto in 1924,[^o18] in 1932 in Zurich, and in 1936 in Oslo. He was elected President of the [Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy) from 1937 to 1940.

In October 1975, to mark the centenary of his birth, UCD hosted the AC Conway Memorial Mathematical Symposium. Speakers included [Roger Penrose](wikipedia://en/26193), [Ian Sneddon](wikipedia://en/34942133), and [William B. Bonnor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_B._Bonnor).

In his obituary, [E.T. Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._Whittaker) referred to Conway as the "most distinguished Irish Catholic man of science of his generation."[^o3]

## Books {#Books}

- 1915: [Relativity](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001080682), G. Bell & Sons (Edinburgh Mathematical Tracts No. 3) via [Internet Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Archive)
- 1931: *The Mathematical Papers of Sir William Rowan Hamilton: Volume 1, Geometrical Optics*, Cambridge (edited with [J L Synge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_L_Synge))
- 1940: *The Mathematical Papers of Sir William Rowan Hamilton: Volume 2, Dynamics*, Cambridge (edited with J L Synge)
