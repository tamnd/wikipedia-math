# 3-step group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="3-step group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **3-step group** is a special sort of [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [Fitting length](wikipedia://en/14943054) at most 3, that is used in the classification of [CN groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CN_group) and in the [Feit–Thompson theorem](wikipedia://en/1656473). The definition of a 3-step group in these two cases is slightly different.

:::

## CN groups {#CN_groups}

In the theory of CN groups, a 3-step group (for some [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*) is a group such that:

- $$G = O_{p,p,p}(G)$$
- $O_{p,p}(G)$ is a [Frobenius group](wikipedia://en/1716123) with kernel $O_{p}(G)$
- $G/O_{p}(G)$ is a Frobenius group with kernel $O_{p,p}(G)/O_{p}(G)$

Any 3-step group is a [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012) CN-group, and conversely any solvable CN-group is either [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/144569), or a Frobenius group, or a 3-step group.

Example: the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>4</sub> is a 3-step group for the prime $p = 2$.

## Odd order groups {#Odd_order_groups}

(Feit Thompson 1963, p.780) defined a three-step group to be a group *G* satisfying the following conditions:

- The derived group of *G* is a Hall subgroup with a cyclic complement *Q*.
- If *H* is the maximal normal nilpotent Hall subgroup of *G*, then *G*⊆*H*C<sub>*G*</sub>(*H*)⊆*G* and *H*C<sub>*G*</sub> is nilpotent and *H* is noncyclic.
- For *q*∈*Q* nontrivial, C<sub>*G*</sub>(*q*) is cyclic and non-trivial and independent of *q*.
