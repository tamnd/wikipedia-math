# Yff center of congruence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yff center of congruence"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Yff center of congruence** is a special point associated with a triangle. This special point is a [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488) and Peter Yff initiated the study of this triangle center in 1987.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Isoscelizer"}

## Isoscelizer {#Isoscelizer}

An **isoscelizer** of an angle $A$ in a triangle $\triangle ABC$ is a line through points $P_{1}, Q_{1}$, where $P_{1}$ lies on $AB$ and $Q_{1}$ on $AC$, such that the triangle $\triangle AP_{1}Q_{1}$ is an [isosceles triangle](wikipedia://en/315428). An isoscelizer of angle $A$ is a line [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to the [bisector](wikipedia://en/152547) of angle $A$. Isoscelizers were invented by Peter Yff in 1963.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Yff central circle"}

## Yff central triangle {#Yff_central_triangle}

*Image: Yff center of congruence.svg.* solid black \#2fcc27 \#4f46f2 \#ff9a36 \#ff5252

Let $\triangle ABC$ be any triangle. Let $P_{1}Q_{1}$ be an isoscelizer of angle $A$, $P_{2}Q_{2}$ be an isoscelizer of angle $B$, and $P_{3}Q_{3}$ be an isoscelizer of angle $C$. Let $\triangle A'B'C'$ be the triangle formed by the three isoscelizers. The four triangles $\triangle A'P_{2}Q_{3}, \triangle Q_{1}B'P_{3}, \triangle P_{1}Q_{2}C',$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ are always [similar](wikipedia://en/39377).

There is a unique set of three isoscelizers $P_{1}Q_{1}, P_{2}Q_{2}, P_{3}Q_{3}$ such that the four triangles $\triangle A'P_{2}Q_{3}, \triangle Q_{1}B'P_{3}, \triangle P_{1}Q_{2}C',$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330). In this special case $\triangle A'B'C'$ formed by the three isoscelizers is called the **Yff central triangle** of $\triangle ABC$.[^o8]

The [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) of the Yff central triangle is called the **Yff central circle** of the triangle.

:::

## Yff center of congruence {#Yff_center_of_congruence}

*Image: Yff center of congruence.gif.* Animation showing the continuous shrinking of the Yff central triangle to the Yff center of congruence. The animation also shows the continuous expansion of the Yff central triangle until the three outer triangles reduce to points on the sides of the triangle.

Let $\triangle ABC$ be any triangle. Let $P_{1}Q_{1}, P_{2}Q_{2}, P_{3}Q_{3}$ be the isoscelizers of the angles $A, B, C$ such that the triangle $\triangle A'B'C'$ formed by them is the Yff central triangle of $\triangle ABC$. The three isoscelizers $P_{1}Q_{1}, P_{2}Q_{2}, P_{3}Q_{3}$ are continuously parallel-shifted such that the three triangles $\triangle A'P_{2}Q_{3}, \triangle Q_{1}B'P_{3}, \triangle P_{1}Q_{2}C'$ are always congruent to each other until $\triangle A'B'C'$ formed by the intersections of the isoscelizers reduces to a point. The point to which $\triangle A'B'C'$ reduces to is called the **Yff center of congruence** of $\triangle ABC$.

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Yff central triangle and its excircles.svg.* Any triangle $\triangle ABC$ is the triangle formed by the lines which are externally tangent to the three [excircles](wikipedia://en/161260) of the Yff central triangle of $\triangle ABC$.

- The [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) of the Yff center of congruence are[^o2] $$\sec\frac{A}{2} : \sec\frac{B}{2} : \sec\frac{C}{2}$$
- Any triangle $\triangle ABC$ is the triangle formed by the lines which are externally tangent to the three excircles of the Yff central triangle of $\triangle ABC$.
- Let $I$ be the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) of $\triangle ABC$. Let $D$ be the point on side $BC$ such that $\angle BID = \angle DIC$, $E$ a point on side $CA$ such that $\angle CIE = \angle EIA$, and $F$ a point on side $AB$ such that $\angle AIF = \angle FIB$. Then the lines $AD, BE, CF$ are concurrent at the Yff center of congruence. This fact gives a geometrical construction for locating the Yff center of congruence.[^o12]
- A computer assisted search of the properties of the Yff central triangle has generated several interesting results relating to properties of the Yff central triangle.[^o13]

## Generalization {#Generalization}

The geometrical construction for locating the Yff center of congruence has an interesting generalization. The generalisation begins with an arbitrary point $P$ in the plane of a triangle $\triangle ABC$. Then points $D, E, F$ are taken on the sides $BC, CA, AB$ such that

$$\angle BPD = \angle DPC, \quad \angle CPE = \angle EPA, \quad \angle APF = \angle FPB.$$

The generalization asserts that the lines $AD, BE, CF$ are concurrent.[^o12]
