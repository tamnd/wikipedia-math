# Nancy Cole (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nancy Cole"}

**Nancy Cole** (October 15, 1902 – July 7, 1991) was an American mathematician who made important and pioneering contributions to [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Cole was the daughter of a Boston grocer and the descendant of [Mayflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayflower) immigrants. As a high school student in [Plymouth, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth,_Massachusetts), her interest in mathematics was sparked by teacher Lucia Richardson. She studied at [Jackson College for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_College_for_Women), the women's sister school of [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University), from 1920 to 1922, mentored there by mathematician [Edith Bush](wikipedia://en/67399485), before transferring to [Vassar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_College), where she earned a bachelor's degree in 1924. She returned to study at [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College) in 1926, earned a master's degree at Radcliffe in 1929, and completed her Ph.D. in 1934. Her dissertation, *The Index Form Associated with an Extremaloid*, was supervised by [Marston Morse](wikipedia://en/856523), and she was acknowledged by Morse for her assistance in preparing his 1934 book *The Calculus of Variations*, which began the study of [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560).

## Career and later life {#Career_and_later_life}

Between finishing at Vassar and beginning graduate study at Radcliffe, Cole taught college preparatory courses in [Hartford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford,_Connecticut), and while at Radcliffe she worked as an instructor at Vassar in 1927–1928 and at [Wells College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_College) in 1931–1932 before taking a faculty position at [Sweet Briar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Briar_College) in 1933, taking a position previously held by Ethel Moody and working at Sweet Briar with [Eugenie Maria Morenus](wikipedia://en/67125555).

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), she moved from Sweet Briar to [Kenyon College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyon_College), where from 1943 to 1944 she taught mathematics for the Army. After working from 1944 to 1947 at the [Connecticut College for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_College_for_Women), she became an assistant professor of mathematics at [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University) in 1947. She was promoted to associate professor in 1952, and retired as associate professor emeritus in 1971.

She died of a heart attack on July 7, 1991, in Plymouth, Massachusetts.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Cole was named a Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 1950.
