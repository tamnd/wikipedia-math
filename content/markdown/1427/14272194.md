# Saint-Venant's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Saint-Venant's theorem"}

In [solid mechanics](wikipedia://en/458866), it is common to analyze the properties of [beams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beam_%28structure%29) with constant cross section. **Saint-Venant's theorem** states that the [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) cross section with maximal [torsional](wikipedia://en/3039253) rigidity is a circle.[^o2] It is named after the French mathematician [Adhémar Jean Claude Barré de Saint-Venant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhémar_Jean_Claude_Barré_de_Saint-Venant).

:::

Given a [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) domain *D* in the plane with area *A*, $\rho$ the radius of its greatest inscribed circle, the torsional rigidity *P* of *D* is defined by

$$P= 4\sup_f \frac{\left( \iint\limits_D f\, dx\, dy\right)^2}{\iint\limits_D {f_x}^2+{f_y}^2\, dx\, dy}.$$

Here the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) is taken over all the continuously differentiable functions vanishing on the boundary of *D*. The existence of this supremum is a consequence of [Poincaré inequality](wikipedia://en/4722074).

Saint-Venant[^o3] conjectured in 1856 that of all domains *D* of equal area *A* the circular one has the greatest torsional rigidity, that is

$$P \le P_{\text{circle}} \le \frac{A^2}{2 \pi}.$$

A rigorous proof of this inequality was not given until 1948 by [Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya).[^o4] Another proof was given by [Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111) and reported in.[^o5] A more general proof and an estimate

$$P< 4 \rho^2 A$$

is given by Makai.[^o2]
