# Nakayama algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nakayama algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Nakayama algebra** or **generalized uniserial algebra** is an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) such that each left or right [indecomposable](wikipedia://en/810549) [projective module](wikipedia://en/364488) has a unique [composition series](wikipedia://en/297493). They were studied by (txt) who called them "generalized uni-serial rings". These algebras were further studied by (txt) and later by (txt), by (txt) and by (txt).

:::

An example of a Nakayama algebra is *k*\[*x*\]/(*x*<sup>*n*</sup>) for *k* a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) and *n* a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563).

Current usage of *uniserial* differs slightly: an explanation of the difference appears [here](wikipedia://en/31157948#Notes_on_alternate,_similar_and_related_terms).
