# BGG correspondence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="BGG correspondence"}

In mathematics, the **Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand correspondence** or **BGG correspondence** for short is the first example of the [Koszul duality](wikipedia://en/42478011).[^o2]

:::

Established by [Joseph Bernstein](wikipedia://en/5815383), [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643), and Sergei Gelfand,[^o3] the correspondence is an explicit triangulated equivalence that relates the bounded [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) of [coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_sheaves) on the projective space $\mathbb{P}(V)$ and the [stable category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_category) of graded modules $\operatorname{gr} \wedge V$ over the [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) $\wedge V$; i.e.,

$$D^b(\mathbb{P}(V)) \simeq \overline{\operatorname{gr} \wedge V}.$$

In the noncommutative setting, Martínez Villa and Saorín generalized the BGG correspondence to finite-dimensional [self-injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-injective) [Koszul algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koszul_algebras) $A$ with [coherent](wikipedia://en/603780) Koszul duals $A^{!}$.[^o4] Roughly speaking, they proved that the stable category of finite-dimensional graded modules over a finite-dimensional self-injective Koszul algebra $A$ is triangulated equivalent to the bounded derived category of the tails category of the Koszul dual $A^{!}$ (when $A^{!}$ is coherent).
