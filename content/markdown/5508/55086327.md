# Werner Romberg

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Werner Romberg"}

**Werner Romberg** (born 16 May 1909 in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin); died 5 February 2003 in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist).

:::

Romberg studied mathematics and physics from 1928 at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University) and the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) and completed his doctorate in 1933 at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München under the supervision of [Arnold Sommerfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Sommerfeld); his thesis was entitled "Zur Polarisation des Kanalstrahllichtes" \["On the polarisation of channel light beams"\]. At the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München, he studied mathematics under, among others, [Oskar Perron](wikipedia://en/1485469) and [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625). In 1933, as a so-called "[half-Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_is_a_Jew?#Nazism)" in the terminology of the new [National Socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Socialist) government of Germany, he sought to immigrate to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). From 1934 to 1937, he worked as a theoretical physicist in the university of [Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro) (then Dnipropetrovsk). In 1938, he went, via the Institute for Astrophysics in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague), to [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway), where he became an assistant to [Egil Hylleraas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egil_Hylleraas) at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo). He also briefly worked at the [Technical University of Trondheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_University_of_Science_and_Technology) with [Johan Holtsmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Peter_Holtsmark), who was building a [Van de Graaff generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_de_Graaff_generator) there. With the [German occupation of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Norway) he fled to [Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala) in [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden). In 1941 the Nazi German state stripped him of his [German citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_citizenship), and in 1943 recognition of his doctorate was revoked. He became a Norwegian citizen in 1947.[^o3]

After the Second World War, from 1949 to 1968, he was a professor in Trondheim; from 1960 he was head of the applied mathematics department. In Norway, he built up his research group in numerical analysis, and part of the introduction of digital computers, such as GIER, the first computer at Trondheim. From 1968 he held the chair for Mathematical Methods in Natural Sciences and Numerics at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University).
