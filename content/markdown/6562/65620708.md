# Budget-feasible mechanism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Budget-feasible mechanism"}

In [mechanism design](wikipedia://en/689895), a branch of [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), a **budget-feasible mechanism** is a mechanism in which the total payment made by the auctioneer is upper-bounded by a fixed pre-specified budget. They were first presented by [Yaron Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaron_Singer),[^o2] and studied by several others.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Overview and Core Concepts {#Overview_and_Core_Concepts}

**Budget-feasible mechanism design** is a subfield of [algorithmic mechanism design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_mechanism_design) that focuses on procurement [auctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auctions) where the auctioneer (buyer) has a strictly limited budget. Unlike standard mechanism design, which often seeks to maximize [social welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_welfare) or revenue without a hard constraint on the total payment, budget-feasible mechanisms must ensure that the sum of payments to strategic agents (sellers) does not exceed a fixed budget $B$.This field is particularly critical in environments such as [crowdsourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowdsourcing), data acquisition for [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), and environmental conservation, where a central agency must procure services from agents with private costs while adhering to financial limits.

## Model {#Model}

In a typical budget-feasible setting, there is a set of sellers $N$, each possessing a single item or service. Each seller $i$ has a **private cost** $c_i$ for providing their service. The buyer has a **valuation function** $v: 2^N \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ that assigns a value to every subset of sellers. The objective is to design a mechanism—consisting of an [allocation rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allocation_rule) and a [payment rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payment_rule)—that satisfies several key properties:

**Truthfulness (Incentive Compatibility):** Sellers maximize their utility by reporting their true costs $c_i$.

**Individual Rationality:** No seller is paid less than their reported cost.

**Budget Feasibility:** The total payment $\sum p_i$ does not exceed the budget $B$.

**Efficiency (Approximation):** The mechanism aims to maximize the valuation $v(S)$ relative to the optimal subset that could be bought if costs were public.

### The Difficulty of the Budget Constraint {#The_Difficulty_of_the_Budget_Constraint}

Designing budget feasible mechanisms is significantly more challenging than unconstrained auctions (such as those using the [VCG mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickrey–Clarke–Groves_auction)) because the budget constraint couples the allocation and payment rules. In standard auctions, one can often determine the optimal allocation and then calculate payments; in budget-feasible auctions, an allocation that seems optimal might require payments that violate the budget, necessitating a "greedy" approach with carefully calculated thresholds.

## Foundational Developments {#Foundational_Developments}

The field was initiated by [Yaron Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaron_Singer) in 2010. Singer demonstrated that for any [submodular set function](wikipedia://en/33273315), i.e. valuation function where the marginal value of an item decreases as the set of items grows (the law of [diminishing returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diminishing_returns)), a constant-factor approximation mechanism exists and introduced the proportional share mechanism. Subsequent work expanded these results to more complex valuation classes:**Subadditive functions:** Functions where the value of the union of two sets is no more than the sum of their individual values. Early results provided an $O(\log^2 n)$ approximation, while more recent work has improved this to $O(\log n)$ by connecting the problem to the [integrality gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrality_gap) of linear programs and the **approximate core** in [cooperative game theory](wikipedia://en/657958).

## Applications {#Applications}

Budget-feasible mechanisms are widely implemented in [Crowdsourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowdsourcing) applications such as [data labeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_labeling) and [data acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_acquisition) selecting data points to train AI models under a limited procurement budget.
