# John A. Thorpe

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John A. Thorpe"}

**John Alden Thorpe** (February 29, 1936 – January 18, 2021) was an American mathematician, known for contributions to the field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).[^o2]

:::

Thorpe obtained his Bachelor's degree in 1958 from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). His Ph.D. was done at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), under the direction of [James Eells](wikipedia://en/37329912) (*Higher Order Sectional Curvature*).[^o3] From 1963 to 1965, he was *Moore Instructor* at MIT and Assistant Professor at [Haverford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College) in 1965. In 1967 and 1968 he was a visitor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). From 1968, he was Associate Professor and then Professor at the [State University of New York at Stony Brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University) (SUNY). From 1987 he was Professor and Dean at the [State University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Buffalo,_The_State_University_of_New_York) in [Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), and from 1993 at [Queens College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_College,_City_University_of_New_York) of [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York), where he also served as Provost. From 1984 to 1987 he served on the Board of Governors of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772). From 1998 to 2001 he was Executive Director of the [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1561691).

He and [Nigel Hitchin](wikipedia://en/1034164) independently found an inequality between topological invariants, which provides a necessary condition for the existence of [Einstein metrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_metric) on four-dimensional smooth compact manifolds. It is now known as the [Hitchin-Thorpe inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchin-Thorpe_inequality).[^o4] Thorpe died on January 18, 2021, at the age of 84.[^o5]
