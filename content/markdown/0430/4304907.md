# Stanisław Jaśkowski

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Stanisław Jaśkowski"}

**Stanisław Jaśkowski** (Polish pronunciation: s; 22 April 1906, in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) – 16 November 1965, in Warsaw) was a Polish [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) who made important contributions to [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478) and [formal semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_semantics_%28logic%29). He was a student of [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064) and a member of the [Lwów–Warsaw School of Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów–Warsaw_School_of_Logic). He is regarded as one of the founders of [natural deduction](wikipedia://en/51072), which he discovered independently of [Gerhard Gentzen](wikipedia://en/46027) in the 1930s.[^o4] He is also known for his research into [paraconsistent logic](wikipedia://en/421085).[^o5] He was the President (rector) of the [Nicolaus Copernicus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus_University) in [Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toruń).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

He was born in 1906 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) to father Feliks Jaśkowski and mother Kazimiera (Dzierzbicka). In 1924, he graduated from high school in [Zakopane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakopane) and enrolled at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) to study [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). He was taught [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) under [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064) and participated in the Polish Mathematicians' Congresses in [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv) (1927) and [Vilnius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius) (1931). In 1932, Jaśkowski earned his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) on the basis of his thesis *Reguły wnioskowania za pomocą założeń* (Rules of Inference by Means of Assumptions) written under the supervision of Łukasiewicz.[^o7]

After the outbreak of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he participated in the [September Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_Campaign) as a volunteer.[^o8] In 1942, he was briefly imprisoned by the Germans. In 1945, he continued his scientific career at the [University of Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus_University) where co-organized the Departments of Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry.[^o9] In 1946, he defended his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at the Jagiellonian University[^o8] and subsequently assumed the post of the head of the Faculty of Mathematical Logic at the University of Toruń.

Since 1950, he collaborated with the [State Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/67042302) of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) (PAN). Between 1959–1962, he served as the Rector of the University. He was among the founders and served as the first President of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791)'s branch in Toruń.

Jaśkowski is considered to be one of the founders of [natural deduction](wikipedia://en/51072), which he discovered independently of [Gerhard Gentzen](wikipedia://en/46027) in the 1930s.[^o10][^o11] Gentzen's approach initially became more popular with logicians because it could be used to prove the [cut-elimination theorem](wikipedia://en/910234). However, Jaśkowski's is closer to the way that proofs are done in practice. He was also one of the first to propose a formal calculus of [inconsistency-tolerant (or paraconsistent) logic](wikipedia://en/421085). Furthermore, Jaśkowski was a pioneer in the investigation of both [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262) and [free logic](wikipedia://en/1635110).

He died in 1965 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) and was buried at the [Powązki Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powązki_Cemetery).[^o12]
