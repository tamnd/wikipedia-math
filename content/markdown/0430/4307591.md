# Leslie Kish

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Leslie Kish"}

**Leslie Kish** (born **László Kiss**, July 27, 1910 – October 7, 2000) was a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_people)-[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_survey) methodologist.[^o4]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Kish emigrated with his family to the US in 1925. His father soon died, and Kish helped support the family by working while continuing his studies in the evenings. In 1937 he volunteered for the [International Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Brigade) to fight against [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco) in the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War). He saw action in a Hungarian battalion, was wounded, and returned to the United States in 1939.[^o6] In 1939, he finished his baccalaureate in mathematics at the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York).[^o4]

He worked at the U.S. [Bureau of the Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_the_Census) from 1940 until 1941, when he moved to the Division of Program Surveys of the [Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Agriculture). For the remainder of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he served as a [meteorologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorologist) in the [U.S. Army Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Air_Corps). After the war he returned to the Department of Agriculture, but in 1947 he joined the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) faculty.[^o4] He moved to the University of Michigan as a member of the newly created Survey Research Center, which in 1949 became a unit of the new [Institute for Social Research (ISR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan_Institute_for_Social_Research). While working full-time, Kish received an M.A. in mathematical statistics in 1948 and a Ph.D. in sociology in 1952. He became a lecturer at the University of Michigan in 1951, an associate professor in 1956, a professor in 1960 and professor emeritus in 1981".[^o7]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1997, the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) gave Kish their [Wilks Memorial Award](wikipedia://en/15966180). The award citation read: "For being a truly outstanding statistician, who has had a profound influence on sample survey practice throughout the world."[^o9]
