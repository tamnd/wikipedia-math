# Walter Craig (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Walter Craig"}

**Walter L. Craig** (1953 – January 18, 2019)[^o3] was a United States born Canadian mathematician and a [Canada Research Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Research_Chair) in Mathematical Analysis and Applications at [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University), [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario).[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Craig was born in [State College, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_College,_Pennsylvania) in 1953. His father, a professor at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) transferred to [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where Craig and his siblings were raised starting in 1959.

Craig was the son of the logician [William Craig](wikipedia://en/11715384) and the husband of mathematician [Deirdre Haskell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deirdre_Haskell).[^o9]

## Education {#Education}

Craig attended the University of California at Berkeley and, after spending two years performing as a jazz musician, returned there to graduate with a bachelor's degree in mathematics in 1977.[^o11] Craig earned his Ph.D. from [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) - [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) in 1981; his dissertation, *A Bifurcation Theory for Periodic Dissipative Wave Equations*, was supervised by [Louis Nirenberg](wikipedia://en/1814725).

## Career {#Career}

After stints at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), Craig moved to [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University) in [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), Canada in 2000. His research topics included [nonlinear partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/16678376), infinite dimensional [Hamiltonian systems](wikipedia://en/1197531), [Schrödinger operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_operator) and [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713), water waves, [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), and [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology).[^o11]

In 2007, he was made a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada);[^o14] he was awarded a Killam Fellowship in 2009.

In 2013, he became one of the inaugural [Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o15] He served as Director of the [Fields Institute](wikipedia://en/2495768) from 2013 to 2015.
