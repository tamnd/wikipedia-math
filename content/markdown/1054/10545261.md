# T-group (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="T-group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **T-group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) in which the property of [normality](wikipedia://en/21918) is transitive, that is, every [subnormal subgroup](wikipedia://en/2774415) is normal. Here are some facts about T-groups:

:::

- Every [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600) is a T-group.
- Every [quasisimple group](wikipedia://en/1642729) is a T-group.
- Every [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) is a T-group.
- Every [Hamiltonian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_group) is a T-group.
- Every [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/144569) T-group is either abelian or Hamiltonian, because in a nilpotent group, every subgroup is subnormal.
- Every normal subgroup of a T-group is a T-group.
- Every homomorphic image of a T-group is a T-group.
- Every [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012) T-group is [metabelian](wikipedia://en/1959273).

The solvable T-groups were characterized by [Wolfgang Gaschütz](wikipedia://en/75172510) as being exactly the solvable groups *G* with an abelian normal [Hall subgroup](wikipedia://en/1735335) *H* of odd [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_order) such that the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) *G*/*H* is a [Dedekind group](wikipedia://en/153106) and *H* is [acted upon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) by [conjugation](wikipedia://en/49176) as a group of [power automorphisms](wikipedia://en/5632274) by *G*.

A [PT-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PT-group) is a group in which permutability is transitive. A finite T-group is a PT-group.
