# N. M. H. Lightfoot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="N. M. H. Lightfoot"}

**Nicholas Morpeth Hutchinson Lightfoot** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (1902–1962) was a British mathematician and academic administrator. He was an expert on [heat conduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_conduction).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Jarrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarrow) in north-east [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) on 14 October 1902, the son of Thomas Lightfoot. He was educated locally, but excelled, winning a place at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) where he graduated BA in 1923 and continued as a postgraduate, gaining a further MA.

In 1929 he began lecturing in mathematics at [Heriot-Watt College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heriot-Watt_University) in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh). In 1931 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were Sir [Edmund Taylor Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Taylor_Whittaker), [James Cameron Smail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cameron_Smail), Sir [Charles Galton Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Galton_Darwin), and [Edward Thomas Copson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Thomas_Copson).[^o3]

In 1943 he moved to the south of England to take on the role of Principal of [South East Essex Technical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_Essex_Technical_College). In 1950 he moved to head [Chelsea Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_Polytechnic). He served as President of the Association of Technical Institutions 1955–6.

He died in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) on 14 November 1962.

## Family {#Family}

In 1929 he married Janet Moulton (d.1961).
