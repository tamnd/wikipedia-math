# Archaeocryptography

*Image: Stonehenge-Green.jpg.* [Stonehenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehenge) located in [Wiltshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiltshire), [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom), is one of the world's best known megalithic structures.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Archaeocryptography"}

**Archaeocryptography** (from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) ἀρχαῖος, *arkhaios*, "ancient" and κρυπτός (*kruptós*), "hidden, secret"; and γράφειν (*graphein*), "to write") is the attempt to [decode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code) an [ancient monument or structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site) by supposing an underlying mathematical order beneath the proportions, size, and placement to find any re-occurring or unusual data in respect to that which is being studied,[^o2] or within another monument or structure.

:::

Archaeocryptography is not a recognized branch of [archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology) or of any other academic discipline. It is an example of [pseudoscience](wikipedia://en/23047) or [pseudoarchaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoarchaeology) that employs contrived calculations involving many free parameters to achieve an impressive-looking result.[^o3]

## Description {#Description}

The word *archaeocryptography* is derived from *archaeology*, which is the study of [human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human) activity in the past, and *[cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432)*, which is the study of techniques for secure communication in the presence of third parties.

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Methods"}

## Methods {#Methods}

*Image: Biholomorphism illustration.svg.* Mathematical coordinates including π may be used to form Grid Point Values

Archaeocryptologists try to find underlying correlations with respect to what is being studied or decoded.[^o7] Some factors taken into consideration while deciphering an object, structure or [megalithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalith) monument can include the features such as faces, stairs, sides, and terraces. The [geolocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geolocation) or mathematical operations are performed on latitude and longitude coordinates[^o2] as well as astronomical alignments, such as those found in [archaeoastronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeoastronomy). The incorporation of grids,[^o8] the use of numerical ordering, mathematical constants, Biblical [gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria), and any other re-occurring number that might stand out from the decoding process are determining factors.

Archaeocryptologists can then use different mathematical formulas to find correlations within that which is being studied or between other monuments or structures that share any underlying factors. Popular examples are the [Orion correlation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_correlation_theory)[^o9] between the [Giza pyramid complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giza_pyramid_complex) and the [three middle stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion's_Belt) of the [constellation Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_%28constellation%29), and also theories[^o10] about the region of [Cydonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cydonia_%28region_of_Mars%29) on [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars).

:::

## History {#History}

The coining of the word *archaeocryptography* is often attributed to Carl P. Munck,[^o3][^o12] who after retiring from the United States military in the late 1970s began studying [cartographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography) material, among other topics, trying to search for better answers as to why certain megalithic monuments exist. This led him to a formula he believes architects used to place and design various megalithic monuments.[^o13] Munck's theory claims that calculations using selected numbers or dimensions found in megalithic monuments or [Egyptian pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramids) yield the [latitude](wikipedia://en/17616) or [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) of the site.[^o3] However, in Munck's findings, the [prime meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_meridian) does not run through [Greenwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich), but through the [Great Pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza) in [Giza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giza). His theory is known simply as "The Code" and asserts that an ancient numerological system known as [gematria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria) is used in the manipulation of numbers to other key locations, mathematical components and positions of sites in the geometry of their construction.

The theory later become popularized and among other practitioners and researchers in pseudo-scientific fields, such as Michael Lawrence Morton, [Richard C. Hoagland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_C._Hoagland), [Bruce Cathie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Cathie), and Hugh Harleston Jr. They adapted archaeocryptography to their own studies, developing different theories [^o14][^o15] and books [^o10][^o16][^o17] based on the topic.

## In literary studies {#In_literary_studies}

The marginal, or even fictional, status of archaeocryptography was suggested in [Zachary Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachary_Mason)'s novel *[The Lost Books of the Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Books_of_the_Odyssey)*, which purports to be a collection of lost works which the author describes as being as old as the [Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey) and which he claims to have decoded from a manuscript he had uncovered. In the author's biography at the conclusion of the book he describes himself as "the John Shade Professor of Archaeocryptography and Paleomathematics at [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford)". The "lost books" were actually written by Mason and, as reviewers have noted, Mason is a novelist and computer scientist who lives in California, [John Shade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Shade) is a fictional poet in [Vladimir Nabokov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Nabokov)'s *[Pale Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_Fire)*, and no such professorship exists.[^o19][^o20]
