# Whitham equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Whitham equation"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), the **Whitham equation** is a non-local model for [non-linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear_system) [dispersive](wikipedia://en/853778) [waves](wikipedia://en/33516).[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

The equation is notated as follows:

This [integro-differential equation](wikipedia://en/11095324) for the oscillatory variable *η*(*x*,*t*) is named after [Gerald Whitham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Whitham) who introduced it as a model to study [breaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_breaking) of non-linear dispersive [water waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_wave) in 1967.[^o6] Wave breaking – bounded solutions with unbounded [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921) – for the Whitham equation has recently been proven.[^o7]

For a certain choice of the [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_kernel) *K*(*x* − *ξ*) it becomes the **Fornberg–Whitham equation**.

## Water waves {#Water_waves}

Using the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) (and its inverse), with respect to the space coordinate *x* and in terms of the [wavenumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavenumber) *k*:

- For [surface gravity waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_gravity_wave), the [phase speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_speed) *c*(*k*) as a function of wavenumber *k* is taken as:[^o6]

$c_\text{ww}(k) = \sqrt{ \frac{g}{k}\, \tanh(kh)},$ 2em while 2em $\alpha_\text{ww} = \frac{3}{2} \sqrt{\frac{g}{h}},$

with *g* the [gravitational acceleration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_gravity) and *h* the [mean](wikipedia://en/60167) water depth. The associated [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_kernel) *K*<sub>ww</sub>(*s*) is, using the inverse Fourier transform:[^o6]

$$K_\text{ww}(s) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} c_\text{ww}(k)\, \text{e}^{iks}\, \text{d}k = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} c_\text{ww}(k)\, \cos(ks)\, \text{d}k,$$

since *c*<sub>ww</sub> is an [even function](wikipedia://en/592151) of the wavenumber *k*.

- The [Korteweg–de Vries equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg–de_Vries_equation) (KdV equation) emerges when retaining the first two terms of a [series expansion](wikipedia://en/1575813) of *c*<sub>ww</sub>(*k*) for [long waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_wave) with *kh* ≪ 1:[^o6]

$c_\text{kdv}(k) = \sqrt{gh} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{6} k^2 h^2 \right),$ 3em $K_\text{kdv}(s) = \sqrt{gh} \left( \delta(s) + \frac{1}{6} h^2\, \delta^{\prime\prime}(s) \right),$ 3em $\alpha_\text{kdv} = \frac{3}{2} \sqrt{\frac{g}{h}},$

with *δ*(*s*) the [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021).

- [Bengt Fornberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengt_Fornberg) and Gerald Whitham studied the kernel *K*<sub>fw</sub>(*s*) – [non-dimensionalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondimensionalization) using *g* and *h*:[^o9]

$K_\text{fw}(s) = \frac12 \nu \text{e}^{-\nu |s|}$ 2em and 2em $c_\text{fw} = \frac{\nu^2}{\nu^2+k^2},$ 2em with 2em $\alpha_\text{fw}=\frac32.$

The resulting [integro-differential equation](wikipedia://en/11095324) can be reduced to the [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) known as the **Fornberg–Whitham equation**:[^o9]

$$\left( \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} - \nu^2 \right) \left( \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial t} + \frac32\, \eta\, \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x} \right) + \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x} = 0.$$

This equation is shown to allow for [peakon](wikipedia://en/16866923) solutions – as a model for waves of limiting height – as well as the occurrence of wave breaking ([shock waves](wikipedia://en/146253), absent in e.g. solutions of the Korteweg–de Vries equation).[^o9][^o4]
