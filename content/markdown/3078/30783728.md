# Holtsmark distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Holtsmark distribution"}

The (one-dimensional) **Holtsmark distribution** is a [continuous probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_probability_distribution). The Holtsmark distribution is a special case of a [stable distribution](wikipedia://en/1117869) with the index of stability or shape parameter $\alpha$ equal to 3/2 and the [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) parameter $\beta$ of zero. Since $\beta$ equals zero, the distribution is symmetric, and thus an example of a symmetric alpha-stable distribution. The Holtsmark distribution is one of the few examples of a stable distribution for which a closed form expression of the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) is known. However, its probability density function is not expressible in terms of [elementary functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_functions); rather, the probability density function is expressed in terms of [hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/361184).

:::

The Holtsmark distribution has applications in plasma physics and astrophysics.[^o10] In 1919, Norwegian physicist [Johan Peter Holtsmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Peter_Holtsmark) proposed the distribution as a model for the fluctuating fields in plasma due to the motion of charged particles.[^o2] It is also applicable to other types of Coulomb forces, in particular to modeling of gravitating bodies, and thus is important in astrophysics.[^o3][^o4]

## Characteristic function {#Characteristic_function}

The [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401) of a symmetric stable distribution is:

$$\varphi(t;\mu,c) = \exp\left[~it\mu \! - \! \left|c t\right|^\alpha\right],$$

where $\alpha$ is the shape parameter, or index of stability, $\mu$ is the [location parameter](wikipedia://en/17516), and *c* is the [scale parameter](wikipedia://en/769503).

Since the Holtsmark distribution has $\alpha=3/2,$ its characteristic function is:[^o6]

$$\varphi(t;\mu,c) = \exp\left[~it\mu \! - \! \left|c t\right|^{3/2}\right] .$$

Since the Holtsmark distribution is a stable distribution with $\alpha > 1$, $\mu$ represents the [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) of the distribution.[^o7][^o8] Since $\beta = 0$, $\mu$ also represents the [median](wikipedia://en/18837) and [mode](wikipedia://en/1432127) of the distribution. And since $\alpha < 2$, the [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) of the Holtsmark distribution is infinite.[^o7] All higher [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of the distribution are also infinite.[^o7] Like other stable distributions (other than the normal distribution), since the variance is infinite the dispersion in the distribution is reflected by the [scale parameter](wikipedia://en/769503), c. An alternate approach to describing the dispersion of the distribution is through fractional moments.[^o7]

## Probability density function {#Probability_density_function}

In general, the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487), $f(x)$, of a continuous [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) can be derived from its characteristic function by:

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty \varphi(t)e^{-ixt}\,dt .$$

Most stable distributions do not have a known closed form expression for their probability density functions. Only the [normal](wikipedia://en/21462), [Cauchy](wikipedia://en/7003) and [Lévy distributions](wikipedia://en/1346871) have known closed form expressions in terms of [elementary functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_functions).[^o10] The Holtsmark distribution is one of two symmetric stable distributions to have a known closed form expression in terms of [hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/361184).[^o10] When $\mu$ is equal to 0 and the scale parameter is equal to 1, the Holtsmark distribution has the probability density function:

$$\begin{aligned} f(x; 0, 1) &= \frac{1}{\pi}\, \Gamma{\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)} \; {_2F_3}\!\left(\frac{5}{12}, \frac{11}{12}; \frac{1}{3}, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{5}{6}; - \frac{4x^6}{729}\right) \\ & {} \quad{} - \frac{x^2}{3\pi} \; {_3F_4}\!\left(\frac{3}{4}, {1}, \frac{5}{4}; \frac{2}{3}, \frac{5}{6}, \frac{7}{6}, \frac{4}{3}; - \frac{4x^6}{729}\right) \\ & {} \quad{} + \frac{7x^4}{81\pi}\, \Gamma{\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)} \; {_2F_3}\!\left(\frac{13}{12}, \frac{19}{12}; \frac{7}{6}, \frac{3}{2}, \frac{5}{3}; - \frac{4x^6}{729}\right), \end{aligned}$$

where ${\Gamma(x)}$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) and $_mF_n(\cdot)$ is a [hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/361184).[^o10] One has also[^o11]

$$\begin{aligned} f(x; 0, 1) &= - \frac{x^2}{6\pi}\left[~_2F_2{\left(1, \frac{3}{2}; \frac{4}{3}, \frac{5}{3}; -\frac{4ix^3}{27}\right)} + ~{_2F_2}{\left(1, \frac{3}{2}; \frac{4}{3}, \frac{5}{3}; \frac{4ix^3}{27}\right)}\right]\\[2pt] &\quad + \frac{4}{3^{5/3}} \left[\operatorname{Bi}'\left(-\frac{x^2}{3^{4/3}}\right) \cos\left(\frac{2x^3}{27}\right)+\frac{x}{3^{2/3}} ~ \operatorname{Bi}\left(-\frac{x^2}{3^{4/3}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{2x^3}{27}\right)\right], \end{aligned}$$

where $\mathrm{Bi}$ is the Airy function of the second kind and $\mathrm{Bi}'$ its derivative. The arguments of the $_2F_2$ functions are pure imaginary complex numbers, but the sum of the two functions is real. For $x$ positive, the function $\mathrm{Bi}(-x)$ is related to the Bessel functions of fractional order $J_{-1/3}$ and $J_{1/3}$ and its derivative to the Bessel functions of fractional order $J_{-2/3}$ and $J_{2/3}$. Therefore, one can write[^o11]

$$\begin{aligned} f(x; 0, 1) &= \frac{4x^2}{3^{7/2}} \cos\left(\frac{2x^3}{27}\right)\left[J_{-2/3}{\left(\frac{2x^3}{27}\right)} + J_{2/3}{\left(\frac{2x^3}{27}\right)}\right] \\[1ex] &+ \frac{4x^2}{3^{7/2}} \sin\left(\frac{2x^3}{27}\right)\left[J_{-1/3}{\left(\frac{2x^3}{27}\right)} - J_{1/3}{\left(\frac{2x^3}{27}\right)}\right] \\[1ex] &-\frac{x^2}{6\pi}\left[~_2F_2{\left(1, \frac{3}{2}; \frac{4}{3}, \frac{5}{3}; -\frac{4ix^3}{27}\right)} + ~_2F_2{\left(1, \frac{3}{2}; \frac{4}{3}, \frac{5}{3}; \frac{4ix^3}{27}\right)}\right]. \end{aligned}$$
