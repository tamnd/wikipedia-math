# Groupoid algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Groupoid algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the concept of **groupoid algebra** generalizes the notion of [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) $(G, \cdot)$ (in the sense of a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) with all [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) invertible) and a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$, it is possible to define the groupoid algebra $KG$ as the [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) over $K$ formed by the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) having the elements of (the morphisms of) $G$ as [generators](wikipedia://en/3171371) and having the [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) of these elements defined by $g * h = g \cdot h$, whenever this product is defined, and $g * h = 0$ otherwise. The product is then extended by [linearity](wikipedia://en/18102).[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Some examples of groupoid algebras are the following:[^o8]

- [Group rings](wikipedia://en/349223)
- [Matrix algebras](wikipedia://en/487541)
- [Algebras of functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_of_functions)

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- When a groupoid has a [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) number of [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) and a finite number of morphisms, the groupoid algebra is a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of [tensor products](wikipedia://en/30844) of group algebras and matrix algebras.[^o11]

:::
