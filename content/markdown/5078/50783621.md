# Pullback (cohomology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pullback"}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), given a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) *f*: *X* → *Y* of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) and a ring *R*, the **pullback** along *f* on [cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory) is a [grade](wikipedia://en/103094)-preserving *R*-[algebra homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_homomorphism):

:::

$$f^*: H^*(Y; R) \to H^*(X; R)$$

from the [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) of *Y* with coefficients in *R* to that of *X*. The use of the superscript is meant to indicate its [contravariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) nature: it reverses the direction of the map. For example, if *X*, *Y* are [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), *R* the field of real numbers, and the cohomology is [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530), then the pullback is induced by the pullback of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519).

The homotopy invariance of cohomology states that if two maps *f*, *g*: *X* → *Y* are [homotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopic) to each other, then they determine the same pullback: *f*<sup>\*</sup> = *g*<sup>\*</sup>.

In contrast, a pushforward for de Rham cohomology for example is given by [integration-along-fibers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integration-along-fibers).

## Definition from chain complexes {#Definition_from_chain_complexes}

We first review the definition of the cohomology of the dual of a [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404). Let *R* be a commutative ring, *C* a chain complex of *R*-[modules](wikipedia://en/276410) and *G* an *R*-module. Just as one lets $H_*(C; G) = H_*(C \otimes_R G)$, one lets

$$H^*(C; G) = H^*(\operatorname{Hom}_R(C, G))$$

where Hom is the special case of the Hom between a chain complex and a cochain complex, with *G* viewed as a cochain complex concentrated in degree zero. (To make this rigorous, one needs to choose signs in the way similar to the signs in the [tensor product of complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_product_of_complexes).) For example, if *C* is the [singular chain complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_chain_complex) associated to a topological space *X*, then this is the definition of the singular cohomology of *X* with coefficients in *G*.

Now, let *f*: *C* → *C'* be a map of chain complexes (for example, it may be induced by a continuous map between topological spaces, see [Pushforward (homology)](wikipedia://en/16649410)). Then there is the map

$$f^*: \operatorname{Hom}_R(C', G) \to \operatorname{Hom}_R(C, G)$$

of cochain complexes, which in turn determines the pullback homomorphism

$$f^*: H^*(C'; G) \to H^*(C; G)$$

on the cohomology modules and cohomology ring.

If *C*, *C'* are singular chain complexes of spaces *X*, *Y*, then this is the pullback for singular cohomology theory.
