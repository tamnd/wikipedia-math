# Graph polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph polynomial"}

In mathematics, a **graph polynomial** is a [graph invariant](wikipedia://en/1655142) whose value is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000). Invariants of this type are studied in [algebraic graph theory](wikipedia://en/1620216). Important graph polynomials include:

:::

- The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268), based on the graph's [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463).
- The [chromatic polynomial](wikipedia://en/1460126), a polynomial whose values at integer arguments give the number of colorings of the graph with that many colors.
- The [dichromatic polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dichromatic_polynomial), a 2-variable generalization of the chromatic polynomial
- The [flow polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_polynomial), a polynomial whose values at integer arguments give the number of [nowhere-zero flows](wikipedia://en/9909979) with integer flow amounts modulo the argument.
- The (inverse of the) [Ihara zeta function](wikipedia://en/1342362), defined as a product of binomial terms corresponding to certain closed walks in a graph.
- The [Martin polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_polynomial), used by Pierre Martin to study [Euler tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_tour)
- The [matching polynomials](wikipedia://en/24956783), several different polynomials defined as the [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) of the [matchings](wikipedia://en/581797) of a graph.
- The [reliability polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliability_polynomial), a polynomial that describes the probability of remaining connected after independent edge failures
- The [Tutte polynomial](wikipedia://en/3892303), a polynomial in two variables that can be defined (after a small change of variables) as the generating function of the numbers of connected components of [induced subgraphs](wikipedia://en/1655188) of the given graph, parameterized by the number of vertices in the subgraph.
