# Jo Ellis-Monaghan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jo Ellis-Monaghan"}

**Joanna Anthony Ellis-Monaghan** is an American mathematician and [mathematics educator](wikipedia://en/326471) whose research interests include [graph polynomials](wikipedia://en/56012962) and [topological graph theory](wikipedia://en/1631772). She is a professor of mathematics at the [Korteweg-de Vries Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/66341573) of the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Ellis-Monaghan grew up in Alaska. She graduated from [Bennington College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennington_College) in 1984 with a double major in mathematics and [studio art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_art), and earned a master's degree in mathematics from the [University of Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vermont) in 1986. After beginning a doctoral program at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College), she transferred to the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill), where she completed her Ph.D. in 1995. Her dissertation, supervised by [Jim Stasheff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Stasheff), was *A unique, universal graph polynomial and its [Hopf algebraic](wikipedia://en/325714) properties, with applications to the Martin polynomial*.

She joined the [Saint Michael's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Michael's_College) faculty in 1992, chaired the department there, and has also held positions at the [University of Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vermont). In 2020, she became professor of Discrete Mathematics at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam). From 2010 to 2020, she served as a subject editor of *[PRIMUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRIMUS_%28journal%29)*, a journal on the teaching of undergraduate mathematics.
