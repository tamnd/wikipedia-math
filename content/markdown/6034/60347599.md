# Scott A. Mitchell

**Scott Alan Mitchell** is a researcher of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) in the [Center for Computing Research](https://cfwebprod.sandia.gov/cfdocs/CompResearch/index.cfm) at [Sandia National Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandia_National_Laboratories).

## Background {#Background}

Mitchell received a B.S in Applied Math, Engineering & Physics from the University of Wisconsin-Madison (1988), and an M.S. (1991) and Ph.D. (1993) in Applied Math from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). He worked the summer of 1991 at Xerox PARC (now *[PARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PARC_%28company%29)*). Since 1992, he has been at [Sandia National Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandia_National_Laboratories) in the Center for Computing Research, with several different roles. He researched [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927) meshing from 1992—1993. According to one publication,[^o4] he is considered a “liaison author” linking two cores of Computational Geometry: theory and applied meshing. He contributed to applied meshing in the [CUBIT project](https://cubit.sandia.gov): R&D 1993—2000, project leader 2000—2002, R&D 2015—. He managed Sandia's Optimization and Uncertainty Estimation department, and had programmatic roles on the Laboratory Directed Research and Development program and [NNSA's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Nuclear_Security_Administration) [ASC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Simulation_and_Computing_Program) program from 2002-2007. He researched informatics and applied persistent homology from 2008-2011. Since 2011, he researches [mesh generation](wikipedia://en/3050716), sampling, and (topological) data analysis.

He served on organizing and steering committees of the [SIAM International Meshing Roundtable](https://internationalmeshingroundtable.com/), and organizing committee of [International Symposium on Computational Geometry SoCG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symposium_on_Computational_Geometry) conference, and program committee for GMP and SPM. He serves as an editor for the journal [CAD](https://ees.elsevier.com/cad/). As an adjunct professor, he taught a small graduate course on computational geometry at the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico). He is a member of [ACM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) and [SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376).

## Research {#Research}

He published algorithms in the areas of [mesh generation](wikipedia://en/3050716), [reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_reconstruction) and [sampling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_tracing_%28graphics%29), for the contexts of [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), [simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation), [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics) and [uncertainty quantification](wikipedia://en/5987648). His main contributions have been geometric algorithms with provable correctness and output quality guarantees. His PhD thesis was the first tetrahedral meshing algorithm with guarantees on both the number of elements and their shape. He is also well known for a series of papers on *whisker weaving* and other algorithms for hexahedral mesh generation using the dual [spatial twist continuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_twist_continuum). He used [optimization](wikipedia://en/52033) for mesh generation, specifically *interval assignment*, deciding the right number of edges locally so the model can be meshed globally. Since 2011, he has contributed sampling algorithms for computer graphics and uncertainty quantification, and algorithms for mesh generation (including duality) and surface reconstruction.
