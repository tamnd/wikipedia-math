# Whitney inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Whitney inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Whitney inequality** gives an upper bound for the error of best approximation of a function by [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) in terms of the [moduli of smoothness](wikipedia://en/44605337). It was first proved by [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794) in 1957,[^o2] and is an important tool in the field of [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713) for obtaining upper estimates on the errors of best approximation.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Denote the value of the best uniform approximation of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f\in C([a,b])$ by algebraic [polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomials) $P_n$ of degree $\leq n$ by

$$E_n(f)_{[a,b]} := \inf_{P_n}{\| f-P_n \|_{C([a,b])}}$$

The [moduli of smoothness](wikipedia://en/44605337) of order $k$ of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f\in C([a,b])$ are defined as:

$$\omega_k(t):=\omega_k(t;f;[a,b]) :=\sup_{h\in [0,t]} \|\Delta_h^k(f;\cdot)\|_{C([a,b-kh])} \quad \text{ for } \quad t\in [0,(b-a)/k],$$

$$\omega_k(t):=\omega_k((b-a)/k)\quad \text{ for} \quad t>(b-a)/k ,$$

where $\Delta_h^k$ is the [finite difference](wikipedia://en/39147) of order $k$.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** [^o6] \[Whitney, 1957\] If $f\in C([a,b])$, then

$$E_{k-1}(f)_{[a,b]}\leq W_k \omega_k\left(\frac{b-a}{k};f;[a,b]\right)$$

where $W_k$ is a constant depending only on $k$. The Whitney constant $W(k)$ is the smallest value of $W_k$ for which the above inequality holds. The theorem is particularly useful when applied on intervals of small length, leading to good estimates on the error of [spline](wikipedia://en/457680) approximation.

:::

:::

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The original proof given by Whitney follows an analytic argument which utilizes the properties of [moduli of smoothness](wikipedia://en/44605337). However, it can also be proved in a much shorter way using Peetre's K-functionals.[^o9]

Let:

$$x_0:=a, \quad h:=\frac{b-a}{k}, \quad x_j:=x+0+jh, \quad F(x) = \int_a^x f(u) \, du,$$

$$G(x):=F(x)-L(x;F;x_0,\ldots,x_k), \quad g(x):=G'(x),$$

$$\omega_k(t):=\omega_k(t;f;[a,b])\equiv \omega_k(t;g;[a,b])$$

where $L(x;F;x_0,\ldots,x_k)$ is the [Lagrange polynomial](wikipedia://en/217523) for $F$ at the nodes $\{x_0,\ldots,x_k\}$.

Now fix some $x\in [a,b]$ and choose $\delta$ for which $(x+k\delta)\in [a,b]$. Then:

$$\int_0^1 \Delta_{t\delta}^k (g;x) \, dt =(-1)^kg(x)+\sum_{j=1}^k (-1)^{k-j} \binom{k}{j} \int_0^1 g(x+jt\delta) \, dt$$

$$=(-1)^kg(x)+\sum_{j=1}^k{(-1)^{k-j} \binom{k}{j} \frac{1}{j\delta}(G(x+j\delta)-G(x))},$$

Therefore:

$$|g(x)| \leq \int_0^1 |\Delta_{t\delta}^k(g;x)| \, dt + \frac{2}{|\delta|}\|G\|_{C([a,b])} \sum_{j=1}^k \binom{k}{j}\frac{1}{j} \leq \omega_k(|\delta|)+\frac{1}{|\delta|}2^{k+1}\|G\|_{C([a,b])}$$

And since we have $\|G\|_{C([a,b])}\leq h\omega_k(h)$, (a property of [moduli of smoothness](wikipedia://en/44605337))

$$E_{k-1}(f)_{[a,b]}\leq \|g\|_{C([a,b])} \leq \omega_k(|\delta|) +\frac{1}{|\delta|} h 2^{k+1}\omega_k(h).$$

Since $\delta$ can always be chosen in such a way that $h\geq |\delta| \geq h/2$, this completes the proof.

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Whitney constants and Sendov's conjecture"}

## Whitney constants and Sendov's conjecture {#Whitney_constants_and_Sendov's_conjecture}

It is important to have sharp estimates of the Whitney constants. It is easily shown that $W(1)=1/2$, and it was first proved by [Burkill](wikipedia://en/33388595) (1952) that $W(2)\leq 1$, who conjectured that $W(k)\leq 1$ for all $k$. [Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794) was also able to prove that [^o6]

$$W(2)=\frac{1}{2}, \quad \frac{8}{15}\leq W(3) \leq 0.7 \quad W(4)\leq 3.3 \quad W(5)\leq 10.4$$

and

$$W(k)\geq \frac{1}{2},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}$$

In 1964, Brudnyi was able to obtain the estimate $W(k)=O(k^{2k})$, and in 1982, Sendov proved that $W(k)\leq (k+1)k^k$. Then, in 1985, Ivanov and Takev proved that $W(k)=O(k\ln k)$, and Binev proved that $W(k)=O(k)$. Sendov conjectured that $W(k)\leq 1$ for all $k$, and in 1985 was able to prove that the Whitney constants are bounded above by an absolute constant, that is, $W(k)\leq 6$ for all $k$. Kryakin, Gilewicz, and Shevchuk (2002)[^o12] were able to show that $W(k)\leq 2$ for $k \leq 82000$, and that $W(k)\leq 2+\frac{1}{e^2}$ for all $k$.

:::
