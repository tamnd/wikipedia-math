# Prime ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prime ring"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a [nonzero](wikipedia://en/1872874) [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* is a **prime ring** if for any two elements *a* and *b* of *R*, *arb* = 0 for all *r* in *R* implies that either *a* = 0 or *b* = 0. This definition can be regarded as a simultaneous generalization of both [integral domains](wikipedia://en/15462) and [simple rings](wikipedia://en/443387).

:::

Although this article discusses the above definition, **prime ring** may also refer to the minimal non-zero [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), which is generated by its identity element 1, and determined by its [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621). For a characteristic 0 field, the prime ring is the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563#Algebraic_properties), and for a characteristic *p* field (with *p* a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666)) the prime ring is the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) of order *p* (cf. [Prime field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_field)).[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Equivalent definitions"}

## Equivalent definitions {#Equivalent_definitions}

A ring *R* is prime [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the [zero ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_ideal) {0} is a [prime ideal in the noncommutative sense](wikipedia://en/24928#Prime_ideals_for_noncommutative_rings).

This being the case, the equivalent conditions for prime ideals yield the following equivalent conditions for *R* to be a prime ring:

- For any two [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) *A* and *B* of *R*, *AB* = {0} implies *A* = {0} or *B* = {0}.
- For any two *right* ideals *A* and *B* of *R*, *AB* = {0} implies *A* = {0} or *B* = {0}.
- For any two *left* ideals *A* and *B* of *R*, *AB* = {0} implies *A* = {0} or *B* = {0}.

Using these conditions it can be checked that the following are equivalent to *R* being a prime ring:

- All nonzero right ideals are [faithful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_module) as right *R*-modules.
- All nonzero left ideals are faithful as left *R*-modules.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any [domain](wikipedia://en/487627) is a prime ring.
- Any [simple ring](wikipedia://en/443387) is a prime ring, and more generally: every left or right [primitive ring](wikipedia://en/485862) is a prime ring.
- Any [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541) over an integral domain is a prime ring. In particular, the ring of 2 × 2 integer [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) is a prime ring.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) is a prime ring if and only if it is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462).
- A nonzero ring is prime if and only if the [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) of its [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) lacks [zero divisors](wikipedia://en/51441).
- The ring of matrices over a prime ring is again a prime ring.

:::
