# Jacobson density theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacobson density theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically non-commutative [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), [modern algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), and [module theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_theory), the **Jacobson density theorem** is a theorem concerning [simple modules](wikipedia://en/29430) over a ring $R$.[^o2]

:::

The theorem can be applied to show that any [primitive ring](wikipedia://en/485862) can be viewed as a "dense" subring of the ring of [linear transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) of a vector space.[^o3][^o4] This theorem first appeared in the literature in 1945, in the famous paper "Structure Theory of Simple Rings Without Finiteness Assumptions" by [Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson).[^o5] This can be viewed as a kind of generalization of the [Artin-Wedderburn theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin-Wedderburn_theorem)'s conclusion about the structure of [simple](wikipedia://en/443387) [Artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273).

## Motivation and formal statement {#Motivation_and_formal_statement}

Let $R$ be a ring and let $U$ be a simple right $R$-module. If $u$ is a non-zero element of $U$, $u \bullet R = U$ (where $u \bullet R$ is the cyclic submodule of $U$ generated by $u$). Therefore, if $u, v$ are non-zero elements of $U$, there is an element of $R$ that induces an [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) of $U$ transforming $u$ to $v$. The natural question now is whether this can be generalized to arbitrary (finite) tuples of elements. More precisely, find necessary and sufficient conditions on the tuple $(x_{1}, ..., x_{n})$ and $(y_{1}, ..., y_{n})$ separately, so that there is an element of $R$ with the property that $x_{i} \bullet r = y_{i}$ for all $i$. If $D$ is the set of all $R$-module endomorphisms of $U$, then [Schur's lemma](wikipedia://en/521696) asserts that $D$ is a division ring, and the Jacobson density theorem answers the question on tuples in the affirmative, provided that the $x_{i}$ are linearly independent over $D$.

With the above in mind, the theorem may be stated this way:

**The Jacobson density theorem.** Let $U$ be a simple right $R$-module, $D = \operatorname{End}(U_{R})$, and $X \subset U$ a finite and $D$-linearly independent set. If $A$ is a $D$-linear transformation on $U$ then there exists $r \in R$ such that $A(x) = x \bullet r$ for all $x$ in $X$.[^o7]

::: {#o9 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

In the Jacobson density theorem, the right $R$-module $U$ is simultaneously viewed as a left $D$-module where $D = \operatorname{End}(U_{R})$, in the natural way: $g \bullet u = g(u)$. It can be verified that this is indeed a left module structure on $U$.[^o10] As noted before, Schur's lemma proves $D$ is a division ring if $U$ is simple, and so $U$ is a vector space over $D$.

The proof also relies on the following theorem proven in (Isaacs 1993) p. 185:

**Theorem.** Let $U$ be a simple right $R$-module, $D = \operatorname{End}(U_{R})$, and $X \subset U$ a finite set. Write $I = \operatorname{ann}_{R}(X)$ for the [annihilator](wikipedia://en/328684) of $X$ in $R$. Let $u$ be in $U$ with $u \bullet I = 0$. Then $u$ is in $XD$; the $D$-[span](wikipedia://en/56353) of $X$.

::: {#o13 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of the Jacobson density theorem"}

### Proof of the Jacobson density theorem {#Proof_of_the_Jacobson_density_theorem}

We use [induction](wikipedia://en/18881) on $|X|$. If $X$ is empty, then the theorem is vacuously true and the base case for induction is verified.

Assume $X$ is non-empty, let $x$ be an element of $X$ and write $Y = X\,\backslash\{x\}.$ If $A$ is any $D$-linear transformation on $U$, by the induction hypothesis there exists $s \in R$ such that $A(y) = y \bullet s$ for all $y$ in $Y$. Write $I = \operatorname{ann}_{R}(Y)$. Now $x \bullet I$ is a submodule of $U$. If $x \bullet I = 0$, then the previous theorem implies that $x$ would be in the $D$-span of $Y$, contradicting the $D$-linear independence of $X$, therefore $x \bullet I \ne 0$. Since $U$ is simple, we have: $x \bullet I = U$. Since $A(x) - x \bullet s \in U = x \bullet I$, there exists $i$ in $I$ such that $x \bullet i = A(x) - x \bullet s$.

Define $r = s + i$ and observe that for all $y$ in $Y$ we have:

$$\begin{aligned} y \cdot r &= y \cdot(s + i) \\ &= y \cdot s + y \cdot i \\ &= y \cdot s && (\text{since } i\in \text{ann}_R(Y)) \\ &= A(y) \end{aligned}$$

Now we do the same calculation for $x$:

$$\begin{aligned} x \cdot r &= x \cdot (s + i) \\ &= x \cdot s + x \cdot i \\ &= x \cdot s + \left ( A(x) - x \cdot s \right )\\ &= A(x) \end{aligned}$$

Therefore, $A(z) = z \bullet r$ for all $z$ in $X$, as desired. This completes the inductive step of the proof. It follows now from mathematical induction that the theorem is true for finite sets $X$ of any size.

## Topological characterization {#Topological_characterization}

A ring $R$ is said to **act densely** on a simple right $R$-module $U$ if it satisfies the conclusion of the Jacobson density theorem.[^o15] There is a topological reason for describing $R$ as "dense". Firstly, $R$ can be identified with a subring of $\operatorname{End}(_{D}U)$ by identifying each element of $R$ with the $D$ linear transformation it induces by right multiplication. If $U$ is given the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology), and if $U^{U}$ is given the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910), and $\operatorname{End}(_{D}U)$ is viewed as a subspace of $U^{U}$ and is given the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926), then $R$ acts densely on $U$ if and only if $R$ is [dense set](wikipedia://en/23523594) in $\operatorname{End}(_{D}U)$ with this topology.[^o16]

## Consequences {#Consequences}

The Jacobson density theorem has various important consequences in the structure theory of rings.[^o18] Notably, the [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin–Wedderburn_theorem)'s conclusion about the structure of [simple](wikipedia://en/443387) right [Artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273) is recovered. The Jacobson density theorem also characterizes right or left [primitive rings](wikipedia://en/485862) as dense subrings of the ring of $D$-linear transformations on some $D$-vector space $U$, where $D$ is a division ring.[^o4]

## Relations to other results {#Relations_to_other_results}

This result is related to the [Von Neumann bicommutant theorem](wikipedia://en/455962), which states that, for a \*-algebra $A$ of operators on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $H$, the double commutant $A''$ can be approximated by $A$ on any given finite set of vectors. In other words, the double commutant is the closure of $A$ in the [weak operator topology](wikipedia://en/458698). See also the [Kaplansky density theorem](wikipedia://en/9523634) in the von Neumann algebra setting.
