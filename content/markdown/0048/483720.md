# Fibonacci polynomials

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fibonacci polynomials"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Fibonacci polynomials** are a [polynomial sequence](wikipedia://en/195990) which can be considered as a generalization of the [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number). The polynomials generated in a similar way from the [Lucas numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_numbers) are called **Lucas polynomials**.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

These Fibonacci [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) are defined by a [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806):[^o4]

$$F_n(x)= \begin{cases} 0, & \mbox{if } n = 0\\ 1, & \mbox{if } n = 1\\ x F_{n - 1}(x) + F_{n - 2}(x),& \mbox{if } n \geq 2 \end{cases}$$

The Lucas polynomials use the same recurrence with different starting values:[^o5]

$$L_n(x) = \begin{cases} 2, & \mbox{if } n = 0 \\ x, & \mbox{if } n = 1 \\ x L_{n - 1}(x) + L_{n - 2}(x), & \mbox{if } n \geq 2. \end{cases}$$

They can be defined for negative indices by[^o6]

$$F_{-n}(x)=(-1)^{n-1}F_{n}(x),$$

$$L_{-n}(x)=(-1)^nL_{n}(x).$$

The Fibonacci polynomials form a sequence of [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718#Recurrence_relation) with $A_n=C_n=1$ and $B_n=0$.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The first few Fibonacci polynomials are:

$$F_0(x)=0 \,$$

$$F_1(x)=1 \,$$

$$F_2(x)=x \,$$

$$F_3(x)=x^2+1 \,$$

$$F_4(x)=x^3+2x \,$$

$$F_5(x)=x^4+3x^2+1 \,$$

$$F_6(x)=x^5+4x^3+3x \,$$

The first few Lucas polynomials are:

$$L_0(x)=2 \,$$

$$L_1(x)=x \,$$

$$L_2(x)=x^2+2 \,$$

$$L_3(x)=x^3+3x \,$$

$$L_4(x)=x^4+4x^2+2 \,$$

$$L_5(x)=x^5+5x^3+5x \,$$

$$L_6(x)=x^6+6x^4+9x^2 + 2. \,$$

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The degree of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> is *n* − 1 and the degree of *L*<sub>*n*</sub> is *n*.
- The Fibonacci and Lucas numbers are recovered by evaluating the polynomials at *x* = 1; [Pell numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pell_numbers) are recovered by evaluating *F*<sub>*n*</sub> at *x* = 2.
- The [ordinary generating functions](wikipedia://en/160993#Ordinary_generating_function) for the sequences are:

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty F_n(x) t^n = \frac{t}{1-xt-t^2}$$

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty L_n(x) t^n = \frac{2-xt}{1-xt-t^2}.$$

- The polynomials can be expressed in terms of [Lucas sequences](wikipedia://en/734635) as

$$F_n(x) = U_n(x,-1),\,$$

$$L_n(x) = V_n(x,-1).\,$$

- They can also be expressed in terms of [Chebyshev polynomials](wikipedia://en/184539) $\mathcal{T}_n(x)$ and $\mathcal{U}_n(x)$ as

$$F_n(x) = i^{n-1}\cdot\mathcal{U}_{n-1}(\tfrac{-ix}2),\,$$

$$L_n(x) = 2\cdot i^n\cdot\mathcal{T}_n(\tfrac{-ix}2),\,$$

where $i$ is the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358).

:::

## Identities {#Identities}

As particular cases of Lucas sequences, Fibonacci polynomials satisfy a number of identities, such as[^o6]

$$F_{m+n}(x)=F_{m+1}(x)F_n(x)+F_m(x)F_{n-1}(x)\,$$

$$L_{m+n}(x)=L_m(x)L_n(x)-(-1)^nL_{m-n}(x)\,$$

$$F_{n+1}(x)F_{n-1}(x)- F_n(x)^2=(-1)^n\,$$

$$F_{2n}(x)=F_n(x)L_n(x).\,$$

Closed form expressions, similar to Binet's formula are:[^o6]

$$F_n(x)=\frac{\alpha(x)^n-\beta(x)^n}{\alpha(x)-\beta(x)},\,L_n(x)=\alpha(x)^n+\beta(x)^n,$$

where

$$\alpha(x)=\frac{x+\sqrt{x^2+4}}{2},\,\beta(x)=\frac{x-\sqrt{x^2+4}}{2}$$

are the solutions (in *t*) of

$$t^2-xt-1=0.\,$$

For Lucas Polynomials *n* > 0, we have

$$L_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor} \frac{n}{n-k} \binom{n-k}{k} x^{n-2k}.$$

A relationship between the Fibonacci polynomials and the standard basis polynomials is given by[^o13]

$$x^n=F_{n+1}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(-1)^k\left[\binom nk-\binom n{k-1}\right]F_{n+1-2k}(x).$$

For example,

$$x^4 = F_5(x)-3F_3(x)+2F_1(x)\,$$

$$x^5 = F_6(x)-4F_4(x)+5F_2(x)\,$$

$$x^6 = F_7(x)-5F_5(x)+9F_3(x)-5F_1(x)\,$$

$$x^7 = F_8(x)-6F_6(x)+14F_4(x)-14F_2(x)\,$$

## Combinatorial interpretation {#Combinatorial_interpretation}

*Image: pascal\_triangle\_fibonacci.svg.* The coefficients of the Fibonacci polynomials can be read off from a left-justified Pascal's triangle following the diagonals (shown in red). The sums of the coefficients are the Fibonacci numbers.

If *F*(*n*,*k*) is the coefficient of *x<sup>k</sup>* in *F<sub>n</sub>*(*x*), namely

$$F_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n F(n,k)x^k,\,$$

then *F*(*n*,*k*) is the number of ways an *n*−1 by 1 rectangle can be tiled with 2 by 1 [dominoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domino) and 1 by 1 squares so that exactly *k* squares are used.[^o4] Equivalently, *F*(*n*,*k*) is the number of ways of writing *n*−1 as an [ordered sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composition_%28number_theory%29) involving only 1 and 2, so that 1 is used exactly *k* times. For example F(6,3)=4 and 5 can be written in 4 ways, 1+1+1+2, 1+1+2+1, 1+2+1+1, 2+1+1+1, as a sum involving only 1 and 2 with 1 used 3 times. By counting the number of times 1 and 2 are both used in such a sum, it is evident that $F(n, k)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\binom{\frac12(n+k-1)}{k} &\text{if }n \not\equiv k \pmod 2,\\[12pt] 0 &\text{else}. \end{cases}$

This gives a way of reading the coefficients from [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497) as shown on the right.
