# Jacobian variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacobian variety"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Jacobian variety** *J*(*C*) of a non-singular [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) *C* of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) *g* is the [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) of degree 0 [line bundles](wikipedia://en/361366). It is the [connected component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28topology%29) of the identity in the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) of *C*, hence an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152).

:::

## Introduction {#Introduction}

The Jacobian variety is named after [Carl Gustav Jacobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Gustav_Jacobi), who proved the complete version of the [Abel–Jacobi theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel–Jacobi_theorem), making the injectivity statement of [Niels Abel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Abel) into an isomorphism. It is a principally polarized [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152), of [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) *g*, and hence, over the complex numbers, it is a [complex torus](wikipedia://en/3055042). If *p* is a point of *C*, then the curve *C* can be mapped to a [subvariety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subvariety_%28algebraic_geometry%29) of *J* with the given point *p* mapping to the identity of *J*, and *C* generates *J* as a [group](wikipedia://en/19447).

## Construction for complex curves {#Construction_for_complex_curves}

Over the complex numbers, the Jacobian variety can be realized as the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/898784) *V*/*L*, where *V* is the dual of the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of all global [holomorphic differentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_differential) on *C* and *L* is the [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) of all elements of *V* of the form

$$[\gamma]:\ \omega \mapsto \int_\gamma \omega$$

where *γ* is a closed [path](wikipedia://en/1189553) in *C*. In other words,

$$J(C) = H^0(\Omega_C^1)^* / H_1(C),$$

with $H_1(C)$ embedded in $H^0(\Omega_C^1)^*$ via the above map. This can be done explicitly with the use of [theta functions](wikipedia://en/449745).[^o4]

The Jacobian of a curve over an arbitrary field was constructed by (Weil 1948) as part of his proof of the [Riemann hypothesis for curves over a finite field](wikipedia://en/244705).

The [Abel–Jacobi theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel–Jacobi_theorem) states that the torus thus built is a variety, the classical Jacobian of a curve, that indeed parametrizes the degree 0 line bundles, that is, it can be identified with its [Picard variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_variety) of degree 0 divisors modulo linear equivalence.

## Algebraic structure {#Algebraic_structure}

As a group, the Jacobian variety of a curve is isomorphic to the quotient of the group of [divisors](wikipedia://en/1693055) of degree zero by the subgroup of principal divisors, i.e., divisors of rational functions. This holds for fields that are not [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed), provided one considers divisors and functions defined over that field.

## Further notions {#Further_notions}

[Torelli's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torelli's_theorem) states that a complex curve is determined by its Jacobian (with its polarization).

The [Schottky problem](wikipedia://en/4125909) asks which principally polarized abelian varieties are the Jacobians of curves.

The [Picard variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_variety), the [Albanese variety](wikipedia://en/1814828), [generalized Jacobian](wikipedia://en/3026330), and [intermediate Jacobians](wikipedia://en/3092994) are generalizations of the Jacobian for higher-dimensional varieties. For varieties of higher dimension the construction of the Jacobian variety as a quotient of the space of holomorphic 1-forms generalizes to give the [Albanese variety](wikipedia://en/1814828), but in general this need not be isomorphic to the Picard variety.
