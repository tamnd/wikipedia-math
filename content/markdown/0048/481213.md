# CDMA2000

*Image: Huawei EC226 with USB cable (3384718751).jpg.* [Huawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huawei) CDMA2000 [EVDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EVDO) USB [wireless modem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_modem) (2009)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CDMA2000"}

**CDMA2000** (also known as **C2K** or **IMT Multi‑Carrier** (**IMT‑MC**)) is a family of [3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G)[^o2] mobile technology standards for sending voice, data, and [signaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signaling_%28telecommunications%29) data between [mobile phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone) and [cell sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_site). It is developed by [3GPP2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3GPP2) as a backwards-compatible successor to [second-generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2G) [cdmaOne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CdmaOne) (IS-95) set of standards and used especially in North America and South Korea.

:::

CDMA2000 compares to [UMTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMTS), a competing set of [3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G) standards, which is developed by [3GPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3GPP) and used in Europe, Japan, China, and Singapore.

The name CDMA2000 denotes a family of standards that represent the successive, evolutionary stages of the underlying technology. These are:

- Voice: CDMA2000 1xRTT, 1X Advanced
- Data: CDMA2000 1xEV-DO ([Evolution-Data Optimized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution-Data_Optimized)): Release 0, Revision A, Revision B, [Ultra Mobile Broadband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_Mobile_Broadband) (UMB)

All are approved radio interfaces for the [ITU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Telecommunication_Union)'s [IMT-2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMT-2000). In the United States, *CDMA2000* is a registered trademark of the [Telecommunications Industry Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_Industry_Association) (TIA-USA).[^o3]

As of 2026, almost all [public CDMA2000 networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_CDMA2000_networks) have been shut down, usually to be replaced by [LTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTE_%28telecommunication%29) and/or [5G NR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5G_NR) networks.

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="1XRTT"}

## 1X {#1X}

**CDMA2000 1X (IS-2000)**, also known as **1x** and **1xRTT**, is the core CDMA2000 wireless air interface standard. The designation "1x", meaning *1 times radio transmission technology*, indicates the same [radio frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency) (RF) bandwidth as [IS-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IS-95): a [duplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duplex_%28telecommunications%29) pair of 1.25 MHz radio channels. 1xRTT almost doubles the capacity of IS-95 by adding 64 more traffic channels to the [forward link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_link), [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/102221) to (in [quadrature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrature_phase) with) the original set of 64. The 1X standard supports packet data speeds of up to 153 [kbit/s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilobit_per_second) with real world data transmission averaging 80–100 kbit/s in most commercial applications.[^o6] IMT-2000 also made changes to the [data link layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_link_layer) for greater use of data services, including medium and link access control protocols and [quality of service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_service) (QoS). The IS-95 data link layer only provided [best-effort delivery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best-effort_delivery) for data and circuit switched channel for voice (i.e., a voice frame once every 20 ms).

:::

## 1xEV-DO {#1xEV-DO}

*Image: 1XEV\_Mobile\_Phone\_Screenshot\_blackberry.png.* [BlackBerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlackBerry) smartphone displaying '1XEV' as the service status in the upper right corner (2011)

**CDMA2000 1xEV-DO (Evolution-Data Optimized)**, often abbreviated as **EV-DO** or **EV**, is a [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunication) standard for the [wireless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless) transmission of data through [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio) signals, typically for [broadband Internet access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadband_Internet_access). It uses [multiplexing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplexing) techniques including [code-division multiple access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code-division_multiple_access) (CDMA) as well as [time-division multiple access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-division_multiple_access) to maximize both individual user's throughput and the overall system throughput. It is standardized (IS-856) by [3rd Generation Partnership Project 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Generation_Partnership_Project_2) (3GPP2) as part of the CDMA2000 family of standards and has been adopted by many [mobile phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone) service providers around the world – particularly those previously employing CDMA networks.

:::

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="1X Advanced"}

## 1X Advanced {#1X_Advanced}

1X Advanced (Rev.E)[^o11][^o12] is the evolution of CDMA2000 1X. It provides up to four times the capacity and 70% more coverage compared to 1X.[^o13]

:::

## Networks {#Networks}

The CDMA Development Group states that, as of April 2014, there are 314 [operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_network_operator) in 118 countries offering CDMA2000 1X and/or 1xEV-DO service.[^o15]

As of 2025, only very few active networks remain, with most of them being private networks maintained for [machine to machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_to_machine) communication for specific critical infrastructure.

::: {#o17 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Access Authentication in CDMA networks"}

## Access authentication in CDMA networks {#Access_authentication_in_CDMA_networks}

Access authentication in CDMA networks for [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunication) and [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing) provide network access for a mobile device. Specific methods such as [CAVE-based Authentication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAVE-based_Authentication) ([IS-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IS-95)/[1xRTT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1xRTT)), and [A12 Authentication](wikipedia://en/11060547) ([1xEV-DO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1xEV-DO)) are possible. The serving network provides the mobile device access authentication mechanism.[^o18][^o19] The exact method employed depends upon the type of service being used:

- [CAVE-based Authentication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAVE-based_Authentication) – Used for access authentication in CDMA/1xRTT
- [AKA](wikipedia://en/1695860) – 3G successor to CAVE-based authentication
- [A12 Authentication](wikipedia://en/11060547) – Used for access authentication in 1xEV-DO

[1xEV-DO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1xEV-DO) Hybrid MS/AT devices may employ both CAVE-based and A12 authentication since these devices connect to both the [1xRTT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1xRTT) and [1xEV-DO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1xEV-DO) networks.

:::

## History {#History}

CDMA2000 technology was developed by [Qualcomm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualcomm) in the late 1990s as an enhancement to the [cdmaOne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CdmaOne) standard.

The intended [4G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4G) successor to CDMA2000 was [UMB (Ultra Mobile Broadband)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_Mobile_Broadband); however, in November 2008, Qualcomm announced it was ending development of the technology, favoring [LTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTE_%28telecommunication%29) instead.[^o21]

In the USA, the last CDMA2000 network was shut down in 2024.[^o22]

## Patent licensing {#Patent_licensing}

In 2007, Qualcomm provided a global patent license for CDMA2000 to the Chinese company [Teleepoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleepoch).[^o24]
