# Joseph Bertrand

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph Bertrand"}

the basketball player

**Joseph Louis François Bertrand** (; 11 March 1822 – 5 April 1900) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [historian of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science) whose work emphasized [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/29952).[^o3]

:::

He lends his name to various paradoxes in mathematics and economics[Bertrand's paradoxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand's_paradox_%28disambiguation%29) and to [Bertrand's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand's_theorem) on the stability of celestial orbits.

## Biography {#Biography}

Joseph Bertrand was the son of physician [Alexandre Jacques François Bertrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Jacques_François_Bertrand) and the brother of archaeologist [Alexandre Bertrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Bertrand). His father died when Joseph was only nine years old; by that time he had learned a substantial amount of mathematics and could speak Latin fluently. At eleven years old he attended the course of the [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique) as an auditor. From age eleven to seventeen, he obtained two bachelor's degrees, a license and a PhD with a thesis concerning the mathematical theory of electricity, and was admitted to the 1839 entrance examination of the École polytechnique. Bertrand was a professor at the École polytechnique and [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France), and was a member of the [Paris Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) of which he was the permanent secretary for twenty-six years.

In 1858 he was elected a foreign member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences).

## Work {#Work}

He conjectured, in 1845, that there is at least one prime number between *n* and 2*n* − 2 for every *n* > 3. [Chebyshev](wikipedia://en/149568) proved this conjecture, now termed [Bertrand's postulate](wikipedia://en/232526), in 1850. He was also famous for two paradoxes of [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), known now as [Bertrand's paradox](wikipedia://en/624291) and the Paradox of [Bertrand's box](wikipedia://en/8849356). There is another paradox concerning [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) that is named for him, known as the [Bertrand paradox](wikipedia://en/528305). In 1849, he was the first to define real numbers using what is now termed a [Dedekind cut](wikipedia://en/45569).[^o6][^o7]

Bertrand translated into French [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125)'s work concerning the [theory of errors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_errors) and the [method of least squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_least_squares) in 1855, with Gauss's approval.

Concerning [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), he reviewed the work on [oligopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligopoly) theory, specifically the [Cournot Competition Model](wikipedia://en/1481119) (1838) of French mathematician [Antoine Augustin Cournot](wikipedia://en/1079244). His [Bertrand Competition Model](wikipedia://en/1462677) (1883) argued that Cournot had reached a very misleading conclusion, and he reworked it using prices rather than quantities as the strategic variables, thus showing that the [equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_equilibrium) price was simply the competitive price.

His 1887 book *Thermodynamique* states in Chapter XII, that thermodynamic entropy and temperature are only defined for [reversible processes](wikipedia://en/416681). He was one of the first people to state this publicly.

## Legacy {#Legacy}

In Barcelona, a street in the Sarrià–Sant Gervasi district is named **Carrer de Josep Bertrand** after Joseph Bertrand, reflecting recognition of his legacy beyond France. The name of the street was officially approved on 16 May 1939 and appears in the official Barcelona street nomenclature, where the Catalan form “Josep” is used instead of the Spanish “José”. Earlier or alternative Spanish-language references to the street use the spelling *José Bertrand*, but the current official name follows standard Catalan orthography. *Carrer de Josep Bertrand* runs through the 08021 postal district and commemorates his contributions by preserving his name in the urban landscape of Barcelona.[^o11]
