# Curvature form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Curvature form"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **curvature form** describes [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) of a [connection](wikipedia://en/706412) on a [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950). The [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) in [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243) can be considered as a special case.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *G* be a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) with [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak g$, and *P* → *B* be a [principal *G*-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950). Let ω be an [Ehresmann connection](wikipedia://en/5731754) on *P* (which is a [$\mathfrak g$-valued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebra-valued_form) [one-form](wikipedia://en/221519) on *P*).

Then the **curvature form** is the $\mathfrak g$-valued 2-form on *P* defined by

$$\Omega=d\omega + {1 \over 2}[\omega \wedge \omega] = D \omega.$$

(In another convention, 1/2 does not appear.) Here $d$ stands for [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782), $[\cdot \wedge \cdot]$ is defined in the article "[Lie algebra-valued form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebra-valued_form)" and *D* denotes the [exterior covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/849738). In other terms,[^o4]

$$\,\Omega(X, Y)= d\omega(X,Y) + {1 \over 2}[\omega(X),\omega(Y)]$$

where *X*, *Y* are tangent vectors to *P*.

There is also another expression for Ω: if *X*, *Y* are horizontal vector fields on *P*, then[^o5]

$$\sigma\Omega(X, Y) = -\omega([X, Y]) = -[X, Y] + h[X, Y]$$

where *hZ* means the horizontal component of *Z*, on the right we identified a vertical vector field and a Lie algebra element generating it ([fundamental vector field](wikipedia://en/3946232)), and $\sigma\in \{1, 2\}$ is the inverse of the normalization factor used by convention in the formula for the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782#In_terms_of_invariant_formula).

A connection is said to be **[flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_vector_bundle)** if its curvature vanishes: Ω = 0. Equivalently, a connection is flat if the structure group can be reduced to the same underlying group but with the discrete topology.

### Curvature form in a vector bundle {#Curvature_form_in_a_vector_bundle}

If *E* → *B* is a vector bundle, then one can also think of ω as a matrix of 1-forms and the above formula becomes the structure equation of E. Cartan:

$$\,\Omega = d\omega + \omega \wedge \omega,$$

where $\wedge$ is the [wedge product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_power). More precisely, if ${\omega^i}_j$ and ${\Omega^i}_j$ denote components of ω and Ω correspondingly, (so each ${\omega^i}_j$ is a usual 1-form and each ${\Omega^i}_j$ is a usual 2-form) then

$$\Omega^i_j = d{\omega^i}_j + \sum_k {\omega^i}_k \wedge {\omega^k}_j.$$

For example, for the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652), the structure group is O(*n*) and Ω is a 2-form with values in the Lie algebra of O(*n*), i.e. the [antisymmetric matrices](wikipedia://en/174055). In this case the form Ω is an alternative description of the [curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526), i.e.

$$\,R(X, Y) = \Omega(X, Y),$$

using the standard notation for the Riemannian curvature tensor.

:::

## Bianchi identities {#Bianchi_identities}

If $\theta$ is the canonical vector-valued 1-form on the [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550), the [torsion](wikipedia://en/706412#Torsion) $\Theta$ of the [connection form](wikipedia://en/706412) $\omega$ is the vector-valued 2-form defined by the structure equation

$$\Theta = d\theta + \omega\wedge\theta = D\theta,$$

where as above *D* denotes the [exterior covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/849738).

The first Bianchi identity takes the form

$$D\Theta = \Omega\wedge\theta.$$

The second Bianchi identity takes the form

$$\, D \Omega = 0$$

and is valid more generally for any [connection](wikipedia://en/706412) in a [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950).

The Bianchi identities can be written in tensor notation as: $R_{abmn;\ell} + R_{ab\ell m;n} + R_{abn\ell;m} = 0.$

The [contracted Bianchi identities](wikipedia://en/57574496) are used to derive the [Einstein tensor](wikipedia://en/1057638) in the [Einstein field equations](wikipedia://en/424540), a key component in the [general theory of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_theory_of_relativity).
