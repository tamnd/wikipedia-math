# Primitive ideal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Primitive ideal"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a left **primitive ideal** is the [annihilator](wikipedia://en/328684) of a (nonzero) [simple](wikipedia://en/29430) left [module](wikipedia://en/276410). A right primitive ideal is defined similarly. Left and right primitive ideals are always two-sided ideals.

:::

Primitive ideals are [prime](wikipedia://en/24928). The [quotient](wikipedia://en/184635) of a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) by a left primitive ideal is a left [primitive ring](wikipedia://en/485862). For [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346) the primitive ideals are [maximal](wikipedia://en/48164), and so commutative primitive rings are all [fields](wikipedia://en/10603).

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Primitive spectrum"}

## Primitive spectrum {#Primitive_spectrum}

The **primitive spectrum** of a ring is a non-commutative analog[^o4] of the [prime spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_spectrum) of a commutative ring.

Let *A* be a ring and $\operatorname{Prim}(A)$ the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of all primitive ideals of *A*. Then there is a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) on $\operatorname{Prim}(A)$, called the **[Jacobson topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobson_topology)**, defined so that the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) *T* is the set of primitive ideals of *A* containing the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of elements of *T*.

Now, suppose *A* is an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) over a field. Then, by definition, a primitive ideal is the kernel of an [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982) $\pi$ of *A* and thus there is a [surjection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjection)

$$\pi \mapsto \ker \pi: \widehat{A} \to \operatorname{Prim}(A).$$

Example: the [spectrum of a unital C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/918414).
