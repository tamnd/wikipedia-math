# Information geometry

*Image: Normal Distribution PDF.svg.* The set of all normal distributions forms a statistical manifold with [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Information geometry"}

**Information geometry** is an interdisciplinary field that applies the techniques of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) to study [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685).[^o2] It studies [statistical manifolds](wikipedia://en/36408395), which are [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) whose points correspond to [probability distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_distributions).

:::

## Introduction {#Introduction}

This should be edited to include some statistical background.

Historically, information geometry can be traced back to the work of [C. R. Rao](wikipedia://en/1418009), who was the first to treat the [Fisher matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_matrix) as a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric).[^o4][^o5] The modern theory is largely due to [Shun'ichi Amari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shun'ichi_Amari), whose work has been greatly influential on the development of the field.[^o6]

Classically, information geometry considered a parametrized [statistical model](wikipedia://en/27576) as a [Riemannian](wikipedia://en/144652), conjugate connection, statistical, and dually flat manifolds. Unlike usual smooth manifolds with tensor metric and Levi-Civita connection, these take into account conjugate connection, torsion, and Amari-Chentsov metric.[^o7] All presented above geometric structures find application in [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773) and [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488). For such models, there is a natural choice of Riemannian metric, known as the [Fisher information metric](wikipedia://en/488687). In the special case that the statistical model is an [exponential family](wikipedia://en/339174), it is possible to induce the statistical manifold with a Hessian metric (i.e a Riemannian metric given by the potential of a convex function). In this case, the manifold naturally inherits two flat [affine connections](wikipedia://en/607690), as well as a canonical [Bregman divergence](wikipedia://en/4491248). Historically, much of the work was devoted to studying the associated geometry of these examples. In the modern setting, information geometry applies to a much wider context, including non-exponential families, [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369), and even abstract statistical manifolds not induced from a known statistical model. The results combine techniques from [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), [affine differential geometry](wikipedia://en/14222703), [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201) and many other fields. One of the most perspective information geometry approaches find applications in [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488). For example, the developing of information-geometric optimization methods (mirror descent[^o8] and natural gradient descent[^o9]).

The standard references in the field are Shun’ichi Amari and Hiroshi Nagaoka's book, *Methods of Information Geometry*,[^o10] and the more recent book by Nihat Ay and others.[^o11] A gentle introduction is given in the survey by Frank Nielsen.[^o12] In 2018, the journal *Information Geometry* was released, which is devoted to the field.
