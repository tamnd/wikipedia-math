# List of superseded scientific theories

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of superseded scientific theories"}

*Image: Cellarius ptolemaic system c2.jpg.* The obsolete [geocentric model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geocentric_model) places Earth at the centre of the [Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe).

This list includes well-known general theories in science and pre-scientific [natural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_history) and [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy) that have since been superseded by other [scientific theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_theory). Many discarded explanations were once supported by a [scientific consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_consensus), but replaced after more empirical information became available that identified flaws and prompted new theories which better explain the available data. Pre-modern explanations originated before the [scientific method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method), with varying degrees of empirical support.

:::

Some scientific theories are discarded in their entirety, such as the replacement of the [phlogiston theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phlogiston_theory) by energy and [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/29952). Some theories known to be incomplete or in some ways incorrect are still used. For example, Newtonian [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586) is accurate enough for practical calculations at everyday distances and velocities, and it is still taught in schools. The more complicated [relativistic mechanics](wikipedia://en/2021419) must be used for long distances and velocities nearing the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light), and [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) for very small distances and objects.

Some aspects of discarded theories are reused in modern explanations. For example, [miasma theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miasma_theory) proposed that all diseases were transmitted by "bad air". The modern [germ theory of disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_theory_of_disease) has found that diseases are caused by microorganisms, which can be transmitted by a variety of routes, including [touching a contaminated object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fomite), [blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood-borne_disease), and [contaminated water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterborne_disease). [Malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria) was discovered to be a [mosquito-borne disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito-borne_disease), explaining why avoiding the "bad air" near swamps prevented it. Increasing ventilation of fresh air, one of the remedies proposed by miasma theory, does remain useful in some circumstances to expel germs spread by [airborne transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_transmission), such as [SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2).[^o2]

Some theories originate in, or are perpetuated by, [pseudoscience](wikipedia://en/23047), which claims to be both scientific and factual, but fails to follow the [scientific method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method). Scientific theories are [testable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testability) and make [falsifiable](wikipedia://en/11283) [predictions](wikipedia://en/246066#Prediction_in_science).[^o3] Thus, it can be a mark of good science if a discipline has a growing list of superseded theories, and conversely, a lack of superseded theories can indicate problems in following the use of the scientific method. *[Fringe science](wikipedia://en/457921)* includes theories that are not currently supported by a consensus in the mainstream [scientific community](wikipedia://en/338705), either because they never had sufficient empirical support, because they were previously mainstream but later disproven, or because they are preliminary theories also known as *[protoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protoscience)* which go on to become mainstream after empirical confirmation. Some theories, such as [Lysenkoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysenkoism), [race science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_racism) or [female hysteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_hysteria) have been generated for political rather than empirical reasons and promoted by force.

## Science {#Science}

### Discarded scientific theories {#Discarded_scientific_theories}

#### Biology {#Biology}

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="Spontaneous generation"}

- [Spontaneous generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_generation) – a principle regarding the spontaneous generation of complex life from inanimate matter, which held that this process was a commonplace and everyday occurrence, as distinguished from univocal generation, or reproduction from parent(s). Falsified by an [experiment by Louis Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_generation#Pasteur_and_Tyndall): where apparently spontaneous generation of microorganisms occurred, it did not happen on repeating the process without access to unfiltered air; on then opening the apparatus to the atmosphere, bacterial growth started.[^o8]

:::

::: {#o9 .entry cue=list-entry name="Transmutation of species"}

- [Transmutation of species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmutation_of_species), [Inheritance of acquired characteristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inheritance_of_acquired_characteristics), [Lysenkoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysenkoism) – first theories of evolution. Not supported by experiment, and rendered obsolete by [Darwinian evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwinian_evolution) and [Mendelian genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendelian_genetics), combined in the [modern synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_synthesis_%2820th_century%29) which finds that genes in the form of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) are the primary way parental characteristics are passed to descendants. Discoveries in [epigenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics) have shown that in some very limited ways, the life experiences of organisms can affect the development of their children.[^o10]

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="Vitalism"}

- [Vitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitalism) – the theory that living things are alive because of some "vital force" independent of matter, as opposed to because of some appropriate assembly of matter. It was gradually discredited by the rise of [organic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry), [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry), and [molecular biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_biology), fields that failed to discover any "vital force." [Friedrich Wöhler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wöhler)'s synthesis of [urea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urea) from [ammonium cyanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonium_cyanate) was only one step in a long road, not a great refutation.[^o12][^o13]

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=list-entry name="Maternal impression"}

- [Maternal impression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_impression) – the theory that the mother's thoughts created [birth defects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_defects). No experimental support (a notion rather than a theory), and rendered obsolete by [genetic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics)[^o15] (see also [fetal origins of adult disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetal_origins_of_adult_disease), [genomic imprinting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genomic_imprinting)).

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=list-entry name="Preformationism"}

- [Preformationism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preformationism) – the theory that all organisms have existed since the beginning of life, and that [gametes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete) contain a miniature but complete preformed individual, and in the case of humans, a [homunculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homunculus). No support when [microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscopy) became available. Rendered obsolete by [cytology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_biology), discovery of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA), and [atomic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_theory).

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=list-entry name="Recapitulation theory"}

- [Recapitulation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recapitulation_theory) – the theory that "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny".[^o18] See [Baer's laws of embryology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baer's_laws_of_embryology).

:::

::: {#o19 .entry cue=list-entry name="Telegony"}

- [Telegony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegony_%28pregnancy%29) – the theory that an offspring can inherit characteristics from a previous mate of its mother's as well as its actual parents, often associated with racism.

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=list-entry name="Out of Asia theory"}

- [Out of Asia theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_Asia_theory) of human origin – The majority view is of a [recent African origin of modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recent_African_origin_of_modern_humans), although a [multiregional origin of modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiregional_origin_of_modern_humans) hypothesis has much support (which incorporates past evidence of Asian origins).

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=list-entry name="Scientific racism"}

- [Scientific racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_racism) – the theory that humanity consists of physically discrete superior or inferior races. Rendered obsolete by [Human evolutionary genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_evolutionary_genetics) and modern anthropology.[^o22]

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=list-entry name="Germ line theory"}

- [Germ line theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_line_theory), explained [immunoglobulin diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunoglobulin_diversity) by proposing that each [antibody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibody) was encoded in a separate germline gene.[^o24][^o25]

:::

#### Chemistry {#Chemistry}

::: {#o27 .entry cue=list-entry name="Energeticism"}

- [Energeticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energeticism) – a theory that attempted to reinterpret all chemistry in terms of energy, rejecting the concept of atoms.

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="Caloric theory"}

- [Caloric theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caloric_theory) – the theory that a self-repelling fluid called "caloric" was the substance of heat. Rendered obsolete by the [mechanical theory of heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_theory_of_heat). Origin of the [calorie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calorie)'s name, a [unit of energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_of_energy) still used for [nutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutrition) in some countries.

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=list-entry name="Classical element"}

- [Classical elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_element) – All matter was once thought composed of various combinations of classical elements (most famously air, earth, fire, and water). [Antoine Lavoisier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Lavoisier) finally refuted this in his 1789 publication, *[Elements of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traité_Élémentaire_de_Chimie)*, which contained the first modern list of chemical elements.

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=list-entry name="Electrochemical dualism"}

- [Electrochemical dualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrochemical_dualism) – the theory that all molecules are salts composed of basic and acidic oxides

:::

::: {#o31 .entry cue=list-entry name="Phlogiston theory"}

- [Phlogiston theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phlogiston_theory) – The theory that combustible goods contain a substance called "phlogiston" that entered air during combustion. Replaced by [Lavoisier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Laurent_Lavoisier)'s work on oxidation.[^o32]

:::

::: {#o33 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dalton's Atomic Theory"}

- Point 2 of [Dalton's Atomic Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dalton#Atomic_theory) was rendered obsolete by discovery of [isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope), and point 3 by discovery of [subatomic particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_particle) and [nuclear reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_reaction).

:::

::: {#o34 .entry cue=list-entry name="Radical theory"}

- [Radical theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_theory) – the theory that organic compounds exist as combinations of radicals that can be exchanged in chemical reactions just as chemical elements can be interchanged in inorganic compounds.[^o35]

:::

- Vitalism – See section on Biology.

::: {#o36 .entry cue=list-entry name="Nascent state"}

- [Nascent state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nascent_state_%28chemistry%29) refers to the form of a [chemical element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element) (or sometimes compound) in the instance of their liberation or formation. Often encountered are [atomic oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allotropes_of_oxygen#Atomic_oxygen) (O<sub>nasc</sub>) and [nascent hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nascent_hydrogen) (H<sub>nasc</sub>), and [chlorine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorine) (Cl<sub>nasc</sub>) or bromine (Br<sub>nasc</sub>).[^o37]

:::

::: {#o38 .entry cue=list-entry name="Polywater"}

- [Polywater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polywater), a hypothesized [polymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer) form of water, the properties of which actually arose from contaminants such as sweat.[^o39]

:::

::: {#o50 .entry cue=list-entry name="Emitter theory"}

::: {#o51 .entry cue=list-entry name="Alessandro Volta"}

- Electromotive force – the original theory by [Alessandro Volta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Volta) misunderstood the active agent of a [voltaic cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltaic_Cell) to be a new type of [force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force) acting on the [charges](wikipedia://en/9804) generated from contact of the [electrodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrode), what he called [contact tension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_tension). [Michael Faraday](wikipedia://en/19727) later correctly explained that the active agent for batteries was a chemical reaction, although Volta's science is correct as part of [contact electrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_electrification).

:::

::: {#o52 .entry cue=list-entry name="Line of force"}

- [Line of force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_force) – pre-existing theory to [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29).

::: {#o53 .entry cue=list-entry name="Balance of nature"}

- [Balance of nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_nature) – superseded by [catastrophe theory](wikipedia://en/434288) and [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295).

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=list-entry name="Atomic theory"}

- Progression of [atomic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_theory)

:::

  - [Democritus](wikipedia://en/8211), the originator of atomic theory, held that everything is composed of atoms that are indestructible. His claim that atoms are indestructible is not the reason it is superseded—as it was later scientists who identified the concept of atoms with particles, which later science showed are destructible. Democritus' theory is superseded because of his position that several kinds of atoms explain pure materials like water or iron, and characteristics that science now identifies with molecules rather than with indestructible primary particles. Democritus also held that between atoms, an empty space of a different nature than atoms allowed atoms to move. This view on space and matter persisted until Einstein described spacetime as being relative and connected to matter.
  - [John Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dalton)'s model of the atom, which held that atoms are indivisible and indestructible (superseded by nuclear physics) and that all atoms of a given element are identical in mass (superseded by discovery of atomic isotopes).[^o55]
  - [Plum pudding model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plum_pudding_model) of the atom—assuming the protons and electrons were mixed together in a single mass
  - [Rutherford model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_model) of the atom with an impenetrable nucleus orbited by electrons
  - [Bohr model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr_model) with quantized orbits
  - [Electron cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_cloud) model following the development of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) in 1925 and the eventual [atomic orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_orbital) models derived from the quantum mechanical solution to the hydrogen atom

### Theories now considered incomplete {#Theories_now_considered_incomplete}

These theories that are no longer considered the most complete representation of reality but remain useful in particular domains or under certain conditions. For some theories, a more complete model is known, but for practical use, the coarser approximation provides good results with much less calculation.

::: {#o116 .entry cue=list-entry name="Newtonian mechanics"}

- [Newtonian mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtonian_mechanics) was extended by the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001) and by [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202). Relativistic corrections to Newtonian mechanics are immeasurably small at velocities not approaching the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light),[^o117] and quantum corrections are usually negligible at atomic or larger scales;[^o118] Newtonian mechanics is totally satisfactory in engineering and physics under most circumstances. The anomalous [perihelion precession of Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perihelion_precession_of_Mercury) was the first observational evidence that relativity was a more accurate model than [Newtonian gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtonian_gravity).

:::

::: {#o119 .entry cue=list-entry name="Classical electrodynamics"}

- [Classical electrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_electrodynamics) is a very close approximation to [quantum electrodynamics](wikipedia://en/25268) except at very small scales and low field strengths.

:::

::: {#o120 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bohr model"}

- The [Bohr model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr_model) of the atom was extended by the quantum mechanical model of the atom.

:::

::: {#o121 .entry cue=list-entry name="Newton's sine-square law of air resistance"}

- The formula known as [Newton's sine-square law of air resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton's_sine-square_law_of_air_resistance) for the [force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force) of a [fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid) on a body was not actually formulated by Newton but by others using a method of calculation used by Newton; it has been found incorrect and not useful except for high-speed [hypersonic flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_flow).[^o122]

:::

::: {#o123 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cycle of erosion"}

- The once-popular [cycle of erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_of_erosion) is now considered one of many possibilities for landscape evolution.[^o124]

:::

::: {#o125 .entry cue=list-entry name="Continental drift"}

- The theory of [continental drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_drift) was incorporated into and improved upon by [plate tectonics](wikipedia://en/24944).

:::

::: {#o126 .entry cue=list-entry name="Rational choice theory"}

- [Rational choice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_choice_theory) as a model of [human behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_behavior)

:::

::: {#o127 .entry cue=list-entry name="Mendelian genetics"}

- [Mendelian genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendelian_genetics), [classical genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_genetics), [Boveri–Sutton chromosome theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boveri–Sutton_chromosome_theory) – first genetic theories. Not invalidated as such, but subsumed into [molecular genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_genetics).

:::
