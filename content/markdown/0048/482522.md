# Carl Wilhelm Borchardt

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Carl Wilhelm Borchardt"}

**Carl Wilhelm Borchardt** (22 February 1817 – 27 June 1880) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

Borchardt was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) family in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin).[^o3] His father, Moritz, was a respected merchant, and his mother was Emma Heilborn.[^o3] Borchardt studied under a number of tutors, including [Julius Plücker](wikipedia://en/52146) and [Jakob Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445). He studied at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) under [Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002) in 1836 and at the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg) in 1839.[^o3] In 1848 he began teaching at the University of Berlin.

He did research in the area of the [arithmetic-geometric mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic-geometric_mean), continuing work by [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) and [Lagrange](wikipedia://en/87793). He generalised the results of [Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117) on [diagonalising](wikipedia://en/246325) symmetric [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859), using [determinants](wikipedia://en/8468) and [Sturm functions](wikipedia://en/1221919). He was also an editor of *[Crelle's Journal](wikipedia://en/973547)* from 1856 to 1880, during which time it was known as *Borchardt's Journal*.

He died in [Rüdersdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rüdersdorf), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany). His grave is preserved in the [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) *Friedhof III der Jerusalems- und Neuen Kirchengemeinde* (Cemetery No. III of the congregations of [Jerusalem's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem's_Church) and [New Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutscher_Dom)) in [Berlin-Kreuzberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreuzberg), south of [Hallesches Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallesches_Tor_%28Berlin_U-Bahn%29).
