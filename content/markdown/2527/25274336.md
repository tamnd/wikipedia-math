# Gabriel's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gabriel's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Gabriel's theorem**, proved by [Pierre Gabriel](wikipedia://en/30716698), classifies the [quivers](wikipedia://en/355100) of finite type in terms of [Dynkin diagrams](wikipedia://en/277109).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

A quiver is of **finite type** if it has only finitely many [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of indecomposable representations. (Gabriel 1972) classified all quivers of finite type, and also their indecomposable representations. More precisely, Gabriel's theorem states that:

1. A ([connected](wikipedia://en/1614492#Connected_graph)) quiver is of finite type if and only if its underlying [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) (when the directions of the arrows are ignored) is one of the [ADE](wikipedia://en/648042) [Dynkin diagrams](wikipedia://en/277109): $A_n$, $D_n$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$.
1. The indecomposable representations are in a one-to-one correspondence with the [positive roots](wikipedia://en/277087#Positive_roots_and_simple_roots) of the [root system](wikipedia://en/277087) of the Dynkin diagram. Specifically, each positive root is a positive combination of simple roots, and the coefficients of its expansion match the dimensions of the vector spaces that the indecomposable has at the vertices of the quiver (those vertices being in correspondence with the simple roots).

:::

## Generalizations and extensions {#Generalizations_and_extensions}

(Dlab Ringel 1973) found a generalization of Gabriel's theorem in which all Dynkin diagrams of finite-dimensional [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1337587) [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) occur. Victor Kac ((Kac 1983)) extended these results to all quivers, not only of Dynkin type, relating their indecomposable representations to the roots of [Kac–Moody algebras](wikipedia://en/1146292).
