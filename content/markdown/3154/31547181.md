# Limiting parallel

*Image: Hyperbolic.svg.* The two lines through a given point *P* and limiting parallel to line *R*.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Limiting parallel"}

In neutral or [absolute geometry](wikipedia://en/699294), and in [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), there may be many lines parallel to a given line $R$ through a point $P$ not on line $R$; however, in the plane, two parallels may be closer to $R$ than all others (one in each direction of $R$).

:::

Thus it is useful to make a new definition concerning parallels in neutral geometry. If there are closest parallels to a given line they are known as the **limiting parallel**, **asymptotic parallel** or **horoparallel** (horo from ὅριον — border).

For [rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_%28geometry%29), the relation of limiting parallel is an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259), which includes the equivalence relation of being coterminal.

If, in a [hyperbolic triangle](wikipedia://en/1283865), the pairs of sides are limiting parallel, then the triangle is an [ideal triangle](wikipedia://en/5532715).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: limiting\_parallels.svg.* The ray *Aa* is a limiting parallel to *Bb*, written: $Aa|||Bb$

A [ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_%28geometry%29) $Aa$ is a limiting parallel to a ray $Bb$ if they are [coterminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coterminal_angles) or if they lie on distinct lines not equal to the line $AB$, they do not meet, and every ray in the interior of the angle $BAa$ meets the ray $Bb$.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Distinct lines carrying limiting parallel rays do not meet.

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

Suppose that the lines carrying distinct parallel rays met. By definition they cannot meet on the side of $AB$ which either $a$ is on. Then they must meet on the side of $AB$ opposite to $a$, call this point $C$. Thus $\angle CAB + \angle CBA < 2 \text{ right angles} \Rightarrow \angle aAB + \angle bBA > 2 \text{ right angles}$. Contradiction.

:::
