# Tautness (topology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tautness"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), a **taut pair** is a [topological pair](wikipedia://en/19067071) $(A,B)$, whose [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) modules $H^{q}(A,B;G)$ are isomorphic to the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of the cohomology modules $H^{q}(U,V;G),$ with $(U,V)$ a pair of open neighborhoods of $(A,B)$, where the direct limit is induced by inclusion.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For a topological pair $(A,B)$ in a topological space $X$, a *neighborhood* $(U,V)$ of such a pair is defined to be a pair such that $U$ and $V$ are [neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28mathematics%29) of $A$ and $B$ respectively.

If we collect all neighborhoods of $(A,B)$, then we can form a [directed set](wikipedia://en/9165) which is directed downward by inclusion. Hence its cohomology module $H^q(U,V;G)$ is a direct system where $G$ is a module over a ring with unity. If we denote its direct limit by

$$\bar{H}^q(A,B;G)=\varinjlim H^q(U,V;G)$$

the [restriction maps](wikipedia://en/1589032) $H^q(U,V;G)\to H^q(A,B;G)$ define a natural homomorphism $i:\bar{H}^q(A,B;G)\to H^q(A,B;G)$.

The pair $(A,B)$ is said to be *tautly embedded* in $X$ (or a *taut pair* in $X$) if $i$ is an isomorphism for all $q$ and $G$.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Basic properties"}

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

- For pair $(A,B)$ of $X$, if two of the three pairs $(B,\emptyset),(A,\emptyset)$, and $(A,B)$ are taut in $X$, so is the third.
- For pair $(A,B)$ of $X$, if $A,B$ and $X$ have [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_%28mathematics%29) [triangulation](wikipedia://en/207174), then $(A,B)$ in $X$ is taut.
- If $U$ varies over the neighborhoods of $A$, there is an isomorphism $\varinjlim \bar{H}^q(U;G)\simeq \bar{H}^q(A;G)$.
- If $(A,B)$ and $(A',B')$ are closed pairs in a normal space $X$, there is an exact relative [Mayer-Vietoris sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayer-Vietoris_sequence) for any coefficient module $G$[^o7]

$$\cdots\to\bar{H}^q(A\cup A',B\cup B')\to\bar{H}^q(A,B)\oplus\bar{H}^q(A',B')\to \bar{H}^q(A\cap A',B\cap B')\to\cdots$$

:::

## Properties related to cohomology theory {#Properties_related_to_cohomology_theory}

- Let $A$ be any subspace of a topological space $X$ which is a [neighborhood retract](wikipedia://en/2120001) of $X$. Then $A$ is a taut subspace of $X$ with respect to [Alexander-Spanier cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander-Spanier_cohomology).
- every retract of an arbitrary [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is a taut subspace of $X$ with respect to Alexander-Spanier cohomology.
- A closed subspace of a paracompactt [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) is a taut subspace of relative to the Alexander cohomology theory[^o9]

### Note {#Note}

Since the [Čech cohomology](wikipedia://en/856614) and the Alexander-Spanier cohomology are naturally isomorphic on the category of all topological pairs,[^o11] all of the above properties are valid for Čech cohomology. However, it's not true for singular cohomology (*see Example*)

## Dependence of cohomology theory {#Dependence_of_cohomology_theory}

::: {#o14 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

Source:[^o15]

Let $X$ be the subspace of $\mathbb R^2\subset S^2$ which is the union of four sets

$$A_1 = \{(x,y)\mid x =0,-2\leq y\leq 1\}$$

$$A_2 = \{(x,y)\mid 0\leq x\leq 1,y =-2\}$$

$$A_3 = \{(x,y)\mid x = 1,-2\leq y\leq 0\}$$

$$A_4 =\{(x,y)\mid 0<x\leq 1,y =\sin 2\pi/x\}$$

The first singular cohomology of $X$ is $H^1(X;Z) = 0$ and using the [Alexander duality theorem](wikipedia://en/3108888) on $S^2-X$, $\varinjlim\{H^q(U;\mathbb Z)\} = \mathbb Z$ as $U$ varies over neighborhoods of $X$.

Therefore, $\varinjlim\{H^q(U;\mathbb Z)\}\to H^1(X;\mathbb Z)$ is not a [monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538) so that $X$ is not a taut subspace of $\mathbb R^2$ with respect to singular cohomology. However, since $X$ is closed in $\mathbb R^2$, it's taut subspace with respect to Alexander cohomology.[^o16]

:::
