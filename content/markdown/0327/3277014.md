# Mergelyan's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mergelyan's theorem"}

**Mergelyan's theorem** is a result from approximation by polynomials in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) proved by the [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_SSR) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Sergei Mergelyan](wikipedia://en/5661318) in 1951.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $K$ be a [compact subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) $\mathbb C$ such that $\mathbb C \setminus K$ is [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_set). Then, every [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) $f : K\to \mathbb C$, such that the [restriction](wikipedia://en/1589032) $f$ to $\text{int}(K)$ is [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110), can be approximated [uniformly](wikipedia://en/50652) on $K$ with [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000). Here, $\text{int}(K)$ denotes the [interior](wikipedia://en/55610) of $K$.[^o4][^o5]

Mergelyan's theorem also holds for open [Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surfaces). Let $\mathcal{A} (K)$ be set of all continuous and complex-valued functions in $\text{int}(K)$, and $\mathcal{O}(X)$ be the set of all functions that are holomorphic in a neighborhood of $K$. Then:

If $K$ is a compact set without holes in an open [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) $X$, then every function in $\mathcal{A} (K)$ can be approximated uniformly on $K$ by functions in $\mathcal{O}(X)$.

Mergelyan's theorem does not always hold in higher dimensions (spaces of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables)), but it has some consequences.

:::

## History {#History}

Mergelyan's theorem is a generalization of the [Weierstrass approximation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_approximation_theorem) and [Runge's theorem](wikipedia://en/3199591).

In the case that $\mathbb C\setminus K$ is *not* connected, in the initial approximation problem the polynomials have to be replaced by [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210). An important step of the solution of this further [rational approximation](wikipedia://en/3090820) problem was also suggested by Mergelyan in 1952. Further deep results on rational approximation are due to, in particular, [A. G. Vitushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._G._Vitushkin).

Weierstrass and Runge's theorems were put forward in 1885, while Mergelyan's theorem dates from 1951. After Weierstrass and Runge, many mathematicians (in particular [Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Leonard_Walsh), [Keldysh](wikipedia://en/2769461), [Lavrentyev](wikipedia://en/63358), [Hartogs](wikipedia://en/2977779), and [Rosenthal](wikipedia://en/33183847)) had been working on the same problem. The method of the proof suggested by Mergelyan is constructive, and remains the only known constructive proof of the result.
