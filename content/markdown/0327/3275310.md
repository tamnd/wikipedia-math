# Line of action

*Image: Torque lever arm w point of application and line of action.svg.* The line of action is shown as the vertical dotted line. It extends in both directions relative to the force vector, but is most useful where it defines the moment arm.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Line of action"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the **line of action** (also called **line of application**) of a [force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force) (*F*) is a geometric representation of how the force is applied. It is the [straight line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29) through the point at which the force is applied, and is in the same [direction](wikipedia://en/54516626) as the [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28physics%29) *F*.[^o2][^o3] The [lever arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lever_arm) is the perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation to the line of action.[^o4]

:::

The concept is essential, for instance, for understanding the [net effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_force) of multiple forces applied to a [body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_object). For example, if two forces of equal [magnitude](wikipedia://en/577301) act upon a [rigid](wikipedia://en/23547165) body along the same line of action but in opposite directions, they cancel and have no net effect. But if, instead, their lines of action are not identical, but merely [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497), then their effect is to create a [moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_%28physics%29) on the body, which tends to [rotate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_around_a_fixed_axis) it.

## Calculation of torque {#Calculation_of_torque}

For the simple geometry associated with the figure, there are three equivalent equations for the magnitude of the [torque](wikipedia://en/30400) associated with a force $\vec F$ directed at displacement $\vec r$ from the axis whenever the force is perpendicular to the axis:

$$\begin{aligned} ||\vec\tau|| & = ||\vec r\times\vec F|| \\ & = rF_\perp \\ & = r_\perp F \\ & = ||r F \sin\theta|| \,, \end{aligned}$$

where $\vec r\times\vec F$ is the [cross-product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-product), $F_\perp$ is the component of $\vec F$ perpendicular to $\hat r$, $r_\perp$ is the [moment arm](wikipedia://en/30400#Moment_arm_formula), and $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec r$ and $\vec F$.
