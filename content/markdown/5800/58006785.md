# Polynomial solutions of P-recursive equations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polynomial solutions of P-recursive equations"}

In mathematics a **[P-recursive equation](wikipedia://en/58006825)** can be solved for **polynomial solutions**. Sergei A. Abramov in 1989 and [Marko Petkovšek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marko_Petkovšek) in 1992 described an [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) which finds all [polynomial](wikipedia://en/373065) solutions of those recurrence equations with polynomial coefficients.[^o2][^o3] The algorithm computes a *degree bound* for the solution in a first step. In a second step an [ansatz](wikipedia://en/1359360) for a polynomial of this degree is used and the unknown coefficients are computed by a [system of linear equations](wikipedia://en/113087). This article describes this algorithm.

:::

In 1995 Abramov, Bronstein and Petkovšek showed that the polynomial case can be solved more efficiently by considering [power series](wikipedia://en/60012) solution of the recurrence equation in a specific power basis (i.e. not the ordinary basis $(x^n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$).[^o4]

Other algorithms which compute [rational](wikipedia://en/58006810) or [hypergeometric](wikipedia://en/39388567) solutions of a linear recurrence equation with polynomial coefficients also use algorithms which compute polynomial solutions.

## Degree bound {#Degree_bound}

Let $\mathbb{K}$ be a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of characteristic zero and $\sum_{k=0}^r p_k(n) \, y (n+k) = f(n)$ a [recurrence equation](wikipedia://en/58006825) of order $r$ with polynomial coefficients $p_k \in \mathbb{K} [n]$, polynomial right-hand side $f \in \mathbb{K}[n]$ and unknown polynomial sequence $y(n) \in \mathbb{K}[n]$. Furthermore $\deg (p)$ denotes the degree of a polynomial $p \in \mathbb{K}[n]$ (with $\deg (0) = - \infty$ for the zero polynomial) and $\text{lc}(p)$ denotes the leading coefficient of the polynomial. Moreover let

$$\begin{aligned} q_i &= \sum_{k=i}^r \binom{k}{i} p_k, & b &= \max_{i=0,\dots,r}(\deg (q_i)-i), \\ \alpha(n) &= \sum_{i=0,\dots,r \atop \deg (q_i) - i = b} \text{lc} (q_i) n^{\underline{i}}, & d_\alpha &= \max \{n \in \mathbb{N} \, : \, \alpha(n) = 0 \} \cup \{ - \infty\} \end{aligned}$$

for $i=0,\dots,r$ where $n^{\underline{i}} = n (n-1) \cdots (n-i+1)$ denotes the [falling factorial](wikipedia://en/230193) and $\mathbb{N}$ the set of nonnegative integers. Then $\deg (y) \leq \max \{ \deg(f) - b, -b-1, d_\alpha \}$. This is called a degree bound for the polynomial solution $y$. This bound was shown by Abramov and Petkovšek.[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o6]

::: {#o8 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Algorithm"}

## Algorithm {#Algorithm}

The algorithm consists of two steps. In a first step the *degree bound* is computed. In a second step an *ansatz* with a polynomial $y$ of that degree with arbitrary coefficients in $\mathbb{K}$ is made and plugged into the recurrence equation. Then the different powers are compared and a system of linear equations for the coefficients of $y$ is set up and solved. This is called the *method undetermined coefficients*.[^o9] The algorithm returns the general polynomial solution of a recurrence equation.

**algorithm** polynomial\_solutions **is** **input:** Linear recurrence equation $\sum_{k=0}^r p_k(n) \, y (n+k) = f(n), p_k, f \in \mathbb{K}[n], p_0, p_r \neq 0$. **output:** The general polynomial solution $y$ if there are any solutions, otherwise false.

**for** $i=0,\dots,r$ **do**

$$q_i = \sum_{k=i}^r \binom{k}{i} p_k$$

**repeat**

$b=\max_{i=0,\dots,r} (\deg (q_i) - i)$ $\alpha(n) = \sum_{i=0,\dots,r \atop \deg (q_i) - i = b} \text{lc} (q_i) n^{\underline{i}}$ $d_\alpha = \max \{n \in \mathbb{N} \, : \, \alpha(n) = 0 \} \cup \{ - \infty\}$ $d = \max \{ \deg (f) - b, -b-1, d_\alpha\}$ $y(n) = \sum_{j=0}^d y_j n^j$ with unknown coefficients $y_j \in \mathbb{K}$ for $j=0,\dots,d$

Compare coefficients of polynomials $\sum_{k=0}^r p_k(n) \, y (n+k)$ and $f(n)$ to get possible values for $y_j, j=0,\dots,d$ **if** there are possible values for $y_j$ **then** **return** general solution $y$ **else** **return** false **end if**

:::

## Example {#Example}

Applying the formula for the degree bound on the recurrence equation

$$(n^2-2) \, y (n) + (-n^2+2n) \, y (n+1)=2n,$$

over $\mathbb{Q}$ yields $\deg (y) \leq 2$. Hence one can use an ansatz with a quadratic polynomial $y(n) =y_2 n^2 + y_1 n + y_0$ with $y_0, y_1, y_2 \in \mathbb{Q}$. Plugging this ansatz into the original recurrence equation leads to

$$2n = (n^2-2) \, y(n) + (-n^2+2n) \, y (n+1) = (y_1 + y_2) \, n^2 + (2 y_0 + 2 y_2 ) \, n - 2 y_0.$$

This is equivalent to the following system of linear equations

$$\begin{aligned} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} y_0 \\ y_1 \\ y_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}$$

with the solution $y_0 = 0, y_1 = -1, y_2 = 1$. Therefore the only polynomial solution is $y (n) = n^2-n$.
