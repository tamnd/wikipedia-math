# Lebesgue's universal covering problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lebesgue's universal covering problem"}

universal cover

*Image: Lebesgue-circle-triangle.svg.* An equilateral triangle of diameter 1 doesn’t fit inside a circle of diameter 1

**Lebesgue's universal covering problem** is an unsolved problem in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) that asks for the [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) shape of smallest area that can cover every planar set of diameter one. The [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) of a set by definition is the least upper bound of the distances between all pairs of points in the set. A shape covers a set if it contains a congruent subset. In other words the set may be rotated, translated or reflected to fit inside the shape.

::: {#o2 .problem cue=unsolved}

What is the minimum area of a convex shape that can cover every planar set of diameter one?

:::

:::

## Formulation and early research {#Formulation_and_early_research}

The problem was posed by [Henri Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514) in a letter to [Gyula Pál](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyula_Pál) in 1914. It was published in a paper by Pál in 1920 along with Pál's analysis.[^o4] He showed that a cover for all [curves of constant width](wikipedia://en/580238) one is also a cover for all sets of diameter one and that a cover can be constructed by taking a regular [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) with an inscribed circle of diameter one and removing two corners from the hexagon to give a cover of area

$$2-\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\approx 0.84529946.$$

*Image: Pál's\_solution\_to\_Lebesgue's\_universal\_covering\_problem.svg.* The shape outlined in black is Pál's solution to Lebesgue's universal covering problem. Within it, planar shapes with diameter one have been included: a circle (in blue), a Reuleaux triangle (in red) and a square (in green).

In 1936, [Roland Sprague](wikipedia://en/42359462) showed that a part of Pál's cover could be removed near one of the other corners while still retaining its property as a cover.[^o5] This reduced the upper bound on the area to $a \le 0.844137708436$.

## Current bounds {#Current_bounds}

After a sequence of improvements to Sprague's solution, each removing small corners from the solution,[^o7][^o8] a 2018 preprint of Philip Gibbs claimed the best upper bound known, a further reduction to area 0.8440935944.[^o9][^o10]

The best known lower bound for the area was provided by Peter Brass and Mehrbod Sharifi using a combination of three shapes in optimal alignment, proving that the area of an optimal cover is at least 0.832.[^o11]

## See also {#See_also}

- [Moser's worm problem](wikipedia://en/30774748), what is the minimum area of a shape that can cover every unit-length curve?
- [Moving sofa problem](wikipedia://en/11413257), the problem of finding a maximum-area shape that can be rotated and translated through an L-shaped corridor
- [Kakeya set](wikipedia://en/588260), a set of minimal area that can accommodate every unit-length line segment (with translations allowed, but not rotations)
- [Blaschke selection theorem](wikipedia://en/11956019), which can be used to prove that Lebesgue's universal covering problem has a solution.
