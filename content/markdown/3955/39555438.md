# Hasse derivative

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hasse derivative"}

In mathematics, the **Hasse derivative** is a generalisation of the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) which allows the formulation of [Taylor's theorem](wikipedia://en/51714) in [coordinate rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_ring) of [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *k*\[*X*\] be a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) over a [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28algebra%29) *k*. The *r*-th Hasse derivative of *X*<sup>*n*</sup> is

$$D^{(r)} X^n = \binom{n}{r} X^{n-r},$$

if *n* ≥ *r* and zero otherwise.[^o4] In [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero we have

$$D^{(r)} = \frac{1}{r!} \left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}X}\right)^r \ .$$

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The Hasse derivative is a generalized derivation on *k*\[*X*\] and extends to a generalized derivation on the [function field](wikipedia://en/2783843) *k*(*X*),[^o4] satisfying an analogue of the product rule

$$D^{(r)}(fg) = \sum_{i=0}^r D^{(i)}(f) D^{(r-i)}(g)$$

and an analogue of the chain rule.[^o7] Note that the $D^{(r)}$ are not themselves [derivations](wikipedia://en/897658) in general, but are closely related.

A form of Taylor's theorem holds for a function *f* defined in terms of a [local parameter](wikipedia://en/21309351) *t* on an algebraic variety:[^o8]

$$f = \sum_r D^{(r)}(f) \cdot t^r \ .$$

:::
