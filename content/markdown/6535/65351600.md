# Perron's irreducibility criterion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perron's irreducibility criterion"}

**Perron's irreducibility criterion** is a sufficient condition for a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) to be [irreducible](wikipedia://en/188725) in [$\mathbb{Z}[x]$](wikipedia://en/373065)—that is, for it to be unfactorable into the product of lower-[degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) polynomials with [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203).

:::

This criterion is applicable only to [monic polynomials](wikipedia://en/314730). However, unlike other commonly used criteria, Perron's criterion does not require any knowledge of [prime decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_decomposition) of the polynomial's coefficients.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Criterion"}

## Criterion {#Criterion}

Suppose we have the following [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) with integer coefficients

$$f(x)=x^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_1x+a_0,$$

where $a_0\neq 0$. If either of the following two conditions applies:

- $$|a_{n-1}|> 1+|a_{n-2}|+\cdots+|a_0|$$
- $$|a_{n-1}|= 1+|a_{n-2}|+\cdots+|a_0|, \quad f(\pm 1) \neq 0$$

then $f$ is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/188725) over the integers (and by [Gauss's lemma](wikipedia://en/2310971) also over the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024)).

:::

## History {#History}

The criterion was first published by [Oskar Perron](wikipedia://en/1485469) in 1907 in [Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_für_die_reine_und_angewandte_Mathematik).[^o5]

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

A short [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) can be given based on the following [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) due to Panaitopol:[^o8][^o9]

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=inline-head name="Lemma"}

**Lemma.** Let $f(x)=x^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_1x+a_0$ be a polynomial with $|a_{n-1}|>1+|a_{n-2}|+\cdots+|a_{1}|+|a_0|$. Then exactly one [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_polynomial) $z$ of $f$ satisfies $|z|>1$, and the other $n-1$ zeroes of $f$ satisfy $|z|<1$.

Suppose that $f(x)=g(x)h(x)$ where $g$ and $h$ are integer polynomials. Since, by the above lemma, $f$ has only one zero with [modulus](wikipedia://en/5826#Polar_complex_plane) not less than $1$, one of the polynomials $g, h$ has all its zeroes strictly inside the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071). Suppose that $z_1,\dots,z_k$ are the zeroes of $g$, and $|z_1|,\dots,|z_k|<1$. Note that $g(0)$ is a nonzero integer, and $|g(0)|=|z_1\cdots z_k|<1$, contradiction. Therefore, $f$ is irreducible.

:::

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

In his publication Perron provided variants of the criterion for multivariate polynomials over arbitrary [fields](wikipedia://en/10603). In 2010, Bonciocat published novel proofs of these criteria.[^o13]

:::
