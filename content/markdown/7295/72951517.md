# Pierre Young

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pierre Young"}

**Pierre Henry John Young** (12 June 1926 – 4 August 1985) was a British mathematician, of French heritage, who was in charge, during the 1960s, of the engine development for [Concorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concorde).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in France, speaking French. He attended [Lycée Condorcet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Condorcet), and school in England from 1938, being evacuated to [Herefordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herefordshire).

At [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) he studied Maths.[^o4] Whilst at Cambridge, his knowledge of the French language was given to the BBC foreign language service, for coded messages to the French Resistance.

## Career {#Career}

He joined Bristol Siddeley in 1949, and in 1959 he became assistant chief engineer.

### Concorde engine {#Concorde_engine}

He was the head of Concorde engine programme from 1962.[^o7] This was taken over by Rolls-Royce from 1966. His knowledge of the French language helped the two teams socialise, and work. He understood the 'volatile French temperament'.

On Wednesday 3 April 1969, he appeared on 'The Change Makers' on BBC1 at 10.50pm.[^o8] [^o9]

On 2 March 1969, he attended the first flight of Concorde, in France.,[^o10] with his deputy, [Peter Calder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Calder).

By the early 1970s, he was Technical Director of the Bristol Engine Division of Rolls-Royce. He gave a talk about Concorde on Wednesday 3 May 1972 at the Bristol branch of the [Royal Aeronautical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Aeronautical_Society).[^o11] In February 1975 he became deputy technical director of Rolls-Royce.[^o12]

He was featured on the 'Faster than the Sun' documentary on BBC1 on Wednesday 13 April 1983, following Speedbird 193 from London, with Captain [Brian Walpole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Walpole) and [Christopher Orlebar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Orlebar), passing by Nova Scotia and [Nantucket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantucket).[^o13][^o14]

He received the RAeS Gold medal in 1984.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He married Lily Irene Cahn (German nationality, from [Fürth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fürth) in northern Bavaria, 14 July 1928- 4 January 1990) in 1953; her elder brother was [Robert W. Cahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_W._Cahn) FRS, and her nephew is Sir [Andrew Cahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Cahn).

His son, Thomas, and daughter, Michele, both attended the University of Cambridge.

He went on holidays, climbing in the Alps. Twice a week he would put on his shorts, and jog around [The Downs, Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Downs,_Bristol). He lived on Rockleaze Avenue in [Sneyd Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sneyd_Park), near [Stoke Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_Bishop).[^o16][^o17] His memorial service was on 20 February 1986 at St Peter's church in [Filton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filton), attended by his wife Irène.[^o18]
