# Associated prime

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Associated prime"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), an **associated prime** of a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) *M* over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* is a type of [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) of *R* that arises as an [annihilator](wikipedia://en/328684) of a (prime) submodule of *M*. The set of associated primes is usually denoted by $\operatorname{Ass}_R(M),$ and sometimes called the *assassin* or *assassinator* of $M$ (word play between the notation and the fact that an associated prime is an *annihilator*).[^o2]

:::

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), associated primes are linked to the [Lasker–Noether primary decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker–Noether_theorem) of ideals in [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346) [Noetherian rings](wikipedia://en/56129). Specifically, if an ideal *J* is decomposed as a finite intersection of [primary ideals](wikipedia://en/3021435), the [radicals](wikipedia://en/252009) of these primary ideals are [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928), and this set of prime ideals coincides with $\operatorname{Ass}_R(R/J).$[^n1] Also linked with the concept of "associated primes" of the ideal are the notions of **isolated primes** and **embedded primes**.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A nonzero *R*-module *N* is called a **prime module** if the annihilator $\mathrm{Ann}_R(N)=\mathrm{Ann}_R(N')\,$ for any nonzero submodule *N'* of *N*. For a prime module *N*, $\mathrm{Ann}_R(N)\,$ is a prime ideal in *R*.[^n2]

An **associated prime** of an *R*-module *M* is an ideal of the form $\mathrm{Ann}_R(N)\,$ where *N* is a prime submodule of *M*. In commutative algebra the usual definition is different, but equivalent:[^n3] if *R* is commutative, an associated prime *P* of *M* is a prime ideal of the form $\mathrm{Ann}_R(m)\,$ for a nonzero element *m* of *M* or equivalently $R/P$ is isomorphic to a submodule of *M*.

In a commutative ring *R*, minimal elements in $\operatorname{Ass}_R(M)$ (with respect to the set-theoretic inclusion) are called **isolated primes** while the rest of the associated primes (i.e., those properly containing associated primes) are called **embedded primes**.

A module is called **coprimary** if *xm* = 0 for some nonzero *m* ∈ *M* implies *x*<sup>*n*</sup>*M* = 0 for some positive integer *n*. A nonzero finitely generated module *M* over a commutative [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) is coprimary [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it has exactly one associated prime. A submodule *N* of *M* is called *P*-primary if $M/N$ is coprimary with *P*. An ideal *I* is a *P*-[primary ideal](wikipedia://en/3021435) if and only if $\operatorname{Ass}_R(R/I) = \{P\}$; thus, the notion is a generalization of a primary ideal.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Most of these properties and assertions are given in (Lam 1999) starting on page 86.

- If *M'*⊆*M*, then $\mathrm{Ass}_R(M')\subseteq\mathrm{Ass}_R(M).$ If in addition *M'* is an [essential submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_submodule) of *M*, their associated primes coincide.
- It is possible, even for a commutative [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391), that the set of associated primes of a [finitely generated module](wikipedia://en/326430) is empty. However, in any ring satisfying the [ascending chain condition](wikipedia://en/3189) on ideals (for example, any right or left Noetherian ring) every nonzero module has at least one associated prime.
- Any [uniform module](wikipedia://en/31553078) has either zero or one associated primes, making uniform modules an example of coprimary modules.
- For a one-sided Noetherian ring, there is a surjection from the set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable [injective modules](wikipedia://en/473775) onto the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) $\mathrm{Spec}(R).$ If *R* is an [Artinian ring](wikipedia://en/489273), then this map becomes a bijection.
- **Matlis' Theorem**: For a commutative Noetherian ring *R*, the map from the isomorphism classes of indecomposable injective modules to the spectrum is a bijection. Moreover, a complete set of representatives for those classes is given by $E(R/\mathfrak{p})\,$ where $E(-)\,$ denotes the [injective hull](wikipedia://en/665821) and $\mathfrak{p}\,$ ranges over the prime ideals of *R*.
- For a [Noetherian module](wikipedia://en/542431) *M* over any ring, there are only finitely many associated primes of *M*.

For the case for commutative Noetherian rings, see also [Primary decomposition#Primary decomposition from associated primes](wikipedia://en/1208391#Primary_decomposition_from_associated_primes).

:::

::: {#o12 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If $R = \mathbb{C}[x,y,z,w]$ the associated prime ideals of $I = ((x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + w^2)\cdot (z^3 - w^3 -3x^3))$ are the ideals $(x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + w^2)$ and $(z^3 - w^3 -3x^3).$
- If *R* is the ring of integers, then non-trivial [free abelian groups](wikipedia://en/247296) and non-trivial [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) of prime power order are coprimary.
- If *R* is the ring of integers and *M* a finite abelian group, then the associated primes of *M* are exactly the primes dividing the order of *M*.
- The group of order 2 is a quotient of the integers **Z** (considered as a [free module](wikipedia://en/326454) over itself), but its associated prime ideal (2) is not an associated prime of **Z**.
