# Van Benthem's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Van Benthem's theorem"}

In [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365), **van Benthem's theorem** characterizes propositional modal logic as the [fragment](wikipedia://en/43571557) of [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983) that is invariant under [bisimulation](wikipedia://en/397571). The theorem was proven by [Johan van Benthem](wikipedia://en/1498176) in his doctoral thesis, *Modal Correspondence Theory* (1976).[^o2] A formal statement and game-theoretic proof of van Benthem's theorem is provided by Otto.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Formal Statement"}

## Formal Statement {#Formal_Statement}

The following is based on Otto.[^o3] Consider [formulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_%28logic%29) in [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983) with the [signature](wikipedia://en/7788156) $(E, P_1, \dots, P_n)$, where $E$ is a binary relation symbol and $P_i$ are unary relation symbols. A [Kripke model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kripke_model) $\mathcal A = (A, E^\mathcal A, P_1^\mathcal A, \dots, P_n^\mathcal A)$ is a [structure](wikipedia://en/4055928) with the same signature. A pointed Kripke model is a pair $(\mathcal A, a)$ where $\mathcal M$ is a [Kripke model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kripke_model) and $a \in A$. We write $(\mathcal A, a) \sim (\mathcal B, b)$ if $(a, b) \in R$ for some [bisimulation](wikipedia://en/397571) $R$.

A formula with one [free variable](wikipedia://en/147460) $\varphi(x)$ in first-order logic is *invariant under bisimulation* if for all pointed Kripke models $(\mathcal A, a), (\mathcal B, b)$, $(\mathcal A, a) \sim (\mathcal B, b)$ implies that $\mathcal A, a \models \varphi(x) \Leftrightarrow \mathcal B, a \models \varphi(x)$.

Van Benthem's theorem states that the following are equivalent:

- $\varphi(x)$ is invariant under bisimulation.
- $\varphi(x)$ is [logically equivalent](wikipedia://en/169324) to a formula in propositional [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365).

The statement above shows that the fragment of first-order logic invariant under bisimulation can be translated into propositional modal logic. The converse is given by the [standard translation](wikipedia://en/25310346), which shows that every formula in modal logic can be translated into a formula in first-order logic. These formulas are invariant under bisimulation, since modal logic is invariant under bisimulation.[^o6]

:::

## Similar Results {#Similar_Results}

While van Benthem showed that the theorem holds over the class of (finite and infinite) Kripke models, Rosen showed that the theorem also holds over the class of finite Kripke models.[^o8]

Theorems analogous to van Benthem's theorem have been found for various modal logics. For example, Janin and Walukiewicz showed that [modal μ-calculus](wikipedia://en/6158953) is the fragment of [monadic second-order logic](wikipedia://en/14650395) invariant under bisimulation.[^o9]
