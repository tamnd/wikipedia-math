# Non-wellfounded mereology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Non-wellfounded mereology"}

In [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), specifically [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), [mereology](wikipedia://en/375380) is the study of parthood relationships. In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [formal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic), [wellfoundedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellfoundedness) prohibits $\cdots<x<\cdots<x<\cdots$ for any *x*.

:::

Thus **non-wellfounded mereology** treats [topologically](wikipedia://en/29954) circular, cyclical, repetitive, or other eventual self-containment.

More formally, non-wellfounded [partial orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) may exhibit $\cdots<x<\cdots<x<\cdots$ for some *x* whereas well-founded orders prohibit that.
