# Sue Ann Campbell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sue Ann Campbell"}

**Sue Ann Campbell** is a Canadian [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [computational neuroscientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_neuroscience) known for her work on [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087), [delay differential equations](wikipedia://en/3941333), and their applications in modeling [neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_network), [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466), and [balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_%28ability%29). She is a professor of applied mathematics at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo), former chair of the Department of Applied Mathematics, associate dean for research and international in the university's Faculty of Mathematics, and president of the Canadian Applied and Industrial Mathematical Society.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Campbell has a bachelor's degree in mathematics (B.Math.) from the University of Waterloo, earned in 1986. She completed her Ph.D. in 1991 at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). Her dissertation, *The Effects of Symmetry on the Dynamics of Low-Dimensional Modal Interactions*, was supervised by [Philip Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Holmes).

She was a postdoctoral researcher at the [University of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montreal) before taking an assistant professor position at [Concordia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_University). She returned to the University of Waterloo as an assistant professor in 1994.

She was elected as president of the Canadian Applied and Industrial Mathematical Society in 2021, for a term beginning in 2023.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Campbell was the 2005 winner of the Arthur Beaumont Distinguished Service Award of the Canadian Applied and Industrial Mathematics Society.
