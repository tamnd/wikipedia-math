# Christoph Thiele

*Image: Christoph Thiele.jpg.* Thiele at [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberwolfach), 2011

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Christoph Thiele"}

**Christoph Thiele** (born 10. September 1968 in [Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt)) is a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in the field of [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147). After completing his undergraduate studies at [TU Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Darmstadt) and [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University), he obtained his Ph.D. in 1995 at [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale) under the supervision of [Ronald Coifman](wikipedia://en/1558980). After spending time at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA), where he was promoted to full professor, he occupied the [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/10989) Chair at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn).[^o2]

:::

He is famous for work (joint with [Michael Lacey](wikipedia://en/6508239)) on the bilinear [Hilbert transform](wikipedia://en/574024) and for giving a simplified proof of [Carleson's theorem](wikipedia://en/24105681); the techniques in this proof have deeply influenced the field of [time–frequency analysis](wikipedia://en/857897). He was a recipient of the 1996 [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize),[^o3] an invited speaker at the 2002 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842)[^o4] and a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o5]
