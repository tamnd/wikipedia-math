# Kurt Vogel (historian)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kurt Vogel"}

**Kurt Vogel** (30 September 1888 – 27 October 1985)[^o2] was a German historian of mathematics.[^o3]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Vogel was born in [Altdorf bei Nürnberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altdorf_bei_Nürnberg) to Johann Georg Vogel and Ehefrau Maria. He attended school in [Bayreuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayreuth), [Schwabach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwabach), and [Ansbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansbach). From 1907 to 1911, he studied mathematics and physics with [Max Noether](wikipedia://en/2482337), [Paul Gordan](wikipedia://en/1059850), and [Erhard Schmidt](wikipedia://en/672191) in [Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlangen), and with [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887), [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), and [Otto Toeplitz](wikipedia://en/547682) in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen). He passed his examination to become a schoolteacher in 1911, then served as an army officer from 1913 to 1920 before taking a teaching post in Munich.[^o3]

In 1940, Vogel was appointed to a professorship at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München), where he spent the remainder of his career. He studied a variety of mathematical texts from Babylonian, Egyptian, Greek, and Chinese scholars, such as the [Rhind Mathematical Papyrus](wikipedia://en/9210114). Self-taught in several ancient languages, Vogel produced German translations of [al-Khwarizmi](wikipedia://en/649861)'s *On the Calculation with Hindu Numerals* (from the Latin translation, *Algoritmi de numero Indorum*), and the Chinese treatise *[The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art](wikipedia://en/204207)*. Vogel officially retired from his post in 1963, but founded and led the university's Institute for the History of Natural Science and Mathematics until 1970.[^o3]

In 1969, he was awarded the [George Sarton Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Sarton_Medal) for his contributions to the history of science.[^o3]
