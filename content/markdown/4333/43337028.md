# Milnor–Moore theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Milnor–Moore theorem"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **Milnor–Moore theorem**, introduced by (txt) classifies an important class of [Hopf algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_algebras), of the sort that often show up as [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) rings in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).

:::

The theorem states: given a connected, graded, [cocommutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocommutative) [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714) *A* over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero with $\dim A_n < \infty$ for all *n*, the natural Hopf algebra homomorphism

$$U(P(A)) \to A$$

from the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) of the graded [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $P(A)$ of [primitive elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_elements_%28Hopf_algebra%29) of *A* to *A* is an isomorphism. Here we say *A* is **connected** if $A_0$ is the field and $A_n = 0$ for negative *n*. The universal enveloping algebra of a graded Lie algebra *L* is the quotient of the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628) of *L* by the two-sided ideal generated by all elements of the form $xy- (-1)^{|x||y|}yx - [x,y]$.

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), the term usually refers to the corollary of the aforementioned result, that for a [pointed](wikipedia://en/1236666), [simply connected space](wikipedia://en/523879) *X*, the following isomorphism holds:

$$U(\pi_{\ast}(\Omega X) \otimes \mathbb{Q}) \cong H_{\ast}(\Omega X;\mathbb{Q}),$$

where $\Omega X$ denotes the [loop space](wikipedia://en/2174332) of *X*, compare with Theorem 21.5 from (Félix Halperin Thomas 2001). This work may also be compared with that of (). Here the multiplication on the right hand side induced by the product $\Omega X \times \Omega X \rightarrow \Omega X$, and then by the Eilenberg-Zilber multiplication $C_*(\Omega X) \times C_*(\Omega X) \rightarrow C_*(\Omega X)$.

On the left hand side, since $X$ is simply connected, $\pi_{\ast}(\Omega X) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space; the notation $U(V)$ stands for the universal enveloping algebra.
