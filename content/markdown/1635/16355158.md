# Iwasawa group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Iwasawa group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is called an **Iwasawa group**, **M-group** or **modular group** if its [lattice of subgroups](wikipedia://en/3983172) is [modular](wikipedia://en/1089311). Alternatively, a group *G* is called an Iwasawa group when every subgroup of *G* is [permutable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutable_subgroup) in *G* (Ballester-Bolinches Esteban-Romero Asaad 2010, 24–25). The name is derived from [Kenkichi Iwasawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenkichi_Iwasawa).

:::

(txt Kenkichi Iwasawa) proved that a [*p*-group](wikipedia://en/24664) *G* is an Iwasawa group if and only if one of the following cases happens:

- *G* is a [Dedekind group](wikipedia://en/153106), or
- *G* contains an [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) *N* such that the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) *G/N* is a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) and if *q* denotes a generator of *G/N*, then for all *n* ∈ *N*, *q*<sup>−1</sup>*nq* = *n*<sup>1+*p*<sup>*s*</sup></sup> where *s* ≥ 1 in general, but *s* ≥ 2 for *p*=2.

In (Berkovich Janko 2008, 257), Iwasawa's proof was deemed to have essential gaps, which were filled by [Franco Napolitani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Napolitani) and [Zvonimir Janko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvonimir_Janko). (txt) has provided an alternative proof along different lines in his textbook. As part of Schmidt's proof, he proves that a finite *p*-group is a modular group if and only if every subgroup is permutable, by (Schmidt 1994, Lemma 2.3.2, p. 55).

Every subgroup of a finite *p*-group is [subnormal](wikipedia://en/2774415), and those finite groups in which subnormality and permutability coincide are called PT-groups. In other words, a finite *p*-group is an Iwasawa group if and only if it is a [PT-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PT-group).

## Examples {#Examples}

The Iwasawa group of order 16 is isomorphic to the [modular maximal-cyclic group](wikipedia://en/1050057) of order 16.
