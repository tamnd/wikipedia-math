# Isoperimetric ratio

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isoperimetric ratio"}

In [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202), the **isoperimetric ratio** of a [simple closed curve](wikipedia://en/267444) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) is the ratio $L^{2}/A$, where $L$ is the [length](wikipedia://en/18542) of the curve and $A$ is its [area](wikipedia://en/1209). It is a [dimensionless quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_quantity) that is [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) under [similarity transformations](wikipedia://en/39377) of the curve.

:::

According to the [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182), the isoperimetric ratio has its minimum value, 4$\pi$, for a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220); any other curve has a larger value.[^o2] Thus, the isoperimetric ratio can be used to measure how far from circular a shape is.

The [curve-shortening flow](wikipedia://en/44089758) decreases the isoperimetric ratio of any smooth [convex curve](wikipedia://en/29747989) so that, in the limit as the curve shrinks to a point, the ratio becomes 4$\pi$.[^o3]

For higher-dimensional bodies of dimension *d*, the isoperimetric ratio can similarly be defined as $B^{d}/V^{d - 1}$ where *B* is the [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) of the body (the measure of its boundary) and *V* is its [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) (the measure of its interior).[^o4] Other related quantities include the [Cheeger constant](wikipedia://en/16531739) of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) and the (differently defined) [Cheeger constant of a graph](wikipedia://en/7762273).[^o5]
