# Besov measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Besov measure"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) — specifically, in the fields of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [inverse problems](wikipedia://en/203956) — **Besov measures** and associated **Besov-distributed random variables** are generalisations of the notions of [Gaussian measures](wikipedia://en/6572909) and [random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variables), [Laplace distributions](wikipedia://en/1106213), and other classical distributions. They are particularly useful in the study of [inverse problems](wikipedia://en/49571) on [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630) for which a Gaussian [Bayesian prior](wikipedia://en/472877) is an inappropriate model. The construction of a Besov measure is similar to the construction of a [Besov space](wikipedia://en/18542594), hence the nomenclature.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let $H$ be a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) of functions defined on a domain $D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d}$, and let $\{ e_{n} \mid n \in \mathbb{N} \}$ be a [complete orthonormal basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_orthonormal_basis) for $H$. Let $s \in \mathbb{R}$ and $1 \leq p < \infty$. For $u = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} u_{n} e_{n} \in H$, define

$$\| u \|_{X^{s, p}} = \left\| \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} u_{n} e_{n} \right\|_{X^{s,p}} := \left( \sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} n^{( \frac{p s}{d} + \frac{p}{2} - 1)} | u_{n} |^{p} \right)^{1/p}.$$

This defines a [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) on the subspace of $H$ for which it is finite, and we let $X^{s, p}$ denote the [completion](wikipedia://en/7832#Completion) of this subspace with respect to this new norm. The motivation for these definitions arises from the fact that $\| u \|_{X^{s, p}}$ is equivalent to the norm of $u$ in the Besov space $B_{p p}^{s}(D)$.

Let $\kappa > 0$ be a scale parameter, similar to the precision (the reciprocal of the [variance](wikipedia://en/32344)) of a Gaussian measure. We now define a $X^{s, p}$-valued random variable $u$ by

$$u := \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} n^{-(\frac{s}{d} + \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{p})} \kappa^{- \frac{1}{p}} \xi_{n} e_{n},$$

where $\xi_{1}, \xi_{2}, \dots$ are sampled independently and identically from the generalized Gaussian measure on $\mathbb{R}$ with Lebesgue [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) proportional to $\exp ( - \tfrac{1}{2} | \xi_{n} |^{p} )$. Informally, $u$ can be said to have a probability density function proportional to $\exp (- \tfrac{\kappa}{2} \| u \|_{X^{s, p}}^{p} )$ with respect to infinite-dimensional Lebesgue measure ([which does not make rigorous sense](wikipedia://en/6607484)), and is therefore a natural candidate for a "typical" element of $X^{s, p}$ (although this Is not quite true — see below).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

It is easy to show that, when *t* ≤ *s*, the *X*<sup>*t*,*p*</sup> norm is finite whenever the *X*<sup>*s*,*p*</sup> norm is. Therefore, the spaces *X*<sup>*s*,*p*</sup> and *X*<sup>*t*,*p*</sup> are nested:

$$X^{s, p} \subseteq X^{t, p} \mbox{ when } t \leq s.$$

This is consistent with the usual nesting of smoothness classes of functions *f*: *D* → **R**: for example, the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) *H*<sup>2</sup>(*D*) is a subspace of *H*<sup>1</sup>(*D*) and in turn of the [Lebesgue space](wikipedia://en/45194) *L*<sup>2</sup>(*D*) = *H*<sup>0</sup>(*D*); the [Hölder space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hölder_space) *C*<sup>1</sup>(*D*) of continuously differentiable functions is a subspace of the space *C*<sup>0</sup>(*D*) of continuous functions.

It can be shown that the series defining *u* converges in *X*<sup>*t*,*p*</sup> [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) for any *t* \< *s* − *d* / *p*, and therefore gives a well-defined *X*<sup>*t*,*p*</sup>-valued random variable. Note that *X*<sup>*t*,*p*</sup> is a larger space than *X*<sup>*s*,*p*</sup>, and in fact thee random variable *u* is [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) *not* in the smaller space *X*<sup>*s*,*p*</sup>. The space *X*<sup>*s*,*p*</sup> is rather the Cameron-Martin space of this probability measure in the Gaussian case *p* = 2. The random variable *u* is said to be **Besov distributed** with parameters (*κ*, *s*, *p*), and the induced [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) is called a **Besov measure**.

:::
