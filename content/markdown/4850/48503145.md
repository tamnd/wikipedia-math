# Total absolute curvature

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Total absolute curvature"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **total absolute curvature** of a [smooth curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_curve) is a number defined by integrating the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) of the [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) around the curve. It is a [dimensionless quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_quantity) that is [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) under [similarity transformations](wikipedia://en/39377) of the curve, and that can be used to measure how far the curve is from being a [convex curve](wikipedia://en/29747989).[^o2]

:::

If the curve is parameterized by its [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401), the total absolute curvature can be expressed by the formula

$$\int |\kappa(s)| ds,$$

where $s$ is the arc length parameter and $\kappa$ is the curvature. This is almost the same as the formula for the [total curvature](wikipedia://en/19172363), but differs in using the absolute value instead of the signed curvature.[^o3]

Because the total curvature of a [simple closed curve](wikipedia://en/267444) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) is always exactly 2$\pi$, the total *absolute* curvature of a simple closed curve is also always *at least* 2$\pi$. It is exactly 2$\pi$ for a [convex curve](wikipedia://en/29747989), and greater than 2$\pi$ whenever the curve has any non-convexities.[^o3] When a smooth simple closed curve undergoes the [curve-shortening flow](wikipedia://en/44089758), its total absolute curvature decreases monotonically until the curve becomes convex, after which its total absolute curvature remains fixed at 2$\pi$ until the curve collapses to a point.[^o4][^o5]

The total absolute curvature may also be defined for curves in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). Again, it is at least 2$\pi$ (this is [Fenchel's theorem](wikipedia://en/3077732)), but may be larger. If a space curve is surrounded by a sphere, the total absolute curvature of the sphere equals the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of the [central projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_projection) of the curve onto a plane tangent to a random point of the sphere.[^o6] According to the [Fáry–Milnor theorem](wikipedia://en/3133127), every nontrivial smooth [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) must have total absolute curvature greater than 4$\pi$.[^o3]
