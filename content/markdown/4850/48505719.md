# Léon Autonne

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Léon Autonne"}

**Léon César Autonne** (28 July 1859, [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa) – 12 January 1916[^o2]) was a French engineer and mathematician, specializing in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), and [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Autonne studied from 1878 to 1880 at l'[École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique) and then at the [École des ponts et chaussées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_des_Ponts_ParisTech) and became there *Ingénieur en chef*. He received in 1882 from the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) his Ph.D. with dissertation *Recherches sur les intégrales algébriques des équations differentielles à coefficients rationnels*, with [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422) as chair of the thesis committee. The dissertation was based on research initiated by [Camille Jordan](wikipedia://en/355931).

An 1891 article by Autonne in the Comptes rendus de l'Académie des Sciences de Paris is one of the earliest uses of the concept of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) (as groups of Monsieur Lie).[^o5][^o6]

Autonne won the Prix Dalmont in 1894.[^o7] He was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in 1897, 1900, 1904 and 1908. On 6 January 1902 he was made [Chevalier de la Légion d'honneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour).

The [Autonne-Takagi factorization of complex symmetric matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_matrices#Complex_symmetric_matrices) is named in his honour.
