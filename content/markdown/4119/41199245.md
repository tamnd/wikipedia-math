# Peter Roquette

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Roquette"}

*Image: Peter Roquette.jpeg.* Roquette in 2006

**Peter Jaques Roquette** (8 October 1927 – 24 February 2023) was a German mathematician working in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Roquette was born in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg) on 8 October 1927. He studied at the [University of Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen), the [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin), and the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg). In 1951, he defended a dissertation at the University of Hamburg under [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118), providing a new proof of the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125) for [algebraic function fields](wikipedia://en/447645) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) (the first proof was given by [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) in 1940). From 1951 to 1952, he was an assistant at the Mathematical Research Institute at [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberwolfach) and from 1952 to 1954 at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München). From 1954 to 1956, he worked at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton. In 1954, he was Privatdozent at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München, and from 1956 to 1959, he worked in the same position at the University of Hamburg. In 1959, he became an associate professor at the [Saarland University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland_University) and in the same year at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen). From 1967, he was professor at [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University), where he retired in 1996.

Roquette worked on [number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) and function fields and especially local [*p*-adic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_field). He applied the methods of [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858) ([nonstandard arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonstandard_arithmetic)) in number theory, joint with [Abraham Robinson](wikipedia://en/46024), with whom he worked on [Mahler's theorem](wikipedia://en/408111) (on the finiteness of integral points on a curve of genus *g* > 0) using [non-standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-standard_analysis) methods. He authored a number of works on the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), in particular on the schools of [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118) and [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896). In 1975, Roquette was co-editor of the collected essays by Helmut Hasse.

From 1978, Roquette was a member of the [Heidelberg Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_Academy_of_Sciences) and from 1985, the [German Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina). He has an honorary doctorate from the [University of Duisburg-Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Duisburg-Essen) and was an honorary member of the [Mathematical Society of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Society_of_Hamburg). In 1958, he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) (on the topic of "Some fundamental theorems on Abelian function fields").

His doctoral students include [Gerhard Frey](wikipedia://en/1021312) and Volker Weispfenning.

Roquette died in [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg) on 24 February 2023, at the age of 95.[^o3]
