# Computational statistics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Computational statistics"}

*Image: London School of Economics Statistics Machine Room 1964.jpg.* Students working in the Statistics [Machine Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_lab) of the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) in 1964

**Computational statistics**, or **statistical computing**, is the study which is the intersection of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), and refers to the statistical methods that are enabled by using computational methods. It is the area of [computational science](wikipedia://en/1181008) (or scientific computing) specific to the mathematical science of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685). This area is fast developing. The view that the broader concept of computing must be taught as part of general [statistical education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_education) is gaining momentum.[^o2]

:::

As in [traditional statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) the goal is to transform [raw data](wikipedia://en/6895400) into [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge),[^o3] but the focus lies on [computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer) intensive [statistical methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_methods), such as cases with very large [sample size](wikipedia://en/1776839) and non-homogeneous [data sets](wikipedia://en/8495).[^o3]

The terms 'computational statistics' and 'statistical computing' are often used interchangeably, although Carlo Lauro (a former president of the [International Association for Statistical Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_for_Statistical_Computing)) proposed making a distinction, defining 'statistical computing' as "the application of computer science to statistics", and 'computational statistics' as "aiming at the design of algorithm for implementing statistical methods on computers, including the ones unthinkable before the computer age (e.g. [bootstrap](wikipedia://en/6885770), [simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_simulation)), as well as to cope with analytically intractable problems" \[*[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic)*\].[^o4]

The term 'Computational statistics' may also be used to refer to computationally *intensive* statistical methods including [resampling](wikipedia://en/3763850) methods, [Markov chain Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/236801) methods, [local regression](wikipedia://en/4146592), [kernel density estimation](wikipedia://en/2090057), [artificial neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_neural_networks) and [generalized additive models](wikipedia://en/3608284).

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="History of computational statistics"}

## History {#History}

Though computational statistics is widely used today, it actually has a relatively short history of acceptance in the [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) community. For the most part, the founders of the field of statistics relied on mathematics and [asymptotic approximations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_approximation) in the development of computational statistical methodology.[^o7]

In 1908, [William Sealy Gosset](wikipedia://en/105651) performed his now well-known [Monte Carlo method simulation](wikipedia://en/56098) which led to the discovery of the [Student’s t-distribution](wikipedia://en/105375).[^o8] With the help of computational methods, he also has plots of the empirical distributions overlaid on the corresponding theoretical distributions. The computer has revolutionized simulation and has made the replication of Gosset’s experiment little more than an exercise.[^o9][^o10]

Later on, the scientists put forward computational ways of generating [pseudo-random](wikipedia://en/23210) deviates, performed methods to convert uniform deviates into other distributional forms using inverse [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) or acceptance-rejection methods, and developed state-space methodology for [Markov chain Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/236801).[^o11] One of the first efforts to generate random digits in a fully automated way, was undertaken by the RAND Corporation in 1947. The [tables](wikipedia://en/35391760) produced were published as a [book in 1955](wikipedia://en/1354812), and also as a series of punch cards.

By the mid-1950s, several articles and patents for devices had been proposed for [random number generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardware_random_number_generator).[^o12] The development of these devices were motivated from the need to use [random digits](wikipedia://en/19196523) to perform simulations and other fundamental components in statistical analysis. One of the most well known of such devices is ERNIE, which produces random numbers that determine the winners of the [Premium Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premium_Bond), a lottery bond issued in the United Kingdom. In 1958, [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974)’s [jackknife](wikipedia://en/9158134) was developed. It is as a method to reduce the [bias](wikipedia://en/40786) of parameter estimates in samples under nonstandard conditions.[^o13] This requires computers for practical implementations. To this point, computers have made many tedious statistical studies feasible.[^o14]

## Methods {#Methods}

### Maximum likelihood estimation {#Maximum_likelihood_estimation}

[Maximum likelihood estimation](wikipedia://en/140806) is used to [estimate](wikipedia://en/1565926) the [parameters](wikipedia://en/413291) of an assumed [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543), given some observed data. It is achieved by [maximizing](wikipedia://en/52033) a [likelihood function](wikipedia://en/44968) so that the [observed data](wikipedia://en/10354022) is most probable under the assumed [statistical model](wikipedia://en/27576).

### Monte Carlo method {#Monte_Carlo_method}

[Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo) is a statistical method that relies on repeated [random sampling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_sampling) to obtain numerical results. The concept is to use [randomness](wikipedia://en/19196523) to solve problems that might be [deterministic](wikipedia://en/522958) in principle. They are often used in [physical](wikipedia://en/22939) and [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) problems and are most useful when it is difficult to use other approaches. Monte Carlo methods are mainly used in three problem classes: [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization), [numerical integration](wikipedia://en/170089), and generating draws from a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543).

### Markov chain Monte Carlo {#Markov_chain_Monte_Carlo}

The [Markov chain Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/236801) method creates samples from a continuous [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685), with [probability density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_density) proportional to a known function. These samples can be used to evaluate an integral over that variable, such as its [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) or [variance](wikipedia://en/32344). The more steps are included, the more closely the distribution of the sample matches the actual desired distribution.

### Bootstrapping {#Bootstrapping}

The [bootstrap](wikipedia://en/6885770) is a resampling technique used to generate samples from an [empirical probability distribution](wikipedia://en/2690455) defined by an original sample of the population. It can be used to find a bootstrapped estimator of a population parameter. It can also be used to estimate the standard error of an estimator as well as to generate bootstrapped confidence intervals. The [jackknife](wikipedia://en/9158134) is a related technique.[^o21]
