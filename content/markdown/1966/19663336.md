# Robert Osserman

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Osserman"}

**Robert "Bob" Osserman** (December 19, 1926 – November 30, 2011) was an American mathematician who worked in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). He is specially remembered for his work on the theory of [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734).[^o5] There are many mathematical concepts named after him.

:::

Raised in [Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx), he went to [Bronx High School of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_High_School_of_Science) (diploma, 1942) and [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University). He earned a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1955 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) with the thesis *Contributions to the Problem of Type* (on [Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surfaces)) supervised by [Lars Ahlfors](wikipedia://en/61636).

He joined [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1955.[^o7] He joined the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in 1990.[^o8] He worked on [geometric function theory](wikipedia://en/5458932), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the two integrated in a theory of [minimal surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_surfaces), [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182), and other issues in the areas of [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), geometry, [cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography) and [complex function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_function) theory.

Osserman was the head of mathematics at [Office of Naval Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Naval_Research), a [Fulbright Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Lecturer) at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) and [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow) at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick). He edited numerous books and promoted mathematics, such as in interviews with celebrities [Steve Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Martin)[^o9][^o10] and [Alan Alda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Alda).[^o11]

He was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) (ICM) of 1978 in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki).[^o12]

He received the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) (1980) of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772)[^o13] for his popular science writings.

[H. Blaine Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Blaine_Lawson), [David Allen Hoffman](wikipedia://en/63668906) and [Michael Gage](wikipedia://en/48573827) were Ph.D. students of his.[^o6]

Robert Osserman died on Wednesday, November 30, 2011 at his home.[^o7]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

### Non-existence for the minimal surface system in higher codimension {#Non-existence_for_the_minimal_surface_system_in_higher_codimension}

In collaboration with his former student [H. Blaine Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Blaine_Lawson), Osserman studied the [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734) problem in the case that the codimension is larger than one. They considered the case of a graphical minimal submanifold of euclidean space. Their conclusion was that most of the analytical properties which hold in the codimension-one case fail to extend. Solutions to the boundary value problem may exist and fail to be unique, or in other situations may simply fail to exist. Such submanifolds (given as graphs) might not solve the [Plateau problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau_problem), as they must in the case of graphical [hypersurfaces](wikipedia://en/525101) of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697).

Their results pointed to the analytical difficulty of general elliptic systems and of the minimal submanifold problem in particular. Many of these issues are still not understood, despite their significance in the theory of [calibrated geometry](wikipedia://en/19364294) and the [Strominger–Yau–Zaslow conjecture](wikipedia://en/17511256).[^o16][^o17]
