# Statistical relational learning

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Statistical relational learning"}

**Statistical relational learning** (**SRL**) is a subdiscipline of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) and [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) that is concerned with [domain models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_model) that exhibit both [uncertainty](wikipedia://en/63778) (which can be dealt with using statistical methods) and complex, [relational](wikipedia://en/22517558) structure.[^o8][^o9] Typically, the [knowledge representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_representation) formalisms developed in SRL use (a subset of) [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983) to describe relational properties of a domain in a general manner ([universal quantification](wikipedia://en/76174)) and draw upon [probabilistic graphical models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_graphical_model) (such as [Bayesian networks](wikipedia://en/203996) or [Markov networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_network)) to model the uncertainty; some also build upon the methods of [inductive logic programming](wikipedia://en/54069). Significant contributions to the field have been made since the late 1990s.[^o8]

:::

As is evident from the characterization above, the field is not strictly limited to learning aspects; it is equally concerned with [reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_reasoner) (specifically [probabilistic inference](wikipedia://en/27577)) and [knowledge representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_representation). Therefore, alternative terms that reflect the main foci of the field include *statistical relational learning and reasoning* (emphasizing the importance of reasoning) and *first-order probabilistic languages* (emphasizing the key properties of the languages with which models are represented). Another term that is sometimes used in the literature is *relational machine learning* (RML).

## Canonical tasks {#Canonical_tasks}

A number of canonical tasks are associated with statistical relational learning, the most common ones being.[^o10]

- [collective classification](wikipedia://en/64671582), i.e. the (simultaneous) [prediction of the class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_%28machine_learning%29) of several objects given objects' attributes and their relations
- [link prediction](wikipedia://en/64507625), i.e. predicting whether or not two or more objects are related
- [link-based clustering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link-based_clustering), i.e. the [grouping](wikipedia://en/669675) of similar objects, where similarity is determined according to the links of an object, and the related task of [collaborative filtering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaborative_filtering), i.e. the filtering for information that is relevant to an entity (where a piece of information is considered relevant to an entity if it is known to be relevant to a similar entity)
- [social network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_network) modelling
- [object identification/entity resolution/record linkage](wikipedia://en/978951), i.e. the identification of equivalent entries in two or more separate databases/datasets

## Representation formalisms {#Representation_formalisms}

One of the fundamental design goals of the representation formalisms developed in SRL is to abstract away from concrete entities and to represent instead general principles that are intended to be universally applicable. Since there are countless ways in which such principles can be represented, many representation formalisms have been proposed in recent years.[^o8] In the following, some of the more common ones are listed in alphabetical order:

- [Bayesian logic program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_logic_program)
- [BLOG model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLOG_model)
- [Markov logic networks](wikipedia://en/3670357)
- [Multi-entity Bayesian network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-entity_Bayesian_network)
- [Probabilistic logic programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_logic_program)
- Probabilistic relational model – a Probabilistic Relational Model (PRM) is the counterpart of a [Bayesian network](wikipedia://en/203996) in statistical relational learning.[^o11][^o12]
- [Probabilistic soft logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_soft_logic)
- [Recursive random field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursive_random_field)
- [Relational Bayesian network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relational_Bayesian_network)
- [Relational dependency network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relational_dependency_network)
- [Relational Markov network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relational_Markov_network)
- [Relational Kalman filtering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relational_Kalman_filtering)
