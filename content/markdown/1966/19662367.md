# Henry O. Pollak

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Henry O. Pollak"}

**Henry Otto Pollak** (December 13, 1927 – July 8, 2026) was an Austrian-American mathematician who has made significant contributions to [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), [signal analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_analysis), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), and [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927).[^o18]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Born in [Vienna, Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna,_Austria), the only child of a lawyer, Pollak fled the Nazis with his family in 1939, first to England and then in 1940 to the US.[^o4] He received his [BS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) (1947) from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). While at Yale, he participated in the William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition and was on the team representing Yale University (along with [Murray Gell-Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Gell-Mann) and [Murray Gerstenhaber](wikipedia://en/30237327)) that won the second prize in 1947.[^o5] He earned an [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) (1951) degree in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University),[^o15] the latter on the thesis *Some Estimates for Extremal Distance* advised by [Lars Ahlfors](wikipedia://en/61636).

Pollak then joined [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) (1951),[^o15] where he later became director of the Mathematics and Statistics Research Center. He has held teaching positions in the mathematics department at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).[^o15]

Pollak died on July 8, 2026, at the age of 98.[^o7]

## Research {#Research}

In several papers with [David Slepian](wikipedia://en/1706809) and [Henry Landau](wikipedia://en/19646350), Pollak developed the theory of what are now known as the Landau–Pollak–Slepian operators on simultaneously time-limited and band-limited functions in [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675). This work marked an early form of [wavelet](wikipedia://en/50903)-based [signal analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_analysis).[^o9]

With [Ronald Graham](wikipedia://en/240694) he is the namesake of the [Graham–Pollak theorem](wikipedia://en/64527577) in graph theory, a result on partitioning the edges of [complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816) into [complete bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/525320) that they published in the early 1970s.[^o10]

With [Edgar Gilbert](wikipedia://en/30980291) he is the namesake of the [Gilbert–Pollak conjecture](wikipedia://en/72307852) relating [Steiner trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steiner_tree) to [Euclidean minimum spanning trees](wikipedia://en/1040597) in computational geometry. After they formulated this problem in 1968, it was believed to be proven by Du and Hwang in the early 1990s, but the proof was later determined to be flawed and the problem remains open.[^o11]

## Awards {#Awards}

- Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) (1971)[^o13]
- [Earle Raymond Hedrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earle_Raymond_Hedrick) lecturer (1973)[^o14]
- [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) chair of New Jersey section (1958–59), governor (1961–63) and president (1975–76).[^o15]
- [Honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate) from [Bowdoin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowdoin_College) (1977)[^o16]
- Honorary doctorate from [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology) (1981)[^o17]
- [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA) Meritorious Service Award (1990)[^o15]
- MAA Gung and Hu Distinguished Service to Mathematics Award (1993)[^o15]
- [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1561691) Lifetime Achievement Award (2010)[^o15]
- [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) [Mary P. Dolciani Award](wikipedia://en/65434570) in 2020.[^o18]
