# Commutator collecting process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Commutator collecting process"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **commutator collecting process** is a method for writing an element of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) as a product of generators and their higher [commutators](wikipedia://en/7193) arranged in a certain order. The commutator collecting process was introduced by [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033) in 1934[^o2] and articulated by [Wilhelm Magnus](wikipedia://en/8337666) in 1937.[^o3] The process is sometimes called a "collection process".

:::

The process can be generalized to define a [totally ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered) subset of a free non-associative algebra, that is, a [free magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_magma); this subset is called the [Hall set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_set). Members of the Hall set are [binary trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_trees); these can be placed in one-to-one correspondence with words, these being called the [Hall words](wikipedia://en/31782613); the [Lyndon words](wikipedia://en/3919355) are a special case. Hall sets are used to construct a basis for a [free Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/3174646), entirely analogously to the commutator collecting process. Hall words also provide a unique [factorization of monoids](wikipedia://en/37206260).

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The commutator collecting process is usually stated for [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735), as a similar theorem then holds for any group by writing it as a [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) of a free group.

Suppose *F*<sub>1</sub> is a free group on generators *a*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *a*<sub>*m*</sub>. Define the descending [central series](wikipedia://en/15202655) by putting

*F*<sub>*n*+1</sub> = \[*F*<sub>*n*</sub>, *F*<sub>1</sub>\]

The basic commutators are elements of *F*<sub>1</sub> defined and ordered as follows:

- The basic commutators of weight 1 are the [generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_generators) *a*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *a*<sub>*m*</sub>.
- The basic commutators of weight *w* > 1 are the elements \[*x*, *y*\] where *x* and *y* are basic commutators whose weights sum to *w*, such that *x* > *y* and if *x* = \[*u*, *v*\] for basic commutators *u* and *v* then *v* ≤ *y*.

Commutators are ordered so that *x* > *y* if *x* has weight greater than that of *y*, and for commutators of any fixed weight some total ordering is chosen.

Then *F*<sub>*n*</sub> / *F*<sub>*n*+1</sub> is a [finitely generated free abelian group](wikipedia://en/58527) with a basis consisting of basic commutators of weight *n*.

Then any element of *F* can be written as

$$g=c_1^{n_1}c_2^{n_2}\cdots c_k^{n_k}c$$

where the *c*<sub>*i*</sub> are the basic commutators of weight at most *m* arranged in order, and *c* is a product of commutators of weight greater than *m*, and the *n*<sub>*i*</sub> are [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).

:::
