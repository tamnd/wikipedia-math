# George Seligman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="George Seligman"}

**George Benham Seligman** (April 30, 1927 – April 25, 2024) was an American mathematician who worked on [Lie algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebras), especially semi-simple Lie algebras.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Seligman was born on April 30, 1927.[^o4] He received his bachelor's degree in 1950 from the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester) and his PhD in 1954 from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) under [Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson) with thesis *Lie algebras of prime characteristic*. After he received his PhD he was a Henry Burchard Fine Instructor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1954–1956. In 1956 he became an instructor and from 1965 a full professor at Yale, where he was chair of the mathematics department from 1974 to 1977.

For the academic year 1958/59 he was a Fulbright Lecturer at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster). His doctoral students include [James E. Humphreys](wikipedia://en/45195882), [Brian J Parshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_J_Parshall), and [Daniel K. Nakano](wikipedia://en/45200100).

Seligman married Irene Schwieder in 1959. The couple had two daughters. He died in [Hamden, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamden,_Connecticut), on April 25, 2024, at the age of 96.[^o6]
