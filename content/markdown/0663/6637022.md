# Primitive recursive arithmetic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Primitive recursive arithmetic"}

**Primitive recursive arithmetic** (**PRA**) is a [quantifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantification_%28logic%29)-free formalization of the [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers). It was first proposed by Norwegian mathematician (Skolem 1923),[^o3] as a formalization of his [finitistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitist) conception of the [foundations of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/169358), and it is widely agreed that all reasoning of PRA is finitistic. Many also believe that all of finitism is captured by PRA,[^n1] but others believe finitism can be extended to forms of recursion beyond primitive recursion, up to [ε<sub>0</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epsilon_zero_%28mathematics%29),[^n2] which is the [proof-theoretic ordinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof-theoretic_ordinal) of [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic).[^o6] PRA's proof theoretic ordinal is ω<sup>ω</sup>, where ω is the smallest [transfinite ordinal](wikipedia://en/189734). PRA is sometimes called *Skolem arithmetic*, although that has another meaning, see [Skolem arithmetic](wikipedia://en/6694711).

:::

The language of PRA can express arithmetic propositions involving [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) and any [primitive recursive function](wikipedia://en/24829), including the operations of [addition](wikipedia://en/61338), [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845), and [exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491). PRA cannot explicitly quantify over the domain of natural numbers. PRA is often taken as the basic [metamathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamathematic) [formal system](wikipedia://en/396102) for [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), in particular for [consistency proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistency_proof) such as [Gentzen's consistency proof](wikipedia://en/4076831) of [first-order arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_arithmetic).

## Language and axioms {#Language_and_axioms}

The language of PRA consists of:

- A [countably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite) number of variables *x*, *y*, *z*,....
- The [propositional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus) [connectives](wikipedia://en/18153);
- The equality symbol *\=*, the constant symbol 0, and the [successor](wikipedia://en/24829) symbol *S* (meaning *add one*);
- A symbol for each [primitive recursive function](wikipedia://en/24829).

The logical axioms of PRA are the:

- [Tautologies](wikipedia://en/4495335) of the [propositional calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus);
- Usual axiomatization of [equality](wikipedia://en/90446) as an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259).

The logical rules of PRA are [modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900) and [variable substitution](wikipedia://en/10983#Rules_of_inference). The non-logical axioms are, firstly:

- $$S(x) \neq 0;$$
- $$S(x)=S(y) \to x = y,$$

where $x \neq y$ always denotes the negation of $x = y$ so that, for example, $S(0) = 0$ is a negated proposition.

Further, recursive defining equations for every [primitive recursive function](wikipedia://en/24829) may be adopted as axioms as desired. For instance, the most common characterization of the primitive recursive functions is as the 0 constant and successor function closed under projection, composition and primitive recursion. So for a (*n*+1)-place function *f* defined by primitive recursion over a *n*-place base function *g* and (*n*+2)-place iteration function *h* there would be the defining equations:

- $$f(0,y_1,\ldots,y_n) = g(y_1,\ldots,y_n)$$
- $$f(S(x),y_1,\ldots,y_n) = h(x,f(x,y_1,\ldots,y_n),y_1,\ldots,y_n)$$

Especially:

- $$x+0 = x\$$
- $$x+S(y) = S(x+y)\$$
- $$x \cdot 0 = 0\$$
- $$x \cdot S(y) = x \cdot y + x\$$
- ... and so on.

PRA replaces the [axiom schema of induction](wikipedia://en/18881) for [first-order arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_arithmetic) with the rule of (quantifier-free) induction:

- From $\varphi(0)$ and $\varphi(x)\to\varphi(S(x))$, deduce $\varphi(y)$, for any predicate $\varphi.$

In [first-order arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_arithmetic), the only [primitive recursive functions](wikipedia://en/24829) that need to be explicitly axiomatized are [addition](wikipedia://en/61338) and [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845). All other primitive recursive predicates can be defined using these two primitive recursive functions and [quantification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantification_%28logic%29) over all [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers). Defining [primitive recursive functions](wikipedia://en/24829) in this manner is not possible in PRA, because it lacks quantifiers.

:::

## Logic-free calculus {#Logic-free_calculus}

It is possible to formalise PRA in such a way that it has no logical connectives at all—a sentence of PRA is just an equation between two terms. In this setting a term is a primitive recursive function of zero or more variables. (Curry 1941) gave the first such system. The rule of induction in Curry's system was unusual. A later refinement was given by (Goodstein 1954). The [rule](wikipedia://en/252311) of induction in Goodstein's system is:

$${F(0) = G(0) \quad F(S(x)) = H(x,F(x)) \quad G(S(x)) = H(x,G(x)) \over F(x) = G(x)}.$$

Here *x* is a variable, *S* is the successor operation, and *F*, *G*, and *H* are any primitive recursive functions which may have parameters other than the ones shown. The only other [inference rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inference_rule) of Goodstein's system are substitution rules, as follows:

$${F(x) = G(x) \over F(A) = G(A)} \qquad {A = B \over F(A) = F(B)} \qquad {A = B \quad A = C \over B = C}.$$

Here *A*, *B*, and *C* are any terms (primitive recursive functions of zero or more variables). Finally, there are symbols for any primitive recursive functions with corresponding defining equations, as in Skolem's system above.

In this way the propositional calculus can be discarded entirely. Logical operators can be expressed entirely arithmetically, for instance, the absolute value of the difference of two numbers can be defined by primitive recursion:

$$\begin{aligned} P(0) = 0 \quad & \quad P(S(x)) = x \\ x \dot - 0 = x \quad & \quad x \mathrel{\dot -} S(y) = P(x \mathrel{\dot -} y) \\ |x - y| = & (x \mathrel{\dot -} y) + (y \mathrel{\dot -} x). \\ \end{aligned}$$

Thus, the equations *x*=*y* and $|x - y| = 0$ are equivalent. Therefore, the equations $|x - y| + |u - v| = 0$ and $|x - y| \cdot |u - v| = 0$ express the logical [conjunction](wikipedia://en/18152) and [disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disjunction), respectively, of the equations *x*=*y* and *u*=*v*. [Negation](wikipedia://en/161019) can be expressed as $1 \dot - |x - y| = 0$.
