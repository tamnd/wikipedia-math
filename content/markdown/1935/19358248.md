# Quaternionic vector space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quaternionic vector space"}

In [noncommutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_algebra), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **quaternionic vector space** is a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) over the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440). Since the quaternion algebra is [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067), these modules are referred to as "vector spaces". However, the quaternion algebra is [noncommutative](wikipedia://en/24062516) so we must distinguish left and right vector spaces. In left vector spaces, linear compositions of vectors $v$ and $w$ have the form $av+bw$ where $a$, $b\in H$. In right vector spaces, linear compositions of vectors $v$ and $w$ have the form $va+wb$.

:::

Similar to [vector spaces over a field](wikipedia://en/32370), if a quaternionic vector space has finite dimension $n$, then it is isomorphic to the direct sum $H^n$ of $n$ copies of the quaternion algebra $H$. In this case we can use a standard basis which has the form

$$e_1=(1,0,\ldots,0)$$

$$\ldots$$

$$e_n=(0,\ldots,0,1)$$

In a left quaternionic vector space $H^n$ we use componentwise sum of vectors and product of vectors over scalars

$$(p_1, \ldots, p_n)+(r_1, \ldots, r_n) = (p_1+ r_1, \ldots, p_n+ r_n)$$

$$q (r_1, \ldots, r_n) = (q r_1, \ldots, q r_n)$$

In a right quaternionic vector space $H^n$ we also use componentwise sum of vectors and product of vectors over scalars

$$(p_1, \ldots, p_n)+(r_1, \ldots, r_n) = (p_1+ r_1, \ldots, p_n+ r_n)$$

$$(r_1, \ldots, r_n)q = ( r_1q, \ldots, r_nq)$$
