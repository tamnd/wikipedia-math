# Senior Wrangler

*Image: Senior Wrangler 1842.jpg.* 1842 in the [Senate House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_House_%28University_of_Cambridge%29), Cambridge: the Senior Wrangler, achiever of "academic supremacy" (here, [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034)), is admitted to his degree as the top scorer in the university's final-year examinations in mathematics.

*Image: 2013 Mathematical Tripos Results at Cambridge University.ogg.* 2013 in the same room: the examiner announces the results of the same examinations. In keeping with recent tradition, he raises his [academic cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_cap) to identify the Senior Wrangler (here, [Arran Fernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arran_Fernandez)); at the end he follows the older tradition of throwing printed notices of the results from the balcony.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Senior Wrangler"}

The **Senior Wrangler** is the top mathematics undergraduate at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in England, a position which has been described as "the greatest intellectual achievement attainable in Britain".[^o2]

:::

Specifically, it is the person who achieves the highest overall mark among the [Wranglers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) – the students at Cambridge who gain [first-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_undergraduate_degree_classification) degrees in mathematics. The Cambridge undergraduate mathematics course, or [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos), is famously difficult.

Many Senior Wranglers went on to become prominent figures in mathematics and other sciences. They include astronomers [Sir John Herschel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Herschel) and [Sir George Biddell Airy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Biddell_Airy); physicists [Sir Gilbert Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Walker_%28physicist%29), [Sir George Stokes](wikipedia://en/212838) and [Lord Rayleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_William_Strutt,_3rd_Baron_Rayleigh); and pure mathematicians such as [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034), [Ben Green](wikipedia://en/1927952) and [John Edensor Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371).

Senior Wranglers were once fêted with torchlit processions and took pride of place in the university's graduation ceremony.[^o3] Years in Cambridge were often remembered by who had been Senior Wrangler that year.[^o2]

The annual ceremony in which the Senior Wrangler becomes known was first held in the 18th century. Standing on the balcony of the university's [Senate House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_House_%28University_of_Cambridge%29), the examiner reads out the [class results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_undergraduate_degree_classification) for mathematics,[^o4] and printed copies of the results are then thrown to the audience below. The examiner no longer announces the students' exact rankings, but they still identify the Senior Wrangler, nowadays tipping their [academic hat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_academic_cap) when reading out the person's name.

## Others who finished in the top 12 {#Others_who_finished_in_the_top_12}

Those who have achieved second place, known as Second Wranglers, include [Alfred Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Marshall), [James Clerk Maxwell](wikipedia://en/28989696), [J. J. Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Thomson), [Lord Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kelvin), [William Clifford](wikipedia://en/250820), and [William Whewell](wikipedia://en/205488).

Those who have finished between third and 12th include [Archibald Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Hill), [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695) and [William Henry Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Bragg) (third), [George Green](wikipedia://en/243343), [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599), and [Alfred North Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395) (fourth), [Adam Sedgwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Sedgwick) (fifth), [John Venn](wikipedia://en/251482) (sixth), [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163), [Nevil Maskelyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevil_Maskelyne) and [Sir James Timmins Chance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Timmins_Chance) (seventh), [Thomas Malthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Robert_Malthus) (ninth), and [John Maynard Keynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Keynes) and [William Henry Fox Talbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Fox_Talbot) (12th).

## History {#History}

Between 1748 and 1909 the university publicly announced the ranking,[^o8] which was then reported in newspapers such as *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)*. The examination was considered to be by far the most important in Britain and the Empire. The prestige of being a high Wrangler was great; the respect accorded to the Senior Wrangler was immense. Andrew Warwick, author of *Masters of Theory*, describes the term 'Senior Wrangler' as "synonymous with academic supremacy".[^o9]

Since 1910 successful students in the examinations have been told their rankings privately, and not all Senior Wranglers have become publicly known as such. In recent years, the custom of discretion regarding ranking has progressively vanished, and all Senior Wranglers since 2010 have announced their identity publicly.

The youngest ever Senior Wrangler was [Arran Fernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arran_Fernandez) in 2013, aged 18 years and 6 days.[^o10] The previous youngest was probably [James Wilkinson](wikipedia://en/301380) in 1939, aged 19 years and nine months.[^o11] The youngest up to 1909 were [Alfred Flux](wikipedia://en/21633880) in 1887, aged 20 years and two months[^o12] and [Peter Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait) in 1852, aged 20 years and eight months.[^o13]

Two individuals have placed first without becoming known as Senior Wrangler. One was the student [Philippa Fawcett](wikipedia://en/639426) in 1890.[^o14][^o15] (She could not receive a degree from Cambridge due to being a woman, and so she could not be the senior wrangler.[^o16][^o17] Cambridge did not offer degrees to women until 1948,[^o17] and no woman became the senior wrangler until Ruth Hendry in 1992.[^o18]) The other was the mathematics professor [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya). As he had contributed to reforming the Tripos with the aim that an excellent performance would be less dependent on solving hard problems and more so on showing a broad mathematical understanding and knowledge, [G.H. Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.H._Hardy) asked Pólya to sit the examinations himself, unofficially, during his stay in England in 1924–5. Pólya did so, and to Hardy's surprise, received the highest mark, an achievement which, had he been a student, would have made him the Senior Wrangler.[^o19]

## Derived uses of the term {#Derived_uses_of_the_term}

Senior Wrangler's Walk is a path in Cambridge, the walk to and along which was considered to be sufficient [constitutional exercise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walking) for a student aspiring to become the Senior Wrangler. The route was shorter than other walks, such as Wranglers' Walk and the [Grantchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grantchester) Grind, undertaken by undergraduates whose aspirations were lower.[^o21]

Senior Wrangler sauce is a Cambridge term for [brandy butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandy_butter), a type of [hard sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_sauce) made from [brandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandy), butter, and sugar, traditionally served in Britain with [Christmas pudding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_pudding) and warm [mince pies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mince_pie).[^o22]

Senior Wrangler is also the name of a [solitaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitaire_%28card_game%29) card game, alternatively known as Mathematics and Double Calculation, played with two [decks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_52-card_deck) of [cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playing_cards) and involving elementary [modular arithmetic](wikipedia://en/20087).[^o23][^o24]

## Literary references {#Literary_references}

Fictional Senior Wranglers appearing in novels include Roger Hamley, a character in [Elizabeth Gaskell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Gaskell)'s *Wives and Daughters*, and Tom Jericho, the [cryptanalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalyst) in [Robert Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Harris_%28novelist%29)'s novel *[Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_%28novel%29)*, who is described as having been Senior Wrangler in 1938. In [Catherine Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Hall_%28novelist%29)'s *The Proof of Love*, Victor Turner is listed as having been Senior Wrangler in 1968.

In [George Bernard Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bernard_Shaw)'s play *[Mrs. Warren's Profession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs._Warren's_Profession)*, the title character's daughter Vivie is praised for "tieing with the third wrangler," and she comments that "the mathematical tripos" means "grind, grind, grind for six to eight hours a day at mathematics, and nothing but mathematics."

In [Ford Madox Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Madox_Ford)'s *[Parade's End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade's_End)*, the character [Christopher Tietjens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Tietjens) is described as having settled deliberately for only being Second Wrangler, to avoid the weight of expectation that the title would create.

In his *[Discworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discworld)* series of novels, [Terry Pratchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Pratchett) has a character called the Senior Wrangler, a [faculty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_%28teaching_staff%29) member at the [Unseen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unseen_University), whose first name is Horace.

The compiler of crosswords for *The Leader* in the 1930s used "Senior Wrangler" as a pseudonym.[^o26]

## Coaches {#Coaches}

The two most successful 19th-century coaches of Senior Wranglers were [William Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hopkins) and [Edward Routh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Routh). Hopkins, the 'Senior Wrangler Maker', who himself was the 7th Wrangler, coached 17 Senior Wranglers. Routh, who had himself been the Senior Wrangler, coached 27.[^o28] Another, described by his student (and Senior Wrangler) [J.E. Littlewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.E._Littlewood) as "the last of the great coaches", was another Senior Wrangler, [Robert Alfred Herman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Alfred_Herman).[^o29]
