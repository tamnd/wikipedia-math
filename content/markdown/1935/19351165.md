# Timeline of category theory and related mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Timeline of category theory and related mathematics"}

This is a **timeline of category theory and related mathematics**. Its scope ("related mathematics") is taken as:

:::

- [Categories](wikipedia://en/53759) of [abstract algebraic](wikipedia://en/19616384) structures including [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) and [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200);
- [Homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410);
- [Homotopical algebra](wikipedia://en/1321050);
- [Topology](wikipedia://en/29954) using categories, including [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), [categorical topology](wikipedia://en/82898265), [quantum topology](wikipedia://en/28075395), [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774);
- [Categorical logic](wikipedia://en/1063799) and [set theory](wikipedia://en/3921716) in the categorical context such as [algebraic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_set_theory);
- [Foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358) building on categories, for instance [topos theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topos_theory);
- [Abstract geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_geometry), including [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [categorical noncommutative geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_noncommutative_geometry), etc.
- Quantization related to category theory, in particular [categorical quantization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_quantization);
- [Categorical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_physics) relevant for mathematics.

In this article, and in category theory in general, ∞ = *ω*.

| 1951 | [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536) | Modern definition of [sheaf theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_theory) in which a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) is defined using [open subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_subset) instead of [closed subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_subset) of a topological space and all the open subsets are treated at once. A sheaf on a topological space *X* becomes a functor resembling a function defined locally on *X*, and taking values in sets, abelian groups, [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346), modules or generally in any category $C$. In fact [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) later made a [dictionary between sheaves and functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictionary_between_sheaves_and_functions). Another interpretation of sheaves is as continuously [varying sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varying_set) (a generalization of [abstract sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_set)). Its purpose is to provide a unified approach to connect local and global properties of topological spaces and to classify the obstructions for passing from local objects to global objects on a topological space by pasting together the local pieces. The $C$-valued sheaves on a topological space and their homomorphisms form a category. |

There are several object in a monoidal category since the relabelling trick makes 2-morphisms of the 2-category to morphisms, morphisms of the 2-category to objects and forgets about the single object. In general a higher relabelling trick works for [**n**-categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-category) with one object to make general monoidal categories. The most common examples include: [ribbon categories](wikipedia://en/39576395), [braided tensor categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braided_tensor_category), [spherical categories](wikipedia://en/41038739), [compact closed categories](wikipedia://en/5557623), [symmetric tensor categories](wikipedia://en/3865249), [modular categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_category), [autonomous categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_category), [categories with duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_with_duality) |
