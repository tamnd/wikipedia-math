# Intersecting chords theorem

*Image: Chord theorem.svg.* $|AS|\cdot|SC|=|BS|\cdot|SD|$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Intersecting chords theorem"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **intersecting chords theorem**, or just the **chord theorem**, is a statement that describes a relation of the four [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) created by two intersecting [chords](wikipedia://en/297947) within a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220). It states that the [products](wikipedia://en/24977) of the lengths of the line segments on each chord are equal. It is Proposition 35 of Book 3 of [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s [*Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107).

:::

For example, for two chords $AC$ and $BD$ intersecting at point $S$, the following equation holds:

$$|AS|\cdot|SC|=|BS|\cdot|SD|$$

::: {#o3 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The theorem can be proven by [triangle similarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Similar_triangles) and the [inscribed angle theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscribed_angle_theorem).

*Image: Chord theorem proof.svg.* $\triangle ASD \sim \triangle BSC$

By the inscribed angle theorem, $\angle BCA=\angle ADB$ and $\angle DAC=\angle CBD$, as each pair [intercepts](wikipedia://en/1999527) a common chord. [Opposite angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposite_angle) at the point $S$ are also congruent, $\angle ASD=\angle BSC$. Therefore $\triangle ASD$ and $\triangle BSC$ are similar triangles, with congruent corresponding angles.

The sides of one triangle are uniformly proportional to the corresponding sides of any similar triangle, so

$$\frac{|AS|}{|BS|}=\frac{|SD|}{|SC|},$$

or, equivalently,

$$|AS|\cdot|SC|=|BS|\cdot|SD|.$$

:::

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

The converse is true as well. That is: If for two line segments $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BD}$ intersecting in $S$ the [equation](wikipedia://en/9284) above holds true, then their four endpoints $A, B, C, D$ lie on a common circle. Or in other words, if the [diagonals](wikipedia://en/349251) of a [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) $ABCD$ intersect in $S$ and fulfill the equation above, then it is a [cyclic quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/194131).

*Image: Chord theorem power.svg.* $\begin{aligned} & |AS|\cdot|SC| = |BS|\cdot|SD| \\ ={}& (r+d)\cdot(r-d) = r^2-d^2 \end{aligned}$

By applying the **chord theorem** to a third chord (a diameter) going through the circle's center $M$ and point $S$, the following relationships can be made:

$$|AS|\cdot|SC| = |BS|\cdot|SD| = r^2-d^2,$$

where $r$ is the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of the circle and $d$ is the distance between the circle's center $M$ and point $S$.

Next to the [tangent-secant theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent-secant_theorem) and the [intersecting secants theorem](wikipedia://en/19359084), the intersecting chords theorem represents one of the three basic cases of a more general theorem about two intersecting lines and a circle: the [power of a point](wikipedia://en/2094044#Theorems) theorem. The distance $d$ from the circle's center $M$ to the [intersection point](wikipedia://en/8240558) $S$ is the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) of the power of $S$ with respect to the circle.
