# Mike Keith (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mike Keith"}

**Michael Keith** (born 1955) is an American mathematician, software engineer, and author of works of [constrained writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constrained_writing).

:::

Keith was employed at [Sarnoff Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnoff_Corporation) from 1980 until 1990 and [Intel Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Corporation) from 1990 to 1998, both tenures involving work in multimedia software. He was part of the original team at Sarnoff that developed [Digital Video Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Video_Interactive), the first PC digital video system, and at Intel he was a member of the group that developed [Indeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeo), another video compression standard. As a result of this work Keith is credited as [inventor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventor) or co-inventor on 60 [US patents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_patent).[^o2] he works as a software designer, developer, and tester.[^o3]

Keith was the first to describe [primeval numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primeval_number) and [Keith numbers](wikipedia://en/485251). His self-published book *From Polychords to Pólya: Adventures in Musical Combinatorics* is about the application of the [Pólya enumeration theorem](wikipedia://en/4396962) to the counting and classification of musical constructs such as chords, scales, and rhythms.

Keith has written several long works of [constrained writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constrained_writing), such as *[Cadaeic Cadenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadaeic_Cadenza)*, a story in which the number of letters in successive words spells out the first 3835 digits of the number [pi](wikipedia://en/23601);[^o4] the book *Not A Wake: A Dream Embodying π's Digits Fully for 10000 Decimals*, which similarly encodes the first 10,000 digits of pi with texts composed in various literary styles;[^o3][^o5] and the book *The Anagrammed Bible: Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Solomon*, co-written with [Richard Brodie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Brodie_%28programmer%29), in which the roughly 95,000 letters of the original text are rearranged into a modern paraphrase. His constrained writing and articles on logology were a regular feature of *[Word Ways: The Journal of Recreational Linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_Ways:_The_Journal_of_Recreational_Linguistics)*.[^o6]
