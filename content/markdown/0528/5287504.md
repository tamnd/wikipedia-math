# Fulton–Hansen connectedness theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fulton–Hansen connectedness theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Fulton–Hansen connectedness theorem** is a result from [intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602) in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), for the case of [subvarieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subvariety_%28algebraic_geometry%29) of [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) with [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149) large enough to make the intersection have components of dimension at least 1. It is named after [William Fulton](wikipedia://en/9642149) and Johan Hansen, who proved it in 1979.

:::

The formal statement is that if *V* and *W* are irreducible algebraic subvarieties of a [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) *P*, all over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018), and if

$$\dim(V) + \dim (W) > \dim (P)$$

in terms of the [dimension of an algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/253251), then the intersection *U* of *V* and *W* is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233).

More generally, the theorem states that if $Z$ is a projective variety and $f\colon Z \to P^n \times P^n$ is any morphism such that $\dim f(Z) > n$, then $f^{-1}\Delta$ is connected, where $\Delta$ is the [diagonal](wikipedia://en/349251) in $P^n \times P^n$. The special case of intersections is recovered by taking $Z = V \times W$, with $f$ the natural inclusion.
