# Thomas Hood (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Hood"}

**Thomas Hood** (1556 – 1620) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician), the first lecturer in mathematics appointed in England, a few years before the founding of [Gresham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham_College). He publicized the [Copernican theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernican_theory), and discussed the nova [SN 1572](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SN_1572).[^o2] (Tycho's Nova). He also innovated in the design of mathematical and astronomical instruments.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He entered [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in 1573, and graduated B.A. in 1578; he was elected to a fellowship in the same year, and graduated M.A. in 1581. His Cambridge licence to practice as a physician was from 1585. He was approached to lecture in mathematics in 1582, by the merchant [Thomas Smythe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Smythe). The lectures in fact began in 1588.[^o5][^o6]

He lectured from 1588 to 1592. The applications in view were military (intended for Captains of train bands, in other words for militia commanders at the time of the [Spanish Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Armada)), and subsequently aimed at naval needs and navigation. The first lectures were in the Staples Inn Chapel, but the regular venue became Smythe's London house, [Leadenhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadenhall) in Gracechurch Street. Other supporters of the lectures were [Sir John Wolstenholme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wolstenholme_%28merchant%29) and [John Lumley, 1st Baron Lumley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lumley,_1st_Baron_Lumley); Hood was a subscriber in 1589 to the [Virginia Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Company), with which his merchant backers were associated. Hood's original publications were probably derived from notes of the talks. He collaborated with the engraver [Augustine Ryther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine_Ryther) on both celestial and terrestrial charts.[^o5][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10]

In later life he lived in [Abchurch Lane, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlewick_%28ward%29), practiced as a physician, and sold copies of his hemisphere charts.[^o11]

## Works {#Works}

- *A Copie of the Speache ...* (1588)
- *The Use of the Celestial Globe in Plano, set forth in two hemispheres* (1590)
- *The Use of Jacobs Staffe*
- *Making and Use of the Sector*
- *Elementes of Geometrie* (1590), translated from the Latin of [Petrus Ramus](wikipedia://en/311650), *Geometriae Septem Et Viginti*
- A translation of the arithmetic of [Christian Wursteisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Wursteisen) (1596)
- Work on surveying (1598).
