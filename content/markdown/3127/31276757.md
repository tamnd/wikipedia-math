# Yao graph

*Image: Yao graph.svg.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yao graph"}

In [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), the **Yao graph**, named after [Andrew Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Yao), is a kind of [geometric spanner](wikipedia://en/11844294), a weighted [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) connecting a set of [geometric points](wikipedia://en/593693) with the property that, for every pair of points in the graph, their [shortest path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortest_path) has a length that is within a constant factor of their [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932).

:::

The basic idea underlying the two-dimensional Yao graph is to surround each of the given points by equally spaced [rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_%28geometry%29), partitioning the plane into sectors with equal angles, and to connect each point to its [nearest neighbor](wikipedia://en/12061759) in each of these sectors.[^o2] Associated with a Yao graph is an integer parameter $k \ge 6$ which is the number of rays and sectors described above; larger values of $k$ produce closer approximations to the Euclidean distance.[^o3] The [stretch factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stretch_factor) is at most $1/(\cos \theta - \sin \theta)$, where $\theta$ is the angle of the sectors.[^o4] The same idea can be extended to point sets in more than two dimensions, but the number of sectors required grows exponentially with the dimension.

[Andrew Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Yao) used these graphs to construct high-dimensional [Euclidean minimum spanning trees](wikipedia://en/1040597).[^o4]
