# Quaternionic projective space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quaternionic projective space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **quaternionic projective space** is an extension of the ideas of [real projective space](wikipedia://en/1014694) and [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977), to the case where coordinates lie in the ring of [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) $\mathbb{H}.$ Quaternionic projective space of dimension *n* is usually denoted by

:::

$$\mathbb{HP}^n$$

and is a [closed manifold](wikipedia://en/669475) of (real) dimension 4*n*. It is a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) for a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) action, in more than one way. The quaternionic projective line $\mathbb{HP}^1$ is homeomorphic to the 4-sphere.

## In coordinates {#In_coordinates}

Its direct construction is as a special case of the [projective space over a division algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_space_over_a_division_algebra). The [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316) of a point can be written

$$[q_0,q_1,\ldots,q_n]$$

where the $q_i$ are quaternions, not all zero. Two sets of coordinates represent the same point if they are 'proportional' by a left multiplication by a non-zero quaternion *c*; that is, we identify all the

$$[cq_0,cq_1\ldots,cq_n].$$

In the language of [group actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29), $\mathbb{HP}^n$ is the [orbit space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_space) of $\mathbb{H}^{n+1}\setminus\{(0,\ldots,0)\}$ by the action of $\mathbb{H}^{\times}$, the multiplicative group of non-zero quaternions. By first projecting onto the unit sphere inside $\mathbb{H}^{n+1}$ one may also regard $\mathbb{HP}^{n}$ as the orbit space of $S^{4n+3}$ by the action of $\text{Sp}(1)$, the group of unit quaternions.[^o3] The sphere $S^{4n+3}$ then becomes a [principal Sp(1)-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) over $\mathbb{HP}^n$:

$$\mathrm{Sp}(1) \to S^{4n+3} \to \mathbb{HP}^n.$$

This bundle is sometimes called a (generalized) [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384).

There is also a construction of $\mathbb{HP}^{n}$ by means of two-dimensional complex subspaces of $\mathbb{H}^{2n}$, meaning that $\mathbb{HP}^{n}$ lies inside a complex [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810).

## Topology {#Topology}

### Homotopy theory {#Homotopy_theory}

The space $\mathbb{HP}^{\infty}$, defined as the union of all finite $\mathbb{HP}^n$'s under inclusion, is the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) *BS*<sup>3</sup>. The homotopy groups of $\mathbb{HP}^{\infty}$ are given by $\pi_i(\mathbb{HP}^{\infty}) = \pi_i(BS^3) \cong \pi_{i-1}(S^3).$ These groups are known to be very complex and in particular they are non-zero for infinitely many values of $i$. However, we do have that

$$\pi_i(\mathbb{HP}^\infty) \otimes \mathbb{Q} \cong \begin{cases} \mathbb{Q} & i = 4 \\ 0 & i \neq 4 \end{cases}$$

It follows that rationally, i.e. after [localisation of a space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localisation_of_a_space), $\mathbb{HP}^\infty$ is an [Eilenberg–Maclane space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg–Maclane_space) $K(\mathbb{Q},4)$. That is $\mathbb{HP}^{\infty}_{\mathbb{Q}} \simeq K(\mathbb{Z}, 4)_{\mathbb{Q}}.$ (cf. the example [K(Z,2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%28Z,2%29)). See [rational homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/13998981).

In general, $\mathbb{HP}^n$ has a cell structure with one cell in each dimension which is a multiple of 4, up to $4n$. Accordingly, its cohomology ring is $\mathbb{Z}[v]/v^{n+1}$, where $v$ is a 4-dimensional generator. This is analogous to complex projective space. It also follows from rational homotopy theory that $\mathbb{HP}^n$ has infinite homotopy groups only in dimensions 4 and $4n+3$.

## Differential geometry {#Differential_geometry}

$\mathbb{HP}^n$ carries a natural [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) analogous to the [Fubini-Study metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fubini-Study_metric) on $\mathbb{CP}^n$, with respect to which it is a compact [quaternion-Kähler symmetric space](wikipedia://en/11018121) with positive curvature.

Quaternionic projective space can be represented as the coset space

$$\mathbb{HP}^n = \operatorname{Sp}(n+1)/\operatorname{Sp}(n)\times\operatorname{Sp}(1)$$

where $\operatorname{Sp}(n)$ is the compact [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977).

### Characteristic classes {#Characteristic_classes}

Since $\mathbb{HP}^1=S^4$, its tangent bundle is stably trivial. The tangent bundles of the rest have nontrivial [Stiefel–Whitney](wikipedia://en/877761) and [Pontryagin classes](wikipedia://en/385944). The total classes are given by the following formulas:

$$w(\mathbb{HP}^n) = (1+u)^{n+1}$$

$$p(\mathbb{HP}^n) = (1+v)^{2n+2} (1+4v)^{-1}$$

where $v$ is the generator of $H^4(\mathbb{HP}^n;\mathbb{Z})$ and $u$ is its reduction mod 2.[^o8]

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

### Quaternionic projective line {#Quaternionic_projective_line}

The one-dimensional projective space over $\mathbb{H}$ is called the "projective line" in generalization of the [complex projective line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_projective_line). For example, it was used (implicitly) in 1947 by P. G. Gormley to extend the [Möbius group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Möbius_group) to the quaternion context with [linear fractional transformations](wikipedia://en/401769). For the linear fractional transformations of an associative [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) with 1, see [projective line over a ring](wikipedia://en/1271901) and the homography group GL(2,*A*).

From the topological point of view the quaternionic projective line is the 4-sphere, and in fact these are [diffeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphic) manifolds. The fibration mentioned previously is from the 7-sphere, and is an example of a [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384).

Explicit expressions for coordinates for the 4-sphere can be found in the article on the [Fubini–Study metric](wikipedia://en/2574486).

### Quaternionic projective plane {#Quaternionic_projective_plane}

The 8-dimensional $\mathbb{HP}^{2}$ has a [circle action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_action), by the group of complex scalars of absolute value 1 acting on the other side (so on the right, as the convention for the action of *c* above is on the left). Therefore, the [quotient manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_manifold)

$$\mathbb{HP}^{2}/\mathrm{U}(1)$$

may be taken, writing [U(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%281%29) for the [circle group](wikipedia://en/508177). It has been shown that this quotient is the 7-[sphere](wikipedia://en/27859), a result of [Vladimir Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490) from 1996, later rediscovered by [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) and [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698). It is called quaternionic [Arnold–Kuiper–Massey theorem](wikipedia://en/84169718).
