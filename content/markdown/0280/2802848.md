# Temperley–Lieb algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Temperley–Lieb algebra"}

In [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), the **Temperley–Lieb algebra** is an algebra from which are built certain [transfer matrices](wikipedia://en/4567165), invented by [Neville Temperley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Neville_Vazeille_Temperley) and [Elliott Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_H._Lieb). It is also related to [integrable models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_model), [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008) and the [braid groups](wikipedia://en/550138), [quantum groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_groups) and [subfactors](wikipedia://en/2780098) of [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185).

:::

## Structure {#Structure}

### Generators and relations {#Generators_and_relations}

Let $R$ be a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) and fix $\delta \in R$. The Temperley–Lieb algebra $TL_n(\delta)$ is the unital associative [$R$-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_%28ring_theory%29) generated by the elements $e_1, e_2, \ldots, e_{n-1}$, subject to the Jones relations:

- $e_i^2 = \delta e_i$ for all $1 \leq i \leq n-1$
- $e_i e_{i+1} e_i = e_i$ for all $1 \leq i \leq n-2$
- $e_i e_{i-1} e_i = e_i$ for all $2 \leq i \leq n-1$
- $e_i e_j = e_j e_i$ for all $1 \leq i,j \leq n-1$ such that $|i-j| \neq 1$

Using these relations, any product of generators $e_i$ can be brought to Jones' normal form:

$$E= \big(e_{i_1}e_{i_1-1}\cdots e_{j_1}\big)\big(e_{i_2}e_{i_2-1}\cdots e_{j_2}\big)\cdots\big(e_{i_r}e_{i_r-1}\cdots e_{j_r}\big)$$

where $(i_1,i_2,\dots,i_r)$ and $(j_1,j_2,\dots,j_r)$ are two strictly increasing sequences in $\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}$. Elements of this type form a basis of the Temperley-Lieb algebra.[^o20]

The dimensions of Temperley-Lieb algebras are [Catalan numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_numbers):[^o24]

$$\dim(TL_n(\delta)) = \frac{(2n)!}{n!(n+1)!}$$

The Temperley–Lieb algebra $TL_n(\delta)$ is a subalgebra of the [Brauer algebra](wikipedia://en/3875858) $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$,[^o25] and therefore also of the [partition algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_algebra) $P_n(\delta)$. The Temperley–Lieb algebra $TL_n(\delta)$ is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/3876151) for $\delta\in\mathbb{C}-F_n$ where $F_n$ is a known, [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742).[^o23] For a given $n$, all semisimple Temperley-Lieb algebras are isomorphic.[^o25]

### Diagram algebra {#Diagram_algebra}

$TL_n(\delta)$ may be represented diagrammatically as the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over noncrossing pairings of $2n$ points on two opposite sides of a rectangle with *n* points on each of the two sides.

The [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) is the diagram in which each point is connected to the one directly across the rectangle from it. The generator $e_i$ is the diagram in which the $i$-th and $(i+1)$-th point on the left side are connected to each other, similarly the two points opposite to these on the right side, and all other points are connected to the point directly across the rectangle.

## Representations {#Representations}

### Structure {#Structure}

For $\delta$ such that $TL_n(\delta)$ is semisimple, a complete set $\{W_\ell\}$ of simple modules is parametrized by integers $0\leq \ell\leq n$ with $\ell\equiv n\bmod 2$. The dimension of a simple module is written in terms of [binomial coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_coefficients) as[^o23]

$$\dim(W_\ell) = \binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell}{2}} - \binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell}{2}-1}$$

A basis of the simple module $W_\ell$ is the set $M_{n,\ell}$ of monic noncrossing pairings from $n$ points on the left to $\ell$ points on the right. (Monic means that each point on the right is connected to a point on the left.) There is a natural [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between $\cup_{\begin{array}{c} 0\leq \ell\leq n \\ \ell\equiv n\bmod 2\end{array}}M_{n,\ell}\times M_{n,\ell}$, and the set of diagrams that generate $TL_n(\delta)$: Any such diagram can be cut into two elements of $M_{n,\ell}$ for some $\ell$.

Then $TL_n(\delta)$ acts on $W_\ell$ by diagram concatenation from the left.[^o25] (Concatenation can produce non-monic pairings, which have to be modded out.) The module $W_\ell$ may be called a **standard module** or **link module**.[^o20]

If $\delta = q+q^{-1}$ with $q$ a [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950), $TL_n(\delta)$ may not be semisimple, and $W_\ell$ may not be irreducible:

$$W_\ell \text{ reducible } \iff \exists j\in\{1,2,\dots,\ell\}, \ q^{2n-4\ell+2+2j} =1$$

If $W_\ell$ is reducible, then its quotient by its maximal proper submodule is irreducible.[^o20]

### Branching rules from the Brauer algebra {#Branching_rules_from_the_Brauer_algebra}

[Simple modules](wikipedia://en/3875858#Brauer-Specht_modules) of the [Brauer algebra](wikipedia://en/3875858) $\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)$ can be decomposed into simple modules of the Temperley-Lieb algebra. The decomposition is called a [branching rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branching_rule), and it is a direct sum with positive integer coefficients:

$$W_\lambda\left(\mathfrak{B}_n(\delta)\right) = \bigoplus_{\begin{array}{c} |\lambda|\leq \ell\leq n \\ \ell\equiv |\lambda|\bmod 2\end{array}} c_\ell^\lambda W_\ell\left(TL_n(\delta)\right)$$

The coefficients $c_\ell^\lambda$ do not depend on $n,\delta$, and are given by[^o23]

$$c_\ell^\lambda = f^\lambda\sum_{r=0}^{\frac{\ell-|\lambda|}{2}} (-1)^r \binom{\ell-r}{r}\binom{\ell-2r}{\ell-|\lambda|-2r}(\ell-|\lambda|-2r)!!$$

where $f^\lambda$ is the number of standard [Young tableaux](wikipedia://en/683368) of shape $\lambda$, given by the [hook length formula](wikipedia://en/41497810).

:::

## Affine Temperley-Lieb algebra {#Affine_Temperley-Lieb_algebra}

The affine Temperley-Lieb algebra $aTL_n(\delta)$ is an infinite-dimensional algebra such that $TL_n(\delta)\subset aTL_n(\delta)$. It is obtained by adding generators $e_n,\tau,\tau^{-1}$ such that[^o22]

- $\tau e_i = e_{i+1}\tau$ for all $1 \leq i \leq n$,
- $$e_1\tau^2 = e_1e_2 \cdots e_{n-1},$$
- $$\tau \tau^{-1}=\tau^{-1}\tau = \text{id}.$$

The indices are supposed to be periodic i.e. $e_{n+1}=e_1,e_n=e_0$, and the Temperley-Lieb relations are supposed to hold for all $1 \leq i \leq n$. Then $\tau^n$ is central. A finite-dimensional quotient of the algebra $aTL_n(\delta)$, sometimes called the **unoriented Jones-Temperley-Lieb algebra**,[^o21] is obtained by assuming $\tau^n=\text{id}$, and replacing non-contractible lines with the same factor $\delta$ as contractible lines (for example, in the case $n=4$, this implies $e_1e_3e_2e_4e_1e_3 = \delta^2 e_1e_3$).

The diagram algebra for $aTL_n(\delta)$ is deduced from the diagram algebra for $TL_n(\delta)$ by turning rectangles into cylinders. The algebra $aTL_n(\delta)$ is infinite-dimensional because lines can wind around the cylinder. If $n$ is even, there can even exist closed winding lines, which are non-contractible.

The Temperley-Lieb algebra is a quotient of the corresponding affine Temperley-Lieb algebra.[^o22]

The **cell module** $W_{\ell,z}$ of $aTL_n(\delta)$ is generated by the set of monic pairings from $n$ points to $\ell$ points, just like the module $W_{\ell}$ of $TL_n(\delta)$. However, the pairings are now on a cylinder, and the right-multiplication with $\tau$ is identified with $z\cdot\text{id}$ for some $z\in\mathbb{C}^*$. If $\ell=0$, there is no right-multiplication by $\tau$, and it is the addition of a non-contractible loop on the right which is identified with $z+z^{-1}$. Cell modules are finite-dimensional, with

$$\dim(W_{\ell,z}) = \binom{n}{\frac{n-\ell}{2}}$$

The cell module $W_{\ell,z}$ is irreducible for all $z\in\mathbb{C}^*-R(\delta)$, where the set $R(\delta)$ is countable. For $z\in R(\delta)$, $W_{\ell,z}$ has an irreducible quotient. The irreducible cell modules and quotients thereof form a complete set of irreducible modules of $aTL_n(\delta)$.[^o22] Cell modules of the unoriented Jones-Temperley-Lieb algebra must obey $z^\ell=1$ if $\ell\neq 0$, and $z+z^{-1} = \delta$ if $\ell=0$.

## Applications {#Applications}

### Temperley–Lieb Hamiltonian {#Temperley–Lieb_Hamiltonian}

$$\mathcal{H} = \sum_{j=1}^{n-1} (\delta - e_j)$$

In what follows we consider the special case $\delta=1$.

Writing $\mathcal{H}$ as a matrix in the basis of possible states we have,

$$\mathcal{H} = \left(\begin{array}{rr} 1 & -1\\ -1 & 1 \end{array}\right)$$

The eigenvector of $\mathcal{H}$ with the *lowest* [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429) is known as the [ground state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_state). In this case, the lowest eigenvalue $\lambda_0$ for $\mathcal{H}$ is $\lambda_0 = 0$. The corresponding [eigenvector](wikipedia://en/2161429) is $\psi_0 = (1, 1)$. As we vary the number of sites $n$ we find the following table[^o14]

::: {#o15 .table cue=table}

| $n$ | $\psi_0$ | $n$ | $\psi_0$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | (1) | 3 | (1, 1) |
| 4 | (2, 1) | 5 | $(3_3, 1_2)$ |
| 6 | $(11, 5_2,4, 1)$ | 7 | $(26_4, 10_2, 9_2, 8_2, 5_2, 1_2)$ |
| 8 | $(170, 75_2, 71, 56_2, 50, 30, 14_4, 6, 1)$ | 9 | $(646, \ldots)$ |
| $\vdots$ | $\vdots$ | $\vdots$ | $\vdots$ |

:::

where we have used the notation $m_j = (m, \ldots, m)$ $j$-times e.g., $5_2 = (5, 5)$.

An interesting observation is that the largest components of the ground state of $\mathcal{H}$ have a combinatorial enumeration as we vary the number of sites,[^o16] as was first observed by [Murray Batchelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Batchelor), Jan de Gier, and Bernard Nienhuis.[^o14] Using the resources of the [on-line encyclopedia of integer sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-line_encyclopedia_of_integer_sequences), Batchelor *et al.* found, for an even numbers of sites

$$1, 2, 11, 170, \ldots = \prod_{j=0}^{\frac{n-2}{2}} \left( 3j + 1\right)\frac{ (2j)!(6j)!}{(4j)!(4j + 1)!} \qquad (n = 2, 4, 6,\dots)$$

and for an odd numbers of sites

$$1, 3, 26, 646, \ldots = \prod_{j=0}^{\frac{n-3}{2}} (3j+2)\frac{ (2j + 1)!(6j + 3)!}{(4j + 2)!(4j + 3)!} \qquad (n=3, 5, 7, \dots)$$

Surprisingly, these sequences corresponded to well known combinatorial objects. For $n$ even, this corresponds to cyclically symmetric transpose complement plane partitions and for $n$ odd, , these correspond to [alternating sign matrices](wikipedia://en/1740155) symmetric about the vertical axis.

### XXZ spin chain {#XXZ_spin_chain}

Correlations for Temperley-Lieb models can be computed by means of the theory of factorized [correlation functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_functions) of the XXZ spin chain (hidden [Grassmann structure](wikipedia://en/373810)).[^o18]
