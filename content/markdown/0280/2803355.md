# David Spiegelhalter

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="David Spiegelhalter"}

**Sir David John Spiegelhalter** (born 16 August 1953) is a British statistician and a Fellow of [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge).[^o5][^o7] From 2007 to 2018 he was [Winton Professor of the Public Understanding of Risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winton_Professorship_of_the_Public_Understanding_of_Risk) in the [Statistical Laboratory](wikipedia://en/3254271) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).[^o8] Spiegelhalter is an [ISI highly cited researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISI_highly_cited_researcher).

:::

He is currently Chair of the Winton Centre for Risk and Evidence Communication in the [Centre for Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Mathematical_Sciences_%28Cambridge%29) at Cambridge.[^o9] On 27 May 2020 he joined the [board](wikipedia://en/16155568#Current_board_members) of the [UK Statistics Authority](wikipedia://en/16155568) as a non-executive director for a period of three years,[^o10] a term which was extended through to 2026.[^o11]

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Spiegelhalter was born on 16 August 1953. He was educated at [Barnstaple Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnstaple_Grammar_School), a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school) grammar school in [Barnstaple, Devon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnstaple,_Devon), from 1963 to 1970.[^o14] He then studied mathematics at [Keble College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keble_College,_Oxford), graduating with a Bachelor of Arts (BA) degree in 1974.[^o15][^o18] He moved to [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), where he gained his Master of Science (MSc) degree in statistics in 1975 and a Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) degree in [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) in 1978.[^o15][^o18] His doctoral thesis was titled "Adaptive Inference Using Finite Mixture Models",[^o16] and was [supervised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor) by [Adrian Smith](wikipedia://en/4097065).[^o2]

## Career {#Career}

Spiegelhalter was research assistant in [Brunel University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunel_University) in 1976[^o18] and then visiting lecturer at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), 1977–78. After his PhD, he was a research assistant for the [Royal College of Physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians); he was based at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham), where his PhD supervisor, [Adrian Smith](wikipedia://en/4097065), had been appointed a professor.

From 1981 he was at the Medical Research Council Biostatistics Unit at Cambridge. He has been an honorary lecturer at the [University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hong_Kong) since 1991. He has also been a consultant for [GlaxoSmithKline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GlaxoSmithKline), [Novartis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novartis) and the [World Anti-Doping Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Anti-Doping_Agency). He played a leading role in the [public inquiries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_inquiries_in_the_United_Kingdom) into children's heart surgery at the [Bristol Royal Infirmary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Royal_Infirmary), the murders by [Harold Shipman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Shipman),[^o19] the independent inquiry on [Mid Staffs Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid_Staffordshire_NHS_Foundation_Trust) and, more recently, the [infected blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infected_Blood_Inquiry) inquiry.

Between 2007 and 2012 he divided his work[^o20] between the Cambridge [Statistical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_Laboratory,_University_of_Cambridge) (three-fifths) and the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_%28UK%29) [Biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) Unit (two-fifths).[^o21] He left the MRC in March 2012[^o22] and worked full-time at the Statistical Laboratory as the [Winton Professor of the Public Understanding of Risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winton_Professorship_of_the_Public_Understanding_of_Risk) until his retirement. He remained chair of the Winton Centre until it closed in 2023. As of 2012 Spiegelhalter has supervised 7 PhD students.[^o18][^o23]

*Image: David\_crop.jpg.* David Spiegelhalter playing with Arco Iris [Samba band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samba_band), July 2009

In 2012, Spiegelhalter hosted the [BBC Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Four) documentary *Tails You Win: The Science of Chance* which described the application of [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) in everyday life.[^o24] He also presented a 2013 [Cambridge Science Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Science_Festival) talk, *How to Spot a Shabby Statistic* at the Babbage Lecture Theatre in Cambridge.[^o7][^o25]

He was elected as President of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664), and took up the position on 1 January 2017. His Presidential address later that year took as its subject *Trust in Numbers*.[^o26]

In March 2020 Spiegelhalter launched a podcast called *Risky Talk* where he interviews experts in risk and evidence communication on topics like genetics, nutrition, climate change and immigration.[^o27] He appeared on [BBC Desert Island Discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Island_Discs) on 6 February 2022.[^o28]

## Research {#Research}

Spiegelhalter's research interests are in statistics[^o4] including

- [Bayesian](wikipedia://en/404412) approach to clinical trials, expert systems and complex modelling and epidemiology.[^o31]
- [Graphical models](wikipedia://en/447298) of conditional independence. He wrote several papers in the 1980s that showed how probability could be incorporated into expert systems, a problem that seemed intractable at the time. Spiegelhalter showed that while [frequentist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequentist) probability did not lend itself to expert systems, [Bayesian probability](wikipedia://en/4890) most certainly did.[^o32]
- [Statistical software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_software).[^o33] In the 1990s Spiegelhalter led the [Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_%28UK%29) team that developed [WinBUGS ("Bayesian analysis Using Gibbs Sampling")](wikipedia://en/10794084), a statistical-modelling system allowing [hierarchical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchical_Bayes_model) [prior distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prior_distribution). WinBUGS and its successor [OpenBUGS](wikipedia://en/3578854) specifies [graphical models](wikipedia://en/447298) using acyclic directed graphs whose nodes are random variables, which are updated using [Gibbs sampling](wikipedia://en/509709) (an updating method for [Markov chain Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/236801) (MCMC) [simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation)).[^o34] Earlier Bayesian software had required that the probability distribution for the observed data be an [exponential family](wikipedia://en/339174) and that the prior be its [conjugate distribution](wikipedia://en/846412). Allowing flexible choices of prior distributions simplified hierarchical modelling and helped to promote [multilevel models](wikipedia://en/4521038), which became widely used in epidemiology and education.
- General issues in [clinical trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_trials),[^o35] including cluster randomisation, meta-analysis and ethical monitoring.
- Monitoring and comparing clinical and public-health outcomes and their associated publication as performance indicators.
- Public understanding of risk,[^o36][^o37] including promoting concepts such as the [micromort](wikipedia://en/13028883) (a one in a million chance of death) and [microlife](wikipedia://en/37987755) (a 30-minute reduction of life expectancy). Media reporting of statistics,[^o38] risk and probability and the wider conception of uncertainty as going beyond what is measured to model uncertainty, the unknown and the unmeasurable.

In 2003 Spiegelhalter and others published a paper on the many murders of physician [Harold Shipman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Shipman), in which they found that routine statistical monitoring of mortality could have revealed the murders in 1996, two years and many murders before suspicions were actually raised.[^o39]
