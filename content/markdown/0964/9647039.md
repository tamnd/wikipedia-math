# Strip algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Strip algebra"}

**Strip Algebra** is a set of elements and operators for the description of [carbon nanotube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_nanotube) structures, considered as a subgroup of [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra), and more precisely, of polyhedra with [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) formed by three edges. This restriction is imposed on the polyhedra because carbon nanotubes are formed of [sp2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sp2_bond) carbon atoms. Strip Algebra was developed initially [^o2] for the determination of the structure connecting two arbitrary nanotubes, but has also been extended to the connection of three identical nanotubes [^o3]

:::

## Background {#Background}

Graphitic systems are [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule) and [crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal) formed of [carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon) atoms in sp2 hybridization. Thus, the atoms are arranged on a [hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) grid. [Graphite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphite), nanotubes, and [fullerenes](wikipedia://en/10628) are examples of graphitic systems. All of them share the property that each atom is bonded to three others (3-[valent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valence_%28chemistry%29)).

The relation between the number of vertices, edges and faces of any finite polyhedron is given by [Euler's polyhedron formula](wikipedia://en/148420):

$$e - f - v = 2 (g -1),\,$$

where *e*, *f* and *v* are the number of edges, faces and vertices, respectively, and *g* is the [genus](wikipedia://en/61891#Topology) of the polyhedron, i.e., the number of "holes" in the surface. For example, a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) is a surface of genus 0, while a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) is of genus 1.

## Nomenclature {#Nomenclature}

A substrip is identified by a pair of natural numbers measuring the position of the last ring in parentheses, together with the turns induced by the defect ring. The number of [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) of the defect can be extracted from these.

$$(n,m) [T_+,T_-]$$

## Elements {#Elements}

A Strip is defined as a set of consecutive rings, that is able to be joined with others, by sharing a side of the first or last ring.

Numerous complex structures can be formed with strips. As said before, strips have both at the beginning and at the end two connections. With strips only, can be formed two of them.

## Applications {#Applications}

- Strip Algebra has been applied to the construction of [nanotube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_nanotube) heterojunctions, and was first implemented in the [CoNTub v1.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoNTub) software, which makes it possible to find the precise position of all the carbon rings needed to produce a heterojunction with arbitrary indices and chirality from two [nanotubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_nanotube).
