# Pokhozhaev's identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pokhozhaev's identity"}

**Pokhozhaev's identity** is an integral relation satisfied by stationary [localized solutions](wikipedia://en/51654) to a [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748) or [nonlinear Klein&ndash;Gordon equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_Klein&ndash;Gordon_equation). It was obtained by S.I. Pokhozhaev[^o2] and is similar to the [virial theorem](wikipedia://en/32664). This relation is also known as [G.H. Derrick's theorem](wikipedia://en/28844639). Similar identities can be derived for other equations of mathematical physics.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="The Pokhozhaev identity for the stationary nonlinear Schrödinger equation"}

## The Pokhozhaev identity for the stationary nonlinear Schrödinger equation {#The_Pokhozhaev_identity_for_the_stationary_nonlinear_Schrödinger_equation}

Here is a general form due to [H. Berestycki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Berestycki) and [P.-L. Lions](wikipedia://en/1270068).[^o5]

Let $g(s)$ be continuous and real-valued, with $g(0)=0$. Denote $G(s)=\int_0^s g(t)\,dt$. Let

$$u\in L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^n), \qquad \nabla u\in L^2(\mathbb{R}^n), \qquad G(u)\in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n), \qquad n\in\mathbb{N},$$

be a solution to the equation

$$-\nabla^2 u=g(u),$$

[in the sense of distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29). Then $u$ satisfies the relation

$$\frac{n-2}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}|\nabla u(x)|^2\,dx=n\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}G(u(x))\,dx.$$

:::

::: {#o7 .statement cue=named-heading name="The Pokhozhaev identity for the stationary nonlinear Dirac equation"}

## The Pokhozhaev identity for the stationary nonlinear Dirac equation {#The_Pokhozhaev_identity_for_the_stationary_nonlinear_Dirac_equation}

There is a form of the virial identity for the stationary [nonlinear Dirac equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_Dirac_equation) in three spatial dimensions (and also the [Maxwell-Dirac equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell-Dirac_equations))[^o8] and in arbitrary spatial dimension.[^o9] Let $n\in\mathbb{N},\,N\in\mathbb{N}$ and let $\alpha^i,\,1\le i\le n$ and $\beta$ be the [self-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint) [Dirac matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_matrices) of size $N\times N$:

$$\alpha^i\alpha^j+\alpha^j\alpha^i=2\delta_{ij}I_N, \quad \beta^2=I_N, \quad \alpha^i\beta+\beta\alpha^i=0, \quad 1\le i,j\le n.$$

Let $D_0=-\mathrm{i}\alpha\cdot\nabla=-\mathrm{i}\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha^i\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}$ be the massless [Dirac operator](wikipedia://en/1079448). Let $g(s)$ be continuous and real-valued, with $g(0)=0$. Denote $G(s)=\int_0^s g(t)\,dt$. Let $\phi\in L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^n,\mathbb{C}^N)$ be a [spinor](wikipedia://en/29276)-valued solution that satisfies the stationary form of the [nonlinear Dirac equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_Dirac_equation),

$$\omega\phi=D_0\phi+g(\phi^\ast\beta\phi)\beta\phi,$$

[in the sense of distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29), with some $\omega\in\mathbb{R}$. Assume that

$$\phi\in H^1(\mathbb{R}^n,\mathbb{C}^N),\qquad G(\phi^\ast\beta\phi)\in L^1(\mathbb{R}^n).$$

Then $\phi$ satisfies the relation

$$\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\phi(x)^\ast\phi(x)\,dx =\frac{n-1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}\phi(x)^\ast D_0\phi(x)\,dx +\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}G(\phi(x)^\ast\beta\phi(x))\,dx.$$

:::
