# Formal system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Formal system"}

A **formal system** (or **deductive system**) is an [abstract structure](wikipedia://en/359377) and [formalization](wikipedia://en/16258342) of an [axiomatic system](wikipedia://en/188401) used for [deducing](wikipedia://en/61093), using [rules of inference](wikipedia://en/252311), [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) from [axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axioms).[^n1]

:::

In 1921, [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) proposed to use formal systems as the foundation of knowledge in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).[^o3] However, in 1931 [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736) proved that any [consistent](wikipedia://en/75802) formal system sufficiently powerful to express basic arithmetic cannot prove its own [completeness](wikipedia://en/31271451). This effectively showed that [Hilbert's program](wikipedia://en/607286) was impossible as stated.

The term *formalism* is sometimes a rough synonym for *formal system*, but it also refers to a given style of [notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notation), for example, [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742)'s [bra–ket notation](wikipedia://en/4542).

## Concepts {#Concepts}

*Image: Formal languages.svg.* This diagram shows the [syntactic entities](wikipedia://en/546039) that may be constructed from [formal languages](wikipedia://en/10939). The symbols and [strings of symbols](wikipedia://en/27701) may be broadly divided into [nonsense](wikipedia://en/387403) and [well-formed formulas](wikipedia://en/404582). A formal language can be thought of as identical to the set of its well-formed formulas, which may be broadly divided into [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) and non-theorems.

A formal system has the following components, as a minimum:[^o5]

- [Formal language](wikipedia://en/10939), which is a set of [well-formed formulas](wikipedia://en/404582), which are strings of [symbols](wikipedia://en/24028642) from an [alphabet](wikipedia://en/4292269), formed by a [formal grammar](wikipedia://en/18020716) (consisting of [production rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_%28computer_science%29) or [formation rules](wikipedia://en/15115411)).
- Deductive system, deductive apparatus, or [proof system](wikipedia://en/1250665), which has [rules of inference](wikipedia://en/252311) that take [axioms](wikipedia://en/928) and infers [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977), both of which are part of the formal language.
- In some cases an [inductive system](wikipedia://en/18881), used to derive a proof by first establishing a simple case, then generalizing it.

A formal system is said to be [recursive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursive_set) (i.e. effective) or recursively enumerable if the set of axioms and the set of inference rules are [decidable sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decidable_set) or [semidecidable sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursively_enumerable_set), respectively.

### Formal language {#Formal_language}

A [formal language](wikipedia://en/10939) is a language that uses a set of strings whose symbols are taken from a specific alphabet, and operations used to form sentences from them. Like languages in [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics), formal languages generally have two aspects:

- the [syntax](wikipedia://en/546039) is what the language looks like (more formally: the set of possible expressions that are valid utterances in the language)
- the [semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantics_of_logic) are what the utterances of the language mean (which is formalized in various ways, depending on the type of language in question)

Usually only the [syntax](wikipedia://en/546039) of a formal language is considered via the notion of a [formal grammar](wikipedia://en/18020716). The two main categories of formal grammar are that of [generative grammars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_grammar), which are sets of rules for how strings in a language can be written, and that of [analytic grammars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_grammar) (or reductive grammar[^o7][^o8]), which are sets of rules for how a string can be analyzed to determine whether it is a member of the language.

### Deductive system {#Deductive_system}

section A *deductive system*, also called a *deductive apparatus*[^o10], consists of the [axioms](wikipedia://en/928#Role_in_mathematical_logic) (or [axiom schemata](wikipedia://en/764613)) and [rules of inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules_of_inference) that can be used to [derive](wikipedia://en/1197184) [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) of the system.[^n1]

In order to sustain its deductive integrity, a *deductive apparatus* must be definable without reference to any [intended interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intended_interpretation) of the language. The aim is to ensure that each line of a [derivation](wikipedia://en/82285) is merely a [logical consequence](wikipedia://en/37531624) of the lines that precede it. There should be no element of any [interpretation](wikipedia://en/14511671) of the language that gets involved with the deductive nature of the system.

The [logical consequence](wikipedia://en/37531624) (or entailment) of the system by its logical foundation is what distinguishes a formal system from others which may have some basis in an abstract model. Often the formal system will be the basis for or even identified with a larger [theory](wikipedia://en/30746) or field (e.g. [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417)) consistent with the usage in modern mathematics such as [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858).

An example of a deductive system would be the rules of inference and [axioms regarding equality](wikipedia://en/10983#Equality_and_its_axioms) used in [first order logic](wikipedia://en/10983).

The two main types of deductive systems are proof systems and formal semantics.[^o13]

#### Proof system {#Proof_system}

Formal proofs are sequences of [well-formed formulas](wikipedia://en/404582) (or WFF for short) that might either be an [axiom](wikipedia://en/928) or be the product of applying an inference rule on previous WFFs in the proof sequence.

Once a formal system is given, one can define the set of theorems which can be proved inside the formal system. This set consists of all WFFs for which there is a proof. Thus all axioms are considered theorems. Unlike the grammar for WFFs, there is no guarantee that there will be a [decision procedure](wikipedia://en/913118) for deciding whether a given WFF is a theorem or not.

The point of view that generating formal proofs is all there is to mathematics is often called *[formalism](wikipedia://en/16258342)*. [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) founded [metamathematics](wikipedia://en/183508) as a discipline for discussing formal systems. Any language that one uses to talk about a formal system is called a *[metalanguage](wikipedia://en/402929)*. The metalanguage may be a natural language, or it may be partially formalized itself, but it is generally less completely formalized than the formal language component of the formal system under examination, which is then called the *object language*, that is, the object of the discussion in question. The notion of *theorem* just defined should not be confused with *theorems about the formal system*, which, in order to avoid confusion, are usually called [metatheorems](wikipedia://en/5106151).

:::

#### Formal semantics of logical system {#Formal_semantics_of_logical_system}

A *logical system* is a deductive system (most commonly [first order logic](wikipedia://en/10983)) together with additional [non-logical axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-logical_axioms). According to [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), a logical system may be given [interpretations](wikipedia://en/14511671) which describe whether a given [structure](wikipedia://en/4055928) - the mapping of formulas to a particular meaning - satisfies a well-formed formula. A structure that satisfies all the axioms of the formal system is known as a [model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_%28model_theory%29) of the logical system.

A logical system is:

- [Sound](wikipedia://en/26819), if each well-formed formula that can be inferred from the axioms is satisfied by every model of the logical system.
- [Semantically complete](wikipedia://en/31271451#Semantic_completeness), if each well-formed formula that is satisfied by every model of the logical system can be inferred from the axioms.

An example of a logical system is [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic). The standard model of arithmetic sets the [domain of discourse](wikipedia://en/229180) to be the [nonnegative integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonnegative_integer) and gives the symbols their usual meaning.[^o17] There are also [non-standard models of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/6251420).

## History {#History}

Early logic systems includes Indian logic of [Pāṇini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pāṇini), syllogistic logic of Aristotle, propositional logic of Stoicism, and Chinese logic of [Gongsun Long](wikipedia://en/202693) (c. 325–250 BCE). In more recent times, contributors include [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298), [Augustus De Morgan](wikipedia://en/18949289), and [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416). [Mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) was developed in 19th century [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe).

[David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) instigated a [formalist](wikipedia://en/16258342) movement called [Hilbert's program](wikipedia://en/607286) as a proposed solution to the [foundational crisis of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundational_crisis_of_mathematics), that was eventually tempered by [Gödel's incompleteness theorems](wikipedia://en/58863).[^o3] The [QED manifesto](wikipedia://en/209532) represented a subsequent, as yet unsuccessful, effort at formalization of known mathematics.
