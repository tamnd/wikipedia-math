# Truism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Truism"}

A **truism** is a claim that is so obvious or [self-evident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-evidence) as to be hardly worth mentioning, except as a reminder or as a [rhetorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhetorical_device) or [literary device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_narrative_techniques), and is the opposite of a **falsism**.[^o2]

:::

In [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), a sentence which asserts incomplete truth conditions for a proposition may be regarded as a truism.[^o3] An example of such a sentence would be "Under appropriate conditions, the sun rises." Without contextual support – a statement of what those appropriate conditions are – the sentence is true but incontestable.[^o4]

[Lapalissades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapalissade), such as "If he were not dead, he would still be alive", are considered to be truisms.
