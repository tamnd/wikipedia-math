# Real computation

*Image: Operational amplifier integrator.svg.* [Circuit diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_diagram) of an [analog computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_computing) element to [integrate](wikipedia://en/15532) a given function. Real computation theory investigates properties of such devices under the [idealizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_ideal) assumption of infinite precision.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Real computation"}

In [computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414), the theory of **real computation** deals with hypothetical computing machines using infinite-precision [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438). They are given this name because they operate on the set of real numbers. Within this theory, it is possible to prove interesting statements such as "The complement of the [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562) is only partially decidable."

:::

These hypothetical computing machines can be viewed as idealised [analog computers](wikipedia://en/2428) which operate on real numbers, whereas [digital computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_computer) are limited to [computable numbers](wikipedia://en/6206). They may be further subdivided into [differential](wikipedia://en/677191) and [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) models (digital computers, in this context, should be thought of as [topological](wikipedia://en/29954), at least insofar as their operation on computable reals is concerned[^o2]). Depending on the model chosen, this may enable real computers to solve problems that are inextricable on digital computers, or vice versa. For example, [Hava Siegelmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hava_Siegelmann)'s [neural nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_nets) can have noncomputable real weights, making them able to compute nonrecursive languages. [Claude Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693)'s idealized analog computer can only solve algebraic differential equations, while a digital computer can solve some transcendental equations as well. However this comparison is not entirely fair since in Claude Shannon's idealized analog computer computations are immediately done; i.e. computation is done in real time. Shannon's model can be adapted to cope with this problem.[^o3]

A canonical model of computation over the reals is the [Blum–Shub–Smale machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blum–Shub–Smale_machine) (BSS).

If real computation were [physically realizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physically_realizable), one could use it to solve [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) problems, and even [\#P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_P)-complete problems, in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time). Unlimited precision real numbers in the physical universe are prohibited by the [holographic principle](wikipedia://en/14286) and the [Bekenstein bound](wikipedia://en/1482061).[^o4]
