# 54 (number)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="54"}

**54** (**fifty-four**) is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) and [positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_%28math%29) [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) following [53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53_%28number%29) and preceding [55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/55_%28number%29). As a multiple of [2](wikipedia://en/64516) but not of [4](wikipedia://en/208161), 54 is an [oddly](wikipedia://en/2481457) [even number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_number) and a [composite number](wikipedia://en/82289).

:::

54 is related to the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) through [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261): the [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) of a 54 degree angle is half of the golden ratio. Also, 54 is a [regular number](wikipedia://en/5941535), and its even division of powers of 60 was useful to ancient mathematicians who used the [Assyro-Babylonian mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyro-Babylonian_mathematics) system.

## In mathematics {#In_mathematics}

### Number theory {#Number_theory}

*Image: Square-sum-54.png.* 54 as the sum of three positive squares

54 is an [abundant number](wikipedia://en/321831) because [the sum of its proper divisors](wikipedia://en/12374274) ([66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/66_%28number%29)), is greater than itself. Like all multiples of [6](wikipedia://en/208171),[^o7] 54 is equal to some of its proper divisors summed together,"54 can be expressed as: = 54." so it is also a [semiperfect number](wikipedia://en/321918). These proper divisors can be summed in various ways to express all positive integers smaller than 54, so 54 is a [practical number](wikipedia://en/1205310) as well. Additionally, as an integer for which the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) of all its [positive](wikipedia://en/7951270) [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) (including itself) is also an integer, 54 is an [arithmetic number](wikipedia://en/36551969).

### Trigonometry and the golden ratio {#Trigonometry_and_the_golden_ratio}

If the [complementary angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complementary_angle) of a triangle's corner is 54 [degrees](wikipedia://en/1195294), the [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) of that angle is half the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).[^o12] This is because the corresponding interior angle is equal to [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601)/5 [radians](wikipedia://en/26003) (or [36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36_%28number%29) degrees)."There are various ways to prove this, but the algebraic method will eventually show that \\sin{54} = \\cos{\\frac{\\pi}{5}} = \\frac{2\\sqrt{5}}{4} = \\frac{\\phi}{2}." If that triangle is [isoceles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoceles_triangle), the relationship with the golden ratio makes it a [golden triangle](wikipedia://en/4525417). The golden triangle is most readily found as the spikes on a regular [pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544).

### Regular number used in Assyro-Babylonian mathematics {#Regular_number_used_in_Assyro-Babylonian_mathematics}

As a [regular number](wikipedia://en/5941535), 54 is a divisor of many powers of 60."60 and its multiples are divisible by 54." This is an important property in [Assyro-Babylonian mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyro-Babylonian_mathematics) because that system uses a [sexagesimal](wikipedia://en/135316) (base-60) number system. In base 60, the [reciprocal](wikipedia://en/229940) of a regular number has a finite representation. Babylonian [computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_%28occupation%29) kept tables of these reciprocals to make their work more efficient. Using regular numbers simplifies multiplication and division in base 60 because dividing $a$ by $b$ can be done by multiplying $a$ by $b$'s reciprocal when $b$ is a regular number.[^o15][^o16]

For instance, division by 54 can be achieved in the Assyro-Babylonian system by multiplying by [4000](wikipedia://en/562999) because 60${}^{3}$ ÷ 54 = 60${}^{3}$ × (1/54) = 4000. In base 60, 4000 can be written as 1:6:40."1:6:40 = = 4000. This is the number written in Babylonian numerals: 𒐕𒐚𒐏." Because the Assyro-Babylonian system does not have a [symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_point) separating the [fractional](wikipedia://en/1704824) and integer parts of a number[^o17] and [does not have the concept](wikipedia://en/152323#Zero) of [0](wikipedia://en/34513) as a number,[^o18] it does not specify the power of the starting digit. Accordingly, 1/54 can also be written as 1:6:40."1:6:40 = 1×60 + 6×60 + 40×60 = 1/54. This is the number written in Babylonian numerals: 𒐕𒐚𒐏."[^o17] Therefore, the result of multiplication by 1:6:40 (4000) has the same Assyro-Babylonian representation as the result of multiplication by 1:6:40 (1/54). To convert from the former to the latter, the result's representation is interpreted as a number shifted three base-60 places to the right, reducing it by a factor of 60${}^{3}$."For example, = 121. The Assyro-Babylonian method is to calculate = 26136000. This result can be written in Babylonian numerals as 𒐖𒐕 (2:1), meaning . To complete the division by 54, one must divide by 60. Shifting the numeral three base-60 digits to the right divides the number by 60, so 𒐖𒐕 (2:1) is already the answer: = 121."

### Graph theory {#Graph_theory}

*Image: Ellingham-Horton 54-graph.svg.* The Ellingham–Horton 54-graph

The second [Ellingham–Horton graph](wikipedia://en/24175436) was published by [Mark N. Ellingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ellingham) and Joseph D. Horton in 1983; it is of order 54.[^o20] These [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806) provided further counterexamples to the conjecture of [W. T. Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021) that every [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) 3-connected [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph).[^o21] Horton disproved the conjecture some years earlier with the [Horton graph](wikipedia://en/24116567), but that was larger at 92 vertices.[^o22] The smallest known counter-example is now 50 vertices.[^o23]

## In literature {#In_literature}

In *[The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hitchhiker's_Guide_to_the_Galaxy)* by [Douglas Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Adams), the "[Answer to the Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Answer_to_the_Ultimate_Question_of_Life,_the_Universe,_and_Everything)" famously was [42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42_%28number%29).[^o25] Eventually, one character's unsuccessful attempt to divine the Ultimate Question elicited "What do you get if you multiply six by nine?"[^o26] The mathematical answer was 54, not 42. Some readers who were trying to find a deeper meaning in the passage soon noticed the fact was true in [base 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_13): the base-10 expression 54${}_{10}$ can be encoded as the base-13 expression 6${}_{13}$ × 9${}_{13}$ = 42${}_{13}$.[^o27] Adams said this was a coincidence.[^o28]
