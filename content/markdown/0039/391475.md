# Category mistake

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Category mistake"}

A **category mistake** (or **category error**, **categorical mistake**, or **mistake of category**) is a [semantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic) or [ontological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) error in which things belonging to a particular category are presented as if they belong to a different category,[^o2] or, alternatively, a property is ascribed to a thing that could not possibly have that property. An example is a person learning that the game of [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket) involves team spirit, and after being given a demonstration of each player's role, asking which player performs the "team spirit".[^o3]

:::

## History {#History}

[Al Martinich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloysius_Martinich) claims that the philosopher [Thomas Hobbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hobbes) was the first to discuss a propensity among philosophers to mistakenly combine words taken from different and incompatible categories.[^o5]

The term "category-mistake" was introduced by [Gilbert Ryle](wikipedia://en/1004355) in his book *[The Concept of Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Concept_of_Mind)* (1949) to remove what he argued to be a confusion over the nature of mind born from [Cartesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes) [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics).[^o6][^o7] Ryle argues that it is a mistake to treat the mind as an object made of an immaterial substance because predications of substance are not meaningful for a collection of dispositions and capacities.[^o8]

The phrase is introduced in the first chapter.[^o9] The first example is of a visitor to [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford). The visitor, upon viewing the [colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleges_of_the_University_of_Oxford) and [library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodleian_Library), reportedly inquires "But where is the University?" The visitor's mistake is presuming that a University is part of the category "units of physical infrastructure", rather than that of an "institution". In his second example, a child witnesses the march-past of a division of soldiers. After having had battalions, batteries, squadrons, etc. pointed out to him, the child asks when the division is going to appear. He is told that "the march-past was not a parade of battalions, batteries, squadrons *and* a division; it was a parade of the battalions, batteries and squadrons *of* a division" (Ryle's italics). His third example is of a foreigner being shown a cricket match. After having the batsmen, bowlers and fielders pointed out to him, the foreigner asks: "who is left to contribute the famous element of team-spirit?"[^o9] He goes on to argue that the [Cartesian dualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_dualism) of mind and body rests on a category mistake.

[Massimo Pigliucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Pigliucci), Professor of Philosophy at the [City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York), argues that the "[hard problem of consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_problem_of_consciousness)", as expressed by [David Chalmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Chalmers) and others, rests on a category mistake, in that explaining "experience" is being incorrectly treated as different from explaining the underlying biological processes which generate experience.[^o10]
