# Pierre Deligne

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Pierre Deligne"}

**Pierre René, Viscount Deligne** (; born 3 October 1944) is a Belgian mathematician. He is best known for work on the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705), leading to a complete proof in 1973. He is the winner of the 1978 [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859), 1988 [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize), 2008 [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) and 2013 [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Deligne was born in [Etterbeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etterbeek), attended school at [Athénée Adolphe Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athénée_Adolphe_Max) and studied at the [Université libre de Bruxelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_libre_de_Bruxelles) (ULB), writing a dissertation titled *Théorème de Lefschetz et critères de dégénérescence de suites spectrales* (Theorem of Lefschetz and criteria of degeneration of spectral sequences). He completed his doctorate at the [University of Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris-Sud) in [Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orsay) 1972 under the supervision of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042), with a thesis titled *Théorie de Hodge*.

## Career {#Career}

Starting in 1965, Deligne worked with Grothendieck at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) (IHÉS) near Paris, initially on the generalization within [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory) of [Zariski's main theorem](wikipedia://en/13969950). In 1968, he also worked with [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483); their work led to important results on the l-adic representations attached to [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000), and the conjectural [functional equations](wikipedia://en/364377) of [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283). Deligne also focused on topics in [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590). He introduced the concept of weights and tested them on objects in [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101). He also collaborated with [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594) on a new description of the [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) for curves. Their work came to be seen as an introduction to one form of the theory of [algebraic stacks](wikipedia://en/717377), and recently has been applied to questions arising from [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305).[^o5] But Deligne's most famous contribution was his proof of the third and last of the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705). This proof completed a programme initiated and largely developed by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) lasting for more than a decade. As a corollary he proved the celebrated [Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture](wikipedia://en/782173) for [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) of weight greater than one; weight one was proved in his work with Serre. Deligne's 1974 paper contains the first proof of the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705). Deligne's contribution was to supply the estimate of the [eigenvalues](wikipedia://en/2161429) of the [Frobenius endomorphism](wikipedia://en/1088270), considered the geometric analogue of the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125). It also led to a proof of the [Lefschetz hyperplane theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Lefschetz_theorem) and the old and new estimates of the classical exponential sums, among other applications. Deligne's 1980 paper contains a much more general version of the Riemann hypothesis.

From 1970 until 1984, Deligne was a permanent member of the IHÉS staff. During this time he did much important work outside of his work on algebraic geometry. In joint work with [George Lusztig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lusztig), Deligne applied [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) to construct representations of [finite groups of Lie type](wikipedia://en/1691490); with [Michael Rapoport](wikipedia://en/26629548), Deligne worked on the moduli spaces from the 'fine' arithmetic point of view, with application to [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000). He received a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1978. In 1984, Deligne moved to the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton.

### Hodge cycles {#Hodge_cycles}

In terms of the completion of some of the underlying Grothendieck program of research, he defined [absolute Hodge cycles](wikipedia://en/898010), as a surrogate for the missing and still largely conjectural theory of [motives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motive_%28mathematics%29). This idea allows one to get around the lack of knowledge of the [Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518), for some applications. The theory of [mixed Hodge structures](wikipedia://en/10297305), a powerful tool in algebraic geometry that generalizes classical Hodge theory, was created by applying weight filtration, Hironaka's [resolution of singularities](wikipedia://en/9914115) and other methods, which he then used to prove the Weil conjectures. He reworked the [Tannakian category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannakian_category) theory in his 1990 paper for the "Grothendieck Festschrift", employing [Beck's theorem](wikipedia://en/2082726) – the Tannakian category concept being the categorical expression of the linearity of the theory of motives as the ultimate [Weil cohomology](wikipedia://en/2960213). All this is part of the *yoga of weights*, uniting [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590) and the l-adic [Galois representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module). The [Shimura variety](wikipedia://en/10389861) theory is related, by the idea that such varieties should parametrize not just good (arithmetically interesting) families of Hodge structures, but actual motives. This theory is not yet a finished product, and more recent trends have used [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748) approaches.

### Perverse sheaves {#Perverse_sheaves}

With [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613), [Joseph Bernstein](wikipedia://en/5815383), and [Ofer Gabber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofer_Gabber), Deligne made definitive contributions to the theory of [perverse sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perverse_sheaves).[^o8] This theory plays an important role in the recent proof of the [fundamental lemma](wikipedia://en/25431009) by [Ngô Bảo Châu](wikipedia://en/11133659). It was also used by Deligne himself to greatly clarify the nature of the [Riemann–Hilbert correspondence](wikipedia://en/10478956), which extends [Hilbert's twenty-first problem](wikipedia://en/1965023) to higher dimensions. Prior to Deligne's paper, [Zoghman Mebkhout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoghman_Mebkhout)'s 1980 thesis and the work of [Masaki Kashiwara](wikipedia://en/4376209) through [D-modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-modules) theory (but published in the 80s) on the problem have appeared.

### Other works {#Other_works}

In 1974 at the IHÉS, Deligne's joint paper with [Phillip Griffiths](wikipedia://en/3660414), [John Morgan](wikipedia://en/8487966) and [Dennis Sullivan](wikipedia://en/7349608) on the real [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) of compact [Kähler manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_manifolds) was a major piece of work in complex differential geometry which settled several important questions of both classical and modern significance. The input from Weil conjectures, Hodge theory, variations of Hodge structures, and many geometric and topological tools were critical to its investigations. His work in complex [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763) generalized [Milnor maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milnor_fibration) into an algebraic setting and extended the [Picard-Lefschetz formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard-Lefschetz_formula) beyond their general format, generating a new method of research in this subject. His paper with [Ken Ribet](wikipedia://en/30876850) on abelian L-functions and their extensions to [Hilbert modular surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_modular_surface) and p-adic L-functions form an important part of his work in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177). Other important research achievements of Deligne include the notion of cohomological descent, motivic L-functions, mixed sheaves, nearby [vanishing cycles](wikipedia://en/5859204), central extensions of [reductive groups](wikipedia://en/1088386), geometry and topology of [braid groups](wikipedia://en/550138), providing the modern axiomatic definition of Shimura varieties, the work in collaboration with [George Mostow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mostow) on the examples of non-arithmetic lattices and monodromy of [hypergeometric differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_equation) in two- and three-dimensional complex [hyperbolic spaces](wikipedia://en/318742), etc.

## Awards {#Awards}

He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1978, the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize) in 1988, the [Balzan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balzan_Prize) in 2004, the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) in 2008, and the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2013, "for seminal contributions to algebraic geometry and for their transformative impact on number theory, representation theory, and related fields". He was elected a foreign member of the Academie des Sciences de Paris in 1978.

In 2006 he was ennobled by the Belgian king as [viscount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_nobility#Viscount).[^o11]

In 2009, Deligne was elected a foreign member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences)[^o12] and a residential member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society).[^o13] He is a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o14]

## Concepts named after Deligne {#Concepts_named_after_Deligne}

The following mathematical concepts are named after Deligne:

- Brylinski–Deligne extensions
- Deligne torus
- [Deligne–Lusztig theory](wikipedia://en/10574794)
- [Deligne–Mumford moduli space of curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deligne–Mumford_moduli_space_of_curves)
- [Deligne–Mumford stacks](wikipedia://en/40639179)
- [Fourier–Deligne transform](wikipedia://en/26250647)
- [Deligne cohomology](wikipedia://en/31615715)
- Deligne [motive](wikipedia://en/430680)[^o28]
- **Deligne tensor product of abelian categories** (denoted $\boxtimes$)[^o29]
- [Deligne's theorem](wikipedia://en/10297305#Mixed_Hodge_structure_in_cohomology_%28Deligne's_theorem%29)
- [Langlands–Deligne local constant](wikipedia://en/32103592)
- [Weil–Deligne group](wikipedia://en/6375618#Weil–Deligne_group)

Additionally, many different conjectures in mathematics have been called the **Deligne conjecture**:

- *[Deligne's conjecture on Hochschild cohomology](wikipedia://en/73028060)*.
- The *[Deligne conjecture on special values of L-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deligne_conjecture_on_special_values_of_L-functions)* is a formulation of the hope for [algebraicity](wikipedia://en/1158) of *L*(*n*) where *L* is an [L-function](wikipedia://en/245283) and *n* is an integer in some set depending on *L*.
- There is a *Deligne conjecture on 1-motives* arising in the theory of [motives](wikipedia://en/430680) in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).
- There is a *Gross–Deligne conjecture* in the theory of [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183).
- There is a *Deligne conjecture on [monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250)*, also known as the *weight monodromy conjecture*, or purity conjecture for the monodromy filtration.
- There is a *[Deligne conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E7½_%28Lie_algebra%29)* in the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [exceptional Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exceptional_Lie_group).
- There is a conjecture named the Deligne–Grothendieck conjecture for the discrete [Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/247261) in [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) 0.
- There is a conjecture named the Deligne–Milnor conjecture for the differential interpretation of a formula of Milnor for Milnor fibres, as part of the extension of nearby cycles and their Euler numbers.
- The Deligne–Milne conjecture is formulated as part of motives and Tannakian categories.
- There is a *Deligne–Langlands conjecture* of historical importance in relation with the development of the [Langlands philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_philosophy).
- *Deligne's conjecture on the Lefschetz trace formula*[^o30] (now called Fujiwara's theorem for equivariant correspondences).[^o31]
