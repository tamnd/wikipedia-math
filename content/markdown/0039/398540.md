# Euler product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Euler product"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), an **Euler product** is an expansion of a [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) into an [infinite product](wikipedia://en/26382006) indexed by [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666). The original such product was given for [the sum of all positive integers raised to a certain power](wikipedia://en/5605079) as proven by [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902). This series and its continuation to the entire complex plane would later become known as the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In general, if $a$ is a bounded [multiplicative function](wikipedia://en/20059), then the Dirichlet series

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{a(n)}{n^s}$$

is equal to

$$\prod_{p\in\mathbb{P}} P(p, s) \quad \text{for } \operatorname{Re}(s) >1 .$$

where the product is taken over prime numbers $p$, and $P(p, s)$ is the sum

$$\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{a(p^k)}{p^{ks}} = 1 + \frac{a(p)}{p^s} + \frac{a(p^2)}{p^{2s}} + \frac{a(p^3)}{p^{3s}} + \cdots$$

In fact, if we consider these as formal [generating functions](wikipedia://en/160993), the existence of such a *formal* Euler product expansion is a necessary and sufficient condition that $a(n)$ be multiplicative: this says exactly that $a(n)$ is the product of the $a(p^{k})$ whenever $n$ factors as the product of the powers $p^{k}$ of distinct primes $p$.

An important special case is that in which $a(n)$ is [totally multiplicative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_multiplicative), so that $P(p, s)$ is a [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630). Then

$$P(p, s)=\frac{1}{1-\frac{a(p)}{p^s}},$$

as is the case for the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809), where $a(n) = 1$, and more generally for [Dirichlet characters](wikipedia://en/174515).

:::

## Convergence {#Convergence}

In practice all the important cases are such that the infinite series and infinite product expansions are [absolutely convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_convergent) in some region

$$\operatorname{Re}(s) > C,$$

that is, in some right [half-plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-plane) in the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). This already gives some information, since the infinite product, to converge, must give a non-zero value; hence the function given by the infinite series is not zero in such a half-plane.

In the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) it is typical to have Euler products with quadratic polynomials in the denominator here. The general [Langlands philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_philosophy) includes a comparable explanation of the connection of polynomials of degree $m$, and the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) for $\operatorname{GL}_{m}$.

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The following examples will use the notation $\mathbb{P}$ for the set of all primes, that is:

$$\mathbb{P}=\{p \in \mathbb{N}\mid p\text{ is prime}\}.$$

The Euler product attached to the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) $\zeta(s)$, also using the sum of the geometric series, is

$$\begin{aligned} \prod_{p\, \in\, \mathbb{P}} \left(\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{p^s}}\right) &= \prod_{p\ \in\ \mathbb{P}} \left(\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{1}{p^{ks}}\right) \\ &= \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s} = \zeta(s). \end{aligned}$$

while for the [Liouville function](wikipedia://en/104463) $\lambda(n) = (-1)^{\Omega(n)}$, it is

$$\prod_{p\, \in\, \mathbb{P}} \left(\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{p^s}}\right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda(n)}{n^{s}} = \frac{\zeta(2s)}{\zeta(s)}.$$

Using their reciprocals, two Euler products for the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961) $\mu(n)$ are

$$\prod_{p\, \in\, \mathbb{P}} \left(1-\frac{1}{p^s}\right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\mu (n)}{n^{s}} = \frac{1}{\zeta(s)}$$

and

$$\prod_{p\, \in\, \mathbb{P}} \left(1+\frac{1}{p^s}\right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{|\mu(n)|}{n^{s}} = \frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(2s)}.$$

Taking the ratio of these two gives

$$\prod_{p\, \in\, \mathbb{P}} \left(\frac{1+\frac{1}{p^s}}{1-\frac{1}{p^s}}\right) = \prod_{p\, \in\, \mathbb{P}} \left(\frac{p^s+1}{p^s-1}\right) = \frac{\zeta(s)^2}{\zeta(2s)}.$$

Since for even values of $s$ the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$ has an analytic expression in terms of a *rational* multiple of $\pi^{s}$, then for even exponents, this infinite product evaluates to a rational number. For example, since $\zeta(2) = \tfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}$, $\zeta(4) = \tfrac{\pi^{4}}{90}$, and $\zeta(8) = \tfrac{\pi^{8}}{9450}$, then

$$\begin{aligned} \prod_{p\, \in\, \mathbb{P}} \left(\frac{p^2+1}{p^2-1}\right) &= \frac53 \cdot \frac{10}{8} \cdot \frac{26}{24} \cdot \frac{50}{48} \cdot \frac{122}{120} \cdots &= \frac{\zeta(2)^2}{\zeta(4)} &= \frac52, \\[6pt] \prod_{p\, \in\, \mathbb{P}} \left(\frac{p^4+1}{p^4-1}\right) &= \frac{17}{15} \cdot \frac{82}{80} \cdot \frac{626}{624} \cdot \frac{2402}{2400} \cdots &= \frac{\zeta(4)^2}{\zeta(8)} &= \frac76, \end{aligned}$$

and so on, with the first result known by [Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan). This family of infinite products is also equivalent to

$$\prod_{p\, \in\, \mathbb{P}} \left(1+\frac{2}{p^s}+\frac{2}{p^{2s}}+\cdots\right) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{2^{\omega(n)}}{n^s} = \frac{\zeta(s)^2}{\zeta(2s)},$$

where $\omega(n)$ counts the number of distinct prime factors of $n$, and $2^{\omega(n)}$ is the number of [square-free](wikipedia://en/29525) divisors.

If $\chi(n)$ is a Dirichlet character of conductor $N$, so that $\chi$ is totally multiplicative and $\chi(n)$ only depends on $n \bmod N$, and $\chi(n) = 0$ if $n$ is not [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) to $N$, then

$$\prod_{p\, \in\, \mathbb{P}} \frac{1}{1- \frac{\chi(p)}{p^s}} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\chi(n)}{n^s}.$$

Here it is convenient to omit the primes $p$ dividing the conductor $N$ from the product.

:::

## Notable constants {#Notable_constants}

Many well known [constants](wikipedia://en/24758132) have Euler product expansions.

The [Leibniz formula for $\pi$](wikipedia://en/1301559)

$$\frac{\pi}{4} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{2n+1} = 1 - \frac13 + \frac15 - \frac17 + \cdots$$

can be interpreted as a [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) using the (unique) Dirichlet character modulo 4, and converted to an Euler product of [superparticular ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superparticular_number) (fractions where numerator and denominator differ by 1):

$$\frac{\pi}{4} = \left(\prod_{p\equiv 1\pmod 4}\frac{p}{p-1}\right)\left( \prod_{p\equiv 3\pmod 4}\frac{p}{p+1}\right)=\frac34 \cdot \frac54 \cdot \frac78 \cdot \frac{11}{12} \cdot \frac{13}{12} \cdots,$$

where each numerator is a prime number and each denominator is the nearest multiple of 4.[^o8]

Dividing the Euler product for $\zeta(2)=\frac{\pi^2}{6}$ by the previous product, known as [Basel problem](wikipedia://en/490303), one finds that

$$\frac{\pi}{2} = \left(\prod_{p\equiv 1\pmod 4}\frac{p}{p+1}\right)\left( \prod_{p\equiv 3\pmod 4}\frac{p}{p-1}\right).$$

Taking the ratio of the previous two products gives

$$2 = \left(\prod_{p\equiv 1\pmod 4}\frac{p-1}{p+1}\right)\left( \prod_{p\equiv 3\pmod 4}\frac{p+1}{p-1}\right).$$

The infinite products must be taken in order of increasing primes.

Other Euler products for known constants include:

- The [Hardy–Littlewood twin prime constant](wikipedia://en/41997#First_Hardy–Littlewood_conjecture):

$$\prod_{p>2} \left(1 - \frac{1}{\left(p-1\right)^2}\right) = 0.660161\ldots$$

- The [Landau–Ramanujan constant](wikipedia://en/620712):

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\pi}{4} \prod_{p \equiv 1\pmod 4} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p^2}\right)^\frac12 &= 0.764223\ldots \\[6pt] \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \prod_{p \equiv 3\pmod 4} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p^2}\right)^{-\frac12} &= 0.764223\ldots \end{aligned}$$

- [Murata's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murata's_constant) :

$$\prod_p \left(1 + \frac{1}{\left(p-1\right)^2}\right) = 2.826419\ldots$$

- The [strongly carefree constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongly_carefree_constant) $\times\zeta(2)^{2}$ :

$$\prod_{p} \left(1 - \frac{1}{\left(p+1\right)^2}\right) = 0.775883\ldots$$

- [Artin's constant](wikipedia://en/2085185) :

$$\prod_p \left(1 - \frac{1}{p(p-1)}\right) = 0.373955\ldots$$

- [Landau's totient constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landau's_totient_constant) :

$$\prod_p \left(1 + \frac{1}{p(p-1)}\right) = \frac{315}{2\pi^4}\zeta(3) = 1.943596\ldots$$

- The [carefree constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carefree_constant) $\times\zeta(2)$ :

$$\prod_p \left(1 - \frac{1}{p(p+1)}\right) = 0.704442\ldots$$

and its reciprocal :

$$\prod_p \left(1 + \frac{1}{p^2+p-1}\right) = 1.419562\ldots$$

- The [Feller–Tornier constant](wikipedia://en/53626824) :

$$\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2} \prod_p \left(1 - \frac{2}{p^2}\right) = 0.661317\ldots$$

- The [quadratic class number constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_class_number_constant) :

$$\prod_p \left(1 - \frac{1}{p^2(p+1)}\right) = 0.881513\ldots$$

- The [totient summatory constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totient_summatory_constant) :

$$\prod_p \left(1 + \frac{1}{p^2(p-1)}\right) = 1.339784\ldots$$

- [Sarnak's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnak's_constant) :

$$\prod_{p>2} \left(1 - \frac{p+2}{p^3}\right) = 0.723648\ldots$$

- The [carefree constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carefree_constant) :

$$\prod_{p} \left(1 - \frac{2p-1}{p^3}\right) = 0.428249\ldots$$

- The [strongly carefree constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongly_carefree_constant) :

$$\prod_p \left(1 - \frac{3p-2}{p^3}\right) = 0.286747\ldots$$

- [Stephens' constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephens'_constant) :

$$\prod_p \left(1 - \frac{p}{p^3-1}\right) = 0.575959\ldots$$

- [Barban's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barban's_constant) :

$$\prod_p \left(1 + \frac{3p^2-1}{p(p+1)\left(p^2-1\right)}\right) = 2.596536\ldots$$

- [Taniguchi's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniguchi's_constant) :

$$\prod_p \left(1 - \frac{3}{p^3}+\frac{2}{p^4}+\frac{1}{p^5}-\frac{1}{p^6}\right) = 0.678234\ldots$$

- The [Heath-Brown and Moroz constant](wikipedia://en/5405595) :

$$\prod_p \left(1 - \frac{1}{p}\right)^7 \left(1 + \frac{7p+1}{p^2}\right) = 0.0013176\ldots$$
