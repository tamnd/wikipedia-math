# Projective line

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Projective line"}

In [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) more generally, a **projective line** is, roughly speaking, the extension of a usual [line](wikipedia://en/946975) by a point called a *[point at infinity](wikipedia://en/403139)*. The statement and the proof of many theorems of geometry are simplified by the resulting elimination of special cases; for example, two distinct projective lines in a [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350) meet in exactly one point (there is no "parallel" case).

:::

There are many equivalent ways to formally define a projective line; one of the most common is to define a projective line over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K*, commonly denoted **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*), as the set of one-dimensional [subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357) of a two-dimensional *K*-[vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). This definition is a special instance of the general definition of a [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135).

The projective line over the [reals](wikipedia://en/20646438) is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470); see *[Real projective line](wikipedia://en/46379102)* for details.

## Homogeneous coordinates {#Homogeneous_coordinates}

An arbitrary point in the projective line **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*) may be represented by an [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) of *[homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316)*, which take the form of a pair

$$[x_1 : x_2]$$

of elements of *K* that are not both zero. Two such pairs are [equivalent](wikipedia://en/9259) if they differ by an overall nonzero factor *λ*:

$$[x_1 : x_2] \sim [\lambda x_1 : \lambda x_2].$$

## Line extended by a point at infinity {#Line_extended_by_a_point_at_infinity}

The projective line may be identified with the line *K* extended by a [point at infinity](wikipedia://en/403139). More precisely, the line *K* may be identified with the subset of **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*) given by

$$\left\{[x : 1] \in \mathbf P^1(K) \mid x \in K\right\}.$$

This subset covers all points in **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*) except one, which is called the *point at infinity*:

$$\infty = [1 : 0].$$

This allows to extend the arithmetic on *K* to **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*) by the formulas

$$\frac {1}{0}=\infty,\qquad \frac {1}{\infty}=0,$$

$$x\cdot \infty = \infty \quad \text{if}\quad x\not= 0$$

$$x+ \infty = \infty \quad \text{if}\quad x\not= \infty$$

Translating this arithmetic in terms of homogeneous coordinates gives, when 0 : 0 does not occur:

$$[x_1 : x_2] + [y_1 : y_2] = [(x_1 y_2 + y_1 x_2) : x_2 y_2],$$

$$[x_1 : x_2] \cdot [y_1 : y_2] = [x_1 y_1 : x_2 y_2],$$

$$[x_1 : x_2]^{-1} = [x_2 : x_1].$$

## Examples {#Examples}

### Real projective line {#Real_projective_line}

The projective line over the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) is called the **real projective line**. It may also be thought of as the line *K* together with an idealised *[point at infinity](wikipedia://en/403139)* ∞; the point connects to both ends of *K* creating a closed loop or topological circle.

An example is obtained by projecting points in **R**<sup>2</sup> onto the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) and then [identifying](wikipedia://en/237213) [diametrically opposite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diametrically_opposite) points. In terms of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) we can take the quotient by the [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) $\{1, -1\}$ under multiplication.

Compare the [extended real number line](wikipedia://en/51698), which distinguishes ∞ and −∞.

### Complex projective line: the Riemann sphere {#Complex_projective_line:_the_Riemann_sphere}

Adding a point at infinity to the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) results in a space that is topologically a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859). Hence the complex projective line is also known as the **[Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799)** (or sometimes the *Gauss sphere*). It is in constant use in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) theory, as the simplest example of a [compact Riemann surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Riemann_surface).

### For a finite field {#For_a_finite_field}

The projective line over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) *F*<sub>*q*</sub> of *q* elements has *q* + 1 points. In all other respects it is no different from projective lines defined over other types of fields. In the terms of homogeneous coordinates \[*x* : *y*\], *q* of these points have the form:

$[a : 1]$ for each $a$ in $F_{q}$,

and the remaining [point *at infinity*](wikipedia://en/403139) may be represented as 1 : 0.

:::

## Symmetry group {#Symmetry_group}

Quite generally, the group of [homographies](wikipedia://en/3051962) with [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) in *K* acts on the projective line **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*). This [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) is [transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29#Types_of_actions), so that **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*) is a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) for the group, often written PGL<sub>2</sub>(*K*) to emphasise the projective nature of these transformations. *Transitivity* says that there exists a homography that will transform any point *Q* to any other point *R*. The *point at infinity* on **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*) is therefore an *artifact* of choice of coordinates: [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316)

$$[X : Y] \sim [\lambda X : \lambda Y]$$

express a one-dimensional subspace by a single non-zero point (*X*, *Y*) lying in it, but the symmetries of the projective line can move the point ∞ = 1 : 0 to any other, and it is in no way distinguished.

Much more is true, in that some transformation can take any given [distinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinct_%28mathematics%29) points *Q*<sub>*i*</sub> for *i* = 1, 2, 3 to any other 3-tuple *R*<sub>*i*</sub> of distinct points (*triple transitivity*). This amount of specification 'uses up' the three dimensions of PGL<sub>2</sub>(*K*); in other words, the group action is [sharply 3-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29). The computational aspect of this is the [cross-ratio](wikipedia://en/401767). Indeed, a generalized converse is true: a sharply 3-transitive group action is always (isomorphic to) a generalized form of a PGL<sub>2</sub>(*K*) action on a projective line, replacing "field" by "KT-field" (generalizing the inverse to a weaker kind of involution), and "PGL" by a corresponding generalization of projective linear maps.[^o10]

## As algebraic curve {#As_algebraic_curve}

The projective line is a fundamental example of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260). From the point of view of algebraic geometry, **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*) is a [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) curve of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) 0. If *K* is [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed), it is the unique such curve over *K*, up to [rational equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_equivalence). In general a (non-singular) curve of genus 0 is rationally equivalent over *K* to a [conic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic) *C*, which is itself birationally equivalent to projective line if and only if *C* has a point defined over *K*; geometrically such a point *P* can be used as origin to make explicit the birational equivalence.

The [function field](wikipedia://en/2783843) of the projective line is the field *K*(*T*) of [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) over *K*, in a single indeterminate *T*. The [field automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_automorphism) of *K*(*T*) over *K* are precisely the group PGL<sub>2</sub>(*K*) discussed above.

Any function field *K*(*V*) of an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *V* over *K*, other than a single point, has a subfield isomorphic with *K*(*T*). From the point of view of [birational geometry](wikipedia://en/382733), this means that there will be a [rational map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_map) from *V* to **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*), that is not constant. The image will omit only finitely many points of **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*), and the inverse image of a typical point *P* will be of dimension dim *V* − 1. This is the beginning of methods in algebraic geometry that are inductive on dimension. The rational maps play a role analogous to the [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857) of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), and indeed in the case of [compact Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Riemann_surface) the two concepts coincide.

If *V* is now taken to be of dimension 1, we get a picture of a typical algebraic curve *C* presented 'over' **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*). Assuming *C* is non-singular (which is no loss of generality starting with *K*(*C*)), it can be shown that such a rational map from *C* to **P**<sup>1</sup>(*K*) will in fact be everywhere defined. (That is not the case if there are singularities, since for example a *[double point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_point)* where a curve *crosses itself* may give an indeterminate result after a rational map.) This gives a picture in which the main geometric feature is [ramification](wikipedia://en/384644).

Many curves, for example [hyperelliptic curves](wikipedia://en/499361), may be presented abstractly, as [ramified covers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramified_cover) of the projective line. According to the [Riemann–Hurwitz formula](wikipedia://en/497413), the genus then depends only on the type of ramification.

A **rational curve** is a curve that is [birationally equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birational_equivalence) to a projective line (see [rational variety](wikipedia://en/865939)); its [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) is 0. A [rational normal curve](wikipedia://en/2380117) in projective space **P**<sup>*n*</sup> is a rational curve that lies in no proper linear subspace; it is known that there is only one example (up to projective equivalence),[^o12] given parametrically in homogeneous coordinates as

\[1 : *t* : *t*<sup>2</sup> : ... : *t*<sup>*n*</sup>\].

See *[Twisted cubic](wikipedia://en/1547121)* for the first interesting case.
