# Wedderburn–Artin theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wedderburn–Artin theorem"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **Wedderburn–Artin theorem** is a [classification theorem](wikipedia://en/4576710) for [semisimple rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring) and [semisimple algebras](wikipedia://en/3876151). The theorem states that a(n Artinian) semisimple ring *R* is isomorphic to the [product](wikipedia://en/25063) of finitely many $n_{i}$-by-$n_{i}$ [matrix rings](wikipedia://en/487541) over [division rings](wikipedia://en/9067) $D_{i}$, for some integers $n_{i}$, both of which are uniquely determined up to permutation of the index $i$. In particular, any [simple](wikipedia://en/443387) left or right [Artinian ring](wikipedia://en/489273) is isomorphic to an *n*-by-*n* [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541) over a [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067) *D*, where both *n* and *D* are uniquely determined.[^n1]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

Let $R$ be a (Artinian) [semisimple ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring). Then the Wedderburn–Artin theorem states that $R$ is isomorphic to the product of finitely many $n_{i}$-by-$n_{i}$ [matrix rings](wikipedia://en/487541) $M_{n_i}(D_i)$ over [division rings](wikipedia://en/9067) $D_{i}$, for some integers $n_{i}$, both of which are uniquely determined up to permutation of the index $i$.

There is also a version of the Wedderburn–Artin theorem for algebras over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $k$. If $R$ is a finite-dimensional semisimple $k$-algebra, then each $D_{i}$ in the above statement is a finite-dimensional [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625) over $k$. The [center](wikipedia://en/53796787) of each $D_{i}$ need not be $k$; it could be a [finite extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_field_extension) of $k$.

Note that if $R$ is a finite-dimensional simple algebra over a division ring *E*, *D* need not be contained in *E*. For example, matrix rings over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) are finite-dimensional simple algebras over the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438).

:::

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

There are various [proofs](wikipedia://en/82285) of the Wedderburn–Artin theorem.[^n2][^n3] A common modern one[^n4] takes the following approach.

Suppose the ring $R$ is semisimple. Then the right $R$-module $R_R$ is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of [simple modules](wikipedia://en/29430) (which are the same as minimal right [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) of $R$). Write this direct sum as

$$R_R \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{i=1}^m I_i^{\oplus n_i}$$

where the $I_i$ are mutually nonisomorphic simple right $R$-modules, the $i$th one appearing with multiplicity $n_i$. This gives an isomorphism of [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) rings

$$\mathrm{End}(R_R) \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{i=1}^m \mathrm{End}\big(I_i^{\oplus n_i}\big)$$

and we can identify $\mathrm{End}\big(I_i^{\oplus n_i}\big)$ with a ring of [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859)

$$\mathrm{End}\big(I_i^{\oplus n_i}\big) \;\cong\; M_{n_i}\big(\mathrm{End}(I_i)\big)$$

where the endomorphism ring $\mathrm{End}(I_i)$ of $I_i$ is a division ring by [Schur's lemma](wikipedia://en/521696), because $I_i$ is simple. Since $R \cong \mathrm{End}(R_R)$ we conclude

$$R \;\cong\; \bigoplus_{i=1}^m M_{n_i}\big(\mathrm{End}(I_i)\big) \,.$$

Here we used right modules because $R \cong \mathrm{End}(R_R)$; if we used left modules $R$ would be isomorphic to the [opposite algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposite_algebra) of $\mathrm{End}({}_R R)$, but the proof would still go through. To see this proof in a larger context, see [Decomposition of a module](wikipedia://en/61406091). For the proof of an important special case, see [Simple Artinian ring](wikipedia://en/489273#Simple_Artinian_ring).

:::

## Consequences {#Consequences}

Since a finite-dimensional algebra over a field is Artinian, the Wedderburn–Artin theorem implies that every finite-dimensional [simple algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_algebra) over a field is isomorphic to an *n*-by-*n* [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541) over some finite-dimensional division algebra *D* over $k$, where both *n* and *D* are uniquely determined.[^n1] This was shown by [Joseph Wedderburn](wikipedia://en/2969494). [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) later generalized this result to the case of simple left or right [Artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273).

Since the only finite-dimensional division algebra over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) is the field itself, the Wedderburn–Artin theorem has strong consequences in this case. Let $R$ be a [semisimple ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring) that is a finite-dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field $k$. Then $R$ is a finite product $\textstyle \prod_{i=1}^r M_{n_i}(k)$ where the $n_i$ are positive integers and $M_{n_i}(k)$ is the algebra of $n_i \times n_i$ matrices over $k$.

Furthermore, the Wedderburn–Artin theorem reduces the problem of classifying finite-dimensional [central simple algebras](wikipedia://en/404082) over a field $k$ to the problem of classifying finite-dimensional **central** division algebras over $k$: that is, division algebras over $k$ whose center is $k$. It implies that any finite-dimensional central simple algebra over $k$ is isomorphic to a matrix algebra $\textstyle M_{n}(D)$ where $D$ is a finite-dimensional central division algebra over $k$.
