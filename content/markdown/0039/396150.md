# Iwasawa theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Iwasawa theory"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Iwasawa theory** is the study of objects of arithmetic interest over infinite [towers](wikipedia://en/28194169) of [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field). It began as a [Galois module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module) theory of [ideal class groups](wikipedia://en/150907), initiated by (txt) (岩澤 健吉), as part of the theory of [cyclotomic fields](wikipedia://en/30872462). In the early 1970s, [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) considered generalizations of Iwasawa theory to [abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152). More recently (early 1990s), [Ralph Greenberg](wikipedia://en/36992735) has proposed an Iwasawa theory for [motives](wikipedia://en/430680).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Iwasawa worked with so-called $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extensions: infinite extensions of a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) $F$ with [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) $\Gamma$ isomorphic to the additive group of [p-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integer) for some prime *p*. (These were called $\Gamma$-extensions in early papers.[^o5]) Every closed subgroup of $\Gamma$ is of the form $\Gamma^{p^n},$ so by Galois theory, a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension $F_\infty/F$ is the same thing as a tower of fields

$$F=F_0 \subset F_1 \subset F_2 \subset \cdots \subset F_\infty$$

such that $\operatorname{Gal}(F_n/F)\cong \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}.$ Iwasawa studied classical Galois modules over $F_n$ by asking questions about the structure of modules over $F_\infty.$

More generally, Iwasawa theory asks questions about the structure of Galois modules over extensions with Galois group a [p-adic Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_Lie_group).

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Let $p$ be a prime number and let $K=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_p)$ be the field generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ by the $p$th roots of unity. Iwasawa considered the following tower of number fields:

$$K = K_{0} \subset K_{1} \subset \cdots \subset K_{\infty},$$

where $K_n$ is the field generated by adjoining to $K$ the *p*<sup>*n*+1</sup>-st roots of unity and

$$K_\infty = \bigcup K_n.$$

The fact that $\operatorname{Gal}(K_n/K)\simeq \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ implies, by infinite Galois theory, that $\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K) \simeq \varprojlim_n \mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}_p.$ In order to get an interesting Galois module, Iwasawa took the ideal class group of $K_n$, and let $I_n$ be its *p*-torsion part. There are [norm](wikipedia://en/450555) maps $I_m\to I_n$ whenever $m>n$, and this gives us the data of an [inverse system](wikipedia://en/15109). If we set

$$I = \varprojlim I_n,$$

then it is not hard to see from the inverse limit construction that $I$ is a module over $\mathbb{Z}_p.$ In fact, $I$ is a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) over the [Iwasawa algebra](wikipedia://en/31746215) $\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma]]$. This is a [2-dimensional](wikipedia://en/56079), [regular local ring](wikipedia://en/506959), and this makes it possible to describe modules over it. From this description it is possible to recover information about the *p*-part of the class group of $K.$

The motivation here is that the *p*-torsion in the ideal class group of $K$ had already been identified by [Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117) as the main obstruction to the direct proof of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953).

:::

## Connections with p-adic analysis {#Connections_with_p-adic_analysis}

From this beginning in the 1950s, a substantial theory has been built up. A fundamental connection was noticed between the module theory, and the [p-adic L-functions](wikipedia://en/22059815) that were defined in the 1960s by [Kubota](wikipedia://en/19487263) and Leopoldt. The latter begin from the [Bernoulli numbers](wikipedia://en/4964), and use [interpolation](wikipedia://en/14569) to define p-adic analogues of the [Dirichlet L-functions](wikipedia://en/400092). It became clear that the theory had prospects of moving ahead finally from Kummer's century-old results on [regular primes](wikipedia://en/323689).

Iwasawa formulated the [main conjecture of Iwasawa theory](wikipedia://en/22091332) as an assertion that two methods of defining p-adic L-functions (by module theory, by interpolation) should coincide, as far as that was well-defined. This was proved by (Mazur Wiles 1984) for $\mathbb{Q}$ and for all [totally real number fields](wikipedia://en/312853) by (Wiles 1990). These proofs were modeled upon [Ken Ribet](wikipedia://en/30876850)'s proof of the converse to Herbrand's theorem (the so-called [Herbrand–Ribet theorem](wikipedia://en/1078637)).

[Karl Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Rubin) found a more elementary proof of the Mazur-Wiles theorem by using [Kolyvagin](wikipedia://en/4559839)'s [Euler systems](wikipedia://en/1052176), described in (Lang 1990) and (Washington 1997), and later proved other generalizations of the main conjecture for imaginary quadratic fields.

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The Galois group of the infinite tower, the starting field, and the sort of arithmetic module studied can all be varied. In each case, there is a *main conjecture* linking the tower to a *p*-adic L-function.

In 2002, [Christopher Skinner](wikipedia://en/59264582) and [Eric Urban](wikipedia://en/63270274) claimed a proof of a *main conjecture* for [GL](wikipedia://en/113564)(2). In 2010, they posted a preprint (Skinner Urban 2010).

:::
