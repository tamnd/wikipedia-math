# Value distribution theory of holomorphic functions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Value distribution theory of holomorphic functions"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **value distribution theory of holomorphic functions** is a division of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). The purpose of the theory is to provide quantitative measures of the number of times a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) *f*(*z*) assumes a [value](wikipedia://en/4095147) *a*, as *z* grows in size, refining the [Picard theorem](wikipedia://en/674050) on behaviour close to an [essential singularity](wikipedia://en/80862). The theory exists for [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) (and [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857)) of one complex variable *z*, or of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables).

:::

In the case of one variable, the term **[Nevanlinna theory](wikipedia://en/393135)**, after [Rolf Nevanlinna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Nevanlinna), is also common. The now-classical theory received renewed interest when [Paul Vojta](wikipedia://en/1802312) suggested some analogies to the problem of integral solutions to [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109). These turned out to involve some close parallels and to lead to fresh points of view on the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture) and related questions.
