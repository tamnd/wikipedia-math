# David Mumford

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Mumford"}

**David Bryant Mumford** (born 11 June 1937) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his work in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and then for research into [vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision) and [pattern theory](wikipedia://en/3709680). He won the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) and was a [MacArthur Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellow). In 2010 he was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science). He is currently a university professor emeritus in the Division of Applied Mathematics at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Mumford was born in [Worth, West Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worth,_West_Sussex) in [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), of an English father and American mother. His father [William Bryant Mumford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bryant_Mumford) was educated at [Manchester Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Grammar_School) and took the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge);[^o6] as his mother Edith Emily Read had done at [Girton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College).[^o8] He started an experimental school in the colonial [Tanganyika Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanganyika_Territory) and at the time of his death in 1951 worked in the [United Nations Department of Public Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Department_of_Public_Information).[^o9][^o10] He married in 1933 Grace Schiott of [Southport, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southport,_Connecticut), and the couple had five children.[^o11]

Mumford first went to [Unquowa School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unquowa_School).[^o12] He attended [Phillips Exeter Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Exeter_Academy), where he received a [Westinghouse Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Science_Talent_Search) prize for his relay-based computer project.[^o13][^o14] Mumford then went to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he became a student of [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352). At Harvard, he became a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) in 1955 and 1956.[^o15] He completed his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in 1961, with a thesis entitled *Existence of the moduli scheme for curves of any genus*.

After a career at Harvard, Mumford moved to [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) in 1996, concentrating on [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969).[^o17][^o18]

## Research {#Research}

### Algebraic geometry {#Algebraic_geometry}

Mumford's work in geometry combined traditional geometric insights with contemporary algebraic techniques.

#### Moduli spaces {#Moduli_spaces}

Mumford published on [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609), with a theory summed up in his book *Geometric Invariant Theory*.

At the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1974, [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) said:

> Mumford's major work has been a tremendously successful multi-pronged attack on problems of the existence and structure of varieties of moduli, that is, varieties whose points parametrize isomorphism classes of some type of geometric object.[^o22]

*Image: ModularGroup-FundamentalDomain.svg.* Diagram of a [fundamental domain](wikipedia://en/469365) (in grey) for the [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019) acting on the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826), the classical picture of moduli for curves of genus 1

The [moduli space of curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_space_of_curves) of a given [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_of_a_curve) *g* was already considered in [complex algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_algebraic_geometry) during the 19th century. When *g* = 1 the number of moduli is 1 also, the term coming from the [elliptic modulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_modulus) function of the theory of [Jacobi elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/450004). [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) contributed the formula, when *g* ≥ 2, for the number of moduli, namely 3*g*−3, a consequence, in [deformation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory), of the [Riemann-Roch theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann-Roch_theorem).[^o23]

The theory of [abstract varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_varieties), as Mumford showed, can be applied to provide an [existence theorem](wikipedia://en/251177) for moduli spaces of curves, over any [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018). It therefore answers the question of in what sense there is a geometric object, with an algebraic definition, that parametrizes algebraic curves, so generalizing the [modular curves](wikipedia://en/669552), having the predicted dimension. In a summary provided by [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084), making use of [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory), the steps are:[^o24]

1. Construction of a certain [closed subscheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_subscheme) *H* of the [Hilbert scheme](wikipedia://en/6612581) of the projective space of dimension *n* over the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613).
1. Construction of an "orbit scheme" *M* of the [projective linear group](wikipedia://en/382667) *PGL<sub>n</sub>* acting on *H*. This step relies on the more tractable theory of [moduli of abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/64177800).

In the introduction to his 1965 book *Geometric Invariant Theory*, Mumford described the construction of "moduli schemes of various types of objects" as essentially "a special and highly non-trivial case" of the problem "when does an [orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) space of an [algebraic scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_scheme) acted on by an [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) exist?". For this area, see [algebraic invariant theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_invariant_theory).[^o25] [David Gieseker](wikipedia://en/56567476) later distinguished, for step #2, going via [Torelli's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torelli's_theorem), which was Mumford's original method, and the alternative approach via the [Chow point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_point) of a curve *C*, in its projective [canonical embedding](wikipedia://en/734893#General_case), which proves the [stability](wikipedia://en/64246583) of this point.[^o26]

The concept of [stable vector bundle](wikipedia://en/18909129) from moduli theory has been consequential in mathematical physics: "This is a mathematical notion of stability, but it also corresponds to physical stability, at least in a regime in which quantum corrections are small."[^o27]

#### Moduli stacks {#Moduli_stacks}

Before the mathematical concept of [stack](wikipedia://en/13185596) had been defined, Mumford gave a detailed treatment of the [moduli stack of elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/49905706), in his paper *Picard Groups of Moduli Problems*. In it, he made a calculation of (the analogue of) the stack's [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134).[^o29] Hartshorne wrote that, in the paper, Mumford

> \[...\] makes the point that to investigate the more subtle properties of curves, the [coarse moduli space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coarse_moduli_space) may not carry enough information, and so one should really work with stacks.[^o23]

### Classification of surfaces {#Classification_of_surfaces}

In three papers written between 1969 and 1976 (the last two in collaboration with [Enrico Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475)), Mumford extended the [Enriques–Kodaira classification](wikipedia://en/3029260) of smooth [projective surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_surface) from the case of the complex [ground field](wikipedia://en/27161748) to the case of an [algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed) ground field of characteristic *p*. The four classes are:[^o31][^o32][^o33]

1. Kodaira dimension minus infinity. These are the [ruled surfaces](wikipedia://en/705158).
1. Kodaira dimension 0. These are the [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116), [abelian surfaces](wikipedia://en/974181), hyperelliptic and [quasi-hyperelliptic surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-hyperelliptic_surface), and [Enriques surfaces](wikipedia://en/3094760). There are classical and non-classical examples in the last two Kodaira dimension zero cases.
1. Kodaira dimension 1. These are the elliptic and [quasi-elliptic surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-elliptic_surface) not contained in the last two groups.
1. Kodaira dimension 2. These are the [surfaces of general type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfaces_of_general_type).

### Explicit equations and algebraic techniques {#Explicit_equations_and_algebraic_techniques}

Mumford wrote on an algebraic approach to the classical theory of [theta functions](wikipedia://en/449745), the [theta representation](wikipedia://en/4433311), showing that its algebraic content was large, with resulting finite analogues of the [Heisenberg group](wikipedia://en/448518).[^o35] This work on the [equations defining abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/6990584) appeared in 1966–7. He also is one of the founders of the [toroidal embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toric_geometry) theory, the geometric approach to varieties defined by [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416).[^o36] With [Dave Bayer](wikipedia://en/1442686) he published a paper "What can be computed in algebraic geometry?" in 1993 on computation and algebraic geometry, in which [Gröbner basis](wikipedia://en/357328) techniques are fundamental.[^o37] The concept of [Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity](wikipedia://en/24560170) has been employed as a complexity measure for Gröbner bases.[^o38]

### Vision {#Vision}

In a paper published in 1989, Mumford and Jayant Shah showed that a numerical method could tackle [image segmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_segmentation), using what became known as the [Mumford–Shah functional](wikipedia://en/35244294). In the *Handbook of Mathematical Methods in Imaging* (2011) the functional was called "classical", and the method described as an [energy minimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_minimization) approach allowing optimal computation for [piecewise function](wikipedia://en/404130) solutions.[^o40][^o41][^o42] Another assessment in 2024 was "a foundational approach for partitioning an image into meaningful regions while preserving edges."[^o43]

Following work of [Shimon Ullman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Ullman) who brought in variational methods, and [Berthold K.P. Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berthold_K.P._Horn), Mumford in 1992 addressed the [amodal completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amodal_completion) problem with concepts from [elastica theory](wikipedia://en/2406419).[^o44] In the 1990s, [Song-Chun Zhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song-Chun_Zhu) at the [University of Science and Technology of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Science_and_Technology_of_China), attracted into the field by the book *Vision* (1982) by [David Marr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Marr_%28scientist%29), studied computer science under Mumford.[^o45]

In 2002, Mumford and Tai Sing Lee proposed a model for the [visual cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_cortex), influenced by work on [pattern theory](wikipedia://en/3709680) by [Ulf Grenander](wikipedia://en/27178142).[^o46][^o47] The book chapter "The Evolving Concept of Cortical Hierarchy" from 2023 by Vezoli, Hou and Kennedy states that Mumford's "[Bayesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian) computational approach to cortical hierarchy was revolutionary".[^o48] The 2023 book by Zhu and Wu, *Computer Vision: Statistical Models for Marr's Paradigm*, in its Preface lists as "pioneers of vision" [Béla Julesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Béla_Julesz) and [King-Sun Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King-Sun_Fu), with Grenander, Maar and Mumford.[^o49]

## Promulgation of the ideas of Alexander Grothendieck {#Promulgation_of_the_ideas_of_Alexander_Grothendieck}

In 1962 Mumford was appointed an assistant professor at Harvard.[^o51] He decided to give a lecture course on a topic in the area of curves on an [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311), to illustrate the importance of the innovative approach in algebraic geometry of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042), based at [IHES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHES) near Paris.[^o52] The class notes of this course, given 1963–4, were published in 1966 as *Lectures on Curves on an Algebraic Surface*, and in the Introduction Mumford wrote that the goal was to give an exposition of a "[corollary](wikipedia://en/168865)" in [Bourbaki seminar #221](wikipedia://en/3019636) given by Grothendieck in early 1961, *Techniques de construction et théorèmes d'existence en géométrie algébrique. IV : Les schémas de Hilbert*, in the [TDTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TDTE) series. It related to a vexed question in the legacy of the [Italian school of algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/383424), called the "completeness of the characteristic system" of a suitable algebraic system of curves. The issue was of a possible "[pathology](wikipedia://en/372742)", and the burden of the course was to show that a [necessary and sufficient condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_and_sufficient_condition) for the validity of the theorem was now available.[^o53] In parallel, [Robin Hartshorne](wikipedia://en/5693379) was leading a seminar at Harvard, "Residues and Duality", on [coherent duality](wikipedia://en/1815224) in Grothendieck's [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) setting, in which Mumford, Tate, [Stephen Lichtenbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Lichtenbaum) and others participated.[^o54]

### *The Red Book* {#The_Red_Book}

Mumford's lecture notes on [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory) circulated for years in unpublished form. A geometric motivation for the theory brought forward by Mumford was "[infinitesimals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimals) well adapted to algebraic and geometric structures."[^o56] In 1979 in the *[Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2668304)* a reviewer wrote of this work that it "initiated a generation of students to the subject when nothing else was available."[^o57] At the time, they were, beside the treatise *[Éléments de géométrie algébrique](wikipedia://en/471387)*, the only accessible introduction.

Starting in 1967, the notes were mimeographed, bound in red cardboard, and distributed by Harvard's mathematics department under the title *Introduction to Algebraic Geometry (Preliminary version of first 3 Chapters)*. Later (1988; 1999, 2nd ed., ), they were published by Springer under the [Lecture Notes in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/47931235) series as *The Red Book of Varieties and Schemes*. In an introduction to the 1988 edition, Mumford wrote

> \[...\] Grothendieck came along and turned a confused world of researchers upside down, overwhelming them with the new terminology of schemes as well as with a huge production of new and very exciting results. These notes attempted to show something that was still very controversial at that time: that schemes really were the most natural language for algebraic geometry \[...\][^o58]

### Grothendieck's relative point of view {#Grothendieck's_relative_point_of_view}

In *Lectures on Curves on an Algebraic Surface*, Mumford wrote "by far the most important categorical notion for algebraic geometry is that of [fibre product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibre_product)."[^o60] Lectures 3 and 4 (with its Appendix on the [Zariski tangent space](wikipedia://en/510207)) were a concise introduction to the circle of ideas, based on scheme theory (the book uses the older terminology of pre-schemes) known as [Grothendieck's relative point of view](wikipedia://en/1816636), with geometric illustrations. The [representable functor](wikipedia://en/844461) point of view is central: Mumford illustrates it by citing contemporary research: [Brown's representability theorem](wikipedia://en/1073746) in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), where representable functors are clearly distinguished; Tate's [rigid analytic spaces](wikipedia://en/5828941); [Jaap Murre](wikipedia://en/64164858)'s work on functors to the [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092); and [Teruhisa Matsusaka](wikipedia://en/47886466)'s theory of Q-varieties as an extension of the functors considered.[^o61]

Grothendieck made a case for the "representable functor" approach to replace the [universal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_object) conceptualization then prevalent in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869). For moduli spaces, the classical idea of a "universal family of curves" would therefore be modified to a functor "of curves", with a proof that it was representable. The satisfactory picture from homotopy theory, of a [universal bundle](wikipedia://en/2179570) with base space a [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) that represents bundles does not, however, apply directly to moduli. Hence the distinction between a "coarse moduli space", via its [geometric points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_point) a universal object, and a [fine moduli space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_moduli_space) which represents a functor of all parametrized families of curves. The latter is in line with the "relative point of view". In *Geometric Invariant Theory* Mumford laid out the issues for moduli theory, stating that currently "one knows very few ways in which to pose a plausible fine moduli problem." He went on to state that in technical terms the fine moduli problem was not "essentially harder", given "descent machinery", a reference to the role [descent morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descent_morphism) play.[^o62]

This background explains why [faithfully flat descent](wikipedia://en/46606018) is introduced in Lecture 4 of *Lectures on Curves on an Algebraic Surface*: as a necessary condition for representability of a functor. Mumford names the representability issue for schemes "Grothendieck's existence problem", a nod to [Riemann's existence theorem](wikipedia://en/78698035) and to Grothendieck's existence theorem for [formal schemes](wikipedia://en/18829483).[^o61] Mumford's remark about fibre products may be unpacked in later terms of [Grothendieck fibrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck_fibration) and [descent theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descent_theory). He went much further into the relative theory, in defining "modular families" of curves via [Grothendieck topologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck_topologies), in *Picard Groups of Moduli Problems*.[^o65]

The lacuna of the mid-1960s theory in terms of sufficient conditions for representability of functors, as would apply to moduli problems, was filled through work of [Michael Artin](wikipedia://en/1519821) and others by 1969. There were two sides to this advance. (1) Further innovation in the foundations came with [algebraic spaces](wikipedia://en/2394027) (more general than schemes but more restricted than Matsusaka's Q-varieties or Mumford's stacks). Algebraic spaces allow more quotients by equivalence relations than schemes. (2) This definition was combined with a thorough use of algebraic infinitesimal techniques around [artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273) (commutative and [local](wikipedia://en/153391)). See [Artin's criterion](wikipedia://en/41952275), [formal moduli](wikipedia://en/3018981), [Schlessinger's theorem](wikipedia://en/38179442).[^o67]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

*Image: Mumford2.jpg.* David Mumford in 1975

Mumford was awarded a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1974. He was a [MacArthur Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellow) from 1987 to 1992. He won the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) in 2006. In 2007 he was awarded the [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize) for Mathematical Exposition by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). In 2008 he was awarded the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize); on receiving the prize in Jerusalem from [Shimon Peres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Peres), Mumford announced that he was donating half of the prize money to [Birzeit University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birzeit_University) in the [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories) and half to [Gisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gisha_%28human_rights_organization%29), an Israeli human rights organization.[^o70][^o71] In 2010 he was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science).[^o72] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o73]
