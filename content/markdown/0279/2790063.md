# Lawrence C. Evans

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lawrence C. Evans"}

**Lawrence Craig Evans** (born November 1, 1949) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and Professor of Mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).

:::

His research is in the field of [nonlinear partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/16678376), primarily [elliptic equations](wikipedia://en/662787). In 2004, he shared the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Seminal Contribution to Research with [Nicolai V. Krylov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolai_V._Krylov) for their proofs, found independently, that solutions of concave, fully nonlinear, uniformly elliptic equations are $C^{2,\alpha}$. Evans also made significant contributions to the development of the theory of [viscosity solutions](wikipedia://en/2300687) of nonlinear equations, to the understanding of the [Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation](wikipedia://en/730585) arising in stochastic [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565) theory, and to the theory of [harmonic maps](wikipedia://en/4577484). He is also well known as the author of the textbook *Partial Differential Equations,*[^o3] which is considered as a standard introduction to the theory at the graduate level. His textbook *Measure theory and fine properties of functions* (coauthored with Ronald Gariepy), an exposition on Hausdorff measure, capacity, Sobolev functions, and sets of finite perimeter, is also widely cited.

Evans is an [ISI highly cited researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISI_highly_cited_researcher).[^o4]

## Biography {#Biography}

Lawrence Evans was born November 1, 1949, in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta), [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_%28U.S._state%29). He received a BA from [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University) in 1971 and a PhD, with thesis advisor [Michael G. Crandall](wikipedia://en/35265777), from the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) in 1975. From 1975 to 1980, he worked at the [University of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kentucky); from 1980 to 1989, at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland); and since 1989, at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o6][^o7]
