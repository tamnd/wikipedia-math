# Circle bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Circle bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **circle bundle** is a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) where the fiber is the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) $S^1$.

:::

Oriented circle bundles are also known as [principal *U*(1)-bundles](wikipedia://en/310950), or equivalently, as principal *SO*(2)-bundles. In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), circle bundles are the natural geometric setting for [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism). A circle bundle is a special case of a [sphere bundle](wikipedia://en/48880896).

## As 3-manifolds {#As_3-manifolds}

Circle bundles over [surfaces](wikipedia://en/27865) are an important example of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257). A more general class of 3-manifolds is [Seifert fiber spaces](wikipedia://en/1631931), which may be viewed as a kind of "singular" circle bundle, or as a circle bundle over a two-dimensional [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504).

## Relationship to electrodynamics {#Relationship_to_electrodynamics}

The [Maxwell equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_equation) correspond to an [electromagnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_field) represented by a [2-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-form) *F*, with $\pi^{\!*}F$ being [cohomologous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomologous) to zero, i.e. [exact](wikipedia://en/404181). In particular, there always exists a [1-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-form) *A*, the [electromagnetic four-potential](wikipedia://en/731884), (equivalently, the [affine connection](wikipedia://en/607690)) such that

$$\pi^{*}F = dA.$$

Given a circle bundle *P* over *M* and its projection

$$\pi:P\to M$$

one has the [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564)

$$\pi^*:H^2(M,\mathbb{Z}) \to H^2(P,\mathbb{Z})$$

where $\pi^{*}$ is the [pullback](wikipedia://en/9413508). Each homomorphism corresponds to a [Dirac monopole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_monopole); the integer [cohomology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group) correspond to the quantization of the [electric charge](wikipedia://en/9804). The [Aharonov–Bohm effect](wikipedia://en/277664) can be understood as the [holonomy](wikipedia://en/644921) of the connection on the associated line bundle describing the electron wave-function. In essence, the Aharonov–Bohm effect is not a quantum-mechanical effect (contrary to popular belief), as no quantization is involved or required in the construction of the fiber bundles or connections.

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384) is an example of a non-trivial circle bundle.
- The unit tangent bundle of a surface is another example of a circle bundle.
- The unit tangent bundle of a non-orientable surface is a circle bundle that is not a principal $U(1)$ bundle. Only orientable surfaces have principal unit tangent bundles.
- Another method for constructing circle bundles is using a complex line bundle $L \to X$ and taking the associated sphere (circle in this case) bundle. Since this bundle has an orientation induced from $L$ we have that it is a principal $U(1)$-bundle.[^o6] Moreover, the characteristic classes from Chern-Weil theory of the $U(1)$-bundle agree with the characteristic classes of $L$.
- For example, consider the analytification $X$ a complex plane curve $\text{Proj}\left( \frac{\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]}{x^n + y^n + z^n} \right)$. Since $H^2(X) = \mathbb{Z} = H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2)$ and the characteristic classes pull back non-trivially, we have that the line bundle associated to the sheaf $\mathcal{O}_X(a) = \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^2}(a)\otimes \mathcal{O}_X$ has Chern class $c_1 = a \in H^2(X)$.

:::

## Classification {#Classification}

The [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of principal $U(1)$-bundles over a manifold *M* are in one-to-one correspondence with the [homotopy classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_class) of maps $M \to BU(1)$, where $BU(1)$ is called the [classifying space for U(1)](wikipedia://en/2819900). Note that $BU(1)= \mathbb{C}P^\infty$ is the infinite-dimensional [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977), and that it is an example of the [Eilenberg–Maclane space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg–Maclane_space) $K(\mathbb{Z},2).$ Such bundles are classified by an element of the second [integral cohomology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_cohomology_group) $H^2(M,\mathbb{Z})$ of *M*, since

$$[M,BU(1)] \equiv [M,\mathbb CP^\infty] \equiv H^2(M).$$

This isomorphism is realized by the [Euler class](wikipedia://en/2932393); equivalently, it is the first [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) of a smooth complex [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) (essentially because a circle is homotopically equivalent to $\mathbb{C}^*$, the complex plane with the origin removed; and so a complex line bundle with the zero section removed is homotopically equivalent to a circle bundle.)

A circle bundle is a principal $U(1)$ bundle if and only if the associated map $M \to B\mathbb Z_2$ is null-homotopic, which is true if and only if the bundle is fibrewise orientable. Thus, for the more general case, where the circle bundle over *M* might not be orientable, the isomorphism classes are in one-to-one correspondence with the [homotopy classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_class) of maps $M \to BO_2$. This follows from the extension of groups, $SO_2 \to O_2 \to \mathbb Z_2$, where $SO_2 \equiv U(1)$.

### Deligne complexes {#Deligne_complexes}

The above classification only applies to circle bundles in general; the corresponding classification for smooth circle bundles, or, say, the circle bundles with an [affine connection](wikipedia://en/607690) requires a more complex cohomology theory. Results include that the smooth circle bundles are classified by the second Deligne cohomology $H_D^2(M, \mathbb{Z})$; circle bundles with an affine connection are classified by $H_D^2(M, \mathbb{Z}(2))$ while $H_D^3(M, \mathbb{Z})$ classifies line bundle [gerbes](wikipedia://en/822043).
