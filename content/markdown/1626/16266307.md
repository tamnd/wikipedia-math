# Timeline of ancient Greek mathematicians

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Timeline of ancient Greek mathematicians"}

This is a timeline of [mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematicians) in [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece).

:::

## Timeline {#Timeline}

Historians traditionally place the beginning of [Greek mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mathematics) proper to the age of [Thales of Miletus](wikipedia://en/30072) (ca. 624–548 BC), which is indicated by the green at 600 BC. The orange at 300 BC indicates the approximate year in which [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s [*Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107) was first published. The red at 300 AD passes through [Pappus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/509300) (c. 290), who was one of the last great [Greek mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mathematics) of [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity). Note that the solid thick black is at [year zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_zero), which is a year that does *not* exist in the *[Anno Domini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_Domini)* (AD) [calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar) year system

The mathematician [Heliodorus of Larissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliodorus_of_Larissa) is not listed due to the uncertainty of when he lived, which was possibly during the 3rd century AD, after [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979). Also not listed is the 1st century AD mathematician [Dionysodorus of Amisene](wikipedia://en/75507964) (not to be confused with [Dionysodorus of Caunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysodorus_of_Caunus)), [Pandrosion](wikipedia://en/61651833) from the 4th century AD, [Hermotimus of Colophon](wikipedia://en/79934242) (born c. 325 BC), [Metrodorus](wikipedia://en/28258486) from likely the 6th century AD (though he may have lived as early as the 3rd century AD), and [Apollodorus Logisticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollodorus_Logisticus) and [Proclus of Laodicea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclus_of_Laodicea).

## Overview of the most important mathematicians and discoveries {#Overview_of_the_most_important_mathematicians_and_discoveries}

Of these mathematicians, those whose work stands out include:

- **[Thales of Miletus](wikipedia://en/30072)** (c. 624/623) is the first known individual to use [deductive reasoning](wikipedia://en/61093) applied to geometry, by deriving four corollaries to [Thales' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales'_theorem).[^o4]
- **[Pythagoras](wikipedia://en/23275)** (c. 570) was credited with many mathematical and scientific discoveries, including the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034), [Pythagorean tuning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagorean_tuning), the [five regular solids](wikipedia://en/23905), the [Theory of Proportions](wikipedia://en/81863), the [sphericity of the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_Earth), and the identity of the [morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus_%28morning_star%29) and [evening stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesperus) as the planet [Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus).
- **[Theaetetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theaetetus_%28mathematician%29)** (c. 417) Proved that there are *exactly* five [regular](wikipedia://en/333299) [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) [polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470) (it is emphasized that it was, in particular, *proved* that there does *not exist* any regular convex polyhedra other than these five). This fact led these five solids, now called the [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905), to play a prominent role in the philosophy of [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954) (and consequently, also influenced later [Western Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Philosophy)) who associated each of the four [classical elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_element) with a regular solid: [earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_%28classical_element%29) with the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_%28classical_element%29) with the [octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458), [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_%28classical_element%29) with the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968), and [fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_%28classical_element%29) with the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521) (of the fifth Platonic solid, the [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361), Plato obscurely remarked, "...the god used \[it\] for arranging the constellations on the whole heaven"). The last book (Book XIII) of the [Euclid's *Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107), which is probably derived from the work of Theaetetus, is devoted to constructing the Platonic solids and describing their properties; [Andreas Speiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Speiser) has advocated the view that the construction of the 5 regular solids is the chief goal of the deductive system canonized in the *Elements*.[^n1] [Astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) proposed a model of the [Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System) in which the five solids were set inside one another and separated by a series of inscribed and circumscribed spheres.
- **[Eudoxus of Cnidus](wikipedia://en/48388)** (c. 408) is considered by some to be the greatest of [classical Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Greece) mathematicians, and in all [antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) second only to [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844).[^o6] Book V of [Euclid's *Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107) is thought to be largely due to Eudoxus.
- **[Aristarchus of Samos](wikipedia://en/74857)** (c. 310) presented the first known [heliocentric model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliocentrism) that placed the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun) at the center of the known universe with the Earth revolving around it. Aristarchus identified the "central fire" with the Sun, and he put the other planets in their correct order of distance around the Sun.[^o7] In *[On the Sizes and Distances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Sizes_and_Distances_%28Aristarchus%29)*, he calculates the sizes of the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun) and [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon), as well as their distances from the Earth in terms of Earth's radius. However, [Eratosthenes](wikipedia://en/46117) (c. 276) was the first person to calculate the circumference of the Earth. [Posidonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posidonius) (c. 135) also measured the diameters and distances of the Sun and the Moon as well as the Earth's diameter; his measurement of the diameter of the Sun was more accurate than Aristarchus', differing from the modern value by about half.
- **[Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)** ([fl.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floruit) 300 BC) is often referred to as the "founder of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446)"[^o8] or the "father of geometry" because of his incredibly influential [treatise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatise) called the *[Elements](wikipedia://en/244107)*, which was the first, or at least one of the first, axiomatized deductive systems.
- **[Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844)** (c. 287) is considered to be the greatest mathematician of [ancient history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history), and one of the greatest of all time.[^o9][^o10] Archimedes anticipated modern [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) and [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) by applying concepts of [infinitesimals](wikipedia://en/160990) and the [method of exhaustion](wikipedia://en/1721284) to derive and rigorously prove a range of [geometrical](wikipedia://en/18973446) [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977), including: the [area of a circle](wikipedia://en/1896782); the [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) and [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859); area of an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277); the area under a [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231); the volume of a segment of a [paraboloid of revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraboloid_of_revolution); the volume of a segment of a [hyperboloid of revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperboloid_of_revolution); and the area of a [spiral](wikipedia://en/61563).[^o11] He was also one of the first to [apply mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) to [physical phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_phenomena), founding [hydrostatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrostatics) and [statics](wikipedia://en/28767), including an explanation of the principle of the [lever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lever). In a lost work, he discovered and enumerated the 13 [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847), which were later rediscovered by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) around 1620 A.D.
- **[Apollonius of Perga](wikipedia://en/257242)** (c. 240) is known for his work on [conic sections](wikipedia://en/19008673) and his study of geometry in 3-dimensional space. He is considered one of the greatest ancient Greek mathematicians.
- **[Hipparchus](wikipedia://en/13600)** (c. 190) is considered the founder of [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261)[^o12] and also solved several problems of [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405). He was the first whose quantitative and accurate models for the motion of the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun) and [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) survive. In his work *[On Sizes and Distances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Sizes_and_Distances_%28Hipparchus%29)*, he measured the apparent diameters of the Sun and Moon and their distances from Earth. He is also reputed to have measured the [Earth's precession](wikipedia://en/72576).
- **[Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110)** (c. 201–215) wrote *[Arithmetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetica)* which dealt with solving [Diophantine equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_equations)

### Hellenic mathematicians {#Hellenic_mathematicians}

The conquests of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) around c. 330 BC led to Greek culture being spread around much of the Mediterranean region, especially in [Alexandria, Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria,_Egypt). This is why the Hellenistic period of Greek mathematics is typically considered as beginning in the 4th century BC. During the Hellenistic period, many people living in those parts of the [Mediterranean region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_region) subject to Greek influence ended up adopting the Greek language and sometimes also Greek culture. Consequently, some of the Greek mathematicians from this period may not have been "ethnically Greek" with respect to the modern [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture) notion of [ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity), which is much more rigid than most other notions of ethnicity that existed in the Mediterranean region at the time. [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979), for example, was said to have originated from [Upper Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Egypt), which is far South of [Alexandria, Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria,_Egypt). Regardless, their contemporaries considered them Greek.
