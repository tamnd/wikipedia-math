# Crypto-1

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Crypto-1"}

**Crypto1** is a proprietary [encryption](wikipedia://en/10294) algorithm ([stream cipher](wikipedia://en/49007)) and authentication protocol created by [NXP Semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NXP_Semiconductors) for its [MIFARE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mifare) Classic [RFID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFID) [contactless smart cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contactless_smart_card) launched in 1994. Such cards have been used in many notable systems, including [Oyster card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster_card), [CharlieCard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CharlieCard) and [OV-chipkaart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OV-chipkaart).

:::

By 2009, cryptographic research had [reverse engineered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_engineering) the cipher and a variety of attacks were published that effectively broke the security.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]

NXP responded by issuing "hardened" (but still backwards compatible) cards, the MIFARE Classic EV1. However, in 2015 a new attack rendered the cards insecure,[^o8][^o9] and NXP recommended migrating away from Mifare Classic.[^o10]

## Technical description {#Technical_description}

Crypto1 is a [stream cipher](wikipedia://en/49007) very similar in its structure to its successor, Hitag2. Crypto1 consists of

- a 48-bit [linear feedback shift register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LFSR) for the state of the cipher,
- a two-layer 20-to-1 nonlinear function used to generate the keystream, and
- a 16-bit LFSR which is used during the authentication phase as a [pseudo random number generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo_random_number_generator)

The usual operation of Crypto1 and Hitag2 ciphers uses nonlinear feedback only during the initialization/authentication stage, switching to operation as a LFSR with a nonlinear output filter (filter generator) for the rest of the communications.
