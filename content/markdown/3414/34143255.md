# Higgs bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Higgs bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Higgs bundle** is a pair $(E,\varphi)$ consisting of a [holomorphic vector bundle](wikipedia://en/4794668) *E* and a [Higgs field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_field) $\varphi$, a holomorphic 1-form taking values in the bundle of endomorphisms of *E* such that $\varphi \wedge \varphi=0$. Such pairs were introduced by (txt),[^o3] who named the field $\varphi$ after [Peter Higgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Higgs) because of an analogy with Higgs bosons. The term 'Higgs bundle', and the condition $\varphi \wedge \varphi=0$ (which is vacuous in Hitchin's original set-up on [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181)) was introduced later by [Carlos Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Simpson).[^o4]

:::

A Higgs bundle can be thought of as a "simplified version" of a flat holomorphic [connection](wikipedia://en/607690) on a holomorphic vector bundle, where the derivative is scaled to zero. The [nonabelian Hodge correspondence](wikipedia://en/46202375) says that, under suitable stability conditions, the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of flat holomorphic connections on a smooth [projective complex algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/320469), the category of representations of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of the variety, and the category of Higgs bundles over this variety are actually equivalent. Therefore, one can deduce results about [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155) with flat connections by working with the simpler Higgs bundles.

## History {#History}

Higgs bundles were first introduced by Hitchin in 1987, for the specific case where the holomorphic vector bundle *E* is over a [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_%28mathematics%29) [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181). Further, Hitchin's paper mostly discusses the case where the vector bundle is rank 2 (that is, the fiber is a 2-dimensional vector space). The rank 2 vector bundle arises as the solution space to [Hitchin's equations](wikipedia://en/68430334) for a [principal SU(2)-bundle](wikipedia://en/80364907).

The theory on Riemann surfaces was generalized by Carlos Simpson to the case where the base manifold is compact and [Kähler](wikipedia://en/390538). Restricting to the dimension one case recovers Hitchin's theory.

## Stability of a Higgs bundle {#Stability_of_a_Higgs_bundle}

Of particular interest in the theory of Higgs bundles is the notion of a **stable** Higgs bundle. To do so, $\varphi$-invariant subbundles must first be defined.

In Hitchin's original discussion, a rank-1 subbundle labelled *L* is $\varphi$-invariant if $\varphi(L) \subset L \otimes K$ with $K$ the canonical bundle over the Riemann surface *M*. Then a Higgs bundle $(E, \varphi)$ is **stable** if, for each $\varphi$ invariant subbundle $L$ of $E$,

$$\operatorname{deg} L < \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{deg}(\wedge^2 E),$$

with $\operatorname{deg}$ being the usual notion of degree for a complex vector bundle over a Riemann surface.
