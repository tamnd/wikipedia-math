# Gowers norm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gowers norm"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [additive combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243392), a **Gowers norm** or **uniformity norm** is a class of [norms](wikipedia://en/990534) on [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) on a finite [group](wikipedia://en/19447) or group-like object which quantify the amount of structure present, or conversely, the amount of [randomness](wikipedia://en/19196523).[^o2] They are used in the study of [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389) in the group. They are named after [Timothy Gowers](wikipedia://en/365715), who introduced it in his work on [Szemerédi's theorem](wikipedia://en/591703).[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $f$ be a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826)-valued function on a finite [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) $G$ and let $J$ denote [complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation). The Gowers $d$-norm is

$$\Vert f \Vert_{U^d(G)}^{2^d} = \sum_{x,h_1,\ldots,h_d \in G} \prod_{\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_d \in \{0,1\}} J^{\omega_1+\cdots+\omega_d} f\left({x + h_1\omega_1 + \cdots + h_d\omega_d}\right) \ .$$

Gowers norms are also defined for complex-valued functions *f* on a segment $[N] = {0, 1, 2, ..., N - 1}$, where *N* is a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). In this context, the uniformity norm is given as $\Vert f \Vert_{U^d[N]} = \Vert \tilde{f} \Vert_{U^d(\mathbb{Z}/\tilde{N}\mathbb{Z})}/\Vert 1_{[N]} \Vert_{U^d(\mathbb{Z}/\tilde{N}\mathbb{Z})}$, where $\tilde N$ is a large integer, $1_{[N]}$ denotes the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) of \[*N*\], and $\tilde f(x)$ is equal to $f(x)$ for $x \in [N]$ and $0$ for all other $x$. This definition does not depend on $\tilde N$, as long as $\tilde N > 2^d N$.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Inverse conjectures"}

## Inverse conjectures {#Inverse_conjectures}

An *inverse conjecture* for these norms is a statement asserting that if a [bounded function](wikipedia://en/311509) *f* has a large Gowers *d*-norm then *f* correlates with a polynomial phase of degree *d* − 1 or other object with polynomial behaviour (e.g. a (*d* − 1)-step [nilsequence](wikipedia://en/75328204)). The precise statement depends on the Gowers norm under consideration.

The Inverse Conjecture for [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) $\mathbb F$ asserts that for any $\delta > 0$ there exists a constant $c > 0$ such that for any [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) vector space *V* over $\mathbb F$ and any complex-valued function $f$ on $V$, bounded by 1, such that $\Vert f \Vert_{U^{d}[V]} \geq \delta$, there exists a polynomial sequence $P \colon V \to \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ such that

$$\left| \frac{1}{|V|} \sum_{x \in V} f(x) e \left( -P(x) \right) \right| \geq c ,$$

where $e(x) := e^{2 \pi i x}$. This conjecture was proved to be true by Bergelson, Tao, and Ziegler.[^o8][^o9][^o10]

The Inverse Conjecture for Gowers $U^{d}[N]$ norm asserts that for any $\delta > 0$, a finite collection of (*d* − 1)-step *[nilmanifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilmanifolds)* $\mathcal{M}_\delta$ and constants $c, C$ can be found, so that the following is true. If $N$ is a positive integer and $f\colon [N]\to \mathbb{C}$ is bounded in absolute value by 1 and $\Vert f \Vert_{U^{d}[N]} \geq \delta$, then there exists a nilmanifold $G/\Gamma \in \mathcal{M}_\delta$ and a [nilsequence](wikipedia://en/75328204) $F(g^nx)$ where $g \in G,\ x \in G/\Gamma$ and $F\colon G/\Gamma \to \mathbb{C}$ bounded by 1 in absolute value and with Lipschitz constant bounded by $C$ such that:

$$\left| \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n =0}^{N-1} f(n) \overline{ F(g^nx}) \right| \geq c .$$

This conjecture was proved to be true by Green, Tao, and Ziegler.[^o11][^o12] It should be stressed that the appearance of nilsequences in the above statement is necessary. The statement is no longer true if we only consider polynomial phases.
