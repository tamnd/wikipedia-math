# Sally Shlaer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sally Shlaer"}

**Sally Shlaer** (December 3, 1938 – November 12, 1998) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [software engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_engineer) and [software methodologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_development_process),[^o3] known as co-developer of the 1980s [Shlaer–Mellor method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlaer–Mellor_method) for software development.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Cleveland, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland,_Ohio), Shlaer received a BS in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in 1960 from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and started a graduate study at the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University).

At Stanford Shlaer had started programming in [Fortran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran) and [assembler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language). In 1965 she started as a software engineer at [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory). In 1977 she became project manager in software development at [Lawrence Berkeley Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Berkeley_Laboratory), where she guided the development of a new Integrated Control System for the [Bay Area Rapid Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Area_Rapid_Transit) system.[^o3]

At Lawrence Berkeley, Laboratory Shlaer met [Stephen J. Mellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_J._Mellor), with whom she developed the [Shlaer–Mellor method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlaer–Mellor_method) for software development. In 1985 together they founded the software development firm Project Technology Inc. Shlaer was also a Fellows of the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery).

## Work {#Work}

### Software engineering {#Software_engineering}

Shlaer started her software engineering career at Los Alamos National Laboratory as a programmer. She designed and implemented an operating system to operate an electron accelerator to work in real time, and this project became her masterpiece.[^o7]

At Lawrence Berkeley Laboratory, she led a team of [software developers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_development) to build a new [control system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_system) for the subway of the Bay Area Rapid Transit system. The existing control system software was considered impossible to continue using, making replacement necessary. Working with Steve Mellor, they replaced the original [Fortran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran) and [assembly language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language) code with new code, going from seventy thousand lines to two thousand. This analysis has since been called "legendary".[^o7]

### Shlaer–Mellor method {#Shlaer–Mellor_method}

In the developing of a new control system for the Bay Area Rapid Transit, Shlaer and Mellor sought to regulate mechanisms of software development and began to design new methods of [project management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_management).[^o7] This resulted in the development of the [Shlaer–Mellor method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlaer–Mellor_method), which in the new millennium has evolved into [Executable UML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executable_UML).[^o9]
