# Almost simple group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Almost simple group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is said to be **almost simple** if it contains a non-[abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600) and is contained within the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of that simple group – that is, if it fits between a (non-abelian) simple group and its automorphism group. In symbols, a group $A$ is almost simple if there is a (non-abelian) simple group *S* such that $S \leq A \leq \operatorname{Aut}(S)$, where the inclusion of $S$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(S)$ is the [action by conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugation_%28group_action%29), which is [faithful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_action) since $S$ has a trivial [center](wikipedia://en/7125).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Trivially, non-abelian simple groups and the full group of automorphisms are almost simple. For $n=5$ or $n \geq 7,$ the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $\mathrm{S}_n$ is the automorphism group of the simple [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) $\mathrm{A}_n,$ so $\mathrm{S}_n$ is almost simple in this trivial sense.
- For $n=6$ there is a proper example, as $\mathrm{S}_6$ sits properly between the simple $\mathrm{A}_6$ and $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{A}_6),$ due to the [exceptional outer automorphism](wikipedia://en/13763478#exceptional_outer_automorphism) of $\mathrm{A}_6.$ Two other groups, the [Mathieu group](wikipedia://en/380207) $\mathrm{M}_{10}$ and the [projective general linear group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_general_linear_group) $\operatorname{PGL}_2(9)$ also sit properly between $\mathrm{A}_6$ and $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathrm{A}_6).$

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The full automorphism group of a non-abelian simple group is a [complete group](wikipedia://en/876770) (the conjugation map is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397) to the automorphism group),[^o7] but proper [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of the full automorphism group need not be complete.

:::

## Structure {#Structure}

By the [Schreier conjecture](wikipedia://en/7030987), now generally accepted as a [corollary](wikipedia://en/168865) of the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445), the outer automorphism group of a [finite](wikipedia://en/323707) simple group is a [solvable group](wikipedia://en/105012). Thus a finite almost simple group is an extension of a solvable group by a simple group.
