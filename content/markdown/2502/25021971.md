# Fréchet mean

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fréchet mean"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Fréchet mean** is a generalization of [centroids](wikipedia://en/187926) to [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018), giving a single representative point or [central tendency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_tendency) for a cluster of points. It is named after [Maurice Fréchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Fréchet). **Karcher mean** is the renaming of the Riemannian [Center of Mass](wikipedia://en/173961) construction developed by [Karsten Grove](wikipedia://en/49517887) and [Hermann Karcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Karcher).[^o2][^o3] On the real numbers, the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612), [median](wikipedia://en/18837), [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046), and [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463) can all be interpreted as Fréchet means for different distance functions.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let (*M*, *d*) be a [complete metric space](wikipedia://en/7832). Let *x*<sub>1</sub>, *x*<sub>2</sub>, …, *x*<sub>*N*</sub> be points in *M*. For any point *p* in *M*, define the **Fréchet variance** to be the sum of squared distances from *p* to the *x*<sub>*i*</sub>:

$$\Psi(p) = \sum_{i=1}^N d^2(p, x_i)$$

The **Karcher means** are then those points, *m* of *M*, which [minimise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_minimum) $\Psi$:[^o3]

$$m = \mathop{\text{arg min}}_{p \in M} \sum_{i=1}^N d^2(p, x_i)$$

If there is a unique *m* of *M* that strictly minimises $\Psi$, then it is **Fréchet mean**.

:::

## Examples of Fréchet means {#Examples_of_Fréchet_means}

### Arithmetic mean and median {#Arithmetic_mean_and_median}

For real numbers, the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) is a Fréchet mean, using the usual [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) as the distance function.

The [median](wikipedia://en/18837) is also a Fréchet mean, if the definition of the function Ψ is generalized to the non-quadratic

$$\Psi(p) = \sum_{i=1}^N d^\alpha(p, x_i),$$

where $\alpha=1$, and the Euclidean distance is the distance function *d*.[^o8] In higher-dimensional spaces, this becomes the [geometric median](wikipedia://en/10324687).

### Geometric mean {#Geometric_mean}

On the positive real numbers, the (hyperbolic) distance function $d(x,y)= | \log(x) - \log(y) |$ can be defined. The [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) is the corresponding Fréchet mean. Indeed $f:x\mapsto e^x$ is then an isometry from the euclidean space to this "hyperbolic" space and must respect the Fréchet mean: the Fréchet mean of the $x_i$ is the image by $f$ of the Fréchet mean (in the Euclidean sense) of the $f^{-1}(x_i)$, i.e. it must be:

$$f\left( \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n f^{-1}(x_i)\right) = \exp \left( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n\log x_i \right) = \sqrt[n]{x_1 \cdots x_n}.$$

### Harmonic mean {#Harmonic_mean}

On the [positive real numbers](wikipedia://en/47172571), the [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) (distance function):

$$d_\operatorname{H}(x,y) = \left| \frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{y} \right|$$

can be defined. The [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463) is the corresponding Fréchet mean.

### Power means {#Power_means}

Given a non-zero real number $m$, the [power mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_mean) can be obtained as a Fréchet mean by introducing the metric

$$d_m(x, y) = \left| x^m - y^m \right|$$

### f-mean {#f-mean}

Given an invertible and continuous function $f$, the f-mean can be defined as the Fréchet mean obtained by using the metric:

$$d_f(x,y) = \left|f(x) - f(y)\right|$$

This is sometimes called the [generalised f-mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalised_f-mean) or [quasi-arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/566869).

### Weighted means {#Weighted_means}

The general definition of the Fréchet mean (which includes the possibility of weighting observations) can be used to derive weighted versions for all of the above types of means.

For the arithmetic mean, the *x*<sub>*i*</sub> are assigned weights *w*<sub>*i*</sub>. Then, the Fréchet variances and the Fréchet mean are defined as:

$$\Psi(p) = \sum_{i=1}^N w_i\, d^2(p, x_i), \;\;\;\; m = \mathop{\text{arg min}}_{p \in M} \sum_{i=1}^N w_i d^2(p, x_i).$$
