# Gerrit van Dijk (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gerrit van Dijk"}

**Gerrit van Dijk** (14 August 1939, [Kampen, Overijssel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampen,_Overijssel) – 16 April 2022) was a Dutch professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939). In 2004 he was appointed an Officer of the [Order of Orange-Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Orange-Nassau).[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Van Dijk studied mathematics at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University), where he received his PhD in 1969 with thesis advisor [T. A. Springer](wikipedia://en/10836474). As a postdoc, van Dijk worked for the academic year 1969–1970 in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o5] There he was inspired by [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190) to study [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) with the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945)[^o2] and prepared the notes for Harish-Chandra's 1969 monograph *Harmonic analysis on reductive p-adic groups*.[^o6] From 1970 to 1972 van Dijk was a staff member at Utrecht University. At [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) he was appointed lecturer in 1972 and was promoted to professor in 1980. From 1988 to 1992 he was Leiden University's [dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_%28education%29) of the faculty of mathematics and natural sciences. He was from 1995 to 1997 he was the director of Leiden University's Lorenz Center[^o2][^o7] and from 1997 to 2004 the director of Leiden University's Mathematical Institute. From 2003 to 2004 he directed the Leiden Institute for Advanced Computer Science (LIACS). He was a professor at Leiden University until 2004, when he retired as professor emeritus.[^o2]

Van Dijk was a visiting professor at various universities in several countries: Germany (Göttingen), France (Nancy), Japan (Fukuoka), Russia (Tambov), and Sweden (Stockholm). He was a member of the [Royal Dutch Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/43504918), the [Batavian Society for Experimental Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavian_Society_for_Experimental_Philosophy), and the *[Koninklijke Hollandsche Maatschappij der Wetenschappen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koninklijke_Hollandsche_Maatschappij_der_Wetenschappen)*. He supervised 14 doctoral dissertations (some of them with [Tom Koornwinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Koornwinder)). Van Dijk did research on Lie groups, p-adic representation theory, harmonic analysis, [symmetric spaces](wikipedia://en/1642877), [homogeneous spaces](wikipedia://en/363325), [Gelfand pairs](wikipedia://en/3018619), and [Berezin transforms](wikipedia://en/12852798) with generalizations.[^o2][^o8] His collaborators include Vladimir Fedorovich Molchanov,[^o9] [Masato Wakayama](wikipedia://en/83679621), Tom Koornwinder, and Erik G. F. Thomas.[^o2] Van Dijk's doctoral students include Gerrit Heckman and [Eric Opdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Opdam)[^o4] (known for [Heckman–Opdam polynomials](wikipedia://en/32049415)).

Van Dijk did historical research on the life and work of the 19th-century Dutch mathematician [Thomas Joannes Stieltjes](wikipedia://en/40589), as well as other Leiden mathematics professors from the past. This research work resulted in a new issue of Stieltjes's collected works, a portrait gallery in Leiden University's Mathematical Institute, and publication of the book *Leidse hoogleraren Wiskunde 1575-1975*, which documents the lives of about forty Leiden mathematics professors.[^o8][^o2]

Van Dijk was married and had three children.
