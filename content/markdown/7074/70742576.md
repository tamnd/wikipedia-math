# Mathias Metternich

Mathias Metternich

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mathias Metternich"}

**Mathias Metternich** (8 May 1747 – 13 September 1825) was a German mathematician and professor at the [University of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mainz). As a revolutionary, he was active in the [Republic of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mainz).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Metternich was born on 8 May 1747 in [Steinefrenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinefrenz), a village in the [Electorate of Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Trier) close to [Montabaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montabaur).[^n1] He was born into a family of farmers who had lived in the area since the 17th century.[^n2] Locally, the Metternich family was influential and respected; both his father and grandfather held the office of Heimberger, the village leader.[^n3] Supported by a nobleman, the count of Waldersdorff, Metternich was able to study at the Jesuit [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29) in [Hadamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadamar).[^n2] In 1770, he went to [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz), where he was educated as an elementary school teacher at the [normal school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_school).[^n4] He became a teacher at the school of the St Emmeran's Church, Mainz and in 1780 at the normal school.[^n1] He also studied mathematics at the [University of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mainz) from 1780 and at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) from 1784, where he studied with [Abraham Gotthelf Kästner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Gotthelf_Kästner).[^n5][^n6] In 1786, he obtained a doctorate in philosophy from the [University of Erfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erfurt), with a thesis about friction.[^n4][^n7] Between 1782 and 1785, he was a member of the [Illuminati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminati) in Mainz with the secret name *Thiusco*.[^n8][^n9]

## Professor and revolutionary {#Professor_and_revolutionary}

Metternich became professor of mathematics and experimental physics in Mainz in 1786.[^n1] He also became a member of the Churfürstlich Mayntzische Academie nützlicher Wissenschaften, the academy of applied sciences in [Erfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erfurt).[^n10] In 1789, his work about friction won a prize from the Societas Jablonoviana in Leipzig.[^n5][^n11] He was a member of the Mainzer Gelehrte Lesegesellschaft, a reading society, and openly supported the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution). In 1791, Metternich and his colleague [Andreas Joseph Hofmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Joseph_Hofmann) initiated a split in the reading society, which separated into a democratic and an aristocratic part.[^n12]

In the [Republic of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mainz) that supported revolutionary France, he was one of the founding members of the Jacobin Club and at times its president.[^n10][^n13] After Mainz was taken by Prussian and other troops after the 1793 [Siege of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Mainz_%281793%29), Metternich was captured and mistreated, then imprisoned at [Ehrenbreitstein Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehrenbreitstein_Fortress) and exiled to France in 1795.[^n14][^n15] He worked as an administrator and journalist in French occupied territories, but was captured by imperial German troops again in October 1796, spending time in prison until he was released in April 1797 owing to the [Treaty of Leoben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Leoben).[^n16]

## Later life {#Later_life}

*Image: Grabstätte Metternich.jpg.* Metternich family tomb at the Mainz Central Cemetery

In 1798, Metternich returned to Mainz and became a teacher at the Zentralschule, a successor organisation of the university.[^n14] Until 1800, he also worked in the administration of the département [Mont-Tonnerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont-Tonnerre).[^n17] He ceased his political activity in the Napoleonic era.[^n14] In 1808, Metternich married Sophie Friederike Treffz.[^n17] One of their children was [Germain Metternich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germain_Metternich), who became a participant in the [German revolutions of 1848–1849](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_revolutions_of_1848–1849).[^n14] Metternich died in Mainz on 13 September 1825.[^n18]
