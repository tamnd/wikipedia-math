# Codazzi tensor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Codazzi tensor"}

In the mathematical field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **Codazzi tensor** (named after [Delfino Codazzi](wikipedia://en/703285)) is a symmetric 2-tensor whose [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848) is also symmetric. Such tensors arise naturally in the study of [Riemannian manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_manifolds) with [harmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic) [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) or harmonic [Weyl tensor](wikipedia://en/1026848). In fact, existence of Codazzi tensors impose strict conditions on the [curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) of the manifold. Also, the second fundamental form of an immersed hypersurface in a [space form](wikipedia://en/1087009) (relative to a local choice of normal field) is a Codazzi tensor.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $(M,g)$ be a n-dimensional Riemannian manifold for $n \geq 3$, let $T$ be a symmetric 2-[tensor](wikipedia://en/29965) field, and let $\nabla$ be the [Levi-Civita connection](wikipedia://en/249176). We say that the tensor $T$ is a Codazzi tensor if

$$(\nabla_X T)(Y,Z) = (\nabla_Y T)(X,Z)$$

for all $X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M).$

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any parallel $(0,2)$-tensor field is, trivially, Codazzi.
- Let $(N,\overline{g})$ be a [space form](wikipedia://en/1087009), let $M$ be a smooth manifold with $1+\dim M=\dim N,$ and let $F:M\to N$ be an immersion. If there is a global choice of unit normal vector field, then relative to this choice, the second fundamental form is a Codazzi tensor on $M.$ This is an immediate consequence of the Gauss-Codazzi equations.
- Let $(M,g)$ be a space form with constant curvature $\kappa.$ Given any function $f$ on $M,$ the tensor $\operatorname{Hess}^gf+\kappa fg$ is Codazzi. This is a consequence of the commutation formula for covariant differentiation.
- Let $(M,g)$ be a two-dimensional Riemannian manifold, and let $K$ be the [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623). Then $2\operatorname{Hess}^gK+K^2g$ is a Codazzi tensor. This is a consequence of the commutation formula for covariant differentiation.
- Let Rm denote the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526). Then $\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{Rm})=0$ ("$g$ has harmonic curvature tensor") if and only if the Ricci tensor is a Codazzi tensor. This is an immediate consequence of the contracted Bianchi identity.
- Let $W$ denote the [Weyl curvature tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_curvature_tensor). Then $\operatorname{div}W=0$ ("$g$ has harmonic Weyl tensor") if and only if the "Schouten tensor"

$$\operatorname{Ric}-\frac{1}{2n-2}Rg$$

is a Codazzi tensor. This is an immediate consequence of the definition of the Weyl tensor and the contracted Bianchi identity.

:::

## Rigidity {#Rigidity}

Matsushima and Tanno showed that, on a Kähler manifold, any Codazzi tensor which is hermitian is parallel. Berger showed that, on a compact manifold of nonnegative sectional curvature, any Codazzi tensor $h$ with $\operatorname{tr}_{g}h$ constant must be parallel. Furthermore, on a compact manifold of nonnegative sectional curvature, if the sectional curvature is strictly positive at least one point, then every symmetric parallel 2-tensor is a constant multiple of the metric.
