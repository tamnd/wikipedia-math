# Misra & Gries edge-coloring algorithm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Misra & Gries edge-coloring algorithm"}

The **Misra & Gries edge-coloring algorithm** is a [polynomial-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) algorithm in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) that finds an [edge coloring](wikipedia://en/690647) of any simple graph. The coloring produced uses at most $\Delta+1$ colors, where $\Delta$ is the maximum [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) of the graph. This is optimal for some graphs, and it uses at most one color more than optimal for all others. The existence of such a coloring is guaranteed by [Vizing's theorem](wikipedia://en/5449464).

:::

It was first published by [Jayadev Misra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayadev_Misra) and [David Gries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gries) in 1992.[^o2] It is a simplification of a prior algorithm by [Béla Bollobás](wikipedia://en/614956).[^o3]

For many years this algorithm was the fastest published almost-optimal algorithm for edge coloring, executing in $O(|E||V|)$ time. A faster time bound of $O\left(|E|\sqrt{|V|\log|V|}\right)$ was claimed in a 1985 technical report by Gabow et al., but was never published.[^o4] In 2025 a group of researchers published a faster algorithm for the same problem, with runtime $O(|E|\log\Delta)$.[^o5]

In general, optimal edge coloring is NP-complete, so it is very unlikely that a polynomial time algorithm exists. There are, however, exponential-time [exact edge-coloring algorithms](wikipedia://en/690647#Exact_algorithms) that give an optimal solution.

## Key concepts {#Key_concepts}

### Free color {#Free_color}

A color *c* is said to be **free** on a vertex *u* if no incident edge of *u* has color *c*.

### Fan {#Fan}

*Image: Fan, Misra and Gries edge coloring algorithm.png.* A fan *F* = \[*x*<sub>1</sub>,*x*<sub>2</sub>,*x*<sub>3</sub>\] of *v* (dashed edges are uncolored), (*v*,*x*<sub>1</sub>), (*v,x*<sub>2</sub>), (*v*,*x*<sub>3</sub>) are the fan edges. *F* = \[*x*<sub>1</sub>,*x*<sub>2</sub>\] is also a fan of *v*, but it is not maximal.

A **fan** of a vertex *X* is a sequence of vertices *F*\[1:*k*\] that satisfies the following conditions:

1. *F*\[1:*k*\] is a non-empty sequence of distinct neighbors of *X*;
1. Edge (*X*,*F*\[1\]) is uncolored;
1. The color of (*X*,*F*\[*i*+1\]) is free on *F*\[*i*\] for 1 ≤ *i* \< *k*.

Given a fan *F*, any edge (*X*,*F*\[*i*\]) for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *k* is a **fan edge**.

### Rotating a fan {#Rotating_a_fan}

*Image: Rotating a fan, Misra and Gries edge coloring algorithm.png.* Rotating the fan *F* = \[*x*<sub>1</sub>,*x*<sub>2</sub>,*x*<sub>3</sub>\] on the left results in the fan on the right

Given a fan *F*\[1:*k*\] of a vertex *X*, the "rotate fan" operation does the following: for *i* = 1, ..., *k*–1, assign the color of (*X*,*F*\[*i* + 1\]) to edge (*X*,*F*\[*i*\]). Finally, uncolor (*X*, *F*\[*k*\]).

This operation leaves the coloring valid because, by the definition of a fan, the color of (*X*,*F*\[*i*+1\]) was free on *F*\[*i*\].

### cd-path {#cd-path}

*Image: Bicolored path, Misra and Gries edge coloring algorithm.png.* Examples of *cd*<sub>*x*</sub> paths: *ac*,*cg*,*gd* is a red-green<sub>*c*</sub> path and *bd*,*dg* is a red-orange<sub>*d*</sub> path.

Let *c* and *d* be colors. A *cd*<sub>*X*</sub>-path is an edge path that goes through vertex *X*, only contains edges colored *c* or *d*, and is maximal. (We cannot add any other edge with color *c* or *d* to the path.) If neither *c* nor *d* is incident on *X*, there is no such path. If such a path exists, it is unique as at most one edge of each color can be incident on *X*.

### Inverting a cd-path {#Inverting_a_cd-path}

*Image: Inverting a bicolored path, Misra and edge coloring algorithm.png.* Inverting the red-green<sub>*a*</sub> path from the graph on the left results in the graph on the right.

The operation "invert the *cd*<sub>*X*</sub>-path" switches every edge on the path colored *c* to *d* and every edge colored *d* to *c*. Inverting a path can be useful to free a color on *X* if *X* is one of the endpoints of the path: if color *c* but not *d* was incident on *X*, now color *d* but not *c* is incident on *X*, freeing *c* for *X*.

This operation leaves the coloring valid. For vertices on the path that are not endpoints, no new color is added. For endpoints, the operation switches the color of one of its edges between *c* and *d*. This is valid: suppose the endpoint was connected by a *c* edge; then *d* was free on this endpoint because otherwise, this vertex cannot be an endpoint. Since *d* was free, this edge can switch to *d*.

::: {#o13 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Algorithm"}

## Algorithm {#Algorithm}

**algorithm** Misra & Gries edge-coloring algorithm **is** **input:** A graph G. **output:** A proper coloring c of the edges of G.

Let U := E(G)

**while** U ≠ ∅ **do** Let (X,v) be any edge in U. Let F\[1:k\] be a maximal fan of X with F\[1\]=v. Let c be a free color on X and d be a free color on F\[k\]. Invert the cd<sub>X</sub>-path. Let w ∈ {1..k} such that F'=F\[1:w\] is a fan and d is free on F\[w\]. Rotate F'. Set the color of (X,w) to d. U := U − {(X,v)} **end while**

:::

## Proof of correctness {#Proof_of_correctness}

The correctness of the algorithm is proved in three parts. First, it is shown that the inversion of the *cd*<sub>*X*</sub>-path guarantees a *w* ∈ {1,..,*k*} such that *F* = *F*\[1:*w*\] is a fan and *d* is free on *F*\[*w*\]. Then, it is shown that the edge coloring is valid and requires at most Δ+1 colors.

### Path inversion guarantee {#Path_inversion_guarantee}

Prior to the inversion, there are two cases:

**Case 1:** the fan has no edge colored *d*. Since *F* is a maximal fan on *X* and *d* is free on *F*\[*k*\], *d* is free on *X*. Otherwise, suppose an edge (*X*,*u*) has color *d*, then *u* can be added to *F* to make a bigger fan, contradicting with *F* being maximal. Thus, *d* is free on *X*, and since *c* is also free on *X*, there is no *cd*<sub>*X*</sub>-path and the inversion has no effect on the graph. Set *w* = *k*.

**Case 2:** the fan has one edge with color *d*. Let (*X*,*F*\[*i*+1\]) be this edge. Note that *i*+1 ≠ 1 since (*X*,*F*\[1\]) is uncolored. By definition of a fan, *d* is free on *F*\[*i*\]. Also, *i* ≠ *k* since the fan has length *k* but there exists a *F*\[*i*+1\]. We can now show that after the inversion,5 (1): for *j* ∈ {1, ..., *k*–1} \\ {*i*}, the color of (*X*,*F*\[*j*+1\]) is free on *F*\[*j*\].

Note that prior to the inversion, *c* is free on *X* and (*X*,*F*\[*i*+1\]) has color *d*, so the other edges in the fan, i.e., all (*X*,*F*\[*j*+1\]) above, cannot have color *c* or *d*. Since the inversion only affects edges that are colored *c* or *d*, (1) holds.

**Case2.1:** *F*\[*i*\] is not on the *cd*<sub>*X*</sub>-path. The inversion will not affect the set of free colors on *F*\[*i*\], and *d* will remain free on it. By (1), *F* = *F*\[1:*i*\] is a valid fan, and we can set *w* = *i*.

**Case2.2:** *F*\[*i*\] is on the *cd*<sub>*X*</sub>-path. Below, we can show that *F*\[1:*k*\] is still a fan after the inversion and *d* remains free on *F*\[*k*\], so we can set *w* = *k*.

Since *d* was free on *F*\[*i*\] before the inversion and *F*\[*i*\] is on the *cd*<sub>*X*</sub>-path, *F*\[*i*\] is an endpoint of the path and *c* will be free on *F*\[*i*\] after the inversion. The inversion will change the color of (*X*,*F*\[*i*+1\]) from *d* to *c*. Thus, since *c* is now free on *F*\[*i*\] and (1) holds, the whole *F* remains a fan. Also, *d* remains free on *F*\[*k*\], since *F*\[*k*\] is not on the *cd*<sub>*X*</sub>-path. (Suppose that it is; since *d* is free on *F*\[*k*\], then it would have to be an endpoint of the path, but *X* and *F*\[*i*\] are the endpoints.)

### The edge coloring is valid {#The_edge_coloring_is_valid}

This can be shown by [induction](wikipedia://en/18881) on the number of colored edges. In the base case, no edge is colored; this is valid. For the inductive step, suppose this was true at the end of the previous iteration. In the current iteration, after inverting the path, *d* will be free on *X*, and by the previous result, it will also be free on *w*. Rotating *F* does not compromise the validity of the coloring. Thus, after setting the color of (*X*,*w*) to *d*, the coloring is still valid.

### The algorithm requires at most Δ+1 colors {#The_algorithm_requires_at_most_Δ+1_colors}

In a given step, only colors *c* and *d* are used. *F*\[*k*\] has at most Δ colored edges, so Δ+1 colors in total ensures that we can pick *d*. This leaves Δ colors for *c*. Since there is at least one uncolored edge incident on *X*, and its degree is bounded by Δ, there are most Δ–1 colors incident on *X* currently, which leaves at least one choice for *c*.

:::

## Complexity {#Complexity}

In every iteration of the loop, one additional edge gets colored. Hence, the loop will run $O(|E|)$ times. Finding the maximal fan, the colors *c* and *d* and invert the *cd*<sub>*X*</sub>-path can be done in $O(|V|)$ time. Finding *w* and rotating *F* takes $O(\Delta)\in O(|V|)$ time. Finding and removing an edge can be done using a stack in constant time (pop the last element) and this stack can be populated in $O(|E|)$ time. Thus, each iteration of the loop takes $O(|V|)$ time, and the total running time is $O(|E|+|E||V|)=O(|E||V|)$.
