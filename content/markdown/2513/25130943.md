# Free independence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Free independence"}

In the mathematical theory of [free probability](wikipedia://en/679696), the notion of **free independence** was introduced by [Dan Voiculescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Voiculescu_%28mathematician%29).[^o2] The definition of free independence is parallel to the classical definition of [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_%28probability%29), except that the role of Cartesian products of [measure spaces](wikipedia://en/45270) (corresponding to [tensor products](wikipedia://en/30844) of their function algebras) is played by the notion of a [free product](wikipedia://en/360036) of (non-commutative) probability spaces.

:::

In the context of Voiculescu's free [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), many classical-probability theorems or phenomena have free probability analogs: the same theorem or phenomenon holds (perhaps with slight modifications) if the classical notion of independence is replaced by free independence. Examples of this include: the free [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406); notions of [free convolution](wikipedia://en/20269925); existence of [free stochastic calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_stochastic_calculus) and so on.

Let $(A,\phi)$ be a [non-commutative probability space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative_probability_space), i.e. a [unital](wikipedia://en/14962) [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) $A$ over $\mathbb{C}$ equipped with a [unital](wikipedia://en/29268538) [linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) $\phi:A\to\mathbb{C}$. As an example, one could take, for a probability measure $\mu$,

$$A = L^\infty(\mathbb{R},\mu),\phi(f) = \int f(t)\,d\mu(t).$$

Another example may be $A=M_N$, the algebra of $N\times N$ matrices with the functional given by the normalized trace $\phi=\frac{1}{N}Tr$. Even more generally, $A$ could be a [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) and $\phi$ a state on $A$. A final example is the [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) $A=\mathbb{C}\Gamma$ of a (discrete) [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $\Gamma$ with the functional $\phi$ given by the group trace $\phi (g) = \delta_{g=e},g\in \Gamma$.

Let $\{A_i : i\in I\}$ be a family of unital subalgebras of $A$.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition"}

**Definition**. The family $\{A_i : i\in I\}$ is called *freely independent* if $\phi(x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n) =0$ whenever $\phi(x_j)=0$, $x_j \in A_{i(j)}$ and $i(1)\neq i(2), i(2)\neq i(3),\dots$.

If $X_i\in A$, $i\in I$ is a family of elements of $A$ (these can be thought of as random variables in $A$), they are called *freely independent* if the algebras $A_i$ generated by $1$ and $X_i$ are freely independent.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples of free independence"}

## Examples of free independence {#Examples_of_free_independence}

- Let $\Gamma$ be the [free product](wikipedia://en/360036) of groups $\Gamma_i,i\in I$, let $A=\mathbb{C}\Gamma$ be the group algebra, $\phi(g)=\delta_{g=e}$ be the group trace, and set $A_i=\mathbb{C}\Gamma_i\subset A$. Then $A_i:i\in I$ are freely independent.
- Let $U_i(N),i=1,2$ be $N\times N$ unitary [random matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrices), taken independently at random from the $N\times N$ [unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993) (with respect to the [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645)). Then $U_1(N),U_2(N)$ become asymptotically freely independent as $N\to\infty$. (Asymptotic freeness means that the definition of freeness holds in the limit as $N\to\infty$).
- More generally, independent [random matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrices) tend to be asymptotically freely independent, under certain conditions.

:::
