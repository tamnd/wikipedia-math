# Functional area

*Image: FR-AAV-2020-simplified.png.* Functional areas (AAV) of [Metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France) in 2020, broken down by [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_France) (commune borders as of January 1, 2020): \#f23f00

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Functional area"}

An ***aire d'attraction d'une ville*** (or **AAV**, literally meaning "catchment area of a city") is a statistical area used by [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France)'s national statistics office [INSEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_national_de_la_statistique_et_des_études_économiques) since 2020, officially translated as **functional area** in English by INSEE,[^o2] which consists of a densely populated [urban agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) and the surrounding [exurbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exurb), towns and intervening rural areas that are socioeconomically tied to the central urban agglomeration, as measured by [commuting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuting) patterns.[^n1] INSEE's functional area (AAV) is therefore akin to what is most often called [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) in English.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

INSEE's AAV follows the same definition as the [Functional Urban Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larger_urban_zone) (FUA) used by [Eurostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostat) and the [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OECD), and the AAVs are thus strictly comparable to the FUAs.[^o2] Before 2020, INSEE used another metropolitan statistical area, the *[aire urbaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area_%28France%29)* (AU), which was defined differently than the AAV, but the AU has now been discontinued and replaced with the AAV in order to facilitate international comparisons with Eurostat's FUAs.[^o2]

The functional area is a grouping of [communes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_France) comprising a 'population and employment centre' (*pôle de population et d'emploi* in French),[^o2][^o5] which Eurostat calls "city" or "greater city" (depending on the FUA),[^o6] defined according to population and employment criteria, and an outlying 'commuting zone' (*couronne* in French),[^o2][^o5] which Eurostats calls "commuting zone" in English,[^o6] like INSEE, but *zone de navettage* in French[^o7] (unlike INSEE which calls it *couronne*), in which at least 15% of the working population work in the population and employment centre.[^o2]

:::
