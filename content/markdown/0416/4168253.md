# Richard Rufus of Cornwall

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Rufus of Cornwall"}

**Richard Rufus** (Ricardus Rufus, lit. "Richard the Red"; d. c. AD 1260) was a [Cornish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornish_people) [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) [scholastic philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholastic_philosophy) and [theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Richard Rufus who studied at [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) and at [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) starting from the 1220s. He became a Franciscan around 1230. Rufus was one of the first medieval philosophers to write on [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308) and his commentaries are the earliest known among those which have survived. He also wrote influential commentaries on [Peter Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Lombard)'s *Sentences*. Rufus was influenced by [Robert Grosseteste](wikipedia://en/94721), [Alexander of Hales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_of_Hales), [Richard Fishacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Fishacre), and [Johannes Philoponus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Philoponus), and in turn influenced [Bonaventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonaventure) and [Franciscus Meyronnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscus_Meyronnes). [Roger Bacon](wikipedia://en/25879) was a fervent critic of Rufus, claiming that his fame was greatest with the ignorant multitude; on the other hand, [Thomas of Eccleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_of_Eccleston) praised him as an excellent lecturer. [Adam de Marisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_de_Marisco) describes him in a letter to Grosseteste as "a man lacking in command of the English tongue, yet of most honest conversation and unblemished reputation, learned in human and divine literature." The reason given for his lack of proficiency with English is that he was primarily a [Cornish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornish_language) speaker.
