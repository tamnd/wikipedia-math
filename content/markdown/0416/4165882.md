# Convex geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Convex geometry"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **convex geometry** is the branch of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) studying [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292), mainly in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). Convex sets occur naturally in many areas: [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201), [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153), [integral geometry](wikipedia://en/349336), [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), etc.

:::

## Classification {#Classification}

According to the [Mathematics Subject Classification](wikipedia://en/3008091) MSC2010,[^o3] the mathematical discipline *Convex and Discrete Geometry* includes three major branches:[^o4]

- general convexity
- polytopes and polyhedra
- discrete geometry

(though only portions of the latter two are included in convex geometry).

General convexity is further subdivided as follows:[^o5]

- axiomatic and generalized convexity
- convex sets without dimension restrictions
- convex sets in topological vector spaces
- convex sets in 2 dimensions (including convex curves)
- convex sets in 3 dimensions (including convex surfaces)
- convex sets in *n* dimensions (including convex hypersurfaces)
- finite-dimensional Banach spaces
- random convex sets and integral geometry
- asymptotic theory of convex bodies
- approximation by convex sets
- variants of convex sets (star-shaped, (*m, n*)-convex, etc.)
- Helly-type theorems and geometric transversal theory
- other problems of combinatorial convexity
- length, area, volume
- [mixed volumes](wikipedia://en/32953045) and related topics
- [valuations on convex bodies](wikipedia://en/63011965)
- inequalities and extremum problems
- convex functions and convex programs
- spherical and hyperbolic convexity

## Historical note {#Historical_note}

Convex geometry is a relatively young mathematical discipline. Although the first known contributions to convex geometry date back to antiquity and can be traced in the works of [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331) and [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844), it became an independent branch of mathematics at the turn of the 20th century, mainly due to the works of [Hermann Brunn](wikipedia://en/32151034) and [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537) in dimensions two and three. A big part of their results was soon generalized to spaces of higher dimensions, and in 1934 [T. Bonnesen](wikipedia://en/50669027) and [W. Fenchel](wikipedia://en/4784468) gave a comprehensive survey of convex geometry in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>n</sup>. Further development of convex geometry in the 20th century and its relations to numerous mathematical disciplines are summarized in the *Handbook of convex geometry* edited by P. M. Gruber and J. M. Wills.
