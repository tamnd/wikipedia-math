# Robin Plackett

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Robin Plackett"}

**Robert "Robin" Lewis Plackett**[^o4] (3 September 1920 – 23 June 2009[^o5]) was an English statistician best known for his contributions to the [history of statistics](wikipedia://en/14986442) and to [experimental design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_design), most notably the [Plackett–Burman designs](wikipedia://en/7300309).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Plackett was born in [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool) and attended [Liverpool Collegiate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Collegiate_School) from 1932 to 1939.[^o2] He then attended [Clare College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge), where he graduated in 1942.[^o7]

## Early career {#Early_career}

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Plackett was requested to work for the [Ministry of Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Supply), in SR17 which was a statistical branch. He began to develop a methodology for applying statistical knowledge, and would pass it down to new recruits.[^o7]

## First scientific paper {#First_scientific_paper}

In 1946, he would publish his first paper which was written jointly with Peter Burman in a journal called *[Biometrika](wikipedia://en/1734178)*. The paper, titled "The design of optimum multifactorial experiments", introduced [Plackett–Burman](wikipedia://en/7300309) experimental designs.[^o7][^o10]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

In 1947, he became a lecturer at Liverpool University. He would also publish research on the history of statistics. Then, in 1962, he took a short post for the Professor of Statistics at [King's College, Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Durham) before the college merged with [Newcastle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_University) in 1963.[^o7]

He was the first professor of statistics at Newcastle University and held the post until his retirement in 1983.[^o12] In 1987 the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) awarded him the [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Gold, having awarded him both the bronze and silver medals earlier in his career.[^o7]

He authored several books on statistics, including *Principles of Regression Analysis* (1960), *The Analysis of Categorical Data* (1974) and *An Introduction to the Interpretation of Quantal Responses in Biology* (1979, with P. S. Hewlett).[^o7]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Plackett had a keen interest in climbing. He was married to his wife, Carol, for 65 years. He also had three children: Adam, [Jane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Plackett) and Martin.[^o7]
