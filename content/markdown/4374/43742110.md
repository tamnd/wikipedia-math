# Baumgartner's axiom

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Baumgartner's axiom"}

In [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), **Baumgartner's axiom (BA)** can be one of three different [axioms](wikipedia://en/928) introduced by [James Earl Baumgartner](wikipedia://en/5825279).

:::

A subset of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) is said to be $\aleph_1$-[dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) if every two points are separated by exactly $\aleph_1$ other points, where $\aleph_1$ is the [smallest uncountable cardinality](wikipedia://en/293961). This would be true for the real line itself under the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705). An axiom introduced by (Baumgartner 1973) states that all $\aleph_1$-[dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) subsets of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) are [order-isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order-isomorphic), providing a higher-cardinality analogue of [Cantor's isomorphism theorem](wikipedia://en/68245955) that countable dense subsets are isomorphic. Baumgartner's axiom is a consequence of the [proper forcing axiom](wikipedia://en/6250799). It is consistent with a combination of [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC), [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783), and the negation of the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705),[^o3] but not implied by those hypotheses.[^o4]

Another axiom introduced by (Baumgartner 1975) states that [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783) for [partially ordered sets](wikipedia://en/23572) MA<sub>*P*</sub>(*κ*) is true for all [partially ordered sets](wikipedia://en/23572) *P* that are countable closed, well met and ℵ<sub>1</sub>-linked and all [cardinals](wikipedia://en/6173) κ less than 2<sup>ℵ<sub>1</sub></sup>.

Baumgartner's **axiom A** is an axiom for partially ordered sets introduced in (Baumgartner 1983, section 7). A partial order (*P*, ≤) is said to satisfy axiom A if there is a family ≤<sub>*n*</sub> of partial orderings on *P* for *n* = 0, 1, 2, ... such that

1. ≤<sub>0</sub> is the same as ≤
1. If *p* ≤<sub>*n*+1</sub>*q* then *p* ≤<sub>*n*</sub>*q*
1. If there is a sequence *p*<sub>*n*</sub> with *p*<sub>*n*+1</sub> ≤<sub>*n*</sub> *p*<sub>*n*</sub> then there is a *q* with *q* ≤<sub>*n*</sub> *p*<sub>*n*</sub> for all *n*.
1. If *I* is a pairwise incompatible subset of *P* then for all *p* and for all natural numbers *n* there is a *q* such that *q* ≤<sub>*n*</sub> *p* and the number of elements of *I* compatible with *q* is countable.
