# Mittag-Leffler's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mittag-Leffler's theorem"}

*Image: Gösta\_Mittag-Leffler\_x\_Brauer.jpg.* Portrait of Gösta Mittag-Leffler

In [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), **Mittag-Leffler's theorem** concerns the existence of [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857) with prescribed [poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_%28complex_analysis%29). Conversely, it can be used to express any meromorphic function as a sum of [partial fractions](wikipedia://en/5217105). It is sister to the [Weierstrass factorization theorem](wikipedia://en/1585155), which asserts existence of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) with prescribed [zeros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_%28complex_analysis%29).

:::

The theorem is named after the Swedish mathematician [Gösta Mittag-Leffler](wikipedia://en/1438792) who published versions of the theorem in 1876 and 1884.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

Let $U$ be an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) in $\mathbb C$ and $E \subset U$ be a subset whose [limit points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_point), if any, occur on the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of $U$. For each $a$ in $E$, let $p_a(z)$ be a polynomial in $1/(z-a)$ without constant coefficient, i.e. of the form

$$p_a(z) = \sum_{n=1}^{N_a} \frac{c_{a,n}}{(z-a)^n}.$$

Then there exists a [meromorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meromorphic) function $f$ on $U$ whose [poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_%28complex_analysis%29) are precisely the elements of $E$ and such that for each such pole $a \in E$, the function $f(z)-p_a(z)$ has only a [removable singularity](wikipedia://en/81644) at $a$; in particular, the [principal part](wikipedia://en/1225337) of $f$ at $a$ is $p_a(z)$. Furthermore, any other meromorphic function $g$ on $U$ with these properties can be obtained as $g=f+h$, where $h$ is an arbitrary [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) function on $U$. (Here, note that a function being analytic and holomorphic are equivalent attributes)

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof sketch"}

### Proof sketch {#Proof_sketch}

One possible proof outline is as follows. If $E$ is finite, it suffices to take $f(z) = \sum_{a \in E} p_a(z)$. If $E$ is not finite, consider the finite sum $S_F(z) = \sum_{a \in F} p_a(z)$ where $F$ is a finite subset of $E$. While the $S_F(z)$ may not converge as *F* approaches *E*, one may subtract well-chosen [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) with poles outside of $U$ (provided by [Runge's theorem](wikipedia://en/3199591)) without changing the principal parts of the $S_F(z)$ and in such a way that convergence is guaranteed.

:::

:::

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Suppose that we desire a meromorphic function with simple poles of [residue](wikipedia://en/73102) 1 at all positive integers. With notation as above, letting

$$p_k(z) = \frac{1}{z-k}$$

and $E = \mathbb{Z}^+$, Mittag-Leffler's theorem asserts the existence of a meromorphic function $f$ with principal part $p_k(z)$ at $z=k$ for each positive integer $k$. More constructively we can let

$$f(z) = z\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{k(z-k)} .$$

This series [converges normally](wikipedia://en/7455080) on any [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) subset of $\mathbb{C} \smallsetminus \mathbb{Z}^+$ (as can be shown using the [M-test](wikipedia://en/444456)) to a meromorphic function with the desired properties.

:::

## Pole expansions of meromorphic functions {#Pole_expansions_of_meromorphic_functions}

Here are some examples of pole expansions of meromorphic functions:

$$\pi\csc(\pi z) = \sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} \frac{(-1)^n}{z-n} = \frac1z + 2z\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{z^2 - n^2}$$

$$\pi\sec(\pi z) = \sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{z-\left(n+\frac12\right)} = 2\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{n-1}(n+\frac12)}{z^2-(n+\frac12)^2}$$

$$\pi\cot(\pi z) = \lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=-N}^N \frac1{z-n} = \frac1z + 2z\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac1{z^2 - n^2}$$

$$\pi\tan(\pi z) = \lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=-N}^N \frac{-1}{z-(n+\frac12)} = 2z\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{-1}{z^2-(n+\frac12)^2}$$

$$(\pi\csc(\pi z))^2 = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \frac1{(z-n)^2}$$

$$(\pi\sec(\pi z))^2 = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \frac1{(z-(n+\frac12))^2}$$
