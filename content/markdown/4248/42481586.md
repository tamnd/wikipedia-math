# Alfred Lodge

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alfred Lodge"}

Professor **Alfred Lodge** MA (1854 – 1 December 1937), was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), author, and the first president of [The Mathematical Association](wikipedia://en/2742403).

:::

Alfred Lodge was born in 1854 at [Penkhull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penkhull), [Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire), one of nine children to Oliver Lodge (1826–1884) and Grace, née Heath (1826–1879). His siblings included [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) [Sir Oliver Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Oliver_Lodge), and historians [Sir Richard Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Richard_Lodge) and [Eleanor Constance Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Constance_Lodge). He attended [Horncastle Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth's_Grammar_School,_Horncastle), afterwards studying at [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford). In 1876 he became a fellow of [St John's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Oxford), and in 1884 joined the [Royal Indian Engineering College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Indian_Engineering_College) at [Egham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egham), there becoming a professor of [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210), succeeding [Joseph Wolstenholme](wikipedia://en/98915) in 1889. From 1904 until after the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) he was a master at [Charterhouse School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charterhouse_School).[^o3][^o4] In 1897 Lodge became the first president of [The Mathematical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Association) after its name change from The [Association for the Improvement of Geometrical Teaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_the_Improvement_of_Geometrical_Teaching).[^o5]

Lodge was at times associated with his brother Oliver Lodge's preoccupation with [psychic phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritualism_%28movement%29), although stating that his interest only lay where this seemed to apply to mathematics.[^o4]

Alfred Lodge died at [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford) on 1 December 1937. He was father of two sons, Charles and Christopher.[^o3][^o4]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- 1895 – *Mensuration for senior students*.
- 1899 – *Matter, Ether and Motion: The Factors and Relations of Physical Science*, edited English edition by Professor Alfred Lodge.[^o7]
- 1904 – *Elementary mechanics including hydrostatics and pneumatics*, with Oliver Lodge and Charles S. Lodge.
- 1905 – *Differential Calculus for Beginners*.
- 1905 – *Integral calculus for beginners*.
- 1906 – "Semi-convergent Series for JnX" in *Report of the [British Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Science_Association)*, 76th. Annual Meeting, York
- 1910 – "Reports on the State of Science" committee report on *The Further tabulation of [Bessel Functions](wikipedia://en/4700)*, for the [British Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), 80th Annual Meeting, Sheffield[^o8]
- 1923 – *Differential Calculus for Beginners – Primary Source Edition*.
- 1927 – "The Graphic Solution of Quadratic Equations", *[The Mathematical Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Gazette)*[^o9]
- 1933 – paper on the "[Larmor-Lorentz transformations](wikipedia://en/18404)", published in the *[Philosophical Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_Magazine)*; co-written Oliver Lodge[^o10]
