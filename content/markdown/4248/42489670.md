# Sidney Holgate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sidney Holgate"}

**Sidney Holgate**, (9 September 1918 – 17 May 2003) was a British mathematician and [academic administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_administrator).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Holgate was born on 9 September 1918 in [Hucknall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hucknall), Nottinghamshire, England.[^o4] He was educated at [Henry Mellish School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Mellish_School_and_Specialist_Sports_College). He won a scholarship to [Hatfield College, Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield_College,_Durham), where he studied mathematics and eventually became "[senior man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_man)" (i.e. president of the [junior common room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_common_room)).[^o4] He was also President of the [Durham Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Union) for [Michaelmas term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michaelmas_term) of 1940.[^o5] He graduated with a [first class honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_honours) [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) (BA) degree in 1940.[^o6] He remained at Durham to train as a teacher and competed the Diploma in the Theory and Practice of Teaching (DThPT) in 1941.[^o6]

Following the outbreak of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Holgate was found unacceptable for wartime service on medical grounds.[^o4] After graduation, he instead taught for a year at [Nottingham High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_High_School).[^o4] However, in 1942, he returned to Durham as a [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) to teach mathematics and navigation to Royal Air Force aircrew cadets.[^o4][^o6] As such, he was commissioned in the [Royal Air Force Volunteer Reserve (Training Branch)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force_Volunteer_Reserve_%28Training_Branch%29), serving from 1942 to 1945.[^o6] During this time he continued his studies and completed a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) (MA) degree in 1943.[^o6] He also undertook research "on the application of [complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variables) to problems in elasticity" and completed his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) (PhD) degree in 1945.[^o4]

In 1946, Holgate became secretary of the [Durham Colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleges_of_Durham_University); in that role, he was the senior administrator of the University's colleges in the city of Durham, excluding King's College in Newcastle.[^o7] He was then [master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_%28college%29) of [Grey College, Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_College,_Durham) from its foundation in 1959.[^o7] Grey College was the first postwar college established in Durham, and he oversaw its development from construction onwards.[^o4] He additionally served as [pro-vice-chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-vice-chancellor) and sub-warden of [Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University) from 1964 to 1969.[^o4][^o7] He retired in September 1980, and was succeeded as master of Grey College by [Eric Halladay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Halladay).[^o8]

In addition to his full-time work at Durham University, Holgate was involved with the [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University) (OU) from its foundation until his retirement.[^o4] He was a member of its academic advisory committee from 1969 to 1981, serving as its vice-chair from 1972 to 1975 and its chair from 1975 to 1977.[^o6] The OU awarded him an honorary [Doctor of the University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_the_University) (DUniv) degree in 1980.[^o6]

In the [1981 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_New_Year_Honours), Holgate was appointed [Commander of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (CBE).

Holgate married Isabel "Belle" Armorey in 1942.[^o6] They did not have any children.[^o4] He died on 17 May 2003, aged 84.[^o4] He had been living in a nursing home in Durham at the time of his death.
