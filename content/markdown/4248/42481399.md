# Henry S. Richardson

the philosopher

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Henry S. Richardson"}

**Henry Shattuck Richardson** (born 1955)[^o3][^o4] is an American philosopher, author, and professor of philosophy at [Georgetown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University), where he is also a senior research scholar at the [Kennedy Institute of Ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Institute_of_Ethics).[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Richardson is the son of Anne Richardson, who was once the chair of [Reading is Fundamental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_is_Fundamental), and the politician and lawyer [Elliot Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_Richardson), who served as [United States Secretary of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_Defense), [Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Attorney_General), and [Secretary of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_Commerce).[^o8]

Richardson graduated *[magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_cum_laude)* from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in 1977. He then received a [J.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor) from [Harvard Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Law_School) and an [M.P.P.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Public_Policy) from the [John F. Kennedy School of Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy_School_of_Government) (supervised by [Martha Nussbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Nussbaum)), both in 1981. In 1986, Richardson received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in Philosophy from Harvard University under the supervision of [John Rawls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rawls); his thesis was titled *Rational deliberation of ends*.[^o5][^o3]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Richardson's main work has centred on [practical reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_reasoning). His first book, *Practical Reasoning about Final Ends*,[^o10] focused on individual reasoning, whilst his second book, *Democratic Autonomy: Public Reasoning about the Ends of Policy*,[^o11] which won the [Herbert A. Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_A._Simon) Best Book Award in Public Administration,[^o12] and the David Easton Award in the Foundations of Political Theory,[^o13] dealt with collective reasoning.

In addition to working on practical reasoning and moral and political philosophy, Richardson has written on [bioethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioethics). He has twice been a visiting scholar at the Department of Bioethics at the (U.S.) National Institutes of Health.

From 2008 to 2018, he was the Editor of the academic journal *[Ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics_%28journal%29)*.[^o14] From 2010 to 2013, through his work as a member of the World Commission on the Ethics of Scientific Knowledge and Technology (COMEST), he has acted as an advisor to the Director General of [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO) on ethical issues relating to science and technology.[^o5][^o6]

In September 2014 Richardson began a two-year tenure as president of the [Human Development and Capability Association (HDCA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_and_Capability_Association).[^o15] He was succeeded by the economist [Ravi Kanbur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_Kanbur).[^o16] From 2014 to 2017, he served as one of two "coordinating lead authors" on the orienting normative chapter of the three-volume report of the [International Panel of Social Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Panel_of_Social_Progress),[^o17] *Rethinking Society for the 21st Century.*[^o18]

In 2019, Richardson was awarded a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship).[^o19]
