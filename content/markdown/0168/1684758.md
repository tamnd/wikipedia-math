# Nielsen–Thurston classification

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nielsen–Thurston classification"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Thurston's [classification theorem](wikipedia://en/4576710)** characterizes [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) of a [compact orientable surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_surface). [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190)'s theorem completes the work initiated by (txt).

:::

Given a homeomorphism *f* : *S* → *S*, there is a map *g* [isotopic](wikipedia://en/212250#Isotopy) to *f* such that at least one of the following holds:

- *g* is periodic, i.e. some power of *g* is the identity;
- *g* preserves some finite union of disjoint simple closed curves on *S* (in this case, *g* is called *reducible*); or
- *g* is [pseudo-Anosov](wikipedia://en/2327355).

The case where *S* is a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) (i.e., a surface whose [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) is one) is handled separately (see [torus bundle](wikipedia://en/680735)) and was known before Thurston's work. If the genus of *S* is two or greater, then *S* is naturally [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/241291), and the tools of [Teichmüller theory](wikipedia://en/1835661) become useful. In what follows, we assume *S* has genus at least two, as this is the case Thurston considered. (Note, however, that the cases where *S* has [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) or is not [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable) are definitely still of interest.)

The three types in this classification are **not** mutually exclusive, though a *pseudo-Anosov* homeomorphism is never *periodic* or *reducible*. A *reducible* homeomorphism *g* can be further analyzed by cutting the surface along the preserved union of simple closed curves *Γ*. Each of the resulting compact surfaces *with [boundary](wikipedia://en/2073470)* is acted upon by some power (i.e. [iterated composition](wikipedia://en/195947)) of *g*, and the classification can again be applied to this homeomorphism.

## The mapping class group for surfaces of higher genus {#The_mapping_class_group_for_surfaces_of_higher_genus}

Thurston's classification applies to homeomorphisms of orientable surfaces of genus ≥ 2, but the type of a homeomorphism only depends on its associated element of the [mapping class group](wikipedia://en/793295) *Mod(S)*. In fact, the proof of the classification theorem leads to a [canonical](wikipedia://en/515096) representative of each mapping class with good geometric properties. For example:

- When *g* is periodic, there is an element of its mapping class that is an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) of a [hyperbolic structure](wikipedia://en/241291) on *S*.
- When *g* is [pseudo-Anosov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-Anosov), there is an element of its mapping class that preserves a pair of [transverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transversality_%28mathematics%29) singular [foliations](wikipedia://en/371227) of *S*, stretching the leaves of one (the *unstable* foliation) while contracting the leaves of the other (the *stable* foliation).

## Mapping tori {#Mapping_tori}

Thurston's original motivation for developing this classification was to find geometric structures on *mapping tori* of the type predicted by the [Geometrization conjecture](wikipedia://en/220642). The [mapping torus](wikipedia://en/21034384) *M<sub>g</sub>* of a homeomorphism *g* of a surface *S* is the [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) obtained from *S* × \[0,1\] by gluing *S* × {0} to *S* × {1} using *g*. If S has genus at least two, the geometric structure of *M<sub>g</sub>* is related to the type of *g* in the classification as follows:

- If *g* is periodic, then *M<sub>g</sub>* has an *H*<sup>2</sup> × R structure;
- If *g* is reducible, then *M<sub>g</sub>* has [incompressible](wikipedia://en/896174) [tori](wikipedia://en/74800), and should be cut along these tori to yield pieces that each have geometric structures (the [JSJ decomposition](wikipedia://en/228599));
- If *g* is [pseudo-Anosov](wikipedia://en/2327355), then *M<sub>g</sub>* has a [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/241291) (i.e. *H*<sup>3</sup>) structure.

The first two cases are comparatively easy, while the existence of a hyperbolic structure on the mapping torus of a pseudo-Anosov homeomorphism is a deep and difficult theorem (also due to [Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190)). The hyperbolic 3-manifolds that arise in this way are called *fibered* because they are [surface bundles over the circle](wikipedia://en/2011690), and these manifolds are treated separately in the proof of Thurston's [geometrization theorem](wikipedia://en/220642) for [Haken manifolds](wikipedia://en/236567). Fibered hyperbolic 3-manifolds have a number of interesting and pathological properties; for example, Cannon and Thurston showed that the surface subgroup of the arising [Kleinian group](wikipedia://en/1362652) has [limit set](wikipedia://en/5492198) which is a [sphere-filling curve](wikipedia://en/352398).

## Fixed point classification {#Fixed_point_classification}

The three types of surface homeomorphisms are also related to the [dynamics](wikipedia://en/9087) of the mapping class group Mod(*S*) on the [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661) *T*(*S*). Thurston introduced a [compactification](wikipedia://en/6617) of *T*(*S*) that is homeomorphic to a closed ball, and to which the action of Mod(*S*) extends naturally. The type of an element *g* of the mapping class group in the Thurston classification is related to its fixed points when acting on the compactification of *T*(*S*):

- If *g* is periodic, then there is a fixed point within *T*(*S*); this point corresponds to a [hyperbolic structure](wikipedia://en/241291) on *S* whose [isometry group](wikipedia://en/1101694) contains an element isotopic to *g*;
- If *g* is [pseudo-Anosov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-Anosov), then *g* has no fixed points in *T*(*S*) but has a pair of fixed points on the Thurston boundary; these fixed points correspond to the *stable* and *unstable* foliations of *S* preserved by *g*.
- For some reducible mapping classes *g*, there is a single fixed point on the Thurston boundary; an example is a [multi-twist](wikipedia://en/765970) along a [pants decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pants_decomposition) *Γ*. In this case the fixed point of *g* on the Thurston boundary corresponds to *Γ*.

This is reminiscent of the classification of [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/241291) [isometries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometries) into *elliptic*, *parabolic*, and *hyperbolic* types (which have fixed point structures similar to the *periodic*, *reducible*, and *pseudo-Anosov* types listed above).
