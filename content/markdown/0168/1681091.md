# Turing jump

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Turing jump"}

In [computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414), the **Turing jump** or **Turing jump operator**, named for [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208), is an operation that assigns to each [decision problem](wikipedia://en/8336) $X$ a successively harder decision problem $X'$ with the property that $X'$ is not decidable by an [oracle machine](wikipedia://en/22431) with an [oracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_%28computer_science%29) for $X$.

:::

The operator is called a *jump operator* because it increases the [Turing degree](wikipedia://en/764405) of the problem $X$. That is, the problem $X'$ is not [Turing-reducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing-reducible) to $X$. [Post's theorem](wikipedia://en/764468) establishes a relationship between the Turing jump operator and the [arithmetical hierarchy](wikipedia://en/186475) of sets of natural numbers.[^o2] Informally, given a problem, the Turing jump returns the set of Turing machines that halt when given access to an oracle that solves that problem.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Turing jump of $X$ can be thought of as an oracle to the [halting problem](wikipedia://en/21391870#Generalized_to_oracle_machines) for [oracle machines](wikipedia://en/22431) with an oracle for $X$.[^o2]

Formally, given a set $X$ and a [Gödel numbering](wikipedia://en/207790) $\varphi_{i}^{X}$ of the [$X$-computable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_computability) functions, the **Turing jump** $X'$ of $X$ is defined as

$$X'= \{x \mid \varphi_x^X(x) \ \mbox{is defined} \}.$$

The **$n$th Turing jump** $X^{(n)}$ is defined inductively by

$$\begin{aligned} X^{(0)} &= X,\\ X^{(n+1)} &= X^{(n)}{'}. \end{aligned}$$

The **$\omega$ jump** $X^{(\omega)}$ of $X$ is the [effective join](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_join) of the sequence of sets $X^{(n)}$ for $n \in N$:

$$X^{(\omega)} = \{p_i^k \mid i \in \mathbb{N} \text{ and } k \in X^{(i)}\},$$

where $p_{i}$ denotes the $i$th prime.

The notation $0'$ or $\emptyset'$ is often used for the Turing jump of the empty set. It is read *zero-jump* or sometimes *zero-prime*.

Similarly, $0^{(n)}$ is the $n$th jump of the empty set. For finite $n$, these sets are closely related to the [arithmetic hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_hierarchy),[^o5] and is in particular connected to [Post's theorem](wikipedia://en/764468).

The jump can be iterated into [transfinite ordinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfinite_ordinal): there are jump operators $j^\delta$ for sets of natural numbers when $\delta$ is an ordinal that has a code in Kleene's $\mathcal O$ (regardless of code, the resulting jumps are the same by a theorem of Spector),[^o5] in particular the sets $0^{(\alpha)}$ for $\alpha < \omega_{1}^{\operatorname{CK}}$, where $\omega_{1}^{\operatorname{CK}}$ is the [Church–Kleene ordinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church–Kleene_ordinal), are closely related to the [hyperarithmetic hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperarithmetic_hierarchy).[^o2] Beyond $\omega_{1}^{\operatorname{CK}}$, the process can be continued through the countable ordinals of the [constructible universe](wikipedia://en/343335), using Jensen's work on fine structure theory of [Gödel's L](wikipedia://en/343335).[^o6][^o5] The concept has also been generalized to extend to uncountable [regular cardinals](wikipedia://en/374128).[^o7]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

- The Turing jump $0'$ of the empty set is Turing equivalent to the [halting problem](wikipedia://en/21391870).[^o10]
- For each $n$, the set $0^{(n)}$ is [m-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-complete) at level $\Sigma^0_n$ in the [arithmetical hierarchy](wikipedia://en/186475) (by [Post's theorem](wikipedia://en/764468)).
- The set of Gödel numbers of true formulas in the language of [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) with a predicate for $X$ is computable from $X^{(\omega)}$.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- $X'$ is $X$-[computably enumerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computably_enumerable) but not $X$-[computable](wikipedia://en/1139338).
- If $A$ is [Turing-equivalent](wikipedia://en/764405) to $B$, then $A'$ is Turing-equivalent to $B'$. The converse of this implication is not true.
- ([Shore](wikipedia://en/18435038) and [Slaman](wikipedia://en/5693320), 1999) The function mapping $X$ to $X'$ is definable in the [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) of the Turing degrees.[^o10]

Many properties of the Turing jump operator are discussed in the article on [Turing degrees](wikipedia://en/764405).
