# Cesare Arzelà

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cesare Arzelà"}

**Cesare Arzelà** (6 March 1847–15 March 1912) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who taught at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) and is recognized for his contributions in the [theory of functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functions_%28mathematics%29), particularly for his characterization of [sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_%28mathematics%29) of [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122), generalizing the one given earlier by [Giulio Ascoli](wikipedia://en/3855752) in the [Arzelà–Ascoli theorem](wikipedia://en/596622).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was a pupil of the [Scuola Normale Superiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore) of [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) where he graduated in 1869. Arzelà came from a poor household; therefore he could not start his study until 1871, when he studied in Pisa under [Enrico Betti](wikipedia://en/979158) and [Ulisse Dini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulisse_Dini).

He was working in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) (from 1875) and in 1878 obtained the Chair of Algebra at the [University of Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Palermo).

After that, he became a professor in 1880 at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) at the Department of analysis. He conducted research in the field of [theory of functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functions_%28mathematics%29). His most famous student was [Leonida Tonelli](wikipedia://en/10018991).

In 1889 he generalized the [Ascoli](wikipedia://en/3855752) theorem to [Arzelà–Ascoli theorem](wikipedia://en/596622), an important [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) in the theory of functions.

He was a member of the [Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_dei_Lincei), and of several other academies.
