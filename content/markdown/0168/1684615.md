# Counterargument

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Counterargument"}

*Image: Archive dot org edition Key to Uncle Tom's Cabin cover title page table of contents 02.jpg.* [A Key to Uncle Tom's Cabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Key_to_Uncle_Tom's_Cabin) is a book by American author [Harriet Beecher Stowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Beecher_Stowe). It was published to document the veracity of the depiction of [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery) in Stowe's [anti-slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States) novel *[Uncle Tom's Cabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncle_Tom's_Cabin)* (1852).

In [reasoning](wikipedia://en/42446) and [argument mapping](wikipedia://en/6190251), a **counterargument** is an [objection](wikipedia://en/8006711) to an objection. A counterargument can be used to [rebut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebuttal) an objection to a [premise](wikipedia://en/7993337), a main contention or a [lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemma_%28logic%29). Synonyms of counterargument may include rebuttal, reply, counterstatement, counterreason, comeback and response. An attempt to rebut an [argument](wikipedia://en/13018310) may involve generating a counterargument, or finding a [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151).[^n1]

:::

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Use"}

## Use {#Use}

To speak of counterarguments is not to assume that there are only two sides to a given issue nor that there is only one type of counterargument.[^n2] For a given argument, there are often a large number of counterarguments, some of which are not compatible with each other.[^n2]

A counterargument might seek to cast doubt on facts of one or more of the first argument's premises, to show that the first argument's contention does not follow from its premises in a valid manner, or the counterargument might pay little attention to the premises and common structure of the first argument and simply attempt to demonstrate that the truth of a conclusion is incompatible with that of the first argument.

A counterargument can be issued against an argument retroactively from the point of reference of that argument. This form of counterargument — invented by the presocratic philosopher [Parmenides](wikipedia://en/23575) – is commonly referred to as a **retroactive refutation**.[^o7]

:::

## Responding {#Responding}

In a [debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debate) or in speaking contextual evidence, a counterargument can be handled in a variety of ways. Responding to a counterargument does not mean utterly obliterating it. You may concede it, minimise it, dismiss it as irrelevant, or attack the supporting evidence or underlying premise. Even if you grant the existence of a problem, you can differ from your audience on the best solution.[^n3]
