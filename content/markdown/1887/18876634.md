# Murray H. Protter

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Murray H. Protter"}

**Murray Harold Protter** (February 13, 1918 – May 1, 2008) was an American mathematician and educator, known for his contributions to the theory of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), as well as his well-selling textbooks in [Calculus](wikipedia://en/5176).

:::

Protter earned a [M.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) (1937) and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) with the thesis *Generalized Spherical Harmonics* advised by [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972) (1946). During the [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) era, he studied the [aeroelasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroelasticity) and [flutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroelasticity#Flutter) of military air planes at the [Vought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought) aircraft company in [Stratford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratford,_Connecticut) (1943–45). After his graduation, he worked as an assistant professor at [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University) (1947–51), was a researcher at [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) (1951–53) and at [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley)[^o4] (1953–88) where he also was the chairman (1962–65). He also was the Miller Research Professor (1959, 1967) and executive director of the [Miller Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Institute) for Basic Research in Science (1981–83). He was the father of operations researcher [Philip Protter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Protter).

Protter developed "self-paced" learning of mathematics. He was a long-time member (from 1941 onward) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), serving as the [treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer) (1968–72) and the [editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editing) of the book review column.
