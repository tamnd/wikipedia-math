# Quasi-finite field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-finite field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **quasi-finite field**[^o2] is a generalisation of a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615). Standard [local class field theory](wikipedia://en/669532) usually deals with [complete valued fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_valued_field) whose residue field is *finite* (i.e., [non-archimedean local fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-archimedean_local_field)), but the theory applies equally well when the residue field is only assumed quasi-finite.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A **quasi-finite field** is a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088) *K* together with an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) of [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315)

$$\phi : \hat{\mathbb Z} \to \operatorname{Gal}(K_s/K),$$

where *K*<sub>*s*</sub> is an [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of *K* (necessarily separable because *K* is perfect). The [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) *K*<sub>*s*</sub>/*K* is infinite, and the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) is accordingly given the [Krull topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krull_topology). The group $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is the [profinite completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profinite_completion) of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) with respect to its subgroups of finite index.

This definition is equivalent to saying that *K* has a unique (necessarily [cyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_extension)) extension *K*<sub>*n*</sub> of degree *n* for each integer *n* ≥ 1, and that the union of these extensions is equal to *K*<sub>*s*</sub>.[^o6] Moreover, as part of the structure of the quasi-finite field, there is a generator *F*<sub>*n*</sub> for each Gal(*K*<sub>*n*</sub>/*K*), and the generators must be *coherent*, in the sense that if *n* divides *m*, the restriction of *F*<sub>*m*</sub> to *K*<sub>*n*</sub> is equal to *F*<sub>*n*</sub>.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The most basic example, which motivates the definition, is the finite field *K* = **F**<sub>*q*</sub>. It has a unique cyclic extension of degree *n*, namely *K*<sub>*n*</sub> = **F**<sub>*q*<sup>*n*</sup></sub>. The union of the *K*<sub>*n*</sub> is the algebraic closure *K*<sub>*s*</sub>. We take *F*<sub>*n*</sub> to be the [Frobenius element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_element); that is, *F*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*) = *x*<sup>*q*</sup>.

Another example is *K* = **C**((*T*)), the ring of [formal Laurent series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_Laurent_series) in *T* over the field **C** of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). (These are simply [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) in which we also allow finitely many terms of negative degree.) Then *K* has a unique cyclic extension

$$K_n = \mathbf C((T^{1/n}))$$

of degree *n* for each *n* ≥ 1, whose union is an algebraic closure of *K* called the field of [Puiseux series](wikipedia://en/2604429), and a generator of Gal(*K*<sub>*n*</sub>/*K*) is given by

$$F_n(T^{1/n}) = e^{2\pi i/n} T^{1/n}.$$

This construction works if **C** is replaced by any algebraically closed field *C* of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero.[^o9]
