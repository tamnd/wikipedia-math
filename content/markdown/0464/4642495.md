# Number sense

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Number sense"}

In [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology), **number sense** is the term used for the hypothesis that some animals, particularly humans, have a biologically determined ability that allows them to represent and manipulate large numerical quantities. The term was popularized by [Stanislas Dehaene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislas_Dehaene) in his 1997 book "The Number Sense," but originally named by the mathematician [Tobias Dantzig](wikipedia://en/1234390) in his 1930 text [Number: The Language of Science](wikipedia://en/42245723).

:::

Psychologists believe that the number sense in humans can be differentiated into the [approximate number system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximate_number_system), a system that supports the estimation of the [magnitude](wikipedia://en/577301), and the [parallel individuation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_individuation_system), which allows the tracking of individual objects, typically for quantities below 4.[^o2]

There are also some differences in how number sense is defined in math [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition). For example, Gersten and Chard say number sense "refers to a child's fluidity and flexibility with numbers, the sense of what numbers mean and an ability to perform mental mathematics and to look at the world and make comparisons."[^o3][^o4][^o5]

In non-human animals, number sense is not the ability to count, but the ability to perceive changes in the number of things in a collection.[^o6] All mammals, and most birds, will notice if there is a change in the number of their young nearby. Many birds can distinguish two from three.[^o7]

Researchers consider number sense to be of prime importance for children in early [elementary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_education), and the [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1561691) has made number sense a focus area of pre-K through 2nd grade mathematics education.[^o8] An active area of research is to create and test teaching strategies to develop children's number sense. Number sense also refers to the contest hosted by the [University Interscholastic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Interscholastic_League). This contest is a ten-minute test where contestants solve math problems mentally—no calculators, scratch-work, or mark-outs are allowed.[^o9]

## Concepts involved in number sense {#Concepts_involved_in_number_sense}

The term *number sense* involves several concepts of [magnitude](wikipedia://en/577301), [ranking](wikipedia://en/1482394), [comparison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_%28mathematics%29), [measurement](wikipedia://en/19022), [rounding](wikipedia://en/169945), [percents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percent), and [estimation](wikipedia://en/36687154), including:[^o11]

- estimating with large numbers to provide reasonable approximations;
- judging the degree of precision appropriate to a situation;
- understanding the hidden meaning of numbers through analytical and critical thinking (e.g., [Freakonomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freakonomics)[^o12]);
- rounding (understanding reasons for rounding large numbers and limitations in comparisons);
- choosing measurement units to make sense for a given situation;
- solving real-life problems involving percentages and decimal portions;
- comparing physical measurements within and between the U.S. and [metric systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_system); and
- comparing degrees [Fahrenheit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit) and [Celsius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius) in real-life situations.[^o11]

Those concepts are taught in elementary-level education.
