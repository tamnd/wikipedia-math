# Train of thought

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Train of thought"}

The **train of thought**, or occasionally, **chain of thought** refers to the interconnection in the sequence of [ideas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idea) expressed during a connected [discourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discourse) or [thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought), as well as the sequence itself, especially in discussion of how this sequence leads from one idea to another. This construct relates the stringing together of ideas to the way [train cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_car) are connected on a track.

:::

When a reader or listener "loses the train of thought" (i.e., loses the relation between consecutive sentences or phrases, or the relation between non-verbal concepts in an argument or presentation), [comprehension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_comprehension) is lost of the expressed or unexpressed thought.[^o2]

## Origins {#Origins}

The term "train of thoughts" was introduced and elaborated as early as in 1651 by [Thomas Hobbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hobbes) in his *[Leviathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leviathan_%28Hobbes_book%29)*, though with a somewhat different meaning (similar to the meaning used by the British [associationists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Ideas)): By Consequence, or train of thoughts, I understand that succession of one thought to another which is called, to distinguish it from discourse in words, mental discourse. When a man thinketh on anything whatsoever, his next thought after is not altogether so casual as it seems to be. Not every thought to every thought succeeds indifferently.
