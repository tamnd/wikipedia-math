# Dirac–von Neumann axioms

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dirac–von Neumann axioms"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), the **Dirac–von Neumann axioms** give a [mathematical formulation of quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/20728) in terms of [operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932). They were introduced by [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742) in 1930 and [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) in 1932.

:::

## Hilbert space formulation {#Hilbert_space_formulation}

The space $\mathbb{H}$ is a fixed complex [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) of [countably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite) [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) (as a hilbert-basis).

- The [observables](wikipedia://en/294248) of a [quantum system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_system) are defined to be the (possibly [unbounded](wikipedia://en/1422584)) [self-adjoint operators](wikipedia://en/187408) $A$ on $\mathbb{H}$.
- A [state](wikipedia://en/30876419) $\psi$ of the quantum system is a [unit vector](wikipedia://en/167053) of $\mathbb{H}$, up to scalar multiples; or equivalently, a [ray](wikipedia://en/796760) of the Hilbert space $\mathbb{H}$.
- The [expectation value](wikipedia://en/11371560) of an observable *A* for a system in a state $\psi$ is given by the [inner product](wikipedia://en/14856) $\langle \psi, A \psi \rangle$.

## Operator algebra formulation {#Operator_algebra_formulation}

The Dirac–von Neumann axioms can be formulated in terms of a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) as follows.

- The bounded [observables](wikipedia://en/294248) of the quantum mechanical system are defined to be the self-adjoint elements of the C\*-algebra.
- The states of the quantum mechanical system are defined to be the [states](wikipedia://en/454959) of the C\*-algebra (in other words the normalized positive linear functionals $\omega$).
- The value $\omega (A)$ of a state $\omega$ on an element $A$ is the [expectation value](wikipedia://en/11371560) of the observable $A$ if the quantum system is in the state $\omega$.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

If the C\*-algebra is the algebra of all bounded operators on a Hilbert space $\mathbb{H}$, then the bounded observables are just the bounded self-adjoint operators on $\mathbb{H}$. If $v$ is a unit vector of $\mathbb{H}$ then $\omega (A) = \langle v, A v \rangle$ is a state on the C\*-algebra, meaning the unit vectors (up to scalar multiplication) give the states of the system. This is similar to Dirac's formulation of quantum mechanics, though Dirac also allowed unbounded operators, and did not distinguish clearly between self-adjoint and Hermitian operators.

:::
