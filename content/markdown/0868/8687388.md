# Clifford bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clifford bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Clifford bundle** is an [algebra bundle](wikipedia://en/1244529) whose fibers have the structure of a [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305) and whose [local trivializations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_trivialization) respect the algebra structure. There is a natural Clifford bundle associated to any ([pseudo](wikipedia://en/287555)) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) *M* which is called the Clifford bundle of *M*.

:::

This is commonly referred to as the Hopf fibration of S<sup>3</sup>, a topological construction pointed out by Heinz Hopf (1931). But Hopf’s procedure was explicitly based (with due reference) on an earlier geometrical construction of ‘[Clifford parallels](wikipedia://en/22384832)’.[^o2]

## General construction {#General_construction}

Let *V* be a ([real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex](wikipedia://en/5826)) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) together with a [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375) \<·,·>. The [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305) *Cℓ*(*V*) is a natural ([unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra) [associative](wikipedia://en/2112)) [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) generated by *V* subject only to the relation

$$v^2 = \langle v,v\rangle$$

for all *v* in *V*.[^o4] One can construct *Cℓ*(*V*) as a quotient of the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628) of *V* by the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) generated by the above relation.

Like other tensor operations, this construction can be carried out fiberwise on a smooth [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524). Let *E* be a smooth vector bundle over a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) *M*, and let *g* be a smooth symmetric bilinear form on *E*. The **Clifford bundle** of *E* is the [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) whose fibers are the Clifford algebras generated by the fibers of *E*:

$$C\ell(E) = \coprod_{x\in M} C\ell(E_x,g_x)$$

The [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_%28structure%29) of *Cℓ*(*E*) is determined by that of *E* via an [associated bundle](wikipedia://en/398874) construction.

One is most often interested in the case where *g* is [positive-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definite_bilinear_form) or at least [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate_form); that is, when (*E*, *g*) is a Riemannian or pseudo-Riemannian vector bundle. For concreteness, suppose that (*E*, *g*) is a Riemannian vector bundle. The Clifford bundle of *E* can be constructed as follows. Let *Cℓ*<sub>*n*</sub>**R** be the Clifford algebra generated by **R**<sup>*n*</sup> with the [Euclidean metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_metric). The standard action of the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) O(*n*) on **R**<sup>*n*</sup> induces a graded [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) of *Cℓ*<sub>*n*</sub>**R**. The homomorphism

$$\rho : \mathrm O(n) \to \mathrm{Aut}(C\ell_n\mathbb R)$$

is determined by

$$\rho(A)(v_1v_2\cdots v_k) = (Av_1)(Av_2)\cdots(Av_k)$$

where *v*<sub>*i*</sub> are all vectors in **R**<sup>*n*</sup>. The Clifford bundle of *E* is then given by

$$C\ell(E) = F(E) \times_\rho C\ell_n\mathbb R$$

where *F*(*E*) is the [orthonormal frame bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthonormal_frame_bundle) of *E*. It is clear from this construction that the [structure group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_group) of *Cℓ*(*E*) is O(*n*). Since O(*n*) acts by graded automorphisms on *Cℓ*<sub>*n*</sub>**R** it follows that *Cℓ*(*E*) is a bundle of [**Z**<sub>2</sub>-graded algebras](wikipedia://en/469950) over *M*. The Clifford bundle *Cℓ*(*E*) can then be decomposed into even and odd subbundles:

$$C\ell(E) = C\ell^0(E) \oplus C\ell^1(E).$$

If the vector bundle *E* is [orientable](wikipedia://en/187446) then one can reduce the structure group of *Cℓ*(*E*) from O(*n*) to SO(*n*) in the natural manner.

## Clifford bundle of a Riemannian manifold {#Clifford_bundle_of_a_Riemannian_manifold}

If *M* is a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) with [metric](wikipedia://en/195795) *g*, then the Clifford bundle of *M* is the Clifford bundle generated by the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) *TM*. One can also build a Clifford bundle out of the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) *T*\**M*. The metric induces a [natural isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) *TM* = *T*\**M* and therefore an isomorphism *Cℓ*(*TM*) = *Cℓ*(*T*\**M*).

There is a natural [vector bundle isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_bundle_morphism) between the Clifford bundle of *M* and the [exterior bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_bundle) of *M*:

$$C\ell(T^*M) \cong \Lambda(T^*M).$$

This is an isomorphism of vector bundles *not* algebra bundles. The isomorphism is induced from the corresponding isomorphism on each fiber. In this way one can think of sections of the Clifford bundle as [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) on *M* equipped with Clifford multiplication rather than the [wedge product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedge_product) (which is independent of the metric).

The above isomorphism respects the grading in the sense that

$$\begin{aligned} C\ell^0(T^*M) &= \Lambda^{\mathrm{even}}(T^*M)\\ C\ell^1(T^*M) &= \Lambda^{\mathrm{odd}}(T^*M). \end{aligned}$$

### Local description {#Local_description}

For a vector $v \in T_{x}M$ at $x\in M$, and a form $\psi \in \Lambda(T_{x}M)$ the Clifford multiplication[^o7] is defined as

$$v\psi= v\wedge \psi + v \lrcorner \psi,$$

where the metric duality to change vector to the one form is used in the first term.

Then the exterior derivative $d$ and coderivative $\delta$ can be related to the metric connection $\nabla$ using the choice of an orthonormal base $\{ e_{a}\}$ by

$$d=e^{a}\wedge \nabla_{e_a}, \quad \delta = -e^{a}\lrcorner\nabla_{e_a}.$$

Using these definitions, the Dirac-Kähler operator[^o8][^o7] is defined by

$$D = e^{a}\nabla_{e_a}=d-\delta.$$

On a [star domain](wikipedia://en/5983414) the operator can be inverted using [Poincaré lemma](wikipedia://en/600030) for [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) and its [Hodge star](wikipedia://en/310914) dual for [coderivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codifferential).[^o9] Practical way of doing this is by [homotopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_operator) and [cohomotopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codifferential) operators.[^o9][^o10]
