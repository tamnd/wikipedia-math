# Quantum Markov semigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quantum Markov semigroup"}

In [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), a **quantum Markov semigroup** describes the dynamics in a [Markovian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_Property) [open quantum system](wikipedia://en/1079106). The axiomatic definition of the prototype of **quantum Markov semigroups** was first introduced by [A. M. Kossakowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Kossakowski)[^o2] in 1972, and then developed by V. Gorini, [A. M. Kossakowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Kossakowski), [E. C. G. Sudarshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._C._George_Sudarshan)[^o3] and [Göran Lindblad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göran_Lindblad_%28physicist%29)[^o18] in 1976.[^o4]

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

In practice, a [quantum system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_system) will not exist in isolation, but instead will be surrounded by and interact with an environment, which typically has a large number of degrees of freedom (for example an [atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom) interacting with the surrounding radiation field). A complete microscopic description of the degrees of freedom of the environment is typically too complicated. Hence, one looks for simpler descriptions of the dynamics of the open system. In principle, one should investigate the [unitary](wikipedia://en/225999) dynamics of the total system, i.e. the system and the environment, to obtain information about the reduced system of interest by averaging the appropriate observables over the degrees of freedom of the environment. To model the dissipative effects due to the interaction with the environment, the [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874) is replaced by a suitable [master equation](wikipedia://en/840106), such as a [Lindblad equation](wikipedia://en/498795) or a stochastic Schrödinger equation in which the infinite degrees of freedom of the environment are "synthesized" as a few [quantum noises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_noise). Mathematically, time evolution in a Markovian open quantum system is no longer described by means of [one-parameter groups](wikipedia://en/754851) of unitary maps, but one needs to introduce quantum Markov semigroups.

## Definitions {#Definitions}

### Quantum dynamical semigroup (QDS) {#Quantum_dynamical_semigroup_(QDS)}

In general, quantum dynamical semigroups can be defined on [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185), so the dimensionality of the system could be infinite. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a von Neumann algebra acting on [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $\mathcal{H}$, a quantum dynamical semigroup on $\mathcal{A}$ is a collection of bounded operators on $\mathcal{A}$, denoted by $\mathcal{T} := \left( \mathcal{T}_t \right)_{t \ge 0}$, with the following properties:[^o9]

1. $\mathcal{T}_0 \left( a \right) = a$, $\forall a \in \mathcal{A}$,
1. $\mathcal{T}_{t + s} \left( a \right) = \mathcal{T}_t \left( \mathcal{T}_s \left( a \right) \right)$, $\forall s, t \ge 0$, $\forall a \in \mathcal{A}$,
1. $\mathcal{T}_t$ is [completely positive](wikipedia://en/6083511) for all $t \ge 0$,
1. $\mathcal{T}_t$ is a $\sigma$-weakly continuous operator in $\mathcal{A}$ for all $t \ge 0$,
1. For all $a \in \mathcal{A}$, the map $t \mapsto \mathcal{T}_t \left( a \right)$ is continuous with respect to the $\sigma$-weak topology on $\mathcal{A}$.

Under the condition of complete positivity, the operators $\mathcal{T}_t$ are $\sigma$-weakly continuous if and only if $\mathcal{T}_t$ are normal.[^o9] Recall that, letting $\mathcal{A}_+$ denote the [convex cone](wikipedia://en/3558266) of positive elements in $\mathcal{A}$, a positive operator $T : \mathcal{A} \rightarrow \mathcal{A}$ is said to be normal if for every increasing [net](wikipedia://en/22170) $\left( x_\alpha \right)_\alpha$ in $\mathcal{A}_+$ with [least upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_upper_bound) $x$ in $\mathcal{A}_+$ one has

$$\lim_{\alpha} \langle u, (T x_\alpha) u \rangle = \sup_{\alpha} \langle u, (T x_\alpha) u \rangle = \langle u, (T x) u \rangle$$

for each $u$ in a [norm-dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) [linear sub-manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_manifold) of $\mathcal{H}$.

### Quantum Markov semigroup (QMS) {#Quantum_Markov_semigroup_(QMS)}

A quantum dynamical semigroup $\mathcal{T}$ is said to be identity-preserving (or conservative, or Markovian) if

::: {#o11 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\mathcal{T}_t \left( \boldsymbol{1} \right) = \boldsymbol{1}, \quad \forall t \ge 0, \tag{1}$$

:::

where $\boldsymbol{1} \in \mathcal{A}$ is the identity element. For simplicity, $\mathcal{T}$ is called quantum Markov semigroup. Notice that, the identity-preserving property and [positivity](wikipedia://en/68445647) of $\mathcal{T}_t$ imply $\left\| \mathcal{T}_t \right\| = 1$ for all $t \ge 0$ and then $\mathcal{T}$ is a [contraction semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraction_semigroup).[^o12]

The Condition ((1)) plays an important role not only in the proof of uniqueness and unitarity of solution of a [Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Lyth_Hudson)–[Parthasarathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._R._Parthasarathy_%28probabilist%29) [quantum stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/41263617), but also in deducing regularity conditions for paths of classical Markov processes in view of [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675).[^o13]

### Infinitesimal generator of QDS {#Infinitesimal_generator_of_QDS}

The infinitesimal generator of a quantum dynamical semigroup $\mathcal{T}$ is the operator $\mathcal{L}$ with domain $\operatorname{Dom} (\mathcal{L})$, where

$$\operatorname{Dom} \left( \mathcal{L} \right) := \left\{ a \in \mathcal{A} ~\left\vert~ \lim_{t \rightarrow 0} \frac{\mathcal{T}_t(a) - a}{t} = b \text{ in } \sigma\text{-weak topology} \right. \right\}$$

and $\mathcal{L}(a) := b$.

:::

## Characterization of generators of uniformly continuous QMSs {#Characterization_of_generators_of_uniformly_continuous_QMSs}

If the quantum Markov semigroup $\mathcal{T}$ is uniformly continuous in addition, which means $\lim_{t \rightarrow 0^+} \left\| \mathcal{T}_t - \mathcal{T}_0 \right\| = 0$, then

- the infinitesimal generator $\mathcal{L}$ will be a [bounded operator](wikipedia://en/455961) on von Neumann algebra $\mathcal{A}$ with domain $\mathrm{Dom} (\mathcal{L}) = \mathcal{A}$,[^o16]
- the map $t \mapsto \mathcal{T}_t a$ will automatically be continuous for every $a \in \mathcal{A}$,[^o16]
- the infinitesimal generator $\mathcal{L}$ will be also $\sigma$-weakly continuous.[^o17]

Under such assumption, the infinitesimal generator $\mathcal{L}$ has the characterization[^o18]

$$\mathcal{L} \left( a \right) = i \left[ H, a \right] + \sum_{j} \left( V_j^\dagger a V_j - \frac{1}{2} \left\{ V_j^\dagger V_j, a \right\} \right)$$

where $a \in \mathcal{A}$, $V_j \in \mathcal{B} (\mathcal{H})$, $\sum_{j} V_j^\dagger V_j \in \mathcal{B} (\mathcal{H})$, and $H \in \mathcal{B} (\mathcal{H})$ is [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408). Moreover, above $\left[ \cdot, \cdot \right]$ denotes the [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193), and $\left\{ \cdot, \cdot \right\}$ the [anti-commutator](wikipedia://en/7193).
