# Interdecile range

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Interdecile range"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **interdecile range** is the difference between the first and the ninth [deciles](wikipedia://en/1577298) (10% and 90%). The interdecile range is a measure of [statistical dispersion](wikipedia://en/23773247) of the values in a set of data, similar to the [range](wikipedia://en/27588) and the [interquartile range](wikipedia://en/14812), and can be computed from the (non-parametric) [seven-number summary](wikipedia://en/18083379).

:::

Despite its simplicity, the interdecile range of a sample drawn from a normal distribution can be divided by 2.56 to give a reasonably [efficient](wikipedia://en/27250866) [estimator](wikipedia://en/10043) of the standard deviation of a normal distribution. This is derived from the fact that the lower (respectively upper) decile of a normal distribution with arbitrary [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) is equal to the mean minus (respectively, plus) 1.28 times the standard deviation.

A more efficient estimator is given by instead taking the 7% trimmed range (the difference between the 7th and 93rd percentiles) and dividing by 3 (corresponding to 86% of the data falling within ±1.5 standard deviations of the mean in a normal distribution); this yields an estimator having about 65% efficiency.[^n1] Analogous measures of location are given by the [median](wikipedia://en/18837), [midhinge](wikipedia://en/12559595), and [trimean](wikipedia://en/9888821) (or statistics based on nearby points).
