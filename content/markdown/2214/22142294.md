# Lothar Göttsche

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lothar Göttsche"}

**Lothar Göttsche** (born January 21, 1961, in [Sonderburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sønderborg_Municipality), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark)) is a German mathematician, known for his work in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

:::

He is a research scientist at the [International Centre for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Theoretical_Physics) in [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste), Italy. He is also editor for [Geometry & Topology](wikipedia://en/2862181).

## Biography {#Biography}

After studying mathematics at the [University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel), he received his [Dr. rer. nat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._rer._nat.) under the direction of [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) in 1989.

Göttsche was invited as speaker to the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Beijing in 2002.[^o3] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o4]

## Work {#Work}

Göttsche received international acclaim with his formula for the [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) for the [Betti numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betti_numbers) of the [Hilbert scheme](wikipedia://en/6612581) of points on an algebraic [surface](wikipedia://en/27865):

If $S$ is a smooth surface over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, then the generating function for the motives of the Hilbert schemes of $S$ can be expressed in terms of the [motivic zeta function](wikipedia://en/22097316) by Göttsche's formula

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty[S^{[n]}]t^n=\prod_{m=1}^\infty Z(S,{\mathbb L}^{m-1}t^m)$$

Here $S^{[n]}$ is the Hilbert scheme of length $n$ subschemes of $S$.

Göttsche is also the author of a celebrated conjecture predicting the number of [curves](wikipedia://en/89246) in certain [linear systems](wikipedia://en/1186886) on algebraic [surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_surfaces).[^o6][^o7]
