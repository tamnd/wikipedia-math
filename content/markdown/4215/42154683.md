# Coralie Colmez

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Coralie Colmez"}

**Coralie Colmez** is a French author and tutor in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471).

:::

## Early life and career {#Early_life_and_career}

Coralie Colmez is the daughter of mathematicians [Pierre Colmez](wikipedia://en/28580245) and [Leila Schneps](wikipedia://en/42154655).[^o4][^o5] Colmez was raised in Paris.[^o4]

After completing her secondary education in Paris, Colmez moved to the United Kingdom and attended [Gonville and Caius College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge) of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) under a Cambridge European Trust scholarship, completing a first-class [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and winning the Ryan Prize in Higher Mathematics.[^o6]

## Professional {#Professional}

Colmez worked for one year as a research assistant on [Carol Vorderman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Vorderman)'s task force, commissioned by the UK government to study the state of [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471) in the United Kingdom,[^o8] and assisted with the presentation of the findings to the [Joint Mathematics Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Mathematics_Council).[^o9] She is now a co-director of [*unifrog*](http://www.unifrog.org/), an organization that helps students discover future career pathways, apprenticeships and university courses, and teachers track their progress.[^o10]

## Writing {#Writing}

*Image: Coralie Colmez lecturing students.jpg.* Coralie Colmez addressing students

With her mother, mathematician [Leila Schneps](wikipedia://en/42154655), Colmez co-authored *[Math on Trial: How Numbers Get Used and Abused in the Courtroom](wikipedia://en/72353740)*.[^o12] This book, published in 2013 by Basic Books, targeted for a general audience, uses ten historical legal cases to show how mathematics, especially statistics, can affect the outcome of [criminal proceedings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_procedure), especially when incorrectly applied or interpreted. In 2022 Coralie published The Irrational Diary of Clara Valentine, a YA novel which includes high level mathematical concepts with the aim to introduce them to younger readers.

## Public speaking {#Public_speaking}

*Image: Coralie Colmez at QEDcon 2014.jpg.* Coralie Colmez speaking at QEDCon conference in 2014

Since the publication of *Math on Trial*, Colmez has been an invited speaker at scientific education events in the UK. She has presented to the [Conway Hall Ethical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway_Hall_Ethical_Society),[^o14] the [Cambridge Centre for Sixth-Form Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Centre_for_Sixth-form_Studies),[^o15] several shows for Maths Inspiration, including one at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge),[^o16] and the 2014 *[QED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merseyside_Skeptics_Society)* conference.[^o17][^o18] She has appeared on BBC Radio 4's *Today Programme*, discussing her book's subject of criminal trials in which math is used incorrectly or insufficiently,[^o19] and on the BBC Radio 4 podcast, *More or Less*, discussing the same topic in relation to the [Amanda Knox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Knox) case.[^o20]
