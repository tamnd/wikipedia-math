# Cheeger constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cheeger constant"}

In [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), the **Cheeger isoperimetric constant** of a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) *M* is a positive real number *h*(*M*) defined in terms of the minimal [area](wikipedia://en/27725) of a [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) that divides *M* into two disjoint pieces. In 1971, [Jeff Cheeger](wikipedia://en/7343761) proved an inequality that related the first nontrivial [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the [Laplace–Beltrami operator](wikipedia://en/1763356) on *M* to *h*(*M*). In 1982, [Peter Buser](wikipedia://en/64453197) proved a reverse version of this inequality, and the two inequalities put together are sometimes called the *Cheeger-Buser inequality*. These inequalities were highly influential not only in Riemannian geometry and [global analysis](wikipedia://en/42027498), but also in the theory of [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876) and in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), where they have inspired the analogous [Cheeger constant of a graph](wikipedia://en/7762273) and the notion of [conductance](wikipedia://en/14496121).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *M* be an *n*-dimensional [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) Riemannian manifold. Let *V*(*A*) denote the volume of an *n*-dimensional submanifold *A* and *S*(*E*) denote the *n*−1-dimensional volume of a submanifold *E* (commonly called "area" in this context). The **Cheeger isoperimetric constant** of *M* is defined to be

$$h(M)=\inf_E \frac{S(E)}{\min(V(A), V(B))},$$

where the [infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum) is taken over all smooth *n*−1-dimensional submanifolds *E* of *M* which divide it into two disjoint submanifolds *A* and *B*. The isoperimetric constant may be defined more generally for noncompact Riemannian manifolds of finite volume.

If the manifold is compact with boundary, the infimum is taken over the submanifolds *E* which do not intersect the boundary, and the volume of the denominator is that of the disjoint piece whose boundary is only *E*[^n1]. In the non-compact case with infinite volume, the infimum is taken over compact submanifolds *E* and the denominator corresponds to the volume of the bounded piece[^n2].

:::

::: {#o7 .statement cue=named-heading name="Cheeger's inequality"}

## Cheeger's inequality {#Cheeger's_inequality}

Jeff Cheeger proved[^n1] a lower bound for the smallest positive eigenvalue ${\lambda_1(M)}$ of the Laplacian on *M* in term of what is now called the Cheeger isoperimetric constant *h*(*M*):

$$\lambda_1(M)\geq \frac{h^2(M)}{4}.$$

This inequality is optimal in the following sense: for any *h* > 0, natural number *k*, and *ε* > 0, there exists a two-dimensional Riemannian manifold *M* with the isoperimetric constant *h*(*M*) = *h* and such that the *k*th eigenvalue of the Laplacian is within *ε* from the Cheeger bound.[^n3]

:::

::: {#o11 .statement cue=named-heading name="Buser's inequality"}

## Buser's inequality {#Buser's_inequality}

[Peter Buser](wikipedia://en/64453197) proved[^n4] an upper bound for the smallest positive eigenvalue $\lambda_1(M)$ of the Laplacian on *M* in terms of the Cheeger isoperimetric constant *h*(*M*). Let *M* be an *n*-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold whose [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582) is bounded below by −(*n*−1)*a*<sup>2</sup>, where *a* ≥ 0. Then

$$\lambda_1(M)\leq 2a(n-1)h(M) + 10h^2(M).$$

:::
