# Demetrios Magiros

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Demetrios Magiros"}

**Demetrios G. Magiros** (Δημήτριος Γ. Μαγείρος, 19 December 1912, [Euboea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euboea), Greece – 19 January 1982, [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia), Pennsylvania) was a Greek-American mathematician, specializing in the stability of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Magiros did his undergraduate and graduate study at the University of Athens ([National and Kapodistrian University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_and_Kapodistrian_University_of_Athens)), where he received his doctorate in pure mathematics in 1940. At the [National Technical University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Technical_University_of_Athens) he was appointed a lecturer in mechanics and [geodesy](wikipedia://en/12608) and subsequently was promoted to professor of mathematics. During WW II he published no papers but in 1946 he published three papers on the [catenary](wikipedia://en/7163). In 1949 he went to the USA. There he studied applied mathematics at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences), and at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT). After holding research positions at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University)'s [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) [Thomas J. Watson Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_J._Watson_Research_Center), the [Republic Aviation Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Aviation_Corporation), and at the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute), he was appointed professor of mathematics and mechanics at [Hofstra University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofstra_University). When he was a professor at Hofstra, he was also a consultant for the [General Electric Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_Company)'s Missile and Space Vehicle Department at the Valley Forge Technology Center.[^o4][^o3] In 1960 he resigned from Hofstra University to work full-time as a researcher at General Electric's Missile and Space Vehicle Department. He worked for [General Electric Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_Aerospace) for the remainder of his career.

During his career Magiros published 54 papers, 2 of them in the *[Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences)*. In 2012 a book containing a selection of 43 of his papers was published with Spyros G. Tzafestas as editor.[^o4] The book is organized into three parts: mathematics applied to engineering modelling and social issues (with 11 papers), nonlinear mechanics (with 18 papers), and dynamic systems analysis (with 12 papers), plus an appendix with 2 papers published in Soviet mathematical journals. The section on nonlinear mechanics contains 8 papers on celestial and orbital mechanics. The section on dynamical systems analysis contains 6 papers on stability analysis, 4 on [precessional](wikipedia://en/24714) phenomena, and 2 on [separatrices](wikipedia://en/2922081) of dynamical systems.[^o5]
