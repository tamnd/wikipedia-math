# Charles Henry Rowe

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Henry Rowe"}

**Charles Henry Rowe** (9 February 1893, [Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_%28city%29) – 4 December 1943) was an Irish mathematician, specializing in geometry.[^o2] He was [Erasmus Smith's Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Smith's_Professor_of_Mathematics) at [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin) (1926–1943).[^o3]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Rowe received his bachelor's degree from [University College Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Cork) in 1914 and his M.A. in Mathematics and Philosophy from [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin) in 1917. He was a close friend of the mathematical physicist [J. L. Synge](wikipedia://en/1252509).[^o5] By winning a competitive examination in 1920, Rowe became a Fellow of Trinity College Dublin and retained the fellowship until his death. He spent the academic year 1920–1921 in Paris, where he studied under [Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675), [Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514), and [Goursat](wikipedia://en/1674255).

From 1923 to 1926 he was the [Donegall Lecturer in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donegall_Lecturer_in_Mathematics) at TCD[^o2] and, after a probationary period as an acting professor,[^o6] was appointed in 1926 to the [Erasmus Smith's Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Smith's_Professor_of_Mathematics_at_Trinity_College_Dublin), retaining the position until his death.[^o2]

In 1932 he was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842), with talk *Subspaces associated with certain systems of curves in a Riemannian space*, in 1932 in Zurich. The Rowe Prize of Trinity College Dublin was established in 1959 by a bequest from his widow, Olive Marjorie Rowe.[^o7]
