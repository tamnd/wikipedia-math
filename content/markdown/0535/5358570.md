# Coxeter graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Coxeter graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Coxeter graph** is a 3-[regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) with 28 vertices and 42 edges. It is one of the 13 known [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) [distance-regular graphs](wikipedia://en/7768943).[^o4] It is named after [Harold Scott MacDonald Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The Coxeter graph has [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) 3, [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) 3, radius 4, diameter 4 and [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) 7. It is also a 3-[vertex-connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) and a 3-[edge-connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-edge-connected_graph). It has [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 3 and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 2.[^o7]

The Coxeter graph is [hypohamiltonian](wikipedia://en/18301304): it does not itself have a Hamiltonian cycle but every graph formed by removing a single vertex from it is Hamiltonian. It has [rectilinear crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298594) 11, and is the smallest cubic graph with that crossing number[^o8] . The graph is [1-planar](wikipedia://en/38249632).[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The simplest construction of a Coxeter graph is from a [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404). Take the [<sup>7</sup>C<sub>3</sub>](wikipedia://en/5308) = 35 possible 3-combinations on 7 objects. Discard the 7 triplets that correspond to the lines of the Fano plane, leaving 28 triplets. Link two triplets if they are disjoint. The result is the Coxeter graph. (See image.) This construction exhibits the Coxeter graph as an [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) of the [odd graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_graph) O<sub>4</sub>, also known as the [Kneser graph](wikipedia://en/2724154) $KG_{7,3}$.

The Coxeter graph may also be constructed from the smaller distance-regular [Heawood graph](wikipedia://en/1396870) by constructing a vertex for each 6-cycle in the Heawood graph and an edge for each disjoint pair of 6-cycles.[^o12]

The Coxeter graph may be derived from the [Hoffman-Singleton graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoffman-Singleton_graph). Take any vertex *v* in the Hoffman-Singleton graph. There is an [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) of size 15 that includes *v*. Delete the 7 neighbors of *v*, and the whole independent set including *v*, leaving behind the Coxeter graph.

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Algebraic properties"}

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

The automorphism group of the Coxeter graph is a group of order 336.[^o15] It acts transitively on the vertices, on the edges and on the arcs of the graph. Therefore, the Coxeter graph is a [symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/1577896). It has automorphisms that take any vertex to any other vertex and any edge to any other edge. According to the *Foster census*, the Coxeter graph, referenced as F28A, is the only cubic symmetric graph on 28 vertices.[^o16]

The Coxeter graph is also uniquely determined by its [graph spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_spectrum), the set of graph eigenvalues of its [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463).[^o17]

As a finite connected vertex-transitive graph that contains no [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle), the Coxeter graph is a counterexample to a variant of the [Lovász conjecture](wikipedia://en/4367424), but the canonical formulation of the conjecture asks for an Hamiltonian path and is verified by the Coxeter graph.

Only five examples of vertex-transitive graph with no Hamiltonian cycles are known : the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K*<sub>2</sub>, the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926), the Coxeter graph and two graphs derived from the Petersen and Coxeter graphs by replacing each vertex with a triangle.[^o18]

The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the Coxeter graph is $(x-3) (x-2)^8 (x+1)^7 (x^2+2 x-1)^6$. It is the only graph with this characteristic polynomial, making it a graph determined by its spectrum.

:::
