# Harish-Chandra isomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harish-Chandra isomorphism"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Harish-Chandra isomorphism**, introduced by (txt), is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) of commutative rings constructed in the theory of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944). The isomorphism maps the [center](wikipedia://en/53796787) $\mathcal{Z}(U(\mathfrak{g}))$ of the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) $U(\mathfrak{g})$ of a [reductive Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/863720) $\mathfrak{g}$ to the elements $S(\mathfrak{h})^W$ of the [symmetric algebra](wikipedia://en/654098) $S(\mathfrak{h})$ of a [Cartan subalgebra](wikipedia://en/1336000) $\mathfrak{h}$ that are invariant under the [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) $W$.

:::

## Introduction and setting {#Introduction_and_setting}

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587), $\mathfrak{h}$ its [Cartan subalgebra](wikipedia://en/1336000) and $\lambda, \mu \in \mathfrak{h}^*$ be two elements of the [weight space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_space_%28representation_theory%29) (where $\mathfrak{h}^*$ is the [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_vector_space) of $\mathfrak{h}$) and assume that a set of [positive roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_root#Positive_roots_and_simple_roots) $\Phi_+$ have been fixed. Let $V_\lambda$ and $V_\mu$ be [highest weight modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight_module) with highest weights $\lambda$ and $\mu$ respectively.

### Central characters {#Central_characters}

The $\mathfrak{g}$-modules $V_\lambda$ and $V_\mu$ are representations of the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) $U(\mathfrak{g})$ and its [center](wikipedia://en/342453) acts on the modules by [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373) (this follows from the fact that the modules are generated by a highest weight vector). So, for $v \in V_\lambda$ and $x \in \mathcal{Z}(U(\mathfrak{g}))$,

$$x\cdot v:=\chi_\lambda(x)v$$

and similarly for $V_\mu$, where the functions $\chi_\lambda, \,\chi_\mu$ are homomorphisms from $\mathcal{Z}(U(\mathfrak{g}))$ to scalars called *central characters*.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Statement of Harish-Chandra theorem"}

## Statement of Harish-Chandra theorem {#Statement_of_Harish-Chandra_theorem}

For any $\lambda, \mu \in \mathfrak{h}^*$, the characters $\chi_\lambda=\chi_\mu$ if and only if $\lambda + \delta$ and $\mu + \delta$ are on the same [orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) of the [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) of $\mathfrak{h}^*$, where $\delta$ is the half-sum of the [positive roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_root), sometimes known as the **Weyl vector**.[^n1]

Another closely related formulation is that the [Harish-Chandra homomorphism](wikipedia://en/33260169) from the center of the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) $\mathcal{Z}(U(\mathfrak{g}))$ to $S(\mathfrak{h})^W$ (the elements of the symmetric algebra of the Cartan subalgebra fixed by the Weyl group) is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828).

### Explicit isomorphism {#Explicit_isomorphism}

More explicitly, the isomorphism can be constructed as the composition of two maps, one from $\mathfrak{Z} = \mathcal{Z}(U(\mathfrak{g}))$ to $U(\mathfrak{h}) = S(\mathfrak{h}),$ and another from $S(\mathfrak{h})$ to itself.

The first is a projection $\gamma: \mathfrak{Z} \rightarrow S(\mathfrak{h})$. For a choice of positive roots $\Phi_+$, defining

$$n^+ = \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi_+} \mathfrak{g}_\alpha, n^- = \bigoplus_{\alpha \in \Phi_-} \mathfrak{g}_\alpha$$

as the corresponding positive nilpotent subalgebra and negative nilpotent subalgebra respectively, due to the [Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem](wikipedia://en/762696) there is a decomposition

$$U(\mathfrak{g}) = U(\mathfrak{h}) \oplus (U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}^+ + \mathfrak{n}^-U(\mathfrak{g})).$$

If $z \in \mathfrak{Z}$ is central, then in fact

$$z \in U(\mathfrak{h}) \oplus (U(\mathfrak{g})\mathfrak{n}^+ \cap \mathfrak{n}^-U(\mathfrak{g})).$$

The restriction of the projection $U(\mathfrak{g}) \rightarrow U(\mathfrak{h})$ to the centre is $\gamma: \mathfrak{Z} \rightarrow S(\mathfrak{h})$, and is a homomorphism of algebras. This is related to the central characters by

$$\chi_\lambda(x) = \gamma(x)(\lambda)$$

The second map is the *twist map* $\tau: S(\mathfrak{h}) \rightarrow S(\mathfrak{h})$. On $\mathfrak{h}$ viewed as a subspace of $U(\mathfrak{h})$ it is defined $\tau(h) = h - \delta(h)1$ with $\delta$ the Weyl vector.

Then $\tilde \gamma = \tau \circ \gamma: \mathfrak{Z} \rightarrow S(\mathfrak{h})$ is the isomorphism. The reason this twist is introduced is that $\chi_\lambda$ is not actually Weyl-invariant, but it can be proven that the twisted character $\tilde \chi_\lambda = \chi_{\lambda - \delta}$ is.

:::

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The theorem has been used to obtain a simple Lie algebraic proof of [Weyl's character formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl's_character_formula) for finite-dimensional irreducible representations.[^n2] The proof has been further simplified by [Victor Kac](wikipedia://en/4697697), so that only the quadratic Casimir operator is required; there is a corresponding streamlined treatment proof of the character formula in the second edition of (Humphreys 1978, 143–144).

Further, it is a necessary condition for the existence of a non-zero homomorphism of some highest weight modules (a homomorphism of such modules preserves central character). A simple consequence is that for [Verma modules](wikipedia://en/2975234) or [generalized Verma modules](wikipedia://en/17114413) $V_\lambda$ with highest weight $\lambda$, there exist only finitely many weights $\mu$ for which a non-zero homomorphism $V_\lambda \rightarrow V_\mu$ exists.

:::

## Fundamental invariants {#Fundamental_invariants}

For $\mathfrak{g}$ a simple Lie algebra, let $r$ be its *rank*, that is, the dimension of any Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ of $\mathfrak{g}$. [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) observed that $S(\mathfrak{h})^W$ is isomorphic to a [polynomial algebra](wikipedia://en/373065) in $r$ variables (see [Chevalley–Shephard–Todd theorem](wikipedia://en/16426096) for a more general statement). Therefore, the center of the universal enveloping algebra of a simple Lie algebra is isomorphic to a polynomial algebra. The degrees of the generators of the algebra are the degrees of the fundamental invariants given in the following table.

::: {#o14 .table cue=table}

| Lie algebra | [Coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_number) *h* | [Dual Coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_Coxeter_number) | Degrees of fundamental invariants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R** | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| A<sub>*n*</sub> | *n* + 1 | *n* + 1 | 2, 3, 4, ..., *n* + 1 |
| B<sub>*n*</sub> | 2*n* | 2*n* − 1 | 2, 4, 6, ..., 2*n* |
| C<sub>*n*</sub> | 2*n* | *n* + 1 | 2, 4, 6, ..., 2*n* |
| D<sub>*n*</sub> | 2*n* − 2 | 2*n* − 2 | *n*; 2, 4, 6, ..., 2*n* − 2 |
| E<sub>6</sub> | 12 | 12 | 2, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12 |
| E<sub>7</sub> | 18 | 18 | 2, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 18 |
| E<sub>8</sub> | 30 | 30 | 2, 8, 12, 14, 18, 20, 24, 30 |
| F<sub>4</sub> | 12 | 9 | 2, 6, 8, 12 |
| G<sub>2</sub> | 6 | 4 | 2, 6 |

:::

The number of the fundamental invariants of a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) is equal to its rank. Fundamental invariants are also related to the [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) of a Lie group. In particular, if the fundamental invariants have degrees $d_1, \cdots, d_r$, then the generators of the cohomology ring have degrees $2d_1-1, \cdots, 2d_r-1$. Due to this, the degrees of the fundamental invariants can be calculated from the [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) of the Lie group and vice versa. In another direction, fundamental invariants are related to cohomology of the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556). The cohomology ring $H^*(BG, \mathbb{R})$ is isomorphic to a polynomial algebra on generators with degrees $2d_1, \cdots, 2d_r$.[^o15]

::: {#o17 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If $\mathfrak{g}$ is the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}(2, \mathbb{R})$, then the center of the universal enveloping algebra is generated by the [Casimir invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_invariant) of degree 2, and the Weyl group acts on the Cartan subalgebra, which is isomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$, by negation, so the invariant of the Weyl group is the square of the generator of the Cartan subalgebra, which is also of degree 2.
- For $\mathfrak{g} = A_2 = \mathfrak{sl}(3,\mathbb{C})$, the Harish-Chandra isomorphism says $\mathcal{Z}(U(\mathfrak{g}))$ is isomorphic to a polynomial algebra of Weyl-invariant polynomials in two variables $h_1, h_2$ (since the Cartan subalgebra is two-dimensional). For $A_2$, the Weyl group is $S_3 \cong D_6$ which acts on the CSA in the standard representation. Since the Weyl group acts by reflections, they are isometries and so the degree 2 polynomial $f_2(h_1, h_2) = h_1^2 + h_2^2$ is Weyl-invariant. The contours of the degree 3 Weyl-invariant polynomial (for a particular choice of standard representation where one of the reflections is across the x-axis) are shown below. These two polynomials generate the polynomial algebra, and are the fundamental invariants for $A_2$.
- For all the Lie algebras in the classification, there is a fundamental invariant of degree 2, the **quadratic Casimir**. In the isomorphism, these correspond to a degree 2 polynomial on the CSA. Since the Weyl group acts by reflections on the CSA, they are isometries, so the degree 2 invariant polynomial is $f_2(\mathbf{h}) = h_1^2 + \cdots + h_r^2$ where $r$ is the dimension of the CSA $\mathfrak{h}$, also known as the rank of the Lie algebra.
- For $\mathfrak{g} = A_1 = \mathfrak{sl}(2, \mathbb{C})$, the Cartan subalgebra is one-dimensional, and the Harish-Chandra isomorphism says $\mathcal{Z}(U(\mathfrak{g}))$ is isomorphic to the algebra of Weyl-invariant polynomials in a single variable $h$. The Weyl group is $S_2$ acting as reflection, with non-trivial element acting on polynomials by $h \mapsto -h$. The subalgebra of Weyl-invariant polynomials in the full polynomial algebra $K[h]$ is therefore only the even polynomials, generated by $f_2(h) = h^2$.
- For $\mathfrak{g} = B_2 = \mathfrak{so}(5) = \mathfrak{sp}(4)$, the Weyl group is $D_8$, acting on two coordinates $h_1, h_2$, and is generated (non-minimally) by four reflections, which act on coordinates as $(h_1 \mapsto -h_1, h_2 \mapsto h_2), (h_1 \mapsto h_1, h_2 \mapsto -h_2), (h_1 \mapsto h_2, h_2 \mapsto h_1), (h_1 \mapsto -h_2, h_2 \mapsto h_1)$. Any invariant quartic must be even in both $h_1$ and $h_2$, and invariance under exchange of coordinates means any invariant quartic can be written $f_4(h_1, h_2) = ah_1^4 + bh_1^2h_2^2 + ah_2^4.$ Despite this being a two-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), this contributes only one new fundamental invariant as $f_2(h_1,h_2)^2$ lies in the space. In this case, there is no unique choice of quartic invariant as any polynomial with $b \neq 2a$ (and $a,b$ not both zero) suffices.

:::

## Generalization to affine Lie algebras {#Generalization_to_affine_Lie_algebras}

The above result holds for [reductive](wikipedia://en/863720), and in particular [semisimple Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1337587). There is a generalization to [affine Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1350865) shown by [Feigin](wikipedia://en/53823921) and [Frenkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Frenkel) showing that an algebra known as the Feigin–Frenkel center is isomorphic to a [W-algebra](wikipedia://en/4791570) associated to the [Langlands dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_dual) Lie algebra $^L\mathfrak{g}$.[^o19][^o20]

The **Feigin–Frenkel center** of an affine Lie algebra $\hat \mathfrak{g}$ is not exactly the center of the universal enveloping algebra $\mathcal{Z}(U(\hat \mathfrak{g}))$. They are elements $S$ of the vacuum [affine vertex algebra](wikipedia://en/1350865#affine_vertex_algebra) at critical level $k = -h^\vee$, where $h^\vee$ is the [dual Coxeter number](wikipedia://en/2148313#definitions) for $\mathfrak{g}$ which are annihilated by the positive [loop algebra](wikipedia://en/1336457) $\mathfrak{g}[t]$ part of $\hat \mathfrak{g}$, that is,

$$\mathfrak{Z}(\hat \mathfrak{g}) := \{S \in V_{\text{cri}}(\mathfrak{g}) | \mathfrak{g}[t]S = 0\}$$

where $V_{\text{cri}}(\mathfrak{g})$ is the affine vertex algebra at the critical level. Elements of this center are also known as *singular vectors* or *Segal–Sugawara vectors*.

The isomorphism in this case is an isomorphism between the Feigin–Frenkel center and the W-algebra constructed associated to the Langlands dual Lie algebra by [Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction](wikipedia://en/4791570#Drinfeld–Sokolov_reduction):

$$\mathfrak{Z}(\hat \mathfrak{g}) \cong \mathcal{W}(^L\mathfrak{g}).$$

There is also a description of $\mathfrak{Z}(\hat \mathfrak{g})$ as a polynomial algebra in a finite number of countably infinite families of generators, $\partial^n S_i, i = 1, \cdots, l, n \geq 0$, where $S_i, i = 1, \cdots, l$ have degrees $d_i +1, i = 1, \cdots, l$ and $\partial$ is the (negative of) the natural derivative operator on the loop algebra.
