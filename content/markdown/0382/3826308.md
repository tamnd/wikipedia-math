# Cake number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cake number"}

*Image: cake\_number\_3.svg.* Three orthogonal planes slice a cake into at most eight (*C*<sub>3</sub>) pieces

*Image: Cake number with 4 cutting planes.gif.* Animation showing the cutting planes required to cut a cake into 15 pieces with 4 slices (representing the 5th cake number). Fourteen of the pieces would have an external surface, with one [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) cut out of the middle.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **cake number**, denoted by *C<sub>n</sub>*, is the maximum of the number of regions into which a 3-dimensional [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) can be partitioned by exactly *n* [planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29). The cake number is so called because one may imagine each partition of the cube by a plane as a slice made by a knife through a cube-shaped [cake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cake). It is the 3D analogue of the [lazy caterer's sequence](wikipedia://en/2038304).

:::

The values of *C<sub>n</sub>* for *n* = 0, 1, 2, ... are given by 1, 2, 4, 8, 15, 26, 42, 64, 93, 130, 176, 232, ... .

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="General formula"}

## General formula {#General_formula}

If *n*! denotes the [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606), and we denote the [binomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/4668) by

$${n \choose k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!},$$

and we assume that *n* planes are available to partition the cube, then the *n*-th cake number is:[^o4]

$$C_n = {n \choose 3} + {n \choose 2} + {n \choose 1} + {n \choose 0} = \tfrac{1}{6}\!\left(n^3 + 5n + 6\right) = \tfrac{1}{6}(n+1)\left(n(n-1) + 6\right).$$

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The cake numbers are the 3-dimensional analogue of the 2-dimensional [lazy caterer's sequence](wikipedia://en/2038304). The difference between successive cake numbers also gives the lazy caterer's sequence.[^o4]

Cake numbers (blue) The fourth column of [Bernoulli's triangle](wikipedia://en/52245438) (*k* = 3) gives the cake numbers for *n* cuts, where *n* ≥ 3.

## Other applications {#Other_applications}

In *n* spatial (not spacetime) dimensions, [Maxwell's equations](wikipedia://en/19737) represent $C_n$ different independent real-valued equations.
