# Donsker's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donsker's theorem"}

*Image: Donskers invariance principle.gif.* Donsker's invariance principle for [simple random walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_random_walk) on $\mathbb Z$.

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Donsker's theorem** (also known as **Donsker's invariance principle**, or the **functional central limit theorem**), named after [Monroe D. Donsker](wikipedia://en/16594147), is a functional extension of the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406) for empirical distribution functions. Specifically, the theorem states that an appropriately centered and scaled version of the empirical distribution function converges to a [Gaussian process](wikipedia://en/302944).

:::

Let $X_1, X_2, X_3, \ldots$ be a sequence of [independent and identically distributed](wikipedia://en/30876902) (i.i.d.) [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) with mean 0 and variance 1. Let $S_n:=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. The stochastic process $S:=(S_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is known as a [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451). Define the diffusively rescaled random walk (partial-sum process) by

$$W^{(n)}(t) := \frac{S_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}{\sqrt{n}}, \qquad t\in [0,1].$$

The [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406) asserts that $W^{(n)}(1)$ [converges in distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Converges_in_distribution) to a standard [Gaussian random variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_random_variable) $W(1)$ as $n\to\infty$. Donsker's invariance principle[^o2][^o11] extends this convergence to the whole function $W^{(n)}:=(W^{(n)}(t))_{t\in [0,1]}$. More precisely, in its modern form, Donsker's invariance principle states that: As [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) taking values in the [Skorokhod space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skorokhod_space) $\mathcal{D}[0,1]$, the random function $W^{(n)}$ [converges in distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Converges_in_distribution) to a [standard Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/149984) $W:=(W(t))_{t\in [0,1]}$ as $n\to \infty.$

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

Let *F*<sub>*n*</sub> be the [empirical distribution function](wikipedia://en/2690455) of the sequence of i.i.d. random variables $X_1, X_2, X_3, \ldots$ with distribution function *F.* Define the centered and scaled version of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> by

$$G_n(x)= \sqrt n ( F_n(x) - F(x) )$$

indexed by *x* ∈ **R**. By the classical [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406), for fixed *x*, the random variable *G*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*) [converges in distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Converges_in_distribution) to a [Gaussian (normal)](wikipedia://en/21462) [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) *G*(*x*) with zero mean and variance *F*(*x*)(1 − *F*(*x*)) as the sample size *n* grows.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem** (Donsker, Skorokhod, Kolmogorov) The sequence of *G*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*), as random elements of the [Skorokhod space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skorokhod_space) $\mathcal{D}(-\infty,\infty)$, [converges in distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_in_distribution) to a [Gaussian process](wikipedia://en/302944) *G* with zero mean and covariance given by

$\operatorname{cov}[G(s), G(t)] = E[G(s) G(t)] = \min\{F(s), F(t)\} - F(s)$${F}(t).$

The process *G*(*x*) can be written as *B*(*F*(*x*)) where *B* is a standard [Brownian bridge](wikipedia://en/1843777) on the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324).

:::

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof sketch"}

### Proof sketch {#Proof_sketch}

For continuous probability distributions, it reduces to the case where the distribution is uniform on $[0, 1]$ by the [inverse transform](wikipedia://en/45705).

Given any finite sequence of times $0 < t_1 < t_2 < \dots < t_n < 1$, we have that $N F_N(t_1)$ is distributed as a [binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/3876) with mean $Nt_1$ and variance $Nt_1(1-t_1)$.

Similarly, the joint distribution of $F_N(t_1), F_N(t_2), \dots, F_N(t_n)$ is a multinomial distribution. Now, the [central limit approximation for multinomial distributions](wikipedia://en/1045553#Asymptotics) shows that $\lim_N \sqrt N (F_N(t_i) - t_i)$ converges in distribution to a gaussian process with covariance matrix with entries $\min(t_i, t_j) - t_i t_j$, which is precisely the covariance matrix for the Brownian bridge.

:::

:::

## History and related results {#History_and_related_results}

Kolmogorov (1933) showed that when *F* is [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122), the supremum $\scriptstyle\sup_t G_n(t)$ and supremum of absolute value, $\scriptstyle\sup_t |G_n(t)|$ [converges in distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_in_distribution) to the laws of the same functionals of the [Brownian bridge](wikipedia://en/1843777) *B*(*t*), see the [Kolmogorov–Smirnov test](wikipedia://en/16714). In 1949 Doob asked whether the convergence in distribution held for more general functionals, thus formulating a problem of [weak convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_convergence_of_measures) of random functions in a suitable [function space](wikipedia://en/340630).[^o9]

In 1952 Donsker stated and proved (not quite correctly)[^o10] a general extension for the Doob–Kolmogorov heuristic approach. In the original paper, Donsker proved that the convergence in law of *G*<sub>*n*</sub> to the Brownian bridge holds for [Uniform\[0,1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29)\] distributions with respect to [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652) in *t* over the interval \[0,1\].[^o11]

However Donsker's formulation was not quite correct because of the problem of measurability of the functionals of discontinuous processes. In 1956 Skorokhod and Kolmogorov defined a separable metric *d*, called the *Skorokhod metric*, on the space of [càdlàg](wikipedia://en/30862839) functions on \[0,1\], such that convergence for *d* to a continuous function is equivalent to convergence for the sup norm, and showed that *G<sub>n</sub>* converges in law in $\mathcal{D}[0,1]$ to the Brownian bridge.

Later Dudley reformulated Donsker's result to avoid the problem of measurability and the need of the Skorokhod metric. One can prove[^o10] that there exist *X*<sub>*i*</sub>, iid uniform in \[0,1\] and a sequence of sample-continuous Brownian bridges *B*<sub>*n*</sub>, such that

$$\|G_n-B_n\|_\infty$$

is measurable and [converges in probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_in_probability) to 0. An improved version of this result, providing more detail on the [rate of convergence](wikipedia://en/999701), is the [Komlós–Major–Tusnády approximation](wikipedia://en/29184958).
