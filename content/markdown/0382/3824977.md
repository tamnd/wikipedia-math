# Richard M. Dudley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard M. Dudley"}

**Richard Mansfield Dudley** (July 28, 1938 – January 19, 2020)[^o3] was an American mathematician. He was Professor of Mathematics at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) from 1967 to his retirement in 2015.[^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Dudley was born in [Cleveland, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland,_Ohio). He earned his BA at [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College) and received his PhD at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1962 under the supervision of [Edward Nelson](wikipedia://en/387561) and [Gilbert Hunt](wikipedia://en/41911457). He was a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) in 1958. He was an instructor and assistant professor at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) between 1962 and 1967, before moving to [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) as a professor in mathematics, where he stayed from 1967 until 2015, when he retired.[^o5]

He died on January 19, 2020, following a long illness.[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

His work mainly concerned fields of probability,[^o8] mathematical statistics, and machine learning, with highly influential contributions to the theory of Gaussian processes and empirical processes. He published over a hundred papers in peer-reviewed journals and authored several books. His specialty was [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), especially [empirical processes](wikipedia://en/3690433).[^o9] He is often noted for [his results](wikipedia://en/21600649) on the so-called *Dudley entropy integral*.[^o10][^o11][^o12] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o13]
