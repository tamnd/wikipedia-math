# Kronecker sum of discrete Laplacians

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kronecker sum of discrete Laplacians"}

In mathematics, the **Kronecker sum of discrete Laplacians**, named after [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450), is a discrete version of the [separation of variables](wikipedia://en/387750) for the continuous [Laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian) in a [rectangular cuboid](wikipedia://en/156802#Rectangular_cuboid) domain.

:::

## General form of the Kronecker sum of discrete Laplacians {#General_form_of_the_Kronecker_sum_of_discrete_Laplacians}

In a general situation of the [separation of variables](wikipedia://en/387750) in the discrete case, the multidimensional [discrete Laplacian](wikipedia://en/1394358) is a [Kronecker sum](wikipedia://en/712430#Kronecker_sum_and_exponentiation) of 1D discrete Laplacians.

### Example: 2D discrete Laplacian on a regular grid with the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition {#Example:_2D_discrete_Laplacian_on_a_regular_grid_with_the_homogeneous_Dirichlet_boundary_condition}

Mathematically, using the [Kronecker sum](wikipedia://en/712430#Kronecker_sum):

$L =\mathbf{D_{xx}}\otimes\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{I}\otimes\mathbf{D_{yy}}, \,$[^o4]

where $\mathbf{D_{xx}}$ and $\mathbf{D_{yy}}$ are 1D discrete Laplacians in the *x*- and *y*-directions, correspondingly, and $\mathbf{I}$ are the identities of appropriate sizes. Both $\mathbf{D_{xx}}$ and $\mathbf{D_{yy}}$ must correspond to the case of the homogeneous [Dirichlet boundary condition](wikipedia://en/423504) at end points of the *x*- and *y*-intervals, in order to generate the 2D discrete Laplacian *L* corresponding to the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition everywhere on the boundary of the rectangular domain.

Here is a sample [OCTAVE](wikipedia://en/48707)/[MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412) code to compute *L* on the regular 10×15 2D grid:

```matlab
nx = 10; % number of grid points in the x-direction
ny = 15; % number of grid points in the y-direction
ex = ones(nx, 1);
Dxx = spdiags([ex -2*ex ex], [-1 0 1], nx, nx); %1D discrete Laplacian in the x-direction
ey = ones(ny, 1);
Dyy = spdiags([ey, -2*ey ey], [-1 0 1], ny, ny); %1D discrete Laplacian in the y-direction
L = kron(Dyy, speye(nx)) + kron(speye(ny), Dxx);
```

## Eigenvalues and eigenvectors of multidimensional discrete Laplacian on a regular grid {#Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors_of_multidimensional_discrete_Laplacian_on_a_regular_grid}

Knowing all [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) and [eigenvectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector) of the factors, all eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the [Kronecker product](wikipedia://en/712430) can be [explicitly calculated](wikipedia://en/712430#Spectrum). Based on this, eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the [Kronecker sum](wikipedia://en/712430#Kronecker_sum_and_exponentiation) can also be explicitly calculated.

The [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) and [eigenvectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector) of the standard [central difference approximation of the second derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_difference#Higher-order_differences) on an interval for traditional combinations of boundary conditions at the interval end points are [well known](wikipedia://en/26992157). Combining these expressions with the formulas of eigenvalues and eigenvectors for the [Kronecker sum](wikipedia://en/712430#Kronecker_sum_and_exponentiation), one can easily obtain the required answer.

### Example: 3D discrete Laplacian on a regular grid with the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition {#Example:_3D_discrete_Laplacian_on_a_regular_grid_with_the_homogeneous_Dirichlet_boundary_condition}

$$L = \mathbf{D_{xx}}\otimes\mathbf{I}\otimes\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{I}\otimes\mathbf{D_{yy}}\otimes\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{I}\otimes\mathbf{I}\otimes\mathbf{D_{zz}}, \,$$

where $\mathbf{D_{xx}},\,\mathbf{D_{yy}}$ and $\mathbf{D_{zz}}$ are 1D discrete Laplacians in every of the 3 directions, and $\mathbf{I}$ are the identities of appropriate sizes. Each 1D discrete Laplacian must correspond to the case of the homogeneous [Dirichlet boundary condition](wikipedia://en/423504), in order to generate the 3D discrete Laplacian *L* corresponding to the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition everywhere on the boundary. The eigenvalues are

$$\lambda_{jx,jy,jz} = -\frac{4}{h_x^2} \sin\left(\frac{\pi j_x}{2(n_x + 1)}\right)^2 -\frac{4}{h_y^2} \sin\left(\frac{\pi j_y}{2(n_y + 1)}\right)^2 -\frac{4}{h_z^2} \sin\left(\frac{\pi j_z}{2(n_z + 1)}\right)^2$$

where $j_x=1,\ldots,n_x,\,j_y=1,\ldots,n_y,\,j_z=1,\ldots,n_z,\,$, and the corresponding eigenvectors are

$$v_{ix,iy,iz,jx,jy,jz} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{n_x+1}} \sin\left(\frac{i_x j_x \pi}{n_x+1}\right) \sqrt{\frac{2}{n_y+1}} \sin\left(\frac{i_y j_y \pi}{n_y+1}\right) \sqrt{\frac{2}{n_z+1}} \sin\left(\frac{i_z j_z \pi}{n_z+1}\right)$$

where the multi-index ${jx,jy,jz}$ pairs the [eigenvalues and the eigenvectors](wikipedia://en/2161429), while the multi-index ${ix,iy,iz}$ determines the location of the value of every eigenvector at the [regular grid](wikipedia://en/3771917). The boundary points, where the homogeneous [Dirichlet boundary condition](wikipedia://en/423504) are imposed, are just outside the grid.

## Available software {#Available_software}

An [OCTAVE](wikipedia://en/48707)/[MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412) code http://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/27279-laplacian-in-1d-2d-or-3d is available under a [BSD License](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSD_License), which computes the [sparse matrix](wikipedia://en/341015) of the 1, 2D, and 3D negative Laplacians on a rectangular grid for combinations of Dirichlet, Neumann, and [Periodic boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/9667552) using [Kronecker sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker_sum) of discrete 1D Laplacians. The code also provides the exact [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) and [eigenvectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector) using the explicit formulas given above.
