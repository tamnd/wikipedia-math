# James H. Bramble

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="James H. Bramble"}

**James Henry Bramble** (December 1, 1930 – July 20, 2021[^o3]) was an American mathematician known for his fundamental contributions in the development of the [finite element methods](wikipedia://en/18233581), including the [Bramble–Hilbert lemma](wikipedia://en/13925681),[^o5] [domain decomposition methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_decomposition_method), and [multigrid methods](wikipedia://en/1747763).[^o6] During his career, he taught at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) and [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University).

:::

James Henry Bramble was born on December 1, 1930, in [Annapolis, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annapolis,_Maryland). He received his undergraduate degree at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) in 1953 and his Ph.D. from the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland) in 1958.[^o7][^o3] Bramble joined Cornell University in 1968, where he worked to develop [analytical methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_technique) for [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). Between 1975 and 1981 he served as director of Cornell's Center for Applied Mathematics. From 1975 to 83, Bramble served as the chief editor for *[Mathematics of Computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_Computation)*.

Bramble retired from Cornell in 1994, later teaching at Texas A&M University.[^o8]

Bramble received an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate) from the [Chalmers University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalmers_University_of_Technology) in 1985.[^o9]
