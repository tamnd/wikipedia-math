# Lucien Szpiro

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lucien Szpiro"}

**Lucien Szpiro** (23 December 1941 – 18 April 2020) was a French mathematician known for his work in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), and [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990). He formulated [Szpiro's conjecture](wikipedia://en/18813336) and was a distinguished professor at the [CUNY Graduate Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUNY_Graduate_Center) and an emeritus Director of Research at the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Lucien Szpiro was born on 23 December 1941 in Paris, France.[^o4] Szpiro attended [Paris-Sud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_University) where he earned his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) under [Pierre Samuel](wikipedia://en/2604194).[^o4] His doctoral work was heavily influenced by the seminars of [Maurice Auslander](wikipedia://en/30337642), [Claude Chevalley](wikipedia://en/366040), and [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042).[^o4] He earned his [Doctorat d'État](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation#France) (DrE) in 1971.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

From 1963 to 1965, Szpiro worked as an assistant high school teacher in Paris.[^o4][^o21] From 1965 to 1969, he was an assistant professor (*maître assistant*) at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris).[^o4][^o21] From 1969 to 1999, Szpiro worked at the CNRS, initially as an [attaché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_ranks_in_France#Temporary_positions) at [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) before rising to the rank of a distinguished professor (*Directeur de Recherche de Classe Exceptionnelle*) at [Paris-Sud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_University).[^o21] In 1999, he became an emeritus professor (*Directeur de Recherche émérite*) at the CNRS and moved to the CUNY Graduate Center as a distinguished professor.[^o4][^o21][^o7] He also held visiting positions at several institutions including [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o21][^o8]

Szpiro was the editor-in-chief of *[Astérisque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astérisque)* from 1991 to 1993 and an editor of the *[Bulletin de la Société Mathématique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_de_la_Société_Mathématique_de_France)* from 1984 to 1990.[^o4] He was also head of the commission that oversaw the [Société mathématique de France](wikipedia://en/1691205) libraries.[^o9]

Szpiro advised 17 doctoral students, including [Ahmed Abbes](wikipedia://en/66166683), [Emmanuel Ullmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Ullmo), and [Shou-Wu Zhang](wikipedia://en/3582724).[^o4][^o5]

## Research {#Research}

In the 1970s, Szpiro's research in commutative algebra led to his proof of the Auslander zero divisor conjecture.[^o24] Together with Christian Peskine, he developed the [liaison theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaison_theory) of algebraic varieties.[^o24]

In the 1980s, Szpiro's research interests shifted to Diophantine geometry, first over [function fields](wikipedia://en/447645) and then over [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field).[^o24] The [Institut des hautes études scientifiques](wikipedia://en/14527) described Szpiro as being "the first to realise the importance of a paper by Arakelov for questions of Diophantine geometry", which ultimately led to the development of [Arakelov theory](wikipedia://en/16364229) as a tool of modern [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205) exemplified by [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226)'s proof of the [Mordell conjecture](wikipedia://en/172199).[^o7][^o24] Szpiro also showed the link between the [positivity](wikipedia://en/1186896) of the [dualising sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualising_sheaf) of a curve and the [Bogomolov conjecture](wikipedia://en/18992933).[^o24]

In 1981, Szpiro formulated a conjecture (now known as Szpiro's conjecture) relating the discriminant of an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) with its [conductor](wikipedia://en/43604164).[^o11] His conjecture inspired the [*abc* conjecture](wikipedia://en/174108),[^o12] which was later shown to be equivalent to a modified form of Szpiro's conjecture in 1988.[^o13] Szpiro's conjecture and its equivalent forms have been described as "the most important unsolved problem in [Diophantine analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_analysis)" by [Dorian Goldfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorian_Goldfeld),[^o14] in part to its large number of consequences in number theory including [Roth's theorem](wikipedia://en/787853), the [Mordell conjecture](wikipedia://en/172199), the [Fermat–Catalan conjecture](wikipedia://en/22736152), and [Brocard's problem](wikipedia://en/11301521).[^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18]

After moving to the CUNY Graduate Center in 1999, Szpiro began working on new research in [arithmetic dynamics](wikipedia://en/17584701).[^o7][^o24]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 1987, Szpiro received the [Prix Doistau–Blutel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Doistau–Blutel) from the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) "for his work in Commutative Algebra and Algebraic Geometry and for his contribution to G. Faltings’ proof of the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture)."[^o7] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o20] He was a [Member of the Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Academia_Europaea).[^o21]

## Death {#Death}

Szpiro died on 18 April 2020 in Paris, France, from [cardiac arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_arrest).[^o23][^o24]
