# Abelson's paradox

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abelson's paradox"}

**Abelson's paradox** is an [applied statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_statistics) [paradox](wikipedia://en/24390) identified by [Robert P. Abelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_P._Abelson).[^o2][^o3][^o4] The paradox pertains to a possible paradoxical relationship between the magnitude of the *r*<sup>2</sup> (i.e., [coefficient of determination](wikipedia://en/1500869)) [effect size](wikipedia://en/437276) and its practical meaning.

:::

Abelson's example was obtained from the analysis of the *r*<sup>2</sup> of [batting average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batting_average_%28baseball%29) in [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball) and skill level. Although batting average is considered among the most significant characteristics necessary for success, the effect size was only a tiny[^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10] 0.003.
