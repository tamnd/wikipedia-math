# Erdős–Kaplansky theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erdős–Kaplansky theorem"}

The **Erdős–Kaplansky theorem** is a theorem from [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422). The theorem makes a fundamental statement about the [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28mathematics%29) of the [dual spaces](wikipedia://en/7988) of infinite-dimensional [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370); in particular, it shows that the [algebraic dual space](wikipedia://en/7988#Algebraic_dual_space) is not [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to the vector space itself. A more general formulation allows to compute the exact dimension of any [function space](wikipedia://en/32370#Function_space).

:::

The theorem is named after [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Irving Kaplansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Kaplansky).

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $E$ be an infinite-dimensional vector space over a [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28algebra%29) $\mathbb{K}$ and let $I$ be some basis of it. Then for the dual space $E^*$,[^o4]

$$\operatorname{dim}(E^*)=\operatorname{card}(\mathbb{K}^I).$$

By [Cantor's theorem](wikipedia://en/341442), this [cardinal](wikipedia://en/6173) is strictly larger than the dimension $\operatorname{card}(I)$ of $E$. More generally, if $I$ is an arbitrary infinite set, the dimension of the space of all functions $\mathbb{K}^I$ is given by:[^o5]

$$\operatorname{dim}(\mathbb{K}^I)=\operatorname{card}(\mathbb{K}^I).$$

When $I$ is finite, it's a standard result that $\dim(\mathbb{K}^I) = \operatorname{card}(I)$. This gives us a full characterization of the dimension of this space.

:::
