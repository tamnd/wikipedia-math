# James Nwoye Adichie

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="James Nwoye Adichie"}

[Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_chieftaincy) **James Nwoye Adichie** Ig-James Nwoye Adichie.ogg (1 March 1932 – 10 June 2020)[^o4] was a Nigerian academic who served as a first professor of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at the [University of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nigeria) [Nsukka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsukka). He was the father of writer [Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimamanda_Ngozi_Adichie).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Adichie was born on 1 March 1932 in Abba, located in [Njikoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Njikoka), [Anambra State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anambra_State).[^o4] Soon after his birth, he relocated to [Awkuzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awkuzu) and later to [Nimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimo,_Nigeria), where he attended Nimo Primary School for his early education.[^o5]

In 1957, he enrolled in the [University College, Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Ibadan), to study mathematics, graduating in 1960. He then joined the research department at the [Central Bank of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Nigeria) (CBN).[^o4] After leaving the CBN, Adichie returned to academia as a mathematics instructor at the [Nigerian College of Arts, Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_College_of_Arts,_Science_and_Technology) in [Enugu State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enugu_State) and later became an assistant lecturer at the [University of Nigeria, Nsukka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nigeria,_Nsukka). In September 1963, he departed Nigeria to pursue a PhD in statistics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he studied under [Erich Leo Lehmann](wikipedia://en/16388476). He earned his doctorate in 1966, becoming the first Nigerian to receive a PhD in statistics from UC Berkeley.[^o6] In 1976, he became the inaugural professor of statistics at the [University of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nigeria) and later served as its [Deputy Vice Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Vice_Chancellor) from 1980 to 1984, retiring in 1997.[^o4]

Throughout his career, Adichie published articles in academic journals and magazines.[^o4] His academic work included a fellowship at the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield) in England and a term as a visiting professor at [San Diego State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_State_University) in California, as well as serving as the first editor of the *[Journal of Statistical Association of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Statistical_Association_of_Nigeria)*.[^o4] On 2 May 2015, while returning to Abba from [Nsukka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsukka), Adichie was kidnapped.[^o7] According to reports from [Pulse Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse_Nigeria), his daughter, [Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimamanda_Ngozi_Adichie), explained in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* that her presence was a factor in the abduction.[^o8] He was released three days later after payment of a ransom.[^o8][^o9]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Adichie was the "Odelora Abba", a [Nigerian chieftaincy title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Chieftaincy). A member of the Nigerian National Advisory Council on Statistics, he was one of the figures who reorganised the Federal Office of Statistics that was later reformed to the [National Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Statistics,_Nigeria).[^o4]
