# Algebraic differential equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Algebraic differential equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **algebraic differential equation** is a [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309) that can be expressed by means of [differential algebra](wikipedia://en/1816587). There are several such notions, according to the concept of differential algebra used.

:::

The intention is to include equations formed by means of [differential operators](wikipedia://en/263886), in which the coefficients are [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) of the variables (e.g. the [hypergeometric equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_equation)). Algebraic differential equations are widely used in [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

A simple concept is that of a **polynomial vector field**, in other words a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) expressed with respect to a standard co-ordinate basis as the first partial derivatives with polynomial coefficients. This is a type of first-order algebraic differential operator.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formulations"}

## Formulations {#Formulations}

- [Derivations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29) *D* can be used as algebraic analogues of the formal part of [differential calculus](wikipedia://en/50416), so that algebraic differential equations make sense in [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346).
- The theory of [differential fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_field) was set up to express [differential Galois theory](wikipedia://en/833506) in algebraic terms.
- The [Weyl algebra](wikipedia://en/491095) *W* of differential operators with polynomial coefficients can be considered; certain [modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_%28algebra%29) *M* can be used to express differential equations, according to the presentation of *M*.
- The concept of [Koszul connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koszul_connection) is something that transcribes easily into [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), giving an algebraic analogue of the way [systems of differential equations](wikipedia://en/63871781) are geometrically represented by [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) with connections.
- The concept of [jet](wikipedia://en/1521924) can be described in purely algebraic terms, as was done in part of [Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck)'s [EGA](wikipedia://en/471387) project.
- The theory of [D-modules](wikipedia://en/3440755) is a global theory of linear differential equations, and has been developed to include substantive results in the algebraic theory (including a [Riemann-Hilbert correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann-Hilbert_correspondence) for higher dimensions).

:::

## Algebraic solutions {#Algebraic_solutions}

It is usually not the case that the general solution of an algebraic differential equation is an [algebraic function](wikipedia://en/974169): solving equations typically produces novel [transcendental functions](wikipedia://en/297021). The case of algebraic solutions is however of considerable interest; the classical [Schwarz list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarz_list) deals with the case of the hypergeometric equation. In differential Galois theory the case of algebraic solutions is that in which the differential Galois group *G* is finite (equivalently, of dimension 0, or of a finite [monodromy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monodromy_group) for the case of [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) and linear equations). This case stands in relation with the whole theory roughly as [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762) does to [group representation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_representation_theory). The group *G* is in general difficult to compute, the understanding of algebraic solutions is an indication of upper bounds for *G*.
