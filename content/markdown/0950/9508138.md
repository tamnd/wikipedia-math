# Ideal sheaf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ideal sheaf"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), an **ideal sheaf** (or **sheaf of ideals**) is the global analogue of an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) in a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404). The ideal sheaves on a geometric object are closely connected to its subspaces.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $X$ be a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) and $A$ a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of rings on $X$; i.e., let $(X,A)$ be a [ringed space](wikipedia://en/248014). An ideal sheaf $J$ in $A$ is a [subobject](wikipedia://en/540476) of $A$ in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of sheaves of $A$-modules, i.e., a [subsheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of $A$ viewed as a sheaf of abelian groups such that

$$\Gamma(U,A)\cdot\Gamma(U,J)\subseteq \Gamma(U,J)$$

for all open subsets $U$ of $X$. In other words, $J$ is a [sheaf of $A$-submodules](wikipedia://en/44887605) of $A$.

:::

## General properties {#General_properties}

If $f:A\to B$ is a homomorphism between two sheaves of rings on the same space $X$, the kernel of $f$ is an ideal sheaf in $A$.

Conversely, for any ideal sheaf $J$ in a sheaf of rings $A$, there is a natural structure of a sheaf of rings on the [quotient sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_sheaf) $A/J$. Note that the canonical map

$$\Gamma(U,A)/\Gamma(U,J) \to \Gamma(U,A/J)$$

for open subsets $U$ is injective, but not surjective in general (see [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357)).

## Motivation {#Motivation}

In the context of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754), the importance of ideal sheaves lies mainly in the correspondence between closed [subschemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscheme) and [quasi-coherent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-coherent_sheaf) ideal sheaves. Consider a scheme $X$ and a quasi-coherent ideal sheaf $J$ in $\mathcal{O}_X$. Then, the support $Z$ of $\mathcal{O}_X/J$ is a closed subspace of $X$, and $(Z,\mathcal{O}_X/J)$ is a scheme (both assertions can be checked locally). It is called the closed subscheme of $X$ defined by $J$. Conversely, let $i:Z\to X$ be a [closed immersion](wikipedia://en/1623986), i.e., a morphism which is a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) onto a closed subspace such that the associated map

$$i^\# : \mathcal{O}_X\to i_*\mathcal{O}_Z$$

is surjective on the stalks. Then, the kernel $J$ of $i^\#$ is a quasi-coherent ideal sheaf, and $i$ induces an isomorphism from $Z$ onto the closed subscheme defined by $J$.[^n1]

A particular case of this correspondence is the unique [reduced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_scheme) subscheme $X_{\mathrm{red}}$ of $X$ having the same underlying space, which is defined by the [nilradical](wikipedia://en/289636) of $\mathcal{O}_X$ (defined stalk-wise, or on open affine charts).[^n2]

For a morphism $f:X\to Y$ and a closed subscheme $Y'\subseteq Y$ defined by an ideal sheaf $J$, the preimage $Y'\times_Y X$ is defined by the ideal sheaf[^n3]

$$f^*(J)\mathcal{O}_X = \operatorname{im}(f^*J\to \mathcal{O}_X).$$

The pull-back of an ideal sheaf $J$ to the subscheme $Z$ defined by $J$ contains important information, it is called the [conormal bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conormal_bundle) of $Z$. For example, the sheaf of [Kähler differentials](wikipedia://en/603916) may be defined as the pull-back of the ideal sheaf defining the diagonal $X\to X\times X$ to $X$. (Assume for simplicity that $X$ is [separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separated_morphism) so that the diagonal is a closed immersion.)[^n4]

## Analytic geometry {#Analytic_geometry}

In the theory of [complex-analytic spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex-analytic_space), the [Oka-Cartan theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oka-Cartan_theorem) states that a closed subset $A$ of a complex space is analytic if and only if the ideal sheaf of functions vanishing on $A$ is [coherent](wikipedia://en/603780). This ideal sheaf also gives $A$ the structure of a reduced closed complex subspace.
