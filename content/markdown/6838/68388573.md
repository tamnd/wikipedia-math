# Hantzsche–Wendt manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hantzsche–Wendt manifold"}

The **Hantzsche–Wendt manifold**, also known as the **HW manifold** or **didicosm**,[^o2] is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable), [flat](wikipedia://en/12756616) [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257), first studied by [Walter Hantzsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Hantzsche) and Hilmar Wendt in 1934.[^o3] It is the only closed flat 3-manifold with first [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) zero. Its [holonomy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holonomy_group) is $\mathbb{Z}_2^2$.[^o4] It has been suggested as a possible [shape of the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_of_the_universe) because its complicated topology can obscure the features in the [cosmic microwave background](wikipedia://en/7376) that would arise if the universe is a closed [flat manifold](wikipedia://en/12756616), such as the [3-torus](wikipedia://en/2455103).[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

*Image: Hantzsche–Wendt construction.svg.* The Hantzsche–Wendt manifold is made from two cubes stacked on top of each other by gluing the identically-marked faces such that the markings line up.[^o8]

The HW manifold can be built from two cubes that share a face. One construction proceeds as follows:[^o8]

1. The top and bottom faces are glued to one another.
1. One of the remaining sides is glued to the opposite side with a 180° rotation.
1. One of the remaining faces on the top cube is glued to the matching face of the bottom cube, reflected across an axis parallel to the long axis of the double-cube.
1. Repeat step 3 for the remaining pair of faces.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

In addition to the orientable one (the Hantzsche–Wendt manifold), there are two non-orientable flat 3-manifolds with holonomy group $\mathbb{Z}_2^2$, known as the **first** and **second amphidicosms**, both with first Betti number 1.[^o2]

Similar flat *n*-dimensional manifolds with holonomy $\mathbb{Z}_2^{n-1}$, known as **generalized Hantzsche–Wendt manifolds**, may be constructed for any *n*≥2, but orientable ones exist only in odd dimensions.[^o2] The number of orientable HW manifolds up to [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) increases exponentially with dimension.[^o4] All of these have first Betti number *β*<sub>1</sub> 0 or 1.[^o2]
