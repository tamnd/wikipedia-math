# Al-Qalqashandi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Al-Qalqashandi"}

**Shihāb al-Dīn Abū 'l-Abbās Aḥmad ibn ‘Alī ibn Aḥmad ‘Abd Allāh [al-Fazārī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Fazara) al-Shāfiʿī**[^n1] better known by the [epithet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nisba_%28onomastics%29) **al-Qalqashandī** (شهاب الدين أحمد بن علي بن أحمد القلقشندي; 1355 or 1356 – 1418), was a medieval [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptians) [encyclopedist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopedia), [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath) and [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematics). A native of the [Nile Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Delta), he became a Scribe of the Scroll (*Katib al-Darj*), or clerk of the [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk) chancery in [Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo), [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt). His [magnum opus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnum_opus) is the voluminous administrative encyclopedia *Ṣubḥ al-Aʿshá*.

:::

## *Ṣubḥ al-aʿshā* {#Ṣubḥ_al-aʿshā}

*Image: Subh alasha manuscript.jpg.* Ṣubḥ al-aʿshā, published 1193 AH

*Ṣubḥ al-Aʿshá fī Ṣināʿat al-Inshāʾ* ('The Dawn of the Blind' or 'Daybreak for the Night-Blind regarding the Composition of Chancery Documents'); a fourteen-volume encyclopedia completed in 1412, is an administrative manual on geography, political history, natural history, zoology, mineralogy, cosmography, and time measurement. Based on the *Masālik al-abṣār fī mamālik al-amṣar* of [Shihab al-Umari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shihab_al-Umari),[^o6] it has been called "one of the final expressions of the genre of [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) administrative literature".[^o7] Selections on "Seats of Government " and "Regulations of the Kingdom " from Early Islam to the Mamluks' have been published separately.[^o8]

The *Ṣubḥ al-aʿshā* was cited by [David Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kahn_%28writer%29) as the first published discussion of the substitution and transposition of ciphers, and the first description of a [polyalphabetic cipher](wikipedia://en/157918), in which each [plaintext](wikipedia://en/157935) letter is assigned more than one substitution. The exposition on [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715) included the use of tables of [letter frequencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_frequencies) and sets of letters which cannot occur together in one word. [^o9] Kahn therefore cited it as the first work in human history that described cryptology, because it described both cryptography and cryptanalysis. Al-Qalqashandi quoted the text relevant to cryptology from the work of [Ibn al-Durayhim](wikipedia://en/35575543) (1312–1361) that was once considered lost. Later discoveries in Istanbul‟s Sulaimaniyyah Ottoman Archives did not just find the work by Ibn Duraihim, but also works of [al-Kindi](wikipedia://en/272065) in the 9th century that is now considered the oldest work on cryptology.[^o10]
