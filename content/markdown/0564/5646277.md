# List of limits

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of limits"}

This is a **list of [limits](wikipedia://en/26551602)** for common [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) such as [elementary functions](wikipedia://en/10412). In this article, the terms *a*, *b* and *c* are constants with respect to *x*.

:::

## Limits for general functions {#Limits_for_general_functions}

### Definitions of limits and related concepts {#Definitions_of_limits_and_related_concepts}

$\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $\forall \varepsilon>0\ \exists \delta>0 : 0 < |x-c| < \delta \implies |f(x)-L| < \varepsilon$. This is the [(ε, δ)-definition of limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%28ε,_δ%29-definition_of_limit).

The [limit superior and limit inferior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_superior_and_limit_inferior) of a sequence are defined as $\limsup_{n \to \infty} x_n = \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(\sup_{m \geq n} x_m\right)$ and $\liminf_{n \to \infty} x_n = \lim_{n \to \infty}\left(\inf_{m \geq n} x_m\right)$.

A function, $f(x)$, is said to be continuous at a point, *c*, if

$$\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = f(c).$$

### Operations on a single known limit {#Operations_on_a_single_known_limit}

If $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$ then:

- $$\lim_{x \to c} \, [f(x) \pm a] = L \pm a$$
- $\lim_{x \to c} \, a f(x) = a L$[^o5][^o6][^o7]
- $\lim_{x \to c} \frac{1}{f(x)}= \frac1L$[^o8] if L is not equal to 0.
- $\lim_{x \to c} \, f(x)^n = L^n$ if *n* is a positive integer[^o5][^o6][^o7]
- $\lim_{x \to c} \, f(x)^{1 \over n} = L^{1 \over n}$ if *n* is a positive integer, and if *n* is even, then *L* > 0.[^o5][^o7]

In general, if *g*(*x*) is continuous at *L* and $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$ then

- $\lim_{x \to c} g\left(f(x)\right) =g(L)$[^o5][^o6]

### Operations on two known limits {#Operations_on_two_known_limits}

If $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L_1$ and $\lim_{x \to c} g(x) = L_2$ then:

- $\lim_{x \to c} \, [f(x) \pm g(x)] = L_1 \pm L_2$[^o5][^o6][^o7]
- $\lim_{x \to c} \, [f(x)g(x)] = L_1 \cdot L_2$[^o5][^o6][^o7]
- $\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{L_1}{L_2} \qquad \text{ if } L_2 \ne 0$[^o5][^o6][^o7]

### Limits involving derivatives or infinitesimal changes {#Limits_involving_derivatives_or_infinitesimal_changes}

In these limits, the infinitesimal change $h$ is often denoted $\Delta x$ or $\delta x$. If $f(x)$ is [differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206) at $x$,

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="Derivative"}

- $\lim_{h \to 0} {f(x+h)-f(x)\over h} = f'(x)$. This is the definition of the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921). All [differentiation rules](wikipedia://en/10308785) can also be reframed as rules involving limits. For example, if *g*(*x*) is differentiable at *x*,

:::

  - $\lim_{h \to 0} {f\circ g(x+h)-f\circ g(x)\over h}=f'[g(x)]g'(x)$. This is the [chain rule](wikipedia://en/6113).
  - $\lim_{h \to 0} {f(x+h)g(x+h)-f(x)g(x)\over h}=f'(x)g(x)+f(x)g'(x)$. This is the [product rule](wikipedia://en/248988).
- $$\lim_{h \to 0} \left(\frac{f(x+h)}{f(x)}\right)^{1/h} = \exp\left(\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\right)$$
- $$\lim_{h \to 0} {\left({f(e^h x)\over{f(x)}}\right)^{1/h} } = \exp\left(\frac{x f'(x)}{f(x)}\right)$$

If $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are differentiable on an open interval containing *c*, except possibly *c* itself, and $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = \lim_{x \to c} g(x) = 0 \text{ or } \pm\infty$, [L'Hôpital's rule](wikipedia://en/18531) can be used:

- $\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to c} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$[^o6]

### Inequalities {#Inequalities}

If $f(x)\leq g(x)$ for all x in an interval that contains *c*, except possibly *c* itself, and the limit of $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ both exist at *c*, then[^o13]

$$\lim_{x\to c}f(x)\leq \lim_{x\to c}g(x)$$

If $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = \lim_{x \to c} h(x) = L$ and $f(x)\leq g(x)\leq h(x)$ for all *x* in an [open interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_interval) that contains *c*, except possibly *c* itself,

$$\lim_{x \to c} g(x) = L.$$

This is known as the [squeeze theorem](wikipedia://en/330994).[^o5][^o6] This applies even in the cases that *f*(*x*) and *g*(*x*) take on different values at *c*, or are discontinuous at *c*.

## Polynomials and functions of the form *x<sup>a</sup>* {#Polynomials_and_functions_of_the_form_xa}

- $\lim_{x \to c} a = a$[^o5][^o6][^o7]

### Polynomials in x {#Polynomials_in_x}

- $\lim_{x \to c} x = c$[^o5][^o6][^o7]
- $$\lim_{x \to c} (ax + b) = ac + b$$
- $\lim_{x \to c} x^n = c^n$ if *n* is a positive integer[^o13]
- $$\lim_{x\to\infty} x/a = \begin{cases} \infty, & a > 0 \\ \text{does not exist}, & a = 0 \\ -\infty, & a < 0 \end{cases}$$

In general, if $p(x)$ is a polynomial then, by the continuity of polynomials,[^o13]

$$\lim_{x \to c} p(x) = p(c)$$

This is also true for [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210), as they are continuous on their [domains](wikipedia://en/50263).[^o13]

### Functions of the form *x<sup>a</sup>* {#Functions_of_the_form_xa}

- $\lim_{x\to c}x^a=c^a.$[^o13] In particular,
  - $$\lim_{x\to\infty}x^a=\begin{cases} \infty, & a > 0 \\ 1, & a = 0 \\ 0, & a < 0 \end{cases}$$
- $\lim_{x\to c}x^{1/a}=c^{1/a}$.[^o13] In particular,
  - $\lim_{x\to\infty} x^{1/a}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\sqrt[a]{x}= \infty \text{ for any } a > 0$[^o17]
- $$\lim_{x \to 0^+} x^{-n} =\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{1}{x^n}= +\infty$$
- $$\lim_{x \to 0^-} x^{-n} =\lim_{x \to 0^-} \frac{1}{x^n} =\begin{cases} -\infty, & \text{if } n \text{ is odd} \\ +\infty, & \text{if } n \text{ is even} \end{cases}$$
- $$\lim_{x\to\infty} ax^{-1}=\lim_{x\to\infty}a/x=0 \text{ for any real }a$$

## Exponential functions {#Exponential_functions}

### Functions of the form *a*<sup>*g*(*x*)</sup> {#Functions_of_the_form_ag(x)}

- $\lim_{x \to c} e^{x} = e^c$, due to the continuity of $e^{x}$
- $$\lim_{x\to\infty}a^x=\begin{cases} \infty, & a > 1 \\ 1, & a = 1 \\ 0, & 0 < a < 1 \end{cases}$$
- $\lim_{x\to\infty}a^{-x}=\begin{cases} 0, & a > 1 \\ 1, & a = 1 \\ \infty, & 0 < a < 1 \end{cases}$[^o17]
- $$\lim_{x\to\infty}\sqrt[x]{a}=\lim_{x\to\infty}{a}^{1/x}=\begin{cases} 1, & a > 0 \\ 0, & a = 0 \\ \text{does not exist}, & a < 0 \end{cases}$$

### Functions of the form *x*<sup>*g*(*x*)</sup> {#Functions_of_the_form_xg(x)}

- $$\lim_{x\to\infty}\sqrt[x]{x}=\lim_{x\to\infty}{x}^{1/x}=1$$

### Functions of the form *f*(*x*)<sup>*g*(*x*)</sup> {#Functions_of_the_form_f(x)g(x)}

- $\lim_{x\to+\infty} \left( \frac{x}{x+k}\right)^x=e^{-k}$[^o6]
- $\lim_{x\to 0} \left(1+x\right)^\frac{1}{x}=e$[^o6]
- $$\lim_{x\to 0} \left(1+kx\right)^\frac{m}{x}=e^{mk}$$
- $\lim_{x\to+\infty} \left(1+\frac{1}{x}\right)^x=e$[^o22]
- $$\lim_{x\to+\infty} \left(1-\frac{1}{x}\right)^x=\frac{1}{e}$$
- $\lim_{x\to+\infty} \left(1+\frac{k}{x}\right)^{mx}=e^{mk}$[^o17]
- $\lim_{x \to 0} \left(1+ a \left({e^{-x} - 1}\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{x}} = e^{a}$. This limit can be derived from this limit.

### Sums, products and composites {#Sums,_products_and_composites}

- $$\lim_{x \to 0} x e^{-x} = 0$$
- $$\lim_{x \to \infty} x e^{-x} = 0$$
- $\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{a^x - 1}{x} \right) = \ln{a},$ for all positive *a*.[^o8][^o22]
- $$\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{e^x - 1}{x} \right) = 1$$
- $$\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{e^{ax} - 1}{x} \right) = a$$

## Logarithmic functions {#Logarithmic_functions}

### Natural logarithms {#Natural_logarithms}

- $\lim_{x \to c} \ln{x} = \ln c$, due to the continuity of $\ln {x}$. In particular,
  - $$\lim_{x\to0^+}\log x=-\infty$$
  - $$\lim_{x\to\infty}\log x=\infty$$
- $$\lim_{x\to1}\frac{\ln(x)}{x-1}=1$$
- $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\ln(x+1)}{x}=1$[^o22]

::: {#o26 .entry cue=list-entry name="L'Hôpital's rule"}

- $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-\ln\left(1+ a \left({e^{-x} - 1}\right)\right)}{x} = a$. This limit follows from [L'Hôpital's rule](wikipedia://en/18531).

:::

- $\lim_{x \to 0} x\ln x = 0$, hence $\lim_{x \to 0} x^x = 1$
- $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\ln x}{x} = 0$[^o17]

### Logarithms to arbitrary bases {#Logarithms_to_arbitrary_bases}

For *b* > 1,

- $$\lim_{x \to 0^+} \log_b x = -\infty$$
- $$\lim_{x \to \infty} \log_b x = \infty$$

For *b* \< 1,

- $$\lim_{x \to 0^+} \log_b x = \infty$$
- $$\lim_{x \to \infty} \log_b x = -\infty$$

Both cases can be generalized to:

- $$\lim_{x \to 0^+} \log_b x = -F(b)\infty$$
- $$\lim_{x \to \infty} \log_b x = F(b)\infty$$

where $F(x) = 2H(x-1) - 1$ and $H(x)$ is the [Heaviside step function](wikipedia://en/87299)

## Trigonometric functions {#Trigonometric_functions}

If $x$ is expressed in radians:

- $$\lim_{x \to a} \sin x = \sin a$$
- $$\lim_{x \to a} \cos x = \cos a$$

These limits both follow from the continuity of sin and cos.

- $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1$.[^o22][^o29] Or, in general,
  - $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin ax}{ax} = 1$, for *a* not equal to 0.
  - $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin ax}{x} = a$$
  - $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin ax}{bx} = \frac{a}{b}$, for *b* not equal to 0.
- $$\lim_{x \to \infty} x\sin \left(\frac1x\right) = 1$$
- $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1-\cos x}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos x - 1}{x} = 0$[^o8][^o29][^o30]
- $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1-\cos x}{x^2} = \frac{1}{2}$$
- $\lim_{x \to n^\pm} \tan \left(\pi x + \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \mp\infty$, for integer *n*.
- $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x} = 1$. Or, in general,
  - $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan ax}{ax} = 1$, for *a* not equal to 0.
  - $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan ax}{bx} = \frac{a}{b}$, for *b* not equal to 0.
- $\lim_{n\to \infty }\ \underbrace{\sin \sin \cdots \sin(x_0)}_n= 0$, where *x*<sub>0</sub> is an arbitrary real number.

::: {#o31 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dottie number"}

- $\lim_{n\to \infty }\ \underbrace{\cos \cos \cdots \cos(x_0)}_n= d$, where d is the [Dottie number](wikipedia://en/54562671). *x*<sub>0</sub> can be any arbitrary real number.

:::

## Sums {#Sums}

In general, any [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) is the limit of its [partial sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_sums). For example, an [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) is the limit of its [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448), within its [radius of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476).

::: {#o33 .entry cue=list-entry name="harmonic series"}

- $\lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{k=1}^n\frac{1}{k}=\infty$. This is known as the [harmonic series](wikipedia://en/142488).[^o17]

:::

::: {#o34 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler Mascheroni constant"}

- $\lim_{n\to\infty}\left( \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}-\log n\right)=\gamma$. This is the [Euler Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_Mascheroni_constant).

:::

## Notable special limits {#Notable_special_limits}

- $$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{n}{\sqrt[n]{n!}}=e$$
- $\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(n!\right)^{1/n}=\infty$. This can be proven by considering the inequality $e^x \geq \frac{x^n}{n!}$ at $x = n$.

::: {#o36 .entry cue=list-entry name="Viète's formula"}

- $\lim_{n\to \infty }\, 2^{n} \underbrace{\sqrt{2-\sqrt{2+\sqrt{2+ \dots +\sqrt{2}}}}}_n= \pi$. This can be derived from [Viète's formula](wikipedia://en/1609504) for [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601).

:::

## Limiting behavior {#Limiting_behavior}

### Asymptotic equivalences {#Asymptotic_equivalences}

[Asymptotic equivalences](wikipedia://en/641995), $f(x)\sim g(x)$, are true if $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=1$. Therefore, they can also be reframed as limits. Some notable asymptotic equivalences include

::: {#o39 .entry cue=list-entry name="Prime number theorem"}

- $\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{x/\ln x}{\pi(x)}=1$, due to the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692), $\pi(x)\sim\frac{x}{\ln x}$, where π(x) is the [prime counting function](wikipedia://en/331826).

:::

::: {#o40 .entry cue=list-entry name="Stirling's approximation"}

- $\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^n}{n!}=1$, due to [Stirling's approximation](wikipedia://en/151783), $n! \sim \sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^n$.

:::

### Big O notation {#Big_O_notation}

The behaviour of functions described by [Big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578) can also be described by limits. For example

- $f(x)\in\mathcal{O}(g(x))$ if $\limsup_{x\to\infty} \frac{|f(x)|}{g(x)}<\infty$
