# Empirical measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Empirical measure"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), an **empirical measure** is a [random measure](wikipedia://en/10040846) arising from a particular realization of a (usually finite) sequence of [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685). The precise definition is found below. Empirical measures are relevant to [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711).

:::

The motivation for studying empirical measures is that it is often impossible to know the true underlying [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) $P$. We collect observations $X_1, X_2, \dots , X_n$ and compute [relative frequencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_frequencies). We can [estimate](wikipedia://en/10043) $P$, or a related distribution function $F$ by means of the empirical measure or empirical distribution function, respectively. These are uniformly good estimates under certain conditions. Theorems in the area of [empirical processes](wikipedia://en/3690433) provide rates of this convergence.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be a sequence of [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_random_variables) identically distributed [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) with values in the state space *S* with probability distribution *P*.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition"}

**Definition**

The *empirical measure* *P*<sub>*n*</sub> is defined for measurable subsets of *S* and given by

$$P_n(A) = {1 \over n} \sum_{i=1}^n I_A(X_i)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \delta_{X_i}(A)$$

where $I_A$ is the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) and $\delta_X$ is the [Dirac measure](wikipedia://en/1019002).

**Properties**

- For a fixed measurable set *A*, *nP*<sub>*n*</sub>(*A*) is a [binomial](wikipedia://en/3876) random variable with mean *nP*(*A*) and variance *nP*(*A*)(1 − *P*(*A*)).
  - In particular, *P*<sub>*n*</sub>(*A*) is an [unbiased estimator](wikipedia://en/8450479) of *P*(*A*).
- For a fixed [partition](wikipedia://en/340240) $A_i$ of *S*, random variables $Y_i=nP_n(A_i)$ form a [multinomial distribution](wikipedia://en/1045553) with *event probabilities* $P(A_i)$
  - The [covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/191752) of this multinomial distribution is $Cov(Y_i,Y_j)=nP(A_i)(\delta_{ij}-P(A_j))$.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition"}

**Definition**

$\bigl(P_n(c)\bigr)_{c\in\mathcal{C}}$ is the *empirical measure* indexed by $\mathcal{C}$, a collection of measurable subsets of *S*.

To generalize this notion further, observe that the empirical measure $P_n$ maps [measurable functions](wikipedia://en/44055) $f:S\to \mathbb{R}$ to their *[empirical mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_mean)*,

$$f\mapsto P_n f=\int_S f \, dP_n=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n f(X_i)$$

In particular, the empirical measure of *A* is simply the empirical mean of the indicator function, *P*<sub>*n*</sub>(*A*) = *P*<sub>*n*</sub> *I*<sub>*A*</sub>.

For a fixed measurable function $f$, $P_nf$ is a random variable with mean $\mathbb{E}f$ and variance $\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}(f -\mathbb{E} f)^2$.

By the strong [law of large numbers](wikipedia://en/157055), *P*<sub>n</sub>(*A*) converges to *P*(*A*) [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) for fixed *A*. Similarly $P_nf$ converges to $\mathbb{E} f$ almost surely for a fixed measurable function $f$. The problem of uniform convergence of *P*<sub>*n*</sub> to *P* was open until [Vapnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vapnik) and [Chervonenkis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chervonenkis) solved it in 1968.[^o6]

If the class $\mathcal{C}$ (or $\mathcal{F}$) is [Glivenko–Cantelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glivenko–Cantelli_class) with respect to *P* then *P*<sub>*n*</sub> converges to *P* uniformly over $c\in\mathcal{C}$ (or $f\in \mathcal{F}$). In other words, with probability 1 we have

$$\|P_n-P\|_\mathcal{C}=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}}|P_n(c)-P(c)|\to 0,$$

$$\|P_n-P\|_\mathcal{F}=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}|P_nf-\mathbb{E}f|\to 0.$$

:::

:::

## Empirical distribution function {#Empirical_distribution_function}

The *empirical distribution function* provides an example of empirical measures. For real-valued [iid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iid) random variables $X_1,\dots,X_n$ it is given by

$$F_n(x)=P_n((-\infty,x])=P_nI_{(-\infty,x]}.$$

In this case, empirical measures are indexed by a class $\mathcal{C}=\{(-\infty,x]:x\in\mathbb{R}\}.$ It has been shown that $\mathcal{C}$ is a uniform [Glivenko–Cantelli class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glivenko–Cantelli_class), in particular,

$$\sup_F\|F_n(x)-F(x)\|_\infty\to 0$$

with probability 1.
