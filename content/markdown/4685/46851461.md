# Japaridze's polymodal logic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Japaridze's polymodal logic"}

**Japaridze's polymodal logic** (**GLP**) is a system of [provability logic](wikipedia://en/618119) with infinitely many provability [modalities](wikipedia://en/333365). This system has played an important role in some applications of provability algebras in [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), and has been extensively studied since the late 1980s. It is named after [Giorgi Japaridze](wikipedia://en/1866981).

:::

## Language and axiomatization {#Language_and_axiomatization}

The language of GLP extends that of the language of classical [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154) by including the infinite series $[0],[1],[2],...$ of necessity operators. Their dual possibility operators $<0>,<1>,<2>,...$ are defined by $<n>p = \neg[n]\neg p$.

The axioms of GLP are all classical tautologies and all formulas of one of the following forms:

- $$[n](p \to q) \to ([n]p \to [n]q)$$
- $$[n]([n]p \to p) \to [n]p$$
- $$[n]p \to [n+1]p$$
- $$<n>p \to [n+1]<n>p$$

And the rules of inference are:

- From $p$ and $p \to q$ conclude $q$
- From $p$ conclude $[0]p$

## Provability semantics {#Provability_semantics}

Consider a sufficiently strong [first-order theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_theory) $T$ such as [Peano Arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_Arithmetic) $PA$. Define the series $T_{0},T_{1},T_{2},...$ of theories as follows:

- $T_{0}$ is $T$
- $T_{n+1}$ is the extension of $T_{n}$ through the additional axioms $\forall xF(x)$ for each formula $F(x)$ such that $T_{n}$ proves all of the formulas $F(0), F(1), F(2),...$

For each $n$, let $\Pr_{n}(x)$ be a natural arithmetization of the predicate $''x$ is the [Gödel number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_number) of a sentence provable in $T_{n}''$.

A *realization* is a function $*$ that sends each nonlogical atom $a$ of the language of GLP to a sentence $a*$ of the language of $T$. It extends to all formulas of the language of GLP by stipulating that $*$ commutes with the Boolean connectives, and that $([n]F)*$ is $\Pr_{n}('F*')$, where $'F*'$ stands for (the numeral for) the Gödel number of $F*$.

An arithmetical completeness theorem[^o4] for GLP states that a formula $F$ is provable in GLP [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922), for every interpretation $*$, the sentence $F*$ is provable in $T$.

The above understanding of the series $T_{0},T_{1},T_{2},...$ of theories is not the only natural understanding yielding the soundness and completeness of GLP. For instance, each theory $T_{n}$ can be understood as $T$ augmented with all true $\Pi_{n}$ sentences as additional axioms. [George Boolos](wikipedia://en/340621) showed[^o5] that GLP remains sound and complete with analysis ([second-order arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3542454)) in the role of the base theory $T$.

## Other semantics {#Other_semantics}

GLP has been shown[^o4] to be incomplete with respect to any class of [Kripke frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kripke_frame).

A natural topological semantics of GLP interprets modalities as derivative operators of a [polytopological space](wikipedia://en/62153058). Such spaces are called GLP-spaces whenever they satisfy all the axioms of GLP. GLP is complete with respect to the class of all GLP-spaces.[^o7]

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

The problem of being a theorem of GLP is [PSPACE-complete](wikipedia://en/54685). So is the same problem restricted to only variable-free formulas of GLP.[^o9]

## History {#History}

GLP, under the name GP, was introduced by [Giorgi Japaridze](wikipedia://en/1866981) in his PhD thesis "Modal Logical Means of Investigating Provability" ([Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), 1986) and published two years later[^o4] along with (a) the [completeness theorem](wikipedia://en/12450) for GLP with respect to its provability interpretation (Beklemishev subsequently came up with a simpler proof of the same theorem[^o11]) and (b) a proof that Kripke frames for GLP do not exist. Beklemishev also conducted a more extensive study of Kripke models for GLP.[^o12] Topological models for GLP were studied by Beklemishev, Bezhanishvili, Icard and Gabelaia.[^o7][^o13] The decidability of GLP in polynomial space was proven by I. Shapirovsky,[^o14] and the PSPACE-hardness of its variable-free fragment was proven by F. Pakhomov.[^o9] Among the most notable applications of GLP has been its use in proof-theoretically analyzing [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic), elaborating a canonical way for recovering [ordinal notation](wikipedia://en/6108552) up to $\text{ɛ}_{0}$ from the corresponding algebra, and constructing simple combinatorial independent statements (Beklemishev [^o15]).

An extensive survey of GLP in the context of provability logics in general was given by [George Boolos](wikipedia://en/340621) in his book *The Logic of Provability*.[^o16]
