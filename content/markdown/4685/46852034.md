# Bhattacharyya angle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bhattacharyya angle"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **Bhattacharyya angle**, also called **statistical angle**, is a measure of distance between two [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) measures defined on a finite probability space. It is defined as

:::

$$\Delta(p,q) = \arccos \operatorname{BC}(p,q)$$

where *p*<sub>*i*</sub>, *q*<sub>*i*</sub> are the probabilities assigned to the point *i*, for *i* = 1, ..., *n*, and

$$\operatorname{BC}(p,q) = \sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt{p_i q_i}$$

is the **Bhattacharya coefficient**.[^o2]

The Bhattacharya distance is the [geodesic distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_distance) in the [orthant](wikipedia://en/1283725) of the sphere $S^{n-1}$ obtained by projecting the probability simplex on the sphere by the transformation $p_i \mapsto \sqrt{p_i},\ i=1,\ldots, n$.

This distance is compatible with [Fisher metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_metric). It is also related to [Bures distance](wikipedia://en/20086937#Bures_distance) and [fidelity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_fidelity) between [quantum states](wikipedia://en/30876419) as for two diagonal states one has

$$\Delta(\rho,\sigma) = \arccos \sqrt{F(\rho, \sigma)}.$$
