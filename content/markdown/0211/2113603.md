# Alice Ambrose

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alice Ambrose"}

**Alice Ambrose Lazerowitz** (November 25, 1906 – January 25, 2001) was an American [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), and author.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Alice Loman Ambrose was born in [Lexington, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Illinois) and orphaned when she was 13 years old.[^o4] She studied philosophy and mathematics at [Millikin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millikin_University) (1924–28).[^o5] After completing her PhD at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) in 1932, she went to [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) ([Newnham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newnham_College)) to study with [G. E. Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._E._Moore) and [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741), where she earned a second PhD in 1938.

## Wittgenstein {#Wittgenstein}

*Image: Alice Ambrose in 1927 (with Josephine Higman, Edgar Allen and Mark E. Penney).png.* Alice Ambrose in 1927

Having become a close disciple of Wittgenstein, Ambrose later related her association with him in *Ludwig Wittgenstein: Philosophy and Language* (1972), a volume co-edited with her husband [Morris Lazerowitz](wikipedia://en/41400681). Along with fellow student [Margaret MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_MacDonald_%28philosopher%29) she secretly (since he did not allow this) made notes during Wittgenstein's lectures, which were later published.[^o7] She was one of a select group of students to whom Wittgenstein dictated the so-called [Blue and Brown Books](wikipedia://en/28341040), which outline the transition in Wittgenstein's thought between his two major works, *[Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus](wikipedia://en/30062)* and *[Philosophical Investigations](wikipedia://en/23674)*. There is also the so-called *Yellow Book* which consists of notes taken by Ambrose and [Margaret Masterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Masterman) during the intervals between the dictation of the *Blue Book*.[^o8]

Wittgenstein terminated their association abruptly in 1935, when Ambrose decided, with encouragement from G. E. Moore, to publish an article entitled "Finitism in Mathematics" in the philosophical journal *[Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_%28journal%29)*[^o9] which was intended to give an account of Wittgenstein's position on the subject.[^o10]

## Career {#Career}

Ambrose began her career at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), when she returned to the United States in 1935. She then took a position in [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College) in 1937, where she remained for the rest of her career. She was awarded the Austin and Sophia Smith chair in Philosophy in 1964 and became professor emeritus in 1972. Between 1953 and 1968, she was editor of the *[Journal of Symbolic Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Symbolic_Logic)*. She worked chiefly in logic and mathematical philosophy, writing a primer on the subject with her husband which became a widely used textbook and was known as "Ambrose and Lazerowitz".[^o12] She collaborated with her husband on a number of works: *Fundamentals of Symbolic Logic* (1948), *Logic: The Theory of Formal Inference* (1961), *Philosophical Theories* (1976) and *Essays in the Unknown Wittgenstein* (1984). Even after her retirement she continued to teach and guest lecture at Smith, [Hampshire College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_College), the [University of Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Delaware), and other universities around the country until her death.

Ambrose died at the age of 94, on January 25, 2001, in [Northampton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton,_Massachusetts).[^o4]

Her personal papers are held at Smith College Archives.[^o13]
