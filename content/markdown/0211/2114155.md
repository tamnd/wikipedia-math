As $m$ and $n$ become large, a duoprism approaches the geometry of [duocylinder](wikipedia://en/2114445) just like a $p$-gonal prism approaches a [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29).

::: {#o11 .subject cue=lead name="Duoprism"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of 4 dimensions or higher, a **double prism**[^o12] or **duoprism** is a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) resulting from the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two polytopes, each of two dimensions or higher. The Cartesian product of an $n$-polytope and an $m$-polytope is an $(n+m)$-polytope, where $n$ and $m$ are dimensions of 2 ([polygon](wikipedia://en/23621)) or higher.

:::

The lowest-dimensional **duoprisms** exist in [4-dimensional space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-dimensional_space) as [4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/24979) being the Cartesian product of two polygons in 2-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). More precisely, it is the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of points:

$$P_1 \times P_2 = \{ (x,y,z,w) | (x,y)\in P_1, (z,w)\in P_2 \}$$

where $P_{1}$ and $P_{2}$ are the sets of the points contained in the respective polygons. Such a duoprism is [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885) if both bases are convex, and is bounded by [prismatic cells](wikipedia://en/283007).

## Nomenclature {#Nomenclature}

Four-dimensional duoprisms are considered to be prismatic 4-polytopes. A duoprism constructed from two [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) of the same edge length is a **uniform duoprism**.

A duoprism made of *n*-polygons and *m*-polygons is named by prefixing 'duoprism' with the names of the base polygons, for example: a *triangular-pentagonal duoprism* is the Cartesian product of a triangle and a pentagon.

An alternative, more concise way of specifying a particular duoprism is by prefixing with numbers denoting the base polygons, for example: 3,5-duoprism for the triangular-pentagonal duoprism.

Other alternative names:

- **q**-gonal-**p**-gonal prism
- **q**-gonal-**p**-gonal double prism
- **q**-gonal-**p**-gonal hyperprism

The term *duoprism* is coined by George Olshevsky, shortened from *double prism*. [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) proposed a similar name [proprism](wikipedia://en/27882423) for *product prism*, a Cartesian product of two or more polytopes of dimension at least two. The duoprisms are proprisms formed from exactly two polytopes.

## Geometry of 4-dimensional duoprisms {#Geometry_of_4-dimensional_duoprisms}

A 4-dimensional **[uniform](wikipedia://en/1645899) duoprism** is created by the product of a regular *n*-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) and a regular *m*-sided polygon with the same edge length. It is bounded by *n* *m*-gonal [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) and *m* *n*-gonal prisms. For example, the Cartesian product of a triangle and a hexagon is a duoprism bounded by 6 triangular prisms and 3 hexagonal prisms.

- When *m* and *n* are identical, the resulting duoprism is bounded by 2*n* identical *n*-gonal prisms. For example, the Cartesian product of two triangles is a duoprism bounded by 6 triangular prisms.
- When *m* and *n* are identically 4, the resulting duoprism is bounded by 8 square prisms ([cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_%28geometry%29)), and is identical to the [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112).

The *m*-gonal prisms are attached to each other via their *m*-gonal faces, and form a closed loop. Similarly, the *n*-gonal prisms are attached to each other via their *n*-gonal faces, and form a second loop perpendicular to the first. These two loops are attached to each other via their square faces, and are mutually perpendicular.

As *m* and *n* approach infinity, the corresponding duoprisms approach the [duocylinder](wikipedia://en/2114445). As such, duoprisms are useful as non-[quadric](wikipedia://en/145570) approximations of the duocylinder.

### Orthogonal projections {#Orthogonal_projections}

The 5,5 projection is identical to the 3D [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025#Orthogonal_projections).

## Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

*Image: Duocylinder ridge animated.gif.* A [stereographic projection](wikipedia://en/143431) of a rotating [duocylinder](wikipedia://en/2114445), divided into a checkerboard surface of squares from the {4,4$|$n} skew polyhedron

The [regular skew polyhedron](wikipedia://en/19193037), {4,4|n}, exists in 4-space as the n<sup>2</sup> square faces of a *n-n duoprism*, using all 2n<sup>2</sup> edges and n<sup>2</sup> vertices. The 2*n* *n*-gonal faces can be seen as removed. (skew polyhedra can be seen in the same way by a n-m duoprism, but these are not *regular*.)

### Duoantiprism {#Duoantiprism}

*Image: Snub p2q verf.png.* p-q duoantiprism [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719), a [gyrobifastigium](wikipedia://en/1177963)

*Image: great duoantiprism.png.* [Great duoantiprism](wikipedia://en/42287126), [stereographic projection](wikipedia://en/143431), centred on one [pentagrammic crossed-antiprism](wikipedia://en/4157338)

Like the [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851) as alternated [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), there is a set of 4-dimensional duoantiprisms: [4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-polytopes) that can be created by an [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) operation applied to a duoprism. The alternated vertices create nonregular tetrahedral cells, except for the special case, the *4-4 duoprism* ([tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112)) which creates the uniform (and regular) [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422). The 16-cell is the only convex uniform duoantiprism.

The duoprisms , t<sub>0,1,2,3</sub>{p,2,q}, can be alternated into , ht<sub>0,1,2,3</sub>{p,2,q}, the "duoantiprisms", which cannot be made uniform in general. The only convex uniform solution is the trivial case of p=q=2, which is a lower symmetry construction of the [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112) , t<sub>0,1,2,3</sub>{2,2,2}, with its alternation as the [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422), , s{2}s{2}.

The only nonconvex uniform solution is p=5, q=5/3, ht<sub>0,1,2,3</sub>{5,2,5/3}, , constructed from 10 [pentagonal antiprisms](wikipedia://en/2860091), 10 [pentagrammic crossed-antiprisms](wikipedia://en/4157338), and 50 tetrahedra, known as the [great duoantiprism](wikipedia://en/42287126) (gudap).[^o43][^o44]

:::

### Ditetragoltriates {#Ditetragoltriates}

Also related are the ditetragoltriates or octagoltriates, formed by taking the [octagon](wikipedia://en/314575) (considered to be a ditetragon or a truncated square) to a p-gon. The *octagon* of a p-gon can be clearly defined if one assumes that the octagon is the convex hull of two perpendicular [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048); then the p-gonal ditetragoltriate is the convex hull of two p-p duoprisms (where the p-gons are similar but not congruent, having different sizes) in perpendicular orientations. The resulting polychoron is isogonal and has 2p p-gonal prisms and p<sup>2</sup> rectangular trapezoprisms (a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) with *D<sub>2d</sub>* symmetry) but cannot be made uniform. The vertex figure is a [triangular bipyramid](wikipedia://en/646933).

### Double antiprismoids {#Double_antiprismoids}

Like the duoantiprisms as alternated duoprisms, there is a set of p-gonal double antiprismoids created by alternating the 2p-gonal ditetragoltriates, creating p-gonal antiprisms and tetrahedra while reinterpreting the non-corealmic triangular bipyramidal spaces as two tetrahedra. The resulting figure is generally not uniform except for two cases: the [grand antiprism](wikipedia://en/4058047) and its conjugate, the pentagrammic double antiprismoid (with p = 5 and 5/3 respectively), represented as the alternation of a decagonal or decagrammic ditetragoltriate. The vertex figure is a variant of the [sphenocorona](wikipedia://en/1142750).

### k<sub>22</sub> polytopes {#k22_polytopes}

The [3-3 duoprism](wikipedia://en/41745711), −1<sub>22</sub>, is first in a dimensional series of uniform polytopes, expressed by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) as k<sub>22</sub> series. The 3-3 duoprism is the vertex figure for the second, the [birectified 5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birectified_5-simplex). The fourth figure is a Euclidean honeycomb, [2<sub>22</sub>](wikipedia://en/19582093), and the final is a paracompact hyperbolic honeycomb, 3<sub>22</sub>, with Coxeter group \[3<sup>2,2,3</sup>\], ${\bar{T}}_7$. Each progressive [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392) is constructed from the previous as its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719).
