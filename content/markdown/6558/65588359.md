# Brezis–Lieb lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brezis–Lieb lemma"}

In the mathematical field of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), the **Brezis–Lieb lemma** is a basic result in [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory). It is named for [Haïm Brézis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haïm_Brézis) and [Elliott Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Lieb), who discovered it in 1983. The lemma can be viewed as an improvement, in certain settings, of [Fatou's lemma](wikipedia://en/291614) to an equality. As such, it has been useful for the study of many [variational problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_problem).

:::

::: {#o3 .proof cue=named-heading name="The lemma and its proof"}

## The lemma and its proof {#The_lemma_and_its_proof}

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Statement of the lemma"}

### Statement of the lemma {#Statement_of_the_lemma}

Let $(X, \mu)$ be a [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) and let $f_{n}$ be a sequence of measurable complex-valued functions on $X$ which converge almost everywhere to a function $f$. The limiting function $f$ is automatically measurable. The Brezis–Lieb lemma asserts that if $p$ is a positive number, then

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_X\Big||f|^p-|f_n|^p+|f-f_n|^p\Big|\,d\mu=0,$$

provided that the sequence $f_{n}$ is uniformly bounded in $L^{p}(X, \mu)$. A significant consequence, which sharpens [Fatou's lemma](wikipedia://en/291614) as applied to the sequence $|f_{n}|^{p}$, is that

$$\int_X|f|^p\,d\mu=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_X|f_n|^p\,d\mu-\int_X|f-f_n|^p\,d\mu\right),$$

which follows by the [triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941). This consequence is often taken as the statement of the lemma, although it does not have a more direct proof.

:::

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

The essence of the proof is in the inequalities

$$\begin{aligned} W_n\equiv \Big||f_n|^p-|f|^p-|f-f_n|^p\Big|&\leq\Big||f_n|^p-|f-f_n|^p\Big|+|f|^p\\ &\leq\varepsilon|f-f_n|^p+C_\varepsilon|f|^p. \end{aligned}$$

The consequence is that $W_{n} - \varepsilon|f - f_{n}|^{p}$, which converges almost everywhere to zero, is bounded above by an integrable function, independently of $n$. The observation that

$$W_n\leq\max\Big(0,W_n-\varepsilon|f-f_n|^p\Big)+\varepsilon|f-f_n|^p,$$

and the application of the [dominated convergence theorem](wikipedia://en/351853) to the first term on the right-hand side shows that

$$\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_XW_n\,d\mu\leq\varepsilon\sup_n\int_X |f-f_n|^p\,d\mu.$$

The finiteness of the supremum on the right-hand side, with the arbitrariness of $\varepsilon$, shows that the left-hand side must be zero.
