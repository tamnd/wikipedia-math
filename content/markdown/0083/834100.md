# Superparticular ratio

*Image: Just diatonic semitone on C.png.* Just diatonic semitone on C: $\tfrac{16}{15}$ = $\tfrac{15 + 1}{15}$ = 1 + $\tfrac{1}{15}$ Just diatonic semitone on C.mid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Superparticular ratio"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **superparticular ratio**, also called a **superparticular number** or **epimoric ratio**, is the [ratio](wikipedia://en/87837) of two consecutive [integer numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_number).

:::

More particularly, the ratio takes the form:

$\frac{n + 1}{n} = 1 + \frac{1}{n}$ where $n$ is a [positive integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer).

Thus: A superparticular number is when a great number contains a lesser number, to which it is compared, and at the same time one part of it. For example, when 3 and 2 are compared, they contain 2, plus the 3 has another 1, which is half of two. When 3 and 4 are compared, they each contain a 3, and the 4 has another 1, which is a third part of 3. Again, when 5, and 4 are compared, they contain the number 4, and the 5 has another 1, which is the fourth part of the number 4, etc.

Superparticular ratios were written about by [Nicomachus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicomachus) in his treatise *[Introduction to Arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduction_to_Arithmetic)*. Although these numbers have applications in modern [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210), the areas of study that most frequently refer to the superparticular ratios by this name are [music theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_theory)[^o10] and the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220).[^o3]

## Mathematical properties {#Mathematical_properties}

As [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) observed, the superparticular numbers (including also the multiply superparticular ratios, numbers formed by adding an integer other than one to a [unit fraction](wikipedia://en/491316)) are exactly the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) whose [simple continued fraction](wikipedia://en/46802) terminates after two terms. The numbers whose continued fraction terminates in one term are the integers, while the remaining numbers, with three or more terms in their continued fractions, are [superpartient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpartient_number).[^o5]

The [Wallis product](wikipedia://en/1301687)

$$\prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{2n}{2n-1} \cdot \frac{2n}{2n+1}\right) = \frac{2}{1} \cdot \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{4}{5} \cdot \frac{6}{5} \cdot \frac{6}{7} \cdots = \frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{16}{15}\cdot\frac{36}{35}\cdots=2\cdot\frac{8}{9}\cdot\frac{24}{25}\cdot\frac{48}{49}\cdots=\frac{\pi}{2}$$

represents the [irrational number](wikipedia://en/20647689) [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601) in several ways as a product of superparticular ratios and their [inverses](wikipedia://en/229940). It is also possible to convert the [Leibniz formula for π](wikipedia://en/1301559) into an [Euler product](wikipedia://en/398540) of superparticular ratios in which each term has a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) as its numerator and the nearest multiple of four as its denominator:[^o6]

$$\frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{5}{4} \cdot \frac{7}{8} \cdot \frac{11}{12} \cdot \frac{13}{12} \cdot\frac{17}{16}\cdots$$

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), superparticular numbers (or rather, their reciprocals, 1/2, 2/3, 3/4, etc.) arise via the [Erdős–Stone theorem](wikipedia://en/10836468) as the possible values of the [upper density](wikipedia://en/2777098) of an infinite graph.[^o7]

## Other applications {#Other_applications}

In the study of [harmony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmony), many musical [intervals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_%28music%29) can be expressed as a superparticular ratio (for example, due to [octave equivalency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave_equivalency), the ninth harmonic, 9/1, may be expressed as a superparticular ratio, 9/8). Indeed, whether a ratio was superparticular was the most important criterion in [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979)'s formulation of musical harmony.[^o9] In this application, [Størmer's theorem](wikipedia://en/3445204) can be used to list all possible superparticular numbers for a given [limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_%28music%29); that is, all ratios of this type in which both the numerator and denominator are [smooth numbers](wikipedia://en/1035039).[^o10]

These ratios are also important in visual harmony. [Aspect ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspect_ratio_%28image%29) of 4:3 and 3:2 are common in [digital photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_photography),[^o11] and aspect ratios of 7:6 and 5:4 are used in [medium format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_format_%28film%29) and [large format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_format) photography respectively.[^o12]

## Ratio names and related intervals {#Ratio_names_and_related_intervals}

Every pair of adjacent positive integers represent a superparticular ratio, and similarly every pair of adjacent harmonics in the [harmonic series (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_series_%28music%29) represent a superparticular ratio.
