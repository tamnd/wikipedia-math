# Gromov's systolic inequality for essential manifolds

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gromov's systolic inequality for essential manifolds"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), [M. Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29)'s **systolic inequality** bounds the length of the shortest [non-contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414) loop on a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) in terms of the volume of the manifold. Gromov's systolic inequality was proved in 1983;[^o2] it can be viewed as a generalisation, albeit non-optimal, of [Loewner's torus inequality](wikipedia://en/12003118) and [Pu's inequality for the real projective plane](wikipedia://en/12004717).

:::

Technically, let *M* be an [essential](wikipedia://en/18617907) Riemannian manifold of dimension *n*; denote by sys*π*<sub>1</sub>(*M*) the homotopy 1-systole of *M*, that is, the least length of a non-contractible loop on *M*. Then Gromov's inequality takes the form

$$\left(\operatorname{sys\pi}_1(M)\right)^n \leq C_n \operatorname{vol}(M),$$

where *C*<sub>*n*</sub> is a universal constant only depending on the dimension of *M*.

## Essential manifolds {#Essential_manifolds}

A closed manifold is called *essential* if its [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) defines a nonzero element in the [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) of its [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004), or more precisely in the homology of the corresponding [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230). Here the fundamental class is taken in homology with integer coefficients if the manifold is orientable, and in coefficients modulo 2, otherwise.

Examples of essential manifolds include [aspherical manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspherical_manifold), [real projective spaces](wikipedia://en/1014694), and [lens spaces](wikipedia://en/885725).

## Proofs of Gromov's inequality {#Proofs_of_Gromov's_inequality}

Gromov's original 1983 proof is about 35 pages long. It relies on a number of techniques and inequalities of global Riemannian geometry. The starting point of the proof is the imbedding of X into the Banach space of Borel functions on X, equipped with the sup norm. The imbedding is defined by mapping a point *p* of *X*, to the real function on *X* given by the distance from the point *p*. The proof utilizes the [coarea inequality](wikipedia://en/17915846), the [isoperimetric inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoperimetry), the cone inequality, and the deformation theorem of [Herbert Federer](wikipedia://en/12185345).

:::

## Filling invariants and recent work {#Filling_invariants_and_recent_work}

One of the key ideas of the proof is the introduction of filling invariants, namely the [filling radius](wikipedia://en/12868362) and the filling volume of *X*. Namely, Gromov proved a sharp inequality relating the systole and the filling radius,

$$\mathrm{sys\pi}_1 \leq 6\; \mathrm{FillRad}(X),$$

valid for all essential manifolds *X*; as well as an inequality

$$\mathrm{FillRad}(X) \leq C_n \mathrm{vol}_n{}^{\tfrac{1}{n}}(X),$$

valid for all closed manifolds *X*.

It was shown by (Brunnbauer 2008) that the filling invariants, unlike the systolic invariants, are independent of the topology of the manifold in a suitable sense.

(Guth 2011) and (Ambrosio Katz 2011) developed approaches to the proof of Gromov's systolic inequality for essential manifolds.

## Inequalities for surfaces and polyhedra {#Inequalities_for_surfaces_and_polyhedra}

Stronger results are available for surfaces, where the asymptotics when the genus tends to infinity are by now well understood, see [systoles of surfaces](wikipedia://en/19060246). A uniform inequality for arbitrary 2-complexes with non-free fundamental groups is available, whose proof relies on the [Grushko decomposition theorem](wikipedia://en/18715724).
