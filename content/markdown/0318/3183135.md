# Electronic Communications Act 2000

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Electronic Communications Act 2000"}

The **Electronic Communications Act 2000** (c. 7) is an [act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Parliament_%28United_Kingdom%29) of the [Parliament of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_United_Kingdom) that:

:::

- Had provisions to regulate the provision of [cryptographic](wikipedia://en/18934432) services in the UK (ss.1-6); and
- Confirms the legal status of [electronic signatures](wikipedia://en/627040) (ss.7-10).

The United Kingdom government had come to the conclusion that encryption, encryption services and electronic signatures would be important to [e-commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-commerce) in the UK.[^o3]

By 1999, however, only the security services still hankered after key escrow. So a "[sunset clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_clause)" was put in the bill. The Electronic Communications Act 2000 gave the [Home Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Office) the power to create a registration regime for encryption services. This was given a five-year period before it would automatically lapse, which eventually happened in May 2006.

The act implemented the Electronic Signatures Directive 1999, which member states had until 19 July 2001 to bring into local effect.[^o4] The act was adopted into the [Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_of_Investigatory_Powers_Act_2000).[^o5]
