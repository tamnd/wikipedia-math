# Max Mason

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Max Mason"}

*Image: Kronprins Gustaf Adolf hedersdoktor Chicago.jpg.* Crown Prince [Gustaf Adolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustaf_VI_Adolf_of_Sweden) of Sweden receives his doctor's diploma as an honorary doctorate from the University of Chicago from the university's president, Professor Max Mason, 1926

**Charles Max Mason** (26 October 1877–22 March 1961), better known as **Max Mason**, was an American mathematician. He served the [4th president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_University_of_Chicago) of the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) from 1925 to 1928 and as the third president of the [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation) from 1929 to 1936.[^o3][^o4]

:::

Mason's mathematical research interests included [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), and electromagnetic theory.[^o5]

## Education {#Education}

- B.Litt., 1898, [University of Wisconsin-Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin-Madison)
- Ph.D., Mathematics, [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), 1903.
  - Dissertation: "Randwertaufgaben bei gewöhnlichen Differentialgleichungen" ([Boundary value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_value) functions with ordinary differential equations)
  - Advisor: [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)

## Career {#Career}

- [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) (MIT), 1903–1904, Instructor of Mathematics.
- [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), 1904–1908, Assistant Professor of Mathematics.
- [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison), 1908–1909, University of Wisconsin–Madison, Associate Professor of Mathematics.
- University of Wisconsin–Madison, 1909–1925, Professor of [Physics](wikipedia://en/22939).
  - [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council), 1917–1919, Submarine Committee. (Invented a [submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine) detection device, which was the basis for [sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonar) detectors used in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).)
- University of Chicago, 1925–1928, President.
- Rockefeller Foundation, 1928–1929, Director, Natural Sciences Division.
- Rockefeller Foundation, 1929–1936, President.
- [Palomar Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palomar_Observatory) (California), 1936–1949, Chairman of the team directing the construction of the observatory.[^o8]

On 2 May 1945, he appeared on [Edgar Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Bergen)'s radio show to chat about the new observatory and trade jokes with [Charlie McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_McCarthy).[^o9] In 1948, he, along with [Lee A. DuBridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Alvin_DuBridge), [William A. Fowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Alfred_Fowler), [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling), and [Bruce H. Sage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Hornbrook_Sage), was awarded the [Medal for Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_for_Merit) by President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman).[^o10]
