# Analytic manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Analytic manifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **analytic manifold**, also known as a $C^\omega$ manifold, is a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) with [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478) transition maps.[^o2] The term usually refers to real analytic manifolds, although [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742) are also analytic.[^o3] In algebraic geometry, [analytic spaces](wikipedia://en/8031773) are a generalization of analytic manifolds such that singularities are permitted.

:::

For $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$, the space of analytic functions, $C^{\omega}(U)$, consists of infinitely differentiable functions $f:U \to \mathbb{R}$, such that the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448)

$$T_f(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{|\alpha| \geq 0}\frac{D^\alpha f(\mathbf{x_0})}{\alpha!} (\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x_0})^\alpha$$

converges to $f(\mathbf{x})$ in a neighborhood of $\mathbf{x_0}$, for all $\mathbf{x_0} \in U$. The requirement that the transition maps be analytic is significantly more restrictive than that they be infinitely differentiable; the analytic manifolds are a proper subset of the [smooth](wikipedia://en/1408000#Differentiability_classes), i.e. $C^\infty$, manifolds.[^o2] There are many similarities between the theory of analytic and smooth manifolds, but a critical difference is that analytic manifolds do not admit analytic partitions of unity, whereas smooth [partitions of unity](wikipedia://en/48636) are an essential tool in the study of smooth manifolds.[^o4] A fuller description of the definitions and general theory can be found at [differentiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219), for the real case, and at [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742), for the complex case.
