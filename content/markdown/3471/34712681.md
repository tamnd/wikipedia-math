# Akio Hattori

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Akio Hattori"}

**Akio Hattori** was a Japanese mathematician working in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) who [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) the [Hattori–Stong theorem](wikipedia://en/34712196). Hattori was the president of the [Mathematical Society of Japan](wikipedia://en/18376786) in 1989–1991.[^o4][^o5]

:::

Hattori received a Doctorate in Science from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in 1959[^o6] with [Shokichi Iyanaga](wikipedia://en/18357287) as his advisor. He then joined the faculty of the University of Tokyo.[^o7] Between 1966 and 1968 Hattori worked as a visiting scholar at both [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) and [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University).

After retirement from University of Tokyo, Hattori was invited to teach at [Meiji University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_University) from 1991[^o8][^o9] to 1999,[^o9] when they opened mathematics major in 1989. Professor Hiroko Morimoto invited Drs. Akio Hattori and Hiroshi Fujita from the University of Tokyo, and Dr. Shiro Goto from [Nihon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihon_University) joined to launch a research and educational system for algebra, geometry and analytics.[^o10]
