# Bitcoin Core

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bitcoin Core"}

**Bitcoin Core** is [free and open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_open-source_software) that serves as a [bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265) [node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Node_%28networking%29) (the set of which form the [Bitcoin network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_network)) and provides a [bitcoin wallet](wikipedia://en/28249265#Wallets) which fully verifies payments. It is considered to be bitcoin's [reference implementation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_implementation).[^o3] Initially, the software was published by [Satoshi Nakamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoshi_Nakamoto) under the name "Bitcoin", and later renamed to "Bitcoin Core" to distinguish it from the [network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_network).[^o4] It is also known as the *Satoshi client*.[^o5] Bitcoin Core includes a transaction verification engine and connects to the bitcoin network as a full [node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Node_%28networking%29).[^o5] As of 2013, peer-reviewed measurements of the Bitcoin network's message propagation showed that new blocks reach 95% of nodes within about 40 seconds and a median delay of 12.6 seconds, underscoring the importance of efficient node software such as Bitcoin Core.[^o6]

:::

The software validates the entire [blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain_%28database%29), which includes all bitcoin transactions ever. This [distributed ledger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_ledger), which has reached more than 608.9 gigabytes (not including database indexes) in size as of October 2024,[^o7] must be downloaded or synchronized before full participation of the client may occur.[^o5] Bitcoin Core includes a scripting language inspired by [Forth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forth_%28programming_language%29) that can define transactions and specify parameters.[^o8]

The original creator of the bitcoin client has described their approach to the software's authorship as it being written first to prove to themselves that the concept of purely peer-to-peer electronic cash was valid and that a paper with solutions could be written. [Gavin Andresen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_Andresen) was the former lead maintainer for the software client. Andresen left the role of lead developer for bitcoin to work on the strategic development of its technology.[^o9] Wladimir J. van der Laan took over as lead maintainer on 8 April 2014[^o9] and held the role until August 2022, when he stepped back from the project, citing burnout and a desire to further decentralize the project's governance.[^o10] Van der Laan removed his own merge privileges in February 2023, and the role of lead maintainer has remained vacant since his departure; Bitcoin Core is now maintained by a group of developers with commit access.[^o10] Bitcoin Core in 2015 was central to a dispute with [Bitcoin XT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_XT), a competing client that sought to increase the blocksize.[^o11]

Bitcoin Core development is funded by a number of organizations and grant programs. In 2019, the [MIT Media Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Media_Lab) announced donations of \$900,000 would be used to fund the Digital Currency Initiative, which would mainly go to developers of Bitcoin Core.[^o12]
