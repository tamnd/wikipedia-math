# Apollonius point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Apollonius point"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **Apollonius point** is a [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488) designated as *X*(181) in [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421)'s [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593) (ETC). It is defined as the point of [concurrence](wikipedia://en/2206157) of the three line segments joining each vertex of the triangle to the points of [tangency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangency) formed by the opposing [excircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) and a larger circle that [is tangent to all three excircles](wikipedia://en/2642185).

:::

In the literature, the term "*Apollonius points*" has also been used to refer to the [isodynamic points](wikipedia://en/5102885) of a triangle.[^o2] This usage could also be justified on the ground that the isodynamic points are related to the three [Apollonian circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollonius_circle) associated with a triangle.

The solution of the Apollonius problem has been known for centuries. But the Apollonius point was first noted in 1987.[^o3][^o4]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Apollonius point.svg.* solid #333333 solid blue solid green solid red

The Apollonius point of a triangle is defined as follows.

Let $\triangle ABC$ be any given triangle. Let the [excircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) of $\triangle ABC$ opposite to the vertices $A, B, C$ be $E_{A}, E_{B}, E_{C}$ respectively. Let $E$ be the circle which touches the three excircles $E_{A}, E_{B}, E_{C}$ such that the three excircles are within $E$. Let $A', B', C'$ be the points of contact of the circle $E$ with the three excircles. The lines $AA', BB', CC'$ are [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157). The point of concurrence is the *Apollonius point* of $\triangle ABC$.

The Apollonius problem is the problem of constructing a circle tangent to three given circles in a plane. In general, there are eight circles touching three given circles. The circle $E$ referred to in the above definition is one of these eight circles touching the three excircles of triangle $\triangle ABC$. In [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593) the circle $E$ is the called the *Apollonius circle* of $\triangle ABC$.

:::

## Trilinear coordinates {#Trilinear_coordinates}

The [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) of the Apollonius point are[^o3]

$$\frac{a(b + c)^2}{b + c - a} : \frac{b(c + a)^2}{c + a - b} : \frac{c(a + b)^2}{a + b - c}$$

$$= \sin^2\! A \, \cos^2\! \frac{B - C}{2} : \sin^2\! B \, \cos^2\! \frac{C - A}{2} : \sin^2\! C \, \cos^2\! \frac{A - B}{2}.$$
