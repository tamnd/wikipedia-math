# Point groups in four dimensions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Point groups in four dimensions"}

*Image: Polychoral group tree.png.* A hierarchy of 4D point groups and some subgroups. Vertical positioning is grouped by order. Blue, green, and pink colors show reflectional, hybrid, and rotational groups.

*Image: Polychoral group tree-conway.png.* Some 4D point groups in Conway's notation

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **[point group](wikipedia://en/1138942) in four dimensions** is an [isometry group](wikipedia://en/1101694) in four dimensions that leaves the origin fixed, or correspondingly, an isometry group of a [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792).

:::

## Isometries of 4D point symmetry {#Isometries_of_4D_point_symmetry}

There are four basic isometries of 4-dimensional [point symmetry](wikipedia://en/44362809#Symmetries_in_general): [reflection symmetry](wikipedia://en/1390873), [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096), [rotoreflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotoreflection), and [double rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_rotation).

## Notation for groups {#Notation_for_groups}

Point groups in this article are given in [Coxeter notation](wikipedia://en/32088502), which are based on [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004), with markups for extended groups and subgroups.[^o10] Coxeter notation has a direct correspondence the Coxeter diagram like \[3,3,3\], \[4,3,3\], \[3<sup>1,1,1</sup>\], \[3,4,3\], \[5,3,3\], and \[p,2,q\]. These groups bound the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) into identical hyperspherical tetrahedral domains. The number of domains is the order of the group. The number of mirrors for an irreducible group is *nh/2*, where *h* is the Coxeter group's [Coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_number), *n* is the dimension (4).[^o11]

For cross-referencing, also given here are [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) based notations by [Patrick du Val](wikipedia://en/10637467) (1964)[^o12] and [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) (2003).[^o13] Conway's notation allows the order of the group to be computed as a product of elements with chiral polyhedral group orders: (T=12, O=24, I=60). In Conway's notation, a (±) prefix implies [central inversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_inversion), and a suffix (.2) implies mirror symmetry. Similarly Du Val's notation has an asterisk (\*) superscript for mirror symmetry.

### Involution groups {#Involution_groups}

There are five [involutional](wikipedia://en/293450) groups: no symmetry \[ \]<sup>\+</sup>, [reflection symmetry](wikipedia://en/1390873) \[ \], 2-fold [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096) \[2\]<sup>\+</sup>, 2-fold [rotoreflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotoreflection) \[2<sup>\+</sup>,2<sup>\+</sup>\], and central [point symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_symmetry) \[2<sup>\+</sup>,2<sup>\+</sup>,2<sup>\+</sup>\] as a 2-fold [double rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_rotation).

### Rank 4 Coxeter groups {#Rank_4_Coxeter_groups}

A **polychoric group** is one of five [symmetry groups](wikipedia://en/28186) of the 4-dimensional [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693). There are also three polyhedral prismatic groups, and an infinite set of duoprismatic groups. Each group defined by a [Goursat tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/24104450#3-sphere_%28finite%29_solutions) [fundamental domain](wikipedia://en/469365) bounded by mirror planes. The [dihedral angles](wikipedia://en/338046) between the mirrors determine order of [dihedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry). The [Coxeter–Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/5358554) is a graph where nodes represent mirror planes, and edges are called branches, and labeled by their dihedral angle order between the mirrors.

The term *polychoron* (plural *polychora*, adjective *polychoric*), from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) roots *poly* ("many") and *choros* ("room" or "space") and was advocated[^o16] by [Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527) and George Olshevsky in the context of [uniform polychora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polychoron) (4-polytopes), and their related 4-dimensional symmetry groups.[^o17]

Rank 4 [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004) allow a set of 4 mirrors to span 4-space, and divides the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) into tetrahedral fundamental domains. Lower rank Coxeter groups can only bound [hosohedron](wikipedia://en/2316192) or [hosotope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosotope) fundamental domains on the 3-sphere.

Like the 3D [polyhedral groups](wikipedia://en/24192415), the names of the 4D polychoric groups given are constructed by the Greek prefixes of the cell counts of the corresponding triangle-faced regular polytopes.[^o19] Extended symmetries exist in uniform polychora with symmetric ring-patterns within the [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) construct. Chiral symmetries exist in [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) uniform polychora.

Only irreducible groups have Coxeter numbers, but duoprismatic groups \[p,2,p\] can be doubled to p,2,p by adding a 2-fold gyration to the fundamental domain, and this gives an effective Coxeter number of 2*p*, for example the \[4,2,4\] and its full symmetry B<sub>4</sub>, \[4,3,3\] group with Coxeter number 8.
