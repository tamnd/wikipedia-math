# Graph algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the fields of [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **graph algebra** is a way of giving a [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986) an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364). It was introduced by McNulty and Shallon,[^n1] and has seen many uses in the field of universal algebra since then.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $D = (V, E)$ be a directed [graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_%28data_structure%29), and $0$ an element not in $V$. The graph algebra associated with $D$ has underlying set $V \cup \{0\}$, and is equipped with a multiplication defined by the rules

- $xy = x$ if $x,y \in V$ and $(x,y) \in E$,
- $xy = 0$ if $x,y \in V \cup \{0\}$ and $(x,y)\notin E$.

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

This notion has made it possible to use the methods of graph theory in universal algebra and several other areas of [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). Graph algebras have been used, for example, in constructions concerning [dualities](wikipedia://en/310885),[^n2] [equational theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equational_theory),[^n3] [flatness](wikipedia://en/8953682),[^n4] [groupoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupoid_%28algebra%29) [rings](wikipedia://en/48404),[^n5] [topologies](wikipedia://en/29954),[^n6] [varieties](wikipedia://en/1021753),[^n7] [finite-state machines](wikipedia://en/10931),[^n8][^n9] tree languages and [tree automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_automata),[^n10] etc.

:::
