# Purity (algebraic geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Purity"}

In the mathematical field of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **purity** is a theme covering a number of results and conjectures, which collectively address the question of proving that "when something happens, it happens in a particular [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149)".

:::

## Purity of the branch locus {#Purity_of_the_branch_locus}

For example, [ramification](wikipedia://en/384644) is a phenomenon of codimension 1 (in the geometry of [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742), reflecting as for [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) that ramify at single points that it happens in real codimension two). A classical result, **Zariski–Nagata purity** of [Masayoshi Nagata](wikipedia://en/3086117) and [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352),[^o3][^o4] called also **purity of the branch locus**, proves that on a non-singular [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) a *branch locus*, namely the set of points at which a morphism ramifies, must be made up purely of codimension 1 subvarieties (a [Weil divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_divisor)). There have been numerous extensions of this result into theorems of [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) and [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory), establishing purity of the branch locus in the sense of description of the restrictions on the possible "open subsets of failure" to be an [étale morphism](wikipedia://en/3022771).

## Cohomological purity {#Cohomological_purity}

There is also a homological notion of purity that is related, namely a collection of results stating that cohomology groups from a particular theory are trivial with the possible exception of one index *i*. Such results were established in [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) by [Michael Artin](wikipedia://en/1519821) (included in [SGA 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SGA_4)), and were foundational in setting up the theory to contain expected analogues of results from [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology). A general statement of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) known as the **[absolute cohomological purity conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_cohomological_purity_conjecture)** was proved by Ofer Gabber.[^o6] It concerns a [closed immersion](wikipedia://en/1623986) of schemes (regular, noetherian) that is purely of codimension *d*, and the relative [local cohomology](wikipedia://en/3212091) in the étale theory. With coefficients mod *n* where *n* is invertible, the cohomology should occur only with index 2*d* (and take on a predicted value).[^o7]
