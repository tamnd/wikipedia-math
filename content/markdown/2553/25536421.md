# Clark Kimberling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clark Kimberling"}

**Clark Kimberling** (born November 7, 1942, in [Hinsdale, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinsdale,_Illinois)) is a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), musician, and composer. He has been a mathematics professor since 1970 at the [University of Evansville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Evansville). His research interests include [triangle centers](wikipedia://en/12401488), [integer sequences](wikipedia://en/51421), and [hymnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymnology).

:::

Kimberling received his PhD in mathematics in 1970 from the [Illinois Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Institute_of_Technology), under the supervision of [Abe Sklar](wikipedia://en/39990462). Since at least 1994, he has maintained a list of triangle centers and their properties. In its current on-line form, the [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593), this list comprises tens of thousands of entries.

He has contributed to *The Hymn*, the journal of the [Hymn Society in the United States and Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymn_Society_in_the_United_States_and_Canada); and in the *[Canterbury Dictionary of Hymnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_Dictionary_of_Hymnology)*.

## Kimberling's golden triangle {#Kimberling's_golden_triangle}

*Image: Supreme\_Isosceles\_Triangle,\_with\_two\_pairs\_of\_angles\_in\_Golden\_Ratio.tif.* Example of a triangle that has angles in [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) ([Phi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi) to 1).

Robert C. Schoen has defined a "golden triangle" as a triangle with two of its sides in the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386). Kimberling has proposed that Schoen's definition of golden triangle be extended to include triangles which have angles that are in the golden ratio.[^o4] Kimberling has described a "doubly golden triangle" which has two sides that are in golden ratio and which also has two angles that are in golden ratio.[^o5]
