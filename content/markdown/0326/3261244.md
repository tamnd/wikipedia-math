# Indecomposable continuum

*Image: ContinuBJK.svg.* The first four stages of the construction of the bucket handle as the limit of a series of nested intersections

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Indecomposable continuum"}

In [point-set topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-set_topology), an **indecomposable continuum** is a [continuum](wikipedia://en/24758520) that is indecomposable, i.e. that cannot be expressed as the union of any two of its [proper](wikipedia://en/27631#Definitions) subcontinua. In 1910, [L. E. J. Brouwer](wikipedia://en/172896) was the first to describe an indecomposable continuum.

:::

Indecomposable continua have been used by topologists as a source of [counterexamples](wikipedia://en/143151). They also occur in [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A *continuum* $C$ is a nonempty [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018). The [arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28topology%29), the [*n*-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782), and the [Hilbert cube](wikipedia://en/216389) are examples of [path-connected](wikipedia://en/6233#Path_connectedness) continua; the [topologist's sine curve](wikipedia://en/292224) is an example of a continuum that is not path-connected. The [Warsaw circle](wikipedia://en/18767352#Warsaw_Circle) is a path-connected continuum that is not locally path-connected. A *subcontinuum* $C'$ of a continuum $C$ is a [closed](wikipedia://en/47279), connected subset of $C$. A space is *nondegenerate* if it is not equal to a single point. A continuum $C$ is *decomposable* if there exist two subcontinua $A$ and $B$ of $C$ such that $A \neq C$ and $B \neq C$ but $A \cup B = C$. It follows that $A$ and $B$ are nondegenerate. A continuum that is not decomposable is an *indecomposable continuum*. A continuum $C$ in which every subcontinuum is indecomposable is said to be *hereditarily indecomposable*. A *[composant](wikipedia://en/3280193)* of an indecomposable continuum $C$ is a maximal set in which any two points lie within some proper subcontinuum of $C$. A continuum $C$ is *irreducible between $c$ and $c'$* if $c, c' \in C$ and no proper subcontinuum contains both points. For a nondegenerate indecomposable metric continuum $X$, there exists an uncountable subset $J$ such that $X$ is irreducible between any two points of $J$.[^o4]

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Lakes of Wada.png.* Fifth stage of the Lakes of Wada

In 1910 L. E. J. Brouwer described an indecomposable continuum that disproved a conjecture made by [Arthur Moritz Schoenflies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Moritz_Schoenflies) that, if $X_1$ and $X_2$ are open, connected, disjoint sets in $\mathbb{R}^2$ such that $\partial X_1 = \partial X_2$, then $\partial X_1 = \partial X_2$ must be the union of two closed, connected proper subsets.[^o6] [Zygmunt Janiszewski](wikipedia://en/362471) described more such indecomposable continua, including a version of the bucket handle. Janiszewski, however, focused on the irreducibility of these continua. In 1917 [Kunizo Yoneyama](wikipedia://en/48146254) described the [Lakes of Wada](wikipedia://en/5774599) (named after [Takeo Wada](wikipedia://en/31487590)) whose common boundary is indecomposable. In the 1920s indecomposable continua began to be studied by the [Warsaw School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1028194) in *[Fundamenta Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/1576701)* for their own sake, rather than as pathological counterexamples. [Stefan Mazurkiewicz](wikipedia://en/629203) was the first to give the definition of indecomposability. In 1922 [Bronisław Knaster](wikipedia://en/1003125) described the [pseudo-arc](wikipedia://en/3264412), the first example found of a hereditarily indecomposable continuum.[^o7]

## Bucket handle example {#Bucket_handle_example}

Indecomposable continua are often constructed as the limit of a sequence of nested intersections, or (more generally) as the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of a sequence of continua. The buckethandle, or Brouwer–Janiszewski–Knaster continuum, is often considered the simplest example of an indecomposable continuum, and can be so constructed (see upper right). Alternatively, take the [Cantor ternary set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor_ternary_set) $\mathcal{C}$ projected onto the interval $[0,1]$ of the $x$-axis in the plane. Let $\mathcal{C}_0$ be the family of semicircles above the $x$-axis with center $(1/2,0)$ and with endpoints on $\mathcal{C}$ (which is symmetric about this point). Let $\mathcal{C}_1$ be the family of semicircles below the $x$-axis with center the midpoint of the interval $[2/3,1]$ and with endpoints in $\mathcal{C} \cap [2/3,1]$. Let $\mathcal{C}_i$ be the family of semicircles below the $x$-axis with center the midpoint of the interval $[2/3^i,3/3^i]$ and with endpoints in $\mathcal{C} \cap [2/3^i,3/3^i]$. Then the union of all such $\mathcal{C}_i$ is the bucket handle.[^o9]

The bucket handle admits no Borel transversal, that is there is no [Borel set](wikipedia://en/43327) containing exactly one point from each composant.

## Properties {#Properties}

In a sense, 'most' continua are indecomposable. Let $M$ be an [$n$-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-cell_%28mathematics%29) with [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) $d$, $2^M$ the set of all nonempty closed subsets of $M$, and $C(M)$ the *hyperspace* of all connected members of $2^M$ equipped with the [Hausdorff metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausdorff_metric) $H_d$ defined by $H_d(A,B) = \max\{\sup\{d(a, B) : a \in A\}, \sup\{d(b, A) : b \in B\}\}$. Then the set of nondegenerate indecomposable subcontinua of $M$ is [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) in $C(M)$.

:::

## In dynamical systems {#In_dynamical_systems}

In 1932 [George Birkhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Birkhoff) described his "remarkable closed curve", a homeomorphism of the [annulus](wikipedia://en/356158) that contained an invariant continuum. [Marie Charpentier](wikipedia://en/55849176) showed that this continuum was indecomposable, the first link from indecomposable continua to dynamical systems. The [invariant set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_set) of a certain [Smale](wikipedia://en/40294) [horseshoe map](wikipedia://en/449364) is the bucket handle. [Marcy Barge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcy_Barge) and others have extensively studied indecomposable continua in dynamical systems.[^o13]
