# Directional symmetry (time series)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Directional symmetry"}

In [statistical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_analysis) of [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) and in [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), **directional symmetry** is a statistical measure of a [model](wikipedia://en/27576)'s performance in predicting the direction of change, positive or negative, of a [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) from one time period to the next.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a time series $t$ with [values](wikipedia://en/4095147) $t_i$ at times $i=1, \ldots, n$ and a model that makes predictions for those values $\hat t_i$, then the directional symmetry (DS) statistic is defined as

$$\operatorname{DS}(t,\hat t) = \frac{100}{n-1}\sum_{i=2}^{n}d_i,$$

$$d_i = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if }(t_i - t_{i-1})(\hat t_i - \hat t_{i-1})> 0 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} .\end{cases}$$

:::

## Interpretation {#Interpretation}

The DS statistic gives the percentage of occurrences in which the sign of the change in value from one time period to the next is the same for both the actual and predicted time series. The DS statistic is a measure of the performance of a model in predicting the direction of value changes. The case $DS=100\%$ would indicate that a model perfectly predicts the direction of change of a time series from one time period to the next.
