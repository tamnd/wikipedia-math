# Alexander Bogomolny

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Bogomolny"}

**Alexander Bogomolny** (January 4, 1948 – July 7, 2018) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)-born [Israeli-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Americans) mathematician. He was [Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus) of Mathematics at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa), and formerly research fellow at the [Moscow Institute of Electronics and Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9974195), senior instructor at [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University) and software consultant at [Ben Gurion University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gurion_University). He wrote extensively about [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118), [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261) and mathematical games.

:::

He was known for his contribution to [heuristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristics) and [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471), creating and maintaining the mathematically themed educational website *Cut-the-Knot*. He was a pioneer in mathematical education on the internet, having started *Cut-the-Knot* in October 1996.[^o3]

## Education and academic career {#Education_and_academic_career}

Bogomolny attended [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) school No. 444, for gifted children, then entered [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), where he graduated with a master's degree in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in 1971.[^o5] From 1971 to 1974 he was a junior research fellow at the [Moscow Institute of Electronic Machine Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Institute_of_Electronic_Machine_Building) (MIEM). He emigrated to Israel and became a senior programmer at Lake Kinneret Research Laboratory in [Tiberias, Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiberias,_Israel) (1974 – 1977) and a software consultant at [Ben Gurion University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gurion_University) in Negev, [Be’er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be’er_Sheva), Israel (1976 – 1977). From 1976 to 1983 he was a senior instructor and researcher at [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University) in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem,_Israel). He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics at Hebrew University in 1981. His dissertation is titled, *A New Numerical Solution for the Stamp Problem* and his thesis advisor was [Gregory I. Eskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_I._Eskin). From 1981 to 1982 he was also a visiting professor at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University), where he taught mathematics.[^o6][^o3]

From 1982 to 1987 he was professor of mathematics at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa).[^o7] From August 1987 to August 1991 he was vice president of software development at CompuDoc, Inc.[^o7]

## Cut-the-Knot {#Cut-the-Knot}

Cut-the-Knot (CTK) is a free, advertisement-funded educational [website](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Website) which Bogomolny maintained from 1996 to 2018. It is devoted to popular exposition of various topics in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). The site was designed for teachers, children and parents, and anyone else curious about mathematics, with an eye to educating, encouraging interest, and provoking curiosity.[^o10] Its name is a reference to the legend of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great)'s solution to the [Gordian knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordian_knot).

CTK won more than 20 awards from scientific and educational publications,[^o11] including a *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* Web Award in 2003,[^o12] the *[Encyclopædia Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopædia_Britannica)*'s Internet Guide Award, and *[Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_%28journal%29)*'s NetWatch award.[^o13]

The site contains extensive analysis of many of the classic problems in [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446) including the [Apollonian gasket](wikipedia://en/1049256), [Napoleon's theorem](wikipedia://en/2728914), [logarithmic spirals](wikipedia://en/42373), the "Futurama Theorem" from the episode "[The Prisoner of Benda](wikipedia://en/28015034#The_theorem)", the [Pitot theorem](wikipedia://en/27623148), and [the monkey and the coconuts](wikipedia://en/58232142) problem. One page includes 122 proofs of the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034).[^o14]

Bogomolny wrote a manifesto for CTK in which he said that "Judging Mathematics by its pragmatic value is like judging symphony by the weight of its score."[^o15] He described the site as "a resource that would help learn, if not math itself, then, at least, ways to appreciate its beauty," and he wondered why it is acceptable among otherwise well-educated people "to confess a dislike and misunderstanding of Mathematics as a whole."[^o16]

Many mathematical ideas were originally illustrated by [Java applets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applet), but most were later replaced by [GeoGebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoGebra) applications, also used for material added later. CTK wiki (powered by [PmWiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PmWiki)) extends the main site with additional mathematical content, especially that with more complicated formulae than available on the main site.

From 2001–2004 several CTK articles with interactive applets were also hosted as a column at the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) website.[^o17]

## Book {#Book}

- *Cut the Knot: Probability Riddles.* Champaign, IL: [Wolfram](wikipedia://en/594109) Media, 2020.

Published after Bogomolny's death, with a foreword by his friend [Nassim Nicholas Taleb](wikipedia://en/1093496),[^o19] this book is based on the collection of probability riddles curated by Bogomolny on his website cut-the-knot.org, presented by topical progression.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Bogomolny had to leave [academia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy) because he had an uncorrectable hearing problem and was practically [deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaf) in his later years.[^o21]

## Tribute {#Tribute}

Bogomolny's older son David chronicled his yearlong recitation of [kaddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaddish) in honor of his father, originally on [The Times of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_Israel) blogs, in a series titled, "The skeptic's kaddish for the atheist", consisting of traditional Jewish sources, religious text analysis, modern interpretations and expressions of kaddish, philosophy, theology, eschatology, creative writing, and the personal reflections; memories; and experiences of a son in mourning.[^o23][^o24]
