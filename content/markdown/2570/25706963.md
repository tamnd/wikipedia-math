# Philosophical razor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Philosophical razor"}

In [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), a **razor** is a [principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle) or [rule of thumb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_thumb) that allows one to eliminate (shave off) unlikely explanations for a phenomenon, or avoid unnecessary actions.[^o2] Common examples include:

:::

- [Alder's razor](wikipedia://en/28117976#Newton's_flaming_laser_sword) (also known as Newton's flaming laser sword): If something cannot be settled by experiment or observation, then it is not worthy of debate.[^o3]
- [Grice's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Grice) razor (also known as [Guillaume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Guillaume)'s razor): As a principle of [parsimony](wikipedia://en/36797), conversational [implicatures](wikipedia://en/393671) are to be preferred over semantic context for [linguistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) explanations.[^o4]
- [Hanlon's razor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanlon's_razor): Never attribute to malice that which can be adequately explained by stupidity.[^o5]
- [Hitchens' razor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchens'_razor): That which can be asserted without evidence can be dismissed without evidence.[^o6]
- [Hume's guillotine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hume's_guillotine): What ought to be cannot be deduced from what is; prescriptive claims cannot be derived solely [from descriptive claims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fact–value_distinction), and must depend on other prescriptions.
- Hume's Proportionality of Cause: "If the cause, assigned for any effect, be not sufficient to produce it, we must either reject that cause, or add to it such qualities as will give it a just proportion to the effect."[^o7]
- [Occam's razor](wikipedia://en/36797): Explanations that require fewer assumptions are more likely to be correct; avoid unnecessary assumptions.
- [Popper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Popper)'s [falsification](wikipedia://en/11283) criterion of [demarcation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demarcation_problem): For a theory to be considered scientific, it must be falsifiable.[^o8]
- [Sagan standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagan_standard): Positive claims require positive evidence, extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence.[^o9]
