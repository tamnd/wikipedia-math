# Burmese numerals

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Burmese numerals"}

Burmese **[Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language) numerals** (မြန်မာ ကိန်းဂဏန်းများ, ) are a set of [numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numeral_%28linguistics%29) traditionally used in the [Burmese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language), although [Arabic numerals](wikipedia://en/1786) are also used. Burmese numerals follow the [Hindu–Arabic numeral system](wikipedia://en/3393371) commonly used in the rest of the world.

:::

## Main numbers {#Main_numbers}

### Zero to ten {#Zero_to_ten}

The Burmese numerals from 1 to 10 are all etymologically traced back to the [Proto-Sino-Tibetan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Sino-Tibetan_language), with shared cognates in related languages like Tibetan and Chinese.

<sup>1</sup> Burmese for *[zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28number%29)* comes from [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) śūnya. <sup>2</sup> Can be abbreviated to in list contexts, such as telephone numbers.

Spoken Burmese has innate pronunciation rules that govern numbers when they are combined with another word, be it a numerical place (e.g. tens, hundreds, thousands, etc.) or a measure word.[^o5]

- For one, two, and seven (all of which end in the rhyme ), when combined, shift to an open vowel, namely the schwa ()
- For three, four, five, and nine which all have the long tone (similar to the [first tone in Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese_phonology#Tones)), when combined, the word immediately following it, given that it begins with a consonant, shifts to a [voiced consonant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_consonant) (e.g., ၄၀, "40" is pronounced , not ). Other suffixes such as ထောင် (; thousand), သောင်း (; ten thousand), သိန်း (; hundred thousand), and သန်း (; million) all shift to (; thousand), (; ten thousand), (; hundred thousand), and ; million), respectively.
- For six and eight, no pronunciation shift occurs.

These pronunciation shifts are exclusively confined to spoken Burmese and are not spelt any differently.

Ten to nineteen are almost always expressed without including တစ် (one).

Another pronunciation rule shifts numerical place name (the tens, hundreds and thousands place) from the low tone to the creaky tone.[^o5]

- Number places from 10 (တစ်ဆယ်) up to 10<sup>7</sup> (ကုဋေ) has increment of 10<sup>1</sup>. Beyond those Number places, larger number places have increment of 10<sup>7</sup>. 10<sup>14</sup> (ကောဋိ) up to 10<sup>140</sup> (အသင်္ချေ) has increment of 10<sup>7</sup>.
- There are totally 27 major number places in Burmese numerals from 1×10<sup>0</sup> to 10<sup>140</sup>
- Numbers in the tens place: shift from ဆယ် (, low tone) to ဆယ့် (, creaky tone), except in numbers divisible by ten (10, 20, 30, etc.) In typical speech, the shift goes farther to ( or ).
- Numbers in the hundreds place: shift from ရာ (, low tone) to ရာ့ (, creaky tone), except for numbers divisible by 100.
- Numbers in the thousands place: shift from ထောင် (, low tone) to ထောင့် (, creaky tone), except for numbers divisible by 1000.

Hence, a number like 301 is pronounced (သုံးရာ့တစ်), while 300 is pronounced (သုံးရာ).

#### Round number rule {#Round_number_rule}

When a number is used as an adjective, the standard word order is number + measure word:

**၅** ခွက်

In spoken Burmese, for [round numbers](wikipedia://en/1201689) (numbers ending in zeroes), the word order is flipped to measure word + number:[^o10]

ခွက် **၂၀**

The exception to this rule is the number 10, which follows the standard word order.[^o5] **၁၀** ခွက်

### Ordinal numbers {#Ordinal_numbers}

Ordinal numbers, from first to tenth, are Burmese pronunciations of their [Pali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pali) equivalents.[^o5] They are prefixed to the noun. Beyond that, cardinal numbers can be raised to the ordinal by suffixing the particle မြောက် (, to raise) to the number in the following order: number + [measure word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_numerical_classifiers) + မြောက်.

### Decimal and fractional numbers {#Decimal_and_fractional_numbers}

Colloquially, decimal numbers are formed by saying ဒသမ (, Pali for 'tenth') where the [decimal separator](wikipedia://en/80503) is located:

ဆယ် ဒသမ တစ်

Half (1/2) is expressed primarily by တစ်ဝက် (), although ထက်ဝက်, အခွဲ and အခြမ်း are also used. Quarter (1/4) is expressed with အစိတ် () or တစ်စိတ်. Other fractional numbers are verbally expressed as follows: denominator + ပုံ () + numerator + ပုံ (portion):

လေး ပုံ သုံး ပုံ

### Abbreviations {#Abbreviations}

To indicate prices of merchandise, a circle is placed above the final zero of the price and a slash is added to indicate currency:[^o19]

'2,500': ၂၅၀၀ိ/ = \$၂၅၀၀
