# Gerunda Hughes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gerunda Hughes"}

**Gerunda Burke Hughes** (born 1951) is an American scholar of education, focusing on [educational assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_assessment) in [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471). She is a [professor emerita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emerita) in the [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University) School of Education, where she taught in the Department of Curriculum and Instruction.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hughes was born in 1951 in [Miami, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami,_Florida), one of five children of Gerald Clayton Burke, an African American mathematics teacher. She majored in mathematics as an undergraduate at the [University of Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rhode_Island). After a master's degree in mathematics from the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park), in the early 1970s, she became a remedial mathematics instructor at Howard University. While continuing to work at Howard University, she completed a Ph.D. in [educational psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_psychology) there, with Sylvia Taylor Johnson as her advisor and mentor.

Several years after completing her Ph.D., she transitioned from an instructor to a regular-rank assistant professor, in the Department of Curriculum and Instruction. As a faculty member at Howard University, she led the university's Office of Institutional Assessment and Evaluation, and served as co-editor-in-chief of the *[Journal of Negro Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Negro_Education)*, before retiring as a professor emerita.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Hughes was the 2026 recipient of the [Louise Hay Award](wikipedia://en/51270001) of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698), honored "for her exceptional contributions to mathematical assessment at all levels, her long-standing service to the education community, and her impactful work as a mentor and advocate for underrepresented students".
