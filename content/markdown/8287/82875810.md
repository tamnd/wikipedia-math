# Brenda Lansdown

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Brenda Lansdown"}

**Brenda Muriel Lansdown** (January 25, 1904 – July 1, 1990) was an English-born American math and science educator and a professor of education at [Brooklyn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_College). Her method for teaching science became popular in China after she lectured at [Beijing Normal University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Normal_University) in the 1980s.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Lansdown was born in London, the daughter of George Arthur Lansdown and Muriel Janet Lansdown. Her father was an architect who served on the [Kensington Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Kensington).[^o4] She graduated from [Lausanne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne),[^o11] and moved to the United States in 1931.[^o5] She earned a bachelor's degree from [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College).[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Lansdown taught at New York City private schools in the 1940s, including the [Dalton School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_School) and the [City and Country School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_and_Country_School). She was known for her hands-on science lessons, which she called the investigation-colloquium method. She taught education courses at Brooklyn College,[^o8] Hunter College, and the [Harvard Graduate School of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Graduate_School_of_Education).[^o11]

Lansdown was interested in both educational technology and preparing students for a more technologically complex future. In 1958, she spoke about science education in the "Sputnik Age".[^o6] In 1963, she coordinated a project to develop best practices for effective and engaging videotaped instruction.[^o9] She traveled to China in 1977 to study science education.[^o10] In 1984, with [UNICEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF) funding, she returned to China to give workshops on her science teaching method, and spoke at Beijing Normal University.[^o11][^o12]

## Publications {#Publications}

Lansdown's academic research was published in scholarly journals including *[The School Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Education),*[^o15] *[Science Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Education_%28journal%29),*[^o16] *Childhood Education,*[^o17] *[The Reading Teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Reading_Teacher),*[^o18] *The Arithmetic Teacher,*[^o19][^o23][^o26] *[Educational Horizons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi_Lambda_Theta),*[^o28] *[Science and Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Teaching_Association),*[^o29] and *[Teachers College Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachers_College_Record).*[^o30]

- "Tutoring Experience for Students in Methods Courses" (1952)[^o15]
- "Scientific thinking can be taught to function in the everyday life of students" (1953)[^o16]

- "The Problem of Identification in Learning to Read" (1954)[^o18]
- "From Cake to Cancellation" (1957)[^o19]
- *Arithmetic for Beginners* (1960)[^o20], published in the U.K. as *My Picture Book of Numbers* (1962)[^o21][^o22]
- "Creating mathematicians: Poeta fit non nascitur" (1961)[^o23]
- "Orbiting a science program" (1962)[^o24]
- *Galumph* (1963, illustrated by [Ernest Crichlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Crichlow))[^o25]
- "Exploring rate graphs with gifted ten-year-olds" (1964)[^o26]

- "Insect Interest Transforms a Neighborhood" (1968, with Lisa Pershouse)[^o29]

- "Sharing Science in the Classroom: An Alternative Teaching Method" (1979)[^o10]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Lansdown died in 1990, at the age of 86, in Boston.[^o11]
