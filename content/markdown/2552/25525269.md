# GJMS operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="GJMS operator"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **GJMS operators** are a family of [differential operators](wikipedia://en/263886), that are defined on a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652). In an appropriate sense, they depend only on the [conformal structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_structure) of the manifold. The GJMS operators generalize the [Paneitz operator](wikipedia://en/23695030) and the [conformal Laplacian](wikipedia://en/15659323#Conformal_Laplacian). The initials GJMS are for its discoverers Graham, Jenne, Mason & Sparling (1992).

:::

Properly, the GJMS operator on a conformal manifold of dimension *n* is a [conformally invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformally_invariant) operator between the [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) of [conformal densities](wikipedia://en/3428935) of weight *k* − *n*/2 for *k* a positive integer

$$L_k : E[k-n/2] \to E[-k-n/2].$$

The operators have [leading symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol_of_a_differential_operator) given by a power of the [Laplace&ndash;Beltrami operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace&ndash;Beltrami_operator), and have lower order correction terms that ensure conformal invariance.

The original construction of the GJMS operators used the [ambient construction](wikipedia://en/5784666) of [Charles Fefferman](wikipedia://en/502831) and [Robin Graham](wikipedia://en/65653497). A conformal density defines, in a natural way, a function on the [null cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_cone) in the ambient space. The GJMS operator is defined by taking a density *ƒ* of the appropriate weight *k* − *n*/2 and extending it arbitrarily to a function *F* off the null cone so that it still retains the same homogeneity. The function Δ<sup>*k*</sup>*F*, where Δ is the ambient [Laplace&ndash;Beltrami operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace&ndash;Beltrami_operator), is then homogeneous of degree −*k* − *n*/2, and its restriction to the null cone does not depend on how the original function *ƒ* was extended to begin with, and so is independent of choices. The GJMS operator also represents the obstruction term to a formal asymptotic solution of the [Cauchy problem](wikipedia://en/1725027) for extending a weight *k* − *n*/2 function off the null cone in the ambient space to a harmonic function in the full ambient space.

The most important GJMS operators are the *critical* GJMS operators. In even dimension *n*, these are the operators *L*<sub>*n*/2</sub> that take a true function on the manifold and produce a multiple of the [volume form](wikipedia://en/1855811).
