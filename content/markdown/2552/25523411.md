# Trivial cylinder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trivial cylinder"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), **trivial cylinders** are certain [pseudoholomorphic curves](wikipedia://en/2294542) appearing in certain cylindrical [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470).[^o2]

:::

In [Floer homology](wikipedia://en/2672856) and its variants, chain complexes or differential graded algebras are generated by certain combinations of closed orbits of [vector fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_fields). In symplectic Floer homology, one considers the [Hamiltonian vector field](wikipedia://en/1818270) of a Hamiltonian function on a [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356); in [symplectic field theory](wikipedia://en/294298), [contact homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_homology), and their variants, one considers the [Reeb vector field](wikipedia://en/10761147) associated to a [contact form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_form), or more generally a [stable Hamiltonian structure](wikipedia://en/5987577).

The differentials all count some flavor of [pseudoholomorphic curves](wikipedia://en/2294542) in a manifold with a cylindrical almost-complex structure whose ends at negative infinity are the given collection of closed orbits. For instance, in symplectic Floer homology, one considers the product of the mapping torus of a symplectomorphism with the real numbers; in symplectic field theory, one considers the symplectization of a contact manifold.

The product of a given embedded closed orbit with R is always a pseudoholomorphic curve, and such a curve is called a *trivial cylinder*. Trivial cylinders do not generally contribute to the aforementioned differentials, but they may appear as components of more complicated curves which do.
