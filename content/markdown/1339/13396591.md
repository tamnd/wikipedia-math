# Möbius–Kantor graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Möbius–Kantor graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Möbius–Kantor graph** is a [symmetric](wikipedia://en/1577896) [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431) [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) with 16 vertices and 24 edges named after [August Ferdinand Möbius](wikipedia://en/37875) and [Seligmann Kantor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seligmann_Kantor). It can be defined as the [generalized Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/23716451) *G*(8,3): that is, it is formed by the vertices of an [octagon](wikipedia://en/314575), connected to the vertices of an eight-point star in which each point of the star is connected to the points three steps away from it (an [octagram](wikipedia://en/2667613)).

:::

## Möbius–Kantor configuration {#Möbius–Kantor_configuration}

*Image: Möbius–Kantor configuration.svg.* The Möbius–Kantor configuration.

(Möbius 1828) asked whether there exists a pair of [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621) with *p* sides each, having the property that the vertices of one polygon lie on the lines through the edges of the other polygon, and vice versa. If so, the vertices and edges of these polygons would form a [projective configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_configuration). For *p* = 4 there is no solution in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), but (Kantor 1882) found pairs of polygons of this type, for a generalization of the problem in which the points and edges belong to the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155). That is, in Kantor's solution, the coordinates of the polygon vertices are [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). Kantor's solution for *p* = 4, a pair of mutually-inscribed quadrilaterals in the complex projective plane, is called the **Möbius–Kantor configuration**. The Möbius–Kantor graph derives its name from being the [Levi graph](wikipedia://en/1396888) of the Möbius–Kantor configuration. It has one vertex per point and one vertex per triple, with an edge connecting two vertices if they correspond to a point and to a triple that contains that point.

The configuration may also be described algebraically in terms of the [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) $\mathbb{Z}_3\times \mathbb{Z}_3$ with nine elements. This group has four subgroups of order three (the subsets of elements of the form $(i,0)$, $(i,i)$, $(i,2i)$, and $(0,i)$), each of which can be used to partition the nine group elements into three [cosets](wikipedia://en/98759) of three elements per coset. These nine elements and twelve cosets form a configuration, the [Hesse configuration](wikipedia://en/35282956). Removing the zero element and the four cosets containing zero gives rise to the Möbius–Kantor configuration.

## As a subgraph {#As_a_subgraph}

The Möbius–Kantor graph is a [subgraph](wikipedia://en/325802#Subgraphs) of the four-dimensional [hypercube graph](wikipedia://en/6706815), formed by removing eight edges from the hypercube.[^n1] Since the hypercube is a [unit distance graph](wikipedia://en/7158665), the Möbius–Kantor graph can also be drawn in the plane with all edges unit length, although such a drawing will necessarily have some pairs of crossing edges.

The Möbius–Kantor graph also occurs many times as an induced subgraph of the [Hoffman–Singleton graph](wikipedia://en/4112667). Each of these instances is in fact an [eigenvector](wikipedia://en/534914) of the Hoffman-Singleton graph, with associated eigenvalue −3. Each vertex *not* in the induced Möbius–Kantor graph is adjacent to exactly four vertices *in* the Möbius–Kantor graph, two each in half of a [bipartition](wikipedia://en/244431) of the Möbius–Kantor graph.

## Topology {#Topology}

*Image: Möbius–Kantor torus.svg.* The Möbius–Kantor graph, embedded on the torus. Edges extending upwards from the central square should be viewed as connecting with the corresponding edge extending downwards from the square, and edges extending leftwards from the square should be viewed as connecting with the corresponding edge extending rightwards.

The Möbius–Kantor graph cannot be embedded without crossings in the plane; it has [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298594) 4, and is the smallest cubic graph with that crossing number.[^o9] Additionally, it provides an example of a graph all of whose subgraphs' crossing numbers differ from it by two or more.[^n2] However, it is a [toroidal graph](wikipedia://en/1633057): it has an embedding in the [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) in which all faces are [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733).[^n3] A [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) of [Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) and [Lajos Szilassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lajos_Szilassi) implies that, in contrast to the analogous case of the [Heawood graph](wikipedia://en/1396870) and [Szilassi polyhedron](wikipedia://en/7652409), this topological embedding of the Möbius–Kantor graph cannot be realized as a non-self-crossing [toroidal polyhedron](wikipedia://en/24804975).[^n4] The [dual graph](wikipedia://en/2536864) of this embedding is the [hyperoctahedral graph](wikipedia://en/360581) *K*<sub>2,2,2,2</sub>.

There is an even more symmetric embedding of Möbius–Kantor graph in the [double torus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_torus) which is a [regular map](wikipedia://en/21533914), with six [octagonal](wikipedia://en/314575) faces, in which all 96 symmetries of the graph can be realized as symmetries of the embedding[^o13] Its 96-element [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) has a [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) that can itself be embedded on the double torus, and was shown by (Tucker 1984) to be the unique group with [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) two. The Cayley graph on 96 vertices is a flag graph of the genus 2 regular map having Möbius–Kantor graph as a skeleton. This means it can be obtained from the regular map as a skeleton of the dual of its barycentric subdivision. A sculpture by [DeWitt Godfrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeWitt_Godfrey) and [Duane Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duane_Martinez) showing the double torus embedding of the symmetries of the Möbius–Kantor graph was unveiled at the Technical Museum of [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia) as part of the 6th Slovenian International Conference on Graph Theory in 2007. In 2013 a rotating version of the sculpture was unveiled at [Colgate University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colgate_University).

The Möbius–Kantor graph admits an embedding into a [triple torus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_torus) (genus 3 torus) that is a [regular map](wikipedia://en/21533914) having four 12-gonal faces, and is the [Petrie dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrie_dual) of the double torus embedding described above.[^n3]

(Lijnen Ceulemans 2004), motivated by an investigation of potential chemical structures of carbon compounds, studied the family of all embeddings of the Möbius–Kantor graph onto 2-[manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470); they showed that there are 759 inequivalent embeddings. The genus 1 embedding, which is not a regular map, is seen in the diagram above.

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

The automorphism group of the Möbius–Kantor graph is a group of order 96.[^n3] It acts transitively on the vertices, on the edges and on the arcs of the graph. Therefore, the Möbius–Kantor graph is a [symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/1577896). It has automorphisms that take any vertex to any other vertex and any edge to any other edge. According to the *Foster census*, the Möbius–Kantor graph is the unique cubic symmetric graph with 16 vertices, and the smallest cubic symmetric graph which is not also [distance-transitive](wikipedia://en/7768222).[^n5] The Möbius–Kantor graph is also a [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277).

The generalized Petersen graph *G*(*n,k*) is vertex-transitive if and only if *n* = 10 and *k* =2 or if *k*<sup>2</sup> ≡ ±1 (mod *n*) and is edge-transitive only in the following seven cases: (*n,k*) = (4,1), (5,2), (8,3), (10,2), (10,3), (12,5), or (24,5).[^n6] So the Möbius–Kantor graph is one of only seven symmetric Generalized Petersen graphs. Its symmetric double torus embedding is correspondingly one of only seven regular cubic maps in which the total number of vertices is twice the number of vertices per face.[^n7] Among the seven symmetric generalized Petersen graphs are the [cubical graph](wikipedia://en/6285) $G(4,1)$, the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) $G(5,2)$, the [dodecahedral graph](wikipedia://en/8407) $G(10,2)$, the [Desargues graph](wikipedia://en/1578059) $G(10,3)$ and the [Nauru graph](wikipedia://en/24052345) $G(12,5)$.

The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the Möbius–Kantor graph is equal to[^o23]

$$(x-3)(x-1)^3(x+1)^3(x+3)(x^2-3)^4.\$$

The Möbius–Kantor graph is a double cover of the graph of the cube.[^n1]
