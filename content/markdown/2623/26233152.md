# Wilhelmus Luxemburg

*Image: WAJ Luxemburg Caltech Late 1980's.jpg.* Luxemburg in the late 1980s

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wilhelmus Luxemburg"}

**Wilhelmus Anthonius Josephus Luxemburg** (11 April 1929 – 2 October 2018)[^o2][^o3] was a Dutch American mathematician who was a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology).

:::

He received his B.A. from the [University of Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leiden) in 1950; his M.A., in 1953; his Ph.D., from the [Delft Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_Institute_of_Technology), in 1955. He was an assistant professor at Caltech from 1958-60; an associate professor from 1960–62; a professor from 1962–2000; and a professor Emeritus, from 2000. He was the Executive Officer for Mathematics during 1970–85. In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o4] Luxemburg became a corresponding member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1974.[^o5]

Luxemburg contributed to the development of [non-standard analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-standard_analysis) by popularizing the construction of [hyperreal numbers](wikipedia://en/51429) in the 1960s. Though [Edwin Hewitt](wikipedia://en/7482590) had shown the construction in 1948, the formalization of non-standard analysis is generally associated with [Abraham Robinson](wikipedia://en/46024).[^o6]

Other notable work he did was in the theory of [Riesz spaces](wikipedia://en/8099018) ([partially ordered](wikipedia://en/23572) [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) where the [order structure](wikipedia://en/361924) is a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304)).[^o7]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- 1955: *Banach function spaces*. Thesis, Technische Hogeschool te Delft, 1955.
- 1969: "A general theory of monads", in *Applications of Model Theory to Algebra, Analysis, and Probability* (Internat. Sympos., Pasadena, Calif., 1967) pp. 18–86 [Holt, Rinehart and Winston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holt,_Rinehart_and_Winston)
- 1971: (with [Zaanen, A. C.](wikipedia://en/35274187)) *Riesz Spaces*. Vol. I. North-Holland Mathematical Library. North-Holland Publishing Co., Amsterdam-London; American Elsevier Publishing Co., New York.
- 1976: (with [Stroyan, K. D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Stroyan)) *Introduction to the Theory of Infinitesimals*. Pure and Applied Mathematics, No. 72. [Academic Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Press)
- 1978: (with Schep, A. R.) "A Radon-Nikodym type theorem for positive operators and a dual", Nederl. Akad. Wetensch. Indag. Math. 40, no. 3, 357–375.
- 1979: *Some Aspects of the Theory of Riesz Spaces*, University of Arkansas Lecture Notes in Mathematics, 4. [University of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas), Fayetteville, Ark.
