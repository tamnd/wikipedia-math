# Direct sum of matrices

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Direct sum of matrices"}

The **direct sum** of two [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) is the [diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/174080) where the top-left and bottom-right corners of the matrix fill the two given matrices, and where the top-right and bottom-left corners are all [zeros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeros).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The direct sum of any pair of matrices **A** of size *m* × *n* and **B** of size *p* × *q* is a matrix of size (*m* + *p*) × (*n* + *q*) defined as:[^n1]

**$\mathbf{A} \oplus \mathbf{B} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A} & \boldsymbol{0} \\ \boldsymbol{0} & \mathbf{B} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} a_{11} & \cdots & a_{1n} & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ a_{m 1} & \cdots & a_{mn} & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & \cdots & 0 & b_{11} & \cdots & b_{1q} \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & \cdots & 0 & b_{p1} & \cdots & b_{pq} \end{bmatrix}$**

For instance,

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \oplus \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

The direct sum of matrices is a special type of [block matrix](wikipedia://en/457464). In particular, the direct sum of square matrices is a [block diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/457464#Block_diagonal_matrices).

The [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of the union of disjoint [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806) (or [multigraphs](wikipedia://en/1774970)) is the direct sum of their adjacency matrices. Any element in the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/58899) of two [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) of matrices can be represented as a direct sum of two matrices.

In general, the direct sum of *n* matrices is:[^n1]

$$\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{A}_{i} = \operatorname{diag}( \mathbf{A}_1, \mathbf{A}_2, \mathbf{A}_3, \ldots, \mathbf{A}_n) = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{A}_1 & \boldsymbol{0} & \cdots & \boldsymbol{0} \\ \boldsymbol{0} & \mathbf{A}_2 & \cdots & \boldsymbol{0} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \boldsymbol{0} & \boldsymbol{0} & \cdots & \mathbf{A}_n \\ \end{bmatrix}\,\!$$

where the zeros are actually blocks of zeros (i.e., zero matrices).
