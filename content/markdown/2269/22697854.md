# Hadamard's method of descent

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hadamard's method of descent"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **method of descent** is the term coined by the French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675) as a method for solving a [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) in several [real](wikipedia://en/1268958) or [complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variable), by regarding it as the specialisation of an equation in more variables, constant in the extra parameters. This method has been used to solve the [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691), the [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415) and other versions of the [Cauchy initial value problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_initial_value_problem).

:::

As (Hadamard 1923) wrote:

We thus have a first example of what I shall call a 'method of descent'. Creating a phrase for an idea which is merely childish and has been used since the first steps of the theory is, I must confess, rather ambitious; but we shall come across it rather frequently, so that it will be convenient to have a word to denote it. It consists in noticing that he who can do more can do less: if we can integrate equations with *m* variables, we can do the same for equations with (*m* – 1) variables.
