# Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality"}

In mathematics, the **Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality** is the inequality

:::

$$c_1^2 \le 3 c_2$$

between [Chern numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern_number) of [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [complex surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311) of [general type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfaces_of_general_type). Its major interest is the way it restricts the possible topological types of the underlying real 4-manifold. It was proved independently by (txt) and (txt), after (txt) and (txt) proved weaker versions with the constant 3 replaced by 8 and 4.

[Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672) and [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) showed that the inequality is best possible by finding infinitely many cases where equality holds. The inequality is false in positive characteristic: (txt) and (txt) gave examples of surfaces in characteristic *p*, such as [generalized Raynaud surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Raynaud_surface), for which it fails.

::: {#o9 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formulation of the inequality"}

## Formulation of the inequality {#Formulation_of_the_inequality}

The conventional formulation of the Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality is as follows. Let *X* be a compact complex surface of [general type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_type), and let *c*<sub>1</sub> = *c*<sub>1</sub>(*X*) and *c*<sub>2</sub> = *c*<sub>2</sub>(*X*) be the first and second [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) of the complex tangent bundle of the surface. Then

$$c_1^2 \le 3 c_2.$$

Moreover if equality holds then *X* is a quotient of a ball. The latter statement is a consequence of Yau's differential geometric approach which is based on his resolution of the [Calabi conjecture](wikipedia://en/5263012).

Since $c_2(X) = e(X)$ is the topological [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) and by the [Thom–Hirzebruch signature theorem](wikipedia://en/7192444) $c_1^2(X) = 2 e(X) + 3\sigma(X)$ where $\sigma(X)$ is the signature of the [intersection form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_form_%284-manifold%29) on the second cohomology, the Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality can also be written as a restriction on the topological type of the surface of general type:

$$\sigma(X) \le \frac{1}{3} e(X),$$

moreover if $\sigma(X) = (1/3)e(X)$ then the universal covering is a ball.

Together with the [Noether inequality](wikipedia://en/22711771) the Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality sets boundaries in the search for complex surfaces. Mapping out the topological types that are realized as complex surfaces is called [geography of surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_surfaces). see [surfaces of general type](wikipedia://en/2368356).

:::

## Surfaces with *c*<sub>1</sub><sup>2</sup> = 3*c*<sub>2</sub> {#Surfaces_with_c12_=_3c2}

If *X* is a surface of general type with $c_1^2 = 3 c_2$, so that equality holds in the Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality, then (Yau 1977) proved that *X* is isomorphic to a quotient of the unit ball in ${\mathbb C}^2$ by an infinite discrete group. Examples of surfaces satisfying this equality are hard to find. (Borel 1963) showed that there are infinitely many values of *c*2 = 3*c*<sub>2</sub> for which a surface exists. (txt) found a [fake projective plane](wikipedia://en/22723502) with *c*2 = 3*c*<sub>2</sub> = 9, which is the minimum possible value because *c*2 + *c*<sub>2</sub> is always divisible by 12, and (Prasad Yeung 2007), (Prasad Yeung 2010), (txt, 11–13) showed that there are exactly 50 fake projective planes.

(Barthel Hirzebruch Höfer 1987) gave a method for finding examples, which in particular produced a surface *X* with *c*2 = 3*c*<sub>2</sub> = 3<sup>2</sup>5<sup>4</sup>. (Ishida 1988) found a quotient of this surface with *c*2 = 3*c*<sub>2</sub> = 45, and taking unbranched coverings of this quotient gives examples with *c*2 = 3*c*<sub>2</sub> = 45*k* for any positive integer *k*. (txt, 11–13) found examples with *c*2 = 3*c*<sub>2</sub> = 9*n* for every positive integer *n*.
