# Cousin problems

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cousin problems"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Cousin problems** are two questions in [several complex variables](wikipedia://en/360243), concerning the existence of [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857) that are specified in terms of local data. They were introduced in special cases by Pierre Cousin (mathematician) in 1895. They are now posed, and solved, for any [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) *M*, in terms of conditions on *M*.

:::

For both problems, an [open cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover) of *M* by sets *U<sub>i</sub>* is given, along with a meromorphic function *f<sub>i</sub>* on each *U<sub>i</sub>*.

## First Cousin problem {#First_Cousin_problem}

The **first Cousin problem** or **additive Cousin problem** assumes that each difference $f_i-f_j$ is a [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110), where it is defined. It asks for a meromorphic function *f* on *M* such that $f-f_i$ is *holomorphic* on *U<sub>i</sub>*; in other words, that *f* shares the [singular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity) behaviour of the given local function. The given condition on the $f_i-f_j$ is evidently *necessary* for this; so the problem amounts to asking if it is sufficient. The case of one variable is the [Mittag-Leffler theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mittag-Leffler_theorem) on prescribing poles, when *M* is an open subset of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628). [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) theory shows that some restriction on *M* will be required. The problem can always be solved on a [Stein manifold](wikipedia://en/1053909).

The first Cousin problem may be understood in terms of [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) as follows. Let **K** be the [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of meromorphic functions and **O** the sheaf of holomorphic functions on *M*. A global section $f$ of **K** passes to a global section $\phi(f)$ of the quotient sheaf **K**/**O**. The converse question is the first Cousin problem: given a global section of **K**/**O**, is there a global section of **K** from which it arises? The problem is thus to characterize the image of the map

$$H^0(M,\mathbf{K}) \, \xrightarrow{\phi} \, H^0(M,\mathbf{K}/\mathbf{O}).$$

By the [long exact cohomology sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_exact_sequence_in_homology),

$$H^0(M,\mathbf{K}) \,\xrightarrow{\phi}\, H^0(M,\mathbf{K}/\mathbf{O})\to H^1(M,\mathbf{O})$$

is exact, and so the first Cousin problem is always solvable provided that the first cohomology group *H*<sup>1</sup>(*M*,**O**) vanishes. In particular, by [Cartan's theorem B](wikipedia://en/621677), the Cousin problem is always solvable if *M* is a Stein manifold.

## Second Cousin problem {#Second_Cousin_problem}

The **second Cousin problem** or **multiplicative Cousin problem** assumes that each ratio $f_i/f_j$ is a non-vanishing holomorphic function, where it is defined. It asks for a meromorphic function *f* on *M* such that $f/f_i$ is holomorphic and non-vanishing. The second Cousin problem is a multi-dimensional generalization of the [Weierstrass theorem](wikipedia://en/1585155) on the existence of a holomorphic function of one variable with prescribed zeros.

The attack on this problem by means of taking [logarithms](wikipedia://en/17860), to reduce it to the additive problem, meets an obstruction in the form of the first [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) (see also [exponential sheaf sequence](wikipedia://en/1881339)). In terms of sheaf theory, let $\mathbf{O}^*$ be the sheaf of holomorphic functions that vanish nowhere, and $\mathbf{K}^*$ the sheaf of meromorphic functions that are not identically zero. These are both then sheaves of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974), and the quotient sheaf $\mathbf{K}^*/\mathbf{O}^*$ is well-defined. The multiplicative Cousin problem then seeks to identify the image of quotient map $\phi$

$$H^0(M,\mathbf{K}^*)\xrightarrow{\phi} H^0(M,\mathbf{K}^*/\mathbf{O}^*).$$

The long exact sheaf cohomology sequence associated to the quotient is

$$H^0(M,\mathbf{K}^*)\xrightarrow{\phi} H^0(M,\mathbf{K}^*/\mathbf{O}^*)\to H^1(M,\mathbf{O}^*)$$

so the second Cousin problem is solvable in all cases provided that $H^1(M,\mathbf{O}^*)=0.$ The quotient sheaf $\mathbf{K}^*/\mathbf{O}^*$ is the sheaf of germs of [Cartier divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartier_divisor) on *M*. The question of whether every global section is generated by a meromorphic function is thus equivalent to determining whether every [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) on *M* is [trivial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_bundle).

The cohomology group $H^1(M,\mathbf{O}^*),$ for the multiplicative structure on $\mathbf{O}^*$ can be compared with the cohomology group $H^1(M,\mathbf{O})$ with its additive structure by taking a logarithm. That is, there is an [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) of sheaves

$$0\to 2\pi i\mathbb{Z}\to \mathbf{O} \xrightarrow{\exp} \mathbf{O}^* \to 0$$

where the leftmost sheaf is the [locally constant sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_constant_sheaf) with fiber $2\pi i\mathbb{Z}$. The obstruction to defining a logarithm at the level of *H*<sup>1</sup> is in $H^2(M,\mathbb{Z})$, from the long exact cohomology sequence

$$H^1(M,\mathbf{O})\to H^1(M,\mathbf{O}^*)\to 2\pi i H^2(M,\mathbb{Z}) \to H^2(M, \mathbf{O}).$$

When *M* is a Stein manifold, the middle arrow is an isomorphism because $H^q(M,\mathbf{O}) = 0$ for $q > 0$ so that a necessary and sufficient condition in that case for the second Cousin problem to be always solvable is that $H^2(M,\mathbb{Z})=0.$
