# Cointerpretability

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cointerpretability"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **cointerpretability** is a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) on [formal theories](wikipedia://en/3512103): a formal theory *T* is **cointerpretable** in another such theory *S* when the language of *S* can be translated into the language of *T* in such a way that *S* proves every [formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_%28logic%29) whose translation is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of *T*. The "translation" here is required to preserve the logical structure of formulas.

:::

This concept, in a sense dual to [interpretability](wikipedia://en/621176), was introduced by (Japaridze 1993), who also proved that, for theories of [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) and any stronger theories with [computable](wikipedia://en/332264) [axiomatizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatization), cointerpretability is equivalent to $\Sigma_1$-conservativity.
