# Ba space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ba space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **ba space** $ba(\Sigma)$ of an [algebra of sets](wikipedia://en/1018676) $\Sigma$ is the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) consisting of all [bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_measure) and finitely additive [signed measures](wikipedia://en/2985811) on $\Sigma$. The norm is defined as the [variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_variation), that is $\|\nu\|=|\nu|(X).$[^n1]

:::

If Σ is a [sigma-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra), then the space $ca(\Sigma)$ is defined as the subset of $ba(\Sigma)$ consisting of [countably additive measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-additive).[^n2] The notation *ba* is a [mnemonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mnemonic) for *bounded additive* and *ca* is short for *countably additive*.

If *X* is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), and Σ is the sigma-algebra of [Borel sets](wikipedia://en/43327) in *X*, then $rca(X)$ is the subspace of $ca(\Sigma)$ consisting of all [regular](wikipedia://en/3390453) [Borel measures](wikipedia://en/4322) on *X*.[^n3]

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

All three spaces are complete (they are [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989)) with respect to the same norm defined by the total variation, and thus $ca(\Sigma)$ is a closed subset of $ba(\Sigma)$, and $rca(X)$ is a closed set of $ca(\Sigma)$ for Σ the algebra of Borel sets on *X*. The space of [simple functions](wikipedia://en/1890594) on $\Sigma$ is [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) in $ba(\Sigma)$.

The ba space of the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474), *ba*(2<sup>**N**</sup>), is often denoted as simply $ba$ and is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) of the [ℓ<sup>∞</sup> space](wikipedia://en/45194).

### Dual of B(Σ) {#Dual_of_B(Σ)}

Let B(Σ) be the space of bounded Σ-measurable functions, equipped with the [uniform norm](wikipedia://en/349755). Then *ba*(Σ) = B(Σ)\* is the [continuous dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space) of B(Σ). This is due to Hildebrandt and Fichtenholtz & Kantorovich. This is a kind of [Riesz representation theorem](wikipedia://en/26452) which allows for a measure to be represented as a linear functional on measurable functions. In particular, this isomorphism allows one to *define* the [integral](wikipedia://en/15532) with respect to a finitely additive measure (note that the usual Lebesgue integral requires *countable* additivity). This is due to Dunford & Schwartz,[^n4] and is often used to define the integral with respect to [vector measures](wikipedia://en/9399072), and especially vector-valued [Radon measures](wikipedia://en/521933).

The topological duality *ba*(Σ) = B(Σ)\* is easy to see. There is an obvious *algebraic* duality between the vector space of *all* finitely additive measures σ on Σ and the vector space of [simple functions](wikipedia://en/1890594) ($\mu(A)=\zeta\left(1_A\right)$). It is easy to check that the linear form induced by σ is continuous in the sup-norm if σ is bounded, and the result follows since a linear form on the dense subspace of simple functions extends to an element of B(Σ)\* if it is continuous in the sup-norm.

### Dual of *L*<sup>∞</sup>(*μ*) {#Dual_of_L∞(μ)}

If Σ is a [sigma-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra) and *μ* is a [sigma-additive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-additive) positive measure on Σ then the [Lp space](wikipedia://en/45194) *L*<sup>∞</sup>(*μ*) endowed with the [essential supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_supremum) norm is by definition the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) of B(Σ) by the closed subspace of bounded *μ*-null functions:

$$N_\mu:=\{f\in B(\Sigma) : f = 0 \ \mu\text{-almost everywhere} \}.$$

The dual Banach space *L*<sup>∞</sup>(*μ*)\* is thus isomorphic to

$$N_\mu^\perp=\{\sigma\in ba(\Sigma) : \mu(A)=0\Rightarrow \sigma(A)= 0 \text{ for any }A\in\Sigma\},$$

i.e. the space of [finitely additive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_additive) signed measures on *Σ* that are [absolutely continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_continuous) with respect to *μ* (*μ*-a.c. for short).

When the measure space is furthermore [sigma-finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-finite) then *L*<sup>∞</sup>(*μ*) is in turn dual to *L*<sup>1</sup>(*μ*), which by the [Radon–Nikodym theorem](wikipedia://en/338746) is identified with the set of all [countably additive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_additive) *μ*-a.c. measures. In other words, the inclusion in the bidual

$$L^1(\mu)\subset L^1(\mu)^{**}=L^{\infty}(\mu)^*$$

is isomorphic to the inclusion of the space of countably additive *μ*-a.c. bounded measures inside the space of all finitely additive *μ*-a.c. bounded measures.
