# Tsirelson space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tsirelson space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **Tsirelson space** is the first example of a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) in which neither an [ℓ<sup>*p*</sup> space](wikipedia://en/45194) nor a [*c*<sub>0</sub> space](wikipedia://en/1701075#c_and_c0) can be embedded. The Tsirelson space is [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091).

:::

It was introduced by [B. S. Tsirelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._S._Tsirelson) in 1974. The same year, Figiel and Johnson published a related article ((Figiel Johnson 1974)) where they used the notation *T* for the *dual* of Tsirelson's example. Today, the letter *T* is the standard notation[^o3] for the dual of the original example, while the original Tsirelson example is denoted by *T*\*. In *T*\* or in *T*, no subspace is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic), as Banach space, to an *ℓ*<sup>*p*</sup> space, 1 ≤ *p* \< ∞, or to *c*<sub>0</sub>.

All classical [Banach](wikipedia://en/3989) spaces known to (Banach 1932), spaces of [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122), of [differentiable functions](wikipedia://en/330206) or of [integrable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_function), and all the Banach spaces used in functional analysis for the next forty years, contain some *ℓ*<sup>*p*</sup> or *c*<sub>0</sub>. Also, new attempts in the early '70s[^o5] to promote a geometric theory of Banach spaces led to ask [^o6] whether or not *every* infinite-dimensional Banach space has a subspace isomorphic to some *ℓ*<sup>*p*</sup> or to *c*<sub>0</sub>. Moreover, it was shown by Baudier, Lancien, and Schlumprecht that *ℓ*<sup>*p*</sup> and *c*<sub>0</sub> do not even coarsely embed into T\*.

The radically new Tsirelson construction is at the root of several further developments in Banach space theory: the [arbitrarily distortable](wikipedia://en/30307851) space of Thomas Schlumprecht ((Schlumprecht 1991)), on which depend [Gowers'](wikipedia://en/365715) solution to Banach's hyperplane problem[^o8] and the Odell–Schlumprecht solution to the [distortion problem](wikipedia://en/30307851). Also, several results of Argyros et al.[^o9] are based on [ordinal](wikipedia://en/26547932) refinements of the Tsirelson construction, culminating with the solution by Argyros–Haydon of the scalar plus compact problem.[^o10]

## Tsirelson's construction {#Tsirelson's_construction}

On the vector space ℓ<sup>∞</sup> of bounded scalar sequences *x* = {*x*<sub>*j*</sub>} <sub>*j*∈**N**</sub>, let *P*<sub>*n*</sub> denote the [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) which sets to zero all coordinates *x*<sub>*j*</sub> of *x* for which *j* ≤ *n*.

A finite sequence $\{x_n\}_{n=1}^N$ of vectors in ℓ<sup>∞</sup> is called *block-disjoint* if there are natural numbers $\textstyle \{a_n, b_n\}_{n=1}^N$ so that $a_1 \leq b_1 < a_2 \leq b_2 < \cdots \leq b_N$, and so that $(x_n)_i=0$ when $i<a_n$ or $i>b_n$, for each *n* from 1 to *N*.

The [unit ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ball) *B*<sub>∞</sub> of ℓ<sup>∞</sup> is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) and [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrization_theorem) for the topology of [pointwise convergence](wikipedia://en/340198) (the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910)). The crucial step in the Tsirelson construction is to let *K* be the *smallest* pointwise closed subset of *B*<sub>∞</sub> satisfying the following two properties:[^o12]

**a.** For every integer *j* in **N**, the [unit vector](wikipedia://en/167053) *e*<sub>*j*</sub> and all multiples $\lambda e_j$, for |λ| ≤ 1, belong to *K*.

**b.** For any integer *N* ≥ 1, if $\textstyle (x_1,\ldots,x_N)$ is a block-disjoint sequence in *K*, then $\textstyle{{1\over2}P_N(x_1 + \cdots + x_N)}$ belongs to *K*.

This set *K* satisfies the following stability property:

**c.** Together with every element *x* of *K*, the set *K* contains all vectors *y* in ℓ<sup>∞</sup> such that |*y*| ≤ |*x*| (for the pointwise comparison).

It is then shown that *K* is actually a subset of *c*<sub>0</sub>, the Banach subspace of ℓ<sup>∞</sup> consisting of scalar sequences tending to zero at infinity. This is done by proving that

**d:** for every element *x* in *K*, there exists an integer *n* such that 2 *P*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*) belongs to *K*,

and iterating this fact. Since *K* is pointwise compact and contained in *c*<sub>0</sub>, it is [weakly compact](wikipedia://en/33662) in *c*<sub>0</sub>. Let *V* be the closed [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of *K* in *c*<sub>0</sub>. It is also a weakly compact set in *c*<sub>0</sub>. It is shown that *V* satisfies **b**, **c** and **d**.

The Tsirelson space *T*\* is the Banach space whose [unit ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ball) is *V*. The unit vector basis is an [unconditional basis](wikipedia://en/3460662) for *T*\* and *T*\* is reflexive. Therefore, *T*\* does not contain an isomorphic copy of *c*<sub>0</sub>. The other *ℓ*<sup>*p*</sup> spaces, 1 ≤ *p* \< ∞, are ruled out by condition **b**.

## Properties {#Properties}

The Tsirelson space $T*$ is [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091) ((Tsirel'son 1974)) and [finitely universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_universal_space), which means that for some constant $C$ ≥ 1, the space $T*$ contains $C$-isomorphic copies of every finite-dimensional normed space, namely, for every finite-dimensional normed space $X$, there exists a subspace $Y$ of the Tsirelson space with [multiplicative Banach–Mazur distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach-Mazur_compactum) to $X$ less than $C$. Actually, every finitely universal Banach space contains *almost-isometric* copies of every finite-dimensional normed space,[^o16] meaning that $C$ can be replaced by 1 + ε for every ε > 0. Also, every infinite-dimensional subspace of $T*$ is finitely universal. On the other hand, every infinite-dimensional subspace in the dual $T$ of $T*$ contains almost isometric copies of $\scriptstyle{\ell^1_n}$, the $n$-dimensional ℓ<sup>1</sup>-space, for all $n$.

The Tsirelson space $T$ is [distortable](wikipedia://en/30307851), but it is not known whether it is [arbitrarily distortable](wikipedia://en/30307851).

The space $T*$ is a *minimal* Banach space.[^o17] This means that every infinite-dimensional Banach subspace of $T*$ contains a further subspace isomorphic to $T*$. Prior to the construction of $T*$, the only known examples of minimal spaces were *ℓ*<sup>*p*</sup> and $c$<sub>0</sub>. The dual space $T$ is not minimal.[^o18]

The space $T*$ is [polynomially reflexive](wikipedia://en/629068).

:::

## Derived spaces {#Derived_spaces}

The **symmetric Tsirelson space** *S*(*T*) is polynomially reflexive and it has the [approximation property](wikipedia://en/628962). As with *T*, it is reflexive and no *ℓ*<sup>*p*</sup> space can be embedded into it.

Since it is symmetric, it can be defined even on an [uncountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable) supporting set, giving an example of non-[separable](wikipedia://en/27855) polynomially reflexive [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989).
