# Pierre Fatou

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Pierre Fatou"}

**Pierre Joseph Louis Fatou** (28 February 1878 – 9 August 1929[^o3]) was a French mathematician and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer). He is known for major contributions to several branches of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). The [Fatou lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatou_lemma) and the [Fatou set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatou_set) are named after him.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: Pierre Fatou.jpg.* Pierre Fatou

Pierre Fatou's parents were Prosper Ernest Fatou (1832–1891) and Louise Eulalie Courbet (1844–1911), both of whom were in the military.[^o3] Pierre's family would have liked for him to enter the military as well, but his health was not sufficiently good for him to pursue a military career.[^o3]

Fatou entered the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in Paris in 1898 to study mathematics and graduated in 1901 when he was appointed an intern (*stagiaire*) in the [Paris Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Observatory). Fatou was promoted to assistant astronomer in 1904 and to astronomer (*astronome titulaire*) in 1928. He worked in this observatory until his death.

Fatou was awarded the [Becquerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Becquerel) prize in 1918; he was a knight of the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour) (1923).[^n1] He was the president of the [French mathematical society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_Mathématique_de_France) in 1927.[^o6]

He was in friendly relations with several contemporary French mathematicians, especially, [Maurice René Fréchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_René_Fréchet) and [Paul Montel](wikipedia://en/1796347).[^n2]

In the summer of 1929 Fatou went on holiday to Pornichet, a seaside town to the west of Nantes. He was staying in Le Brise-Lames Villa near the port and it was there at 8 p.m. on Friday 9 August that he died in his room.[^o3] No cause of death was given on the death certificate but Audin argues that he died as a result of a stomach ulcer that burst. Fatou's funeral was held on 14 August in the church of Saint-Louis, and he was buried in the Carnel Cemetery in Lorient.[^o3]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Fatou's work had a very large influence on the development of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) in the 20th century.

Fatou's PhD thesis *Séries trigonométriques et séries de Taylor* (Fatou 1906) was the first application of the [Lebesgue integral](wikipedia://en/26064288) to concrete problems of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), mainly to the study of analytic and harmonic functions in the unit disc. In this work, Fatou studied for the first time the [Poisson integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_integral) of an arbitrary [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) on the unit circle. This work of Fatou is influenced by [Henri Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514) who invented his integral in 1901.

The [Fatou theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatou_theorem), which says that a bounded [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) in the unit disc has radial limits [almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688) on the unit circle was published in 1906 (Fatou 1906). This theorem was at the origin of a large body of research in 20th-century mathematics under the name of *bounded analytic functions*.[^o11] See also the Wikipedia article on functions of [bounded type](wikipedia://en/30340342).

A number of fundamental results on the [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) of a [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) belong to Fatou.[^o12]

*Image: Julia set of the Fatou function.png.* Julia set of *z*+1+*e*<sup>−*z*</sup> investigated by Fatou in 1926

*Image: Julia set of a map in the sine family.png.* Julia set of a sine function studied by Fatou in 1926

In 1917–1920 Fatou created the area of mathematics which is called [holomorphic dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_dynamics) (). It deals with a global study of iteration of analytic functions. He was the first to introduce and study the set which is called now the [Julia set](wikipedia://en/56434). (The complement of this set is sometimes called the [Fatou set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatou_set)). Some of the basic results of holomorphic dynamics were also independently obtained by [Gaston Julia](wikipedia://en/249107) and Samuel Lattes in 1918.[^o14] Holomorphic dynamics has experienced a strong revival since 1982 because of the new discoveries of [Dennis Sullivan](wikipedia://en/7349608), [Adrien Douady](wikipedia://en/1604604), [John Hubbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hamal_Hubbard) and others. In 1926, Fatou pioneered the study of dynamics of [transcendental](wikipedia://en/297021) [entire functions](wikipedia://en/10372) (Fatou 1926), a subject which is [intensively developing](wikipedia://en/29115268) at this time.

As a byproduct of his studies in holomorphic dynamics, Fatou discovered what are now called [Fatou–Bieberbach domains](wikipedia://en/4684859) (Fatou 1922). These are proper subregions of the complex space of dimension *n*, which are biholomorphically equivalent to the whole space. (Such regions cannot exist for *n=1*.)

Fatou did important work in [celestial mechanics](wikipedia://en/102182). He was the first to prove rigorously[^o17] a theorem (conjectured by [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Friedrich_Gauss)) on the averaging of a [perturbation](wikipedia://en/102140) produced by a periodic force of short period (Fatou 1928). This work was continued by [Leonid Mandelstam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Mandelstam) and [Nikolay Bogolyubov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Bogolyubov) and his students and developed into a large area of modern applied mathematics. Fatou's other research in celestial mechanics includes a study of the movement of a planet in a resisting medium.(Fatou 1923b)
