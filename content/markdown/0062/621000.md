# Eugenio Calabi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eugenio Calabi"}

**Eugenio Calabi** (May 11, 1923 – September 25, 2023) was an Italian-born American mathematician and the [Thomas A. Scott Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Scott_Professorship_of_Mathematics) at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), specializing in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and their applications.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Calabi was born in [Milan, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan) on May 11, 1923, into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Jews) family.[^o4] His sister was the journalist [Tullia Zevi Calabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullia_Zevi). In 1938, the family left Italy because of the [racial laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_racial_laws), and in 1939 arrived in the United States.[^o5][^o6]

In the fall of 1939, aged only 16, Calabi enrolled at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), studying [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering). His studies were interrupted when he was [drafted in the US military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription_in_the_United_States) in 1943 and served during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). Upon his discharge in 1946, Calabi was able to finish his bachelor's degree under the [G.I. Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.I._Bill), and was a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition).[^o6][^o7] He received a master's degree in mathematics from the [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign) in 1947 and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1950. His doctoral dissertation, titled "Isometric complex analytic imbedding of [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538)", was done under the supervision of [Salomon Bochner](wikipedia://en/987847).

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

From 1951 to 1955 he was an assistant professor at [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University), and he moved to the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) in 1955, where he became a full professor in 1960. In 1964, Calabi joined the mathematics faculty at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania). Following the retirement of [Hans Rademacher](wikipedia://en/1864916), he was appointed to the [Thomas A. Scott Professorship of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_A._Scott_Professorship_of_Mathematics) at the University of Pennsylvania in 1968. In 1994, Calabi assumed [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) status, and in 2014 the university awarded him an [honorary doctorate of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree).[^o10][^o11][^o12]

In 1982, Calabi was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o13] He won the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) from the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 1991, where his "fundamental work on global differential geometry, especially complex differential geometry" was cited as having "profoundly changed the landscape of the field".[^o12] In 2012, he became a fellow of the American Mathematical Society.[^o14] In 2021, he was awarded Commander of the [Order of Merit of the Italian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit_of_the_Italian_Republic).[^o15][^o11]

Calabi married Giuliana Segre in 1952, with whom he had a son and a daughter. He died on September 25, 2023, aged 100.[^o11][^o16]

## Research {#Research}

Calabi made a number of contributions to the field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). Other contributions, not discussed here, include the construction of a holomorphic version of the [long line](wikipedia://en/338403) with [Maxwell Rosenlicht](wikipedia://en/26817022), a study of the [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) of [space forms](wikipedia://en/1087009), a characterization of when a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) can be found so that a given [differential form](wikipedia://en/221519) is harmonic, and various works on [affine geometry](wikipedia://en/243890). In the comments on his collected works in 2021, Calabi cited his article "Improper affine hyperspheres of convex type and a generalization of a theorem by K. Jörgens" as that which he was "most proud of".

### Kähler geometry {#Kähler_geometry}

At the 1954 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), Calabi announced a theorem on how the [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582) of a [Kähler metric](wikipedia://en/390538) could be prescribed.C54 He later found that his proof, via the [method of continuity](wikipedia://en/23547438), was flawed, and the result became known as the [Calabi conjecture](wikipedia://en/5263012). In 1957, Calabi published a paper in which the conjecture was stated as a proposition, but with an openly incomplete proof.C57 He gave a complete proof that any solution of the problem must be uniquely defined, but was only able to reduce the problem of existence to the problem of establishing *[a priori estimates](wikipedia://en/30669047)* for certain [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). In the 1970s, [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137) began working on the Calabi conjecture, initially attempting to disprove it. After several years of work, he found a proof of the conjecture, and was able to establish several striking [algebro-geometric](wikipedia://en/1997) consequences of its validity. As a particular case of the conjecture, Kähler metrics with zero Ricci curvature are established on a number of [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742); these are now known as *Calabi–Yau metrics*. They have become significant in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) research since the 1980s.[^o19][^o20][^o21]

In 1982, Calabi introduced a [geometric flow](wikipedia://en/14617515), now known as the [Calabi flow](wikipedia://en/2309327), as a proposal for finding Kähler metrics of constant [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622).C82a More broadly, Calabi introduced the notion of an [extremal Kähler metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremal_Kähler_metric), and established (among other results) that they provide strict global minima of the *Calabi functional* and that any constant scalar curvature metric is also a global minimum.C85 Later, Calabi and [Xiuxiong Chen](wikipedia://en/60414667) made an extensive study of the metric introduced by [Toshiki Mabuchi](wikipedia://en/64788574), and showed that the Calabi flow contracts the Mabuchi distance between any two Kähler metrics.CC02 Furthermore, they showed that the Mabuchi metric endows the space of Kähler metrics with the structure of a [Alexandrov space](wikipedia://en/14609741) of nonpositive curvature. The technical difficulty of their work is that geodesics in their infinite-dimensional context may have low differentiability.[^o19]

A well-known construction of Calabi's puts complete Kähler metrics on the total spaces of hermitian vector bundles whose curvature is bounded below.C79 In the case that the base is a complete Kähler–Einstein manifold and the vector bundle has rank one and constant curvature, one obtains a complete Kähler–Einstein metric on the total space. In the case of the cotangent bundle of a complex space form, one obtains a [hyperkähler metric](wikipedia://en/646120). The [Eguchi–Hanson space](wikipedia://en/29321898) is a special case of Calabi's construction.[^o19]

### Geometric analysis {#Geometric_analysis}

Calabi found the *Laplacian comparison theorem* in [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), which relates the [Laplace–Beltrami operator](wikipedia://en/1763356), as applied to the [Riemannian distance function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric), to the Ricci curvature.C58a The Riemannian distance function is generally not differentiable everywhere, which poses a difficulty in formulating a global version of the theorem. Calabi made use of a generalized notion of differential inequalities, predating the later [viscosity solutions](wikipedia://en/2300687) introduced by [Michael Crandall](wikipedia://en/35265777) and [Pierre-Louis Lions](wikipedia://en/1270068). By extending the [strong maximum principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_maximum_principle) of [Eberhard Hopf](wikipedia://en/6021730) to his notion of viscosity solutions, Calabi was able to use his Laplacian comparison theorem to extend recent results of [Joseph Keller](wikipedia://en/4454126) and [Robert Osserman](wikipedia://en/19663336) to Riemannian contexts. Further extensions, based on different uses of the [maximum principle](wikipedia://en/1436104), were later found by [Shiu-Yuen Cheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiu-Yuen_Cheng) and Yau, among others.[^o19][^o23][^o24]

In parallel to the classical [Bernstein problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernstein_problem) for [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734), Calabi considered the analogous problem for [maximal surfaces](wikipedia://en/65492548), settling the question in low dimensions.C70 An unconditional answer for the **Bernstein problem for maximal surfaces** was found later by [Shiu-Yuen Cheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiu-Yuen_Cheng) and [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137), making use of the *Calabi trick* that Calabi had pioneered to circumvent the non-differentiability of the Riemannian distance function. In analogous work, Calabi had earlier considered the convex solutions of the [Monge–Ampère equation](wikipedia://en/4914066) which are defined on all of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) and with 'right-hand side' equal to one. [Konrad Jörgens](wikipedia://en/34870269) had earlier studied this problem for functions of two variables, proving that any solution is a quadratic polynomial. By interpreting the problem as one of [affine geometry](wikipedia://en/243890), Calabi was able to apply his earlier work on the Laplacian comparison theorem to extend Jörgens' work to some higher dimensions.C58b The problem was completely resolved later by [Aleksei Pogorelov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksei_Pogorelov), and the result is commonly known as the *Jörgens–Calabi–Pogorelov theorem*.[^o25]

Later, Calabi considered the problem of [affine hyperspheres](wikipedia://en/31202259), first characterizing such surfaces as those for which the [Legendre transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legendre_transform) solves a certain Monge–Ampère equation. By adapting his earlier methods in extending Jörgens' theorem, Calabi was able to classify the complete affine elliptic hyperspheres.C72 Further results were later obtained by Cheng and Yau.[^o25][^o26]

### Differential geometry {#Differential_geometry}

Calabi and [Beno Eckmann](wikipedia://en/19613365) discovered the [Calabi–Eckmann manifold](wikipedia://en/22670842) in 1953.CE53 It is notable as a [simply-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply-connected) complex manifold which does not admit any [Kähler metrics](wikipedia://en/390538).[^o28][^o29]

Inspired by recent work of [Kunihiko Kodaira](wikipedia://en/646326), Calabi and [Edoardo Vesentini](wikipedia://en/34938661) considered the infinitesimal rigidity of compact holomorphic quotients of [Cartan domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan_domain).CV60 Making use of the [Bochner technique](wikipedia://en/3988777) and Kodaira's developments of [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357), they proved the rigidity of higher-dimensional cases. Their work was an influence on the later work of [George Mostow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mostow) and [Grigori Margulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigori_Margulis), who established their global rigidity results out of attempts to understand infinitesimal rigidity results such as Calabi and Vesentini's, along with related works by [Atle Selberg](wikipedia://en/2021) and [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019).[^o30]

Calabi and Lawrence Markus considered the problem of [space forms](wikipedia://en/1087009) of positive curvature in [Lorentzian geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold).CM62 Their results, which [Joseph A. Wolf](wikipedia://en/41199481) considered "very surprising", assert that the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) must be finite, and that the corresponding group of isometries of [de Sitter spacetime](wikipedia://en/301666) (under an orientability condition) will act faithfully by isometries on an equatorial sphere. As such, their space form problem reduces to the problem of [Riemannian space forms](wikipedia://en/1087009) of positive curvature.[^o31][^o32]

Work of [John Nash](wikipedia://en/102567) in the 1950s considered the problem of [isometric embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometric_embedding). His work showed that such embeddings are very flexible and deformable. In his PhD thesis, Calabi had previously considered the special case of holomorphic isometric embeddings into [complex-geometric space forms](wikipedia://en/1087009).C53 A striking result of his shows that such embeddings are completely determined by the intrinsic geometry and the curvature of the space form in question. Moreover, he was able to study the problem of existence via his introduction of the *diastatic function*, which is a locally defined function built from [Kähler potentials](wikipedia://en/390538) and which mimics the Riemannian distance function. Calabi proved that a holomorphic isometric embedding must preserve the diastatic function. As a consequence, he was able to obtain a criterion for local existence of holomorphic isometric embeddings.[^o29]

Later, Calabi studied the two-dimensional [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734) (of high codimension) in round spheres.C67 He proved that the area of topologically spherical minimal surfaces can only take on a discrete set of values, and that the surfaces themselves are classified by [rational curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_curve) in a certain [hermitian symmetric space](wikipedia://en/4127357).[^o33][^o34][^o35]
