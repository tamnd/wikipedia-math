# Approximation property

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Approximation property"}

*Image: MazurGes.jpg.* The construction of a Banach space without the approximation property earned [Per Enflo](wikipedia://en/7180897) a live goose in 1972, which had been promised by [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162) (left) in 1936.[^o2]

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) is said to have the **approximation property** (**AP**) if every [compact operator](wikipedia://en/577441) is a limit of [finite-rank operators](wikipedia://en/8405295). The converse is always true.

:::

Every [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) has this property. There are, however, [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) which do not; [Per Enflo](wikipedia://en/7180897) published the first counterexample in a 1973 article. However, much work in this area was done by [Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck) (1955).

Later many other counterexamples were found. The space $\mathcal L(H)$ of [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on an infinite-dimensional [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $H$ does not have the approximation property.[^o3] The spaces $\ell^p$ for $p\neq 2$ and $c_0$ (see [Sequence space](wikipedia://en/1701075)) have closed subspaces that do not have the approximation property.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [locally convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex) topological vector space *X* is said to have **the approximation property**, if the identity map can be approximated, uniformly on [precompact sets](wikipedia://en/596600), by continuous linear maps of finite rank.[^n1]

For a locally convex space *X*, the following are equivalent:[^n1]

1. *X* has the approximation property;
1. the closure of $X^{\prime} \otimes X$ in $\operatorname{L}_p(X, X)$ contains the identity map $\operatorname{Id} : X \to X$;
1. $X^{\prime} \otimes X$ is dense in $\operatorname{L}_p(X, X)$;
1. for every locally convex space *Y*, $X^{\prime} \otimes Y$ is dense in $\operatorname{L}_p(X, Y)$;
1. for every locally convex space *Y*, $Y^{\prime} \otimes X$ is dense in $\operatorname{L}_p(Y, X)$;

where $\operatorname{L}_p(X, Y)$ denotes the space of continuous linear operators from *X* to *Y* endowed with the topology of uniform convergence on pre-compact subsets of *X*.

If *X* is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) this requirement becomes that for every [compact set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) $K\subset X$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there is an [operator](wikipedia://en/22213) $T\colon X\to X$ of finite rank so that $\|Tx-x\|\leq\varepsilon$, for every $x \in K$.

:::

## Related definitions {#Related_definitions}

Some other flavours of the AP are studied:

Let $X$ be a Banach space and let $1\leq\lambda<\infty$. We say that *X* has the $\lambda$*\-approximation property* ($\lambda$**\-AP**), if, for every compact set $K\subset X$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there is an [operator](wikipedia://en/22213) $T\colon X \to X$ of finite rank so that $\|Tx - x\|\leq\varepsilon$, for every $x \in K$, and $\|T\|\leq\lambda$.

A Banach space is said to have **bounded approximation property** (**BAP**), if it has the $\lambda$-AP for some $\lambda$.

A Banach space is said to have **metric approximation property** (**MAP**), if it is 1-AP.

A Banach space is said to have **compact approximation property** (**CAP**), if in the definition of AP an operator of finite rank is replaced with a compact operator.

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every subspace of an arbitrary product of Hilbert spaces possesses the approximation property.[^n1] In particular,
  - every Hilbert space has the approximation property.
  - every projective limit of Hilbert spaces, as well as any subspace of such a projective limit, possesses the approximation property.[^n1]
  - every [nuclear space](wikipedia://en/2702957) possesses the approximation property.
- Every separable Frechet space that contains a Schauder basis possesses the approximation property.[^n1]
- Every space with a [Schauder basis](wikipedia://en/3460662) has the AP (we can use the projections associated to the base as the $T$'s in the definition), thus many spaces with the AP can be found. For example, the [$\ell^p$ spaces](wikipedia://en/45194), or the [symmetric Tsirelson space](wikipedia://en/628352).

:::
