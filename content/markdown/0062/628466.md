# Outer automorphism group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Outer automorphism group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **outer automorphism group** of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447), $G$, is the [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526), $\operatorname{Aut}(G) / \operatorname{Inn}(G)$, where $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of $G$ and $\operatorname{Inn}(G$) is the subgroup consisting of [inner automorphisms](wikipedia://en/61839). The outer automorphism group is usually denoted $\operatorname{Out}(G)$. If $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ is trivial and $G$ has a trivial [center](wikipedia://en/7125), then $G$ is said to be [complete](wikipedia://en/876770).

:::

An automorphism of a group that is not inner is called an **outer automorphism**.[^o2] The [cosets](wikipedia://en/98759) of $\operatorname{Inn}(G)$ with respect to outer automorphisms are then the elements of $\operatorname{Out}(G)$; this is an instance of the fact that quotients of groups are not, in general, (isomorphic to) subgroups. If the inner automorphism group is trivial (when a group is abelian), the automorphism group and outer automorphism group are naturally identified; that is, the outer automorphism group does act on the group.

For example, for the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726), $A_{n}$, the outer automorphism group is usually the group of order 2, with exceptions noted below. Considering $A_{n}$ as a subgroup of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901), $S_{n}$, conjugation by any [odd permutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_permutation) is an outer automorphism of $A_{n}$ or more precisely "represents the class of the (non-trivial) outer automorphism of $A_{n}$", but the outer automorphism does not correspond to conjugation by any *particular* odd element, and all conjugations by odd elements are equivalent up to conjugation by an even element.

## Structure {#Structure}

The [Schreier conjecture](wikipedia://en/7030987) asserts that $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ is always a [solvable group](wikipedia://en/105012) when $G$ is a finite [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600). This result is now known to be true as a corollary of the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445), although no simpler proof is known.

## As dual of the center {#As_dual_of_the_center}

The outer automorphism group is [dual](wikipedia://en/609737) to the center in the following sense: conjugation by an element of $G$ is an automorphism, yielding a map $\sigma : G \to \operatorname{Aut}(G)$. The [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) of the conjugation map is the center, while the [cokernel](wikipedia://en/279715) is the outer automorphism group (and the image is the [inner automorphism](wikipedia://en/61839) group). This can be summarized by the [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215)

$$Z(G) \hookrightarrow G \, \overset{\sigma}{\longrightarrow} \, \mathrm{Aut}(G) \twoheadrightarrow \mathrm{Out}(G)$$

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The outer automorphism group of a group acts on [conjugacy classes](wikipedia://en/49176), and accordingly on the [character table](wikipedia://en/911520). See details at [character table: outer automorphisms](wikipedia://en/911520#Outer_automorphisms).

### Topology of surfaces {#Topology_of_surfaces}

The outer automorphism group is important in the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of [surfaces](wikipedia://en/27865) because there is a connection provided by the [Dehn&ndash;Nielsen theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehn&ndash;Nielsen_theorem): the extended [mapping class group](wikipedia://en/793295) of the surface is the outer automorphism group of its [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004).

:::

## In finite groups {#In_finite_groups}

For the outer automorphism groups of all finite simple groups see the [list of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/1749665). Sporadic simple groups and alternating groups (other than the alternating group, $A_{6}$; see below) all have outer automorphism groups of order 1 or 2. The outer automorphism group of a finite simple [group of Lie type](wikipedia://en/1691490) is an extension of a group of "diagonal automorphisms" (cyclic except for $D_{n}(q)$, when it has order 4), a group of "field automorphisms" (always cyclic), and a group of "graph automorphisms" (of order 1 or 2 except for $D_{4}(q)$, when it is the symmetric group on 3 points). These extensions are not always [semidirect products](wikipedia://en/48246), as the case of the alternating group $A_{6}$ shows; a precise criterion for this to happen was given in 2003.[^o9]

## In symmetric and alternating groups {#In_symmetric_and_alternating_groups}

The outer automorphism group of a finite simple group in some infinite family of finite simple groups can almost always be given by a uniform formula that works for all elements of the family. There is just one exception to this:[^o12] the alternating group $A_{6}$ has outer automorphism group of order 4, rather than 2 as do the other simple alternating groups (given by conjugation by an [odd permutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_permutation)). Equivalently the symmetric group $S_{6}$ is the only symmetric group with a non-trivial outer automorphism group.

$$\begin{aligned} n \neq 6: \operatorname{Out}(\mathrm{S}_n) & = \mathrm{C}_1 \\ n \geq 3,\ n \neq 6: \operatorname{Out}(\mathrm{A}_n) & = \mathrm{C}_2 \\ \operatorname{Out}(\mathrm{S}_6) & = \mathrm{C}_2 \\ \operatorname{Out}(\mathrm{A}_6) & = \mathrm{C}_2 \times \mathrm{C}_2 \end{aligned}$$

Note that, in the case of $G = A_{6} = \operatorname{PSL}(2, 9)$, the sequence $1 \longrightarrow G \longrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(G) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Out}(G) \longrightarrow 1$ does not split. A similar result holds for any $\operatorname{PSL}(2, q^{2})$, $q$ odd.

## In reductive algebraic groups {#In_reductive_algebraic_groups}

*Image: Dynkin diagram D4.png.* The symmetries of the [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109), $D_{4}$, correspond to the outer automorphisms of $\operatorname{Spin}(8)$ in triality.

Let $G$ now be a connected [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018). Then any two [Borel subgroups](wikipedia://en/588356) are conjugate by an inner automorphism, so to study outer automorphisms it suffices to consider automorphisms that fix a given Borel subgroup. Associated to the Borel subgroup is a set of [simple roots](wikipedia://en/277087#Positive_roots_and_simple_roots), and the outer automorphism may permute them, while preserving the structure of the associated [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277087#Classification_of_root_systems_by_Dynkin_diagrams). In this way one may identify the automorphism group of the Dynkin diagram of $G$ with a subgroup of $\operatorname{Out}(G)$.

$D_{4}$ has a very symmetric Dynkin diagram, which yields a large outer automorphism group of $\operatorname{Spin}(8)$, namely $\operatorname{Out}(\operatorname{Spin}(8)) = S_{3}$; this is called [triality](wikipedia://en/683109).

## In complex and real simple Lie algebras {#In_complex_and_real_simple_Lie_algebras}

The preceding interpretation of outer automorphisms as symmetries of a Dynkin diagram follows from the general fact, that for a complex or real simple Lie algebra, $\text{𝔤}$, the automorphism group $\operatorname{Aut}(\text{𝔤})$ is a [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of $\operatorname{Inn}(\text{𝔤})$ and $\operatorname{Out}(\text{𝔤})$; i.e., the [short exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215)

$$1 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Inn}(\text{𝔤}) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(\text{𝔤}) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Out}(\text{𝔤}) \longrightarrow 1$$

splits. In the complex simple case, this is a classical result,[^o15] whereas for real simple Lie algebras, this fact was proven as recently as 2010.[^o16]

## Word play {#Word_play}

The term *outer automorphism* lends itself to [word play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_play): the term *outermorphism* is sometimes used for *outer automorphism*, and a particular [geometry](wikipedia://en/12746935) on which $\operatorname{Out}(F_{n})$ acts is called *[outer space](wikipedia://en/12747184#Outer_space)*.
