# Double Mersenne number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Double Mersenne number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **double Mersenne number** is a [Mersenne number](wikipedia://en/18908) of the form $M_{M_p} = 2^{2^p-1}-1$ where $p$ is [prime](wikipedia://en/23666).

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The first four terms of the [sequence](wikipedia://en/51421) of double Mersenne numbers are[^o4] :

$$M_{M_2} = M_3 = 7$$

$$M_{M_3} = M_7 = 127$$

$$M_{M_5} = M_{31} = 2147483647$$

$$M_{M_7} = M_{127} = 170141183460469231731687303715884105727$$

:::

## Double Mersenne primes {#Double_Mersenne_primes}

A double Mersenne number that is [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) is called a **double Mersenne prime**. Since a Mersenne number *M*<sub>*p*</sub> can be prime only if *p* is prime, (see [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908) for a proof), a double Mersenne number $M_{M_p}$ can be prime only if *M*<sub>*p*</sub> is itself a Mersenne prime. For the first values of *p* for which *M*<sub>*p*</sub> is prime, $M_{M_{p}}$ is known to be prime for *p* = 2, 3, 5, and 7 while explicit factors of $M_{M_{p}}$ have been found for *p* = 13, 17, 19, and 31.

Thus, the smallest candidate for the next double Mersenne prime is $M_{M_{61}}$, or 2<sup>2305843009213693951</sup> − 1. Being approximately 1.695, this number is far too large for any currently known [primality test](wikipedia://en/183751). It has no prime factor below 1 × 10<sup>36</sup>.[^o8] There are probably no other double Mersenne primes than the four known.[^o4][^o9]

Smallest prime factor of $M_{M_{p}}$ (where *p* is the *n*th prime) are

7, 127, 2147483647, 170141183460469231731687303715884105727, 47, 338193759479, 231733529, 62914441, 2351, 1399, 295257526626031, 18287, 106937, 863, 4703, 138863, 22590223644617, ... (next term is > 1 × 10<sup>36</sup>)

## Catalan–Mersenne number conjecture {#Catalan–Mersenne_number_conjecture}

The [recursively](wikipedia://en/25407) defined sequence

$$c_0 = 2$$

$$c_{n+1} = 2^{c_n}-1 = M_{c_n}$$

is called the sequence of **Catalan–Mersenne numbers**. The first terms of the sequence are:

$$c_0 = 2$$

$$c_1 = 2^2-1 = 3$$

$$c_2 = 2^3-1 = 7$$

$$c_3 = 2^7-1 = 127$$

$$c_4 = 2^{127}-1 = 170141183460469231731687303715884105727$$

$$c_5 = 2^{170141183460469231731687303715884105727}-1 \approx 5.45431 \times 10^{51217599719369681875006054625051616349} \approx 10^{10^{37.70942}}$$

[Catalan](wikipedia://en/639553) discovered this sequence after the discovery of the primality of $M_{127}=c_4$ by [Lucas](wikipedia://en/150102) in 1876.[^o4][^o13][^o14]<sup>p. 22</sup> Catalan [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) that they are prime "up to a certain limit". Although the first five terms are prime, no known methods can prove that any further terms are prime (in any reasonable time) simply because they are too huge. However, if $c_5$ is not prime, there is a chance to discover this by computing $c_5$ [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) some small prime $p$ (using recursive [modular exponentiation](wikipedia://en/903032)). If the resulting residue is zero, $p$ represents a factor of $c_5$ and thus would disprove its primality. Since $c_5$ is a [Mersenne number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_number), such a prime factor $p$ would have to be of the form $2kc_4 +1$. Additionally, because $2^n-1$ is [composite](wikipedia://en/82289) when $n$ is composite, the discovery of a composite term in the sequence would preclude the possibility of any further primes in the sequence.

If $c_5$ were prime, it would also contradict the [New Mersenne conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mersenne_conjecture). It is known that $\frac{2^{c_4} + 1}{3}$ is composite, with factor $886407410000361345663448535540258622490179142922169401 = 5209834514912200c_4 + 1$.[^o15]

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

In the *[Futurama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurama)* movie [*The Beast with a Billion Backs*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurama:_The_Beast_with_a_Billion_Backs), the double Mersenne number $M_{M_7}$ is briefly seen in "an [elementary proof](wikipedia://en/9318685) of the [Goldbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldbach_conjecture)". In the movie, this number is known as a "Martian prime".
