# Stefan Mazurkiewicz

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Stefan Mazurkiewicz"}

**Stefan Mazurkiewicz** (Polish: m; 25 September 1888 – 19 June 1945) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was a representative of the [Warsaw School of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_School_of_Mathematics) and worked in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), and [probability](wikipedia://en/22934).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Mazurkiewicz was born on 25 September 1888 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), then part of [Congress Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland), to Jan, a lawyer, and Michalina (Piotrowska).[^o4] In 1906, he matriculated at the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University) to study mathematics. After a year, he travelled to [Western Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe) and studied in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) until 1912. During his time in Munich, Mazurkiewicz collaborated with [Zygmunt Janiszewski](wikipedia://en/362471) working in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). Upon his return in 1913, he earned his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) at the [University of Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv) under [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122) based on his dissertation *O krzywych wypełniajacych kwadrat* (On Square-filling Curves).[^o5]

In 1915, he became a professor at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw).[^o6] In 1916, Mazurkiewicz introduced the concept of an [opaque set](wikipedia://en/41162601): a collection of curves or segments that intersects all lines passing through a given region. In 1917, he became a member of the [Warsaw Scientific Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Scientific_Society).[^o4]

During the [Polish–Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish–Soviet_War) (1919–21), Mazurkiewicz as early as 1919 broke the most common [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) [cipher](wikipedia://en/5244) for the Polish [General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Staff)'s [cryptological agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biuro_Szyfrów). Thanks to this, orders issued by Soviet commander [Mikhail Tukhachevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Tukhachevsky)'s staff were known to [Polish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Armed_Forces) leaders. This contributed substantially, perhaps decisively, to Polish victory at the critical [Battle of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Warsaw_%281920%29) and possibly to Poland's survival as an independent country.

Following Janiszewski’s death in 1920, Mazurkiewicz and Sierpiński assumed responsibility for editing *[Fundamenta Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/1576701)*, the journal he had established.[^o6]

The [Hahn-Mazurkiewicz theorem](wikipedia://en/352398#The_Hahn–Mazurkiewicz_theorem), a basic result on [curves](wikipedia://en/89246) prompted by the phenomenon of [space-filling curves](wikipedia://en/352398), is named for Mazurkiewicz and [Hans Hahn](wikipedia://en/1075212). His 1935 paper *Sur l'existence des continus indécomposables* is generally considered the most elegant piece of work in [point-set topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-set_topology).

In 1933-1935, Mazurkiewicz served as president of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791). He was elected Dean of the Department of Mathematical and Natural Sciences of the University of Warsaw, holding this position for nine years.[^o6]

Mazurkiewicz exerted a major influence on the emergence of the [Polish School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/3008223).[^o6] He was a member of the [Polish Academy of Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Learning) (*PAU*). During his scientific career, he published 141 papers and supervised 11 doctoral students.[^o4] His students included [Karol Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk), [Bronisław Knaster](wikipedia://en/1003125), [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075), [Stanisław Saks](wikipedia://en/362649), and [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477).[^o5] For a time Mazurkiewicz was a professor at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris); however, he spent most of his career as a professor at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw).
