# Adriaan van Roomen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adriaan van Roomen"}

**Adriaan van Roomen** (29 September 1561 – 4 May 1615), also known as **Adrianus Romanus**, was a mathematician, professor of medicine and [medical astrologer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_astrology) from the [Duchy of Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Brabant) in the [Habsburg Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Netherlands) who was active throughout Central Europe in the late 16th and early 17th centuries. As a mathematician he worked in algebra, trigonometry and geometry; and on the [decimal expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_expansion) of [pi](wikipedia://en/23601). He solved the [Problem of Apollonius](wikipedia://en/2642185) using a new method that involved intersecting hyperbolas. He also wrote on the [Gregorian calendar reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar_reform).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Van Roomen was born in [Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuven), the son of Adriaan Van Roomen and Maria Van Den Daele. He was educated partly in Leuven and partly After studying at the Jesuit College in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), also attending the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne) where he began his study of medicine.[^o3] He also briefly studied medicine at [Leuven University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_University_of_Leuven). Roomen was professor of mathematics and medicine at Louvain from 1586 to 1592. He met [Kepler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler), and discussed with [François Viète](wikipedia://en/310238) two questions about equations and tangencies. He then spent some time in Italy, particularly with [Clavius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clavius) in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) in 1585. His publication of 1595, *Parvum theatrum urbium*, contained Latin verse on the cities of Italy (possibly written by [Thomas Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Edwards_%28poet%29)).[^o4]

In June 1593 Van Roomen became the inaugural professor of medicine at the newly refounded [University of Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Würzburg).[^o3] He was also appointed physician in ordinary to the court of [Rudolf II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_II).[^o3] From around 1595 to 1603 he produced calendars, almanacs and prognostications published under the patronage of [Julius Echter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Echter_von_Mespelbrunn), [prince-bishop of Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-bishop_of_Würzburg).[^o5] At the same time, he served as mathematician of the king of Poland and become famous for the computation of the value of [pi](wikipedia://en/23601) to sixteen decimals,[^o6][^o7] surpassing [François Viète](wikipedia://en/310238) who had arrived at ten digits.[^o8] After being widowed he was ordained to the priesthood in 1604 and on 8 October 1608 was installed as a canon of the [collegiate church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_church) of St John the Evangelist in Würzburg.[^o3]

His *Mathesis Polemica*, published in Frankfurt in 1605, explained the military applications of mathematics. In June 1610 he was in Prague, after which he travelled to Poland at the invitation of [Jan Zamoyski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Zamoyski) to give public lectures on mathematics at [Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamość) in [Red Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Ruthenia). He made the return journey via Hungary, arriving back in Würzburg at the end of 1611.[^o3]

Struggling with health problems, Van Roomen undertook a journey to [Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa,_Belgium) to take the waters but died en route at [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz) in the arms of his son, who was travelling with him.[^o3]
