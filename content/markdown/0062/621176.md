# Interpretability

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Interpretability"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **interpretability** is a relation between formal theories that expresses the possibility of interpreting or translating one into the other.

:::

## Informal definition {#Informal_definition}

Assume *T* and *S* are [formal theories](wikipedia://en/3512103). Slightly simplified, *T* is said to be *interpretable* in *S* if and only if the language of *T* can be translated into the [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language) of *S* in such a way that *S* proves the translation of every [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of *T*. Of course, there are some natural conditions on admissible translations here, such as the necessity for a translation to preserve the logical structure of [formulas](wikipedia://en/404582).

This concept, together with [weak interpretability](wikipedia://en/655334), was introduced by [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) in 1953. Three other related concepts are [cointerpretability](wikipedia://en/621215), [logical tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolerance_%28in_logic%29), and [cotolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotolerance), introduced by [Giorgi Japaridze](wikipedia://en/1866981) in 1992–93.
