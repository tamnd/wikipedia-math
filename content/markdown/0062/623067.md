# Henry William Watson

*Image: Henry William Watson.jpg.* Henry William Watson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henry William Watson"}

Rev. **Henry William Watson** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (25 February 1827, [Marylebone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylebone), [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) – 11 January 1903, [Brighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton)) was a mathematician and author of a number of mathematics books. He was an ordained [priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priest) and [Cambridge Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Apostle).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Watson was born at [Marylebone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylebone) to Thomas Watson, R.N., and Eleanor Mary Kingston.

He was educated at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) and at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). He graduated as second [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) and Smith's prizeman in 1850, Dr. [W. H. Besant](wikipedia://en/38744654) being senior wrangler. In 1851 he became fellow of Trinity college, and from 1851 to 1853 was assistant tutor there. Watson formed a close friendship with [James Fitzjames Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Fitzjames_Stephen), who entered Trinity in 1847.[^o3] From 1853 he was mathematical master in the City of London School, and in 1857 he became mathematical lecturer at King′s College.

Watson was ordained [deacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deacon) in 1856, and two years later received priest′s orders. He was an assistant master at [Harrow School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow_School) from 1857 to 1865, when he became [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28ecclesiastical%29) of [Berkswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkswell) near [Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry). He kept this position for 37 years, resigning in August 1902, when he moved to Brighton. His time at the rectory provided ample time to pursue mathematical and scientific studies, and he was a frequent contributor to scientific literature.[^o5]

He and [Francis Galton](wikipedia://en/49072) introduced the [Galton&ndash;Watson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galton&ndash;Watson_process) in 1875. He was made a Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) (FRS) in 1881, and took the degree [D.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) in 1883.

He died in Brighton on 11 January 1903.[^o5]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Watson married Emily Rowe in 1856. They had three children, one son and two daughters.[^o7]
