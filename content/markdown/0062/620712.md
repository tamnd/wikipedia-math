# Landau–Ramanujan constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Landau–Ramanujan constant"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and the field of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **Landau–Ramanujan constant** is the positive real number *b* that occurs in a theorem proved by [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230) in 1908,[^o2] stating that for large $x$, the number of [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer) below $x$ that are the sum of two [square numbers](wikipedia://en/213919) [behaves asymptotically as](wikipedia://en/44578)

:::

$$\dfrac{bx}{\sqrt{\log(x)}}.$$

This [constant](wikipedia://en/48779551) *b* was rediscovered in 1913 by [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717), in the first letter he wrote to [G.H. Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.H._Hardy).[^o3]

## Sums of two squares {#Sums_of_two_squares}

By the [sum of two squares theorem](wikipedia://en/46248976), the numbers that can be expressed as a sum of two squares of integers are the ones for which each [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) congruent to 3 mod 4 appears with an even exponent in their [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorization). For instance, *45 = 9 + 36* is a sum of two squares; in its prime factorization, 3<sup>2</sup> × 5, the prime 3 appears with an even exponent, and the prime 5 is congruent to 1 mod 4, so its exponent can be odd.

Landau's theorem states that if $N(x)$ is the number of positive integers less than $x$ that are the sum of two squares, then

$\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\ \left(\dfrac{N(x)}{\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{\log(x)}}}\right)=b\approx 0.764223653589220662990698731250092328116790541$ ,

where $b$ is the Landau–Ramanujan constant.

The Landau-Ramanujan constant can also be written as an infinite product:

$$b = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\prod_{p\equiv 3 \pmod{4}} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p^2}\right)^{-1/2} = \frac{\pi}{4} \prod_{p\equiv 1 \pmod{4}} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p^2}\right)^{1/2}.$$

## History {#History}

This constant was stated by Landau in the limit form above; Ramanujan instead approximated $N(x)$ as an integral, with the same constant of proportionality, and with a slowly growing error term.
