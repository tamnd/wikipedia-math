# Tolerant sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tolerant sequence"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), a **tolerant sequence** is a sequence

:::

$T_1$,...,$T_n$

of [formal theories](wikipedia://en/3512103) such that there are [consistent](wikipedia://en/3512103#Consistency_and_completeness) [extensions](wikipedia://en/3512103#Subtheories_and_extensions)

$S_1$,...,$S_n$

of these theories with each $S_{i+1}$ [interpretable](wikipedia://en/621176) in $S_i$. Tolerance naturally generalizes from sequences of theories to trees of theories. [Weak interpretability](wikipedia://en/655334) can be shown to be a special, binary case of tolerance.

This concept, together with its dual concept of [cotolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotolerance), was introduced by [Japaridze](wikipedia://en/1866981) in 1992, who also proved that, for [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) and any stronger theories with [computable](wikipedia://en/332264) [axiomatizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatization), tolerance is equivalent to $\Pi_1$-consistency.
