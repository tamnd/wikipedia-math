# Cartan's theorems A and B

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cartan's theorems A and B"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Cartan's theorems A and B** are two results [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536) around 1951, concerning a [coherent sheaf](wikipedia://en/603780) $F$ on a [Stein manifold](wikipedia://en/1053909) $X$. They are significant both as applied to [several complex variables](wikipedia://en/360243), and in the general development of [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357).

:::

::: {#o2 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem A"}

**Theorem A.**

$F$ is [spanned by its global sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_spanned_by_global_sections).

:::

Theorem B is stated in cohomological terms (a formulation that Cartan (1953, p. 51) attributes to J.-P. Serre):

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem B"}

**Theorem B.**

$H^{p}(X, F) = 0$ for all $p > 0$.

:::

Analogous properties were established by [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) (1957) for coherent sheaves in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), when $X$ is an [affine scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme). The analogue of Theorem B in this context is as follows (Hartshorne 1977, Theorem III.3.7):

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem B (Scheme theoretic analogue)"}

**Theorem B (Scheme theoretic analogue).**

Let $X$ be an affine scheme, $F$ a [quasi-coherent sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-coherent_sheaf) of $O_{X}$-modules for the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) on $X$. Then $H^{p}(X, F) = 0$ for all $p > 0$.

:::

These theorems have many important applications. For instance, they imply that a holomorphic function on a closed complex submanifold, $Z$, of a Stein manifold $X$ can be extended to a holomorphic function on all of $X$. At a deeper level, these theorems were used by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) to prove the [GAGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAGA) theorem.

Theorem B is sharp in the sense that if $H^{1}(X, F) = 0$ for all coherent sheaves $F$ on a complex manifold $X$ (resp. quasi-coherent sheaves $F$ on a noetherian scheme $X$), then $X$ is Stein (resp. affine); see (Serre 1956) (resp. (Serre 1957) and (Hartshorne 1977, Theorem III.3.7)).
