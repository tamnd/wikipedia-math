# Superegg

*Image: Superaeg.jpg.* Brass superegg by [Piet Hein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Hein_%28Denmark%29).

*Image: Piet Hein Superegg.stl.* Piet Hein Superegg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Superegg"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **superegg** is a [solid of revolution](wikipedia://en/329400) obtained by rotating an elongated [superellipse](wikipedia://en/52211) with [exponent](wikipedia://en/99491) greater than 2 around its [longest axis](wikipedia://en/23409699#Ellipse). It is a special case of [superellipsoid](wikipedia://en/19681409).

:::

Unlike an [elongated ellipsoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolate_spheroid), an elongated superegg can stand upright on a flat surface, or on top of another superegg.[^o2] This is due to its [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) being zero at the tips. The shape was popularized by Danish poet and scientist [Piet Hein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Hein_%28Denmark%29) (1905–1996). Supereggs of various materials, including brass, were sold as novelties or "[executive toys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_toy)" in the 1960s.

## Mathematical description {#Mathematical_description}

The superegg is a superellipsoid whose horizontal cross-sections are circles. It is defined by the inequality

$$\left|\frac{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}{R}\right|^p + \left|\frac{z}{h}\right|^p \leq 1 \, ,$$

where *R* is the horizontal radius at the "equator" (the widest part as defined by the circles), and *h* is one half of the height. The [exponent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent) *p* determines the degree of flattening at the tips and equator. Hein's choice was *p* = 2.5 (the same one he used for the [Sergels Torg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergels_Torg) roundabout), and *R*/*h* = 6/5.[^o4]

The definition can be changed to have an equality rather than an inequality; this changes the superegg to being a [surface of revolution](wikipedia://en/329549) rather than a solid.[^o5]

## Volume {#Volume}

The volume of a superegg can be derived via [squigonometry](wikipedia://en/77345250), a generalization of [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261) to [squircles](wikipedia://en/5565333).[^o7] It is related to the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316):

$$V = \frac{4\pi hR^2}{3p}\frac{\Gamma(1/p) \Gamma(2/p)}{\Gamma(3/p)} \, .$$
