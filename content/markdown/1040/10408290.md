# Fritz Ursell

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Fritz Ursell"}

**Fritz Joseph Ursell** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o5] (28 April 1923 – 11 May 2012) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) noted for his contributions to [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988), especially in the area of wave-structure interactions.[^o6] He held the [Beyer Chair of Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyer_Chair_of_Applied_Mathematics) at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester) from 1961 to 1990,[^o7] was elected [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1972 and retired in 1990.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Ursell came to England as a Jewish refugee in 1937 from Germany,[^o2][^o11] and was educated at [Marlborough College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlborough_College). From 1941 to 1943 he studied at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), graduating with a [bachelor degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_degree) in mathematics.

## Career {#Career}

At the end of 1943 Ursell joined the [Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty) as a part of a team—headed by [George Deacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Deacon) —whose task was to formulate rules for forecasting waves for the allied landings in Japan. Their findings have become the basis of modern [wave-forecasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave-forecasting). Ursell stayed in the Admiralty until 1947. In 1947 he was appointed to a [post-doctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-doctoral) fellowship in applied mathematics at Manchester University without a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate). In 1950 he returned to Cambridge as [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer). There he met [G. I. Taylor](wikipedia://en/610133). In 1957 he spent a year at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), having been invited by [Arthur Ippen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ippen). In 1961 Ursell moved back to Manchester.[^o14]

In 1994 Ursell was awarded the [Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMA_Gold_Medal) of the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) in recognition of his "outstanding contributions to mathematics and its applications over a period of years".[^o15]

## Scientific work {#Scientific_work}

In 1957 he published together with Clive R. Chester and Bernard Friedman a classic paper that introduced a method to find [asymptotic expansions](wikipedia://en/642090) for contour integrals with coalescing saddle points.[^o17] The method is now called [method of Chester–Friedman–Ursell](wikipedia://en/73119456).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Fritz Ursell was married to Katharina Renate Zander in 1959. They had two daughters.[^o2] Susie and Ruth, Susie is married and has two children.[^o3] Following his death on 11 May, in hospital, Ursell's funeral took place on 15 May 2012 at Manchester Crematorium.[^o19]
