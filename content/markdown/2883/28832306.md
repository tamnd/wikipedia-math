# Hugh Blackburn

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hugh Blackburn"}

[Bailie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailie) **Hugh Blackburn** () (2 July 1823 – 9 October 1909) was a Scottish mathematician. A lifelong friend of William Thomson (later [Lord Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kelvin)) and the husband of illustrator [Jemima Blackburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jemima_Blackburn), he was [professor of mathematics at the University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_of_mathematics_at_the_University_of_Glasgow) from 1849 to 1879. He succeeded Thomson's father [James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Thomson_%28mathematician%29) in the chair of mathematics.

:::

## Life {#Life}

*Image: Hugh Blackburn2.jpg.* An 1877 caricature of Hugh Blackburn

Blackburn was born on 2 July 1823 in Craigflower, [Torryburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torryburn), [Fife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fife). He was brought up at [Killearn House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killearn_House), Stirlingshire, the seventh of eight children of wealthy Glasgow merchant John Blackburn and his wife Rebecca Leslie Gillies, the daughter of a [Church of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scotland) minister and a relative of [Colin Maclaurin](wikipedia://en/7824).[^o4] His elder brother was Judge [Colin Blackburn, Baron Blackburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Blackburn,_Baron_Blackburn). His father became wealthy off sugar and slavery in Jamaica, becoming a merchant on his return to Glasgow. In the 1830s, when the British government emancipated the slaves, John received compensation for the ownership of over 550 slaves.[^o5]

Blackburn was educated at the [Edinburgh Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Academy) and [Eton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College) before entering [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in 1840. There he met Thomson, who entered in the same year; he was also a member of the [Cambridge Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Apostles). During this time he invented the [Blackburn pendulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackburn_pendulum).[^o7] In the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) examinations of 1845 he graduated fifth [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29), while Thomson graduated second wrangler.

He entered the [Inner Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Temple) in 1847 but was never called to the bar; his name was withdrawn in 1849, the year in which he became a professor of mathematics at the University of Glasgow.[^o6] He married [Jemima Wedderburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jemima_Blackburn) (cousin of [James Clerk Maxwell](wikipedia://en/28989696)), the daughter of [James Wedderburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wedderburn_%28judge%29), [Solicitor General for Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solicitor_General_for_Scotland).[^o6] He died on 9 October 1909 at his estate in [Roshven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roshven), [Inverness-shire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverness-shire).

## Works {#Works}

- *A treatise on trigonometry*, London, 1855. Part of the *[Encyclopedia Metropolitana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopedia_Metropolitana)*.
- *A short sketch of the constitutional history of the University of Glasgow and of Glasgow College in that University: with remarks on the Universities (Scotland) Bill*, 1858
- (ed. with [William Thomson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kelvin) ) *Sir [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627)'s [Principia](wikipedia://en/48781)*, 1871
- *Elements of plane trigonometry for the use of the junior class of mathematics in the University of Glasgow*, 1871
