# Siegel's theorem on integral points

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Siegel's theorem on integral points"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Siegel's theorem on integral points** states that a curve of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) greater than zero has only finitely many integral points over any given [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field).

:::

The theorem was first proved in 1929 by [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602) and was the first major result on [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109) that depended only on the genus and not any special algebraic form of the equations. For *g* > 1 it was superseded by [Faltings' theorem](wikipedia://en/172199) in 1983.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

> **Siegel's theorem on integral points:** For a [smooth](wikipedia://en/981915) [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) *C* of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) *g* defined over a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) *K*, presented in [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) in a given coordinate system, there are only finitely many points on *C* with coordinates in the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) *O* of *K*, provided *g* > 0.

:::

## History {#History}

In 1926, Siegel proved the theorem effectively in the special case $g=1$, so that he proved this theorem conditionally, provided the [Mordell's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell's_conjecture) is true.

In 1929, Siegel proved the theorem unconditionally by combining a version of the [Thue–Siegel–Roth theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thue–Siegel–Roth_theorem), from [diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558), with the [Mordell–Weil theorem](wikipedia://en/23087727) from [diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205) (required in Weil's version, to apply to the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) of *C*).

In 2002, [Umberto Zannier](wikipedia://en/58056511) and [Pietro Corvaja](wikipedia://en/62816286) gave a new proof by using a new method based on the [subspace theorem](wikipedia://en/8830237).[^o5]

## Effective versions {#Effective_versions}

Siegel's result was ineffective for $g\ge2$ (see [effective results in number theory](wikipedia://en/250237)), since [Thue](wikipedia://en/635533)'s method in diophantine approximation also is ineffective in describing possible very good rational approximations to almost all [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158) of degree $d\ge5$. Siegel proved it effectively only in the special case $g=1$ in 1926. Effective results in some cases derive from [Baker's method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker's_method).
