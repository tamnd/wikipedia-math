# Bochner's formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bochner's formula"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Bochner's formula** is a statement relating [harmonic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_functions) on a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $(M, g)$ to the [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582). The formula is named after the [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Salomon Bochner](wikipedia://en/987847).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Formal statement"}

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

If $u \colon M \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a smooth function, then

$$\tfrac12 \Delta|\nabla u|^2 = g(\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u) + |\nabla^2 u|^2 + \mbox{Ric}(\nabla u, \nabla u),$$

where $\nabla u$ is the [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461) of $u$ with respect to $g$, $\nabla^2 u$ is the [Hessian](wikipedia://en/412108) of $u$ with respect to $g$ and $\mbox{Ric}$ is the [Ricci curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/276582).[^o4] If $u$ is harmonic (i.e., $\Delta u = 0$, where $\Delta=\Delta_g$ is the [Laplacian](wikipedia://en/1763356) with respect to the metric $g$), Bochner's formula becomes

$$\tfrac12 \Delta|\nabla u| ^2 = |\nabla^2 u|^2 + \mbox{Ric}(\nabla u, \nabla u).$$

Bochner used this formula to prove the [Bochner vanishing theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bochner_vanishing_theorem).

As a corollary, if $(M, g)$ is a Riemannian manifold without boundary and $u \colon M \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is a smooth, compactly supported function, then

$$\int_M (\Delta u)^2 \, d\mbox{vol} = \int_M \Big( |\nabla^2 u|^2 + \mbox{Ric}(\nabla u, \nabla u) \Big) \, d\mbox{vol}.$$

This immediately follows from the first identity, observing that the integral of the left-hand side vanishes (by the [divergence theorem](wikipedia://en/151864)) and integrating by parts the first term on the right-hand side.

:::
