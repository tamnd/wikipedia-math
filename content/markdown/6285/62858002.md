# Pamela E. Harris

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Pamela E. Harris"}

**Pamela Estephania Harris** (born November 28, 1983) is a Mexican-American mathematician, educator and advocate for immigrants. She is currently a professor at the [University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin-Milwaukee) in [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee), [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin),[^o3] was formerly an associate professor at [Williams College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_College) in [Williamstown, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamstown,_Massachusetts) and is co-founder of the online platform Lathisms.[^o14][^o4] She is also an editor of the e-mentoring blog of the [American Mathematical Society (AMS).](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o5]

:::

## Early life and career {#Early_life_and_career}

Harris first emigrated with her family from Mexico to the United States when she was 8 years old.[^o7][^o27] They returned to Mexico, before eventually settling in Wisconsin when Harris was 12.[^o7][^o8] Because she was undocumented, she could not attend university.[^o23][^o8] Instead, she studied at the [Milwaukee Area Technical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Area_Technical_College), where she earned two associate degrees in two and a half years.[^o8][^o23] After she married a US citizen and her immigration status changed, she transferred to [Marquette University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquette_University), where she obtained a bachelor's degree in mathematics.[^o23][^o7] She went on to complete her master's degree and in 2012 a PhD at the [University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Milwaukee). Her Ph.D. dissertation was advised by [Jeb F. Willenbring.](wikipedia://en/68758573) Harris was a [Project NExT](wikipedia://en/14984955) (New Experiences in Teaching) fellow in 2012.[^o10] She was a Davies Research Fellow at the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy),[^o7][^o8] and, in 2016, joined the faculty at Williams College[^o11][^o8] where she was an associate professor.[^o12] In 2022, she joined the faculty at the [University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin-Milwaukee) as an associate professor.[^o3]

Harris studies [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582), in particular the representation of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/318712).[^o8][^o13] In order to understand this representation she studies vector partition functions, in particular [Kostant's partition function](wikipedia://en/23994987).[^o7] She is also interested in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o13] In 2016 she co-founded an online platform called 'Lathisms' which aims to promote the contributions of Latinxs and Hispanics in the Mathematical Sciences.[^o14][^o7] In 2020 she co-authored the book "Asked and Answered: Dialogues On Advocating For Students of Color in Mathematics".[^o15] Harris, along with Aris Winger, run a podcast, Mathematically Uncensored, through the Center for Minorities in the Mathematical Sciences. Starting in October 2020, they discussed current issues in mathematics that minorities encounter.[^o16]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2020, Harris was selected as part of the inaugural class of [Karen EDGE Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDGE_Foundation).[^o18] In 2019, Harris won the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) Henry L. Alder Award for Distinguished Teaching by a Beginning College or University Mathematics Faculty Member,[^o19] for her mentorship towards undergraduate research and for being a "fierce advocate for a diverse and inclusive mathematics community."[^o20] She further received the early career Faculty Mentor Award from the Council of Undergraduate Research in the Mathematics and Computer Sciences Division.[^o11] She was a 2022 winner of the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Awards for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics).[^o21]

She gave one of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) Invited Addresses at the 2019 [Joint Mathematics Meetings](wikipedia://en/14985143).[^o22] In 2019 she was a featured speaker at the national conference of the [Society for the Advancement of Chicanos/Hispanics and Native Americans in Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_the_Advancement_of_Chicanos/Hispanics_and_Native_Americans_in_Science) (SACNAS).[^o23][^o28][^o24] She was named a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), in the 2022 class of fellows, "for contributions to algebraic combinatorics, for mentorship of undergraduate researchers, and for contributions to a more equitable and inclusive mathematical community".[^o25] In 2022 she will become a fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698), "For exceptional leadership in establishing programs and mentoring networks that support, encourage, and advance women and underrepresented minorities in the mathematical sciences; and for contributions through public speaking that create positive systemic change in the culture and climate of the mathematics profession."[^o26]

In 2018 Harris was featured in the book *[Power in Numbers: The Rebel Women of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/63361034)*.[^o27][^o28]
