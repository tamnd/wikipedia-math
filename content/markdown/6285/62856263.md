# Finite algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Finite algebra"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) $A$ over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $R$ is called **finite** if it is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) as an $R$-[module](wikipedia://en/276410). An $R$-algebra can be thought as a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) of [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) $f: R \to A$, in this case $f$ is called a **finite morphism** if $A$ is a finite $R$-algebra.[^n1]

:::

Being a finite algebra is a stronger condition than being an [algebra of finite type](wikipedia://en/11985661#finite_algebras_vs_algebras_of_finite_type).

## Finite morphisms in algebraic geometry {#Finite_morphisms_in_algebraic_geometry}

This concept is closely related to that of [finite morphism](wikipedia://en/2139357) in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997); in the simplest case of [affine varieties](wikipedia://en/320468), given two affine varieties $V\subseteq\mathbb{A}^n$, $W\subseteq\mathbb{A}^m$ and a [dominant regular map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant_morphism) $\phi: V\to W$, the induced homomorphism of $k$-algebras $\phi^* : \Gamma(W)\to\Gamma(V)$ defined by $\phi^*f=f\circ\phi$ turns $\Gamma(V)$ into a $\Gamma(W)$-algebra:

$\phi$ is a **finite morphism of affine varieties** if $\phi^* : \Gamma(W)\to\Gamma(V)$ is a finite morphism of $k$-algebras.[^n2]

The generalisation to [schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_%28algebraic_geometry%29) can be found in the article on [finite morphisms](wikipedia://en/2139357).
