# Star (graph theory)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Star"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **star** $S_{k}$ is the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{1,\,k}$, that is, it is a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) with one internal node and $k$ leaves. Alternatively, some authors define $S_{k}$ to be the tree of [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_%28graph_theory%29) $k$ with maximum [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) 2, in which case a star of $k > 2$ has $k - 1$ leaves.

:::

A star with 3 edges is called a **claw**.

The star $S_{k}$ is [edge-graceful](wikipedia://en/1821467) when $k$ is even and not when $k$ is odd. It is an [edge-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-transitive) [matchstick graph](wikipedia://en/25730128), and has diameter 2 (when $k > 1$), [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) $\infty$ (it has no cycles), [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) $k$, and [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) 2 (when $k > 0$). Additionally, the star has large automorphism group, namely, the symmetric group on $k$ letters.

Stars may also be described as the only connected graphs in which at most one vertex has [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) greater than one.

## Relation to other graph families {#Relation_to_other_graph_families}

Claws are notable in the definition of [claw-free graphs](wikipedia://en/12415907), graphs that do not have any claw as an [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188).[^o4][^o5] They are also one of the exceptional cases of the [Whitney graph isomorphism theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_graph_isomorphism_theorem): in general, graphs with [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) [line graphs](wikipedia://en/675231) are themselves isomorphic, with the exception of the claw and the triangle $K_{3}$.[^o6]

A star is a special kind of [tree](wikipedia://en/48560). As with any tree, stars may be encoded by a [Prüfer sequence](wikipedia://en/1650168); the Prüfer sequence for a star $K_{1,k}$ consists of $k - 1$ copies of the center vertex.[^o7]

Several [graph invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_invariant) are defined in terms of stars. [Star arboricity](wikipedia://en/2476311) is the minimum number of forests that a graph can be partitioned into such that each tree in each forest is a star,[^o8] and the [star chromatic number](wikipedia://en/20933302) of a graph is the minimum number of colors needed to color its vertices in such a way that every two color classes together form a subgraph in which all connected components are stars.[^o9] The graphs of [branchwidth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branchwidth) 1 are exactly the graphs in which each connected component is a star.[^o10]

## Other applications {#Other_applications}

The set of distances between the vertices of a claw provides an example of a finite [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) that cannot be embedded [isometrically](wikipedia://en/254777) into a [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) of any dimension.[^o12]

The [star network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_network), a [computer network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network) modeled after the star graph, is important in [distributed computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_computing).

A geometric realization of the star graph, formed by identifying the edges with intervals of some fixed length, is used as a local model of curves in [tropical geometry](wikipedia://en/867041). A tropical curve is defined to be a metric space that is locally isomorphic to a star-shaped metric graph.
