# Variational series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Variational series"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **variational series** is a non-decreasing sequence $X_{(1)} \leqslant X_{(2)} \leqslant \cdots \leqslant X_{(n-1)} \leqslant X_{(n)}$composed from an initial series of [independent and identically distributed random variables](wikipedia://en/30876902) $X_1,\ldots,X_n$. The members of the variational series form [order statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_statistics), which form the basis for [nonparametric statistical methods](wikipedia://en/223369).

:::

$X_{(k)}$ is called the *k*th order statistic, while the values $X_{(1)}=\min_{1 \leq k \leq n}{X_k}$ and $X_{(n)}=\max_{1 \leq k \leq n}{X_k}$ (the 1st and $n$th order statistics, respectively) are referred to as the extremal terms.[^o2] The [sample range](wikipedia://en/27588) is given by $R_n = X_{(n)}-X_{(1)}$,[^o2] and the [sample median](wikipedia://en/18837#Medians_for_samples) by $X_{(m+1)}$ when $n=2m+1$ is odd and $(X_{(m+1)} + X_{(m)})/2$ when $n=2m$ is even.

The variational series serves to construct the [empirical distribution function](wikipedia://en/2690455) $\hat{F}(x) = \mu(x)/n$ , where $\mu(x)$ is the number of members of the series which are less than $x$. The empirical distribution $\hat{F}(x)$ serves as an estimate of the true distribution $F(x)$ of the random variables$X_1,\ldots,X_n$, and according to the [Glivenko–Cantelli theorem](wikipedia://en/3828409) converges [almost surely](wikipedia://en/50723#Almost_sure_convergence) to $F(x)$.
