# Nigel Kalton

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nigel Kalton"}

**Nigel John Kalton** (June 20, 1946 – August 31, 2010) was a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom)-[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his contributions to [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180).[^o3]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Kalton was born in [Bromley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromley) and educated at Dulwich College, where he excelled at both mathematics and chess. After studying [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), he received his PhD, which was awarded the Rayleigh Prize for research excellence, from [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) in 1970. He then held positions at Lehigh University in Pennsylvania, Warwick, Swansea, University of Illinois, and Michigan State University, before becoming full professor at the University of Missouri, Columbia, in 1979.[^o3][^o5]

He received the [Stefan Banach Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Banach_Medal) from the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2005.[^o6] A conference in honour of his 60th birthday was held in Miami University of Ohio in 2006.[^o5] He died in [Columbia, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_Missouri), aged 64.
