# Kolmogorov population model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kolmogorov population model"}

*Image: Andrej Nikolajewitsch Kolmogorov.jpg.* [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161)

In [biomathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomathematics), the **Kolmogorov population model**, also known as the **Kolmogorov equations in population dynamics**, is a [mathematical framework](wikipedia://en/20590) developed by [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Andrei Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Kolmogorov) in 1936 that generalizes [predator-prey interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predator-prey_interactions) and [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466). The model was an improvement over earlier predator-prey models, notably the [Lotka–Volterra equations](wikipedia://en/462534), by incorporating more realistic biological assumptions and providing a [qualitative analysis](wikipedia://en/49405695) of population dynamics.[^o2]

:::

## History {#History}

The development of the Kolmogorov population model was influenced by Kolmogorov's early interest in biology during his schoolboy years. Despite being primarily known for his contributions to [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), Kolmogorov made several large contributions to [biomathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomathematics).[^o4] The model was particularly inspired by the work of [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_people) [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) [Vito Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Volterra), who had developed his predator-prey equations based on observations of fish populations in the [Adriatic Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adriatic_Sea) during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I). Volterra's work showed that during the war, when fishing was reduced due to military activities, the proportion of predator fish increased while prey fish decreased.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Kolmogorov population model is expressed as a system of [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations)

$$\dot{x} = xS(x,y)$$

$$\dot{y} = yW(x,y)$$

where $x$ represents the prey population, $y$ represents the predator population, and $S$ and $W$ are [continuously differentiable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuously_differentiable_function) describing the growth rates of the respective populations. The rates of population change decrease as predator numbers increase:

$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial y} < 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{\partial W}{\partial y} < 0.$$

The system must admit invasion by predators when prey is present in isolation; that is, $W(K,0) > 0$, where $K$ represents the [carrying capacity](wikipedia://en/47544) of the prey population.[^o8]

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

The Kolmogorov model addresses a limitation of the [Volterra equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volterra_equations) by imposing self-limiting growth in prey populations, preventing unrealistic exponential growth scenarios. It also provides a predictive model for the qualitative behavior of predator-prey systems without requiring explicit functional forms for the interaction terms.[^o11] The model's contributions to theoretical ecology were not immediately recognized, with significant appreciation only emerging in the 1960s through the work of American [ecologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecologist) [Michael Rosenzweig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Rosenzweig) and [Robert H. MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._MacArthur). Their research demonstrated how the model can be used to understand non-transitory [oscillations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillations) in ecological systems and the conditions for [local stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_stability) of predator-prey interactions.[^o12]

Recent research has shown that Kolmogorov systems can exhibit complex behaviors, including the existence of [strange attractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_attractors) and robust permanent subsystems, implying that even deterministic predator-prey interactions can lead to unpredictable long-term dynamics.[^o13]
