# Tate's thesis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tate's thesis"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Tate's thesis** is the 1950 [PhD thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) of [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) completed under the supervision of [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). In it, Tate used a translation invariant integration on the locally compact group of [ideles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idele) to lift the [zeta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeta_function) twisted by a [Hecke character](wikipedia://en/3149443), i.e. a Hecke [L-function](wikipedia://en/245283), of a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) to a zeta integral and study its properties. Using [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), more precisely the [Poisson summation formula](wikipedia://en/502042), he proved the [functional equation](wikipedia://en/364377) and [meromorphic continuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meromorphic_continuation) of the zeta integral and the Hecke L-function. He also located the poles of the twisted zeta function.

:::

Tate's work can be viewed as an elegant and powerful reformulation of a work of [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990) on the proof of the functional equation of the Hecke L-function. Hecke had used a generalized [theta series](wikipedia://en/449745) associated to an algebraic number field and a lattice in its ring of integers.

The thesis was published as a book chapter in 1967.[^n1][^n2]

## Iwasawa–Tate theory {#Iwasawa–Tate_theory}

[Kenkichi Iwasawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenkichi_Iwasawa) independently discovered essentially the same method (without an analog of the local theory in Tate's thesis) during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) and announced it in his 1950 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) paper and his letter to [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084) written in 1952. Hence this theory is often called **Iwasawa–Tate theory**. Iwasawa, in his letter to Dieudonné, derived on several pages not only the [meromorphic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) and [functional equation](wikipedia://en/364377) of the L-function, but also proved finiteness of the class number and [Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002)'s theorem on units as immediate byproducts of the main computation. The theory in positive characteristic was developed one decade earlier by [Ernst Witt](wikipedia://en/2619911), [Wilfried Schmid](wikipedia://en/22473601), and [Oswald Teichmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Teichmüller).

Iwasawa–Tate theory uses several structures which come from [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695); however, it does not use any deep result of class field theory.

## Generalisations {#Generalisations}

In 1972, [Roger Godement](wikipedia://en/971810) and [Hervé Jacquet](wikipedia://en/25600925) extended Iwasawa–Tate theory to the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) GL(*n*, *k*) over an algebraic number field *k*, and to automorphic representations of its adelic group, which formed the foundations of the [Langlands correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_correspondence). Tate's thesis can thus be viewed as the GL(1) case of the work by Godement–Jacquet.
