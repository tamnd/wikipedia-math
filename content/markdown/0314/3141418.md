# Normal surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Normal surface"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **normal surface** is a [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) inside a triangulated [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) that intersects each tetrahedron in several components called **normal disks.** Each normal disk is either a *triangle* which cuts off a vertex of the tetrahedron, or a *quadrilateral* which separates pairs of vertices. In a given tetrahedron there cannot be two quadrilaterals separating different pairs of vertices, since such quadrilaterals would intersect in a line, causing the surface to be self-intersecting.

:::

*Image: Normal\_surface.png.* A normal surface intersects a tetrahedron in (possibly many) triangles (see above left) and quadrilaterals (see above right)

Dually, a normal surface can be considered as a surface that intersects each handle of a given handle structure on the 3-manifold in a prescribed manner, similar to the above.

The concept of a normal surface can be generalized to arbitrary polyhedra. There are also related notions of **almost normal surfaces** and **spun normal surfaces**.

In an almost normal surface, one tetrahedron in the triangulation has a single exceptional piece. This is either an *octagon* that separates pairs of vertices, or an *annulus* that connects two triangles and/or quadrilaterals by a tube.

*Image: AlmostNormalSurface.png.* An example of an octagon and annulus piece in an almost normal surface

The concept of normal surfaces is due to [Hellmuth Kneser](wikipedia://en/1368525), who utilized it in his proof of the [prime decomposition theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_decomposition_%283-manifold%29) for 3-manifolds. Later, [Wolfgang Haken](wikipedia://en/311546) extended and refined the notion to create *normal surface theory*, which forms the basis of many algorithms in 3-manifold theory. The notion of almost normal surfaces is due to [Hyam Rubinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyam_Rubinstein). The notion of spun normal surface is due to [Bill Thurston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Thurston).

[Regina](wikipedia://en/4781110) is software that enumerates normal and almost-normal surfaces in triangulated 3-manifolds, implementing Rubinstein's 3-sphere recognition algorithm, among other functionalities.
