# Belyi's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Belyi's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Belyi's theorem** on [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) states that any [non-singular](wikipedia://en/981915) algebraic curve *C*, defined by [algebraic number](wikipedia://en/1158) coefficients, represents a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) which is a [ramified covering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramified_covering) of the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799), ramified at three points only.

:::

This is a result of [G. V. Belyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._V._Belyi) from 1979. At the time it was considered surprising, and it spurred [Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck) to develop his theory of [dessins d'enfant](wikipedia://en/1585922), which describes non-singular algebraic curves over the algebraic numbers using combinatorial data.

## Quotients of the upper half-plane {#Quotients_of_the_upper_half-plane}

It follows that the Riemann surface in question can be taken to be the quotient

*H*/Γ

(where *H* is the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826) and Γ is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581) in the [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019)) compactified by [cusps](wikipedia://en/6851758). Since the modular group has [non-congruence subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-congruence_subgroup), it is *not* the conclusion that any such curve is a [modular curve](wikipedia://en/669552).

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Belyi function"}

## Belyi functions {#Belyi_functions}

A **Belyi function** is a [holomorphic map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_map) from a compact Riemann surface *S* to the [complex projective line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_projective_line) **P**<sup>1</sup>(**C**) ramified only over three points, which after a [Möbius transformation](wikipedia://en/314493) may be taken to be $\{0, 1, \infty\}$. Belyi functions may be described combinatorially by [dessins d'enfants](wikipedia://en/1585922).

Belyi functions and dessins d'enfants – but not Belyi's theorem – date at least to the work of [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887); he used them in his article (Klein 1879) to study an 11-fold cover of the complex projective line with monodromy group PSL(2,11).[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Belyi's theorem is an [existence theorem](wikipedia://en/251177) for Belyi functions, and has subsequently been much used in the [inverse Galois problem](wikipedia://en/739166).

:::
