# Arithmetic combinatorics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetic combinatorics"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **[arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) combinatorics** is a field in the intersection of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986) and [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147).

:::

## Scope {#Scope}

Arithmetic combinatorics is about combinatorial estimates associated with arithmetic operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division). [Additive combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243392) is the special case when only the operations of addition and subtraction are involved.

[Ben Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_J._Green) explains arithmetic combinatorics in his review of "Additive Combinatorics" by [Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) and [Vu](wikipedia://en/21419416).[^o3]

## Important results {#Important_results}

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Szemerédi's theorem"}

### Szemerédi's theorem {#Szemerédi's_theorem}

[Szemerédi's theorem](wikipedia://en/591703) is a result in arithmetic combinatorics concerning [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389) in subsets of the integers. In 1936, [Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Turán](wikipedia://en/614998) conjectured[^o7] that every set of integers *A* with positive [natural density](wikipedia://en/1819983) contains a *k* term arithmetic progression for every *k*. This conjecture, which became Szemerédi's theorem, generalizes the statement of [van der Waerden's theorem](wikipedia://en/157178).

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Green–Tao theorem and extensions"}

### Green–Tao theorem and extensions {#Green–Tao_theorem_and_extensions}

The [Green–Tao theorem](wikipedia://en/7006166), proved by [Ben Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_J._Green) and [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) in 2004,[^o10] states that the sequence of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) contains arbitrarily long [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389). In other words, there exist arithmetic progressions of primes, with *k* terms, where *k* can be any natural number. The proof is an extension of [Szemerédi's theorem](wikipedia://en/591703).

In 2006, Terence Tao and [Tamar Ziegler](wikipedia://en/47407832) extended the result to cover polynomial progressions.[^o11] More precisely, given any [integer-valued polynomials](wikipedia://en/585797) *P*<sub>1</sub>,..., *P*<sub>*k*</sub> in one unknown *m* all with constant term 0, there are infinitely many integers *x*, *m* such that *x* + *P*<sub>1</sub>(*m*), ..., *x* + *P*<sub>*k*</sub>(*m*) are simultaneously prime. The special case when the polynomials are *m*, 2*m*, ..., *km* implies the previous result that there are length *k* arithmetic progressions of primes.

:::

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Breuillard–Green–Tao theorem"}

### Breuillard–Green–Tao theorem {#Breuillard–Green–Tao_theorem}

The Breuillard–Green–Tao theorem, proved by [Emmanuel Breuillard](wikipedia://en/36434056), [Ben Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_J._Green), and [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) in 2011,[^o14] gives a complete classification of [approximate groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximate_group). This result can be seen as a nonabelian version of [Freiman's theorem](wikipedia://en/2240299), and a generalization of [Gromov's theorem on groups of polynomial growth](wikipedia://en/635483).

:::

::: {#o16 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

If *A* is a set of *N* integers, how large or small can the [sumset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumset)

$$A+A := \{x+y: x,y \in A\},$$

the difference set

$$A-A := \{x-y: x,y \in A\},$$

and the product set

$$A\cdot A := \{xy: x,y \in A\}$$

be, and how are the sizes of these sets related? (Not to be confused: the terms [difference set](wikipedia://en/2992310) and [product set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_set) can have other meanings.)

:::

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extensions"}

## Extensions {#Extensions}

The sets being studied may also be subsets of algebraic structures other than the integers, for example, [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) and [fields](wikipedia://en/10603).[^o19]

:::
