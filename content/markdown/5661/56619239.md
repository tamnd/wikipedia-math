# Sum coloring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sum coloring"}

*Image: Tree sum coloring.svg.* Sum coloring of a tree. The sum of the labels is 11, smaller than could be achieved using only two labels.

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **sum coloring** of a graph is a labeling of its vertices by positive integers, with no two adjacent vertices having equal labels, that minimizes the sum of the labels. The minimum sum that can be achieved is called the **chromatic sum** of the graph. Chromatic sums and sum coloring were introduced by Supowit in 1987 using non-graph-theoretic terminology, and first studied in graph theoretic terms by [Ewa Kubicka](wikipedia://en/56619304) (independently of Supowit) in her 1989 doctoral thesis.

:::

Obtaining the chromatic sum may require using more distinct labels than the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of the graph, and even when the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of a graph is bounded, the number of distinct labels needed to obtain the optimal chromatic sum may be arbitrarily large.

Computing the chromatic sum is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard). However it may be computed in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time) for [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) and [pseudotrees](wikipedia://en/13511542), and in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) for [outerplanar graphs](wikipedia://en/352343). There is a constant-factor [approximation algorithm](wikipedia://en/563105) for [interval graphs](wikipedia://en/363423) and for [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431). The interval graph case remains NP-hard. It is the case arising in Supowit's original application in [VLSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VLSI) design, and also has applications in [scheduling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduling).
