# Olympia Nicodemi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Olympia Nicodemi"}

**Olympia E. Nicodemi** is a mathematician and [mathematics educator](wikipedia://en/326471) whose research interests range from [wavelets](wikipedia://en/50903) to the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220). She was a distinguished teaching professor of mathematics at the [State University of New York at Geneseo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York_at_Geneseo) until 2020, when she retired.

:::

## Career and publications {#Career_and_publications}

Nicodemi did her undergraduate studies at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), and completed her Ph.D. at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester). She joined the faculty at SUNY Geneseo in 1981. She is the author of *Discrete Mathematics: A Bridge to Computer Science and Advanced Mathematics* (West Publishing, 1987) and *An Introduction to Abstract Algebra: With Notes to the Future Teacher* (with Melissa A. Sutherland and Gary W. Towsley, Pearson, 2007).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Nicodemi was one of the 2004 winners of the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Awards for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics), an award given by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) to outstanding mathematics teachers whose effectiveness extends beyond their own campuses. The award particularly cited her role in the growth of the number of mathematics students at her campus, approximately 2/3 of whom were female.
