# Schwarz triangle function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schwarz triangle function"}

*Image: Schwarz triangle function.svg.* The upper half-plane, and the image of the upper half-plane transformed by the Schwarz triangle function with various parameters.

In [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), the **Schwarz triangle function** or **Schwarz s-function** is a function that [conformally maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_mapping) the [upper half plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_half_plane) to a triangle in the upper half plane having lines or circular arcs for edges. The target triangle is not necessarily a [Schwarz triangle](wikipedia://en/2069325), although that is the most mathematically interesting case. When that triangle is a non-overlapping Schwarz triangle, i.e. a [Möbius triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Möbius_triangle), the inverse of the Schwarz triangle function is a [single-valued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-valued) [automorphic function](wikipedia://en/974148) for that triangle's [triangle group](wikipedia://en/2069342). More specifically, it is a [modular function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_function).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formula"}

## Formula {#Formula}

Let *πα*, *πβ*, and *πγ* be the interior angles at the vertices of the triangle in [radians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radians). Each of *α*, *β*, and *γ* may take values between 0 and 1 inclusive. Following Nehari,[^n1] these angles are in clockwise order, with the vertex having angle *πα* at the origin and the vertex having angle *πγ* lying on the real line. The Schwarz triangle function can be given in terms of [hypergeometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_functions) as:

$$s(\alpha, \beta, \gamma; z) = z^{\alpha} \frac{_2 F_1 \left(a', b'; c'; z\right)}{_2 F_1 \left(a, b; c; z\right)}$$

where

*a* = (1−α−β−γ)/2,

*b* = (1−α+β−γ)/2,

*c* = 1−α,

*a*′ = *a* − *c* + 1 = (1+α−β−γ)/2,

*b*′ = *b* − *c* + 1 = (1+α+β−γ)/2, and

*c*′ = 2 − *c* = 1 + α.

This function maps the upper half-plane to a [spherical triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_triangle) if α + β + γ > 1, or a [hyperbolic triangle](wikipedia://en/1283865) if α + β + γ \< 1. When α + β + γ = 1, then the triangle is a Euclidean triangle with straight edges: *a* = 0, $_2 F_1 \left(a, b; c; z\right) = 1$, and the formula reduces to that given by the [Schwarz–Christoffel transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarz–Christoffel_transformation).

### Derivation {#Derivation}

Through the theory of complex [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753) with [regular singular points](wikipedia://en/3166061) and the [Schwarzian derivative](wikipedia://en/555745), the triangle function can be expressed as the quotient of two solutions of a [hypergeometric differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_differential_equation) with real coefficients and singular points at 0, 1 and ∞. By the [Schwarz reflection principle](wikipedia://en/3213223), the reflection group induces an action on the two dimensional space of solutions. On the orientation-preserving normal subgroup, this two-dimensional representation corresponds to the [monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250) of the ordinary differential equation and induces a group of [Möbius transformations](wikipedia://en/314493) on quotients of [hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/2055223).[^n2]

:::

## Singular points {#Singular_points}

This mapping has [regular singular points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_singular_points) at *z* = 0, 1, and ∞, corresponding to the vertices of the triangle with angles πα, πγ, and πβ respectively. At these singular points,[^n3]

$$\begin{aligned} s(0) &= 0, \\[4mu] s(1) &= \frac {\Gamma(1-a')\Gamma(1-b')\Gamma(c')} {\Gamma(1-a)\Gamma(1-b)\Gamma(c)}, \\[8mu] s(\infty) &= \exp\left(i \pi \alpha \right)\frac {\Gamma(1-a')\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c')} {\Gamma(1-a)\Gamma(b')\Gamma(c)}, \end{aligned}$$

where $\Gamma(x)$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316).

Near each singular point, the function may be approximated as

$$\begin{aligned} s_0(z) &= z^\alpha (1+O(z)), \\[6mu] s_1(z) &= (1-z)^\gamma (1+O(1-z)), \\[6mu] s_\infty(z) &= z^\beta (1+O(1/z)), \end{aligned}$$

where $O(x)$ is [big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578).

## Inverse {#Inverse}

When *α, β*, and *γ* are rational, the triangle is a Schwarz triangle. When each of *α, β*, and *γ* are either the reciprocal of an integer or zero, the triangle is a [Möbius triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Möbius_triangle), i.e. a non-overlapping Schwarz triangle. For a Möbius triangle, the inverse is a [modular function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_function).

In the spherical case, that modular function is a [rational function](wikipedia://en/361210). For Euclidean triangles, the inverse can be expressed using [elliptical functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptical_function).[^o17]

## Ideal triangles {#Ideal_triangles}

When *α* = 0 the triangle is degenerate, lying entirely on the real line. If either of *β* or *γ* are non-zero, the angles can be permuted so that the positive value is *α*, but that is not an option for an [ideal triangle](wikipedia://en/5532715) having all angles zero.

Instead, a mapping to an ideal triangle with vertices at 0, 1, and ∞ is given by in terms of the [complete elliptic integral of the first kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_elliptic_integral_of_the_first_kind):

$$i\frac{K(1-z)}{K(z)}.$$

This expression is the inverse of the [modular lambda function](wikipedia://en/27903678).[^n4]

## Extensions {#Extensions}

The [Schwarz–Christoffel transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarz–Christoffel_transformation) gives the mapping from the upper half-plane to any Euclidean polygon.

The methodology used to derive the Schwarz triangle function earlier can be applied more generally to arc-edged polygons. However, for an *n*-sided polygon, the solution has *n* − 3 additional parameters, which are difficult to determine in practice.[^n5] See Schwarzian derivative#Conformal mapping of circular arc polygons for more details.

:::

::: {#o16 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

[L. P. Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._P._Lee) used Schwarz triangle functions to derive [conformal map projections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_map_projection) onto [polyhedral](wikipedia://en/69388599) surfaces.[^o17]

:::
