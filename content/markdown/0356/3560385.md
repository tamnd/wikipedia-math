# Case fatality rate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Case fatality rate"}

In [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997), **case fatality rate** (**CFR**), or sometimes more accurately **case-fatality risk**, is the proportion of people who have been [diagnosed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_diagnosis) with a certain disease and end up [dying of it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cause_of_death). Unlike a disease's [mortality rate](wikipedia://en/191305), the CFR does not take into account the time period between disease onset and death. A CFR is generally expressed as a percentage. It is a measure of disease [lethality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethality), and thus may change with different [treatments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therapy).[^o2] CFRs are most often used for discrete, limited-time courses, such as acute [infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infection).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Terminology"}

## Terminology {#Terminology}

The *[mortality rate](wikipedia://en/191305)* – often confused with the CFR – is a measure of the relative number of deaths (either in general, or due to a specific cause) within the entire population per unit of time.[^o5] A CFR, in contrast, is the number of deaths among the number of diagnosed cases only, regardless of time or total population.[^o6]

From a mathematical point of view, by taking values between 0 and 1 or 0% and 100%, CFRs are actually a measure of [risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk) (**case fatality risk**) – that is, they are a [proportion of incidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidence_proportions), although they do not reflect a [disease's incidence](wikipedia://en/167008). They are neither [rates](wikipedia://en/332372), [incidence rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidence_rate), nor [ratios](wikipedia://en/87837) (none of which are limited to the range 0–1). They do not take into account time from disease onset to death.[^o7][^o8]

Sometimes the term **case fatality ratio** is used interchangeably with *case fatality rate*, but they are not the same. A case fatality ratio is a comparison between two different case fatality rates, expressed as a ratio. It is used to compare the severity of different diseases or to assess the impact of interventions.[^o9]

Because the CFR is not an [incidence rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidence_rate) by not measuring frequency, some authors note that a more appropriate term is **case fatality proportion**.[^o10]

:::

## Example calculation {#Example_calculation}

If 100 people in a community are diagnosed with the same disease, and 9 of them subsequently die from the effects of the disease, the CFR would be 9%. If some of the cases have not yet resolved (neither died nor fully recovered) at the time of analysis, a later analysis might take into account additional deaths and arrive at a higher estimate of the CFR, if the unresolved cases were included as recovered in the earlier analysis. Alternatively, it might later be established that a higher number of people were subclinically infected with the pathogen, resulting in an IFR below the CFR.

A CFR may only be calculated from cases that have been resolved through either death or recovery. The preliminary CFR, for example, of a newly occurring disease with a high daily increase and long resolution time would be substantially lower than the final CFR, if unresolved cases were not excluded from the calculation, but added to the denominator only.

$\text{CFR in }{\%} = \frac\text{Number of deaths from disease}\text{Number of confirmed cases of disease}\times100$[^o12]

::: {#o14 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Infection fatality rate"}

## Infection fatality rate {#Infection_fatality_rate}

Like the case fatality rate, the term **infection fatality rate** (IFR) also applies to [infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease), but represents the proportion of deaths among all infected individuals, including all [asymptomatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptomatic) and undiagnosed subjects. It is closely related to the CFR, but attempts to additionally account for inapparent infections among healthy people.[^o15] The IFR differs from the CFR in that it aims to estimate the fatality rate in both sick and healthy infected: the detected disease (cases) and those with an undetected disease (asymptomatic and not tested group).[^o16] Individuals who are infected, but show no symptoms, are said to have [*inapparent*, *silent* or *subclinical*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subclinical_infection) infections and may inadvertently infect others. By definition, the IFR cannot exceed the CFR, because the former adds asymptomatic cases to its denominator.

$\text{IFR in }{\%} = \frac\text{Number of deaths from disease}\text{Number of infected individuals}\times100$[^o12]

:::

::: {#o18 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Some examples will suggest the range of possible CFRs for diseases in the real world:

- The CFR for the [Spanish (1918) flu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1918_flu_pandemic) was greater than 2.5%, while the [Asian (1957–58)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_flu) and [Hong Kong (1968–69) flus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_flu) both had a CFR of about 0.2%.[^o19][^o20][^o21]
- As of , [coronavirus disease 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_disease_2019) has an overall CFR of %, although the CFRs of earlier strains of COVID-19 was around 2%, the CFRs for original [SARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS) and [MERS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MERS) are about 11% and 34%, respectively.[^o23][^o24]
- The CFR for [yellow fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_fever) is about 5-6% (but 40-50% in severe cases).[^o25][^o26][^o27]
- [Legionnaires' disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legionnaires'_disease) has a CFR of about 15%.[^o28]
- Left untreated, [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague) will have a CFR of up to 60%.[^o29] With antibiotic treatment, the CFR for bubonic plague is 17%, [pneumonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonic_plague) 29% and [septicaemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septicaemic_plague) 45%.[^o30][^o31]
- Active [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis), the infection with the highest mortality rate, has a CFR of 43% in the absence of [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV).[^o32][^o33]
- [Ebola virus disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_virus_disease), one of the infections with the highest lethality, has a CFR as high as 90%.[^o34]
- [Naegleriasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_amoebic_meningoencephalitis) (also known as primary amoebic meningoencephalitis), has a CFR greater than 95%, with a few of the survivors having been treated with [heroic doses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroic_measure) of amphotericin B and other [off-label drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-label_use).[^o35]
- [Rabies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabies) has a CFR greater than 99% in unvaccinated individuals.[^o36] A few people have survived either by being vaccinated (but [after symptoms started, or else later than ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-exposure_prophylaxis)), or more recently, by being put into a medically induced coma.
