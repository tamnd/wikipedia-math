# Whitehead link

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Whitehead link"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **Whitehead link**, named for [J. H. C. Whitehead](wikipedia://en/385179), is one of the most basic [links](wikipedia://en/1161784). It can be drawn as an [alternating link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_link) with five crossings, from the overlay of a circle and a [figure-eight shaped](wikipedia://en/5210590) loop.

:::

## Structure {#Structure}

A common way of describing this knot is formed by overlaying a [figure-eight shaped](wikipedia://en/5210590) loop with another circular loop surrounding the crossing of the figure-eight. The above-below relation between these two [unknots](wikipedia://en/60075) is then set as an [alternating link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_link), with the consecutive crossings on each loop alternating between under and over. This drawing has five crossings, one of which is the self-crossing of the figure-eight curve, which does not count towards the [linking number](wikipedia://en/451999). Because the remaining crossings have equal numbers of under and over crossings on each loop, its linking number is 0. It is not [isotopic](wikipedia://en/212250#Isotopy) to the [unlink](wikipedia://en/6718898), but it is [link homotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_homotopic) to the unlink.

Although this construction of the knot treats its two loops differently from each other, the two loops are topologically symmetric: it is possible to deform the same link into a drawing of the same type in which the loop that was drawn as a figure eight is circular and vice versa. Alternatively, there exist realizations of this knot in three dimensions in which the two loops can be taken to each other by a geometric symmetry of the realization.

In [braid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braid_theory) notation, the link is written

$$\sigma^2_1\sigma^2_2\sigma^{-1}_1\sigma^{-2}_2.\,$$

Its [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) is

$$\Delta (t)=t^{3/2}-3t^{1/2}+3t^{-1/2}-t^{-3/2},$$

since $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -1\end{pmatrix}$ is a possible [Seifert matrix](wikipedia://en/1343951), or because of its [Conway polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander-Conway_polynomial), which is

$$\nabla(z)=z^3 .$$

Its [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642) is

$$V(t) = t^{-{3 \over 2}}\left(-1 + t - 2t^2 + t^3 - 2t^4 + t^5\right).$$

This polynomial and $V(1/t)$ are the two factors of the Jones polynomial of the [L10a140 link](wikipedia://en/31520188). Notably, $V(1/t)$ is the Jones polynomial for the mirror image of a link having Jones polynomial $V(t)$.

## Volume {#Volume}

The [hyperbolic volume](wikipedia://en/8158628) of the complement of the Whitehead link is $4$ times [Catalan's constant](wikipedia://en/46860), approximately 3.66. The Whitehead link complement is one of two two-cusped hyperbolic manifolds with the minimum possible volume, the other being the complement of the [pretzel link](wikipedia://en/1286130) with parameters $(-2, 3, 8)$.

Dehn filling on one component of the Whitehead link can produce the sibling manifold of the complement of the [figure-eight knot](wikipedia://en/59565), and Dehn filling on both components can produce the [Weeks manifold](wikipedia://en/4201216), respectively one of the minimum-volume hyperbolic manifolds with one cusp and the minimum-volume hyperbolic manifold with no cusps.

## History {#History}

*Image: Torshammare Ödeshög (Montelius 1906 s309).jpg.* Old [Thor's hammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mjölnir) archaeological artefact

The Whitehead link is named for [J. H. C. Whitehead](wikipedia://en/385179), who spent much of the 1930s looking for a proof of the [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798). In 1934, he used the link as part of his construction of the now-named [Whitehead manifold](wikipedia://en/1192013), which refuted his previous purported proof of the conjecture.[^o6]
