# Whitney umbrella

*Image: Whitney\_unbrella.png.* Section of the surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Whitney umbrella"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Whitney umbrella** or **Whitney's umbrella**, named after American mathematician [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794), and sometimes called a **Cayley umbrella**, is a specific self-intersecting [ruled surface](wikipedia://en/705158) placed in [three dimensions](wikipedia://en/3054853). It is the [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of all [straight lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_line) that pass through points of a fixed [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231) and are [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to a fixed straight line which is parallel to the axis of the parabola and lies on its perpendicular [bisecting](wikipedia://en/152547) plane.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formulas"}

## Formulas {#Formulas}

Whitney's umbrella can be given by the [parametric equations](wikipedia://en/576108) in [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates)

$$\left\{\begin{aligned} x(u, v) &= uv, \\ y(u, v) &= u, \\ z(u, v) &= v^2, \end{aligned}\right.$$

where the parameters *u* and *v* range over the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438). It is also given by the [implicit equation](wikipedia://en/250896)

$$x^2 - y^2 z = 0.$$

This formula also includes the negative *z* axis (which is called the *handle* of the umbrella).

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Whitney\_umbrella.gif.* Whitney umbrella as a ruled surface, generated by a moving straight line

*Image: Whitney umbrella.jpg.* Whitney umbrella made with a single string inside a plastic cube

Whitney's umbrella is a [ruled surface](wikipedia://en/705158) and a [right conoid](wikipedia://en/23756785). It is important in the field of [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763), as a simple [local](wikipedia://en/1462457) model of a [pinch point](wikipedia://en/19426773) [singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity). The pinch point and the [fold singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fold_catastrophe) are the only [stable local singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_singularity) of maps from **R**<sup>2</sup> to **R**<sup>3</sup>.

It is named after the [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794).

In [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305), a [Whitney brane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_brane) is a D7-brane wrapping a variety whose singularities are locally modeled by the Whitney umbrella. Whitney branes appear naturally when taking Sen's weak coupling limit of [F-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-theory).
