# Multiresolution analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multiresolution analysis"}

A **multiresolution analysis** (**MRA**) or **multiscale approximation** (**MSA**) is the design method of most of the practically relevant [discrete wavelet transforms](wikipedia://en/857564) (DWT) and the justification for the [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) of the [fast wavelet transform](wikipedia://en/1558844) (FWT). It was introduced in this context in 1988/89 by [Stephane Mallat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephane_Mallat) and [Yves Meyer](wikipedia://en/2096465) and has predecessors in the [microlocal analysis](wikipedia://en/6108841) in the theory of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) (the *ironing method*) and the [pyramid methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid_%28image_processing%29) of [image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_processing) as introduced in 1981/83 by Peter J. Burt, Edward H. Adelson and [James L. Crowley](http://www-prima.inrialpes.fr/Prima/Homepages/jlc/jlc.html).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A multiresolution analysis of the [Lebesgue space](wikipedia://en/45194) $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ consists of a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of nested [subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357)

$$\{0\}\subset \dots\subset V_1\subset V_0\subset V_{-1}\subset\dots\subset V_{-n}\subset V_{-(n+1)}\subset\dots\subset L^2(\mathbb{R})$$

that satisfies certain [self-similarity](wikipedia://en/28782) relations in time-space and scale-frequency, as well as [completeness](wikipedia://en/7832) and regularity relations.

- *Self-similarity* in *time* demands that each subspace *V<sub>k</sub>* is invariant under shifts by [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) [multiples](wikipedia://en/3912709) of *2<sup>k</sup>*. That is, for each $f\in V_k,\; m\in\mathbb{Z}$ the function *g* defined as $g(x)=f(x-m2^{k})$ also contained in $V_k$.
- *Self-similarity* in *scale* demands that all subspaces $V_k\subset V_l,\; k>l,$ are time-scaled versions of each other, with [scaling](wikipedia://en/651752) respectively [dilation](wikipedia://en/1591770) factor 2<sup>*k-l*</sup>. I.e., for each $f\in V_k$ there is a $g\in V_l$ with $\forall x\in\mathbb{R}:\;g(x)=f(2^{k-l}x)$.
- In the sequence of subspaces, for *k*>*l* the space resolution 2<sup>*l*</sup> of the *l*-th subspace is higher than the resolution 2<sup>*k*</sup> of the *k*-th subspace.
- *Regularity* demands that the model [subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) *V<sub>0</sub>* be generated as the [linear hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_hull) ([algebraically](wikipedia://en/3129) or even [topologically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically_closed)) of the integer shifts of one or a finite number of generating functions $\phi$ or $\phi_1,\dots,\phi_r$. Those integer shifts should at least form a frame for the subspace $V_0\subset L^2(\mathbb{R})$, which imposes certain conditions on the decay at [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824). The generating functions are also known as **[scaling functions](wikipedia://en/50903#Scaling_function)** or **[father wavelets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father_wavelets)**. In most cases one demands of those functions to be [piecewise continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise_continuous) with [compact support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support).
- *Completeness* demands that those nested subspaces fill the whole space, i.e., their union should be [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, and that they are not too redundant, i.e., their [intersection](wikipedia://en/41780044) should only contain the [zero element](wikipedia://en/3476379).

:::

## Important conclusions {#Important_conclusions}

In the case of one continuous (or at least with bounded variation) compactly supported scaling function with orthogonal shifts, one may make a number of deductions. The proof of existence of this class of functions is due to [Ingrid Daubechies](wikipedia://en/1430989).

Assuming the scaling function has compact support, then $V_0\subset V_{-1}$ implies that there is a finite sequence of coefficients $a_k=2 \langle\phi(x),\phi(2x-k)\rangle$ for $|k|\leq N$, and $a_k=0$ for $|k|>N$, such that

$$\phi(x)=\sum_{k=-N}^N a_k\phi(2x-k).$$

Defining another function, known as **mother wavelet** or just **the wavelet**

$$\psi(x):=\sum_{k=-N}^N (-1)^k a_{1-k}\phi(2x-k),$$

one can show that the space $W_0\subset V_{-1}$, which is defined as the (closed) linear hull of the mother wavelet's integer shifts, is the orthogonal complement to $V_0$ inside $V_{-1}$.[^o5] Or put differently, $V_{-1}$ is the [orthogonal sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_direct_sum) (denoted by $\oplus$) of $W_0$ and $V_0$. By self-similarity, there are scaled versions $W_k$ of $W_0$ and by completeness one has

$$L^2(\mathbb{R})=\mbox{closure of }\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}W_k,$$

thus the set

$$\{\psi_{k,n}(x)=\sqrt2^{-k}\psi(2^{-k}x-n):\;k,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}$$

is a countable complete [orthonormal wavelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthonormal_wavelet) basis in $L^2(\mathbb{R})$.
