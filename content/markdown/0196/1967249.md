# Circular sector

*Image: Circle arc.svg.* The minor sector is shaded in green while the major sector is shaded white.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Circular sector"}

A **circular sector**, also known as **circle sector** or **disk sector** or simply a **sector** (symbol: **⌔**), is the portion of a [disk](wikipedia://en/329542) (a [closed region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_region) bounded by a circle) enclosed by two [radii](wikipedia://en/1780815) and an [arc](wikipedia://en/5578920), with the smaller [area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_%28geometry%29) being known as the *minor sector* and the larger being the *major sector*.[^o2] In the diagram, $\theta$ is the [central angle](wikipedia://en/462705), $r$ the radius of the circle, and $L$ is the arc length of the minor sector.

:::

## Types {#Types}

A sector with the central angle of 180° is called a *[half-disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disk_%28geometry%29)* and is bounded by a [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) and a [semicircle](wikipedia://en/617831). Sectors with other central angles are sometimes given special names, such as **quadrants** (90°), **sextants** (60°), and **octants** (45°), which come from the sector being one quarter, sixth or eighth part of a full circle, respectively.

## Area {#Area}

The total area of a circle is $\pi r2$. The area of a sector in terms of $L$ can be obtained by multiplying the total area $\pi r2$ by the ratio of $L$ to the total perimeter $2\pi r$.

$$A = \pi r^2\, \frac{L}{2\pi r} = \frac{rL}{2}$$

The area of the sector can be obtained by multiplying the circle's area by the ratio of the angle $\theta$ (expressed in radians) and $2\pi$ (because the area of the sector is directly proportional to its angle, and $2\pi$ is the angle for the whole circle, in radians):

$$A = \pi r^2\, \frac{\theta}{2 \pi} = \frac{r^2 \theta}{2}$$

Another approach is to consider this area as the result of the following integral:

$$A = \int_0^\theta\int_0^r dA = \int_0^\theta\int_0^r \tilde{r}\, d\tilde{r}\, d\tilde{\theta} = \int_0^\theta \frac 1 2 r^2\, d\tilde{\theta} = \frac{r^2 \theta}{2}$$

Converting the central angle into [degrees](wikipedia://en/1195294) gives[^o5]

$$A = \pi r^2 \frac{\theta^\circ}{360^\circ}$$

## Arc length {#Arc_length}

The formula for the length of an arc is:[^o7]

$$L = r \theta$$

where $L$ represents the arc length, $r$ represents the radius of the circle and $\theta$ represents the angle in radians made by the arc at the centre of the circle.[^o8]

If the value of angle is given in degrees, then we can also use the following formula by:[^n1]

$$L = 2 \pi r \frac{\theta}{360^\circ}$$

## Perimeter {#Perimeter}

The length of the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of a sector is the sum of the arc length and the two radii:

$$P = L + 2r = \theta r + 2r = r (\theta + 2)$$

where $\theta$ is in radians.

## Chord length {#Chord_length}

The length of a [chord](wikipedia://en/297947) formed with the extremal points of the arc is given by

$$C = 2r\sin\frac{\theta}{2}$$

where $C$ represents the chord length, $r$ represents the radius of the circle, and $\theta$ represents the angular width of the sector in radians.
