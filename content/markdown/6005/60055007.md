# Lyapunov dimension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lyapunov dimension"}

In the [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), the concept of **Lyapunov dimension** was suggested by [Kaplan and Yorke](wikipedia://en/44357173)[^o2] for estimating the [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) of [attractors](wikipedia://en/191094). Further the concept has been developed and rigorously justified in a number of papers, and nowadays various different approaches to the definition of Lyapunov dimension are used. Attractors with noninteger Hausdorff dimension are called [strange attractors](wikipedia://en/191094#Strange_attractor).[^o3] Since the direct [numerical computation](wikipedia://en/21506) of the Hausdorff dimension of attractors is often a problem of high numerical complexity, estimations via the Lyapunov dimension became widely spread. The Lyapunov dimension was named[^o4] after the Russian mathematician [Aleksandr Lyapunov](wikipedia://en/65425) because of the close connection with the [Lyapunov exponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyapunov_exponents).

:::

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Consider a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) $\big(\{\varphi^t\}_{t\geq0}, (U\subseteq \mathbb{R}^n, \|\cdot\|)\big)$, where $\varphi^t$ is the [shift operator](wikipedia://en/467384) along the solutions: $\varphi^t(u_0) = u(t,u_0)$, of [ODE](wikipedia://en/32742753) $\dot{u} = f({u})$, $t \leq 0$, or difference equation ${u}(t+1) = f({u}(t))$, $t=0,1,...$, with continuously differentiable vector-function $f$. Then $D\varphi^t(u)$ is the [fundamental matrix of solutions](wikipedia://en/10125619) of linearized system and denote by $\sigma_i(t,u) = \sigma_i(D\varphi^t(u)), \ i = 1...n$, [singular values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_values) with respect to their [algebraic multiplicity](wikipedia://en/2161429#Algebraic_multiplicity), ordered by decreasing for any $u$ and $t$.

### Definition via finite-time Lyapunov dimension {#Definition_via_finite-time_Lyapunov_dimension}

The concept of **finite-time Lyapunov dimension** and related definition of the Lyapunov dimension, developed in the works by [N. Kuznetsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_V._Kuznetsov),[^o8][^o9] is convenient for the numerical experiments where only finite time can be observed. Consider an analog of the [Kaplan–Yorke formula](wikipedia://en/44357173) for the finite-time Lyapunov exponents:

$$d_{\rm KY}(\{ {\rm LE}_i(t,u)\}_{i=1}^n)=j(t,u) + \frac{ {\rm LE}_1(t,u) + \cdots + {\rm LE}_{j(t,u)}(t,u)}{| {\rm LE}_{j(t,u)+1}(t,u)|},$$

$$j(t,u) = \max\{m: \sum_{i=1}^m {\rm LE}_i(t,u) \geq 0\},$$

with respect to the ordered set of *finite-time Lyapunov exponents* $\{{\rm LE}_i(t,u)\}_{i=1}^n = \{\frac{1}{t}\ln\sigma_i(t,u)\}_{i=1}^n$ at the point $u$. The *finite-time Lyapunov dimension* of dynamical system with respect to [invariant set](wikipedia://en/29763944) $K$ is defined as follows

$$\dim_{\rm L}(t, K) = \sup\limits_{u \in K} d_{\rm KY}(\{{\rm LE}_i(t,u)\}_{i=1}^n).$$

In this approach the use of the analog of Kaplan–Yorke formula is rigorously justified by the Douady–Oesterlè theorem,[^o10] which proves that for any fixed $t > 0$ the *finite-time Lyapunov dimension* for a closed bounded invariant set $K$ is an upper estimate of the Hausdorff dimension:

$$\dim_{\rm H} K \leq \dim_{\rm L}(t, K).$$

Looking for best such estimation $\inf_{t>0} \dim_{\rm L} (t, K) = \liminf_{t \to +\infty}\sup\limits_{u \in K} \dim_{\rm L}(t,u)$, *the Lyapunov dimension* is defined as follows:[^o8][^o9]

$$\dim_{\rm L} K = \liminf_{t \to +\infty}\sup\limits_{u \in K} \dim_{\rm L}(t,u).$$

The possibilities of changing the order of the time limit and the [supremum](wikipedia://en/39382) over set is discussed, e.g., in.[^o11][^o12]

Note that the above defined Lyapunov dimension is invariant under Lipschitz [diffeomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphisms).[^o8][^o13]

#### Exact Lyapunov dimension {#Exact_Lyapunov_dimension}

Let the Jacobian matrix $Df(u_\text{eq})$ at one of the equilibria have simple real eigenvalues: $\{\lambda_i(u_\text{eq})\}_{i=1}^n, \lambda_{i}(u_\text{eq}) \geq \lambda_{i+1}(u_\text{eq})$, then

$$\dim_{\rm L}u_\text{eq} = d_{\rm KY}(\{\lambda_i(u_\text{eq})\}_{i=1}^n).$$

If the supremum of local Lyapunov dimensions on the global attractor, which involves all equilibria, is achieved at an [equilibrium point](wikipedia://en/1441435), then this allows one to get analytical formula of the exact Lyapunov dimension of the global attractor (see corresponding [Eden’s conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden’s_conjecture)).

### Definition via statistical physics approach and ergodicity {#Definition_via_statistical_physics_approach_and_ergodicity}

Following the [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics) approach and assuming the [ergodicity](wikipedia://en/5456824) the Lyapunov dimension of attractor is estimated[^o2] by limit value of the local Lyapunov dimension $\lim_{t\to+\infty}\dim_{\rm L} (t, u_0)$ of a *typical* trajectory, which belongs to the attractor. In this case $\{\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}{\rm LE}_i(t,u_0)\}_{i}^n = \{ {\rm LE}_i(u_0)\}_1^n$ and $\dim_{\rm L}u_0= d_{\rm KY}(\{ {\rm LE}_i(u_0)\}_{i=1}^n)=j(u_0) + \frac{ {\rm LE}_1(u_0) + \cdots + {\rm LE}_{j(u_0)}(u_0)}{| {\rm LE}_{j(u_0)+1}(u_0)|}$. From a practical point of view, the rigorous use of [ergodic Oseledec theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oseledec_theorem), verification that the considered trajectory $u(t,u_0)$ is a *typical* trajectory, and the use of corresponding [Kaplan–Yorke formula](wikipedia://en/44357173) is a challenging task (see, e.g. discussions in[^o16]). The exact limit values of finite-time Lyapunov exponents, if they exist and are the same for all $u_0 \in U$, are called the *absolute* ones[^o4] $\{\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}{\rm LE}_i(t,u_0)\}_{i}^n = \{ {\rm LE}_i(u_0)\}_1^n \equiv \{ {\rm LE}_i \}_1^n$ and used in the [Kaplan–Yorke formula](wikipedia://en/44357173). Examples of the rigorous use of the [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986) for the computation of the Lyapunov exponents and dimension can be found in.[^o17][^o18][^o19]
