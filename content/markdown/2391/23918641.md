# Rehuel Lobatto

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rehuel Lobatto"}

*Image: RehuelLabatto.jpg.* Rehuel Lobatto

*Image: Delft34.JPG.* Plaque in Delft

**Rehuel Lobatto** (6 June 1797 – 9 February 1866 [^o3] ) was a [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). The [Gauss-Lobatto quadrature method](wikipedia://en/50579#Gauss–Lobatto_rules) is named after him, as are [his variants](wikipedia://en/13454825#Lobatto_methods) on the [Runge–Kutta methods](wikipedia://en/213671) for solving [ODEs](wikipedia://en/32742753), and the [Lobatto polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718). [^o2] He was the author of a great number of articles in scientific periodicals, as well as various schoolbooks.

:::

Lobatto was born in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam) to a [Portuguese Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jews_in_the_Netherlands) family. As a schoolboy Lobatto already displayed remarkable talent for mathematics. [^o3] He studied mathematics under [Jean Henri van Swinden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Henri_van_Swinden) at the [Athenaeum Illustre of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenaeum_Illustre_of_Amsterdam), earning his [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1812; and then with [Adolphe Quetelet](wikipedia://en/184215) (coediting a volume of ["Correspondance Mathématique et Physique"](https://books.google.com/books?id=ndhSAAAAcAAJ)).

Working for the Dutch government - initially for the [Ministry of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_and_Kingdom_Relations) - he became secretary of a statistical commission in 1831.[^o5] From 1826 till 1849 he was editor of the *"Jaarboekje van Lobatto"*, as those editions of the official annual of statistics came to be known. In 1841 he was appointed by Minister [Rochussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Jacob_Rochussen) as a member of a commission for the conversion of the public debt. In 1842 he was appointed Professor [^o6] of Higher Mathematics [^o7] at the [Polytechnical School of Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnical_School_of_Delft), remaining there until his final years.

In 1836 he became member of the Royal Institute, predecessor of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o7] The [Order of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion) was conferred upon him; he received his Doctoral degree *[honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa)* from [Groningen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groningen_University). [^o3]
