# Nathaniel Nye

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nathaniel Nye"}

*Image: Wenceslas Hollar - Nathaniel Nye.jpg.* A 1644 engraving of Nathaniel Nye by [Wenceslas Hollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslas_Hollar)

**Nathaniel Nye** (baptised 1624 – after 1647) was an [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), [cartographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartographer) and [gunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_support_weapon).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: Nye-Almanacke.jpg.* Frontispiece of Nye's *New Almanacke and Prognostication* for 1642

Nye was baptised in [St Martin in the Bull Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Martin_in_the_Bull_Ring), [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham) on 18 April 1624, and was probably the son of a governor of the town's [King Edward's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward's_School,_Birmingham).[^o3]

In 1642 he published *A New Almanacke and Prognostication calculated exactly for the faire and populous Towne of Birmicham in Warwickshire, where the Pole is elevated above the Horizon 52 degrees and 38 minutes, and may serve for any part of this Kingdome*, in which he described himself as a "Practitioner of Astronomy".[^o4] The dedication to this book suggests that he must have issued an earlier [almanac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almanac) in 1640, possibly from [Arnhem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem) in the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands), when he would have been aged 17.[^o5] A further almanac was published in 1643, in which he was described as "Mathematitian, Practitioner of Astronomy", and two more were forthcoming in 1645.[^o3]

Nye also developed an interest in guns – Birmingham's principal trade during the [English Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War) – and he is recorded as testing a Birmingham cannon in 1643 and experimenting with a [saker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saker_%28cannon%29) in [Deritend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deritend) in 1645.[^o3] From 1645 he was the master gunner to the [Parliamentarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundhead) garrison at [Evesham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evesham) and in 1646 he successfully directed the artillery at the [Siege of Worcester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Worcester), detailing his experiences and in his 1647 book *The Art of Gunnery*.[^o3] Believing that war was as much a science as an art,[^o6] his explanations focused on [triangulation](wikipedia://en/207174), [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118), theoretical mathematics,[^o7] and [cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography)[^o3] as well as practical considerations such as the ideal specification for [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder) or [slow matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_match).[^o8] His book acknowledged mathematicians such as [Robert Recorde](wikipedia://en/310247) and [Marcus Jordanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Jordanus) as well as earlier military writers on artillery such as [Niccolò Tartaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccolò_Tartaglia) and [Thomas Malthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Malthus).[^o3]

Nothing is known of Nye's life after 1647, though further editions of *The Art of Gunnery* were produced in 1648 and 1670.[^o3]
