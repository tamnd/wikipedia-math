# Plane symmetry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Plane symmetry"}

A **plane symmetry** is a symmetry of a pattern in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410): that is, a transformation of the plane that carries any direction lines to lines and preserves many different distances.[^o2] If one has a pattern in the plane, the set of plane symmetries that preserve the pattern forms a [group](wikipedia://en/19447). The groups that arise in this way are **plane symmetry groups** and are of considerable mathematical interest.

:::

There are several kinds of plane symmetry groups:

- [Reflection groups](wikipedia://en/2422023). These are plane symmetry groups that are generated by reflections, possibly limited to reflections in lines through the origin.
- [Rotation groups](wikipedia://en/508177). These groups consist of rotations around a point.
- [Translation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_group).
- Symmetries of [geometrical figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrical_figure). Some of these are reflection groups, e.g., the group of symmetries of the [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) or the [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048). The symmetry group of the [flag of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Hong_Kong) or any similar figure without an [axis of symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_symmetry) is a rotation group.
