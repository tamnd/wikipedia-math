# Von Neumann's elephant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Von Neumann's elephant"}

*Image: FermiNeumannElephant (2010).png.* Fermi–von Neumann elephant (4 complex parameters, 2010)

In [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446), **von Neumann's elephant** is a problem of constructing a planar curve in the shape of an [elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant) from only four fixed parameters. It originated from a discussion between physicists [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) and [Enrico Fermi](wikipedia://en/10264), and the expression is used in physics to characterize a model with so many parameters that it is [overfit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overfit), will consequently match any set of experimental data, and therefore is [unfalsifiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfalsifiable) and unscientific.

:::

## History {#History}

In a 2004 article in the journal *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29)*, [Freeman Dyson](wikipedia://en/11397) recounts his meeting with Fermi in 1953. Fermi, while discussing a novel theory Dyson was proposing, offered the harsh critique: "There are two ways of doing calculations in theoretical physics. One way ... is to have a clear physical picture of the process that you are calculating. The other way is to have a precise and self-consistent mathematical formalism. You have neither." Dyson countered by stating that his theory matched Fermi's own data. Fermi asked about a number of arbitrary parameters Dyson used and, upon learning that there were four of them, quoted his friend von Neumann:[^o3]

By this he meant that the Dyson's simulations relied on too many [free parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_parameters), presupposing an [overfitting](wikipedia://en/173332) phenomenon. "Stunned" Dyson agreed with the argument, finished the set of articles in order for his students to get their names into the research journals, and switched to another field of study.[^o3]

The phrase became popular enough to be used in the titles of unrelated research works, like "There is More Than One Way to Model an Elephant. Experiment-Driven Modeling of the Actin Cytoskeleton".[^o4]

## Construction {#Construction}

Over time, solving the problem (defining four complex numbers to draw an elephantine shape) became a topic in [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446). A 1975 attempt through [least-squares function approximation](wikipedia://en/34561385) required dozens of terms.[^o7] An approximation using four parameters was found by three physicists in 2010.[^o8]

The construction is based on complex [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659).[^o8]

The curve found in 2010 is parameterized by

$$\begin{aligned} x(t) &= -60\cos t + 30\sin t - 8\sin 2t + 10\sin 3t, \\ y(t) &= 50\sin t + 18\sin 2t - 12\cos 3t + 14\cos 5t. \end{aligned}$$

The four fixed parameters used are complex, with affixes $z_{1} = 50 - 30i$, $z_{2} = 18 + 8i$, $z_{3} = 12 - 10i$, $z_{4} = -14 - 60i$. The affix point $z_{5} = 40 + 20i$ is added to make the eye of the elephant, and this value also serves as a parameter for the movement of the "trunk".[^o9][^o8]

:::

## Other von Neumann elephants {#Other_von_Neumann_elephants}

Von Neumann also used the word "elephant" as a synonym for [linearities](wikipedia://en/722503) and [equilibrium points](wikipedia://en/1441435): elephants, equilibria, and linear systems are all equally rare in nature, thus statements about them are nontrivial and the corresponding theories meaningful. Statements and theories about non-elephants in general (non-equilibriums and non-linearities) are inevitably too broad to be of any practical use.[^o11]
