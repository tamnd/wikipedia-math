# Harsanyi's utilitarian theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harsanyi's utilitarian theorem"}

In [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216), the **Harsanyi's utilitarian theorem** proves mathematically that a rational [social welfare function](wikipedia://en/217104) that satisfies the expected utility axioms and respects the [Pareto indifference principle](wikipedia://en/24562) must be a weighted sum of individuals' [utility functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_functions).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

### Assumptions {#Assumptions}

1. The society (or group) is [rational](wikipedia://en/1187268) and maximizes expected [social welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_welfare).
1. Each individual in the group is rational and maximizes his individual's expected [utility](wikipedia://en/45479).
1. If every individual in the group is indifferent between two [probability distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_distributions), then the group as a whole is indifferent as well.

::: {#o7 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

### Statement {#Statement}

[John Harsanyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harsanyi), through this theorem, proves that a [social welfare function](wikipedia://en/217104) that satisfies these three assumptions, would be the weighted sum of expected individual utility functions.[^o8]

Mathematically, the social welfare function $W$ social welfare function $W$ can be represented as a linear combination of individual utilities:[^o9][^o10]

$$W(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i u_i(x) + c$$

where:

- $u_i(x)$ is the von Neumann–Morgenstern (vNM) utility function of individual $i$.
- $a_i$ is the weight assigned to individual $i$, reflecting the individual's contribution to social welfare.
- $c$ is a normalization constant, that does not affect the ordering of social preferences.
