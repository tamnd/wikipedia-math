# Shapiro's lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shapiro's lemma"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the areas of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) dealing with [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) or relative homological algebra, **Shapiro's lemma**, also known as the **Eckmann–Shapiro lemma**, relates extensions of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over one [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) to extensions over another, especially the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) and of a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011). It thus relates the group cohomology with respect to a group to the cohomology with respect to a subgroup. Shapiro's lemma is named after [Arnold S. Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_S._Shapiro), who [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) it in 1961;[^o2] however, [Beno Eckmann](wikipedia://en/19613365) had discovered it earlier, in 1953.[^o3]

:::

## Statement for rings {#Statement_for_rings}

Let *R* → *S* be a [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411), so that *S* becomes a left and right *R*-module. Let *M* be a left *S*-module and *N* a left *R*-module. By [restriction of scalars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restriction_of_scalars), *M* is also a left *R*-module.

- If *S* is [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) as a right *R*-module, then:

$$\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(N, {}_R M) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^n_S(S \otimes_R N, M)$$

- If *S* is projective as a left *R*-module, then:

$$\operatorname{Ext}^n_R({}_R M,N) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^n_S(M,\operatorname{Hom}_R(S,N))$$

See (Benson 1991, 47). The projectivity conditions can be weakened into conditions on the vanishing of certain Tor- or Ext-groups: see (Cartan Eilenberg 1956, 118).

## Statement for group rings {#Statement_for_group_rings}

When *H* is a subgroup of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581) in *G*, then the group ring *R*\[*G*\] is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) projective as a left and right *R*\[*H*\] module, so the previous theorem applies in a simple way. Let *M* be a finite-dimensional representation of *G* and *N* a finite-dimensional representation of *H*. In this case, the module *S* ⊗<sub>*R*</sub> *N* is called the [induced representation](wikipedia://en/357452) of *N* from *H* to *G*, and <sub>*R*</sub>*M* is called the [restricted representation](wikipedia://en/880754) of *M* from *G* to *H*. One has that:

$$\operatorname{Ext}^n_G( M, N\uparrow_H^G) \cong \operatorname{Ext}^n_H( M\downarrow_H^G, N)$$

When *n* = 0, this is called [Frobenius reciprocity](wikipedia://en/55690071) for completely reducible modules, and Nakayama reciprocity in general. See (Benson 1991, 42), which also contains these higher versions of the Mackey decomposition.

## Statement for group cohomology {#Statement_for_group_cohomology}

Specializing *M* to be the [trivial module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_module) produces the familiar Shapiro's lemma. Let *H* be a subgroup of *G* and *N* a representation of *H*. For *N*<sup>*G*</sup> the [induced representation](wikipedia://en/357452) of *N* from *H* to *G* using the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844), and for H<sub>$\ast$</sub> the [group homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_homology):

H<sub>$\ast$</sub>(*G*, *N*<sup>*G*</sup>) = H<sub>$\ast$</sub>(*H*, *N*)

Similarly, for *N*<sup>*G*</sup> the co-induced representation of *N* from *H* to *G* using the [Hom functor](wikipedia://en/2741037), and for H<sup>$\ast$</sup> the [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635):

H<sup>$\ast$</sup>(*G*, *N*<sup>*G*</sup>) = H<sup>$\ast$</sup>(*H*, *N*)

When *H* has finite index in *G*, then the induced and coinduced representations coincide and the lemma is valid for both homology and cohomology.

See (Weibel 1994, 172).
