# Diamagnetic inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Diamagnetic inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the **diamagnetic inequality** relates the [Sobolev norm](wikipedia://en/611964) of the absolute value of a [section](wikipedia://en/367621) of a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) to its [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848). The diamagnetic inequality has an important physical interpretation, that a [charged](wikipedia://en/9804) particle in a [magnetic field](wikipedia://en/36563) has more energy in its [ground state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_state) than it would in a [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum).[^o2][^o3]

:::

To precisely state the inequality, let $L^2(\mathbb R^n)$ denote the usual [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) of [square-integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square-integrable) functions, and $H^1(\mathbb R^n)$ the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) of square-integrable functions with square-integrable derivatives. Let $f, A_1, \dots, A_n$ be [measurable functions](wikipedia://en/44055) on $\mathbb R^n$ and suppose that $A_j \in L^2_{\text{loc}} (\mathbb R^n)$ is real-valued, $f$ is complex-valued, and $f , (\partial_1 + iA_1)f, \dots, (\partial_n + iA_n)f \in L^2(\mathbb R^n)$. Then for [almost every](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_every) $x \in \mathbb R^n$,

$$|\nabla |f|(x)| \leq |(\nabla + iA)f(x)|.$$

In particular, $|f| \in H^1(\mathbb R^n)$.

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

For this proof we follow [Elliott H. Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_H._Lieb) and [Michael Loss](wikipedia://en/45224765).[^o2] From the assumptions, $\partial_j |f| \in L^1_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb R^n)$ when viewed in the sense of [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) and

$$\partial_j |f|(x) = \operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{\overline f(x)}{|f(x)|} \partial_j f(x)\right)$$

for almost every $x$ such that $f(x) \neq 0$ (and $\partial_j |f|(x) = 0$ if $f(x) = 0$). Moreover,

$$\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{\overline f(x)}{|f(x)|} i A_j f(x)\right) = \operatorname{Im}(A_j) = 0.$$

So

$$\nabla |f|(x) = \operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{\overline f(x)}{|f(x)|} \mathbf D f(x)\right) \leq \left|\frac{\overline f(x)}{|f(x)|} \mathbf D f(x)\right| = |\mathbf D f(x)|$$

for almost every $x$ such that $f(x) \neq 0$. The case that $f(x) = 0$ is similar.

:::

## Application to line bundles {#Application_to_line_bundles}

Let $p: L \to \mathbb R^n$ be a [U(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%281%29) line bundle, and let $A$ be a [connection 1-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connection_1-form) for $L$. In this situation, $A$ is real-valued, and the [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848) $\mathbf D$ satisfies $\mathbf Df_j = (\partial_j + iA_j)f$ for every section $f$. Here $\partial_j$ are the components of the trivial connection for $L$. If $A_j \in L^2_{\text{loc}} (\mathbb R^n)$ and $f , (\partial_1 + iA_1)f, \dots, (\partial_n + iA_n)f \in L^2(\mathbb R^n)$, then for [almost every](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_every) $x \in \mathbb R^n$, it follows from the diamagnetic inequality that

$$|\nabla |f|(x)| \leq |\mathbf Df(x)|.$$

The above case is of the most physical interest. We view $\mathbb R^n$ as [Minkowski spacetime](wikipedia://en/230488). Since the [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group) of [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism) is $U(1)$, connection 1-forms for $L$ are nothing more than the valid [electromagnetic four-potentials](wikipedia://en/731884) on $\mathbb R^n$. If $F = dA$ is the [electromagnetic tensor](wikipedia://en/1912367), then the massless [Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_equations)–[Klein–Gordon](wikipedia://en/209627) system for a section $\phi$ of $L$ are

$$\begin{cases} \partial^\mu F_{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{Im}(\phi \mathbf D_\nu \phi) \\ \mathbf D^\mu \mathbf D_\mu \phi = 0\end{cases}$$

and the [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) of this physical system is

$$\frac{||F(t)||_{L^2_x}^2}{2} + \frac{||\mathbf D \phi(t)||_{L^2_x}^2}{2}.$$

The diamagnetic inequality guarantees that the energy is minimized in the absence of electromagnetism, thus $A = 0$.[^o7]
