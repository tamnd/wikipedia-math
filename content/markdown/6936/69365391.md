# Aaron Robertson (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Aaron Robertson"}

**Aaron Robertson** (born November 8, 1971) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specializes in [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175). He is a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at [Colgate University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colgate_University).[^o3]

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Aaron Robertson was born in [Torrance, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrance,_California), and moved with his parents to [Midland, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland,_Michigan) at the age of 4. He studied [actuarial science](wikipedia://en/189373) as an [undergraduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate_education) at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), and went on to graduate school in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University) in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia), where he was supervised by [Doron Zeilberger](wikipedia://en/1724825). Robertson received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1999 with his thesis titled *Some New Results in Ramsey Theory*.[^o6]

Following his Ph.D., Robertson became an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) of mathematics at [Colgate University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colgate_University), where he is currently a full professor.

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Robertson's work in mathematics since 1998 has consisted predominantly of topics related to [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175).

One of Robertson's earliest publications is a paper, co-authored with his supervisor Doron Zeilberger, which came out of his Ph.D. work. The authors prove that "the minimum number (asymptotically) of monochromatic [Schur Triples](wikipedia://en/1522286) that a 2-colouring of $[1,n]$ can have $n^2/22 + O(n)$".[^o6]

After completing his dissertation, Robertson worked with 3-term [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389) where he found the best-known values that were close to each other and titled this piece *New Lower Bounds for Some Multicolored Ramsey Numbers*.[^o7]

Another notable piece of Robertson's research is a paper co-authored with Doron Zeilberger and [Herbert Wilf](wikipedia://en/3782688) titled *Permutation Patterns and Continued Fractions*.[^o8] In the paper, they "find a generating function for the number of (132)-avoiding permutations that have a given number of (123) patterns"[^o8] with the result being "in the form of a continued fraction".[^o8] Robertson's contribution to this specific paper includes discussion on permutations that avoid a certain pattern but contain others.

A notable paper Robertson wrote titled *A Probalistic Threshold For Monochromatic Arithmetic Progressions*[^o9] explores the function $f_r(k) = \sqrt k \cdot r^{k/2}$ (where $r\geq 2$ is fixed) and the r-colourings of $[1,n_k] = \{1,2,\ldots,n_k\}$. Robertson analyzes the threshold function for $k$-term arithmetic progressions and improves the bounds found previously.

In 2004, Robertson and Bruce M. Landman published the book *Ramsey Theory on the Integers*, of which a second expanded edition appeared in 2014.[^o10] The book introduced new topics such as rainbow Ramsey theory, an “inequality” version of [Schur's theorem](wikipedia://en/1522286), monochromatic solutions of [recurrence relations](wikipedia://en/146806), Ramsey results involving both sums and products, monochromatic sets avoiding certain differences, Ramsey properties for polynomial progressions, generalizations of the **Erdős–Ginzberg–Ziv** theorem, and the number of [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389) under arbitrary colourings.

More recently, in 2021, Robertson published a book titled *Fundamentals of Ramsey Theory.*[^o11] Robertson's goal in writing this book was to "help give an overview of Ramsey theory from several points of view, adding intuition and detailed proofs as we go, while being, hopefully, a bit gentler than most of the other books on Ramsey theory".[^o11] Throughout the book, Robertson discusses several theorems including [Ramsey's Theorem](wikipedia://en/184898), [Van der Waerden's Theorem](wikipedia://en/157178), [Rado's Theorem](wikipedia://en/1706360), and [Hales–Jewett Theorem](wikipedia://en/640714).
