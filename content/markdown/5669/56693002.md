# Andrew Ranicki

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Andrew Ranicki"}

**Andrew Alexander Ranicki** (born **Andrzej Aleksander Ranicki**; 30 December 1948 – 21 February 2018) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked on [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). He was a professor of mathematics at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Ranicki was the only child of the well-known literary critic [Marcel Reich-Ranicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Reich-Ranicki)[^o4] and the artist Teofila Reich-Ranicki; he spoke Polish in his family. Born in London, he lived in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), in [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main) and [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg), and attended school in England at the [King's School, Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_School,_Canterbury) from the age of sixteen.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

Ranicki studied Mathematics at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), and graduated with a BA in 1969.[^o7] At Cambridge, he was a student of topologists [Andrew Casson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Casson) and [John Frank Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Frank_Adams).[^o8] He earned his doctoral degree in 1973 with a thesis on [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) [L-theory](wikipedia://en/696568). Ranicki received numerous awards and honors for his scientific achievements during his studies. From 1972 to 1977, he was a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of Trinity College.[^o9]

From 1977 to 1982, he was assistant professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). In 1982, he began at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) as a [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer); in 1987, he was promoted to [reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_%28academic_rank%29). In 1992, he became a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh).[^o10] From 1995, Ranicki was the Chair of [Algebraic Surgery](wikipedia://en/5961115) at the University of Edinburgh.[^o11] Several times, he stayed as a visiting scientist at the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1386960) in Bonn, most recently in 2011.[^o12]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Ranicki married American paleontologist Ida Thompson in 1979; they had a daughter. Ranicki suffered from [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia); he died peacefully in the presence of his wife.[^o14]

A conference celebrating his legacy was held at the [International Centre for Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/8298904) (Edinburgh) in summer 2020.[^o15]
