# Margaret Baron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Margaret Baron"}

**Margaret E. Baron** (1915 – 16 August 1996) was a British [mathematics educator](wikipedia://en/326471) and [historian of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) known for her book on the [history of calculus](wikipedia://en/746117).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Baron was originally from [Gateshead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateshead), in north-eastern England, and earned a bachelor's degree from [Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University) through [King's College, Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Newcastle), which later became [Newcastle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_University). Baron worked for a year as an English teacher in [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt), and in 1938 became a mathematics teacher at the Bede School for Girls, later to become part of [Sunderland College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunderland_College). Because she married George Baron, a teacher at the corresponding boys' school, she was dismissed as a teacher in 1940. She took two more teaching posts, at the [Royal Grammar School, Newcastle upon Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Grammar_School,_Newcastle_upon_Tyne) and the [High Storrs School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Storrs_School) in [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield), before leaving work to raise her family in Gateshead.

Her husband returned from war service in 1946, and they moved to London. Eventually she returned to teaching, at [Goldsmiths' College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldsmiths,_University_of_London) and then, in 1957, as head of mathematics at the [Stockwell College of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockwell_College_of_Education). While there, she wrote a master's thesis on the history of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) in 1960, and completed a PhD in 1965. She retired in 1977, and died on 16 August 1996.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Baron's book *The Origins of the Infinitesimal Calculus*, originally published in 1969 by Pergamon, was reprinted in 1987 by [Dover Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover_Publications), and has been suggested for inclusion in undergraduate mathematics libraries by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772). She also wrote several other publications in the history of mathematics, including contributions to the *[Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_Scientific_Biography)*.

She became one of the founders of the [Association of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/7786248) in the early 1950s, and wrote additional publications in mathematics education, including the book *Number in Elementary Mathematics* (Hutchinson, 1973).
