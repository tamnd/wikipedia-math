# Jacobson–Bourbaki theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacobson–Bourbaki theorem"}

In algebra, the **Jacobson–Bourbaki theorem** is a theorem used to extend [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) to [field extensions](wikipedia://en/11634) that need not be separable. It was introduced by (txt) for commutative [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) and extended to [division rings](wikipedia://en/9067) by (Jacobson 1947), and (txt) who credited the result to unpublished work by [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394). The extension of Galois theory to [normal extensions](wikipedia://en/929925) is called the **Jacobson–Bourbaki correspondence**, which replaces the correspondence between some [subfields](wikipedia://en/11634) of a field and some subgroups of a [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) by a correspondence between some sub division rings of a [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067) and some [subalgebras](wikipedia://en/45239) of an associative algebra.

:::

The Jacobson–Bourbaki theorem implies both the usual Galois correspondence for subfields of a Galois extension, and Jacobson's Galois correspondence for subfields of a [purely inseparable extension](wikipedia://en/4770099) of exponent at most 1.

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose that *L* is a [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067). The Jacobson–Bourbaki theorem states that there is a natural 1:1 correspondence between:

- Division rings *K* in *L* of finite index *n* (in other words *L* is a finite-dimensional left vector space over *K*).
- Unital *K*-algebras of finite dimension *n* (as *K*-vector spaces) contained in the ring of endomorphisms of the additive group of *K*.

The sub division ring and the corresponding subalgebra are each other's commutants.

(Jacobson 1956, Chapter 7.2) gave an extension to sub division rings that might have infinite index, which correspond to closed subalgebras in the finite topology.

:::
