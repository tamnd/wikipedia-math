# Cindy Greenwood

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cindy Greenwood"}

**Priscilla E. (Cindy) Greenwood** (born 1937)[^o2] is a Canadian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who is a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) of mathematics at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia).[^o3] She is known for her research in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Greenwood graduated from [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) with a B.A. in 1959. She began her graduate studies in [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), where she became exposed to probability theory through a course on [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895) offered in 1960 by [Henry McKean](wikipedia://en/35051081).[^o4] Soon afterwards, she switched to the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison), where she completed her Ph.D. in 1963 under the supervision of Joshua Chover.[^o4] She taught for two years at [North Carolina College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_College) before moving to the University of British Columbia in 1966. She has also been associated with [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University), as a visiting professor from 2000 to 2003 and since 2004 as a research professor.[^o4]

## Research {#Research}

Greenwood's research in the 1970s concerned [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436), [Lévy processes](wikipedia://en/1025748), and [Wiener–Hopf factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener–Hopf_factorization). During this method she developed the theory of the martintote, a process similar to a [martingale](wikipedia://en/274536) used to study asymptotic properties of processes.[^o4]

In the 1980s Greenwood began working with [Ed Perkins](wikipedia://en/32648473) on [nonstandard analysis](wikipedia://en/21708), which they used to study [local time](wikipedia://en/5678101) and [excursions](wikipedia://en/37881516). In this timeframe she also began working on set-indexed processes, a topic that would lead her to the theory of [random fields](wikipedia://en/540838), and on [semimartingales](wikipedia://en/9653775). She traveled to Russia, and wrote a monograph on [chi-squared tests](wikipedia://en/226680) with Mikhail Nikulin.[^o4]

In 1990 she and Igor Evstigneev wrote a second monograph, on random fields. Her research in this period also concerned [metric entropy](wikipedia://en/398931) and [asymptotic efficiency](wikipedia://en/27250866). She began her work in [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878), involving studies of different mammalian populations, and led a major study on statistical estimation near critical points of a parameter.[^o4]

Beginning in 2000, at Arizona State, she studied [pink noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_noise) and [stochastic resonance](wikipedia://en/965419), which she applied to epidemic models in biostatistics as well as to the firing patterns of neurons.[^o4]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Greenwood was elected as a fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1985. She won the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829) of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) in 2002.[^o4][^o9]

## Books {#Books}

- *Contiguity and the statistical invariance principle* (with [A. N. Shiryayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Shiryaev), Gordon & Breach, 1985)[^o11]
- *Markov fields over countable partially ordered sets: extrema and splitting* (with I. V. Evstigneev, Memoirs of the American Mathematical Society 112, American Mathematical Society, 1994)[^o12]
- *A guide to chi-squared testing* (with Mikhail S. Nikulin, Wiley, 1996)[^o13]
- *Stochastic neuron models* (with Lawrence M. Ward, Mathematical Biosciences Institute Lecture Series, Springer, 2016)
