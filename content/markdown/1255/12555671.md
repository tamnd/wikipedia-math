# Relaxation (iterative method)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Relaxation"}

In [numerical mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_mathematics), **relaxation methods** are [iterative methods](wikipedia://en/15237) for solving [systems of equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_equations), including nonlinear systems.[^o2]

:::

Relaxation methods were developed for solving large [sparse](wikipedia://en/341015) [linear systems](wikipedia://en/722503), which arose as [finite-difference](wikipedia://en/39147) [discretizations](wikipedia://en/330017) of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309).[^o6][^o7] They are also used for the solution of linear equations for [linear least-squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_least_squares_%28mathematics%29) problems[^o8] and also for systems of linear inequalities, such as those arising in [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730).[^o3][^o4][^o5] They have also been developed for solving nonlinear systems of equations.[^o2]

Relaxation methods are important especially in the solution of linear systems used to model [elliptic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/662787), such as [Laplace's equation](wikipedia://en/36941) and its generalization, [Poisson's equation](wikipedia://en/193735). These equations describe [boundary-value problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary-value_problem), in which the solution-function's values are specified on boundary of a domain; the problem is to compute a solution also on its interior. Relaxation methods are used to solve the linear equations resulting from a discretization of the differential equation, for example by finite differences.[^o6][^o7][^o8]

Iterative relaxation of solutions is commonly dubbed [smoothing](wikipedia://en/4155662) because with certain equations, such as [Laplace's equation](wikipedia://en/36941), it resembles repeated application of a local smoothing filter to the solution vector. These are not to be confused with [relaxation](wikipedia://en/6347835) methods in [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033), which [approximate](wikipedia://en/1092713) a difficult problem by a simpler problem whose "relaxed" solution provides information about the solution of the original problem.[^o5]

## Model problem of potential theory {#Model_problem_of_potential_theory}

When φ is a smooth [real-valued function](wikipedia://en/22284647) on the real numbers, its [second derivative](wikipedia://en/3224219) can be approximated by:

$$\frac{d^2\varphi(x)}{{dx}^2} = \frac{\varphi(x{-}h)-2\varphi(x)+\varphi(x{+}h)}{h^2}\,+\,\mathcal{O}(h^2)\,.$$

Using this in both dimensions for a function φ of two arguments at the point (*x*, *y*), and solving for φ(*x*, *y*), results in:

$$\varphi(x, y) = \tfrac{1}{4}\left(\varphi(x{+}h,y)+\varphi(x,y{+}h)+\varphi(x{-}h,y)+\varphi(x,y{-}h) \,-\,h^2{\nabla}^2\varphi(x,y)\right)\,+\,\mathcal{O}(h^4)\,.$$

To approximate the solution of the Poisson equation:

$${\nabla}^2 \varphi = f\,$$

numerically on a two-dimensional grid with grid spacing *h*, the relaxation method assigns the given values of function φ to the grid points near the boundary and arbitrary values to the interior grid points, and then repeatedly performs the assignment φ := φ\* on the interior points, where φ\* is defined by:

$$\varphi^*(x, y) = \tfrac{1}{4}\left(\varphi(x{+}h,y)+\varphi(x,y{+}h)+\varphi(x{-}h,y)+\varphi(x,y{-}h) \,-\,h^2f(x,y)\right)\,,$$

until convergence.[^o6][^o7]

The method[^o6][^o7] is easily generalized to other numbers of dimensions.

## Convergence and acceleration {#Convergence_and_acceleration}

While the method converges under general conditions, it typically makes slower progress than competing methods. Nonetheless, the study of relaxation methods remains a core part of [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), because the transformations of relaxation theory provide excellent [preconditioners](wikipedia://en/5165246) for new methods. Indeed, the choice of preconditioner is often more important than the choice of iterative method.[^o11]

[Multigrid methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multigrid_methods) may be used to accelerate the methods. One can first compute an approximation on a coarser grid – usually the double spacing 2*h* – and use that solution with [interpolated](wikipedia://en/14569) values for the other grid points as the initial assignment. This can then also be done recursively for the coarser computation.[^o11][^o12]

## See also {#See_also}

- In linear systems, the two main classes of relaxation methods are [stationary iterative methods](wikipedia://en/15237#Stationary_iterative_methods), and the more general [Krylov subspace methods](wikipedia://en/15237#Krylov_subspace_methods).
- The [Jacobi method](wikipedia://en/4047104) is a simple relaxation method.
- The [Gauss–Seidel method](wikipedia://en/4046824) is an improvement upon the Jacobi method.
- [Successive over-relaxation](wikipedia://en/4068447) can be applied to either of the Jacobi and Gauss–Seidel methods to speed convergence.
- [Multigrid methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multigrid_methods)
