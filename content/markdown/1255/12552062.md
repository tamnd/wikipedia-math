# Lemoine's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lemoine's conjecture"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Lemoine's conjecture**, also sometimes known as **Levy's conjecture**, states that all [odd integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_integer) greater than 5 can be represented as the sum of an odd [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) and an even [semiprime](wikipedia://en/339542).

:::

The conjecture was first proposed by [Émile Lemoine](wikipedia://en/15956333) in 1895, but was erroneously attributed by [MathWorld](wikipedia://en/374235) to [Hyman Levy](wikipedia://en/17775783) who pondered it in the 1960s.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Algebraically, the conjecture states that $2n + 1 = p + 2q$ always has a solution in primes $p$ and $q$ (not necessarily distinct) for $n > 2$. The Lemoine conjecture implies [Goldbach's weak conjecture](wikipedia://en/147164).

In 2008, [Zhi-Wei Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhi-Wei_Sun) similarly conjectured that all odd integers greater than 3 can be represented as the sum of a prime number and the product of two consecutive positive integers; that is, $2n + 1 = p + m(m+1)$ for all $n > 1$.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

For example, the odd integer $47$ can be expressed as the sum of a prime and an even semiprime in four different ways:

$$47 = 13 + 2\cdot 17 = 37 + 2\cdot5 = 41 + 2\cdot3 = 43 + 2\cdot2$$

The number of ways this can be done is given by . Lemoine's conjecture is that this sequence contains no zeros after the first three.

:::

## Evidence {#Evidence}

According to [MathWorld](wikipedia://en/374235), the conjecture has been verified by Corbitt up to 10<sup>9</sup>.[^o2] A blog post in June of 2019 additionally claimed to have verified the conjecture up to 10<sup>10</sup>.[^o9]

A proof was claimed in 2017 by Agama and Gensel, but this was later found to be flawed.[^o10]
