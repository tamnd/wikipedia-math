# Midhinge

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Midhinge"}

::: {#o2 .definition cue=dfn name="MH"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **midhinge** (**MH**) is the average of the first and third [quartiles](wikipedia://en/25170) and is thus a measure of [location](wikipedia://en/17516). Equivalently, it is the 25% [trimmed](wikipedia://en/18506510) [mid-range](wikipedia://en/2814021) or 25% [midsummary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsummary); it is an [L-estimator](wikipedia://en/17398955). The midhinge $\operatorname{MH}$ is defined as

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{MH}(X) &= \overline{Q_{1, 3}(X)} \\ &= \frac{Q_1(X) + Q_3(X)}{2} \\ &= \frac{P_{25}(X) + P_{75}(X)}{2} \\ &= M_{25}(X). \end{aligned}$$

:::

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=dfn name="IQR"}

The midhinge is related to the [interquartile range](wikipedia://en/14812) (**IQR**), the difference of the third and first [quartiles](wikipedia://en/25170) (i.e. $\operatorname{IQR} = Q_{3} - Q_{1}$), which is a measure of [statistical dispersion](wikipedia://en/23773247). The two are complementary in sense that if one knows the midhinge and the IQR, one can find the first and third quartiles.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=dfn name="TM"}

The use of the term *hinge* for the lower or upper quartiles derives from [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974)'s work on [exploratory data analysis](wikipedia://en/416589) in the late 1970s,[^o5] and *midhinge* is a fairly modern term dating from around that time. The midhinge is slightly simpler to calculate than the [trimean](wikipedia://en/9888821) (**TM**), which originated in the same context and equals the average of the [median](wikipedia://en/18837) ($lh=.7em = Q_{2}$ $= P_{50}$) and the midhinge:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{MH}(X) &= 2 \operatorname{TM}(X) - \operatorname{med}(X) \\ &= 2\; \frac{Q_1 + 2Q_2 + Q_3}{4} - Q_2. \end{aligned}$$

:::
