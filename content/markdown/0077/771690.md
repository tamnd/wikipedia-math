# Adolf Hurwitz

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Adolf Hurwitz"}

**Adolf Hurwitz** (; 26 March 1859 – 18 November 1919) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked on [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in [Hildesheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildesheim), then part of the [Kingdom of Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hanover), to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family and died in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich), Switzerland. His father Salomon Hurwitz, a merchant, was not wealthy. Hurwitz's mother, Elise Wertheimer, died when he was three years old.[^o7] Family records indicate that he had siblings and cousins, but their names have yet to be confirmed except for an older brother, Julius, with whom he developed an arithmetical theory for complex continued fractions circa 1890.[^o4] Hurwitz entered the Gymnasium Andreanum in Hildesheim in 1868. He was taught mathematics there by [Hermann Schubert](wikipedia://en/7998163).[^o5] Schubert persuaded Hurwitz's father to allow him to attend university, and arranged for Hurwitz to study with [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München).[^o5] Salomon Hurwitz could not afford to send his son to university, but his friend, Mr. Edwards, assisted financially.

## Educational career {#Educational_career}

Hurwitz entered the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) in 1877 at 18 years of age. He spent one year there attending lectures by Klein, before spending the academic year 1877–1878 at the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin) where he attended classes by [Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117), [Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459) and [Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450), after which he returned to the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München.

In October 1880, Felix Klein moved to [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University). Hurwitz followed him there, and became a doctoral student under Klein's direction, finishing a dissertation on [elliptic modular functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_modular_function) in 1881. Following two years at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), in 1884 he was invited to become an Extraordinary Professor at the [Albertus Universität Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albertus_Universität_Königsberg); there he encountered the young [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) and [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537), on whom he had a major influence. Following the departure of [Frobenius](wikipedia://en/341048), Hurwitz took a chair at the [Eidgenössische Polytechnikum Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eidgenössische_Polytechnikum_Zürich) (today [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich)) in 1892 (having to turn down a position at Göttingen shortly after[^o7]), and remained there for the rest of his life.

Throughout his time in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich), Hurwitz was in continual ill health, which had been originally caused when he contracted [typhoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoid) while a student in Munich. He had severe [migraines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migraines), and in 1905 developed kidney disease, resulting in one being removed.

## Contributions to mathematics {#Contributions_to_mathematics}

*Image: Adolf Hurwitz 1910s.jpg.* Adolf Hurwitz

He was one of the early students of the [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) theory, and used it to prove many of the foundational results on [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260); for instance [Hurwitz's automorphisms theorem](wikipedia://en/1113637). This work anticipates a number of later theories, such as the general theory of algebraic correspondences, [Hecke operators](wikipedia://en/521837), and [Lefschetz fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/582530). He also had deep interests in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He studied the [maximal order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_order) theory (as it now would be) for the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440), defining the [Hurwitz quaternions](wikipedia://en/772241) that are now named for him. In the field of [control systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_systems) and [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632) he derived the [Routh–Hurwitz stability criterion](wikipedia://en/1705432) for determining whether a linear system is stable in 1895, independently of [Edward John Routh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_John_Routh) who had derived it earlier by a different method.[^o9] In Lie theory, Hurwitz proved the existence of the [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) on [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) (which Haar then extended to locally compact groups).[^o10]

## Family {#Family}

In 1884, while at [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg), Hurwitz met and married Ida Samuel, the daughter of a professor in the faculty of medicine. They had three children.
