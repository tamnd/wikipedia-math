# Scholarly method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Scholarly method"}

*Image: Gerbrand van den Eeckhout 003.jpg.* *Scholar and His Books* by [Gerbrand van den Eeckhout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerbrand_van_den_Eeckhout)

The **scholarly method** or **scholarship** is the body of [principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle) and [practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practice_theory) used by [scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholar) and [academics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy#Academic_personnel) to make their claims about their subjects of expertise. These principles and practices aim to ensure the validity and trustworthiness of the claims, and to disseminate them to the scholarly community. It comprises the methods that systematically advance the [teaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaching), [research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research), and practice of a scholarly or academic field of study through [rigorous](wikipedia://en/655974) inquiry. Scholarship is creative, can be documented, replicated, elaborated, and [peer reviewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-reviewed) through various methods.[^o2] The scholarly method includes the subcategories of the [scientific method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method), with which scientists use to bolster their claims, and the [historical method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_method), which historians use to verify their claims.[^o3]

## Methods {#Methods}

The [historical method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_method) comprises the techniques and guidelines by which [historians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian) research [primary sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_source) and other evidence, and then [write history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography). The question of the nature, and indeed the possibility, of sound historical method is raised in the [philosophy of history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_history), as a question of [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology). History guidelines commonly used by historians in their work require external criticism, internal criticism, and [synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis,_antithesis,_synthesis).

The [empirical method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_method) is generally taken to mean the collection of data on which to base a [hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis) or derive a conclusion in [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science). It is part of the [scientific method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method), but is often mistakenly assumed to be synonymous with other methods. The empirical method is not sharply defined and is often contrasted with the precision of experiments, where data emerges from the systematic manipulation of variables. The [experimental method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_method) investigates [causal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality) relationships among [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109). An experiment is a cornerstone of the [empirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empiricism) approach to acquiring data about the world and is used in both [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science) and [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science). An experiment can be used to help solve practical problems and to support or negate [theoretical](wikipedia://en/30746) assumptions.

The scientific method refers to a body of techniques for investigating [phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomenon), acquiring new [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge), or correcting and integrating previous knowledge. To be termed scientific, a method of [inquiry](wikipedia://en/3657365) must be based on gathering [observable](wikipedia://en/294248), [empirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical) and measurable [evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence) subject to specific principles of [reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasoning).[^o6] A scientific method consists of the collection of [data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data) through [observation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation) and experimentation, and the formulation and testing of hypotheses.[^o7]
