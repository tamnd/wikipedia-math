# William S. Zwicker

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William S. Zwicker"}

**William Seymour Zwicker** (born 1949) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and the William D. Williams Professor of Mathematics at [Union College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_College) in [Schenectady, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schenectady,_New_York).[^o2]

:::

Zwicker earned a bachelor's degree from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1971, and a Ph.D from [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1976, under the supervision of Eugene M. Kleinberg. He joined the Union College faculty in 1975, was given his named chair in 2006, and retired in 2021.[^o2]

Zwicker has done research in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and [social choice theory](wikipedia://en/1745670). He is credited with inventing the concept of a [supergame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_Game#Supergame) and the related [hypergame paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_Game#Hypergame_Paradox).[^o4] With Alan D. Taylor, he is the author of *Simple Games: Desirability Relations, Trading, Pseudoweightings* (Princeton University Press, 1999). Zwicker is also known for his work in [fair division](wikipedia://en/669899), including for the [Brams–Taylor–Zwicker procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brams–Taylor–Zwicker_procedure) for envy-free cake cutting.
