# Frederick Gehring

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Frederick Gehring"}

**Frederick William Gehring**[^o4] (7 August 1925 – 29 May 2012) was an American mathematician who worked in the area of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) ([quasi-conformal mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-conformal_mappings)).

:::

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Both of Fred Gehring's parents graduated from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). His father, Carl Ernst Gehring, was a journalist who worked for the [Ann Arbor News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor_News) and a music critic. His mother, Hester Reed Gehring, was a foreign language examiner for students who needed to prove competency as a requirement for their graduate degree. She was also the daughter of [John Oren Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Oren_Reed), a physics professor and Dean of the College of Literature, Science and the Arts at the University of Michigan.

Gehring graduated from [University High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_High_School_%28Michigan%29) in 1943 and hoped to attend the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). However, because of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he was about to be drafted into the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy). So he instead enrolled in the [V-12 Navy College Training Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-12_Navy_College_Training_Program) at the University of Michigan where he earned a BE in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) and a BA in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in addition to completing various other Navy courses. He finished his coursework close to [Victory in Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_in_Europe_Day). After graduating, the Navy sent him to serve on a [destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyer) in the [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic) and [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean). When the war ended a few months later, Gehring was discharged from the Navy and returned to the University of Michigan, where he obtained a master's degree in mathematics.

In 1949, Gehring went to the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) to study mathematics under [John Charles Burkill](wikipedia://en/33388595) at [Peterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge). While there he met Lois Caroline Bigger, who was also working towards a Ph.D. (at [Girton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College)). Both were attending the University of Cambridge on [Fulbright scholarships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program). Gehring received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics in 1952 while Lois Bigger received her Ph.D. three months earlier in [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry). They married one year after returning to the US on August 25, 1953, and have two sons, Kalle (born 21 December 1958) and Peter (born 29 September 1960).

## Career {#Career}

Gehring served as a [Benjamin Peirce](wikipedia://en/364270) instructor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) for three years after completing his doctoral work at the University of Cambridge. In 1955 he returned to Ann Arbor, MI, to assume a post on the faculty of the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) where he worked until he retired at age 70. During this time he supervised 29 Ph.D. students, six of whom are women, as well as 40 postdoctoral visitors.[^o7] He also served as chairman of the department on three separate occasions, serving for a total of eight years.

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

- 1986 – awarded the [Order of the White Rose of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_White_Rose_of_Finland), Commander class, Finland's highest scientific honor for foreigners.
- 1989 – elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).
- 1995 – awarded the [Onsager Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onsager_Medal).
- 1997 – received an [honorary degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) (dr. philos. h.c.) from [The Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_University_of_Science_and_Technology).[^o9]
- 2006 – awarded the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize) for Lifetime Achievement.

## Gehring's Lemma {#Gehring's_Lemma}

In a 1973 paper[^o12] which has been cited over 800 times, Gehring proved the following lemma:[^o13] Assume that $f$ is a non–negative locally integrable function on $R^{n}$ and 1 \< $p$ \< ∞. If there is a constant c<sub>1</sub> such that the inequality

$\begin{aligned}&\left(\int_B |f(x)|^p\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}} \end{aligned}$ ≤ c<sub>1</sub>$\begin{aligned}&\left(\int_B f(x)\,dx\right) \end{aligned}$

holds for all balls $B$ of $R^{n}$, then there exists $\varepsilon$ > 0 and there exists a constant c<sub>2</sub> such that

$\begin{aligned}&\left(\int_B |f(x)|^{p+\varepsilon}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p+\varepsilon}} \end{aligned}$ ≤ c<sub>2</sub>$\begin{aligned}&\left(\int_B f(x)\,dx\right) \end{aligned}$ holds for all balls $B$ of $R^{n}$.

:::
