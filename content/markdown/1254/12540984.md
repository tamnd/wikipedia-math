# Hankel singular value

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hankel singular value"}

In [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039), **Hankel singular values**, named after [Hermann Hankel](wikipedia://en/932515), provide a measure of energy for each state in a system. They are the basis for [balanced model reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balanced_model_reduction), in which high energy states are retained while low energy states are discarded. The reduced model retains the important features of the original model.

:::

Hankel singular values are calculated as the square roots, {σ<sub>*i*</sub> ≥ 0, *i* = 1,…,*n*}, of the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue), {λ<sub>*i*</sub> ≥ 0, *i* = 1,…,*n*}, for the product of the [controllability Gramian](wikipedia://en/5347320), *W<sub>C</sub>*, and the [observability Gramian](wikipedia://en/5347359), *W<sub>O</sub>*.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The square of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of the Hankel operator associated with a linear system is the sum of squares of the Hankel singular values of this system. Moreover, the area enclosed by the oriented [Nyquist diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyquist_diagram) of an [BIBO stable](wikipedia://en/1145733) and strictly proper linear system is equal π times the square of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of the Hankel operator associated with this system.[^o4]
- Hankel singular values also provide the optimal range of analog filters.[^o5]

:::
