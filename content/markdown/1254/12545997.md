# British Social Attitudes Survey

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="British Social Attitudes Survey"}

The **British Social Attitudes Survey** (**BSA**) is an annual [statistical survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_survey) conducted in the UK by the [National Centre for Social Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Centre_for_Social_Research) since 1983.[^o2] The BSA involves in-depth interviews with over 3,000 respondents, selected using [random probability sampling](wikipedia://en/27578),[^o3] focused on topics including [newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper) readership, [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_parties) and trust, [public expenditure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_expenditure), [welfare benefits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_benefits), [health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care), [childcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childcare), [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty), the [labour market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_market) and the workplace, [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education), [charitable giving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_%28practice%29), the [countryside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countryside), [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport) and the [environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_policy), the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union), [economic prospects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy), [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_%28classification_of_humans%29), [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion), [civil liberties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_liberties), [immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration), [sentencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentence_%28law%29) and [prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prison), [fear of crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear_of_crime) and the portrayal of sex and [violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence) in the [media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media).[^o4] The survey is funded by [government departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_department), [quasi-governmental bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QUANGO), charities and think tanks. The BSA was not conducted in 1988 and 1992, when funding was devoted instead to studies of voting behaviour and political attitudes in the British Election Study.[^o2]

:::

## Findings {#Findings}

### Abortion {#Abortion}

*Image: British Social Attitudes Survey (2).png.* Attitudes towards abortion, 1983–2016

The proportion of people who believe abortion should be allowed if a woman does not want a child has increased gradually during the period from 1983 to 2016, from 40% in 1983 to 72% in 2016. Similarly an increasing number of people believe abortion should be allowed if a couple cannot afford a child, which reached a high of 68% in 2016. Over 90% of people have consistently believed that abortion is acceptable if the pregnancy is a result of rape.[^o7]

### Capital punishment {#Capital_punishment}

*Image: British Social Attitudes Survey - Capital Punishment.png.* "For some crimes, the death penalty is the most appropriate sentence"

Support for the death penalty has gradually decreased from 75% in 1986 to 43% in 2019. From 2014 onwards, less than half of people supported the use of capital punishment.[^o7]

### LGBT rights {#LGBT_rights}

#### Same-sex relationships {#Same-sex_relationships}

*Image: British Social Attitudes Survey - Same-sex relationships.png.* Attitudes towards same-sex relationships, 1986–2012

17% of people believed same-sex relationships were 'not wrong at all' in 1983 and the proportion of people holding this view reached a low of 11% in 1987 during the height of the [AIDS pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS_pandemic). An increasing number of people were comfortable with same-sex relationships during the period 1989-2017 and as of 2018 66% of people do not consider same-sex relationships to be 'wrong at all'.[^o19]

#### Transgender rights {#Transgender_rights}

Attitudes towards transgender people were first examined in the 2016 report, which found that 49% of people view prejudice against transgender people as "always wrong", compared with 6% who believe it is "rarely or never wrong". 34% of people believed prejudice against transgender people is only "mostly" or "sometimes" wrong.[^o19]

### Social security {#Social_security}

*Image: BSA - Social Security.png.* "Benefits for unemployed people are too low and cause hardship"

From 1983 until the late 1990s, most people thought that benefits for the unemployed were too low and caused hardship. Following the election of the [Labour government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Labour) in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_United_Kingdom_general_election), there was a sharp decline in this view and the majority of people now believed that unemployment benefit was too high until 2016, when an increasing number of people began to consider unemployment benefits as too low and the proportion of people holding this view reached a twenty five year high of 51% in 2020.[^o23]
