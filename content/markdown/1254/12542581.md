# Cut point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cut point"}

*Image: Cut-point.svg.* The "neck" of this eight-like figure is a cut-point.

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a **cut-point** is a point of a [connected space](wikipedia://en/6233) such that its removal causes the resulting space to be disconnected. If removal of a point doesn't result in disconnected spaces, this point is called a **non-cut point**.

:::

For example, every point of a line is a cut-point, while no point of a circle is a cut-point.

Cut-points are useful to determine whether two connected spaces are [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) by counting the number of cut-points in each space. If two spaces have different number of cut-points, they are not homeomorphic. A classic example is using cut-points to show that lines and circles are not homeomorphic.

Cut-points are also useful in the characterization of [topological continua](wikipedia://en/24758520), a class of spaces which combine the properties of [compactness](wikipedia://en/6042) and [connectedness](wikipedia://en/6233) and include many familiar spaces such as the [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324), the circle, and the [torus](wikipedia://en/74800).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definitions"}

### Formal definitions {#Formal_definitions}

*Image: Cut-point line and circle.png.* A line (closed interval) has infinitely many cut points between the two endpoints. A circle has no cut point. Since they have different numbers of cut points, lines are not homeomorphic to circles.

A point $p$ of a [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_%28topology%29) [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is called a **cut point**[^n1][^n2] of $X$ if $X\setminus\{p\}$ is not connected. A point $p$ of a connected space $X$ is called a **non-cut point**[^n1] of $X$ if $X\setminus\{p\}$ is connected.

Note that these two notions only make sense if the space $X$ is connected to start with. Also, for a space to have a cut point, the space must have at least three points, because removing a point from a space with one or two elements always leaves a connected space.

A non-empty connected topological space X is called a **cut-point space**[^n2] if every point in X is a cut point of X.

:::

### Basic examples {#Basic_examples}

- A [closed interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_interval) \[a,b\] has infinitely many cut points. All points except for its endpoints are cut points and the endpoints {a,b} are non-cut points.
- An [open interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_interval) (a,b) has infinitely many cut points, like closed intervals. Since open intervals don't have endpoints, it has no non-cut point.
- A circle has no cut point. Every point of a circle is a non-cut point.

::: {#o12 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notations"}

### Notations {#Notations}

- A **cutting** of X is a set $\{p, U, V\}$ where $p$ is a cut-point of $X$, $U$ and $V$ form a [separation](wikipedia://en/6233) of $X-\{p\}.$
- Also can be written as $X \setminus \{p\}=U|V.$

:::

## Theorems {#Theorems}

### Cut-points and homeomorphisms {#Cut-points_and_homeomorphisms}

- Cut-points are not necessarily preserved under [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122). For example: *f*: \[0, 2$\pi$\] → **R**<sup>2</sup>, given by *f*(*x*) = (cos *x*, sin *x*). Every point of the interval (except the two endpoints) is a cut-point, but f(x) forms a circle which has no cut-points.
- Cut-points are preserved under homeomorphisms. Therefore, cut-points are a [topological invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_invariant).

### Cut-points and continua {#Cut-points_and_continua}

- Every continuum (compact connected [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637)) with more than one point has at least two non-cut points. Specifically, each open set which forms a separation of resulting space contains at least one non-cut point.
- Every continuum with exactly two noncut-points is homeomorphic to the unit interval.
- If K is a continuum with points a,b and K-{a,b} isn't connected, K is homeomorphic to the unit circle.

### Topological properties of cut-point spaces {#Topological_properties_of_cut-point_spaces}

- Let X be a connected space and x be a cut point in X such that X\\{x}=A|B. Then {x} is either [open](wikipedia://en/39358) or [closed](wikipedia://en/47279). if {x} is open, A and B are closed. If {x} is closed, A and B are open.
- Let X be a cut-point space. The set of closed points of X is infinite.

:::

## The Khalimsky line {#The_Khalimsky_line}

The **Khalimsky line** is the set $\mathbb Z$ of integers with a base for the topology given by $\{ \{2i-1,2i,2i+1\} : i \in \mathbb{Z} \} \cup \{ \{2i+1\} : i \in \mathbb{Z}\}$.[^n3] The topology is an [Alexandrov topology](wikipedia://en/542198). Every odd number is an [isolated point](wikipedia://en/390557). And the smallest neighborhood of an even number is the point itself together with the two adjacent elements.

The Khalimsky line is a cut-point space since every point is a cut point, but no proper subspace of it is a cut-point space. It is the only space with that property: A cut-point space for which no proper subspace is a cut-point space must be homeomorphic to the Khalimsky line.[^n4]
