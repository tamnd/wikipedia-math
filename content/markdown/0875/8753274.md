# Shaun Wylie

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shaun Wylie"}

**Shaun Wylie** (17 January 1913 – 2 October 2009[^o3][^o4]) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) codebreaker.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Wylie was born in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England). The fourth son of [Sir Francis Wylie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_James_Wylie) (later the first Warden of [Rhodes House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_House),[^o6] Oxford) and his wife Kathleen (formerly Kelly), he was educated at the [Dragon School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_School) (in Oxford) and then [Winchester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_College).[^o7] He won a scholarship to [New College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College,_Oxford) where he studied mathematics and [classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literae_Humaniores).[^o7] In 1934, he went to study [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), obtaining a PhD in 1937 with [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384) as his supervisor. At Princeton he met fellow English mathematician [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208).[^o7] He became a fellow of [Trinity Hall, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Hall,_Cambridge) in 1938/1939.[^o4][^o9]

## World War II codebreaking {#World_War_II_codebreaking}

*Image: Enigma rotors and spindle showing contacts rachet and notch.jpg.* Wylie contributed to the breaking of the [Enigma machine](wikipedia://en/9256) code.

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Turing had been recruited to work at [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037), Britain's codebreaking centre. Turing wrote to Wylie around December 1940, who was by then teaching at [Wellington College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_College,_Berkshire), inviting him to work at Bletchley Park.[^o11] He accepted, and arrived in February 1941.[^o11] He joined Turing's section, [Hut 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hut_8), which was working on solving the [Enigma machine](wikipedia://en/9256) as used by the *[Kriegsmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kriegsmarine)*. He became head of the [crib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crib_%28cryptanalysis%29) subsection,[^o12] and allocated time on the [bombe](wikipedia://en/615354) codebreaking machines.[^o13] [Hugh Alexander](wikipedia://en/2784711), successor to Turing as head of Hut 8, commented that "except for Turing, no-one made a bigger contribution to the success of Hut 8 than Shaun Wylie; he was astonishingly quick and resourceful and contributed to theory and practice in a number of different directions".[^o14]

Wylie transferred in Autumn 1943 to work on "[Tunny](wikipedia://en/301173)", a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) teleprinter cipher.[^o15] He married Odette Murray, a [WREN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Royal_Naval_Service) in the section.[^o16] In 1945, soon after the [victory in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_in_Europe), Wylie demonstrated how [Colossus](wikipedia://en/6229) – electronic machines used to help solve Tunny – could have been used unmodified to break the Tunny "motor wheels", a task which had been previously done by hand.[^o17] While at Bletchley Park, he became president of the dramatic club.[^o18] He had also played international [hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey) for [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland),[^o18] but according to fellow codebreaker [I. J. Good](wikipedia://en/404404), he "never mentioned any of his successes".[^o19]

## Post-war {#Post-war}

section After the war, he was a fellow at Trinity Hall until 1958[^o4] and lectured in mathematics.[^o21] He was the [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) advisor for [Frank Adams](wikipedia://en/1223959), [Max Kelly](wikipedia://en/18996838), [Crispin Nash-Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crispin_St._J._A._Nash-Williams), [William Tutte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tutte) and [Christopher Zeeman](wikipedia://en/871415).[^o8] With [Peter Hilton](wikipedia://en/8951286), he authored *Homology Theory: An Introduction to Algebraic Topology*, published in 1960.[^o19]

In 1958, he became Chief Mathematician at [GCHQ](wikipedia://en/12884), the UK [signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence) organisation.[^o8] In July 1969, he was sent a draft paper by [James H. Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_H._Ellis), another GCHQ mathematician, about the possibility of what was termed "non-secret encryption", or what is now more commonly known as [public-key cryptography](wikipedia://en/24222), on which Wylie commented "unfortunately, I can't see anything wrong with this".[^o22] He retired in 1973, and taught at [Cambridgeshire High School for Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire_High_School_for_Boys) (later [Hills Road Sixth Form College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hills_Road_Sixth_Form_College)) in Cambridge for seven years.[^o8] He was elected an [honorary fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_title_%28academic%29) at Trinity Hall in 1980.[^o4]

Wylie supervised five PhD students at Cambridge, through whom he had over 1600 "descendants" in 2021 according to the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) [Mathematical Genealogy Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Genealogy_Project).[^o8] In addition he influenced the intellectual development of generations of pupils at the [Cambridgeshire High School for Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridgeshire_High_School_for_Boys)/[Hills Road Sixth Form College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hills_Road_Sixth_Form_College) where he taught maths (including statistics, with his beloved chi-squared distribution) and classical Greek and where he also produced plays (such as [Chekhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Chekov)'s *[The Cherry Orchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cherry_Orchard)*) and supervised the school chess team. He also came out of retirement temporarily to teach Mathematics at Long Road Sixth Form College.

After retirement from teaching, Wylie was instrumental in the founding of the [Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_%28UK%29) and in the Cambridge-based [University of the Third Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Third_Age) and at the time of his death was preparing to read in the next [Cambridge Greek Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Greek_Play), [Aeschylus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeschylus)' *[Agamemnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agamemnon_%28play%29)*.

His eldest son, the late [Keith Wylie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Wylie) (1945–1999), a [barrister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrister), was a [croquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croquet) international and open champion of Great Britain.[^o23] His second son Malcolm (b1949) pioneered the walk from John o'Groats to Land's End along the [British Watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_watershed) between 1996 and 2009.[^o24]

Shaun Wylie died on 2 October 2009, aged 96.[^o3][^o4]
