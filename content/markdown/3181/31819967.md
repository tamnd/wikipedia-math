# Garnir relations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Garnir relations"}

In mathematics, the **Garnir relations** give a way of expressing a basis of the [Specht modules](wikipedia://en/5122533) *V*<sub>λ</sub> in terms of standard polytabloids.

:::

## Specht modules in terms of polytabloids {#Specht_modules_in_terms_of_polytabloids}

Given an [integer partition](wikipedia://en/104790) *λ* of *n*, one has the Specht module *V*<sub>λ</sub>. In characteristic 0, this is an irreducible representation of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>*n*</sub>. One can construct *V<sub>λ</sub>* explicitly in terms of polytabloids as follows:

- Start with the permutation representation of *S*<sub>*n*</sub> acting on all [Young tableaux](wikipedia://en/683368) of shape *λ*, which are fillings of the Young diagram of *λ* with numbers 1, 2, ... *n*, each used once (note that we do not require the tableaux to be standard, there are no conditions imposed along rows or columns). The group *S*<sub>*n*</sub> acts by permuting the positions in each tableau (for instance there is a [cyclic permutation](wikipedia://en/449166) the cycles the entries of the first row one place forward).
- A [Young tabloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_tabloid) is an orbit of Young tableaux under the action of the row permutations, the subgroup of *S*<sub>*n*</sub> of permutations that permute the positions in each row separately (this "[Young subgroup](wikipedia://en/76019869)" is a product of symmetric groups, one for each row). The Young tabloid of *T* is denoted {*T*}.
- Now consider the free Abelian group of polytabloids, the formal linear combinations with integer coefficients of Young tabloids. To any Young tableau *T* one associates a polytabloid *e*<sub>T</sub> as follows. One first forms the orbit of *T* under the action of the group ${\rm col}(T)$ of column permutations (another Young subgroup, defined similarly to row permutations but permuting positions within individual columns only). Then, writing the result of action on a tableau *T* by a column permutation *σ* as *Tσ*, defines:

$$e_T = \sum_{\sigma \in {\rm col}(T)} \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) \{T \sigma\}.$$

$\{T\}=\{T'\}$ *does not* imply $e_T=e_{T'}$, since the actions of row and column permutations do not commute in general.

- The Specht module *V<sub>λ</sub>* is then the subspace of the space of all polytabloids spanned by the polytabloids *e*<sub>T</sub> for all Young tableaux *T* of shape *λ*.

## Straightening polytabloids and the Garnir elements {#Straightening_polytabloids_and_the_Garnir_elements}

The above construction gives an explicit description of the Specht module *V*<sub>λ</sub>. However, the polytabloids associated to different Young tableaux are not necessarily linearly independent, indeed, the dimension of *V*<sub>λ</sub> is exactly the number of **standard** Young tableaux of shape λ. In fact, the polytabloids associated to standard Young tableaux span *V*<sub>λ</sub>; to express other polytabloids in terms of them, one uses a **straightening algorithm**.

Given a Young tableau *S*, we construct the polytabloid *e*<sub>*S*</sub> as above. Without loss of generality, all columns of *S* are increasing, otherwise we could instead start with the modified Young tableau with increasing columns, whose polytabloid will differ at most by a sign. *S* is then said to not have any *column descents*. We want to express *e*<sub>*S*</sub> as a [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of standard polytabloids, i.e. polytabloids associated to standard Young tableaux. To do this, we would like permutations π<sub>*i*</sub> such that in all tableaux *S*π<sub>i</sub>, a row descent has been eliminated, with $S(1 + \sum_i \pi_i) = 0$. This then expresses *S* in terms of polytabloids that are closer to being standard. The permutations that achieve this are the **Garnir elements**.

Suppose we want to eliminate a row descent from box $(x,y)$ to box $(x+1,y)$ in the Young tableau. We pick a subset $A = \{(x,y+k) \ | \ k \geq 0\}$ and a subset $B = \{(x+1,y-k) \ | \ k \geq 0\}$ of the boxes like the red and blue sets in the following diagram:

*Image: Row descent.svg.*

Then the Garnir element $g_{A,B}$ is defined to be $\sum_i \operatorname{sgn}(\pi_i) \pi_i$, where the π<sub>*i*</sub> are the permutations of the entries of the boxes of $A \cup B$ that keep both subsets *A* and *B* without column descents.

Consider the following Young tableau:

There is a row descent in the second row, so we choose the subsets *A* and *B* as indicated, which gives us the following:

This gives us the Garnir element $g_{A,B} = 1 - (4 5) + (2 4 5) + (4 6 5) - (2 4 6 5) + (2 5) (4 6)$. This allows us to remove the row descent in the second row, but this has also introduced other descents in other places. But there is a way in which all tableaux obtained like this are closer to being standard, this is measured by a *dominance order* on polytabloids. Therefore, one can repeatedly apply this procedure to *straighten* a polytabloid, eventually writing it as a linear combination of standard polytabloids, showing that the Specht module is spanned by the standard polytabloids. As they are also linearly independent, they form a basis of this module.

## Other interpretations {#Other_interpretations}

There is a similar description for the irreducible representations of *GL*<sub>*n*</sub>. In that case, one can consider the [Weyl modules](wikipedia://en/32084687) associated to a partition λ, which can be described in terms of [bideterminants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bideterminant). One has a similar straightening algorithm, but this time in terms of semistandard Young tableaux.
