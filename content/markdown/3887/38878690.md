# KIK-30

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="KIK-30"}

The **KIK-30** "Really Simple Key loader" (RASKL) is a [fill device](wikipedia://en/4319754) made by [Sypris Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sypris_Electronics) and approved by the US [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) for the distribution of NSA [Type 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_1_product) cryptographic [keys](wikipedia://en/53039). It can also store and transfer related communications security material, including control data ("load sets") for [frequency hopping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_hopping) radios, such as [SINCGARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SINCGARS) and [Have Quick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Have_Quick).[^o2] It can store up to 40 cryptographic keys and has male and female [U-229](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-229) connectors for the NSA DS-101 and 102 fill protocol, allowing it to be plugged into most other NSA fill devices and [EKMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EKMS) equipment. It is 6.14 inches (159 mm) long, weighs less than one pound (454 g) and is powered by four [AAA batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AAA_battery). The operator interface has an 8 line of 20 characters and 6 buttons, with what Sypris calls "1-button key squirt" and 2-button zeroize (clear memory).[^o3]

:::

A simpler device than the [AN/CYZ-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/CYZ-10), the KIK-30 is now planned to replace the venerable [KYK-13](wikipedia://en/4013761) fill devices, with up to \$200 million budgeted in 2009 to procure the newer units in quantity.[^o4]
