# Samuel Sanford Shapiro

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Samuel Sanford Shapiro"}

**Samuel Sanford Shapiro** (July 13, 1930 – November 5, 2023) was an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/26685) and engineer. He was a professor emeritus of statistics at [Florida International University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_International_University). He was known for his co-authorship of the [Shapiro–Wilk test](wikipedia://en/2690983) and the [Shapiro–Francia test](wikipedia://en/53902171).

:::

A native of [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City), Shapiro graduated from [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York) with a degree in statistics in 1952, and took an MS in industrial engineering at Columbia University in 1954. He briefly served as a statistician in the [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army) [Chemical Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Corps), before earning a MS (1960) and PhD (1963) in statistics at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University). In 1972 he joined the faculty at Florida International University.

In 1987 he was elected a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o3]

Shapiro died on November 5, 2023, at the age of 93.[^o4]
