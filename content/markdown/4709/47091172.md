# Wiener–Araya graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wiener–Araya graph"}

The **Wiener–Araya graph** is, in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a graph on 42 vertices with 67 edges. It is [hypohamiltonian](wikipedia://en/18301304), which means that it does not itself have a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle) but every graph formed by removing a single vertex from it is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph). It is also [planar](wikipedia://en/24314).

:::

## History {#History}

Hypohamiltonian graphs were first studied by Sousselier in *Problèmes plaisants et délectables* (1963).[^o4] In 1967, Lindgren built an infinite sequence of hypohamiltonian graphs.[^o5] He first cited Gaudin, Herz and Rossi,[^o6] then Busacker and Saaty[^o7] as pioneers on this topic.

From the start, the smallest [hypohamiltonian graph](wikipedia://en/18301304) is known: the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926). However, the hunt for the smallest [planar](wikipedia://en/24314) [hypohamiltonian graph](wikipedia://en/18301304) continues. This question was first raised by [Václav Chvátal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Václav_Chvátal) in 1973.[^o8] The first candidate answer was provided in 1976 by [Carsten Thomassen](wikipedia://en/7460839), who exhibited a 105-vertices construction, the [105-Thomassen graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/105-Thomassen_graph).[^o9] In 1979, Hatzel improved this result with a [planar](wikipedia://en/24314) [hypohamiltonian graph](wikipedia://en/18301304) on 57 vertices : the [Hatzel graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatzel_graph).[^o10] This bound was lowered in 2007 by the [48-Zamfirescu graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/48-Zamfirescu_graph).[^o11]

In 2009, a graph built by Gábor Wiener and Makoto Araya became (with its 42 vertices) the smallest [planar](wikipedia://en/24314) [hypohamiltonian graph](wikipedia://en/18301304) known.[^o12][^o13] In their paper, Wiener and Araya conjectured their graph to be optimal arguing that its order ([42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42_%28number%29)) appears to be the [answer to The Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrases_from_The_Hitchhiker's_Guide_to_the_Galaxy#Answer_to_the_Ultimate_Question_of_Life,_the_Universe,_and_Everything_%2842%29) from [The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hitchhiker's_Guide_to_the_Galaxy), a [Douglas Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Adams) novel. However, subsequently, smaller planar hypohamiltonian graphs have been discovered.[^o14]
