# Brahmagupta theorem

*Image: Brahmaguptra's theorem.svg.* Diagram of Brahmagupta's theorem. solid red 2px solid black 2px solid blue 3px

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brahmagupta theorem"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **Brahmagupta's theorem** states that if a [cyclic quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/194131) is [orthodiagonal](wikipedia://en/30425383) (that is, has [diagonals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonals) that are [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944)), then the perpendicular to a side from the point of intersection of the diagonals always [bisects](wikipedia://en/152547) the opposite side.[^o2] It is named after the [Indian mathematician](wikipedia://en/1158496) [Brahmagupta](wikipedia://en/297206) (598-668).[^o3]

:::

More specifically, let $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ be four points on a circle such that the lines $AC$ and $BD$ are perpendicular. Denote the intersection of $AC$ and $BD$ by $M$. Drop the perpendicular from $M$ to the line $BC$, calling the intersection $E$. Let $F$ be the intersection of the line $EM$ and the edge $AD$. Then, the theorem states that $F$ is the [midpoint](wikipedia://en/437979) of $AD$.

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

*Image: Proof of Brahmagupta's theorem.svg.* Proof of the theorem

We need to prove that $AF = FD$. We will prove that both $AF$ and $FD$ are in fact equal to $FM$.

To prove that $AF = FM$, first note that the angles $\angle FAM$ and $\angle CBM$ are equal, because they are [inscribed angles](wikipedia://en/462730) that intercept the same [arc](wikipedia://en/5578920) of the circle ($CD$). Furthermore, the angles $\angle CBM$ and $\angle CME$ are both [complementary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complementary_angles) to angle $\angle BCM$ (i.e., they add up to 90°), and are therefore equal. Finally, the angles $\angle CME$ and $\angle FMA$ are the same. Hence, $\triangle AFM$ is an [isosceles triangle](wikipedia://en/315428), and thus the sides $AF$ and $FM$ are equal.

The proof that $FD = FM$ goes similarly: the angles $\angle FDM$, $\angle BCM$, $\angle BME$ and $\angle DMF$ are all equal, so $\triangle DFM$ is an isosceles triangle, so $FD = FM$. It follows that $AF = FD$, as the theorem claims.

:::
