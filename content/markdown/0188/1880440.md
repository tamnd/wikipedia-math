# Freudenthal suspension theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Freudenthal suspension theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and specifically in the field of [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), the **Freudenthal suspension theorem** is the fundamental result leading to the concept of stabilization of [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) and ultimately to [stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/2946655). It explains the behavior of simultaneously taking [suspensions](wikipedia://en/1251702) and increasing the index of the homotopy groups of the space in question. It was proved in 1937 by [Hans Freudenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Freudenthal).

:::

The theorem is a corollary of the [homotopy excision theorem](wikipedia://en/40769910).

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *X* be an [*n*-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-connected) [pointed space](wikipedia://en/1236666) (a pointed [CW-complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex) or pointed [simplicial set](wikipedia://en/1027784)). The map

$$X \to \Omega(\Sigma X)$$

induces a map

$$\pi_k(X) \to \pi_k(\Omega(\Sigma X))$$

on homotopy groups, where Ω denotes the [loop functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loop_functor) and Σ denotes the [reduced suspension functor](wikipedia://en/1251702). The suspension theorem then states that the induced map on homotopy groups is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) if *k* ≤ 2*n* and an [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539) if *k* = 2*n* + 1.

A basic result on loop spaces gives the relation

$$\pi_k(\Omega (\Sigma X)) \cong \pi_{k+1}(\Sigma X)$$

so the theorem could otherwise be stated in terms of the map

$$\pi_k(X) \to \pi_{k+1}(\Sigma X),$$

with the small caveat that in this case one must be careful with the indexing.

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

As mentioned above, the Freudenthal suspension theorem follows quickly from [homotopy excision](wikipedia://en/40769910); this proof is in terms of the natural map $\pi_k(X)\to\pi_{k+1}(\Sigma X)$. If a space $X$ is $n$-connected, then the pair of spaces $(CX,X)$ is $(n+1)$-connected, where $CX$ is the [reduced cone](wikipedia://en/782162) over $X$; this follows from the [relative homotopy long exact sequence](wikipedia://en/297506#Relative_homotopy_groups). We can decompose $\Sigma X$ as two copies of $CX$, say $(CX)_+, (CX)_-$, whose intersection is $X$. Then, homotopy excision says the inclusion map:

$$((CX)_+, X)\subset (\Sigma X,(CX)_-)$$

induces isomorphisms on $\pi_i, i < 2n+2$ and a surjection on $\pi_{2n+2}$. From the same relative long exact sequence, $\pi_i(X)=\pi_{i+1}(CX,X),$ and since in addition cones are contractible,

$$\pi_i(\Sigma X,(CX)_-)=\pi_i(\Sigma X).$$

Putting this all together, we get

$$\pi_i(X)=\pi_{i+1}((CX)_+,X)=\pi_{i+1}((\Sigma X,(CX)_-)=\pi_{i+1}(\Sigma X)$$

for $i+1<2n+2$, i.e. $i\leqslant 2n$, as claimed above; for $i=2n+1$ the left and right maps are isomorphisms, regardless of how connected $X$ is, and the middle one is a surjection by excision, so the composition is a surjection as claimed.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=named-heading name="Corollary 1"}

### Corollary 1 {#Corollary_1}

Let *S<sup>n</sup>* denote the *n*-sphere and note that it is (*n* − 1)-connected so that the groups $\pi_{n+k}(S^n)$ stabilize for $n \geqslant k+2$ by the Freudenthal theorem. These groups represent the *k*th stable [homotopy group of spheres](wikipedia://en/1104697).

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=named-heading name="Corollary 2"}

### Corollary 2 {#Corollary_2}

More generally, for fixed *k* ≥ 1, *k* ≤ 2*n* for sufficiently large *n*, so that any *n*-connected space *X* will have corresponding stabilized homotopy groups. These groups are actually the homotopy groups of an object corresponding to *X* in the [stable homotopy category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_homotopy_category).

:::
