# Exponential sheaf sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exponential sheaf sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **exponential sheaf sequence** is a fundamental [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) of [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) used in [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101).

:::

Let *M* be a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742), and write *O*<sub>*M*</sub> for the sheaf of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) on *M*. Let *O*<sub>*M*</sub>\* be the subsheaf consisting of the non-vanishing holomorphic functions. These are both sheaves of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974). The [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) gives a sheaf homomorphism

$$\exp : \mathcal O_M \to \mathcal O_M^*,$$

because for a holomorphic function *f*, exp(*f*) is a non-vanishing holomorphic function, and exp(*f* + *g*) = exp(*f*)exp(*g*). Its [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) is the sheaf 2π*i***Z** of [locally constant functions](wikipedia://en/295629) on *M* taking the values 2π*in*, with *n* an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). The **exponential sheaf sequence** is therefore

$$0\to 2\pi i\,\mathbb Z \to \mathcal O_M\to\mathcal O_M^*\to 0.$$

The exponential mapping here is not always a surjective map on sections; this can be seen for example when *M* is a [punctured disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punctured_disk) in the complex plane. The exponential map *is* surjective on the [stalks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalk_of_a_sheaf): Given a [germ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_of_a_function) *g* of an holomorphic function at a point *P* such that *g*(*P*) ≠ 0, one can take the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) of *g* in a neighborhood of *P*. The [long exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_exact_sequence) of [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) shows that we have an exact sequence

$$\cdots\to H^0(\mathcal O_U) \to H^0(\mathcal O_U^*)\to H^1(2\pi i\,\mathbb Z|_U) \to \cdots$$

for any open set *U* of *M*. Here *H*<sup>0</sup> means simply the sections over *U*, and the sheaf cohomology *H*<sup>1</sup>(2π*i***Z**|<sub>*U*</sub>) is the [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology) of *U*.

One can think of *H*<sup>1</sup>(2π*i***Z**|<sub>*U*</sub>) as associating an integer to each loop in *U*. For each section of *O*<sub>*M*</sub>\*, the connecting homomorphism to *H*<sup>1</sup>(2π*i***Z**|<sub>*U*</sub>) gives the [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113) for each loop. So this homomorphism is therefore a generalized [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113) and measures the failure of *U* to be [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible). In other words, there is a potential topological obstruction to taking a *global* logarithm of a non-vanishing holomorphic function, something that is always *locally* possible.

A further consequence of the sequence is the exactness of

$$\cdots\to H^1(\mathcal O_M)\to H^1(\mathcal O_M^*)\to H^2(2\pi i\,\mathbb Z)\to \cdots.$$

Here *H*<sup>1</sup>(*O*<sub>*M*</sub>\*) can be identified with the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) of [holomorphic line bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_line_bundle) on *M*. The connecting homomorphism sends a line bundle to its first [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349).
