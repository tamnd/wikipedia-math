# Littlewood's three principles of real analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Littlewood's three principles of real analysis"}

**Littlewood's three principles of [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478)** are [heuristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristics) of [J. E. Littlewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._E._Littlewood) to help teach the essentials of [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="The Principles"}

## The Principles {#The_Principles}

Littlewood stated the principles in his 1944 *Lectures on the Theory of Functions* [^o4] as: There are three principles, roughly expressible in the following terms: Every ([measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_set)) set is nearly a finite sum of intervals; every function (of class *L*<sup>p</sup>) is nearly [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122); every [convergent](wikipedia://en/285773) sequence of functions is nearly [uniformly convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_convergent).

The first principle is based on the fact that the [inner measure](wikipedia://en/5798355) and [outer measure](wikipedia://en/488605) are equal for measurable sets, the second is based on [Lusin's theorem](wikipedia://en/2257262), and the third is based on [Egorov's theorem](wikipedia://en/1767634).

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Littlewood's three principles are quoted in several real analysis texts, for example Royden,[^o7] Bressoud,[^o8] and Stein & Shakarchi.[^o9]

Royden[^o10] gives the [bounded convergence theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_convergence_theorem) as an application of the third principle. The theorem states that if a uniformly bounded sequence of functions converges pointwise, then their integrals on a set of finite measure converge to the integral of the limit function. If the convergence were uniform this would be a trivial result, and Littlewood's third principle tells us that the convergence is almost uniform, that is, uniform outside of a set of arbitrarily small measure. Because the sequence is bounded, the contribution to the integrals of the small set can be made arbitrarily small, and the integrals on the remainder converge because the functions are uniformly convergent there.

:::
