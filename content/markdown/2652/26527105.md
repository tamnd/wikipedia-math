# Carlo Severini

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Carlo Severini"}

**Carlo Severini** (10 March 1872 – 11 May 1951) was an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902): he was born in [Arcevia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcevia) ([Province of Ancona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Ancona)) and died in [Pesaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesaro). Severini, independently from [Dmitri Fyodorovich Egorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Egorov), proved and published earlier a proof of the theorem now known as [Egorov's theorem](wikipedia://en/1767634).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He graduated in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) on November 30, 1897:[^o4][^o5] the title of his "[Laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea)" [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) was "*Sulla rappresentazione analitica delle funzioni arbitrarie di variabili reali*".[^o6] After obtaining his [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_degree), he worked in [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna) as an assistant to the chair of [Salvatore Pincherle](wikipedia://en/11950956) until 1900.[^o7] From 1900 to 1906, he was a senior high school teacher, first teaching in the [Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Technology) of [La Spezia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Spezia) and then in the [lyceums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyceum) of [Foggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foggia) and of [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin);[^o8] then, in 1906 he became full professor of [Infinitesimal Calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_Calculus) at the [University of Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Catania). He worked in [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania) until 1918, then he went to the [University of Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genova), where he stayed until his retirement in 1942.[^o8]

## Work {#Work}

He authored more than 60 papers, mainly in the areas of [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713) and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), according to (Tricomi 1962). His main contributions belong to the following fields of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831):[^o11]

### Approximation theory {#Approximation_theory}

In this field, Severini proved a generalized version of the [Weierstrass approximation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_approximation_theorem). Precisely, he extended the original result of [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459) to the class of [bounded](wikipedia://en/311509) [locally integrable functions](wikipedia://en/4258398), which is a class including particular [discontinuous functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discontinuous_function) as members.[^o13]

### Measure theory and integration {#Measure_theory_and_integration}

Severini proved [Egorov's theorem](wikipedia://en/1767634) one year earlier than [Dmitri Egorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Egorov)[^o15] in the paper (Severini 1910, 3—4), whose main theme is nevertheless the study of [sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_%28mathematics%29) of [orthogonal functions](wikipedia://en/453041) and their properties.[^o17]

### Partial differential equations {#Partial_differential_equations}

Severini proved an [existence theorem](wikipedia://en/251177) for the [Cauchy problem](wikipedia://en/1725027) for the [non linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear_partial_differential_equation) [hyperbolic partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/1575825) of first order

$$\left\{ \begin{array}{lc} \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}=f\left(x,y,u,\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right) & (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^+\times[a,b]\\ u(0,y)=U(y) & y\in[a,b]\Subset\mathbb{R} \end{array}\right.,$$

assuming that the Cauchy data $U$ (defined in the [bounded interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_interval) $[a,b]$) and that the [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f$ has [Lipschitz continuous](wikipedia://en/51731) first order [partial derivatives](wikipedia://en/52565),[^o19] jointly with the obvious requirement that the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $\{(x,y,z,p)=(0,y,U(y),U^\prime(y));y\in[a,b]\}$ is contained in the [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) of $f$.[^o20]

### Real analysis and unfinished works {#Real_analysis_and_unfinished_works}

According to (Straneo 1952, 99), he worked also on the foundations of the theory of [real functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_function).[^o23] Severini also left an unpublished and unfinished [treatise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatise) on the theory of [real functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_function), whose title was planned to be "*Fondamenti dell'analisi nel campo reale e i suoi sviluppi"*.[^o24]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- Severini, Carlo. (1897). "Sulla rappresentazione analitica delle funzioni reali discontinue di variabile reale". *Atti della Reale Accademia delle Scienze di Torino.*. 33. p. 1002–1023. <https://archive.org/stream/attidellaraccad04unkngoog#page/n1120/mode/1up>. In this paper Severini extends the standard Weierstrass approximation theorem to a wider class of functions characterised by the fact that they can have a particular kind of discontinuities.
- Severini, C. (1910). "Sulle successioni di funzioni ortogonali". *Atti dell'Accademia Gioenia*. 3 (5). p. Memoria XIII, 1–7. <https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/page/48602347>. This paper contains Severini's most known and cited result, i.e. the Severini–Egorov theorem.
