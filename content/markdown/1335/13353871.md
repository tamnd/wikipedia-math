# Dawson–Gärtner theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dawson–Gärtner theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Dawson–Gärtner theorem** is a result in [large deviations theory](wikipedia://en/3772909). Heuristically speaking, the Dawson–Gärtner theorem allows one to transport a [large deviation principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_deviation_principle) on a “smaller” [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) to a “larger” one.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let (*Y*<sub>*j*</sub>)<sub>*j*∈*J*</sub> be a [projective system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_system) of [Hausdorff topological spaces](wikipedia://en/13637) with maps *p*<sub>*ij*</sub> : *Y*<sub>*j*</sub> → *Y*<sub>*i*</sub>. Let *X* be the projective limit (also known as the inverse limit) of the system (*Y*<sub>*j*</sub>, *p*<sub>*ij*</sub>)<sub>*i*,*j*∈*J*</sub>, i.e.

$$X = \varprojlim_{j \in J} Y_{j} = \left\{ \left. y = (y_{j})_{j \in J} \in Y = \prod_{j \in J} Y_{j} \quad \right| \quad i < j \implies y_{i} = p_{ij} (y_{j}) \right\}.$$

Let (*μ*<sub>*ε*</sub>)<sub>*ε*>0</sub> be a family of [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) on *X*. Assume that, for each *j* ∈ *J*, the [push-forward measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Push-forward_measure) (*p*<sub>*j*∗</sub>*μ*<sub>*ε*</sub>)<sub>*ε*>0</sub> on *Y*<sub>*j*</sub> satisfy the large deviation principle with [good rate function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_rate_function) *I*<sub>*j*</sub> : *Y*<sub>*j*</sub> → **R** ∪ {+∞}. Then the family (*μ*<sub>*ε*</sub>)<sub>*ε*>0</sub> satisfies the large deviation principle on *X* with good rate function *I* : *X* → **R** ∪ {+∞} given by

$$I(x) = \sup_{j \in J} I_{j}(p_{j}(x)).$$

:::
