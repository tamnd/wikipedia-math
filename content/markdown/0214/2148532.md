# Plastic ratio

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Plastic ratio"}

In mathematics, the **plastic ratio** is a geometrical [proportion](wikipedia://en/1021), given by the unique real [solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_root) of the equation $x3 = x + 1.$ Its decimal expansion begins with $1.324717957244746$ . It is the smallest [Pisot number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot_number).

:::

The adjective *plastic* does not refer to [the artificial material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic), but to the formative and sculptural qualities of this ratio, as in *plastic arts*.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Plastic cuboid.svg.* ρ = $\tfrac{a}{b}$ = $\tfrac{b+c}{a}$ = $\tfrac{b}{c}$. For b = 1 the boxes have volumes $\rho3 = \rho (\operatorname{red}) + 1 (\operatorname{green}).$

Three quantities $a > b > c > 0$ are in the plastic ratio if

$$\frac{b}{c} = \frac{a}{b} = \frac{b+c}{a}$$

This ratio is commonly denoted $\rho$ ([rho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rho)).

Substituting $b=\rho c \,$ and $a=\rho b =\rho^2 c \,$ in the last fraction,

$$\rho =\frac{c(\rho+1)}{\rho^2 c}.$$

It follows that the plastic ratio is the unique real solution of the [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787) $\rho^3 -\rho -1 =0.$

Solving with [Cardano's formula](wikipedia://en/180787#Cardano's_formula),

$$\begin{aligned} w_{1,2} &=\frac12 \left( 1 \pm \frac13 \sqrt{\frac{23}{3}} \right) \\ \rho &=\sqrt[3]{w_1} +\sqrt[3]{w_2} \end{aligned}$$

or, using the [hyperbolic cosine](wikipedia://en/180787#Trigonometric_and_hyperbolic_solutions),[^o6]

$$\rho =\frac{2}{ \sqrt{3}} \cosh \left( \frac{1}{3} \operatorname{arcosh} \left( \frac{3 \sqrt{3}}{2} \right) \right).$$

*Image: Plastic Newton map.svg.* Newton's method for $p(z) = z3 - z - 1:$ $\rho$ (right) and its complex conjugates at the nuclei of their [basins of attraction](wikipedia://en/191094#Basins_of_attraction). [Julia set](wikipedia://en/56434) of the Newton map in orange, with unit circle and real curve for reference.

$\rho$ is the superstable [fixed point](wikipedia://en/7344320) of the iteration $x \gets (2x^3 +1) /(3x^2 -1) ,$ which is the update step of [Newton's method](wikipedia://en/22145) applied to $x^3 -x -1 =0.$

The iteration $x \gets \sqrt{1 +\tfrac{1}{x}}$ results in the continued reciprocal square root

$$\rho =\sqrt{1 +\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 +\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 +\cfrac{1}{\ddots}}}}}}$$

Dividing the defining trinomial $x^3 -x -1$ by $x -\rho$ one obtains $x^2 +\rho x +1 /\rho ,$ and the [conjugate elements](wikipedia://en/458467) of $\rho$ are

$$x_{1,2} = \frac12 \left( -\rho \pm i \sqrt{3 \rho^2 - 4} \right),$$

with $x_1 +x_2 =-\rho \;$ and $\; x_1x_2 =1 /\rho.$

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: PlasticSquare\_6.png.* Rectangles in aspect ratios $\rho, \rho^{2}, \rho^{3}$ (top) and $\rho^{2}, \rho, \rho^{3}$ (bottom row) tile the square.

The plastic ratio $\rho$ and [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) $\varphi$ are the only morphic numbers: real numbers $x > 1$ for which there exist natural numbers m and n such that $x +1 =x^m$ and $\;x -1 =x^{-n}.$ Morphic numbers can serve as basis for a system of measure.[^o9]

Properties of $\rho$ (m=3 and n=4) are related to those of $\varphi$ (m=2 and n=1). For example, The plastic ratio satisfies the [continued radical](wikipedia://en/2503693#Infinitely_nested_radicals)

$$\rho =\sqrt[3]{1 +\sqrt[3]{1 +\sqrt[3]{\phantom{|} 1 +\cdots}}} ,$$

while the golden ratio satisfies the analogous

$$\varphi =\sqrt{1 +\sqrt{1 +\sqrt{\phantom{|} 1 +\cdots}}}$$

The plastic ratio can be expressed in terms of itself as the infinite [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630)

$$\begin{aligned} \rho^5 &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \rho^{-n} \\ \rho^3 &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \rho^{-2n} \\ \rho^2 &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \rho^{-3n} \\ \frac{\rho^5}{\rho^2 +1} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \rho^{-4n} \\ \rho &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \rho^{-5n} \\ \frac{\rho^5}{\rho +2} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \rho^{-6n} \\ \frac{\rho^3}{2} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \rho^{-7n},\end{aligned}$$

in comparison to the series for the golden ratio

$$\begin{aligned} \varphi^2 &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \varphi^{-n} \\ \varphi &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \varphi^{-2n} \\ \frac{\varphi^2}{2} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \varphi^{-3n}.\end{aligned}$$

Additionally, $\sum_{n=0}^{13} \rho^{-n} =4,$ while $\sum_{n=0}^{2} \varphi^{-n} =2.$

For every integer $n$ one has

$$\begin{aligned} \rho^n &=\rho^{n-2} +\rho^{n-3}\\ &=\rho^{n-1} +\rho^{n-5}\\ &=\rho^{n-3} +\rho^{n-4} +\rho^{n-5} \end{aligned}$$

from this an infinite number of further relations can be found.

The algebraic solution of a reduced quintic equation can be written in terms of square roots, cube roots and the [Bring radical](wikipedia://en/3191883). If $y =x^{5} +x$ then $x = BR(y).$ Since $\rho^{-5} +\rho^{-1} =1, \;\rho =1 /BR(1).$

[Continued fraction](wikipedia://en/46802) pattern of a few low powers

$$\begin{aligned} \rho^{-1} &= [0;1,3,12,1,1,3,2,3,2,...] \approx 0.7549 \;(\tfrac{25}{33}) \\ \rho^0 &= [1] \\ \rho^1 &= [1;3,12,1,1,3,2,3,2,4,...] \approx 1.3247 \;(\tfrac{45}{34}) \\ \rho^2 &= [1;1,3,12,1,1,3,2,3,2,...] \approx 1.7549 \;(\tfrac{58}{33}) \\ \rho^3 &= [2;3,12,1,1,3,2,3,2,4,...] \approx 2.3247 \;(\tfrac{79}{34}) \\ \rho^4 &= [3;12,1,1,3,2,3,2,4,2,...] \approx 3.0796 \;(\tfrac{40}{13}) \\ \rho^5 &= [4;12,1,1,3,2,3,2,4,2,...] \approx 4.0796 \;(\tfrac{53}{13})\,... \\ \rho^7 &= [7;6,3,1,1,4,1,1,2,1,1,...] \approx 7.1592 \;(\tfrac{93}{13})\,... \\ \rho^9 &= [12;1,1,3,2,3,2,4,2,141,...] \approx 12.5635 \;(\tfrac{88}{7}) \end{aligned}$$

The convergents of the continued fraction expansion of the plastic ratio are good rational approximations:

$$\tfrac{4}{3}, \tfrac{49}{37}, \tfrac{53}{40}, \tfrac{102}{77}, \tfrac{257}{194}, \tfrac{359}{271}, \tfrac{820}{619}, \tfrac{2819}{2128}, \tfrac{6458}{4875}, \tfrac{28651}{21628}, \tfrac{63760}{48131}, \ldots$$

The plastic ratio is the smallest [Pisot number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot_number).[^o10] By definition of these numbers, the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991#Complex_numbers) $1 /\sqrt{\rho}$ of the algebraic conjugates is smaller than 1, thus powers of $\rho$ generate [almost integers](wikipedia://en/14041933). For example: $\rho^{29} =3480.0002874...$ $\approx 3480 +1/3479$. After 29 rotation steps the [phases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_%28waves%29) of the inward spiraling conjugate pair – initially close to $\pm 45 \pi/58$ – nearly align with the imaginary axis.

The infinite radicals

$$\begin{aligned} \rho &=\sideset{^{\tfrac13}}{ } (1 +\sideset{^{\tfrac13}}{ } (1 +\sideset{^{\tfrac13}}{ } (1 +\cdots))), \\ 1/\rho &=\sideset{^{-\tfrac12}}{} (1 +\sideset{^{-\tfrac12}}{} (1 +\sideset{^{-\tfrac12}}{} (1 +\cdots ))) \end{aligned}$$

are analogous to

$$\begin{aligned} \xi &=\sideset{^{\tfrac14}}{ } (1 +\sideset{^{\tfrac14}}{ } (1 +\sideset{^{\tfrac14}}{ } (1 +\cdots))), \\ 1/\xi &=\sideset{^{-\tfrac13}}{ } (1 +\sideset{^{-\tfrac13}}{ } (1 +\sideset{^{-\tfrac13}}{ } (1 +\cdots))), \end{aligned}$$

with [Perron number](wikipedia://en/5362893) $\xi$ the positive real root of $x^4 =x +1.$ The other real root is the negative reciprocal of the second Pisot number $\chi$.

The three zeros of the cubic $z^3 +z^2 -\rho z -(\rho +1)$ lie on a circle with radius $\rho$. Similarly, the zeros of $z^3 +\tfrac{1}{\xi} z^2 -z -(\tfrac{1}{\xi} +1)$ lie on a circle with radius $\xi$. In both cases, the [complex conjugate zeros](wikipedia://en/458467) have approximate [argument](wikipedia://en/14699765) $\pm \tfrac56 \pi$.

The [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) of the plastic ratio $m(x) =x^3 -x -1$ has [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) $\Delta=-23 .$ The [Hilbert class field](wikipedia://en/3021036) of imaginary [quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781) $K = \mathbb{Q}( \sqrt{\Delta})$ can be formed by adjoining $\rho$. With argument $\tau=(1 +\sqrt{\Delta})/2\,$ a generator for the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of $K$, one has the special value of [Dedekind eta](wikipedia://en/447020) quotient

$$\rho =\frac{ e^{\pi i/24}\,\eta(\tau)}{ \sqrt{2}\,\eta(2\tau)} .$$

Expressed in terms of the [Weber-Ramanujan class invariant G<sub>n</sub>](wikipedia://en/33559472)

$$\rho =\frac{ \mathfrak{f} ( \sqrt{ \Delta} ) }{ \sqrt{2} } = \frac{ G_{23} }{ \sqrt[4]{2} }.$$

Properties of the related [Klein j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988#The_q-expansion_and_moonshine) $j(\tau)$ result in near identity $e^{\pi \sqrt{- \Delta}} \approx \left( \sqrt{2}\,\rho \right)^{24} - 24 .$ The difference is $< 1/12659$.

The [elliptic integral singular value](wikipedia://en/27903678#Lambda-star) $k_{r} =\lambda^{*}(r)$ for $r=23$ has closed form expression

$$\lambda^{*}(23) =\sin ( \arcsin \left( ( \sqrt[4]{2}\,\rho)^{-12} \right) /2)$$

(which is less than 1/3 the [eccentricity](wikipedia://en/1239472#Ellipses) of the orbit of Venus).

## Van der Laan sequence {#Van_der_Laan_sequence}

*Image: Plastic5\_Rauzy\_sqr.png.* A fan of plastic Rauzy tiles with areas in ratio Ⴔ. The fractal boundary has [box-counting](wikipedia://en/601025) [dimension](wikipedia://en/285907) 1.11

In his quest for perceptible clarity, the Dutch [Benedictine monk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine_monk) and architect Dom [Hans van der Laan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_van_der_Laan) (1904-1991) asked for the minimum difference between two sizes, so that we will clearly perceive them as distinct. Also, what is the maximum ratio of two sizes, so that we can still relate them and perceive nearness. According to his observations, the answers are $1/4$ and $7/1$, spanning a single *order of size*.[^o17] Requiring proportional continuity, he constructed a [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630) of eight measures (*types of size*) with common ratio $2 / (3/4 + 1/7^{1/7}) \approx \rho.$ Put in rational form, this architectonic system of measure is constructed from a subset of the numbers that bear his name.

The Van der Laan numbers have a close connection to the [Perrin](wikipedia://en/30863738) and [Padovan sequences](wikipedia://en/2161709). In combinatorics, the number of [compositions](wikipedia://en/550741) of n into parts 2 and 3 is counted by the *n*th Van der Laan number.

The Van der Laan sequence is defined by the third-order [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)

$$V_n =V_{n-2} +V_{n-3} \text{ for } n > 2,$$

with initial values

$$V_1 =0, V_0 =V_2 =1.$$

The first few terms are 1, 0, 1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 12, 16, 21, 28, 37, 49, 65, 86,... . The limit ratio between consecutive terms is the plastic ratio:$\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} V_{n+1}/V_n =\rho.$

*Image: Nombre plastique2.svg.* The 1924 Cordonnier cut. With $S_{1} = 3, S_{2} = 4, S_{3} = 5$, the [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463) of $\tfrac{S_{2}}{S_{1}}, \tfrac{S_{1} + S_{2}}{S_{3}}\text{ and } \tfrac{S_{3}}{S_{2}}$ is $3 / s=133\% \approx \rho + 1/4922.$

The first 14 indices n for which $V_n$ is prime are n = 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 16, 21, 32, 39, 86, 130, 471, 668, 1264 . The last number has 154 decimal digits.

The sequence is extended to negative indices using

$$V_n =V_{n+3} -V_{n+1},$$

obtaining (1), 0, 0, 1,−1, 1, 0,−1, 2,−2, 1, 1,−3, 4,−3, 0, 4,−7, 7,−3,... .

Powers of the plastic ratio can be written with Van der Laan numbers as quadratic coefficients

$$\rho^n =\rho^2 V_{n-2} +\rho V_{n-1} +V_{n-3},$$

which is proved by [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881) on $n.$ This relation also holds for $n < 0.$ The order of the coefficients corresponds to the bottom row of matrix $Q$ below.

The [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) of the Van der Laan sequence for non-negative $n$ is given by

$$\frac{1}{1 -x^2 -x^3} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} V_n x^n \text{ for } x <\tfrac{1}{\rho}$$

The sequence is related to sums of [binomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/4668) by

$$V_n =\sum_{k =\lfloor (n +2)/3 \rfloor}^{\lfloor n /2 \rfloor}{k \choose n -2k}$$

The [characteristic equation](wikipedia://en/31050469) of the recurrence is $x^3 -x -1 =0.$ If the three solutions are real root $\rho$ and conjugate pair $\beta$ and $\gamma$, the Van der Laan numbers are given by the [Binet formula](wikipedia://en/10918#Binet's_formula) [^o20]

$$V_{n-1} =a \rho^n +b \beta^n +c \gamma^n ,$$

with real $a$ and conjugates $b$ and $c$ the roots of $23x^3 +x -1 =0.$

Since $\left\vert b \beta^n +c \gamma^n \right\vert < \rho^{-n/2}$, the number $V_n$ is the [nearest integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nearest_integer_function) to $\,a\,\rho^{n+1}$, with $n > 1$ and coefficient $a =\rho^2 /(\rho^3 +\rho +2) =$ 0.31062

Coefficients $a =b =c =1$ result in the Binet formula for the related sequence $P_n =2V_n +V_{n-3} .$

The first few terms are 3, 0, 2, 3, 2, 5, 5, 7, 10, 12, 17, 22, 29, 39, 51, 68, 90, 119,... .

This [Perrin sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perrin_sequence) has the [Fermat property](wikipedia://en/53453): if p is prime, $P_{p} \equiv P_1 \bmod p .$ The converse does not hold, but the small number of [pseudoprimes](wikipedia://en/28787420) $\,n \mid P_n$ makes the sequence special.[^o21] The only 7 composite numbers below $10^{8}$ to pass the test are n = 521${}^{2}$, 904631, 16532714, 24658561, 27422714, 27664033, 46672291.

*Image: Plastic\_Rauzy\_ac.png.* A plastic Rauzy fractal: the combined surface and the three separate tiles have areas in the ratios $\rho^{5} : \rho^{2} : \rho : 1.$

The Van der Laan numbers are obtained as integral powers $n > 2$ of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) with real [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429#Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors_of_matrices) $\rho$ [^o19]

$$Q = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,$$

$$Q^n = \begin{pmatrix} V_n & V_{n+1} & V_{n-1} \\ V_{n-1} & V_n & V_{n-2} \\ V_{n-2} & V_{n-1} & V_{n-3} \end{pmatrix}$$

The [trace](wikipedia://en/43270#Relationship_to_eigenvalues) of $Q^n$ gives the Perrin numbers.

Alternatively, $Q$ can be interpreted as [incidence matrix](wikipedia://en/420919) for a [D0L](wikipedia://en/37432570#D0L_system) [Lindenmayer system](wikipedia://en/46565) on the alphabet $\{a,b,c\}$ with corresponding [substitution rule](wikipedia://en/2452154)

$$\begin{cases} a \;\mapsto \;b \\ b \;\mapsto \;ac \\ c \;\mapsto \;a \end{cases}$$

and initiator $w_0=c$. The series of words $w_n$ produced by iterating the substitution have lengths $l(w_n) =V_{n+2}$. The number of $c, b\text{ and } a's$ in each word is equal to successive Van der Laan numbers. Associated to this string rewriting process is a set composed of three overlapping [self-similar](wikipedia://en/28782) tiles called the [Rauzy fractal](wikipedia://en/29344658), that visualizes the [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/20913490) information contained in a multiple-generation letter sequence.[^o23]

The ratio between the number of digits in successive strings for the ternary version of the [look-and-say sequence](wikipedia://en/563980) converges to $\rho$.[^o25]

## Geometry {#Geometry}

### Partitioning the square {#Partitioning_the_square}

*Image: Plastic square partitions.svg.* Three partitions of a square into similar rectangles, $1 = 3\cdot\tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{2}{3} + 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{6} = \tfrac{1}{\rho^{2}} + \tfrac{1}{\rho^{4}} + \tfrac{1}{\rho^{8}}$.

There are precisely three ways of partitioning a square into three similar rectangles:[^o28][^o29]

1. The trivial solution given by three congruent rectangles with aspect ratio 3:1.
1. The solution in which two of the three rectangles are congruent and the third one has twice the side lengths of the other two, where the rectangles have aspect ratio 3:2.
1. The solution in which the three rectangles are all of different sizes and where they have aspect ratio *ρ*<sup>2</sup>. The ratios of the linear sizes of the three rectangles are: *ρ* (large:medium); *ρ*<sup>2</sup> (medium:small); and *ρ*<sup>3</sup> (large:small). The internal, long edge of the largest rectangle (the square's fault line) divides two of the square's four edges into two segments each that stand to one another in the ratio *ρ.* The internal, coincident short edge of the medium rectangle and long edge of the small rectangle divides one of the square's other, two edges into two segments that stand to one another in the ratio *ρ*<sup>4</sup>.

The fact that a rectangle of aspect ratio *ρ*<sup>2</sup> can be used for dissections of a square into similar rectangles is equivalent to an algebraic property of the number *ρ*<sup>2</sup> related to the [Routh–Hurwitz theorem](wikipedia://en/2067581): all of its conjugates have positive real part.[^o30][^o31]

### Cubic Lagrange interpolation {#Cubic_Lagrange_interpolation}

*Image: Rho-Lagrange.svg.* The relation $\operatorname{crimson} + \operatorname{green} = \surd\rho$ in a rho-squared rectangle.

The unique positive node $t$ that optimizes cubic [Lagrange interpolation](wikipedia://en/217523) on the interval $[-1,1]$ is equal to $0.41779130...$ The square of $t$ is the single real root of polynomial $P(x) =25x^3 +17x^2 +2x -1$ with discriminant $D =-23^3.$[^o33] Expressed in terms of the plastic ratio, $t =\sqrt{\rho} /(\rho^2 +1),$ which is verified by [insertion](wikipedia://en/5438948#Algebra) into $P.$

With optimal node set $T =\{-1,-t, t, 1\},$ the [Lebesgue function](wikipedia://en/2070188) $\lambda_3(x)$ evaluates to the minimal cubic Lebesgue constant $\Lambda_3(T) = \frac{1 +t^2}{1 -t^2}\,$ at [critical point](wikipedia://en/1951424) $x_c =\rho^2 t.$[^o34]

The constants are related through $x_c +t =\sqrt{\rho}$ and can be expressed as infinite [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630)

$$\begin{aligned} x_c &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \sqrt{\rho^{-(8n +5)}} \\ t &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \sqrt{\rho^{-(8n +9)}}.\end{aligned}$$

Each term of the series corresponds to the diagonal length of a rectangle with edges in ratio $\rho^2,$ which results from the relation $\rho^n =\rho^{n-1} +\rho^{n-5},$ with $n$ odd. The diagram shows the sequences of rectangles with common shrink rate $\rho^{-4}$ converge at a single point on the diagonal of a rho-squared rectangle with length $\sqrt{\rho \vphantom{/}} =\sqrt{1 +\rho^{-4}}.$

### Plastic pentagon {#Plastic_pentagon}

*Image: Plastic pentagons.svg.* A [triskeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triskelion) of plastic pentagons.

A spiral of [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) with edges in ratio $\rho$ tiles a *plastic [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014)* with four angles of 120 and one of 60 degrees.[^o36] The initial triangle is positioned at the left-hand side of a [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) with base to side ratio $\rho$ and left base angle 60 degrees, so that two edges of the triangle are collinear with sides of the parallelogram. Scaling the parallelogram in ratio $\tfrac{1}{\rho},$ accompanied with a clockwise rotation by 60 degrees, the horizontal base is mapped onto the third edge of the triangle. The [centre of rotation](wikipedia://en/410009#Related_definitions_and_terminology) is on the short (falling) diagonal, dividing it in ratio $\rho^3$, the expansion rate for a half-turn. Iteration of the process traces an infinite, closed sequence of equilateral triangles with pentagonal boundary.

The [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373) through the vertices of all triangles has polar slope $k =\frac{3}{\pi} \ln( \rho).$ For parallelogram base $\rho$, the length of the short diagonal is $\sqrt{\rho^2 - \rho + 1}$ with angle $\arctan( \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{1 -2\rho}).$ The length of the discrete spiral is $\rho^5 =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \rho^{-n};$ the pentagon has area $\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \rho^3 =\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \rho^{-2n}.$

In the vector image, the construction is repeated on each side of a $\rho$ triangle. [John Rutherford Boyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_Boyd) discovered a related figure, build on the sides of the $\rho^{-4}$ triangle.[^o37]

*Image: Plastic pentagons\_alt.svg.* Spiral design by Rutherford Boyd.

### Plastic spiral {#Plastic_spiral}

A *plastic spiral* is a [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373) that gets wider by a factor of $\rho$ for every quarter turn. It is described by the [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation) $r( \theta) =a \exp(k \theta),$ with initial radius $a$ and parameter $k =\frac{2}{\pi} \ln( \rho).$ If drawn on a rectangle with sides in ratio $\rho$, the spiral has its pole at the foot of altitude of a triangle on the diagonal and passes through vertices of rectangles with aspect ratio $\rho^2$ which are perpendicularly aligned and successively scaled by a factor $\rho^{-1}.$

In 1838 [Henry Moseley](wikipedia://en/40756330) noticed that whorls of a shell of the [chambered nautilus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambered_nautilus) are in geometrical progression: "It will be found that the distance of any two of its whorls measured upon a radius vector is *one-third* that of the next two whorls measured upon the same radius vector ... The curve is therefore a logarithmic spiral."[^o39] Moseley thus gave the expansion rate $\sqrt[4]{3} \approx \rho -1/116$ for a quarter turn. Considering the plastic ratio a three-dimensional equivalent of the ubiquitous golden ratio, it appears to be a natural candidate for measuring the shell.

## History and names {#History_and_names}

$\rho$ was first studied by [Axel Thue](wikipedia://en/635533) in 1912 and by [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) in 1919.[^o43] French high school student Gérard Cordonnier discovered the ratio for himself in 1924. In his correspon­dence with [Hans van der Laan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_van_der_Laan) a few years later, he called it the radiant number (le nombre radiant). Van der Laan initially referred to it as the fundamental ratio (de grondverhouding), using the plastic number (het plastische getal) from the 1950s onward.[^n1] In 1944 [Carl Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602) showed that $\rho$ is the smallest possible [Pisot–Vijayaraghavan number](wikipedia://en/519303) and suggested naming it in honour of Thue.

*Image: Interieur bovenkerk, zicht op de middenbeuk met koorbanken voor de monniken - Mamelis - 20536587 - RCE.jpg.* The 1967 St. Benedictusberg Abbey church designed by Hans van der Laan.

Unlike the names of the [golden](wikipedia://en/12386) and [silver ratios](wikipedia://en/920526), the word plastic was not intended by van der Laan to refer to a specific substance, but rather in its adjectival sense, meaning something that can be given a three-dimensional shape.[^o45] This, according to [Richard Padovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Padovan), is because the characteristic ratios of the number, $\tfrac{3}{4}$ and $\tfrac{1}{7}$, relate to the limits of human perception in relating one physical size to another. Van der Laan designed the 1967 [St. Benedictusberg Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Benedictusberg_Abbey) church to these plastic number proportions.[^o46]

The plastic number is also sometimes called the silver number, a name given to it by [Midhat J. Gazalé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midhat_J._Gazalé) [^o47] and subsequently used by [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435),[^o48] but that name is more commonly used for the [silver ratio](wikipedia://en/920526) $1 + \sqrt{2}$, one of the ratios from the family of [metallic means](wikipedia://en/44108116) first described by [Vera W. de Spinadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_W._de_Spinadel). Gardner suggested referring to $\rho^{2}$ as "high phi", and [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095) created a special typographic mark for this name, a variant of the Greek letter phi ("φ") with its central circle raised, resembling the Georgian letter [pari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pari_%28letter%29) ("Ⴔ").
