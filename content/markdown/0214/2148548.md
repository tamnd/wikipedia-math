# Uniformization (set theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uniformization"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **axiom of uniformization** is a weak form of the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840). It states that if $R$ is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of $X\times Y$, where $X$ and $Y$ are [Polish spaces](wikipedia://en/396622), then there is a subset $f$ of $R$ that is a [partial function](wikipedia://en/23577) from $X$ to $Y$, and whose domain (the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of all $x$ such that $f(x)$ exists) equals

:::

$$\{x \in X \mid \exists y \in Y: (x,y) \in R\}\,$$

Such a function is called a **uniformizing function** for $R$, or a **uniformization** of $R$.

*Image: Uniformization ill.png.* Uniformization of relation *R* (light blue) by function *f* (red).

To see the relationship with the axiom of choice, observe that $R$ can be thought of as associating, to each element of $X$, a subset of $Y$. A uniformization of $R$ then picks exactly one element from each such subset, whenever the subset is [non-empty](wikipedia://en/9566). Thus, allowing arbitrary sets *X* and *Y* (rather than just Polish spaces) would make the axiom of uniformization equivalent to the axiom of choice.

A [pointclass](wikipedia://en/9304834) $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$ is said to have the **uniformization property** if every [relation](wikipedia://en/3931) $R$ in $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$ can be uniformized by a partial function in $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$. The uniformization property is implied by the [scale property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_property), at least for [adequate pointclasses](wikipedia://en/10192324) of a certain form.

It follows from [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) alone that $\boldsymbol{\Pi}^1_1$ and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^1_2$ have the uniformization property. It follows from the existence of sufficient [large cardinals](wikipedia://en/383644) that

- $\boldsymbol{\Pi}^1_{2n+1}$ and $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^1_{2n+2}$ have the uniformization property for every [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $n$.
- Therefore, the collection of [projective sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_set) has the uniformization property.
- Every relation in [L(R)](wikipedia://en/2250604) can be uniformized, but *not necessarily* by a function in L(R). In fact, L(R) does not have the uniformization property (equivalently, L(R) does not satisfy the axiom of uniformization).
  - (Note: it's trivial that every relation in L(R) can be uniformized *in V*, assuming V satisfies the axiom of choice. The point is that every such relation can be uniformized in some transitive inner model of V in which the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300) holds.)
