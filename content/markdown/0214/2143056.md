# Quasi-finite morphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-finite morphism"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) *f* : *X* → *Y* of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) is **quasi-finite** if it is of [finite type](wikipedia://en/70728366) and satisfies any of the following equivalent conditions:[^o2]

:::

- Every point *x* of *X* is isolated in its fiber *f*<sup>−1</sup>(*f*(*x*)). In other words, every [fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_%28algebraic_geometry%29) is a discrete (hence finite) set.
- For every point *x* of *X*, the scheme *f*<sup>−1</sup>(*f*(*x*)) = *X* ×<sub>Y</sub>Spec κ(*f*(*x*)) is a [finite](wikipedia://en/2139357) κ(*f*(*x*))-scheme. (Here κ(*p*) is the [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) at a point *p*.)
- For every point *x* of *X*, $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\otimes \kappa(f(x))$ is finitely generated over $\kappa(f(x))$.

Quasi-finite morphisms were originally defined by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) in [SGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Séminaire_de_géométrie_algébrique) 1 and did not include the finite type hypothesis. This hypothesis was added to the definition in [EGA](wikipedia://en/471387) II 6.2 because it makes it possible to give an algebraic characterization of quasi-finiteness in terms of [stalks](wikipedia://en/15879621).

For a general morphism *f* : *X* → *Y* and a point *x* in *X*, *f* is said to be **quasi-finite** at *x* if there exist open affine neighborhoods *U* of *x* and *V* of *f*(*x*) such that *f*(*U*) is contained in *V* and such that the restriction *f* : *U* → *V* is quasi-finite. *f* is **locally quasi-finite** if it is quasi-finite at every point in *X*.[^o3] A [quasi-compact](wikipedia://en/12410777) locally quasi-finite morphism is quasi-finite.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

For a morphism *f*, the following properties are true.[^o6]

- If *f* is quasi-finite, then the induced map *f*<sub>red</sub> between [reduced schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_scheme) is quasi-finite.
- If *f* is a [closed immersion](wikipedia://en/1623986), then *f* is quasi-finite.
- If *X* is [noetherian](wikipedia://en/2783891) and *f* is an immersion, then *f* is quasi-finite.
- If g : *Y* → *Z*, and if *g* ∘ *f* is quasi-finite, then *f* is quasi-finite if any of the following are true:
  1. *g* is [separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separated_morphism),
  1. *X* is noetherian,
  1. *X* ×<sub>*Z*</sub> *Y* is locally noetherian.

Quasi-finiteness is preserved by base change. The composite and fiber product of quasi-finite morphisms is quasi-finite.[^o6]

If *f* is [unramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#Unramified_and_étale_morphisms) at a point *x*, then *f* is quasi-finite at *x*. Conversely, if *f* is quasi-finite at *x*, and if also $\mathcal{O}_{f^{-1}(f(x)),x}$, the [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) of *x* in the fiber *f*<sup>−1</sup>(*f*(*x*)), is a field and a [finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) [separable extension](wikipedia://en/363340) of κ(*f*(*x*)), then *f* is unramified at *x*.[^o7]

[Finite morphisms](wikipedia://en/2139357) are quasi-finite.[^o8] A quasi-finite [proper morphism](wikipedia://en/603782) locally of finite presentation is finite.[^o9] Indeed, a morphism is finite [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is proper and locally quasi-finite.[^o10] Since proper morphisms are of finite type and finite type morphisms are quasi-compact[^o11] one may omit the qualification *locally*, i.e., a morphism is finite if and only if it is proper and quasi-finite.

A generalized form of **[Zariski Main Theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski_Main_Theorem)** is the following:[^o12] Suppose *Y* is [quasi-compact](wikipedia://en/6042#Introduction_and_definition) and [quasi-separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-separated). Let *f* be quasi-finite, separated and of finite presentation. Then *f* factors as $X \hookrightarrow X' \to Y$ where the first morphism is an open immersion and the second is finite. (*X* is open in a finite scheme over *Y*.)

:::
