# Alan Cobham (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alan Cobham"}

**Alan Belmont Cobham** (4 November 1927 – 28 June 2011) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) known for (with [Jack Edmonds](wikipedia://en/3625459) and [Michael O. Rabin](wikipedia://en/298404)) inventing the concept of [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) and the [complexity class P](wikipedia://en/658550),B for [Cobham's thesis](wikipedia://en/3262179) stating that the problems that have practically usable computer solutions are characterized by having polynomial time,B and for [Cobham's theorem](wikipedia://en/68044793) on the sets of numbers that can be recognized by [finite automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_automaton).C He also did foundational work on [automatic sequences](wikipedia://en/7740302),D invented [priority queues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priority_queue) and studied them from the point of view of [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578),A and wrote a program for playing [contract bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_bridge) that was one of the best in the world during the mid-1980s.

:::

Cobham was a student at [Oberlin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberlin_College), the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), and the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), but did not complete a doctorate. He became an [operations researcher](wikipedia://en/43476) for the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy), a researcher for [IBM Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Research) at the [Thomas J. Watson Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_J._Watson_Research_Center), and a professor and founding department chair of the computer science department at [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University).
