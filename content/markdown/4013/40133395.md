# Manuel Kauers

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Manuel Kauers"}

**Manuel Kauers** (born 20 February 1979 in [Lahnstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahnstein), [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany)) is a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist). He works on [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805) and its applications to [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492). He is currently a professor of algebra at [Johannes Kepler University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kepler_University) (JKU) in [Linz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linz), Austria, and leader of the Institute for Algebra at JKU. Before that, he was affiliated with that university's [Research Institute for Symbolic Computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Institute_for_Symbolic_Computation) (RISC).

:::

Kauers studied computer science at the [University of Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Karlsruhe) in Germany from 1998 to 2002 and then moved to RISC, where he completed his PhD in [symbolic computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_computation) in 2005 under the supervision of [Peter Paule](wikipedia://en/45070912). He earned his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in mathematics from JKU in 2008.

Together with [Doron Zeilberger](wikipedia://en/1724825) and [Christoph Koutschan](wikipedia://en/48644256), Kauers [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) two famous open [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) using large scale computer algebra calculations. Both proofs appeared in the [Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences). The first concerned a conjecture formulated by [Ira Gessel](wikipedia://en/53794712) on the number of certain lattice walks restricted to the quarter plane. This result was later generalized by [Alin Bostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alin_Bostan) and Kauers when they showed, also using computer algebra, that the [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) for these walks is [algebraic](wikipedia://en/974169). The second conjecture proven by Kauers, Koutschan and Zeilberger was the so-called [q-TSPP conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-TSPP_conjecture), a product formula for the orbit generating function of [totally symmetric plane partitions](wikipedia://en/2933308#Totally_symmetric_plane_partitions), which was formulated by [George Andrews](wikipedia://en/3356273) and [David Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_P._Robbins) in the early 1980s.

In 2009, Kauers received the [Start-Preis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Start-Preis), which is considered the most prestigious award for young scientists in Austria. In 2016, together with [Christoph Koutschan](wikipedia://en/48644256) and [Doron Zeilberger](wikipedia://en/1724825) he received the [David P. Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_P._Robbins) prize of the American Mathematical Society.
