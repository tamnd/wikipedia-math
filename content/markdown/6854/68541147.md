# Alois Höfler

*Image: Hoefler.jpg.* Alois Höfler

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alois Höfler"}

**Alois Höfler** (; April 6, 1853 – February 26, 1922) was an Austrian [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) and university professor of education. He was seen by the logical positivist [Otto Neurath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Neurath) as an important link between [Bernard Bolzano](wikipedia://en/302185)'s work and the [Vienna Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Circle).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Alois Höfler was born in [Kirchdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirchdorf_an_der_Krems) in Upper Austria. His father and mother died while he was in his early and middle teens. He and his two younger sisters were then raised by a second mother, Amalie Boheim.[^n1] He had four sons with his wife Auguste Dornhöffer,[^n2] including [Otto Höfler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Höfler).[^n3]

In the fall of 1871, Höfler entered the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) where he studied mathematics and physics with [Ludwig Boltzmann](wikipedia://en/544255) and [Josef Stefan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Stefan).[^n4] After his teaching examination in 1876 at the age of 23, he taught in the Josefstädter-[gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) and other gymnasiums (advanced secondary schools) in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866).[^n1][^n5] He received his doctoral degree at Vienna in 1885,[^n2][^n6] under [Alexius Meinong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexius_Meinong)'s remote supervision,[^n7][^n8] with a thesis titled *Some Laws of Incompatibility between Judgments* (*Einige Gesetze der Unverträglichkeiten zwischen Urteilen*).[^n2][^n6] He received his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at Vienna in 1894[^n7] with a thesis titled *Psychic Work* (*Psychische Arbeit*).[^n9][^n6]

During the 1870s and 1880s Höfler also attended evening classes taught by [Franz Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Brentano) and Meinong. His philosophical view was strongly influenced by Meinong. From 1881 to 1903 he taught mathematics, physics and philosophical propaedeutics at the grammar school of the [Theresian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresian_Academy) in Vienna.[^n6] He became professor of philosophy and pedagogy at the [German University of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_University_of_Prague) in 1903 (as successor to [Otto Willmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Willmann)) and at the University of Vienna in 1907 (as successor to Theodor Vogt).[^n10][^n11]

His increasing interest in philosophy and in making it more scientific led him to study and write increasingly on logic and psychology as well. In the opinion of the ardent [logical positivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_positivism) [Otto Neurath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Neurath), through his involvement in the [School of Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Brentano), Höfler was an important connection between the work on logic of [Bernard Bolzano](wikipedia://en/302185) and the [Vienna Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Circle).[^n12][^n13] In 1914, he reprinted the 77 years old Bolzano's *Theory of Science*, which was said to be almost impossible to find at the time.[^n14] He also loved music, poetry, and the arts. He strongly opposed attempting to reduce their role in schools.[^n2]

Höfler died in Vienna.
