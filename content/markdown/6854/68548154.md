# Amy C. King

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Amy C. King"}

*Image: AmyKing.jpg.*

**Amy Catheryne Patterson King** (December 30, 1928 – June 7, 2014) was an American mathematician and [mathematics educator](wikipedia://en/326471) who became Foundation Professor of mathematics at [Eastern Kentucky University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Kentucky_University), and was recognized for her distinguished teaching by the Kentucky Section of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).

:::

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Amy Catheryne Patterson was born on December 30, 1928, in [Douglas, Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas,_Wyoming). She married Don King, who became a professor of dentistry; they had no children. Her brother, James D. Patterson, was also an academic, a professor of physics at the [Florida Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Institute_of_Technology).

She became a member of the Centenary [United Methodist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Methodist_Church) of [Lexington, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Kentucky). She died on June 7, 2014, of burns and smoke inhalation from a fire at her home in Lexington.

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

King was a graduate of the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri). She earned a master's degree from [Wichita State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_State_University) in 1960, with the master's thesis *Selected methods for solving eigenvalue problems by variational procedures*. She joined the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) in 1961, and coauthored the 1963 book *Pathways to Probability: History of the Mathematics of Certainty and Chance* with Cecil Byron Read, published by Holt, Rinehart and Winston. She became a mathematics instructor, including stints at Wichita State University, [Washburn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washburn_University), the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas), and the [University of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kentucky).

In later life she returned to graduate study, working with [S. M. Shah](wikipedia://en/22900376) at the University of Kentucky on [transcendental functions](wikipedia://en/297021) of bounded [index](wikipedia://en/86113). She completed her Ph.D. in 1970, with the dissertation *A Class of Entire Functions of Bounded Index and Radii of Univalence of Some Functions of Zero Order* supervised by Shah. Her work in the early 1970s also included surveying the contributions of [women in mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_mathematics).

She taught mathematics at Eastern Kentucky University from 1972 until her retirement as Foundation Professor emerita in 1998.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

King became the inaugural winner of the annual Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics of the Kentucky Section of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), in 1992. She was named Foundation Professor at Eastern Kentucky University in 1993; this was "the highest honor the school gives to its teachers". A technology-enhanced mathematics classroom at Eastern Kentucky University was named for her in 2011, honoring her role as a "central figure in mathematics education at EKU".

Wichita State University offers a scholarship named for her, as does Eastern Kentucky University.
