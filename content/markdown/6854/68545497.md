# Mikhail Borovoi

*Image: Mikhail\_Borovoi.jpg.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mikhail Borovoi"}

**Mikhail Vol'fovich Borovoi** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Михаи́л Во́льфович Борово́й, [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): מיכאל בורובוי; born February 17, 1951) is a Soviet and Israeli mathematician. He has worked on [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) and on the arithmetic of [linear algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/395885), homogeneous spaces, [Shimura varieties](wikipedia://en/10389861), and [spherical varieties](wikipedia://en/39305846).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Borovoi was born in Moscow. He obtained his diploma (M.Sc.) in Mathematics at [Lomonosov Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), and his Ph.D. at the [Leningrad Department (now: St. Petersburg Department) of the Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/16031996) in 1980; his doctoral advisor was [Arkady L. Onishchik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkady_Lvovich_Onishchik). Because of [Antisemitism in the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_the_Soviet_Union) of the time, Borovoi could not find a job as a mathematician, and only with the start of [Perestroika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perestroika), in 1987 he got a position of a Senior Researcher at the [Khabarovsk Division of the Institute for Applied Mathematics of the Far Eastern Branch of the USSR Academy of Sciences](http://www.iam.khv.ru/index_eng.php)[^o4] in the [Far Eastern city of Khabarovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabarovsk). He spent the 1990-1991 academic year at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o4] Since 1992 he has been at [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University),[^o4] where now he is Professor Emeritus.[^o5]

## Research {#Research}

Borovoi is known for the Borovoi fundamental group[^o7] of a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386). Jointly with [James S. Milne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_S._Milne),[^o8] Borovoi proved[^o9] Shimura's conjecture in the theory of [Shimura varieties](wikipedia://en/10389861), which was the subject of Borovoi's invited talk[^o10] at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), Berkeley, 1986. He proved that the [Brauer-Manin obstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brauer-Manin_obstruction) is the only obstruction to the [Hasse principle](wikipedia://en/489707) and weak approximation for homogeneous spaces of connected [linear algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/395885) over [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) with connected geometric stabilizers.[^o11] Jointly with Cyril Demarche, he proved a similar result for the Brauer-Manin obstruction to strong approximation[^o12]
