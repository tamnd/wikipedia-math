# Brauer's height zero conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brauer's height zero conjecture"}

The **Brauer Height Zero Conjecture** is a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) in [modular representation theory](wikipedia://en/1110685) of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) relating the degrees of the [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) irreducible [characters](wikipedia://en/691741) in a [Brauer block](wikipedia://en/1110685) and the structure of its [defect groups](wikipedia://en/1110685#Defect_groups). It was formulated by [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134) in 1955. The proof was completed in 2024.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $G$ be a finite group and $p$ a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666). The set ${\rm Irr}(G)$ of irreducible complex [characters](wikipedia://en/691741) can be partitioned into Brauer $p$-[blocks](wikipedia://en/1110685#Blocks_and_the_structure_of_the_group_algebra). To each $p$-block $B$ is canonically associated a conjugacy class of [$p$-subgroups](wikipedia://en/24664) of $G$, called the [defect groups](wikipedia://en/1110685#Defect_groups) of $B$. The set of irreducible characters belonging to $B$ is denoted by $\mathrm{Irr}(B)$.

Let $\nu$ be the [discrete valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_valuation) defined on the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) by $\nu(mp^a)=a$ where $m$ is coprime to $p$. Brauer proved that if $B$ is a block with defect group $D$ then $\nu(\chi(1))\geq \nu(|G:D|)$ for each $\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(B)$. The *height* of $\chi$ is defined to be the non-negative integer *$h:=\nu(\chi(1))-\nu(|G:D|)$* .

**Brauer's Height Zero Conjecture** asserts that $\nu(\chi(1))=\nu(|G:D|)$ for all $\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(B)$ if and only if $D$ is [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974). In other words: all irreducible complex characters belonging to a block have height zero if and only if the block's defect group is abelian.

:::

## History {#History}

Brauer's Height Zero Conjecture was formulated by [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134) in 1955.[^o5] It also appeared as Problem 23 in Brauer's list of problems.[^o6] Brauer's Problem 12 of the same list asks whether the [character table](wikipedia://en/911520) of a finite group $G$ determines if its Sylow $p$-subgroups are abelian. Solving Brauer's height zero conjecture for blocks whose defect groups are Sylow $p$-subgroups (or equivalently, that contain a character of degree coprime to $p$) also gives a solution to Brauer's Problem 12.

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

The proof of the *if* direction of the conjecture was completed by [Radha Kessar](wikipedia://en/57412986) and [Gunter Malle](wikipedia://en/58001049)[^o9] in 2013 after a reduction to [finite simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group) by Thomas R. Berger and Reinhard Knörr.[^o10]

The *only if* direction was proved for [$p$-solvable](wikipedia://en/105012#p-solvable) groups by David Gluck and Thomas R. Wolf in 1984.[^o11] The so-called generalized Gluck—Wolf theorem, which was a main obstacle towards a proof of the Height Zero Conjecture was proven by [Gabriel Navarro](wikipedia://en/78201740) and [Pham Huu Tiep](wikipedia://en/58794546) in 2013.[^o12] Gabriel Navarro and Britta Späth showed in 2014 that the so-called **inductive Alperin—McKay condition** for simple groups implied Brauer's Height Zero Conjecture.[^o13] Lucas Ruhstorfer completed the proof of these conditions for the case $p=2$ in 2022.[^o14] The case of odd primes was finally settled by Gunter Malle, Gabriel Navarro, A. A. Schaeffer Fry and Pham Huu Tiep in 2024 using a different reduction theorem.[^o15]

:::
