# Outer space (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Outer space"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) subject of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), the **Culler–Vogtmann Outer space** or just **Outer space** of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) *F*<sub>*n*</sub> is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) consisting of the so-called "marked metric graph structures" of volume 1 on *F*<sub>*n*</sub>. The Outer space, denoted *X*<sub>*n*</sub> or *CV*<sub>*n*</sub>, comes equipped with a natural [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of the [group of outer automorphisms](wikipedia://en/628466) [Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>)](wikipedia://en/12747184) of *F*<sub>*n*</sub>. The Outer space was introduced in a 1986 paper[^o2] of [Marc Culler](wikipedia://en/29258614) and [Karen Vogtmann](wikipedia://en/20455422), and it serves as a free group analog of the [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661) of a [hyperbolic surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_surface). Outer space is used to study homology and cohomology groups of Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) and to obtain information about [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923), [geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) and dynamical properties of Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>), of its [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) and individual outer automorphisms of *F*<sub>*n*</sub>. The space *X*<sub>*n*</sub> can also be thought of as the set of *F*<sub>*n*</sub>-equivariant [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) types of minimal [free](wikipedia://en/12781#Remarkable_properties_of_actions) discrete isometric actions of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> on [**R**-trees](wikipedia://en/371914) *T* such that the quotient metric graph *T*/*F*<sub>*n*</sub> has volume 1.

:::

## History {#History}

The Outer space $X_n$ was introduced in a 1986 paper[^o2] of [Marc Culler](wikipedia://en/29258614) and [Karen Vogtmann](wikipedia://en/20455422), inspired by analogy with the [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661) of a hyperbolic surface. They showed that the natural action of $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$ on $X_n$ is [properly discontinuous](wikipedia://en/12781#Remarkable_properties_of_actions), and that $X_n$ is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414).

In the same paper Culler and Vogtmann constructed an embedding, via the *translation length functions* discussed below, of $X_n$ into the infinite-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) $\mathbb{P}^{\,\mathcal{C}} = \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{C}} \!-\! \{0\}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}$, where $\mathcal{C}$ is the set of nontrivial [conjugacy classes](wikipedia://en/49176) of elements of $F_n$. They also proved that the closure $\overline X_n$ of $X_n$ in $\mathbb{P}^{\,\mathcal{C}}$ is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042).

Later a combination of the results of Cohen and Lustig[^o4] and of Bestvina and Feighn[^o5] identified (see Section 1.3 of [^o6]) the space $\overline X_n$ with the space $\overline{CV}_n$ of projective classes of "very small" minimal isometric actions of $F_n$ on [$\mathbb R$-trees](wikipedia://en/371914).

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Marked metric graph"}

### Marked metric graphs {#Marked_metric_graphs}

Let *n* ≥ 2. For the free group *F*<sub>*n*</sub> fix a "rose" *R*<sub>*n*</sub>, that is a wedge, of *n* circles [wedged](wikipedia://en/684143) at a vertex *v*, and fix an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397) between *F*<sub>*n*</sub> and the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) $\pi$<sub>1</sub>(*R*<sub>*n*</sub>, *v*) of *R*<sub>*n*</sub>. From this point on we identify *F*<sub>*n*</sub> and $\pi$<sub>1</sub>(*R*<sub>*n*</sub>, *v*) via this isomorphism.

A **marking** on *F*<sub>*n*</sub> consists of a [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence) *f* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ where Γ is a finite [connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph) without degree-one and degree-two vertices. Up to a (free) [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250), *f* is uniquely determined by the isomorphism *f*<sub>\#</sub> : $\pi$<sub>1</sub>(*R*<sub>*n*</sub>) → $\pi$<sub>1</sub>(Γ), that is by an isomorphism *F*<sub>*n*</sub> → $\pi$<sub>1</sub>(Γ).

A **metric graph** is a finite connected graph $\gamma$ together with the assignment to every topological edge *e* of Γ of a positive [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) *L*(*e*) called the *length* of *e*. The *volume* of a metric graph is the sum of the lengths of its topological edges.

A **marked metric graph structure** on *F*<sub>*n*</sub> consists of a marking *f* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ together with a metric graph structure *L* on Γ.

Two marked metric graph structures *f*<sub>1</sub> : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ<sub>1</sub> and *f*<sub>2</sub> : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ<sub>2</sub> are *equivalent* if there exists an isometry *θ* : Γ<sub>1</sub> → Γ<sub>2</sub> such that, up to free homotopy, we have *θ* ∘ *f*<sub>1</sub> = *f*<sub>2</sub>.

The **Outer space** *X*<sub>*n*</sub> consists of [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of all the volume-one marked metric graph structures on *F*<sub>*n*</sub>.

:::

### Weak topology on the Outer space {#Weak_topology_on_the_Outer_space}

#### Open simplices {#Open_simplices}

Let *f* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ where Γ is a marking and let *k* be the number of topological edges in Γ. We order the edges of Γ as *e*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *e*<sub>*k*</sub>. Let

$$\Delta_k = \left\{ (x_1, \dots, x_k)\in \mathbb{R}^k \,\Big|\, \sum_{i=1}^k x_i =1, x_i >0 \text{ for } i=1,\dots, k\right\}$$

be the standard (*k* − 1)-dimensional open [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) in **R**<sup>*k*</sup>.

Given *f*, there is a natural map *j* : Δ<sub>*k*</sub> → *X*<sub>*n*</sub>, where for *x* = (*x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*k*</sub>) ∈ Δ<sub>*k*</sub>, the point *j*(*x*) of *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is given by the marking *f* together with the metric graph structure *L* on Γ such that *L*(*e*<sub>*i*</sub>) = *x*<sub>*i*</sub> for *i* = 1, ..., *k*.

One can show that *j* is in fact an [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) map, that is, distinct points of Δ<sub>*k*</sub> correspond to non-equivalent marked metric graph structures on *F*<sub>*n*</sub>.

The set *j*(Δ<sub>*k*</sub>) is called *open simplex* in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> corresponding to *f* and is denoted *S*(*f*). By construction, *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is the union of open simplices corresponding to all markings on *F*<sub>*n*</sub>. Note that two open simplices in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> either are disjoint or coincide.

#### Closed simplices {#Closed_simplices}

Let *f* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ where Γ is a marking and let *k* be the number of topological edges in Γ. As before, we order the edges of Γ as *e*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *e*<sub>*k*</sub>. Define Δ<sub>*k*</sub>′ ⊆ **R**<sup>*k*</sup> as the set of all *x* = (*x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*k*</sub>) ∈ **R**<sup>*k*</sup>, such that $\textstyle{\sum_{i=1}^k x_i=1}$, such that each *x*<sub>*i*</sub> ≥ 0 and such that the set of all edges *e*<sub>*i*</sub> in *$\Gamma$* with *x*<sub>*i*</sub> = 0 is a subforest in Γ.

The map *j* : Δ<sub>*k*</sub> → *X*<sub>*n*</sub> extends to a map *h* : Δ<sub>*k*</sub>′ → *X*<sub>*n*</sub> as follows. For *x* in Δ<sub>*k*</sub> put *h*(*x*) = *j*(*x*). For *x* ∈ Δ<sub>*k*</sub>′ − Δ<sub>*k*</sub> the point *h*(*x*) of *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is obtained by taking the marking *f*, contracting all edges *e*<sub>*i*</sub> of *$\Gamma$* with *x*<sub>*i*</sub> = 0 to obtain a new marking *f*<sub>1</sub> : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ<sub>1</sub> and then assigning to each surviving edge *e*<sub>*i*</sub> of Γ<sub>1</sub> length *x*<sub>*i*</sub> > 0.

It can be shown that for every marking *f* the map *h* : Δ<sub>*k*</sub>′ → *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is still injective. The image of *h* is called the *closed simplex* in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> corresponding to *f* and is denoted by *S*′(*f*). Every point in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> belongs to only finitely many closed simplices and a point of *X*<sub>*n*</sub> represented by a marking *f* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ where the graph Γ is tri-valent belongs to a unique closed simplex in *X*<sub>*n*</sub>, namely *S*′(*f*).

The **weak topology** on the Outer space *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is defined by saying that a subset *C* of *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is closed [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) for every marking *f* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ the set *h*<sup>−1</sup>(*C*) is closed in Δ<sub>*k*</sub>′. In particular, the map *h* : Δ<sub>*k*</sub>′ → *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is a [topological embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_embedding).

### Points of Outer space as actions on trees {#Points_of_Outer_space_as_actions_on_trees}

Let *x* be a point in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> given by a marking *f* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ with a volume-one metric graph structure *L* on Γ. Let *T* be the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of Γ. Thus *T* is a [simply connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected_graph), that is *T* is a topological tree. We can also lift the metric structure *L* to *T* by giving every edge of *T* the same length as the length of its image in Γ. This turns *T* into a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) (*T*, *d*) which is a [real tree](wikipedia://en/371914). The fundamental group $\pi$<sub>1</sub>(Γ) acts on *T* by [covering transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_transformation) which are also isometries of (*T*, *d*), with the quotient space *T*/$\pi$<sub>1</sub>(Γ) = Γ. Since the [induced homomorphism](wikipedia://en/17018463) *f*<sub>\#</sub> is an isomorphism between *F*<sub>*n*</sub> = $\pi$<sub>1</sub>(*R*<sub>*n*</sub>) and $\pi$<sub>1</sub>(Γ), we also obtain an isometric action of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> on *T* with *T*/*F*<sub>*n*</sub> = Γ. This action is [free](wikipedia://en/12781#Remarkable_properties_of_actions) and [discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discontinuous_action). Since Γ is a finite connected graph with no degree-one vertices, this action is also *minimal*, meaning that *T* has no proper *F*<sub>*n*</sub>-invariant subtrees.

Moreover, every minimal free and discrete isometric action of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> on a real tree with the quotient being a metric graph of volume one arises in this fashion from some point *x* of *X*<sub>*n*</sub>. This defines a bijective correspondence between *X*<sub>*n*</sub> and the set of equivalence classes of minimal free and discrete isometric actions of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> on a real trees with volume-one quotients. Here two such actions of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> on real trees *T*<sub>1</sub> and *T*<sub>2</sub> are *equivalent* if there exists an *F*<sub>*n*</sub>-equivariant isometry between *T*<sub>1</sub> and *T*<sub>2</sub>.

#### Length functions {#Length_functions}

Give an action of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> on a real tree *T* as above, one can define the *translation length function* associate with this action:

$$\ell_T: F_n \to \mathbb R, \quad \ell_T(g)=\min_{t\in T} d(t,gt), \quad\text{ for } g\in F_n.$$

For *g* ≠ 1 there is a (unique) isometrically embedded copy of **R** in *T*, called the *axis* of *g*, such that *g* acts on this axis by a translation of magnitude $\ell_T(g)>0$. For this reason $\ell_T(g)$ is called the *translation length* of *g*. For any *g*, *u* in *F*<sub>*n*</sub> we have $\ell_T(ugu^{-1}) = \ell_T(g)$, that is the function $\ell_T$ is constant on each [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) in *G*.

In the marked metric graph model of Outer space translation length functions can be interpreted as follows. Let *T* in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> be represented by a marking *f* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ with a volume-one metric graph structure *L* on Γ. Let *g* ∈ *F*<sub>*n*</sub> = $\pi$<sub>1</sub>(*R*<sub>*n*</sub>). First push *g* forward via *f*<sub>\#</sub> to get a closed loop in Γ and then tighten this loop to an immersed circuit in Γ. The *L*-length of this circuit is the translation length $\ell_T(g)$ of *g*.

A basic general fact from the theory of group actions on real trees says that a point of the Outer space is uniquely determined by its translation [length function](wikipedia://en/3021393). Namely if two trees with minimal free isometric actions of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> define equal translation length functions on *F*<sub>*n*</sub> then the two trees are *F*<sub>*n*</sub>-equivariantly isometric. Hence the map $T\mapsto \ell_T$ from *X*<sub>*n*</sub> to the set of **R**-valued functions on *F*<sub>*n*</sub> is injective.

One defines the **length function topology** or **axes topology** on *X*<sub>*n*</sub> as follows. For every *T* in *X*<sub>*n*</sub>, every finite subset *K* of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> and every *ε* > 0 let

$$V_T(K,\epsilon)= \{ T'\in X_n: |\ell_T(g)-\ell_{T'}(g)| < \epsilon \text{ for every } g\in K\}.$$

In the length function topology for every *T* in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> a basis of [neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28mathematics%29) of *T* in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is given by the family *V*<sub>*T*</sub>(*K*, *ε*) where *K* is a finite subset of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> and where *ε* > 0.

[Convergence of sequences](wikipedia://en/285773#Topological_spaces) in the length function topology can be characterized as follows. For *T* in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> and a sequence *T*<sub>*i*</sub> in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> we have $\lim_{i\to\infty} T_i = T$ if and only if for every *g* in *F*<sub>*n*</sub> we have $\lim_{i\to\infty} \ell_{T_i}(g) = \ell_T(g).$

### Gromov topology {#Gromov_topology}

Another topology on $X_n$ is the so-called *Gromov topology* or the *equivariant Gromov–Hausdorff convergence topology*, which provides a version of [Gromov–Hausdorff convergence](wikipedia://en/482366) adapted to the setting of an isometric group action.

When defining the Gromov topology, one should think of points of $X_n$ as actions of $F_n$ on $\mathbb R$-trees. Informally, given a tree $T\in X_n$, another tree $T'\in X_n$ is "close" to $T$ in the Gromov topology, if for some large finite subtrees of $Y\subseteq T, Y'\subseteq T'\in X_n$ and a large finite subset $B\subseteq F_n$ there exists an "almost isometry" between $Y'$ and $Y$ with respect to which the (partial) actions of $B$ on $Y'$ and $Y$ almost agree. For the formal definition of the Gromov topology see.[^o17]

#### Coincidence of the weak, the length function and Gromov topologies {#Coincidence_of_the_weak,_the_length_function_and_Gromov_topologies}

An important basic result states that the Gromov topology, the [weak topology](wikipedia://en/33662) and the length function topology on *X*<sub>*n*</sub> coincide.[^o19]

## Action of Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) on Outer space {#Action_of_Out(Fn)_on_Outer_space}

The group [Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>)](wikipedia://en/12747184) admits a natural right [action](wikipedia://en/12781) by [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) on *X*<sub>*n*</sub>.

First we define the action of the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) Aut(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) on *X*<sub>*n*</sub>. Let *α* ∈ Aut(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) be an [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) of *F*<sub>*n*</sub>. Let *x* be a point of *X*<sub>*n*</sub> given by a marking *f* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ with a volume-one metric graph structure *L* on Γ. Let *τ* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → *R*<sub>*n*</sub> be a homotopy equivalence whose [induced homomorphism](wikipedia://en/17018463) at the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) level is the automorphism *α* of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> = $\pi$<sub>1</sub>(*R*<sub>*n*</sub>). The element *xα* of *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is given by the marking *f* ∘ *τ* : *R*<sub>*n*</sub> → Γ with the metric structure *L* on Γ. That is, to get *xα* from *x* we simply precompose the marking defining *x* with *τ*.

In the real tree model this action can be described as follows. Let *T* in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> be a real tree with a minimal free and discrete co-volume-one isometric action of *F*<sub>*n*</sub>. Let *α* ∈ Aut(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>). As a metric space, *Tα* is equal to *T*. The action of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> is twisted by *α*. Namely, for any *t* in *T* and *g* in *F*<sub>*n*</sub> we have:

$$g\underset{T\alpha}{\cdot} t= \alpha(g) \underset{T}{\cdot} t.$$

At the level of translation length functions the tree *Tα* is given as:

$$\ell_{T\alpha}(g)=\ell_T(\alpha(g)) \quad \text{ for } g\in F_n.$$

One then checks that for the above action of Aut(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) on Outer space *X*<sub>*n*</sub> the subgroup of [inner automorphisms](wikipedia://en/61839) Inn(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) is contained in the kernel of this action, that is every inner automorphism acts trivially on *X*<sub>*n*</sub>. It follows that the action of Aut(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) on *X*<sub>*n*</sub> quotients through to an action of Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) = Aut(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>)/Inn(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) on *X*<sub>*n*</sub>. namely, if *φ* ∈ Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) is an outer automorphism of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> and if *α* in Aut(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) is an actual automorphism representing *φ* then for any *x* in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> we have *xφ* = *xα*.

The right action of Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) on *X*<sub>*n*</sub> can be turned into a left action via a standard conversion procedure. Namely, for *φ* ∈ Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) and *x* in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> set

*φx* = *xφ*<sup>−1</sup>.

This left action of Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) on *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is also sometimes considered in the literature although most sources work with the right action.

### Moduli space {#Moduli_space}

The quotient space *M*<sub>*n*</sub> = *X*<sub>*n*</sub>/Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) is the **moduli space** which consists of isometry types of finite connected graphs Γ without degree-one and degree-two vertices, with [fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004) isomorphic to *F*<sub>*n*</sub> (that is, with the first [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) equal to *n*) equipped with volume-one metric structures. The [quotient topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_topology) on *M*<sub>*n*</sub> is the same as that given by the [Gromov–Hausdorff distance](wikipedia://en/482366) between metric graphs representing points of *M*<sub>*n*</sub>. The [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) *M*<sub>*n*</sub> is not [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) and the "cusps" in *M*<sub>*n*</sub> arise from decreasing towards zero lengths of edges for homotopically nontrivial subgraphs (e.g. an essential circuit) of a metric graph Γ.

## Basic properties and facts about Outer space {#Basic_properties_and_facts_about_Outer_space}

- Outer space *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414) and the action of Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>) on *X*<sub>*n*</sub> is [properly discontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Properly_discontinuous_action), as was proved by Culler and [Vogtmann](wikipedia://en/20455422) in their original 1986 paper[^o2] where Outer space was introduced.
- The space *X*<sub>*n*</sub> has [topological dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_dimension) 3*n* − 4. The reason is that if Γ is a finite connected graph without degree-one and degree-two vertices with [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) isomorphic to *F*<sub>*n*</sub>, then Γ has at most 3*n* − 3 edges and it has exactly 3*n* − 3 edges when Γ is trivalent. Hence the top-dimensional open simplex in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> has dimension 3*n* − 4.
- Outer space *X*<sub>*n*</sub> contains a specific [deformation retract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_retract) *K*<sub>*n*</sub> of *X*<sub>*n*</sub>, called the **spine** of Outer space. The spine *K*<sub>*n*</sub> has dimension 2*n* − 3, is Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>)-invariant and has compact quotient under the action of Out(*F*<sub>*n*</sub>).

## Unprojectivized Outer space {#Unprojectivized_Outer_space}

The *unprojectivized Outer space* $cv_n$ consists of equivalence classes of all marked metric graph structures on *F*<sub>*n*</sub> where the volume of the metric graph in the marking is allowed to be any positive real number. The space $cv_n$ can also be thought of as the set of all free minimal discrete isometric actions of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> on **R**-trees, considered up to *F*<sub>*n*</sub>-equivariant isometry. The unprojectivized Outer space inherits the same structures that $X_n$ has, including the coincidence of the three topologies (Gromov, axes, weak), and an $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$-action. In addition, there is a natural action of $\mathbb R_{>0}$ on $cv_n$ by [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373).

Topologically, $cv_n$ is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to $X_n\times (0,\infty)$. In particular, $cv_n$ is also contractible.

## Projectivized Outer space {#Projectivized_Outer_space}

The projectivized Outer space is the quotient space $CV_n:=cv_n/\mathbb R_{>0}$ under the action of $\mathbb R_{>0}$ on $cv_n$ by scalar multiplication. The space $CV_n$ is equipped with the quotient topology. For a tree $T\in cv_n$ its projective equivalence class is denoted $[T]=\{cT \mid c>0\}\subseteq cv_n$. The action of $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$ on $cv_n$ naturally quotients through to the action of $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$ on $CV_n$. Namely, for $\phi\in \operatorname{Out}(F_n)$ and $T\in cv_n$ put $[T]\phi:= [T\phi]$.

A key observation is that the map $X_n \to CV_n, T\mapsto [T]$ is an $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$-equivariant homeomorphism. For this reason the spaces $X_n$ and $CV_n$ are often identified.

## Lipschitz distance {#Lipschitz_distance}

The Lipschitz distance,[^o27] named for [Rudolf Lipschitz](wikipedia://en/339965), for Outer space corresponds to the Thurston metric in Teichmüller space. For two points $x, y$ in *X*<sub>*n*</sub> the (right) Lipschitz distance $d_R$ is defined as the (natural) [logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) of the maximally stretched closed path from $x$ to $y$:

$\Lambda_R(x,y):=\sup_{\gamma \in F_n\setminus \{1\}} \frac{\ell_y(\gamma)}{\ell_x(\gamma)}$ and $d_R(x,y):= \log \Lambda_R(x,y)$

This is an asymmetric metric (also sometimes called a [quasimetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasimetric)), i.e. it only fails symmetry $d_R(x,y)=d_R(y,x)$. The symmetric Lipschitz metric normally denotes:

$$d(x,y):=d_R(x,y)+d_R(y,x)$$

The [supremum](wikipedia://en/39382) $\Lambda_R(x,y)$ is always obtained and can be calculated by a [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742) the so called candidates of $x$.

$$\Lambda_R(x,y) = \max_{\gamma \in \operatorname{cand}(x)} \frac{\ell_y(\gamma)}{\ell_x(\gamma)}$$

*Image: Candidates.jpg.* A [simple loop](wikipedia://en/1189560), a [figure of eight](wikipedia://en/3526954), and a barbell

Where $\operatorname{cand}(x)$ is the finite set of conjugacy classes in *F*<sub>*n*</sub> which correspond to embeddings of a [simple loop](wikipedia://en/1189560), a [figure of eight](wikipedia://en/3526954), or a barbell into $x$ via the marking (see the diagram).

The stretching factor also equals the minimal Lipschitz constant of a homotopy equivalence carrying over the marking, i.e.

$$\Lambda_R(x,y)=\min_{h \in H(x,y)} Lip(h)$$

Where $H(x,y)$ are the continuous functions $h: x \to y$ such that for the marking $f_x$ on $x$ the marking $h \circ f_x$ is freely homotopic to the marking $f_y$ on $y$.

The induced topology is the same as the weak topology and the isometry group is $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$ for both, the symmetric and asymmetric Lipschitz distance.[^o28]

## Applications and generalizations {#Applications_and_generalizations}

- The closure $\overline{cv}_n$ of $cv_n$ in the length function topology is known to consist of (*F*<sub>*n*</sub>-equivariant isometry classes of) all *very small* minimal isometric actions of *F*<sub>*n*</sub> on **R**-trees.[^o30] Here the closure is taken in the space of all minimal isometric "irreducible" actions of $F_n$ on $\mathbb R$-trees, considered up to equivariant isometry. It is known that the Gromov topology and the axes topology on the space of irreducible actions coincide,[^o17] so the closure can be understood in either sense. The projectivization of $\overline{cv}_n$ with respect to multiplication by positive scalars gives the space $\overline{CV}_n$ which is the *length function compactification* of $CV_n$ and of $X_n$, analogous to Thurston's compactification of the Teichmüller space.
- Analogs and generalizations of the Outer space have been developed for [free products](wikipedia://en/360036),[^o31] for [right-angled Artin groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-angled_Artin_group),[^o32] for the so-called *deformation spaces* of group actions[^o19] and in some other contexts.
- A base-pointed version of Outer space, called *Auter space*, for marked metric graphs with base-points, was constructed by Hatcher and Vogtmann in 1998.[^o33] The Auter space $A_n$ shares many properties in common with the Outer space, but $A_n$ only comes with an action of $\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$.
