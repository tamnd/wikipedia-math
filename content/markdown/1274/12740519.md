# Edge-matching puzzle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edge-matching puzzle"}

*Image: Eternity\_II\_2.jpg.* A partially completed [Eternity II](wikipedia://en/12229416) edge-matching puzzle

An **edge-matching puzzle** is a type of [tiling puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiling_puzzle) involving [tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiling_%28mathematics%29) an area with (typically regular) [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621) whose edges are distinguished with colours or patterns, in such a way that the edges of adjacent tiles match.

:::

Edge-matching puzzles are known to be [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete), and adaptable for conversion to and from equivalent [jigsaw puzzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jigsaw_puzzle) and [polyomino](wikipedia://en/72634) packing puzzle.[^o2]

The first edge-matching puzzles were patented in the U.S. by [E. L. Thurston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._L._Thurston_%28puzzle_inventor%29) in 1892.[^o3] Current examples of commercial edge-matching puzzles include the [Eternity II puzzle](wikipedia://en/12229416), [Tantrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantrix), Kadon Enterprises' range of edge-matching puzzles, and the Edge Match Puzzles iPhone app.

## Notable variations {#Notable_variations}

### MacMahon Squares {#MacMahon_Squares}

*Image: MacMahon Squares solution.png.* A solution to MacMahon Squares with the largest single-color area[^o6]

MacMahon Squares is the name given to a [recreational math](wikipedia://en/26446) puzzle suggested by British mathematician [Percy MacMahon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Alexander_MacMahon), who published a treatise on edge-colouring of a variety of shapes in 1921.[^o7] This particular puzzle uses 24 tiles consisting of all permutations of 3 colors for the edges of a square. The tiles must be arranged into a 6×4 rectangular area such that all edges match and, furthermore, only one color is used for the outside edge of the rectangle.[^o8]

This puzzle can be extended to tiles with permutations of 4 colors, arranged in 10×7.[^o9] In either case, the squares are a subset of the [Wang tiles](wikipedia://en/65798), reducing tiles that are similar under rotation. Solutions number well into the thousands.[^o10]

MacMahon Squares, along with variations on the idea, was commercialized as Multimatch.

### TetraVex {#TetraVex}

*Image: GNOME Tetravex screenshot.png.* [GNOME Tetravex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNOME_Games)

TetraVex is a computer game that presents the player with a square grid and a collection of tiles, by default nine square tiles for a 3×3 grid. Each tile has four single-digit numbers, one on each edge. The objective of the game is to place the tiles into the grid in the proper position, completing this puzzle as quickly as possible. The tiles cannot be rotated, and two can be placed next to each other only if the numbers on adjacent edges match.[^o13][^o14]

TetraVex was inspired by "the problem of tiling the plane" as described by [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095) on page 382 of *Volume 1: Fundamental Algorithms*, the first book in his series *[The Art of Computer Programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_Computer_Programming)*. It was named by Scott Ferguson, the development lead and an architect of the first version of Visual Basic, who wrote it for [Windows Entertainment Pack 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_Entertainment_Pack_3).[^o15]

TetraVex is also available as an open source game in the [GNOME Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNOME_Games) collection.[^o16]

The possible number of TetraVex positions can be counted. On a $n\times{}n$ board there are $n(n-1)$ horizontal and vertical pairs that must match and $4n$ numbers along the edges that can be chosen arbitrarily. Hence there are $2n(n-1)+4n=2n(n+1)$ choices of 10 digits, i.e. $10^{2n(n+1)}$ possible boards.

Deciding if a TetraVex puzzle has a solution is in general [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete).[^o17] Its computational approach involves the [Douglas-Rachford algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas-Rachford_algorithm).[^o18]

:::

### Hexagons {#Hexagons}

*Image: Serpentile 021 3C 0-GR-B.svg.* A single-cross serpentile

[Serpentiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpentiles) are the hexagonal tiles used in various [abstract strategy games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_strategy_game) such as Psyche-Paths, Kaliko, and [Tantrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantrix). Within each serpentile, the edges are paired, thus restricting the set of tiles in such a way that no edge color occurs an odd number of times within the hexagon.

### Three dimensions {#Three_dimensions}

Mathematically, edge-matching puzzles are [two-dimensional](wikipedia://en/511647). A 3D edge-matching puzzle is such a puzzle that is not flat in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), so involves [tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiling_%28mathematics%29) a three-dimensional area such as the surface of a [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299). As before, [polygonal](wikipedia://en/23621) pieces have distinguished edges to require that the edges of adjacent pieces match.

3D edge-matching puzzles are not currently under direct U.S. patent protection, since the 1892 patent by E. L. Thurston has expired.[^o3] Current examples of commercial puzzles include the [Dodek Duo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodek_Duo), The Enigma, Mental Misery,[^o21] and Kadon Enterprises' range of three-dimensional edge-matching puzzles.[^o22]

## Incorporation of edge matching {#Incorporation_of_edge_matching}

The [Carcassonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcassonne_%28board_game%29) board game employs edge matching to constrain where its square tiles may be placed. The original game has three types of edges: fields, roads and cities.
