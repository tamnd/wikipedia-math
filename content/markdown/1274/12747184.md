# Out(Fn)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Out(Fn)"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Out(*F<sub>n</sub>*)** is the [outer automorphism group](wikipedia://en/628466) of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) on *n* [generators](wikipedia://en/99945). These groups are at universal stage in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), as they act on the set of [presentations](wikipedia://en/99494) with $n$ generators of any [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042).[^o2] Despite geometric analogies with general [linear groups](wikipedia://en/113564) and [mapping class groups](wikipedia://en/31060586), their complexity is generally regarded as more challenging, which has fueled the development of new techniques in the field.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $F_n$ be the free nonabelian group of rank $n \ge 1$. The set of [inner automorphisms](wikipedia://en/61839) of $F_n$, i.e. automorphisms obtained as conjugations by an element of $F_n$, is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) $\mathrm{Inn}(F_n) \triangleleft \mathrm{Aut}(F_n)$. The outer automorphism group of $F_n$ is the quotient

$$\mathrm{Out}(F_n) := \mathrm{Aut}(F_n)/\mathrm{Inn}(F_n).$$

An element of $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ is called an outer class.

:::

## Relations to other groups {#Relations_to_other_groups}

### Linear groups {#Linear_groups}

The [abelianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianization) map $F_n \to \mathbb{Z}^n$ induces a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) from $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ to the general linear group $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})$, the latter being the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of $\mathbb{Z}^n$. This map is onto, making $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ a [group extension](wikipedia://en/345807),

$$1\to \mathrm{Tor}(F_n) \to \mathrm{Out}(F_n) \to \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})\to 1.$$

The kernel $\mathrm{Tor}(F_n)$ is the [Torelli group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torelli_group) of $F_n$.

The map $\mathrm{Out}(F_2) \to \mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})$ is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828). This no longer holds for higher ranks: the Torelli group of $F_3$ contains the automorphism fixing two basis elements and multiplying the remaining one by the commutator of the two others.

### Aut(F<sub>n</sub>) {#Aut(Fn)}

By definition, $\mathrm{Aut}(F_n)$ is an extension of the inner automorphism group $\mathrm{Inn}(F_n)$ by $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$. The inner automorphism group itself is the image of the [action by conjugation](wikipedia://en/49176#Conjugacy_as_group_action), which has kernel the [center](wikipedia://en/7125) $Z(F_n)$. Since $Z(F_n)$ is trivial for $n\ge 2$, this gives a short exact sequence

$$1 \rightarrow F_n \rightarrow \mathrm{Aut}(F_n) \rightarrow \mathrm{Out}(F_n) \rightarrow 1.$$

For all $n \ge 2$, there are embeddings $\mathrm{Aut}(F_n) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Out}(F_{n+1})$ obtained by taking the outer class of the extension of an automorphism of $F_n$ fixing the additional generator. Therefore, when studying properties that are inherited by subgroups and quotients, the theories of $\mathrm{Aut}(F_n)$ and $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ are essentially the same.

### Mapping class groups of surfaces {#Mapping_class_groups_of_surfaces}

Because $F_n$ is the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a [bouquet of *n* circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouquet_of_circles), $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ can be described topologically as the [mapping class group](wikipedia://en/793295) of a bouquet of *n* circles (in the [homotopy category](wikipedia://en/2040756)), in analogy to the mapping class group of a closed [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) which is isomorphic to the outer automorphism group of the fundamental group of that surface.

Given any finite graph with fundamental group $F_n$, the graph can be "thickened" to a surface $S$ with one boundary component that retracts onto the graph. The [Birman exact sequence](wikipedia://en/31060586#The_Birman_exact_sequence) yields a map from the mapping class group $\mathrm{MCG}(S) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Out}(F_n)$. The elements of $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ that are in the image of such a map are called geometric. Such outer classes must leave invariant the cyclic word corresponding to the boundary, hence there are many non-geometric outer classes. A converse is true under some irreducibility assumptions,[^o9] providing geometric realization for outer classes fixing a conjugacy class.

## Known results {#Known_results}

- For $n\ge 4$, $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ is not linear, i.e. it has no [faithful representation](wikipedia://en/2617872) by matrices over a field (Formanek, Procesi, 1992);[^o11]
- For $n\ge 3$, the [isoperimetric function](wikipedia://en/20017026) of $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ is exponential (Hatcher, Vogtmann, 1996);[^o12]
- The [Tits Alternative](wikipedia://en/12704641) holds in $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$: each subgroup is either [virtually](wikipedia://en/10377971) [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012) or else it contains a free group of rank 2 (Bestvina, Feighn, Handel, 2000);[^o13]
- For $n\ge 3$, $\mathrm{Out}(\mathrm{Out}(F_n)) = {1}$ (Bridson and Vogtmann, 2000);[^o14]
- Every solvable subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ has a finitely generated [free abelian](wikipedia://en/247296) subgroup of finite index (Bestvina, Feighn, Handel, 2004);[^o15]
- For $i>0$, all but finitely many of the [$i$<sup>th</sup>-degree homology](wikipedia://en/248635#Group_homology) morphisms induced by the sequence $$\ldots \rightarrow \mathrm{Out}(F_{n-1}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Out}(F_n) \rightarrow \mathrm{Out}(F_{n+1}) \rightarrow \ldots$$ are isomorphisms (Hatcher and Vogtmann, 2004);[^o16]
- For $n\ge 2$, the reduced [$C^*$-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) of $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ (i.e. the closure of its image under the [regular representation](wikipedia://en/360011)) is simple;[^o17]
- For $n\ge 4$, if $\Gamma$ is a finite index subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$, then any subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ isomorphic to $\Gamma$ is a conjugate of $\Gamma$ (Farb and Handel, 2007);[^o18]
- For $n\ge 5$, $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ has [Kazhdan's property (T)](wikipedia://en/1362724) (Kaluba, Nowak, Ozawa, 2019 for $n=5$; Kaluba, Kielak, Nowak, 2021 for $n\ge 6$);[^o19]
- Actions on [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/1285375) complexes satisfying [acylindricity](wikipedia://en/55866076) conditions were constructed, in analogy with complexes like the [complex of curves](wikipedia://en/51024643) for mapping class groups;[^o20]
- For $n\ge 3$, $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ is rigid with respect to [measure equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measured_group_theory) (Guirardel and Horbez, 2021 preprint).[^o21]

## Outer space {#Outer_space}

Out(*F<sub>n</sub>*) acts [geometrically](wikipedia://en/12746935) on a [cell complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_complex) known as [Culler](wikipedia://en/29258614)–[Vogtmann](wikipedia://en/20455422) Outer space, which can be thought of as the [Fricke-Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661) for a [bouquet of circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouquet_of_circles).

::: {#o24 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

A point of the outer space is essentially an $\mathbb{R}$-graph *X* homotopy equivalent to a bouquet of *n* circles together with a certain choice of a free [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) class of a [homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/212250#Homotopy_equivalence_and_null-homotopy) from *X* to the bouquet of *n* circles. An $\mathbb{R}$-graph is just a weighted [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) with weights in $\mathbb{R}$. The sum of all weights should be 1 and all weights should be positive. To avoid ambiguity (and to get a finite dimensional space) it is furthermore required that the valency of each vertex should be at least 3.

A more descriptive view avoiding the homotopy equivalence *f* is the following. We may fix an identification of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of the bouquet of *n* circles with the [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) $F_n$ in *n* variables. Furthermore, we may choose a [maximal tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanning_tree_%28mathematics%29) in *X* and choose for each remaining edge a direction. We will now assign to each remaining edge *e* a word in $F_n$ in the following way. Consider the closed path starting with *e* and then going back to the origin of *e* in the maximal tree. Composing this path with *f* we get a closed path in a bouquet of *n* circles and hence an element in its fundamental group $F_n$. This element is not well defined; if we change *f* by a free homotopy we obtain another element. It turns out, that those two elements are conjugate to each other, and hence we can choose the unique [cyclically reduced](wikipedia://en/59735#Construction) element in this conjugacy class. It is possible to reconstruct the free homotopy type of *f* from these data. This view has the advantage, that it avoids the extra choice of *f* and has the disadvantage that additional ambiguity arises, because one has to choose a maximal tree and an orientation of the remaining edges.

The operation of Out(*F<sub>n</sub>*) on the outer space is defined as follows. Every automorphism *g* of $F_n$ induces a self homotopy equivalence *g′* of the bouquet of *n* circles. Composing *f* with *g′* gives the desired action. And in the other model it is just application of *g* and making the resulting word cyclically reduced.

:::

### Connection to length functions {#Connection_to_length_functions}

Every point in the outer space determines a unique length function $l_X \colon F_n \to \mathbb{R}$. A word in $F_n$ determines via the chosen homotopy equivalence a closed path in *X*. The length of the word is then the minimal length of a path in the free homotopy class of that closed path. Such a length function is constant on each conjugacy class. The assignment $X \mapsto l_X$ defines an embedding of the outer space to some infinite dimensional projective space.

### Simplicial structure on the outer space {#Simplicial_structure_on_the_outer_space}

In the second model an open simplex is given by all those $\mathbb{R}$-graphs, which have combinatorically the same underlying graph and the same edges are labeled with the same words (only the length of the edges may differ). The boundary simplices of such a simplex consists of all graphs, that arise from this graph by collapsing an edge. If that edge is a loop it cannot be collapsed without changing the homotopy type of the graph. Hence there is no boundary simplex. So one can think about the outer space as a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686) with some simplices removed. It is easy to verify, that the action of $\mathrm{Out}(F_n)$ is simplicial and has finite isotropy groups.
