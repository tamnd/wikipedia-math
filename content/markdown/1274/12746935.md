# Geometric group action

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geometric group action"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), a **geometric group action** is a certain type of [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of a [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536) on a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In geometric group theory, a **geometry** is any [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_metric_space), [geodesic metric space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_metric_space). An action of a [finitely-generated group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely-generated_group) *G* on a geometry *X* is **geometric** if it satisfies the following conditions:

1. Each element of *G* acts as an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) of *X*.
1. The action is [cocompact](wikipedia://en/12746615), i.e. the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) *X*/*G* is a [compact space](wikipedia://en/6042).
1. The action is [properly discontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Properly_discontinuous), with each point having a finite [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_%28group_theory%29).

:::

## Uniqueness {#Uniqueness}

If a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* acts geometrically upon two geometries *X* and *Y*, then *X* and *Y* are [quasi-isometric](wikipedia://en/12747259). Since any group acts geometrically on its own [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277), any space on which *G* acts geometrically is quasi-isometric to the Cayley graph of *G*.

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

[Cannon's conjecture](wikipedia://en/19404886) states that any [hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/3172817) with a 2-sphere at infinity acts geometrically on hyperbolic 3-space.

:::
