# Strassmann's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Strassmann's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Strassmann's theorem** is a result in [field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_%28mathematics%29). It states that, for suitable fields, suitable formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) with coefficients in the [valuation ring](wikipedia://en/966856) of the field have only finitely many zeroes.

:::

## History {#History}

It was introduced by [Reinhold Strassman](wikipedia://en/33749667).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $K$ be a field with a [non-Archimedean absolute value](wikipedia://en/5042667) $|\cdot|$ and let $R$ be the [valuation ring](wikipedia://en/966856) of $K$. Let $f(x) = a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2 + \dots$ be a formal power series which is not identically zero, with coefficients $a_n \in R$ converging to zero with respect to $|\cdot|$. Then $f(x)$ has only finitely many zeroes in $R$. More precisely, the number of zeros is at most $N$, where $N$ is the largest index with $|a_N| = \max_{n \geq 0} |a_n|$.

:::

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

A corollary of the theorem is that there is no analogue of [Euler's identity](wikipedia://en/46740), $e^{2 \pi i} = 1$ in $\mathbb{C}_p$, the field of [p-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) complex numbers.

Strassman's theorem may also be used to prove the [Skolem-Mahler-Lech theorem](wikipedia://en/30258454), which states that the set of indices at which a [linear recurrence sequence](wikipedia://en/49700795) is equal to zero is composed of a union of finitely many arithmetic progressions and a finite set.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Related results"}

## Related results {#Related_results}

The [Weierstrass preparation theorem](wikipedia://en/372198#Formal_power_series_in_complete_local_rings) over complete local rings generalises Strassman's theorem. While Strassman's theorem states that $f(x)$ has at most $N$ zeros in $R$, a corollary of the Weierstrass preparation theorem is that $f(x)$ has **exactly** $N$ zeros in the valuation ring of the [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of $K$.

:::
