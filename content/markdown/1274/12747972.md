# Reduct

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reduct"}

In [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) and in [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), a **reduct** of an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) is obtained by omitting some of the [operations](wikipedia://en/4140245) and [relations](wikipedia://en/22517558) of that structure. The opposite of "reduct" is "expansion".

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *A* be an algebraic structure (in the sense of [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200)) or a [structure](wikipedia://en/4055928) in the sense of [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), organized as a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) *X* together with an [indexed family](wikipedia://en/23624339) of operations and relations φ<sub>*i*</sub> on that set, with [index set](wikipedia://en/393055) *I*. Then the **reduct** of *A* defined by a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) *J* of *I* is the structure consisting of the set *X* and *J*-indexed family of operations and relations whose *j*-th operation or relation for *j* ∈ *J* is the *j*-th operation or relation of *A*. That is, this reduct is the structure *A* with the omission of those operations and relations φ<sub>*i*</sub> for which *i* is not in *J*.

A structure *A* is an **expansion** of *B* just when *B* is a reduct of *A*. That is, reduct and expansion are mutual converses.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) (**Z**, +, 0) of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) under [addition](wikipedia://en/61338) is a reduct of the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) (**Z**, +, −, 0) of integers under addition and [negation](wikipedia://en/228312), obtained by omitting negation. By contrast, the monoid (**N**, +, 0) of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) under addition is not the reduct of any group.

Conversely the group (**Z**, +, −, 0) is the expansion of the monoid (**Z**, +, 0), expanding it with the operation of negation.

:::
