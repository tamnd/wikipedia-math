# Golden triangle (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Golden triangle"}

*Image: Golden Triangle.svg.* A golden triangle. a/b is the golden ratio φ. The vertex angle θ is 36°. Base angles are 72° each.

*Image: GoldenGnomon.png.* Golden gnomon. Side lengths are in the proportions 1 : 1 : φ.

A **golden triangle**, also called a **sublime triangle**,[^o2] is an [isosceles triangle](wikipedia://en/315428) in which the duplicated side is in the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) $\varphi$ to the [base](wikipedia://en/9579379) side:

:::

$${a \over b} = \varphi = {1+\sqrt5 \over 2} \approx 1.618~034.$$

## Angles {#Angles}

- The [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) angle is:[^o4]

$$\theta = 2 \arcsin{b \over 2a} = 2 \arcsin{1 \over 2\varphi} = 2 \arcsin{{\sqrt5 - 1} \over 4} = {\pi \over 5}~\text{rad} = 36^\circ.$$

Hence, the golden triangle is an [acute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_triangle) (isosceles) triangle.

- Since the angles of a triangle sum to $\pi$ [radians](wikipedia://en/26003), each of the base angles (CBX and CXB) is:

$\beta = {{\pi - {\pi \over 5}} \over 2}~\text{rad} = {2\pi \over 5}~\text{rad} = 72^\circ.$[^o2]

Note:

$$\beta = \arccos\left(\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{4}\right)~\text{rad} = {2\pi \over 5}~\text{rad} = 72^\circ.$$

- The golden triangle is the triangle whose angles are in the ratios 1 : 2 : 2 (namely 36°, 72°, 72°).[^o5]

## In other geometric figures {#In_other_geometric_figures}

*Image: golden\_triangle\_decagon.svg.* A golden triangle in a regular decagon.

- Golden triangles can be found in the spikes of [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) [pentagrams](wikipedia://en/197544).
- Golden triangles can also be found in a regular [decagon](wikipedia://en/327393), an equiangular and equilateral ten-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621), by connecting any two adjacent vertices to the center. This is because: 180×(10−2)/10 = 144° is the [interior angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_angle), and [bisecting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_bisector) it through the vertex to the center: 144/2 = 72°.[^o2]
- Also, golden triangles are found in the [nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_%28geometry%29) of several stellations of [dodecahedrons](wikipedia://en/8407) and [icosahedrons](wikipedia://en/43856084).

### Logarithmic spiral {#Logarithmic_spiral}

*Image: Golden triangle and Fibonacci spiral.svg.* Golden triangles inscribed in a logarithmic spiral.

The golden triangle is used to form some points of a [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373). By bisecting one of the base angles, a new point is created that in turn, makes another golden triangle.[^o8] The bisection process can be continued indefinitely, creating an infinite number of golden triangles. A logarithmic spiral can be drawn through the vertices. This spiral is also known as an *equiangular* spiral, a term coined by [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525). "If a straight line is drawn from the pole to any point on the curve, it cuts the curve at precisely the same angle," hence *equiangular*.[^o9] This spiral is different from the [golden spiral](wikipedia://en/219370): the golden spiral grows by a factor of the golden ratio in each quarter-turn, whereas the spiral through these golden triangles takes an angle of 108° to grow by the same factor.[^o10]

## Golden gnomon {#Golden_gnomon}

*Image: Golden triangle (math).svg.* Golden triangle bisected into Robinson triangles: a golden triangle and a golden gnomon.

*Image: golden\_triangle\_pentagram.svg.* A golden triangle (red), a large (blue) and a small (green) golden gnomons in a regular pentagram.

Closely related to the golden triangle is the golden [gnomon](wikipedia://en/6673051), which is the isosceles triangle in which the ratio of the equal side lengths to the base length is the [reciprocal](wikipedia://en/229940) $\tfrac{1}{\varphi}$ of the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) $\varphi$.

"The golden triangle has a ratio of base length to side length equal to the golden section φ, whereas the golden gnomon has the ratio of side length to base length equal to the golden section φ."[^o13]

$${a' \over b'} = {1\over\varphi} = {{\sqrt5 - 1} \over 2} \approx 0.618~034.$$

### Angles {#Angles}

(The distances AX and CX are both *a*′ = *a* = φ, and the distance AC is *b*′ = φ², as seen in the figure.)

- The apex angle AXC is:

$$\theta' = 2 \arcsin{b' \over 2a'} = 2 \arcsin{\varphi^2 \over 2\varphi} = 2 \arcsin{1+\sqrt5 \over 4} = {3\pi \over 5}~\text{rad} = 108^\circ.$$

Hence, the golden gnomon is an [obtuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtuse_triangle) (isosceles) triangle.

Note: $\theta' = \arccos\left(\frac{1-\sqrt5}{4}\right)~\text{rad} = {3\pi \over 5}~\text{rad} = 108^\circ.$

- Since the angles of the triangle AXC sum to $\pi$ radians, each of the base angles CAX and ACX is:

$$\beta' = \theta = {\pi - {3\pi \over 5} \over 2}~\text{rad} = {\pi \over 5}~\text{rad} = 36^\circ.$$

Note: $\beta' = \theta = \arccos\left(\frac{1+\sqrt5}{4}\right)~\text{rad} = {\pi \over 5}~\text{rad} = 36^\circ.$

- The golden gnomon is the triangle having its three angles in the ratios 1 : 1 : 3 (namely 36°, 36°, 108°). Its base angles are 36° each, which is the same as the apex angle of the golden triangle.

:::

## Bisections {#Bisections}

- By [bisecting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_bisector) one of its base angles, a golden triangle can be subdivided into a golden triangle and a golden gnomon.
- By [trisecting](wikipedia://en/91111) its apex angle, a golden gnomon can be subdivided into a golden triangle and a golden gnomon.
- A golden gnomon and a golden triangle with their equal sides matching each other in length, are also referred to as the obtuse and acute Robinson triangles.[^o5]

## Tilings {#Tilings}

- A golden triangle and two golden gnomons tile a regular [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014).[^o17]
- These isosceles triangles can be used to produce [Penrose tilings](wikipedia://en/26611936). Penrose tiles are made from kites and darts. Such a kite is made from two [adjacent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjacent_side_%28polygon%29) golden triangles, and such a dart is made from two adjacent golden gnomons.
