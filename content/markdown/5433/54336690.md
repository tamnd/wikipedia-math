# Megumi Harada

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Megumi Harada"}

*Image: Megumi Harada.jpg.* Megumi Harada

**Megumi Harada** is a mathematician who works as a professor in the department of mathematics and statistics at [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University), where she holds a tier-two [Canada Research Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Research_Chair) in Equivariant Symplectic and Algebraic Geometry.

:::

## Research {#Research}

Harada's research involves the symmetries of [symplectic spaces](wikipedia://en/294298) and their connections to other areas of mathematics including [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [*K*-theory](wikipedia://en/246748), and [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582).

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Harada graduated in 1996 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), with a bachelor's degree in mathematics. She completed her doctorate in 2003 from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). Her dissertation, *The Symplectic Geometry of the Gel'fand-Cetlin-Molev Basis for Representations of Sp(2n, C)*, concerned [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298) and was supervised by [Allen Knutson](wikipedia://en/36163134).

After postdoctoral studies at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto), she joined the McMaster faculty in 2006.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2013, Harada won the Ruth I. Michler Memorial Prize of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698), funding her travel to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) for research collaborations with Reyer Sjamar, [Tara S. Holm](wikipedia://en/45294890), and Allen Knutson. She was given her Canada Research Chair in 2014. She is the 2018 winner of the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829) "for her research on Newton–Okounkov bodies, Hessenberg varieties, and their relationships to symplectic geometry, combinatorics, and equivariant topology, among others".

She was elected as a [Fellow of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society), in the 2025 class of fellows.
