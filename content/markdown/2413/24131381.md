# Ljubljana graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ljubljana graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Ljubljana graph** is an [undirected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) with 112 [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and 168 [edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29), rediscovered in 2002 and named after [Ljubljana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana) (the capital of [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia)).[^o3]

:::

It is a [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) with diameter 8, radius 7, [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) 2 and [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) 3. Its girth is 10 and there are exactly 168 cycles of length 10 in it. There are also 168 cycles of length 12.[^o3]

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The Ljubljana graph is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph) and can be constructed from the [LCF notation](wikipedia://en/24186720) : \[47, −23, −31, 39, 25, −21, −31, −41, 25, 15, 29, −41, −19, 15, −49, 33, 39, −35, −21, 17, −33, 49, 41, 31, −15, −29, 41, 31, −15, −25, 21, 31, −51, −25, 23, 9, −17, 51, 35, −29, 21, −51, −39, 33, −9, −51, 51, −47, −33, 19, 51, −21, 29, 21, −31, −39\]<sup>2</sup>.

The Ljubljana graph is the [Levi graph](wikipedia://en/1396888) of the Ljubljana configuration, a quadrangle-free configuration with 56 lines and 56 points.[^o3] In this configuration, each line contains exactly 3 points, each point belongs to exactly 3 lines and any two lines intersect in at most one point.

:::

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the Ljubljana graph is a group of order 168. It acts transitively on the edges the graph but not on its vertices: there are [symmetries](wikipedia://en/15094186) taking every edge to any other edge, but not taking every vertex to any other vertex. Therefore, the Ljubljana graph is a [semi-symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/2554240), the third smallest possible cubic semi-symmetric graph after the [Gray graph](wikipedia://en/2554738) on 54 vertices and the [Iofinova-Ivanov graph on 110 vertices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/110-vertex_Iofinova-Ivanov_graph).[^o9]

The characteristic polynomial of the Ljubljana graph is

$$(x-3)x^{14}(x+3)(x^2-x-4)^7(x^2-2)^6(x^2+x-4)^7(x^4-6x^2+4)^{14}.\$$

:::

## History {#History}

The Ljubljana graph was first published in 1993 by [Brouwer](wikipedia://en/22709916), [Dejter](wikipedia://en/38075487) and [Thomassen](wikipedia://en/7460839)[^o11] as a self-complementary subgraph of the [Dejter graph](wikipedia://en/40015294).[^o12]

In 1972, Bouwer was already talking of a 112-vertices edge- but not vertex-transitive cubic graph found by [R. M. Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._M._Foster), nonetheless unpublished.[^o13] [Conder](wikipedia://en/38305099), Malnič, [Marušič](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragan_Marušič), [Pisanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomaž_Pisanski) and Potočnik rediscovered this 112-vertices graph in 2002 and named it the [Ljubljana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana) graph after the capital of [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia).[^o14] They proved that it was the unique 112-vertices edge- but not vertex-transitive cubic graph and therefore that was the graph found by Foster.
