# Even and odd ordinals

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Even and odd ordinals"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **even and odd ordinals** extend the concept of [parity](wikipedia://en/143135) from the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) to the [ordinal numbers](wikipedia://en/26547932). They are useful in some [transfinite induction](wikipedia://en/30461) [proofs](wikipedia://en/82285).

:::

The literature contains a few equivalent definitions of the parity of an ordinal α:

- Zero and each [limit ordinal](wikipedia://en/373137) are even. The [successor](wikipedia://en/375256) of an even ordinal is odd, and vice versa.[^o2][^o3]
- Let α = λ + *n*, where λ is a limit ordinal and *n* is a natural number. The parity of α is the parity of *n*.[^o4]
- Let *n* be the finite term of the [Cantor normal form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor_normal_form) of α. The parity of α is the parity of *n*.[^o5]
- Let α = ωβ + *n*, where *n* is a natural number. The parity of α is the parity of *n*.[^o6]
- If α = 2β, then α is even. Otherwise α = 2β + 1 and α is odd.[^o6][^o7]

Unlike the case of even [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), one cannot go on to characterize even ordinals as ordinal numbers of the form β2 = β + β. [Ordinal multiplication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_multiplication) is not commutative, so in general 2β ≠ β2. In fact, the even ordinal ω + 4 cannot be expressed as β + β, and the ordinal number

(ω + 3)2 = (ω + 3) + (ω + 3) = ω + (3 + ω) + 3 = ω + ω + 3 = ω2 + 3

is not even.

A simple application of ordinal parity is the [idempotence](wikipedia://en/14972) law for [cardinal addition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_addition) (given the [well-ordering theorem](wikipedia://en/33458)). Given an infinite cardinal κ, or generally any limit ordinal κ, κ is order-isomorphic to both its subset of even ordinals and its subset of odd ordinals. Hence one has the cardinal sum κ + κ = κ.[^o3][^o8]
