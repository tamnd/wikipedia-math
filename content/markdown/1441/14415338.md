::: {#o10 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform 5-polytope"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **uniform [5-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821670)** is a five-dimensional [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392). By definition, a uniform 5-polytope is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) and constructed from [uniform 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/1645899) [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647).

:::

The complete set of **convex uniform 5-polytopes** has not been determined, but many can be made as [Wythoff constructions](wikipedia://en/2950777) from a small set of [symmetry groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_groups). These construction operations are represented by the permutations of rings of the [Coxeter diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram).

## History of discovery {#History_of_discovery}

- **[Regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693)**: (convex faces)
  - **1852**: [Ludwig Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408) proved in his manuscript *Theorie der vielfachen Kontinuität* that there are exactly 3 regular polytopes in 5 or more [dimensions](wikipedia://en/8398).
- **Convex [semiregular polytopes](wikipedia://en/11684875)**: (Various definitions before Coxeter's **uniform** category)
  - **1900**: [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) enumerated the list of nonprismatic semiregular convex polytopes with regular facets ([convex regular 4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_regular_4-polytope)) in his publication *On the Regular and Semi-Regular Figures in Space of n Dimensions*.[^o12]
- **Convex uniform polytopes**:
  - **1940-1988**: The search was expanded systematically by [H.S.M. Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter) in his publication *Regular and Semi-Regular Polytopes I, II, and III*.
  - **1966**: [Norman W. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_W._Johnson) completed his Ph.D. dissertation under Coxeter, *The Theory of Uniform Polytopes and Honeycombs*, University of Toronto
- **Non-convex uniform polytopes**:
  - **1966**: Johnson describes two non-convex uniform antiprisms in 5-space in his dissertation.[^o13]
  - **2000-2024**: Jonathan Bowers and other researchers search for other non-convex uniform 5-polytopes,[^o14] with a current count of 1333 known uniform 5-polytopes outside infinite families (convex and non-convex), excluding the prisms of the uniform 4-polytopes. The list is not proven complete.[^o15][^o16]

## Regular 5-polytopes {#Regular_5-polytopes}

Regular 5-polytopes can be represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {p,q,r,s}, with **s** {p,q,r} 4-polytope [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29) around each [face](wikipedia://en/149926). There are exactly three such regular polytopes, all convex:

- {3,3,3,3} - [5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex)
- {4,3,3,3} - [5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215)
- {3,3,3,4} - [5-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/7594615)

There are no nonconvex regular polytopes in 5 dimensions or above.

## Convex uniform 5-polytopes {#Convex_uniform_5-polytopes}

::: {#o19 .problem cue=unsolved}

What is the complete set of convex uniform 5-polytopes?[^o20]

:::

There are 104 known convex uniform 5-polytopes, plus a number of infinite families of [duoprism](wikipedia://en/2114155) prisms, and polygon-polyhedron duoprisms. All except the *grand antiprism prism* are based on [Wythoff constructions](wikipedia://en/2950777), reflection symmetry generated with [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004).

### Symmetry of uniform 5-polytopes in four dimensions {#Symmetry_of_uniform_5-polytopes_in_four_dimensions}

The [5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex) is the regular form in the A<sub>5</sub> family. The [5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215) and [5-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/7594615) are the regular forms in the B<sub>5</sub> family. The bifurcating graph of the D<sub>5</sub> family contains the [5-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/7594615), as well as a [5-demicube](wikipedia://en/7186232) which is an [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) [5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215).

Each reflective uniform 5-polytope can be constructed in one or more reflective point group in 5 dimensions by a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777), represented by rings around permutations of nodes in a [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram). Mirror [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) can be grouped, as seen by colored nodes, separated by even-branches. Symmetry groups of the form \[a,b,b,a\], have an extended symmetry, \[\[a,b,b,a\]\], like \[3,3,3,3\], doubling the symmetry order. Uniform polytopes in these group with symmetric rings contain this extended symmetry.

If all mirrors of a given color are unringed (inactive) in a given uniform polytope, it will have a lower symmetry construction by removing all of the inactive mirrors. If all the nodes of a given color are ringed (active), an [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) operation can generate a new 5-polytope with chiral symmetry, shown as "empty" circled nodes", but the geometry is not generally adjustable to create uniform solutions.

### Enumerating the convex uniform 5-polytopes {#Enumerating_the_convex_uniform_5-polytopes}

- [Simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) family: A<sub>5</sub> \[3<sup>4</sup>\]
  - 19 uniform 5-polytopes
- [Hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783)/[Orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex) family: B<sub>5</sub> \[4,3<sup>3</sup>\]
  - 31 uniform 5-polytopes
- [Demihypercube](wikipedia://en/6833695) D<sub>5</sub>/E<sub>5</sub> family: \[3<sup>2,1,1</sup>\]
  - 23 uniform 5-polytopes (8 unique)
- Polychoral prisms:
  - 56 uniform 5-polytope (45 unique) constructions based on prismatic families: \[3,3,3\]×\[ \], \[4,3,3\]×\[ \], \[5,3,3\]×\[ \], \[3<sup>1,1,1</sup>\]×\[ \].
  - One [non-Wythoffian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Wythoffian) - The [grand antiprism prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_antiprism_prism) is the only known non-Wythoffian convex uniform 5-polytope, constructed from two [grand antiprisms](wikipedia://en/4058047) connected by polyhedral prisms.

That brings the tally to: 19+31+8+45+1=104

In addition there are:

- Infinitely many uniform 5-polytope constructions based on duoprism prismatic families: \[*p*\]×\[*q*\]×\[ \].
- Infinitely many uniform 5-polytope constructions based on duoprismatic families: \[3,3\]×\[*p*\], \[4,3\]×\[*p*\], \[5,3\]×\[*p*\].

### The A<sub>5</sub> family {#The_A5_family}

There are 19 forms based on all permutations of the [Coxeter diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) with one or more rings. (16+4-1 cases)

They are named by [Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527) from the Wythoff construction operations upon regular 5-simplex (hexateron).

The [A<sub>5</sub> family](wikipedia://en/297004#Finite_Coxeter_groups) has symmetry of order 720 (6 [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606)). 7 of the 19 figures, with symmetrically ringed Coxeter diagrams have doubled symmetry, order 1440.

The coordinates of uniform 5-polytopes with 5-simplex symmetry can be generated as permutations of simple integers in 6-space, all in hyperplanes with normal vector (1,1,1,1,1,1).

### The B<sub>5</sub> family {#The_B5_family}

The [B<sub>5</sub> family](wikipedia://en/297004#Finite_Coxeter_groups) has symmetry of order 3840 (5!×2<sup>5</sup>).

This family has 2<sup>5</sup>−1=31 Wythoffian uniform polytopes generated by marking one or more nodes of the [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram). Also added are 8 uniform polytopes generated as alternations with half the symmetry, which form a complete duplicate of the D<sub>5</sub> family as ... = ..... (There are more alternations that are not listed because they produce only repetitions, as ... = .... and ... = .... These would give a complete duplication of the uniform 5-polytopes numbered 20 through 34 with symmetry broken in half.)

For simplicity it is divided into two subgroups, each with 12 forms, and 7 "middle" forms which equally belong in both.

### The D<sub>5</sub> family {#The_D5_family}

The [D<sub>5</sub> family](wikipedia://en/297004#Finite_Coxeter_groups) has symmetry of order 1920 (5! x 2<sup>4</sup>).

This family has 23 Wythoffian uniform polytopes, from *3×8-1* permutations of the D<sub>5</sub> [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) with one or more rings. 15 (2×8-1) are repeated from the B<sub>5</sub> family and 8 are unique to this family, though even those 8 duplicate the alternations from the B<sub>5</sub> family.

In the 15 repeats, both of the nodes terminating the length-1 branches are ringed, so the two kinds of element are identical and the symmetry doubles: the relations are ... = .... and ... = ..., creating a complete duplication of the uniform 5-polytopes 20 through 34 above. The 8 new forms have one such node ringed and one not, with the relation ... = ... duplicating uniform 5-polytopes 51 through 58 above.

## Notes on the Wythoff construction for the uniform 5-polytopes {#Notes_on_the_Wythoff_construction_for_the_uniform_5-polytopes}

Construction of the reflective 5-dimensional [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392) are done through a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) process, and represented through a [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram), where each node represents a mirror. Nodes are ringed to imply which mirrors are active. The full set of uniform polytopes generated are based on the unique permutations of ringed nodes. Uniform 5-polytopes are named in relation to the [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693) in each family. Some families have two regular constructors and thus may have two ways of naming them.

Here are the primary operators available for constructing and naming the uniform 5-polytopes.

The last operation, the snub, and more generally the alternation, are the operations that can create nonreflective forms. These are drawn with "hollow rings" at the nodes.

The prismatic forms and bifurcating graphs can use the same truncation indexing notation, but require an explicit numbering system on the nodes for clarity.
