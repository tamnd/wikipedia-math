# Lester's theorem

*Image: Lester theorem.svg.* The Fermat points $X_{13},X_{14}$, the center $X_5$ of the nine-point circle (light blue), and the circumcenter $X_3$ of the green triangle lie on the Lester circle (black).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lester's theorem"}

In [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **Lester's theorem** states that in any [scalene triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalene_triangle), the two [Fermat points](wikipedia://en/2666205), the [nine-point center](wikipedia://en/4517570), and the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) lie on the same circle. The result is named after June Lester, who published it in 1997, and the circle through these points was called the **Lester circle** by [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421). Lester proved the result by using the properties of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826); subsequent authors have given elementary proofs, proofs using vector arithmetic, and computerized proofs. The center of the **Lester circle** is also a triangle center. It is the center designated as X(1116) in the [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593).[^o2] Recently, Peter Moses discovered 21 other [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) centers lie on the **Lester circle**. The points are numbered X(15535) – X(15555) in the [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593).[^o3]

:::

## Gibert's generalization {#Gibert's_generalization}

In 2000, [Bernard Gibert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Gibert) proposed a generalization of the Lester Theorem involving the [Kiepert hyperbola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiepert_hyperbola) of a triangle. His result can be stated as follows: Every circle with a diameter that is a chord of the Kiepert hyperbola and perpendicular to the triangle's [Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622) passes through the [Fermat points](wikipedia://en/2666205).[^o5]
