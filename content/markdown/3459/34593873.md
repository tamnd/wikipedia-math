# Albert Crumeyrolle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Albert Crumeyrolle"}

**Albert J. Crumeyrolle** (1919–1992) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of mathematics at the [Paul Sabatier University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Sabatier_University), known for his contributions to [spinor](wikipedia://en/29276) structures and [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305).

:::

## Work {#Work}

Crumeyrolle was a student of [André Lichnerowicz](wikipedia://en/2389188) under whose supervision he completed a thesis in 1961.[^o5]

His first important paper after completing his doctorate addressed spinor structures using methods of Clifford algebras developed by [Claude Chevalley](wikipedia://en/366040).[^o6]

Crumeyrolle is known for his major contributions to theories of Clifford algebras and spinor structures. In 1975 he laid the foundations for [symplectic Clifford algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_Clifford_algebra) and the symplectic [spinor](wikipedia://en/29276).[^o7] An earlier publication by two other authors, Nouazé and Revoy, had appeared three years before in which Weyl algebras were treated from a Cliffordian point of view. Crumeyrolle however drew more attention to the topic, and, as emphasized by Jacques Helmstetter, he contributed original ideas of his own.[^o8] His work on symplectic Clifford algebras however came under serious critique on mathematical grounds.[^o9]

The mathematician Artibano Micali recalled Crumeyrolle stating that periodicity of Clifford algebras should play a similar role for elementary particle physics as the periodic classification of elements by [Dmitri Mendeleev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Mendeleev) has played for the [periodic table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table) of elements.[^o6]

Crumeyrolle taught in Iran in 1966, in several Europe countries and, in 1973, at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) summer school.[^o5]
