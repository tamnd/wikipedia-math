# Whitehead's point-free geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Whitehead's point-free geometry"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **point-free geometry** is a [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) whose primitive [ontological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) notion is *[region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_%28mathematics%29)* rather than [point](wikipedia://en/593693). Two [axiomatic systems](wikipedia://en/188401) are set out below, one grounded in [mereology](wikipedia://en/375380), the other in [mereotopology](wikipedia://en/1664427) and known as *connection theory*.

:::

Point-free geometry was first formulated by [Alfred North Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395),[^o2] not as a theory of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) or of [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758), but of "events" and of an "extension [relation](wikipedia://en/22517558)" between events. Whitehead's purposes were as much [philosophical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical) as scientific and mathematical.[^o3]

## Formalizations {#Formalizations}

Whitehead did not set out his theories in a manner that would satisfy present-day canons of formality. The two formal [first-order theories](wikipedia://en/3690584) described in this entry were devised by others in order to clarify and refine Whitehead's theories. The [domain of discourse](wikipedia://en/229180) for both theories consists of "regions." All [unquantified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantification_%28logic%29) variables in this entry should be taken as tacitly [universally quantified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universally_quantified); hence all axioms should be taken as [universal closures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_closure). No axiom requires more than three quantified variables; hence a translation of first-order theories into [relation algebra](wikipedia://en/4363670) is possible. Each set of axioms has but four [existential quantifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_quantifier).

## Inclusion-based point-free geometry (mereology) {#Inclusion-based_point-free_geometry_(mereology)}

The fundamental primitive [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) is *inclusion*, denoted by the [infix operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infix_operator) "≤", which corresponds to the binary *Parthood* relation that is a standard feature in [mereological](wikipedia://en/375380) theories. The intuitive meaning of *x* ≤ *y* is "*x* is part of *y*." Assuming that equality, denoted by the infix operator "=", is part of the background logic, the binary relation *Proper Part*, denoted by the infix operator "\<", is defined as:

$$x<y \leftrightarrow (x \le y \land x \not = y).$$

The axioms are:[^o6]

- Inclusion [partially orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) the [domain](wikipedia://en/229180).

**G1.** $x \le x.$ ([reflexive](wikipedia://en/200458))

**G2.** $(x \le z \land z \le y) \rightarrow x \le y.$ ([transitive](wikipedia://en/200463)) **WP4**.

**G3.** $(x \le y \land y \le x) \rightarrow x = y.$ ([antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/1176))

- Given any two regions, there exists a region that includes both of them. **WP6**.

**G4.** $\exists z[x \le z \land y\le z].$

- Proper Part [densely orders](wikipedia://en/6320997) the [domain](wikipedia://en/229180). **WP5**.

**G5.** $x<y\rightarrow\exists z [x<z<y].$

- Both [atomic regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_%28order_theory%29) and a [universal region](wikipedia://en/375380) do not exist. Hence the [domain](wikipedia://en/229180) has neither an upper nor a lower bound. **WP2**.

**G6.** $\exists y \exists z[y<x \land x<z].$

- Proper Parts Principle. If all the proper parts of *x* are proper parts of *y*, then *x* is included in *y*. **WP3**.

**G7.** $\forall z[z<x \rightarrow z<y] \rightarrow x\le y.$

A [model](wikipedia://en/19858) of **G1–G7** is an *inclusion space*.

::: {#o7 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition"}

**Definition**.[^o8] Given some inclusion space S, an **abstractive class** is a class *G* of regions such that *S\\G* is [totally ordered](wikipedia://en/30330) by inclusion. Moreover, there does not exist a region included in all of the regions in *G*.

Intuitively, an abstractive class defines a geometrical entity whose dimensionality is less than that of the inclusion space. For example, if the inclusion space is the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), then the corresponding abstractive classes are [points](wikipedia://en/593693) and [lines](wikipedia://en/946975).

Inclusion-based point-free geometry (henceforth "point-free geometry") is essentially an axiomatization of Simons's system **W.**[^o9] In turn, **W** formalizes a theory of Whitehead[^o10] whose axioms are not made explicit. Point-free geometry is **W** with this defect repaired. Simons did not repair this defect, instead proposing in a footnote that the reader do so as an exercise. The primitive relation of **W** is Proper Part, a [strict partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strict_partial_order). The theory[^o11] of Whitehead (1919) has a single primitive binary relation *K* defined as *xKy* ↔ *y* \< *x*. Hence *K* is the [converse](wikipedia://en/3941020) of Proper Part. Simons's **WP1** asserts that Proper Part is [irreflexive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreflexive) and so corresponds to **G1**. **G3** establishes that inclusion, unlike Proper Part, is [antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/1176).

Point-free geometry is closely related to a [dense linear order](wikipedia://en/3690584) **D**, whose axioms are **G1-3**, **G5**, and the totality axiom $x \le y \lor y \le x.$[^o12] Hence inclusion-based point-free geometry would be a proper extension of **D** (namely **D** ∪ {**G4**, **G6**, **G7**}), were it not that the **D** relation "≤" is a [total order](wikipedia://en/30330).

:::

## Connection theory (mereotopology) {#Connection_theory_(mereotopology)}

A different approach was proposed in Whitehead (1929), one inspired by De Laguna (1922). Whitehead took as primitive the [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological) notion of "contact" between two regions, resulting in a primitive "connection relation" between events. Connection theory **C** is a [first-order theory](wikipedia://en/3690584) that distills the first 12 of Whitehead's 31 assumptions[^o14] into 6 axioms, **C1-C6**.[^o15] **C** is a proper fragment of the theories proposed by Clarke,[^o16] who noted their [mereological](wikipedia://en/375380) character. Theories that, like **C**, feature both inclusion and topological primitives, are called [mereotopologies](wikipedia://en/1664427).

**C** has one primitive [relation](wikipedia://en/22517558), binary "connection," denoted by the [prefixed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefix) [predicate letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_%28mathematical_logic%29) *C*. That *x* is included in *y* can now be defined as *x* ≤ *y* ↔ ∀z\[*Czx*→*Czy*\]. Unlike the case with inclusion spaces, connection theory enables defining "non-tangential" inclusion,[^o17] a total order that enables the construction of abstractive classes. Gerla and Miranda (2008) argue that only thus can mereotopology unambiguously define a [point](wikipedia://en/593693).

- *C* is [reflexive](wikipedia://en/200458). **C.1.**

**C1.**$\ Cxx.$

- *C* is [symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric). **C.2.**

**C2.** $Cxy\rightarrow Cyx.$

- *C* is [extensional](wikipedia://en/294056). **C.11.**

**C3.** $\forall z[Czx \leftrightarrow Czy] \rightarrow x = y.$

- All regions have proper parts, so that **C** is an [atomless](wikipedia://en/998824) theory. **P.9.**

**C4.** $\exists y[y<x].$

- Given any two regions, there is a region connected to both of them.

**C5.** $\exists z[Czx\land Czy].$

- All regions have at least two unconnected parts. **C.14.**

**C6.** $\exists y \exists z[(y\le x)\land (z\le x)\land\neg Cyz].$

A model of **C** is a *connection space*.

Following the verbal description of each axiom is the identifier of the corresponding axiom in Casati and Varzi (1999). Their system **SMT** (*strong mereotopology*) consists of **C1-C3**, and is essentially due to Clarke (1981).[^o18] Any mereotopology can be made [atomless](wikipedia://en/998824) by invoking **C4**, without risking paradox or triviality. Hence **C** extends the atomless variant of **SMT** by means of the axioms **C5** and **C6**, suggested by chapter 2 of part 4 of *Process and Reality*.[^o19]

Biacino and Gerla (1991) showed that every [model](wikipedia://en/19858) of Clarke's theory is a [Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959), and models of such algebras cannot distinguish connection from overlap. It is doubtful whether either fact is faithful to Whitehead's intent.
