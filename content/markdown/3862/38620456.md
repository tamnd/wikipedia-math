# Assaf Naor

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Assaf Naor"}

**Assaf Naor** (אסף נאור; born May 7, 1975) is an [Israeli American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_American) and [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), and a professor of mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Naor earned a baccalaureate from [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem) in 1996 and a doctorate from the same university in 2002, under the supervision of [Joram Lindenstrauss](wikipedia://en/20653945).[^o6] He worked at [Microsoft Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Research) from 2002 until 2007, with an affiliated faculty position at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington), and joined the NYU faculty in 2006.[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

Naor's research concerns [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018), their properties, and related algorithms, including improved upper bounds on the [Grothendieck inequality](wikipedia://en/9395279), applications of this inequality, and research on [metrical task systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrical_task_system).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Naor won the Bergmann award of the [United States – Israel Binational Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_–_Israel_Binational_Science_Foundation) in 2007,[^o10] and the Pazy award of the BSF in 2011.[^o11] In 2012 he was one of four faculty winners of the [Leonard Blavatnik Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blavatnik_Awards_for_Young_Scientists) of the [New York Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Academy_of_Sciences), given to young scientists and engineers in New York, New Jersey, and Connecticut.[^o12]

He won the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize) in 2008 for "contributions to the structural theory of metric spaces and its applications to computer science",[^o13] and in the same year was given a [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470) Prize[^o6] (one of ten awarded to outstanding younger mathematicians). He won the [Bôcher Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/1099872) in 2011 "for introducing new invariants of metric spaces and for applying his new understanding of the distortion between various metric structures to theoretical computer science".[^o14] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o15] He received the [Nemmers Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1252473) in 2018 and in 2019 the [Ostrowski Prize](wikipedia://en/3159411).[^o16]

He gave an [invited talk at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2010, on the topic of "Functional Analysis and Applications".[^o17]
