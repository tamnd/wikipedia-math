# J. Nigro Sansonese

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="J. Nigro Sansonese"}

**J. Nigro Sansonese** is the pen name of author **Joseph Sansonese** (born May 13, 1946).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

J. Nigro Sansonese was born Joseph Sansonese in [Buffalo, NY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_NY), where he attended [Canisius High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canisius_High_School). He was trained in physics and mathematics at [Fordham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University) (Sc.B 1968) ([Bronx, NY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx,_NY)) and [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) ([Providence, RI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_RI)). In 1996–97, he taught a course in the history of science at [Stonehill College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehill_College) ([Easton, MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easton,_MA)) and from 2001 to 2009 courses in mathematics and science on [St. Croix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Croix) at the [University of the Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Virgin_Islands) (Kingshill, VI). Over the course of 30 years he also taught mathematics and science at various high schools, public and private, in the United States and its territories. He retired from teaching in 2012.

His main published work is *The Body of Myth: Mythology, Shamanic Trance, and the Sacred Geography of the Body,* , a nonfiction work of some 400 pages on the relationship between [mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythology) and human [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology),[^o3] that appeared in 1994.

He was co-screenwriter of *Hurricane Festival*, a 1997 feature film, with Chi Y. Lee.[^o4] His plays include *The House on the Point*, produced at the [Hult Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hult_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts), [Eugene, OR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_OR), November 1985, and *The Mill at Tour d'Ivoire*, read at the [Interstate Firehouse Cultural Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Firehouse_Cultural_Center), [North Portland, OR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Portland,_OR), September 1984, and the Greene Street Theater, New York City, April 1986.

## Works {#Works}

In addition to his work in print Sansonese has discussed his theory of [mythopoesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythopoesis) (Gk., "story making") in a lengthy 1994 video interview with San Francisco psychologist Jeffrey Mishlove of the series *Thinking Allowed* (PBS).[^o6]

Nigro Sansonese's mythology, elaborated in *The Body of Myth: Mythology, Shamanic Trance, and the Sacred Geography of the Body* (1994), might be summarized as follows: Early myth-making (before, say, 5000 BC) among archaic peoples—especially but not exclusively [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages) speakers—may have originated in an esoteric oral *cephelosophy* or "skull wisdom" automatically imparted, primarily to young men at the age of puberty, in secret [initiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initiation) rituals, during which venerated ancestral skulls might have been displayed for purposes of illustrating the meaning of particular myths. Recent excavations at [Göbekli Tepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göbekli_Tepe) in southeastern Turkey, the oldest known site of religious architecture in the world (c. 10,000 BC), have revealed that a skull cult may indeed have been centered there for nearly 2000 years.[^o7]

The aboriginal inspiration for myth, Sansonese argues, lay in heightened [awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awareness) (see **[proprioception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprioception)**) of certain internal bodily activities important in religiously oriented [meditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditation). The means of heightening proprioception are closely guarded meditative techniques orally communicated from teacher ([adept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adept)) to student, in particular but not exclusively, techniques associated with [respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiration_%28physiology%29).

Subject to numerous cultural contingencies, the techniques likely first appeared in history many thousands of years ago in the [trance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trance)-inducing practices of [shamans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamans) and became more systematized, refined, and elaborate over millennia. Because the activities attended to in many meditative traditions, for example, respiration and heart rate, are physiologically fundamental to *all* human bodies everywhere, an explicit argument of the book is that a proprioceptive interpretation probably applies to *all* mythologies that are sufficiently archaic in origin, which he defines as no later than approximately 800 BC. About a quarter of the book focuses on interpreting the biblical narratives of Judaism and Christianity.

A myth, then, according to Sansonese is a veiled, culturally conditioned [description](wikipedia://en/19759799) of a trance-inducing technique and resulting proprioceptions. Myths, therefore, on this view are (very) early attempts at articulating what, in 1945, [Aldous Huxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldous_Huxley) called [perennial philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perennial_philosophy), and are simultaneously [mystical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystical) and [practical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical) in their origins.

Much of the practical aim of a myth is instruction in what Sansonese calls "the art of dying," a narrative prefiguring of the literal experience of [somatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_symptom_disorder) death, which is a matter of the practical mastery of a critical, unpredictable, and irreversible endocrinal event that he suggestively describes as a "[pituitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary) catastrophe," in the sense that the pituitary gland initiates it. In Nigro Sansonese's view, death is an event triggered by the [endocrine system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine_system).[^o8] In sum, Nigro Sansonese defines an "authentic myth" as "an esoteric description of a heightened proprioception,"[^o9] meaning a verbal description, albeit a necessarily imperfect one, of a literal experience undergone by an adept while in a trance state, which, very critically, *must* be distinguished from mere [symbolism](wikipedia://en/37673) or [metaphor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphor). Understanding religious practice (praxis) eclipses understanding religious belief (dogma) in its importance for understanding both the origin and the meaning of a specific myth of sufficient antiquity (see also his lengthy discussion of the [Eleusinian Mysteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleusinian_Mysteries)).

In deep meditation, the region of the cranial sinciput, or forehead, is in emphasis, particularly the [glabella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glabella), a fissure between the brows esoterically described in myth, Sansonese claims, as a portal or entry such as the Scæan Gate into [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy) (see Book VI of *[The Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Iliad)* and *passim*) or the Hellespont on the way to [Colchis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colchis), mythic locale of the [Golden Fleece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Fleece), to give just two of numerous examples cited. Another vivid illustration is the mythical figure [Sisyphus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisyphus) (Σίσυφος, *Sísyphos*), king of [Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Corinth), whose myths Sansonese explores in detail, and whose name, he suggests, is literally an onomatopoetic rendering of the sussurant sound ("siss phuss") the moving breath makes in the nostrils, the breath of course being an important object of meditative concentration (a *bija* in [yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga)). Repetitive inhalations–exhalations are described esoterically in the myth as an up–down motion of Sisyphus and a boulder on a hill.

Various animals, particularly strenuous breathers such as horses, swine, and asses, describe respiration in myth. The descriptive principle is widely distributed geographically. For example, Jesus' entry into Jerusalem while riding on an ass' colt is a Semitic myth equivalent to Odysseus' entry into Troy inside a horse.

As a compendium Sansonese provides five [axioms](wikipedia://en/928) to guide the interpretation of archaic mythopoesis. A critical contention of the book is that myths are not so much [symbols](wikipedia://en/37673) natural to human ideation, as [Carl Jung](wikipedia://en/42031) proposed in 1933, as they are culture-specific, esoteric [descriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descriptions) of somatic activity proprioceived during exalted [trance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trance) states, for example, those attained through, but not limited to, yoga (see also *[samyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samyama)* and *[pranayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pranayama))*.[^o10]

Nigro Sansonese's work also has relevance to the scientific study of [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness), specifically, by proposing that an implicit [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology)—namely, a description of [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge) that is in its nature, i.e., essentially, *[a priori](wikipedia://en/6679056)*, also known as **consciousness**—unavoidably orders the fundamental [laws of physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_physics) differentially, from the (macroscopically) perceived and proprioceived 17th-century [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics) of [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) to the indispensability of mathematics in the 20th-century [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) of [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg). That historical development is interpreted by Sansonese as one of successively elaborating a physics originally based on [empirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical) knowledge obtained via proprioception and [perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perception) into a physics that is a [conformal map](wikipedia://en/50627) onto the [psychodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychodynamics) of [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition). A succinct summary of that assertion would be "[Ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) is epistemology: Every state of [being](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being) is *ipso facto* a state of [knowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge)." On such a view, quantum mechanics arguably might be said to be more "[meta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_%28prefix%29)-physics" than physics.[^o11]

Finally, Sansonese implies that the physics of the universe is fundamentally that of a particular and well-known harmonic oscillator known as a critically damped [tank circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_circuit) at or near [resonance](wikipedia://en/41660) (sometimes called an *LC circuit*), providing as well a multi-dimensional "experience space" in which that hypothesis may be investigated mathematically. One conclusion might then be that a tank-circuit universe is probably a hologrammatic [Anti-de Sitter space](wikipedia://en/301670).[^o12]
