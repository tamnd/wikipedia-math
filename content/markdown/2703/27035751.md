# Selection theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Selection theorem"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a branch of mathematics, a **selection theorem** is a theorem that guarantees the existence of a single-valued [selection function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selection_function) from a given [set-valued map](wikipedia://en/12506030). There are various selection theorems, and they are important in the theories of [differential inclusions](wikipedia://en/13725281), [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565), and [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255).[^o2]

:::

## Preliminaries {#Preliminaries}

Given two sets *X* and *Y*, let *F* be a [set-valued function](wikipedia://en/12506030) from *X* and *Y*. Equivalently, $F:X\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(Y)$ is a function from *X* to the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of *Y*.

A function $f: X \rightarrow Y$ is said to be a **selection** of *F* if

$$\forall x \in X: \,\,\, f(x) \in F(x) \,.$$

In other words, given an input *x* for which the original function *F* returns multiple values, the new function *f* returns a single value. This is a special case of a [choice function](wikipedia://en/1432664).

The [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) implies that a selection function always exists; however, it is often important that the selection have some "nice" properties, such as [continuity](wikipedia://en/6122) or [measurability](wikipedia://en/44055). This is where the selection theorems come into action: they guarantee that, if *F* satisfies certain properties, then it has a selection *f* that is continuous or has other desirable properties.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Selection theorems for set-valued functions"}

## Selection theorems for set-valued functions {#Selection_theorems_for_set-valued_functions}

The [Michael selection theorem](wikipedia://en/17970233)[^o6] says that the following conditions are sufficient for the existence of a [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) selection:

- *X* is a [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact) space;
- *Y* is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989);
- *F* is [lower hemicontinuous](wikipedia://en/2945778);
- for all *x* in *X*, the set *F*(*x*) is nonempty, [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) and [closed](wikipedia://en/47279).

The **approximate selection theorem**[^o7] states the following:

> Suppose *X* is a compact metric space, *Y* a non-empty [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) subset of a [normed vector space](wikipedia://en/21538), and Φ: *X →* $\mathcal P(Y)$ a multifunction all of whose values are compact and convex. If graph(Φ) is closed, then for every ε > 0 there exists a continuous function *f* : *X* → *Y* with graph(*f*) ⊂ \[graph(Φ)\]<sub>ε</sub>.

Here, $[S]_\varepsilon$ denotes the $\varepsilon$-dilation of $S$, that is, the union of radius-$\varepsilon$ open balls centered on points in $S$. The theorem implies the existence of a continuous *approximate* selection.

Another set of sufficient conditions for the existence of a continuous approximate selection is given by the **Deutsch–Kenderov theorem**,[^o8] whose conditions are more general than those of Michael's theorem (and thus the selection is only approximate):

- *X* is a [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact) space;
- *Y* is a [normed vector space](wikipedia://en/21538);
- *F* is *almost lower hemicontinuous*, that is, at each $x \in X$, for each neighborhood $V$ of $0$ there exists a neighborhood $U$ of $x$ such that $\bigcap_{u \in U} \{F(u)+V\} \ne \emptyset$;
- for all *x* in *X*, the set *F*(*x*) is nonempty and [convex](wikipedia://en/6292).

In a later note, Xu proved that the Deutsch–Kenderov theorem is also valid if $Y$ is a locally convex [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752).[^o9]

The **Yannelis-Prabhakar selection theorem**[^o10] says that the following conditions are sufficient for the existence of a [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) selection:

- *X* is a [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracompact) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637);
- *Y* is a [linear topological space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_topological_space);
- for all *x* in *X*, the set *F*(*x*) is nonempty and [convex](wikipedia://en/6292);
- for all *y* in *Y*, the inverse set *F*<sup>−1</sup>(*y*) is an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) in *X*.

The [Kuratowski and Ryll-Nardzewski measurable selection theorem](wikipedia://en/46310740) says that if *X* is a [Polish space](wikipedia://en/396622) and $\mathcal B$ its [Borel](wikipedia://en/43327) [σ-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra), $\mathrm{Cl}(X)$ is the set of nonempty closed subsets of *X*, $(\Omega, \mathcal F)$ is a [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333), and $F : \Omega \to \mathrm{Cl}(X)$ is an $\mathcal F$-weakly measurable map (that is, for every open subset $U \subseteq X$ we have $\{\omega \in \Omega : F(\omega) \cap U \neq \emptyset \} \in \mathcal F$), then $F$ has a [selection](wikipedia://en/1432664) that is $(\mathcal F, \mathcal B)$-[measurable](wikipedia://en/44055).[^o11]

Other selection theorems for set-valued functions include:

- [Bressan–Colombo directionally continuous selection theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bressan–Colombo_directionally_continuous_selection_theorem)
- [Castaing representation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castaing_representation_theorem)
- [Fryszkowski decomposable map selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryszkowski_decomposable_map_selection)
- [Helly's selection theorem](wikipedia://en/6740565)
- [Zero-dimensional Michael selection theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-dimensional_Michael_selection_theorem)
- [Robert Aumann measurable selection theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Aumann_measurable_selection_theorem)
