# Nina Holden

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nina Holden"}

**Nina Holden** is a Norwegian mathematician interested in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895), including [graphons](wikipedia://en/26987628), random planar maps, the [Schramm–Loewner evolution](wikipedia://en/6706108), and their applications to [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312). She was a Junior Fellow at the Institute for Theoretical Studies at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich), and is currently an associate professor at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University).

:::

## Education {#Education}

As a student at [Berg Upper Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berg_Upper_Secondary_School) in [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo), Norway, Holden became the first woman to win the Abel competition, Norway's national Mathematical Olympiad. She competed in 2005 in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208), where she earned an honorable mention with one of the two top scores on the Norwegian team.

She became a student at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) in Norway, where she earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics and computational science in 2008 and a master's degree in applied mathematics in 2010. While a student in Oslo, she also visited the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) from 2006 to 2007.

After three years of work as an energy market analyst, she went to the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) for graduate study, and completed her Ph.D. there in 2018. Her dissertation, *Cardy embedding of random planar maps and a KPZ formula for mated trees*, was supervised by [Scott Sheffield](wikipedia://en/40341916).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In association with the 2021 [Breakthrough Prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize), Holden was awarded one of three 2021 [Maryam Mirzakhani New Frontiers Prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryam_Mirzakhani_New_Frontiers_Prize), for early-career achievements by a woman mathematician. The citation reads: "for work in random geometry, particularly on Liouville Quantum Gravity as a scaling limit of random triangulations." The particular work refers to her joint work with [Xin Sun](wikipedia://en/83658670) on the convergence of uniform triangulations under a conformal embedding. The other two winners of the prize were [Urmila Mahadev](wikipedia://en/65365972) and [Lisa Piccirillo](wikipedia://en/64011543). She received the 2022 Viggo Brun Prize of the [Norwegian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/15255049), "for her exceptionally deep and broad contributions to probability theory, especially for her work on random surfaces and quantum gravity in two dimensions". In 2023, she was a recipient of the [Rollo Davidson Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollo_Davidson_Prize), and in the following year, she was awarded the [EMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMS_Prize) "for her profound contributions to probability theory and its applications to statistical physics, including results linking Liouville quantum gravity, the Schramm-Loewner evolution, and random triangulations".

Holden was elected to the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters) in 2026.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Holden's mother, Marit Holden, is the Chief Research Scientist at the [Norwegian Computing Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Computing_Center).[^o6][^o7] Her father, Lars Holden, was its managing director from 2001 to 2022 and is the brother of [Helge Holden](wikipedia://en/24884384).[^o6][^o8][^o9]
