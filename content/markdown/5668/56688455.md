# Uta Merzbach

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Uta Merzbach"}

**Uta Caecilia Merzbach** (February 9, 1933 – June 27, 2017) was a German-American [historian of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) who became the first curator of [mathematical instruments](wikipedia://en/26722913) at the [Smithsonian Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Merzbach was born in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), where her mother was a [philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philologist) and her father was an economist who worked for the [Reich Association of Jews in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reich_Association_of_Jews_in_Germany) during World War II. The Nazi government closed the association in June 1943; they arrested the family, along with other leading members of the association, and sent them to the [Theresienstadt concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresienstadt_concentration_camp) on August 4, 1943. The Merzbachs survived the war and the camp, and after living for a year in a refugee camp in [Deggendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deggendorf) they moved to [Georgetown, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown,_Texas) in 1946, where her father found a faculty position at [Southwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_University).

## Education {#Education}

After high school in [Brownwood, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownwood,_Texas), Merzbach entered Southwestern, but transferred after two years to the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin), where she graduated in 1952 with a bachelor's degree in mathematics. In 1954, she earned a master's degree there, also in mathematics. Merzbach became a school teacher, but soon returned to graduate study at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).

She completed her Ph.D. at Harvard in 1965. Her dissertation, *Quantity of Structure: Development of Modern Algebraic Concepts from Leibniz to Dedekind*, combined mathematics and the history of science; it was jointly supervised by mathematician [Garrett Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/1180916) and [historian of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian_of_science) [I. Bernard Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._Bernard_Cohen).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Merzbach joined the Smithsonian as an associate curator in 1964 (later curator), and served there until 1988 in the [National Museum of American History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_American_History). As well as collecting mathematical objects at the Smithsonian, she also collected interviews with many of the pioneers of computing. In 1991, she co-authored the second edition of *A History of Mathematics*, originally published in 1968 by [Carl Benjamin Boyer](wikipedia://en/6694636).

After her retirement she returned to Georgetown, Texas, where she died in 2017.
