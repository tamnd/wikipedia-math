# Quotient stack

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quotient stack"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **quotient stack** is a [stack](wikipedia://en/13185596) that parametrizes equivariant objects. Geometrically, it generalizes a quotient of a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) or a [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) by a [group](wikipedia://en/19447): a quotient variety, say, would be a coarse approximation of a quotient stack.

:::

The notion is of fundamental importance in the study of stacks: a stack that arises in nature is often either a quotient stack itself or admits a stratification by quotient stacks (e.g., a [Deligne–Mumford stack](wikipedia://en/40639179)). A quotient stack is also used to construct other stacks like **classifying stacks**.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A quotient stack is defined as follows. Let *G* be an affine smooth [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) over a scheme *S* and *X* an *S*-scheme on which *G* [acts](wikipedia://en/56600253). Let the quotient stack $[X/G]$ be the [category over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibered_category) the category of *S*-schemes, where

- an object over *T* is a [principal *G*-bundle](wikipedia://en/41910974) $P\to T$ together with equivariant map $P\to X$;
- a morphism from $P\to T$ to $P'\to T'$ is a [bundle map](wikipedia://en/7220589) (i.e., forms a commutative diagram) that is compatible with the equivariant maps $P\to X$ and $P'\to X$.

Suppose the [quotient](wikipedia://en/39561038) $X/G$ exists as an [algebraic space](wikipedia://en/2394027) (for example, by the [Keel–Mori theorem](wikipedia://en/39560494)). The canonical map

$$[X/G] \to X/G,$$

that sends a bundle *P* over *T* to a corresponding *T*-point,[^o4] need not be an isomorphism of stacks; that is, the space "X/G" is usually coarser. The canonical map is an isomorphism if and only if the stabilizers are trivial (in which case $X/G$ exists).

In general, $[X/G]$ is an [Artin stack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_stack) (also called algebraic stack). If the stabilizers of the [geometric points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_point) are finite and reduced, then it is a [Deligne–Mumford stack](wikipedia://en/40639179).

(txt) has shown: let *X* be a normal Noetherian algebraic stack whose stabilizer groups at closed points are affine. Then *X* is a quotient stack if and only if it has the [resolution property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolution_property); i.e., every coherent sheaf is a quotient of a vector bundle. Earlier, [Robert Wayne Thomason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wayne_Thomason) proved that a quotient stack has the resolution property.

Remark: It is possible to approach the construction from the point of view of [simplicial sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_sheaf).[^o6] See also: [simplicial diagram](wikipedia://en/79777766).

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

An effective quotient [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504), e.g., $[M/G]$ where the $G$ action has only finite stabilizers on the smooth space $M$, is an example of a quotient stack.[^o9]

If $X = S$ with trivial action of $G$ (often $S$ is a point), then $[S/G]$ is called the **classifying stack** of $G$ (in analogy with the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) of $G$) and is usually denoted by $BG$. [Borel's theorem](wikipedia://en/43948116) describes the [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) of the classifying stack.

### Moduli of line bundles {#Moduli_of_line_bundles}

One of the basic examples of quotient stacks comes from the moduli stack $B\mathbb{G}_m$ of line bundles $[*/\mathbb{G}_m]$ over $\text{Sch}$, or $[S/\mathbb{G}_m]$ over $\text{Sch}/S$ for the trivial $\mathbb{G}_m$-action on $S$. For any scheme (or $S$-scheme) $X$, the $X$-points of the moduli stack are the groupoid of principal $\mathbb{G}_m$-bundles $P \to X$.

### Moduli of line bundles with n-sections {#Moduli_of_line_bundles_with_n-sections}

There is another closely related moduli stack given by $[\mathbb{A}^n/\mathbb{G}_m]$ which is the moduli stack of line bundles with $n$-sections. This follows directly from the definition of quotient stacks evaluated on points. For a scheme $X$, the $X$-points are the groupoid whose objects are given by the set

> $$[\mathbb{A}^n/\mathbb{G}_m](X) = \left\{ \begin{matrix} P & \to & \mathbb{A}^n \\ \downarrow & & \\ X \end{matrix} : \begin{aligned} &P \to \mathbb{A}^n \text{ is }\mathbb{G}_m\text{ equivariant and} \\ &P \to X \text{ is a principal } \mathbb{G}_m\text{-bundle} \end{aligned} \right\}$$

The morphism in the top row corresponds to the $n$-sections of the associated line bundle over $X$. This can be found by noting giving a $\mathbb{G}_m$-equivariant map $\phi: P \to \mathbb{A}^1$ and restricting it to the fiber $P|_x$ gives the same data as a section $\sigma$ of the bundle. This can be checked by looking at a chart and sending a point $x \in X$ to the map $\phi_x$, noting the set of $\mathbb{G}_m$-equivariant maps $P|_x \to \mathbb{A}^1$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{G}_m$. This construction then globalizes by gluing affine charts together, giving a global section of the bundle. Since $\mathbb{G}_m$-equivariant maps to $\mathbb{A}^n$ is equivalently an $n$-tuple of $\mathbb{G}_m$-equivariant maps to $\mathbb{A}^1$, the result holds.

::: {#o13 .statement cue=named-heading name="Moduli of formal group laws"}

### Moduli of formal group laws {#Moduli_of_formal_group_laws}

Example:[^o14] Let *L* be the [Lazard ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazard_ring); i.e., $L = \pi_* \operatorname{MU}$. Then the quotient stack $[\operatorname{Spec}L/G]$ by $G$,

$$G(R) = \{g \in R[\![t]\!] | g(t) = b_0 t + b_1t^2+ \cdots, b_0 \in R^\times \},$$

is called the [moduli stack of formal group laws](wikipedia://en/42478068), denoted by $\mathcal{M}_\text{FG}$.
