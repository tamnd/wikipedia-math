# Chicago school (mathematical analysis)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chicago school"}

The **Chicago school of mathematical analysis** is a [school of thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_thought) in mathematics that emphasizes the applications of [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659) to the study of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations).[^o2] Mathematician [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477) co-founded the school with his doctoral student [Alberto Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Calderón) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in the 1950s.[^o3][^o4][^o5] Over the years, Zygmund mentored over 40 doctoral students at the University of Chicago.[^o2]

:::

## Comments {#Comments}

The Chicago school of analysis is considered to be one of the strongest schools of mathematical analysis in the 20th century, which was responsible for some of the most important developments in analysis.[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 1986, Antoni Zygmund received the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science), in part for his "creation and leadership of the strongest school of analytical research in the contemporary mathematical world."[^o9]
