# Jane M. Hawkins

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jane M. Hawkins"}

**Jane Margaret Hawkins** is an American mathematician known for her research in [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) and [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518), including [cellular automata](wikipedia://en/54342) and [Julia sets](wikipedia://en/56434). She is a professor of mathematics at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill).[^o2] More recent research has included work on cellular automata models for the spread of [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV), [Hepatitis C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_C) and [Ebola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola).[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hawkins was raised in [Schenectady, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schenectady,_New_York). Her father was a [chemical engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineer).[^o2] She graduated from the [College of the Holy Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_the_Holy_Cross) in [Worcester, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester,_Massachusetts), with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in mathematics in 1976 as class valedictorian. She then won a [Marshall Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Scholarship) to pursue graduate studies in England, earning her [M.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.S.) in mathematics and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics specializing in [catastrophe theory](wikipedia://en/434288) from the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) in 1981. Her doctoral dissertation, completed under [Klaus Schmidt](wikipedia://en/39443367), was titled, *Type III Diffeomorphisms of Manifolds.*[^o5]

She taught at the [State University of New York, Stony Brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York,_Stony_Brook) from 1980 to 1987, before moving to the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.[^o6] Her book *Ergodic Dynamics* was published by Springer in 2021.[^o7] Her book *The Mathematics of Cellular Automata* was published by AMS in 2024.[^o8] And her third book, *Mathematics for Artificial Intelligence*, was published in 2026.[^o9]

## Service {#Service}

In 2004, Hawkins presented testimony to a subcommittee of the [House Committee on Appropriations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Appropriations) in support of grants to mathematical research, and again in 2012.[^o11][^o12] She served as Treasurer of the American Mathematical Society, chair of the AMS Investment Committee, and Board of Trustees Member from 2011 until February 1, 2021.[^o13] She is also a former AMS Council member at large.[^o14] Hawkins has done a lot of outreach for women in mathematics, most notably participating in the George Washington University's Summer Program for Women in Math (SPWM) as a faculty member, guest lecturer, and panel moderator between 1999 and its final year in 2013.[^o15]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Hawkins was the first female valedictorian at the College of the Holy Cross.[^o17] In 2012, Hawkins became an inaugural fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o18]

## Personal {#Personal}

Hawkins is married to mathematician [Michael E. Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_E._Taylor), whom she credits with encouraging her with continuing in research.[^o2]
