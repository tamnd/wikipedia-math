# Magne S. Espedal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Magne S. Espedal"}

**Magne S. Espedal** (23 September 1942 – 25 January 2010) was a Norwegian mathematician.

:::

He was born in [Fjaler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fjaler) as a son of a smallholder. He [finished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examen_artium) his secondary education in 1962 and graduated from the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) with the [cand.real.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cand.real.) degree in 1966.[^o2]

Espedal was appointed as an [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor) at the [University of Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bergen) in 1971 before finishing his [dr.philos.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr.philos.) degree in 1974. He was promoted to [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent) in 1982 and professor of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) in 1985. He was also a [visiting scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_scholar) at the [University of Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wyoming) (1985–86) and [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University) (1993–94).[^o2] He was inducted into the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters) in 1998.[^o3]

As a science and education administrator, Espedal sat on the Academic College (the board) of the University of Bergen from 1989, the council of science of [NAVF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAVF) from 1977 to 1984 (chair from 1982–84), the council of [NTNF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NTNF) from 1986 to 1993 (deputy chair from 1991–92),[^o2] and the board of science and technology in the [Research Council of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Council_of_Norway) from 1993 to 1996.[^o4] He was also one of the founders of the Centre for Integrated Petroleum Research.[^o5] Espedal was a visiting scholar at the [University of Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Stuttgart) when he died in January 2010, aged 67.[^o4] After his death, an [adjunct professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunct_professor) at the University of Bergen was named in his honor; the first holder being [Margot Gerritsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Gerritsen).[^o5]
