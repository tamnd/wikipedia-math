# Martin Bridson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Martin Bridson"}

**Martin Robert Bridson** (born 22 October 1964) is a [Manx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manx_people) mathematician. He is Whitehead professor of pure mathematics at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), and the president of the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655). He was previously the head of Oxford's [Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Institute). He is a fellow of [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford) and an honorary fellow of [Hertford College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertford_College,_Oxford). Specializing in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), Bridson is best known for his work in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164).

:::

## Education and early life {#Education_and_early_life}

*Image: Martin\_Bridson2.jpg.* Bridson at [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach) in 2013

Bridson is a native of the [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man).[^o7] He was educated at [St Ninian's High School, Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Ninian's_High_School,_Douglas), then [Hertford College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertford_College,_Oxford), and [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), receiving a Master of Arts degree from Oxford in 1986, and a Master of Science degree in 1988 followed by a PhD in 1991 from Cornell.[^o9][^o3] His PhD thesis was supervised by [Karen Vogtmann](wikipedia://en/20455422),[^o3] and was entitled *Geodesics and Curvature in Metric Simplicial Complexes*.

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

He was an assistant professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) until 1996, was twice a visiting professor at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva) (1992 and 2006), and was Professor of Mathematics at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) from 2002 to 2007. From 1993 to 2002 he was a Tutorial Fellow of Pembroke College, Oxford, and Reader (1996) then Professor of Topology (2000) in the University of Oxford. He remains a Supernumerary Fellow of Pembroke College.[^o11] In 2016, Bridson became only the second Manxman to ever be elected to the Royal Society, after [Edward Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Forbes). In 2020, he was elected to [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea).[^o12] With [André Haefliger](wikipedia://en/20280849), he won the 2020 [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize) for Mathematical Exposition for the highly influential book *Metric Spaces of Non-positive Curvature*, published by Springer-Verlag in 1999.
