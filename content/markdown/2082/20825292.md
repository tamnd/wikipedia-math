# Golden rhombus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Golden rhombus"}

*Image: GoldenRhombus.svg.* The golden rhombus.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **golden rhombus** is a [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) whose diagonals are in the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386):[^o2]

:::

$${D\over d} = \varphi = {{1+\sqrt5}\over2} \approx 1.618~034$$

Equivalently, it is the [Varignon parallelogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varignon_parallelogram) formed from the edge midpoints of a [golden rectangle](wikipedia://en/219374).[^o2] Rhombi with this shape form the faces of several notable polyhedra. The golden rhombus should be distinguished from the two rhombi of the [Penrose tiling](wikipedia://en/26611936), which are both related in other ways to the golden ratio but have different shapes than the golden rhombus.[^o3]

## Angles {#Angles}

(See the [characterizations](wikipedia://en/183884) and the [basic properties](wikipedia://en/183884) of the general [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) for angle properties.)

The internal supplementary angles of the golden rhombus are:[^o5]

- Acute angle: $\alpha=2\arctan{1\over\varphi}$ ;

by using the [arctangent addition formula](wikipedia://en/374220) (see [inverse trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/374220)):

$$\alpha=\arctan{{2\over\varphi}\over{1-({1\over\varphi})^2}}=\arctan{{2\over\varphi}\over{1\over\varphi}}=\arctan2\approx63.43495^\circ.$$

- Obtuse angle: $\beta=2\arctan\varphi=\pi-\arctan2\approx116.56505^\circ,$

which is also the [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) of the [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361).[^o6]

Note: an "anecdotal" equality: $\pi- \arctan2=\arctan1+ \arctan3~.$

## Edge and diagonals {#Edge_and_diagonals}

By using the [parallelogram law](wikipedia://en/362953) (see the [basic properties](wikipedia://en/183884) of the general [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884)):

The edge length of the golden rhombus in terms of the diagonal length $d$ is:

- $a={1\over2}\sqrt{d^2+(\varphi d)^2}={1\over2}\sqrt{1+\varphi^2}~d={{\sqrt{2+\varphi}}\over2}~d={1\over4}\sqrt{10+2\sqrt5}~d\approx0.95106~d~.~$ Hence:

The diagonal lengths of the golden rhombus in terms of the edge length $a$ are:[^o5]

- $$d={2a\over\sqrt{2+\varphi}}=2\sqrt{{3-\varphi}\over5}~a=\sqrt{2-{2\over\sqrt5}}~a\approx1.05146~a~.$$
- $$D={2\varphi a\over\sqrt{2+\varphi}}=2\sqrt{{2+\varphi}\over5}~a=\sqrt{2+{2\over\sqrt5}}~a\approx1.70130~a~.$$

## Area {#Area}

- By using the [area](wikipedia://en/183884) formula of the general [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) in terms of its diagonal lengths $D$ and $d$ :

The area of the golden rhombus in terms of its diagonal length $d$ is:[^o10]

$$A = {{(\varphi d)\cdot d}\over2} = {{\varphi}\over2}~d^2 = {{1+\sqrt5}\over4}~d^2 \approx 0.80902~d^2~.$$

- By using the [area](wikipedia://en/183884) formula of the general [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) in terms of its edge length $a$ :

The area of the golden rhombus in terms of its edge length $a$ is:[^o5]

$$A = (\sin(\arctan2))~a^2 = {2\over\sqrt5}~a^2 \approx 0.89443~a^2~.$$

Note: $\alpha+\beta = \pi$ , hence: $\sin\alpha = \sin\beta~.$

## As the faces of polyhedra {#As_the_faces_of_polyhedra}

Several notable polyhedra have golden rhombi as their faces. They include the two [golden rhombohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_rhombohedra) (with six faces each), the [Bilinski dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/50652823) (with 12 faces), the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) (with 12 faces), the [rhombic icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3691443) (with 20 faces), the [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025) (with 30 faces), and the nonconvex [rhombic hexecontahedron](wikipedia://en/43733185) (with 60 faces). The first five of these are the only convex polyhedra with golden rhomb faces, but there exist infinitely many nonconvex polyhedra having this shape for all of their faces.[^o12]
