# Medial rhombic triacontahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Medial rhombic triacontahedron"}

Uniform dual polyhedron stat table

*Image: Medial rhombic triacontahedron.stl.* 3D model of a medial rhombic triacontahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **medial rhombic triacontahedron** (or **midly rhombic triacontahedron**) is a nonconvex [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470). It is a [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of the [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025), and can also be called **small stellated triacontahedron**. Its [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) is the [dodecadodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157379).

:::

Its 24 vertices are all on the 12 axes with 5-fold symmetry (i.e. each corresponds to one of the 12 vertices of the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968)). This means that on each axis there is an inner and an outer vertex. The ratio of outer to inner [vertex radius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumsphere) is $\varphi \approx 1.618$, the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).

It has 30 intersecting [rhombic](wikipedia://en/183884) faces, which correspond to the faces of the convex [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025). The diagonals in the rhombs of the convex solid have a ratio of 1 to $\varphi$. The medial solid can be generated from the convex one by stretching the shorter diagonal from length 1 to $\varphi^3 \approx 4.236$. So the ratio of rhomb diagonals in the medial solid is 1 to $\varphi^2 \approx 2.618$.

This solid is to the [compound of small stellated dodecahedron and great dodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_of_small_stellated_dodecahedron_and_great_dodecahedron) what the convex one is to the [compound of dodecahedron and icosahedron](wikipedia://en/8010901): The crossing edges in the [dual compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_compound) are the diagonals of the rhombs. The faces have two angles of $\arccos(\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{5})\approx 41.810\,314\,895\,78^{\circ}$, and two of $\arccos(-\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{5})\approx 138.189\,685\,104\,22^{\circ}$. Its [dihedral angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_angles) equal $\arccos(-\frac{1}{2})= 120^{\circ}$. Part of each rhomb lies inside the solid, hence is invisible in solid models.

## Related hyperbolic tiling {#Related_hyperbolic_tiling}

It is topologically equivalent to a quotient space of the [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/241291) [order-5 square tiling](wikipedia://en/9421082), by distorting the rhombi into [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29). As such, it is topologically a [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299) of index two:[^o3]

| *Image: Bring sextic with squares.svg.* Graph of the m. r. t. embedded in [Bring's curve](wikipedia://en/35477794) | *Image: H2-5-4-primal.svg.* order-5 square tiling |
|---|---|

Note that the order-5 square tiling is dual to the [order-4 pentagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/9421111), and a quotient space of the order-4 pentagonal tiling is topologically equivalent to the dual of the medial rhombic triacontahedron, the [dodecadodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157379).
