# H. J. Ryser

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="H. J. Ryser"}

**Herbert John Ryser** (July 28, 1923 – July 12, 1985) was a professor of mathematics, widely regarded as one of the major figures in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) in the 20th century.[^o3][^o4] He is the namesake of the [Bruck–Ryser–Chowla theorem](wikipedia://en/456781), [Ryser's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryser's_formula) for the [computation of the permanent of a matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computation_of_the_permanent_of_a_matrix), and [Ryser's conjecture](wikipedia://en/64550992).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Ryser was born to the family of Fred G. and Edna (Huels) Ryser. He received the B.A. (1945), M.A. (1947), and Ph.D. (1948) from the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin).[^o6] His doctoral thesis "Rational Vector Spaces" was supervised by [Cornelius Joseph Everett, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius_Joseph_Everett,_Jr.) and [Cyrus C. MacDuffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Colton_MacDuffee). (Ryser was Everett's only doctoral student.)[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

After his Ph.D., Ryser spent a year at Princeton's [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), then joined the faculty of [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University). In 1962 he took a professorship at [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University), and in 1967 moved to [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech).[^o10] His doctoral students include [Richard A. Brualdi](wikipedia://en/22929637), [Clement W. H. Lam](wikipedia://en/45187642), and [Marion Tinsley](wikipedia://en/48162).

Ryser contributed to the theory of [combinatorial designs](wikipedia://en/2761968), finite [set systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_system), the [permanent](wikipedia://en/215889), combinatorial functions, and to many other topics in combinatorics.[^o3] He served as editor of the journals *[Journal of Combinatorial Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Combinatorial_Theory)*, *[Linear and Multilinear Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_and_Multilinear_Algebra)*, and *[Journal of Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Algebra)*.[^o3] Ryser's estate funded an endowment creating undergraduate mathematics scholarships at Caltech known as the H. J. Ryser Scholarships.[^o11]

The *Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A* denoted two issues after Ryser's passing as the "Herbert J. Ryser Memorial Issue", parts 1 and 2.[^o12]
