# Sidon sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sidon sequence"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **Sidon sequence** is a sequence $A=\{a_0,a_1,a_2,\dots\}$ of natural numbers in which all pairwise sums $a_i+a_j$ (for $i\le j$) are different. Sidon sequences are also called **Sidon sets**; they are named after the Hungarian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Simon Sidon](wikipedia://en/35273851), who introduced the concept in his investigations of [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038).

:::

The main problem in the study of Sidon sequences, posed by Sidon,[^o2] is to find the maximum number of elements that a Sidon sequence can contain, up to some bound $x$. Despite a large body of research,[^o3] the question has remained unsolved.[^o4]

## Early results {#Early_results}

[Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Pál Turán](wikipedia://en/614998) proved that, for every $x>0$, the number of elements smaller than $x$ in a Sidon sequence is at most $\sqrt{x}+O(\sqrt[4]{x})$. Several years earlier, James Singer had constructed Sidon sequences with $\sqrt{x}(1-o(1))$ terms less than *x*. The upper bound was improved to $\sqrt{x}+\sqrt[4]{x}+1$ in 1969[^o6] and to $\sqrt{x}+0.998\sqrt[4]{x}$ in 2023.[^o7]

In 1994 Erdős offered 500 dollars for a proof or disproof of the bound $\sqrt{x}+o(x^\varepsilon)$.[^o8]

## Dense Sidon Sets {#Dense_Sidon_Sets}

A Sidon subset $A\subset [n] := \{1,2,\dots ,n\}$ is called *dense* if $\left | A\right | = \max \left | S\right |$ where the maximum is taken over all Sidon subsets of $[n]$. The structure of dense Sidon sets has a rich literature[^o10][^o11] and classic constructions by Erdős–Turán,[^o12] Singer,[^o13] [Bose](wikipedia://en/1162266),[^o14] Spence,[^o15][^o16] Hughes[^o17] and Cilleruelo[^o18] have established that a dense Sidon set $A$ satisfies $\left | A\right | \ge \left(1-o(1)\right)\sqrt{n}$. As remarked by [Ruzsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Z._Ruzsa), "somehow all known constructions of dense Sidon sets involve the primes".[^o19]

A recent result of [Balasubramanian](wikipedia://en/18734196) and Dutta[^o20] shows that if a dense Sidon set $A = \{a_1,\dots , a_{\left | A\right |}\}\subset [n]$ has cardinality $|A|=n^{1/2}-L^\prime$, then

$$a_m = m\cdot n^{1/2} + \mathcal O\left( n^{7/8}\right) + \mathcal O\left(L^{1/2}\cdot n^{3/4}\right)$$

where $L=\max\{0,L^\prime\}$. This directly gives some useful asymptotic results including

$$\sum_{a\in A} a^\ell = \frac {1}{\ell +1} \cdot n^{\frac{2\ell +1}{2}} + \mathcal O \left( n^{\frac{8\ell +3}{8}} \right) + \mathcal O\left( L^{1/2}\cdot n^{\frac{4\ell +1}{4}}\right)$$

for any positive integer $\ell$.

Dense Sidon sets often exhibit surprising symmetries. For example, it is known that dense Sidon sets are uniformly distributed,[^o21][^o22][^o23] equidistributed in residue classes,[^o24][^o25] and even in smooth Bohr neighbourhoods.[^o26]

## Infinite Sidon sequences {#Infinite_Sidon_sequences}

Erdős also showed that, for any particular infinite Sidon sequence $A$ with $A(x)$ denoting the number of its elements up to $x$,

$$\liminf_{x \to \infty} \frac{A(x)\sqrt{\log x}}{\sqrt{x}}\leq 1.$$

That is, infinite Sidon sequences are thinner than the densest finite Sidon sequences.

For the other direction, [Chowla](wikipedia://en/292142) and Mian observed that the [greedy algorithm](wikipedia://en/89247) gives an infinite Sidon sequence with $A(x)>c\sqrt[3]{x}$ for every $x$.[^o28] [Ajtai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miklós_Ajtai), [Komlós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/János_Komlós_%28mathematician%29), and [Szemerédi](wikipedia://en/663489) improved this with a construction[^o29] of a Sidon sequence with

$$A(x)>\sqrt[3]{x\log x}.$$

The best lower bound to date was given by [Imre Z. Ruzsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Z._Ruzsa), who proved[^o30] that a Sidon sequence with

$$A(x)>x^{\sqrt{2}-1-o(1)}$$

exists. Erdős conjectured that an infinite Sidon set $A$ exists for which $A(x)>x^{1/2-o(1)}$ holds. He and [Rényi](wikipedia://en/614988) showed[^o31] the existence of a sequence $\{a_0,a_1,\dots\}$ with the conjectural density but satisfying only the weaker property that there is a constant $k$ such that for every natural number $n$ there are at most $k$ solutions of the equation $a_i+a_j=n$. (To be a Sidon sequence would require that $k=1$.)

Erdős further conjectured that there exists a nonconstant [integer](wikipedia://en/14563)-[coefficient](wikipedia://en/51203) [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) whose values at the [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers) form a Sidon sequence. Specifically, he asked if the set of fifth powers is a Sidon set. Ruzsa came close to this by showing that there is a [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) $c$ with $0<c<1$ such that the range of the function $f(x)=x^5+\lfloor cx^4\rfloor$ is a Sidon sequence, where $\lfloor\ \rfloor$ denotes the [integer part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_part). As $c$ is irrational, this function $f(x)$ is not a polynomial. The statement that the set of fifth powers is a Sidon set is a special case of the later conjecture of [Lander, Parkin and Selfridge](wikipedia://en/9660#Generalizations).

## Sidon sequences which are asymptotic bases {#Sidon_sequences_which_are_asymptotic_bases}

The existence of Sidon sequences that form an asymptotic basis of order $m$ (meaning that every sufficiently large natural number $n$ can be written as the sum of $m$ numbers from the sequence) has been proved for $m=5$ in 2010,[^o33] $m=4$ in 2014,[^o34] $m=3+\varepsilon$ (the sum of four terms with one smaller than $n^\varepsilon$, for arbitrarily small positive $\varepsilon$) in 2015[^o35] and $m=3$ in 2024.[^o36][^o37] This last one was posed as a problem in a paper of Erdős, [Sárközy](wikipedia://en/22916190) and [Sós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_T._Sós) in 1994.[^o38]

## Relationship to Golomb rulers {#Relationship_to_Golomb_rulers}

All finite Sidon sets are [Golomb rulers](wikipedia://en/48211), and vice versa.

To see this, suppose for a [contradiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_by_contradiction) that $S$ is a Sidon set and not a Golomb ruler. Since it is not a Golomb ruler, there must be four members such that $a_i-a_j=a_k-a_l$. It follows that $a_i+a_l=a_k+a_j$, which contradicts the proposition that $S$ is a Sidon set. Therefore all Sidon sets must be Golomb rulers. By a similar argument, all Golomb rulers must be Sidon sets.
