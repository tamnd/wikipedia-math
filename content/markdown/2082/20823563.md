# Great rhombic triacontahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Great rhombic triacontahedron"}

Uniform dual polyhedron stat table

*Image: Great rhombic triacontahedron.stl.* 3D model of a great rhombic triacontahedron

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great rhombic triacontahedron** is a nonconvex [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120), [isotoxal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotoxal) [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470). It is the [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of the [great icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157391) (U54). Like the convex [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025) it has 30 [rhombic](wikipedia://en/183884) faces, 60 edges and 32 vertices (also 20 on 3-fold and 12 on 5-fold axes).

:::

It can be constructed from the convex solid by expanding the faces by factor of $\varphi^3 \approx 4.236$, where $\varphi\!$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).

This solid is to the [compound of great icosahedron and great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8010947) what the convex one is to the [compound of dodecahedron and icosahedron](wikipedia://en/8010901): The crossing edges in the [dual compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_compound) are the diagonals of the rhombs.

What resembles an "excavated" rhombic triacontahedron (compare [excavated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/32917096) and [excavated icosahedron](wikipedia://en/2860441)) can be seen within the middle of this compound. The rest of the polyhedron strikingly resembles a [rhombic hexecontahedron](wikipedia://en/43733185).

The rhombs have two angles of $\arccos(\frac{1}{5}\sqrt{5})\approx 63.434\,948\,822\,92^{\circ}$, and two of $\arccos(-\frac{1}{5}\sqrt{5})\approx 116.565\,051\,177\,08^{\circ}$. Its [dihedral angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_angles) equal $\arccos(-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{5})= 72^{\circ}$. Part of each rhomb lies inside the solid, hence is invisible in solid models. The ratio between the lengths of the long and short diagonal of the rhombs equals the golden ratio $\varphi$.
