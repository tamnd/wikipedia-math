# De Longchamps point

*Image: De Longchamps point.svg.* The de Longchamps point $L$ of triangle $\triangle ABC$, formed as the reflection of the orthocenter $H$ about the circumcenter $O$ or as the orthocenter of the [anticomplementary triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticomplementary_triangle) $\triangle A'B'C'$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="De Longchamps point"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **de Longchamps point** of a triangle is a [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488) named after French mathematician [Gaston Albert Gohierre de Longchamps](wikipedia://en/36941284). It is the [reflection](wikipedia://en/12487536) of the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) of the triangle about the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let the given triangle have vertices $A$, $B$, and $C$, opposite the respective sides $a$, $b$, and $c$, as is the standard notation in triangle geometry. In the 1886 paper in which he introduced this point, de Longchamps initially defined it as the center of a circle $\Delta$ orthogonal to the three circles $\Delta_a$, $\Delta_b$, and $\Delta_c$, where $\Delta_a$ is centered at $A$ with radius $a$ and the other two circles are defined symmetrically. De Longchamps then also showed that the same point, now known as the de Longchamps point, may be equivalently defined as the orthocenter of the [anticomplementary triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticomplementary_triangle) of $ABC$, and that it is the reflection of the orthocenter of $ABC$ around the circumcenter.[^o5]

The [Steiner circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steiner_circle) of a triangle is concentric with the [nine-point circle](wikipedia://en/161243) and has radius 3/2 the circumradius of the triangle; the de Longchamps point is the [homothetic center](wikipedia://en/15685517) of the Steiner circle and the circumcircle.[^o7]

:::

## Additional properties {#Additional_properties}

As the reflection of the orthocenter around the circumcenter, the de Longchamps point belongs to the line through both of these points, which is the [Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622) of the given triangle. Thus, it is collinear with all the other triangle centers on the Euler line, which along with the orthocenter and circumcenter include the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) and the center of the [nine-point circle](wikipedia://en/161243).[^o2][^o7][^o8]

The de Longchamp point is also collinear, along a different line, with the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) and the [Gergonne point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergonne_point) of its triangle.[^o2][^o9] The three circles centered at $A$, $B$, and $C$, with radii $s-a$, $s-b$, and $s-c$ respectively (where $s$ is the [semiperimeter](wikipedia://en/886617)) are mutually tangent, and there are two more circles tangent to all three of them, the inner and outer Soddy circles; the centers of these two circles also lie on the same line with the de Longchamp point and the incenter.[^o2][^o7] The de Longchamp point is the point of concurrence of this line with the Euler line, and with three other lines defined in a similar way as the line through the incenter but using instead the three [excenters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excenter) of the triangle.[^o7][^o9]

The [Darboux cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darboux_cubic) may be defined from the de Longchamps point, as the locus of points $X$ such that $X$, the [isogonal conjugate](wikipedia://en/2083100) of $X$, and the de Longchamps point are collinear. It is the only cubic curve invariant of a triangle that is both isogonally self-conjugate and centrally symmetric; its center of symmetry is the circumcenter of the triangle.[^o10] The de Longchamps point itself lies on this curve, as does its reflection the orthocenter.[^o2]
