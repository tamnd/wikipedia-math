# Ordered geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ordered geometry"}

**Ordered geometry** is a form of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) featuring the concept of intermediacy (or "betweenness") but, like [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), omitting the basic notion of [measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29). Ordered geometry is a fundamental geometry forming a common framework for [affine](wikipedia://en/243890), [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9417), [absolute](wikipedia://en/699294), and [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291) (but not for projective geometry).

:::

## History {#History}

[Moritz Pasch](wikipedia://en/4449765) first defined a geometry without reference to measurement in 1882. His axioms were improved upon by [Peano](wikipedia://en/13043) (1889), [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) (1899), and [Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen) (1904).[^o3] [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331) anticipated Pasch's approach in definition 4 of *The Elements*: "a straight line is a line which lies evenly with the points on itself".[^o4]

## Primitive concepts {#Primitive_concepts}

The only [primitive notions](wikipedia://en/23706) in ordered geometry are [points](wikipedia://en/593693) *A*, *B*, *C*, ... and the [ternary relation](wikipedia://en/3784565) of intermediacy \[*ABC*\] which can be read as "*B* is between *A* and *C*".

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

The *segment* *AB* is the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of points *P* such that \[*APB*\].

The *interval* *AB* is the segment *AB* and its end points *A* and *B*.

The *ray* *A*/*B* (read as "the ray from *A* away from *B*") is the set of points *P* such that \[*PAB*\].

The *line* *AB* is the interval *AB* and the two rays *A*/*B* and *B*/*A*. Points on the line *AB* are said to be *collinear*.

An *angle* consists of a point *O* (the *vertex*) and two non-collinear rays out from *O* (the *sides*).

A *triangle* is given by three non-collinear points (called *vertices*) and their three *segments* *AB*, *BC*, and *CA*.

If three points *A*, *B*, and *C* are non-collinear, then a *plane* *ABC* is the set of all points collinear with pairs of points on one or two of the sides of triangle *ABC*.

If four points *A*, *B*, *C*, and *D* are non-coplanar, then a *space* ([3-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-space)) *ABCD* is the set of all points collinear with pairs of points selected from any of the four *faces* (planar regions) of the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) *ABCD*.

:::

## Axioms of ordered geometry {#Axioms_of_ordered_geometry}

1. There exist at least two points.
1. If *A* and *B* are distinct points, there exists a *C* such that \[ABC\].
1. If \[*ABC*\], then *A* and *C* are distinct (*A* ≠ *C*).
1. If \[*ABC*\], then \[*CBA*\] but not \[*CAB*\].
1. If *C* and *D* are distinct points on the line *AB*, then *A* is on the line *CD*.
1. If *AB* is a line, there is a point *C* not on the line *AB*.
1. ([Axiom of Pasch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_of_Pasch)) If *ABC* is a triangle and \[*BCD*\] and \[*CEA*\], then there exists a point *F* on the line *DE* for which \[*AFB*\].
1. Axiom of [dimensionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionality):
   1. For planar ordered geometry, all points are in one plane. Or
   1. If *ABC* is a plane, then there exists a point *D* not in the plane *ABC*.
1. All points are in the same plane, space, etc. (depending on the dimension one chooses to work within).
1. (Dedekind's Axiom) For every partition of all the points on a line into two nonempty sets such that no point of either lies between two points of the other, there is a point of one set which lies between every other point of that set and every point of the other set.

These axioms are closely related to [Hilbert's axioms of order](wikipedia://en/838151#II._Order). For a comprehensive survey of axiomatizations of ordered geometry see Pambuccian (2011).[^o10]

## Results {#Results}

### Sylvester's problem of collinear points {#Sylvester's_problem_of_collinear_points}

The [Sylvester–Gallai theorem](wikipedia://en/1052632) can be proven within ordered geometry.[^o14][^o3]

### Parallelism {#Parallelism}

[Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125), [Bolyai](wikipedia://en/98912), and [Lobachevsky](wikipedia://en/648527) developed a notion of [parallelism](wikipedia://en/33731493) which can be expressed in ordered geometry.[^o3]

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem (existence of parallelism)"}

**Theorem (existence of parallelism):** Given a point *A* and a line *r*, not through *A*, there exist exactly two limiting rays from *A* in the plane *Ar* which do not meet *r*. So there is a *parallel* line through *A* which does not meet *r*.

:::

::: {#o17 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem (transmissibility of parallelism)"}

**Theorem (transmissibility of parallelism):** The parallelism of a ray and a line is preserved by adding or subtracting a segment from the beginning of a ray.

The [transitivity](wikipedia://en/200463) of parallelism cannot be proven in ordered geometry.[^o18] Therefore, the "ordered" concept of parallelism does not form an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) on lines.

:::
