# BLISS signature scheme

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="BLISS signature scheme"}

**BLISS** (short for Bimodal Lattice Signature Scheme) is a digital signature scheme proposed by Léo Ducas, Alain Durmus, Tancrède Lepoint and Vadim Lyubashevsky in their 2013 paper "Lattice Signature and Bimodal Gaussians".

:::

In cryptography, a [digital signature](wikipedia://en/59644) ensures that a message is authentically from a specific person who has the [private key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_key) to create such a signature, and can be verified using the corresponding [public key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_key). Current signature schemes rely either on [integer factorization](wikipedia://en/15491), [discrete logarithm](wikipedia://en/181334) or [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) discrete logarithm problem, all of which can be effectively attacked by a [quantum computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computer). BLISS on the other hand, is a post-quantum algorithm, and is meant to resist quantum computer attacks.

Compared to other post-quantum schemes, BLISS claims to offer better computational efficiency, smaller signature size, and higher security. A [presentation](https://csrc.nist.gov/groups/ST/post-quantum-2015/papers/session9-oneill-paper.pdf) once anticipated that BLISS would become a potential candidate for standardization, however it was not submitted to NIST. NIST's criteria for selecting schemes to standardize includes side-channel resistance. However, BLISS and derivative schemes like GALACTICS have shown vulnerabilities to a number of side-channel and timing attacks. [^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5]

## Features {#Features}

- *Lower Rejection Rate*: As a [Fiat-Shamir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat–Shamir_heuristic) lattice signature scheme, BLISS improves upon previous ones by replacing uniform and discrete Gaussian sampling with bimodal samples, thereby reducing sampling rejection rate.
- *Memory-Efficient Gaussian Sampling*: In the paper describing BLISS, the authors constructed a discrete Gaussian sampler of arbitrary standard deviation, from a sampler of a fixed standard deviation then rejecting samples based on pre-computed [Bernoulli constants](wikipedia://en/199189).
- *Signature Compression*: As the coefficients of the signature polynomials are distributed according to discrete Gaussian, the final signature can be compressed using Huffman coding.
