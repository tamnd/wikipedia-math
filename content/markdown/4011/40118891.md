# Alexander Jamieson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Jamieson"}

**Alexander Jamieson** (1782–1850) was a Scottish writer and schoolmaster, now best known as a [rhetorician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhetorician). He has been described as effectively a professional textbook writer.[^o2] After the failure of his school, he worked as an [actuary](wikipedia://en/43405).[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Some of Jamieson's background is obscure.[^o5] He was born at [Rothesay, Bute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothesay,_Bute), to William Jamieson, a [wheelwright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelwright), and Margaret Stewart. In 1821 he obtained a degree of M.A. from [Marischal College, Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marischal_College,_Aberdeen), and an LL.D. there in 1823.[^o3] In 1825, he was admitted as a [sizar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sizar) at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), and became a [ten-year man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten-year_man).[^o6] In 1826 he became a member of the [Astronomical Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society).[^o3]

Jamieson was active in the period between 1814 and 1846, writing textbooks and running a school. In 1824, it was teaching at Heston House on [Hounslow Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hounslow_Heath), where some [Hindustani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_language) was on the syllabus. From 1826 to 1838 it was at [Wyke House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyke_House) Academy in [Middlesex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex), which was advertised as a preparation for the Army, Navy, civil engineers, architects and surveyors.[^o3][^o7][^o8] Among his pupils there was [George Windsor Earl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Windsor_Earl);[^o9] [John Rouse Bloxam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rouse_Bloxam) also taught there.

Jamieson was declared bankrupt in 1838.[^o11] He then worked as an actuary.

Towards the end of his life, he suffered a stroke, then moved to [Bruges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruges) in Belgium with his wife, Frances (*née* Thurtle), known as a writer, whom he had married in 1820. She was the author of the relatively successful *Ashford Rectory; or, The Spoiled Child Reformed. Containing a short introduction to the sciences of architecture and heraldry...*.[^o12] He died in Bruges on 6 July 1850.[^o3][^o13]

## Works {#Works}

Jamieson was the author of two highly successful grammars: *A Grammar of Rhetoric and Polite Literature* (1818, at least fifty-three American editions) and *A Grammar of Logic and Intellectual Philosophy* (1819 and at least ten American editions).[^o5] The former drew on [Hugh Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Blair)'s *Lectures on Rhetoric and Belles Lettres*, and the *Philosophy of Rhetoric* of [George Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Campbell_%28minister%29), of which Jamieson published an abridgement in 1823.[^o15][^o16] Jamieson also abridged the *Elements of Criticism* by [Lord Kames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kames).[^o2]

All these Scottish authors, along with [Alexander Bain](wikipedia://en/143110), were widely used in 19th-century American colleges for rhetoric texts;[^o17] and the *Grammar* of Jamieson went through 24 editions by 1844.[^o18] It quoted freely from [Joseph Addison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Addison) and [Mark Akenside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Akenside), as well as sources such as Shakespeare and [John Milton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Milton),[^o19] and was a typical text of the [female education](wikipedia://en/9384153) of the period.[^o20] The *Grammar of Rhetoric* combined Blair, Campbell, and Kames with the *Lectures on Belles Lettres and Logic* (1806) of [William Barron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Barron_%28historian%29) and the grammar of [Lindley Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindley_Murray).[^o2]

Other works were:

*Image: Alexander Jamieson Celestial Atlas cover.jpg.* Cover of *A Celestial Atlas* by Alexander Jamieson

- *A Treatise on the Construction of Maps*[^o2][^o21]
- *A Grammar of Universal Geography* (1820?, 1823)[^o22]
- *[A Celestial Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Celestial_Atlas)* (1822)[^o2][^o23] This work was inspired by the [star atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_atlas) of [Johann Elert Bode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Elert_Bode), but restricted itself to stars that could be seen with the naked eye. It further inspired *[Urania's Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urania's_Mirror)*, a work that has been attributed to Richard Rouse Bloxam.[^o24][^o25]
- *Conversations on General History, exhibiting a Progressive View of the State of Mankind* (2nd edition 1823)[^o26]
- *A Dictionary of Mechanical Science, Arts, Manufactures, and Miscellaneous Knowledge* Volume 1 (1827),[^o27] Volume 2 (1829)[^o27]
- *Mechanics of Fluids for Practical Men* (1837)[^o2][^o28]
- *Report on the Constitution and Operations of Life Assurance Societies* (1841)[^o3]
- *A Manual of Map-making and Mechanical Geography* (1846)[^o29]
- *Elements of Algebra*[^o2]
