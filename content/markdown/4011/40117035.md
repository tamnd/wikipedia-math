# Commutator subspace

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Commutator subspace"}

In mathematics, the **commutator subspace** of a two-sided [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of [bounded linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operators) on a separable [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) is the [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) spanned by [commutators](wikipedia://en/7193#Ring_theory) of operators in the ideal with bounded operators. Modern characterisation of the commutator subspace is through the [Calkin correspondence](wikipedia://en/40116557) and it involves the invariance of the Calkin sequence space of an [operator ideal](wikipedia://en/47986929) to taking [Cesàro means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesàro_mean). This explicit spectral characterisation reduces problems and questions about commutators and [traces](wikipedia://en/40083877) on two-sided ideals to (more resolvable) problems and conditions on sequence spaces.

:::

## History {#History}

Commutators of linear operators on Hilbert spaces came to prominence in the 1930s as they featured in the [matrix mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_mechanics), or Heisenberg, formulation of quantum mechanics. Commutator subspaces, though, received sparse attention until the 1970s. American mathematician [Paul Halmos](wikipedia://en/318598) in 1954 showed that every [bounded operator](wikipedia://en/455961) on a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space is the sum of two commutators of bounded operators.[^o3] In 1971 [Carl Pearcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Pearcy) and David Topping revisited the topic and studied commutator subspaces for [Schatten ideals](wikipedia://en/10698304).[^o4] As a student American mathematician Gary Weiss began to investigate spectral conditions for commutators of [Hilbert–Schmidt operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert–Schmidt_operators).[^o5][^o6] British mathematician [Nigel Kalton](wikipedia://en/34221149), noticing the spectral condition of Weiss, characterised all trace class commutators.[^o7] Kalton's result forms the basis for the modern characterisation of the commutator subspace. In 2004 Ken Dykema, [Tadeusz Figiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Figiel), Gary Weiss and [Mariusz Wodzicki](wikipedia://en/57858922) published the spectral characterisation of normal operators in the commutator subspace for every two-sided ideal of compact operators.[^o8]

::: {#o10 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The commutator subspace of a two-sided ideal *J* of the bounded linear operators *B*(*H*) on a separable Hilbert space *H* is the [linear span](wikipedia://en/56353) of operators in *J* of the form \[*A*,*B*\] = *AB* − *BA* for all operators *A* from *J* and *B* from *B*(*H*).

The commutator subspace of *J* is a linear subspace of *J* denoted by Com(*J*) or \[*B*(*H*),*J*\].

:::

## Spectral characterisation {#Spectral_characterisation}

The [Calkin correspondence](wikipedia://en/40116557) states that a [compact operator](wikipedia://en/577441) *A* belongs to a two-sided ideal *J* [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the [singular values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_values) μ(*A*) of *A* belongs to the Calkin sequence space *j* associated to *J*. [Normal operators](wikipedia://en/190167) that belong to the commutator subspace Com(*J*) can characterised as those *A* such that μ(*A*) belongs to *j* *and* the [Cesàro mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesàro_mean) of the sequence μ(*A*) belongs to *j*.[^o8] The following theorem is a slight extension to differences of normal operators[^o12] (setting *B* = 0 in the following gives the statement of the previous sentence).

**Theorem.** Suppose *A,B* are compact normal operators that belong to a two-sided ideal *J*. Then *A* − *B* belongs to the commutator subspace Com(*J*) if and only if

$$\left\{ \frac{1}{1+n} \sum_{k=0}^n \left( \mu(k,A) - \mu(k,B) \right) \right\}_{n=0}^\infty \in j$$

where *j* is the Calkin sequence space corresponding to *J* and *μ*(*A*), *μ*(*B*) are the singular values of *A* and *B*, respectively.

Provided that the [eigenvalue sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of all operators in *J* belong to the Calkin sequence space *j* there is a spectral characterisation for arbitrary (non-normal) operators. It is not valid for every two-sided ideal but necessary and sufficient conditions are known. Nigel Kalton and American mathematician Ken Dykema introduced the condition first for countably generated ideals.[^o13][^o14] Uzbek and Australian mathematicians Fedor Sukochev and Dmitriy Zanin completed the eigenvalue characterisation.

**Theorem.** Suppose *J* is a two-sided ideal such that a bounded operator *A* belongs to *J* whenever there is a bounded operator *B* in *J* such that

::: {#o16 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\prod_{k=0}^n \mu(k,A) \leq \prod_{k=0}^n \mu(k,B), \quad n=0,1,2, \ldots . \tag{1}$$

:::

If the bounded operator *A* and *B* belong to *J* then *A* − *B* belongs to the commutator subspace Com(*J*) if and only if

$$\left\{ \frac{1}{1+n} \sum_{k=0}^n \left( \lambda(k,A) - \lambda(k,B) \right) \right\}_{n=0}^\infty \in j$$

where *j* is the Calkin sequence space corresponding to *J* and *λ*(*A*), *λ*(*B*) are the sequence of eigenvalues of the operators *A* and *B*, respectively, rearranged so that the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) of the eigenvalues is decreasing.

Most two-sided ideals satisfy the condition in the Theorem, included all Banach ideals and quasi-Banach ideals.

## Consequences of the characterisation {#Consequences_of_the_characterisation}

- Every operator in *J* is a sum of commutators if and only if the corresponding Calkin sequence space *j* is invariant under taking [Cesàro means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesàro_mean). In symbols, Com(*J*) = *J* is equivalent to C(*j*) = *j*, where C denotes the Cesàro operator on sequences.
- In any two-sided ideal the difference between a positive operator and its diagonalisation is a sum of commutators. That is, *A* − diag(*μ*(*A*)) belongs to Com(*J*) for every positive operator *A* in *J* where diag(*μ*(*A*)) is the diagonalisation of *A* in an arbitrary orthonormal basis of the separable Hilbert space *H*.
- In any two-sided ideal satisfying ((1)) the difference between an arbitrary operator and its diagonalisation is a sum of commutators. That is, *A* − diag(*λ*(*A*)) belongs to Com(*J*) for every operator *A* in *J* where diag(*λ*(*A*)) is the diagonalisation of *A* in an arbitrary orthonormal basis of the separable Hilbert space *H* and *λ*(*A*) is an eigenvalue sequence.
- Every [quasi-nilpotent operator](wikipedia://en/671772) in a two-sided ideal satisfying ((1)) is a sum of commutators.

## Application to traces {#Application_to_traces}

A trace φ on a two-sided ideal *J* of *B*(*H)* is a linear functional φ:*J* → $\mathbb{C}$ that vanishes on Com(*J*). The consequences above imply

- The two-sided ideal *J* has a non-zero trace if and only if C(*j*) ≠ *j*.
- *φ*(*A*) = *φ* $\circ$ diag(*μ*(*A*)) for every positive operator *A* in *J* where diag(*μ*(*A*)) is the diagonalisation of *A* in an arbitrary orthonormal basis of the separable Hilbert space *H*. That is, traces on *J* are in direct correspondence with [symmetric functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_functional) on *j*.
- In any two-sided ideal satisfying ((1)), *φ*(*A*) = *φ* $\circ$ diag(*λ*(*A*)) for every operator *A* in *J* where diag(*λ*(*A*)) is the diagonalisation of *A* in an arbitrary orthonormal basis of the separable Hilbert space *H* and *λ*(*A*) is an eigenvalue sequence.
- In any two-sided ideal satisfying ((1)), *φ*(*Q*) = 0 for every [quasi-nilpotent operator](wikipedia://en/671772) *Q* from *J* and every trace *φ* on *J*.

## Examples {#Examples}

Suppose *H* is a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space.

- **Compact operators.** The [compact linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_operator_on_hilbert_space) *K*(*H*) correspond to the space of converging to zero sequences, *c*<sub>0</sub>. For a converging to zero sequence the [Cesàro means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesàro_mean) converge to zero. Therefore, C(*c*<sub>0</sub>) = *c*<sub>0</sub> and Com(*K*(*H*)) = *K*(*H*).
- **Finite rank operators.** The [finite rank operators](wikipedia://en/8405295) *F*(*H*) correspond to the space of sequences with finite non-zero terms, *c*<sub>00</sub>. The condition

$$\left\{ \frac{a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n}{n} \right\}_{n=1}^\infty \in c_{00}$$

occurs if and only if

$$a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_N = 0$$

for the sequence (*a*<sub>1</sub>, *a*<sub>2</sub>, ... , *a*<sub>*N*</sub>, 0, 0 , ...) in *c*<sub>00</sub>. The kernel of the [operator trace](wikipedia://en/192266#Definition) Tr on *F*(*H*) and the commutator subspace of the finite rank operators are equal, ker Tr = Com(*F*(*H*)) ⊊ *F*(*H*).

- **Trace class operators.** The [trace class operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_class_operator) *L*<sub>1</sub> correspond to the [summable sequences](wikipedia://en/1701075). The condition

$$\left\{ \frac{a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n}{n} \right\}_{n=1}^\infty \in \ell_{1}$$

is stronger than the condition that *a*<sub>1</sub> + *a*<sub>2</sub> ... = 0. An example is the sequence with

$$a_n = \frac{1}{n \log^2(n)} , \quad n \geq 2 .$$

and

$$a_1 = - \sum_{n=2}^\infty a_n.$$

which has sum zero but does not have a summable sequence of Cesàro means. Hence Com(*L*<sub>1</sub>) ⊊ ker Tr ⊊ *L*<sub>1</sub>.

- **Weak trace class operators**. The [weak trace class operators](wikipedia://en/40116145) *L*<sub>1,∞</sub> correspond to the [weak-*l*<sub>1</sub> sequence space](wikipedia://en/45194). From the condition

$$\left\{ \frac{a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n}{n} \right\}_{n=1}^\infty \in \ell_{1,\infty}$$

or equivalently

$$\left\{ a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n \right\}_{n=1}^\infty = O(1)$$

it is immediate that Com(*L*<sub>*1*,∞</sub>)<sub>\+</sub> = (*L*<sub>1</sub>)<sub>\+</sub>. The commutator subspace of the weak trace class operators contains the trace class operators. The [harmonic sequence](wikipedia://en/142488) 1,1/2,1/3,...,1/*n*,... belongs to *l*<sub>1,∞</sub> and it has a divergent series, and therefore the Cesàro means of the harmonic sequence do not belong to *l*<sub>1,∞</sub>. In summary, *L*<sub>1</sub> ⊊ Com(*L*<sub>1,∞</sub>) ⊊ *L*<sub>1,∞</sub>.
