::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tridecahedron"}

A **tridecahedron**, or **triskaidecahedron**, is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) with thirteen [faces](wikipedia://en/149926). There are numerous topologically distinct forms of a tridecahedron, for example the [dodecagonal pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodecagonal_pyramid) and [hendecagonal prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendecagonal_prism). However, a tridecahedron cannot be a regular polyhedron, because there is no regular polygon that can form a regular tridecahedron, and there are only five known regular convex polyhedra.[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Convex {#Convex}

*Image: P8\_net.png.* A polyhedron with 13 faces fills space with its [chiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirality_%28chemistry%29) copy.

There are 96,262,938 topologically distinct *convex* tridecahedra, excluding mirror images, having at least 9 vertices.[^o5] Two polyhedra are "topologically distinct" if they have intrinsically different arrangements of faces and vertices, such that it is impossible to distort one into the other simply by changing the lengths of edges or the angles between edges or faces. There is a pseudo-[space-filling tridecahedron](wikipedia://en/58607583) that can fill all of 3-space together with its mirror-image.[^o6]

### Hendecagonal prism {#Hendecagonal_prism}

*Image: Hendecagonal prism.png.* Regular hendecagonal prism

A **hendecagonal prism** is a [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) with a [hendecagon](wikipedia://en/614840) base. It is a type of tridecahedron, which consists of 13 faces, 22 vertices, and 33 sides. A regular hendecagonal prism is a hendecagonal prism whose faces are regular hendecagons, and each of its vertices is a common vertex of 2 squares and 1 hendecagon. The [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) of a hendecagonal prism is represented by $4{.}4{.}11$; in [Schläfli notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schläfli_notation) it can be represented by {11}×{} or *t*{2, 11}; can be used in a [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) to represent it; its [Wythoff symbol](wikipedia://en/3691548) is 2 11 $|$ 2; in [Conway polyhedron notation](wikipedia://en/5021705) it can be represented by P11. If the side length of the base of a regular hendecagonal prism is $s$ and the height is $h$, then its [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) $V$ and [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) $S$ are:

$$V=\frac{11 h s^2 \cot{\frac{\pi}{11}}}{4}\approx 9.36564 h s^2$$

$$S=11s\left(h+\frac{1}{2}s\cot{\frac{\pi}{11}}\right)\approx 11s\left(h+1.70284s\right)$$

### Dodecagonal pyramid {#Dodecagonal_pyramid}

*Image: Dodecagonal pyramid.svg.* Dodecagonal pyramid

A **dodecagonal pyramid** is a pyramid with a [dodecagonal](wikipedia://en/508486) [base](wikipedia://en/9579379). It is a type of tridecahedron, which has 13 [faces](wikipedia://en/149926), 24 [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), and 13 [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), and its [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) is itself. A regular dodecagonal pyramid is a dodecagonal pyramid whose base is a regular dodecagon. If the side length of the base of a regular twelve-sided pyramid is $s$ and the height is $h$, then its volume $V$ and surface area $S$ are:[^o19]

$$V=\left(2+\sqrt{3}\right)hs^2\approx 3.73205 h s^2$$

$$S=3s\left(\sqrt{4h^2+\left(7+4\sqrt{3}\right)s^2}+\left(2+\sqrt{3}\right)s\right)\approx 3s\left(\sqrt{4h^2+13.9282s^2}+3.73205s\right)$$

### Space-filling tridecahedron {#Space-filling_tridecahedron}

*Image: Space-Filling\_Triskaidecahedron.svg.* Space-filling tridecahedron

*Image: Space-Filling\_Triskaidecahedral\_Honeycomb.svg.* Space-filling tridecahedron tiling

A **space-filling tridecahedron**[^o21][^o22] is a tridecahedron that can, along with its mirror image, completely fill three-dimensional space without leaving gaps. It has 13 faces, 30 edges, and 19 [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417). Among the thirteen faces, there are six [quadrilaterals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrilaterals), six [pentagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagons) and one [regular hexagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_hexagon).[^o23] Since it must tile with its mirror image, the space-filling tridecahedron is pseudo-space-filling rather than truly space-filling. The space-filling tridecahedron tiles with the cubic symmetry group Pa$\overline{3}$.[^o24]

**Dual polyhedron**

The polyhedron's [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) is an [enneadecahedron](wikipedia://en/45642340). It is similar to a twisted half-cube, but one of its vertices is treated as a face before twisting.
