::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pentadecahedron"}

A **pentadecahedron** (or **pentakaidecahedron**) is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) with 15 [faces](wikipedia://en/149926). No pentadecahedron is [regular](wikipedia://en/333299); hence, the name is ambiguous. There are numerous topologically distinct forms of a pentadecahedron, for example the [tetradecagonal pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetradecagonal_pyramid), and [tridecagonal prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tridecagonal_prism). In the pentadecahedron, none of the shapes are regular polyhedra. In other words, a regular pentadecahedron does not exist, and the pentadecahedron cannot fill space; a space-filling pentadecahedron does not exist.[^o2]

:::

In chemistry, some clusters of atoms are in the form of pentadecahedra.[^o3] Calculations have shown that there is a unit cell of the pentadecahedron that is stable in the crystal.[^o4]

## Convex {#Convex}

There are 23,833,988,129 topologically distinct *convex* pentadecahedra, excluding mirror images, having at least 10 vertices.[^o6] (Two polyhedra are "topologically distinct" if they have intrinsically different arrangements of faces and vertices, such that it is impossible to distort one into the other simply by changing the lengths of edges or the angles between edges or faces.)
