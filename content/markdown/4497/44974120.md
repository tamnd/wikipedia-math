# Localizing subcategory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Localizing subcategory"}

In mathematics, **Serre** and **localizing subcategories** form important classes of [subcategories](wikipedia://en/310959) of an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063). Localizing subcategories are certain Serre subcategories. They are strongly linked to the notion of a [quotient category](wikipedia://en/4004632).

:::

## Serre subcategories {#Serre_subcategories}

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063). A non-empty full [subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959) $\mathcal{C}$ is called a *Serre subcategory* (or also a *dense subcategory*), if for every short [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) $0\rightarrow A' \rightarrow A\rightarrow A''\rightarrow 0$ in $\mathcal{A}$ the object $A$ is in $\mathcal{C}$ if and only if the objects $A'$ and $A''$ belong to $\mathcal{C}$. In words: $\mathcal{C}$ is closed under subobjects, quotient objects and extensions.

Each Serre subcategory $\mathcal{C}$ of $\mathcal{A}$ is itself an abelian category, and the inclusion functor $\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}$ is [exact](wikipedia://en/453945). The importance of this notion stems from the fact that kernels of [exact functors](wikipedia://en/453945) between abelian categories are Serre subcategories, and that one can build (for locally small $\mathcal{A}$) the [quotient category](wikipedia://en/48897477) (in the sense of [Gabriel](wikipedia://en/30716698), [Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042), [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483)) $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}$, which has the same objects as $\mathcal{A}$, is abelian, and comes with an exact functor (called the quotient functor) $T\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}$ whose kernel is $\mathcal{C}$.

## Localizing subcategories {#Localizing_subcategories}

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be locally small. The Serre subcategory $\mathcal{C}$ is called *localizing* if the quotient functor $T\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}$ has a [right adjoint](wikipedia://en/53991) $S\colon\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}$. Since then $T$, as a left adjoint, preserves [colimits](wikipedia://en/39248), each localizing subcategory is closed under colimits. The functor $T$ (or sometimes $ST$) is also called the *localization functor*, and $S$ the *section functor*. The section functor is [left-exact](wikipedia://en/453945) and [fully faithful](wikipedia://en/876534).

If the abelian category $\mathcal{A}$ is moreover [cocomplete](wikipedia://en/62781) and has [injective hulls](wikipedia://en/665821) (e.g. if it is a [Grothendieck category](wikipedia://en/30051538)), then a Serre subcategory $\mathcal{C}$ is localizing if and only if $\mathcal{C}$ is closed under arbitrary coproducts (a.k.a. direct sums). Hence the notion of a localizing subcategory is equivalent to the notion of a hereditary [torsion class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_theory).

If $\mathcal{A}$ is a Grothendieck category and $\mathcal{C}$ a localizing subcategory, then $\mathcal{C}$ and the quotient category $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}$ are again Grothendieck categories.

The [Gabriel-Popescu theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel-Popescu_theorem) implies that every Grothendieck category is the quotient category of a [module category](wikipedia://en/11231812) $\operatorname{Mod}(R)$ (with $R$ a suitable [ring](wikipedia://en/48404)) modulo a localizing subcategory.
