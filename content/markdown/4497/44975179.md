# Giraud subcategory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Giraud subcategory"}

In mathematics, **Giraud subcategories** form an important class of subcategories of [Grothendieck categories](wikipedia://en/30051538). They are named after [Jean Giraud](wikipedia://en/572753).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a [Grothendieck category](wikipedia://en/30051538). A full subcategory $\mathcal{B}$ is called *reflective*, if the inclusion functor $i\colon\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}$ has a [left adjoint](wikipedia://en/53991). If this left adjoint of $i$ also preserves [kernels](wikipedia://en/142616), then $\mathcal{B}$ is called a *Giraud subcategory*.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Let $\mathcal{B}$ be Giraud in the Grothendieck category $\mathcal{A}$ and $i\colon\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}$ the inclusion functor.

- $\mathcal{B}$ is again a Grothendieck category.
- An object $X$ in $\mathcal{B}$ is [injective](wikipedia://en/473779) if and only if $i(X)$ is injective in $\mathcal{A}$.
- The left adjoint $a\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}$ of $i$ is [exact](wikipedia://en/453945).
- Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a [localizing subcategory](wikipedia://en/44974120) of $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}$ be the associated [quotient category](wikipedia://en/48897477). The section functor $S\colon\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}$ is [fully faithful](wikipedia://en/876534) and induces an [equivalence](wikipedia://en/379051) between $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}$ and the Giraud subcategory $\mathcal{B}$ given by the $\mathcal{C}$-closed objects in $\mathcal{A}$.

:::
