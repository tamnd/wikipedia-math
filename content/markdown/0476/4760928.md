# Michel Raynaud

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michel Raynaud"}

**Michel Raynaud** (; 16 June 1938 – 10 March 2018[^o3]) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997)[^o4] and a professor at [Paris-Sud 11 University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_11_University).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

He was born in [Riom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riom), France as a single son to a modest household. His father was a carpenter and his mother cleaned houses.[^o6] He attended the local primary school at [Châtel Guyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Châtel-Guyon) and Riom, and attended high school at the boarding school in [Clermont-Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand).[^o6]

Raynaud entered the [École normale supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29) where he studied from 1958 to 1962, while being first of the class in the "agrégation" exam where the new high school teachers were selected in 1961.[^o6] In 1962, he entered the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) where he studied together with his future wife [Michèle Chaumartin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michèle_Chaumartin). Both had the same doctoral advisor in [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042). Raynaud received his doctoral degree in 1967.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Raynaud was hired as professor at the [Orsay Faculty of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orsay_Faculty_of_Sciences) in Paris where he was employed until 2001, when he retired.[^o6]

Raynaud died on 10 March 2018 in [Rueil-Malmaison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rueil-Malmaison), France.[^o3]

## Research {#Research}

In 1983, Raynaud published a proof of the [Manin–Mumford conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manin–Mumford_conjecture).[^o9] In 1985, he proved [Raynaud's isogeny theorem](wikipedia://en/35194042) on [Faltings heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faltings_height) of isogenous [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves).[^o10] With [David Harbater](wikipedia://en/3521313) and following the work of [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), Raynaud proved [Abhyankar's conjecture](wikipedia://en/3832555) in 1994.[^o11][^o12][^o13]

The [Raynaud surface](wikipedia://en/22712664) was named after him by William E. Lang in 1979.[^o14][^o15]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

In 1970 Raynaud was an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice). In 1987 he received the [Prize Ampère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prize_Ampère) from the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences). In 1995 he received the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177), together with [David Harbater](wikipedia://en/3521313), for his solution of the [Abhyankar conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhyankar_conjecture).[^o6]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He practiced skiing (especially in [Val-d'Isère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-d'Isère)), tennis, and rock climbing (in [Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontainebleau)).[^o4] He was married to the mathematician [Michèle Raynaud](wikipedia://en/62009114) (Chaumartin)[^o6] who also worked with [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042).
