# Natural bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Natural bundle"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a field in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **natural bundle** is any [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) associated to the higher order [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550) $F^r(M)$, for some $r \geq 1$. In other words, its transition functions depend functionally on local changes of coordinates in the base manifold $M$ together with their partial derivatives up to order at most $r$.[^o2][^o8]

:::

The concept of a natural bundle was introduced in 1972 by [Albert Nijenhuis](wikipedia://en/11222925) as a modern reformulation of the classical concept of an arbitrary bundle of geometric objects.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathcal{M}f$ denote the [category of smooth manifolds](wikipedia://en/10059981) and [smooth maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_map) and $\mathcal{M}f_n$ the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of smooth $n$-dimensional manifolds and [local diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/1728483). Consider also the category $\mathcal{FM}$ of [fibred manifolds](wikipedia://en/3247635) and bundle morphisms, and the functor $B: \mathcal{FM} \to \mathcal{M}f$ associating to any fibred manifold its base manifold.

A natural bundle (or bundle functor) is a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $F: \mathcal{M}f_n \to \mathcal{FM}$ satisfying the following three properties:

1. $B \circ F = \mathrm{id}$, i.e. $F(M)$ is a fibred manifold over $M$, with projection denoted by $p_M: F(M) \to M$;
1. if $U \subseteq M$ is an open [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095), with inclusion map $i: U \hookrightarrow M$, then $F(U)$ coincides with $p_M^{-1}(U) \subseteq F(M)$, and $F(i): F(U) \to F(M)$ is the inclusion $p^{-1}(U) \hookrightarrow F(M)$;
1. for any smooth map $f: P \times M \to N$ such that $f (p, \cdot): M \to N$ is a local diffeomorphism for every $p \in P$, then the function $P \times F(M) \to F(N), (p,x) \mapsto F(f (p,\cdot)) (x)$ is smooth.

As a consequence of the first condition, one has a [natural transformation](wikipedia://en/45022) $p: F \to \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{M}f_n}$.

:::

## Finite order natural bundles {#Finite_order_natural_bundles}

A natural bundle $F: \mathcal{M}f_n \to \mathcal{FM}$ is called of **finite order** $r$ if, for every local diffeomorphism $f: M \to N$ and every point $x \in M$, the map $F(f)_x: F(M)_{x} \to F(N)_{f(x)}$ depends only on the [jet](wikipedia://en/1521924) $j^r_x f$. Equivalently, for every local diffeomorphisms $f,g: M \to N$ and every point $x \in M$, one has

$$j^r_x f = j^r_x g \Rightarrow F(f)|_{F(M)_x} = F(g)|_{F(M)_x}.$$

Natural bundles of order $r$ coincide with the associated fibre bundles to the $r$-th order [frame bundles](wikipedia://en/366550) $F^r(M)$.

After various intermediate cases,[^o2][^o7] it was proved by [Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_B._A._Epstein) and [Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190) that all natural bundles have finite order.[^o8]

## Natural $\Gamma$-bundles {#Natural_\Gamma-bundles}

The notion of natural $\Gamma$-bundle arises from that of natural bundle by restricting to the suitable categories of [$\Gamma$-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1255458#Pseudogroups_of_symmetries_and_geometric_structures) and of $\Gamma$-fibred manifolds, where $\Gamma$ is a [pseudogroup](wikipedia://en/1255458). The case when $\Gamma$ is the pseudogroup of all diffeomorphisms between open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ recovers the ordinary notion of natural bundle.

Under suitable assumptions, natural $\Gamma$-bundles have finite order as well.[^o10][^o11][^o12]

::: {#o14 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

An example of natural bundle (of first order) is the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) $TM$ of a manifold $M$.

Other examples include the [cotangent bundles](wikipedia://en/221600), the bundles of [metrics](wikipedia://en/195795) of [signature](wikipedia://en/463225) $(r,s)$ and the bundle of [linear connections](wikipedia://en/5760393).[^o15]

:::
