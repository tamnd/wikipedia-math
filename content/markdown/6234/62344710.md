# Monica Allanach

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Monica Allanach"}

**Monica Christine Allanach** was a British [actuary](wikipedia://en/43405). She was the first woman to be elected to the Council of the [Institute of Actuaries](wikipedia://en/852871).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Allanach's father died when she was four,[^o5] and she was brought up by her widowed mother.[^o5] She was educated at [Wimbledon High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wimbledon_High_School) from 1931–1938.[^o5][^o6] She was good at mathematics at school, and her teachers suggested that she become an actuary.[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

Allanach joined the [Prudential Assurance Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudential_Assurance_Company) in 1938 as an actuarial trainee.[^o9] She qualified as an actuary in 1951.[^o5] Women had been permitted to gain membership of the Institute of Actuaries for thirty years, but by 1951, less than one dozen women had become fully qualified.[^o5] Allanach was moved to the male salary scale at Prudential in 1960, several years before the company introduced a single, non-gendered salary scale.[^o9] She was appointed as the Deputy Actuary at the Prudential Assurance Company in 1970, thus becoming the first woman to reach management level at the company.[^o9][^o10] In 1974 she was promoted to Actuary (UK),[^o11][^o12] and held that position until her retirement in 1981.[^o5]

She became a member of the council of the [Institute of Actuaries](wikipedia://en/852871) in 1968, the first woman to be elected to the council.[^o5][^o13][^o14] She was its honorary secretary from 1972 to 1974[^o15] and its vice-president from 1976 to 1979.[^o5][^o16][^o17]

In 1977, Allanach was appointed by the [Secretary of State for Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Trade), [Edmund Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Dell), to his panel of insurance advisers.[^o5][^o18][^o19] She retired from the panel in 1980.[^o20]

In 1954 she and Pat Merriman and others began a series of informal meetings, such as tea-parties or wine and cheese parties, for the small minority of women in the profession,[^o5][^o21] from which developed the **Lady Actuaries Dining Club** (LADS).[^o5] LADS was wound up in 2011, by which time the Institute of Actuaries had its first woman president, [Jane Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Curtis).[^o22]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Allanach's writing, including a 1964 paper 'The treatment of expenses in the calculation of ordinary branch premiums', was required reading for exams at the Institute of Actuaries.[^o5] In 2015, the Institute and Faculty of Actuaries held a Monica Allanach lecture.[^o26]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Allanach was a member of the Friends of the [Girls' Public Day School Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girls'_Public_Day_School_Trust), serving as its Honorary Treasurer from 1962–1972.[^o5][^o6] She lived near Wimbledon, and had a keen interest in tennis.[^o5][^o9] She did not marry, and believed that she would not have been promoted to senior positions had she done so.[^o7] In July 1981, she was made a [Freeman of the City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeman_of_the_City_of_London).[^o5]
