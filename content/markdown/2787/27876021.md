# Mark Mahowald

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mark Mahowald"}

**Mark Edward Mahowald** (December 1, 1931 – July 20, 2013) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for work in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Mahowald was born in [Albany, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany,_Minnesota) in 1931.[^o5] He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) in 1955 under the direction of [Bernard Russell Gelbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Russell_Gelbaum) with a thesis on *Measure in Groups*. In the sixties, he became professor at [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University) and around 1963 he went to [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) in [Evanston, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evanston,_Illinois).

## Work {#Work}

Much of Mahowald's most important works concerns the [homotopy groups of spheres](wikipedia://en/1104697), especially using the [Adams spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/4720689) at the prime 2. He is known for constructing one of the first known infinite families of elements in the stable homotopy groups of spheres by showing that the classes $h_1h_j$ survive the Adams spectral sequence for $j\geq 3$. In addition, he made extensive computations of the structure of the Adams spectral sequence and the 2-primary stable homotopy groups of spheres up to dimension 64 together with Michael Barratt, Martin Tangora, and Stanley Kochman. Using these computations, he could show that a manifold of [Kervaire invariant](wikipedia://en/13816037) 1 exists in dimension 62.

In addition, he contributed to the chromatic picture of the homotopy groups of spheres: His earlier work contains much on the image of the [J-homomorphism](wikipedia://en/3061995) and recent work together with Paul Goerss, Hans-Werner Henn, Nasko Karamanov, and Charles Rezk does computations in stable homotopy localized at the [Morava K-theory](wikipedia://en/1033664) $K(2)$.

Besides the work on the homotopy groups of spheres and related spaces, he did important work on [Thom spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_spectrum). This work was used heavily in the proof of the [nilpotence theorem](wikipedia://en/31480741) by Ethan Devinatz, [Michael J. Hopkins](wikipedia://en/20652569), and Jeffrey Smith.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1998 he was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o8] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o9]
