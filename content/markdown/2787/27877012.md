# Douglas Ravenel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Douglas Ravenel"}

**Douglas Conner Ravenel** (born February 17, 1947) is an American mathematician known for work in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Ravenel received his PhD from [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) in 1972 under the direction of [Edgar H. Brown, Jr.](wikipedia://en/57293598) with a thesis on exotic characteristic classes of spherical fibrations. From 1971 to 1973 he was a [C. L. E. Moore instructor](wikipedia://en/21957255) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), and in 1974/75 he visited the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). He became an assistant professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1973 and at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle) in 1976, where he was promoted to associate professor in 1978 and professor in 1981. From 1977 to 1979 he was a [Sloan Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship). Since 1988 he has been a professor at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester). He was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki), 1978, and is an editor of [The New York Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Journal_of_Mathematics) since 1994.

In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o5] In 2022 he received the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075).[^o6]

## Work {#Work}

Ravenel's main area of work is [stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/2946655). Two of his most famous papers are *Periodic phenomena in the Adams–Novikov spectral sequence*, which he wrote together with [Haynes R. Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haynes_Miller) and [W. Stephen Wilson](wikipedia://en/47567676) ([Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785) 106 (1977), 469–516) and *Localization with respect to certain periodic homology theories* ([American Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Mathematics) 106 (1984), 351–414).

In the first of these two papers, the authors explore the stable [homotopy groups of spheres](wikipedia://en/1104697) by analyzing the $E^2$-term of the [Adams–Novikov spectral sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams–Novikov_spectral_sequence). The authors set up the so-called chromatic spectral sequence relating this $E^2$-term to the cohomology of the Morava stabilizer group, which exhibits certain periodic phenomena in the Adams–Novikov spectral sequence and can be seen as the beginning of [chromatic homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/42245935). Applying this, the authors compute the second line of the Adams–Novikov spectral sequence and establish the non-triviality of a certain family in the stable homotopy groups of spheres. In all of this, the authors use work by [Jack Morava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Morava) and themselves on [Brown–Peterson cohomology](wikipedia://en/13698492) and [Morava K-theory](wikipedia://en/1033664).

In the second paper, Ravenel expands these phenomena to a global picture of stable homotopy theory leading to the [Ravenel conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenel_conjectures). In this picture, [complex cobordism](wikipedia://en/1033666) and Morava K-theory control many qualitative phenomena, which were understood before only in special cases. Here Ravenel uses [localization](wikipedia://en/611460) in the sense of [Aldridge K. Bousfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldridge_K._Bousfield) in a crucial way. All but one of the Ravenel conjectures were proved by Ethan Devinatz, [Michael J. Hopkins](wikipedia://en/20652569) and Jeff Smith[^o8] not long after the article got published. [Frank Adams](wikipedia://en/1223959) said on that occasion:

In June 2023, Robert Burklund, Jeremy Hahn, Ishan Levy, and Tomer Schlank announced a disproof of the last remaining conjecture.[^o10]

In further work, Ravenel calculates the Morava K-theories of several spaces and proves important theorems in chromatic homotopy theory together with Hopkins. He was also one of the founders of [elliptic cohomology](wikipedia://en/10295395). In 2009, he solved together with Michael Hill and Michael Hopkins the [Kervaire invariant problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kervaire_invariant_problem) for large dimensions.[^o11]

Ravenel has written three books: the first on the computation of the stable homotopy groups of spheres; the second on the Ravenel conjectures—colloquially known among topologists as the ‘green’ and ‘orange’ books, respectively (though the former is now burgundy in its current edition); and the third, coauthored with Hill and Hopkins, on the resolution of the Kervaire invariant problem.
