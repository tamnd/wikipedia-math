# Koichiro Harada

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Koichiro Harada"}

**Koichiro Harada** is a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working on [finite group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_group_theory).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Harada first came to the United States in 1968, as a student visitor to the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). He received his PhD from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in 1972.

## Career {#Career}

[Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) was the scene from 1969 to 1973 of his collaboration with [Daniel Gorenstein](wikipedia://en/1569478) on the classification challenge in finite groups. In 1971 he first taught at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University), and in 1973 he was a visitor at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) where the [Harada-Norton group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harada-Norton_group) was discovered.[^o5]

The [Gorenstein–Harada theorem](wikipedia://en/33107560) classifies finite simple groups of sectional 2-rank at most 4.[^o6]

In 1996 Ohio State held a Special Research Quarter on the [Monster group](wikipedia://en/47422) and [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) with *Proceedings* edited by Joseph Ferrar and Harada.[^o7]

In 2000 [Mathematical Society of Japan](wikipedia://en/18376786) awarded Harada the Algebra Prize.[^o8]

After the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445) was announced, Harada proposed the following challenges to group theorists:[^o9]

1. Find natural [mathematical objects](wikipedia://en/19453961) realizing all [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600) as their [automorphism groups](wikipedia://en/56661772).
1. Prove that there are only finitely many [sporadic simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_simple_group).
1. Find the reason why the 26 sporadic simple groups exist.
1. Find a generalization of the Glauberman [Z\* theorem](wikipedia://en/9757648).
1. Find an arithmetic to give the [Schur multipliers](wikipedia://en/1121587) of finite simple groups.
1. Complete the theory of [modular representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_representation).
1. Classify the [2-groups](wikipedia://en/24664) that can be the [Sylow 2-subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_2-subgroup) of finite simple groups.
1. Look for a completely new proof of the classification.
1. Classify finite simple groups having a strongly p-embedded subgroup.
1. Solve problems around the [restricted Burnside problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restricted_Burnside_problem).

## Publications {#Publications}

- 1974: (with [Daniel Gorenstein](wikipedia://en/1569478)) *Finite simple groups whose 2-subgroups are generated by at least 4 elements*, [Memoirs of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30441553).
- 1975: *On the simple group F of order 2<sup>14</sup> · 3<sup>6</sup> · 5<sup>6</sup> · 7 · 11 · 19*. Proc. Group Theory Conference in Park City, Utah, pp. 119–276.
- 1989: *Some elliptic curves arising from the Leech lattice*, [Journal of Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Algebra) 125: 289–310.
- 1999: *Monster*. Iwanami Publishing, (in Japanese; book on the [Monster group](wikipedia://en/47422)).
- 2010: *"Moonshine" of Finite Groups*, [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470)
