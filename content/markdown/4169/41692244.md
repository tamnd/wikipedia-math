# John Seton (priest)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Seton"}

**John Seton** D.D. (c. 1498 - July 20, 1567) was an English Roman Catholic priest, known as the author of a standard logic text.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in or about 1498, Seton was educated at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), where he graduated B.A. in 1528. Soon afterwards he was elected a fellow of St. John's, and graduated M.A. in 1532, B.D. in 1541.[^o3]

Seton taught philosophy in his college, and gained a reputation as a tutor.[^o3] He belonged to the "conservative humanist" group associated to St John's, a college sharply divided by the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), with others of similar views including [John Christopherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Christopherson) and [Thomas Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Watson_%28bishop_of_Lincoln%29).[^o5] He was also a good friend, however, of [Roger Ascham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ascham) who was one of the reformers there.[^o6]

After being ordained priest, Seton became one of Bishop [John Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fisher)'s chaplains, and attended him the [Tower of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_London). In 1542 he was one of the fellows of St John's who signed an appeal to the Visitor against [John Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Taylor_%28bishop_of_Lincoln%29), the Master. In 1544 he proceeded D.D., and about that time was appointed one of the chaplains to [Stephen Gardiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Gardiner), at that time [bishop of Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester) and chancellor of the university, who collated him to the rectory of [Hinton, Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinton,_Hampshire); at Gardiner's trial in 1551 Seton gave testimony in his favour.[^o3]

Seton was present at the disputation with [Peter Martyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Martyr_Vermigli) held at Oxford in 1550. In 1553 he was installed canon of [Winchester Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_Cathedral), and in the following year prebendary of Ulskelf in the [York Minster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Minster). He was one of the doctors of divinity who, by the direction of Bishop Gardiner, went to Oxford for the disputation with [Thomas Cranmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Cranmer), [Nicholas Ridley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Ridley_%28martyr%29), and [Hugh Latimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Latimer), concerning matters of religion, and on this occasion he was incorporated D.D. there on 14 April 1554.[^o3]

In 1555 Seton visited the Protestant [John Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bradford_%28martyr%29) in prison, for the purpose of inducing him to recant; in 1558 he attended Thomas Benbridge on the same mission. Seton's name was on a list of Catholic clergy drawn up in 1561, described as learned but settled in [papistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papistry), and as having been ordered to remain within the [City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London), or with twenty miles of it.[^o3]

After suffering imprisonment, Seton left England and went to Rome, where he died on 20 July 1567.[^o3] He is buried on the wall of the college chapel of the Venerable [English College, Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_College,_Rome), close to the altar together with his long time friend Nicholas Morton (died after 1586).[^o7]

## Works {#Works}

Seton wrote:[^o3]

- *Panegyrici in victoriam illustrissimæ D. Mariæ Angliæ, Franciæ, & Hiberniæ Reginæ, &c. Item in Coronationem ejusdem Sereniss. Reginæ Congratulatio. Ad hæc de Sacrosancta Eucharistia Carmen D. Joanne Setono authore*, London, 1553; dedicated to Queen Mary.
- Latin verses before Dr. [Alban Langdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alban_Langdale)'s *Catholica Confutatio Nic. Ridlei*, Paris, 1556.
- *Dialectica brevem in contextum constricta,...*, London, 1563. Seton's lectures before his death and before Carter's notes were added.
- *Dialectica; annotationibus Petri Carteri, ut clarissimis, ita brevissimis, explicata. Huic accessit, ob artium ingenuarum inter se cognationem, Gulielmi Buclæi arithmetica*, London, 1572, reissued 1574, 1577, 1584, 1599; Cambridge, 1631; dedicated to Bishop Gardiner, with notes by [Peter Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Carter_%28logician%29). This work was circulated in manuscript among students before it appeared in print, and for nearly a century was recognised as a standard treatise on logic.[^o3] It was heavily influenced by the *De inventione dialecticae* of [Rudolphus Agricola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolphus_Agricola).[^o9] In contrast to typical rhetoric treatises that emphasised *[inventio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventio)*, it placed weight on the organisation of arguments.[^o10] Roger Ascham is considered to have been influenced by Seton in exactly this area.[^o11]
