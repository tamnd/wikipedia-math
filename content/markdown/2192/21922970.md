# Teichmüller–Tukey lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Teichmüller–Tukey lemma"}

In mathematics, the **Teichmüller–Tukey lemma** (sometimes named just **Tukey's lemma**), named after [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974) and [Oswald Teichmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Teichmüller), is a [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) that states that every nonempty collection of [finite character](wikipedia://en/2549522) has a [maximal element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_element) with respect to [inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_%28set_theory%29). Over [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214), the Teichmüller–Tukey lemma is equivalent to the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840), and therefore to the [well-ordering theorem](wikipedia://en/33458), [Zorn's lemma](wikipedia://en/51442), and the [Hausdorff maximal principle](wikipedia://en/13665).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A family of sets $\mathcal{F}$ is of **finite character** provided it has the following properties:

1. For each $A\in \mathcal{F}$, every [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of $A$ belongs to $\mathcal{F}$.
1. If every finite subset of a given set $A$ belongs to $\mathcal{F}$, then $A$ belongs to $\mathcal{F}$.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Statement of the lemma"}

## Statement of the lemma {#Statement_of_the_lemma}

Let $Z$ be a set and let $\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(Z)$. If $\mathcal{F}$ is of finite character and $X\in\mathcal{F}$, then there is a maximal $Y\in\mathcal{F}$ (according to the inclusion relation) such that $X\subseteq Y$.[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), the lemma may be used to show the existence of a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420). Let *V* be a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). Consider the collection $\mathcal{F}$ of [linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent) sets of vectors. This is a collection of [finite character](wikipedia://en/2549522). Thus, a maximal set exists, which must then [span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Span_%28linear_algebra%29) *V* and be a basis for *V*.

:::
