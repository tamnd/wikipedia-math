# Kikuchi Dairoku

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kikuchi Dairoku"}

Kikuchi

[Baron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron) **Kikuchi Dairoku** was a Japanese mathematician, educator, and education administrator during the [Meiji era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era). After earning degrees in mathematics and physics from [St John's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), he became one of the first Japanese professors of mathematics at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo). Later in his life, he served as president of the University of Tokyo, [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University), [Gakushuin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakushūin), and the [Riken Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riken).

:::

## Early life and family {#Early_life_and_family}

Kikuchi was born in [Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo) (present-day [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo)), as the second son of [Mitsukuri Shūhei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsukuri_Shūhei), a professor at [Bansho Shirabesho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bansho_Shirabesho), himself the adopted son of Mitsukuri Gempo, a [Shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate) professor. The [Mitsukuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsukuri) family had distinguished themselves as scholars, and were at the centre of Japan's educational system in the Meiji era. His grandfather had been a student of [Dutch studies ("rangaku")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangaku).

Kikuchi Dairoku changed his surname from Mitsukuri to Kikuchi upon succeeding as the heir to his father's original family; the requisite legal procedures were completed in 1877.[^o5]

## Education {#Education}

After attending the *[Bansho Shirabesho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bansho_Shirabesho)*, the Shogunal institute for western studies, he was sent to Great Britain, in 1866, at age 11, the youngest of a group of Japanese sent by the [Tokugawa shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate) to the [University College School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_School), on the advice of the then British foreign minister [Edward Stanley, 15th Earl of Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Stanley,_15th_Earl_of_Derby).

Kikuchi returned to England in 1870 and was the first Japanese student to graduate from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) ([St. John's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John's_College,_Cambridge)) and the only one to graduate from the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) in the 19th century.[^o7] His specialisation was in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and mathematics. In 1884 he attended the [International Meridian Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Meridian_Conference) in Washington, D.C., and the [master class of Kelvin in Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_class#Kelvin_in_Baltimore).

## Career {#Career}

After returning to Japan, Kikuchi later became president of [Tokyo Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo), [Minister of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_%28Japan%29) (1901–1903) and president of [Kyoto Imperial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University). In 1909 he lectured in London on Japanese Education[^o9] and 1910 at New York on New Japan: Its Intellectual and Moral Development.[^o10]

His textbook on [elementary geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_geometry) was the most widely used geometry [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) in Japan until the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).

Kikuchi was made a [baron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron) under the *[kazoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazoku)* peerage system in 1902 and was the eighth president of the [Gakushūin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakushūin) Peers' School. In 1917 he became the first president of [Riken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riken), but died that same year.

## Family and issue {#Family_and_issue}

Kikuchi's children became well-known scientists, and his grandson [Minobe Ryōkichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryokichi_Minobe) became governor of Tokyo.
