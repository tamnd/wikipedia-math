# Centered nonagonal number

*Image: Centered nonagonal number.svg.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Centered nonagonal number"}

A **centered nonagonal number,** (or **centered enneagonal number**), is a [centered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centered_number) [figurate number](wikipedia://en/325260) that represents a [nonagon](wikipedia://en/414824) with a dot in the center and all other dots surrounding the center dot in successive nonagonal layers. The centered [nonagonal number](wikipedia://en/910285) for *n* layers is given by the formula

:::

$$Nc(n) = \frac{(3n-2)(3n-1)}{2}.$$

*Image: visual\_proof\_centered\_nonagonal\_numbers\_are\_triangular.svg.* [Proof without words](wikipedia://en/18045517) that nonagonal numbers are triangular

Multiplying the (*n* − 1)th [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915) by 9 and then adding 1 yields the *n*th centered nonagonal number, but centered nonagonal numbers have an even simpler relation to triangular numbers: every third triangular number (the 1st, 4th, 7th, etc.) is also a centered nonagonal number.[^o2]

Thus, the first few centered nonagonal numbers are[^o2]

[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29), [10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_%28number%29), [28](wikipedia://en/362213), [55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/55_%28number%29), [91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/91_%28number%29), [136](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/136_%28number%29), [190](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/190_%28number%29), [253](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/253_%28number%29), [325](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/325_%28number%29), 406, [496](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/496_%28number%29), 595, 703, 820, 946.

The list above includes the [perfect numbers](wikipedia://en/23670) 28 and 496. All [even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_number) perfect numbers are triangular numbers whose index is an odd [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908).[^o3] Since every Mersenne prime greater than 3 is congruent to 1 [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) 3, it follows that every even perfect number greater than 6 is a centered nonagonal number.

In 1850, [Sir Frederick Pollock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Frederick_Pollock,_1st_Baronet) conjectured that every natural number is the sum of at most eleven centered nonagonal numbers.[^o4] Pollock's conjecture was confirmed as true in 2023.[^o5]

## Congruence Relations {#Congruence_Relations}

- All centered nonagonal numbers are congruent to 1 mod 3.
  - Therefore the sum of any 3 centered nonagonal numbers and the difference of any two centered nonagonal numbers are divisible by 3.
