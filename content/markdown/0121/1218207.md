# Jarl Waldemar Lindeberg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jarl Waldemar Lindeberg"}

**Jarl Waldemar Lindeberg** (4 August 1876, [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki)[^o2] – 24 December 1932, Helsinki) was a [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland) mathematician known for work on the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Lindeberg was son of a teacher at the [Helsinki Polytechnical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_University_of_Technology) and at an early age showed mathematical talent and interest. The family was well off and later Jarl Waldemar would prefer to be a reader than a full professor. Lindeberg's career centred on the [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki). His early interests were in [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) but from 1920 he worked in [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685). In 1920 he published his first paper on the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406). His result was similar to that obtained earlier by [Lyapunov](wikipedia://en/65425) whose work he did not then know. However, their approaches were quite different; Lindeberg's was based on a [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) argument while Lyapunov used the [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401). Two years later Lindeberg used his method to obtain a stronger result: the so-called *[Lindeberg condition](wikipedia://en/39406#Lindeberg_condition)*. His work on probability led to him becoming involved in applied fields. He developed what we know as [Kendall's τ](wikipedia://en/7287830) and he found the first two moments of its sampling distribution. Lindeberg used [line transect methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_transect_method) in [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry), and when in 1926 determining the necessary number of transects to obtain a sufficiently precise [confidence interval](wikipedia://en/280911), he seems to have rediscovered [Student's t-distribution](wikipedia://en/105375).

The Swedish mathematician [Harald Cramér](wikipedia://en/223129) met Lindeberg in 1922. He later recalled this story about Lindeberg and the beautiful farm he owned. "When he was reproached for not being sufficiently active in his scientific work, he said 'Well, I am really a farmer.' And if somebody happened to say that his farm was not properly cultivated, his answer was 'Of course my real job is to be a professor.' I was very fond of him and saw him often during the following years."

Lindeberg's work was unknown to [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208), who proved the central limit theorem in his dissertation in 1935.[^o4]
