# Megarian school

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Megarian school"}

*Image: Map of Attika.png.* [Megara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megara) in [Megaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megaris), lying equidistant from [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens), [Thebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thebes,_Greece), and [Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Corinth)

The **Megarian school** of philosophy, which flourished in the 4th century BC, was founded by [Euclides of Megara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclides_of_Megara), one of the pupils of [Socrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrates). Its ethical teachings were derived from Socrates, recognizing a single [good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Form_of_the_Good), which was apparently combined with the [Eleatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleatic) doctrine of [Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monism). Some of Euclides' successors developed [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) to such an extent that they became a separate school, known as the **Dialectical school**. Their work on [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365), [logical conditionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_conditional), and [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154) played an important role in the development of logic in antiquity.

:::

## History {#History}

The Megarian school of philosophy was founded by [Euclides of Megara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclides_of_Megara), who had been one of the pupils of [Socrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrates) in the late 5th century BC.[^o3] His successors, as head of the school in [Megara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megara), were said to have been [Ichthyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichthyas) (mid 4th century BC), and [Stilpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stilpo) (late 4th century BC).[^o4] It is unlikely, however, that the Megarian school was a genuine institution, but more of a casual description of its geographic origin, like the [Austrian school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_school) in economics. Nor did it have a unified philosophical position.[^o5] It was said that the philosophers of the school were first called *Megarians* and that later they were called *Eristics*, and then *Dialecticians*,[^o6] but it is probable that these names designated splinter groups distinct from the Megarian school.[^o7] Besides Ichthyas, Euclides' most important pupils were [Eubulides of Miletus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eubulides_of_Miletus)[^o8] and [Clinomachus of Thurii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinomachus).[^o9] It seems to have been under Clinomachus that a separate Dialectical school was founded,[^o10] which placed great emphasis on [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [dialectic](wikipedia://en/52813), and Clinomachus was said to have been "the first to write about [propositions](wikipedia://en/81094) and [predicates](wikipedia://en/285109)."[^o9] However, Euclides himself taught logic,[^o11] and his pupil, Eubulides, who was famous for employing celebrated [paradoxes](wikipedia://en/24390),[^o8] was the teacher of several later dialecticians.

Via Stilpo, the Megarian school is said to have influenced the [Eretrian school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eretrian_school) under [Menedemus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menedemus) and [Asclepiades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asclepiades_of_Phlius); [Pyrrho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrho), the founder of [Pyrrhonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrhonism); and [Zeno of Citium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeno_of_Citium), the founder of [Stoicism](wikipedia://en/19224834). Zeno was said to have studied under Stilpo and [Diodorus Cronus](wikipedia://en/2437676),[^o12] and to have disputed with [Philo the Dialectician](wikipedia://en/14125745). It was perhaps the Dialecticians, Diodorus and Philo, who were the biggest influence on the development of Stoic logic, and that Zeno studied under Stilpo to learn his moral teachings, although Stilpo, too, is said to have excelled "in the invention of arguments and in sophistry."[^o4]

## Philosophy {#Philosophy}

[Euclides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid_of_Megara) had been a pupil of Socrates, but ancient historians regarded him as a successor to the [Eleatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleatics), hence his philosophy was seen as a fusion of Eleatic and Socratic thought. Thus, the Eleatic idea of "The One" was identified with the Socratic "[Form of the Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Form_of_the_Good),"[^o14] and the opposite of Good was regarded by Euclides as non-existent.[^o6] But the emphasis of his thought is not on *being* but on the *good*, and idea that what is opposite to the good does not exist arises from the understanding of the good's unity.[^o15] This theme is typically Socratic; what matters is the moral good and the will of the good person to strive towards it. Stilpo is said to have continued the Eleatic tendency, by asserting a strict [monism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monism) and denying all change and motion,[^o16] and he also rejected [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954)'s [Theory of Forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_Forms).[^o17] In [ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics), Stilpo taught freedom, self-control, and self-sufficiency, approaching the teachings of the [Cynics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynicism_%28philosophy%29), another Socratic school.[^o18]

Besides studying logical puzzles and paradoxes, the Dialecticians made two important logical innovations, by re-examining [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365), and by starting an important debate on the nature of [conditional statements](wikipedia://en/658808).[^o19] This was the work of [Diodorus Cronus](wikipedia://en/2437676) and [Philo the Dialectician](wikipedia://en/14125745), the only two members of the Dialectical school we have detailed information about. Through their development of [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154), the Dialectical school played an important role in the development of logic, which was an important precursor of [Stoic logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoic_logic).
