# Hodograph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hodograph"}

A **hodograph** is a [diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagram) that gives a [vectorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometric%29) visual representation of the movement of a [body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_object) or a [fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid). It is the [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of one end of a variable vector, with the other end fixed.[^o2] The position of any plotted data on such a diagram is proportional to the [velocity](wikipedia://en/20491903) of the moving particle.[^o3] It is also called a **velocity diagram**. It appears to have been used by [James Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bradley), but its practical development is mainly from Sir [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817), who published an account of it in the *[Proceedings of the Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_Royal_Irish_Academy)* in 1846.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

It is used in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), [solid](wikipedia://en/458866) and [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988) to plot deformation of material, motion of planets or any other data that involves the velocities of different parts of a body.

### Meteorology {#Meteorology}

*Image: Hodographe NOAA.PNG.* Hodograph plot of upper air winds from [radiosonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiosonde)

In [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904), hodographs are used to plot [winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind) from soundings of the [Earth's atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_atmosphere). It is a polar diagram where wind direction is indicated by the angle from the center axis and its strength by the distance from the center. In the figure, at the bottom one finds values of wind at 4 heights above ground. They are plotted by the [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometric%29) $\vec V_0$ to $\vec V_4$. One has to notice that direction are plotted as mentioned in the upper right corner.

With the hodograph and [thermodynamic diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic_diagram) like the [tephigram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tephigram), meteorologists can calculate:

- **Wind shear**: The lines uniting the extremities of successive vectors represent the variation in direction and value of the wind in a layer of the atmosphere. [Wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear) is important information in the development of [thunderstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderstorm) and future evolution of wind at these levels.
- **Turbulence**: wind shear indicates possible [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664) that would cause a [hazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard) to [aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation).
- **Temperature advection**: change of [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050) in a layer of air can be calculated by the direction of the wind at that level and the direction of the wind shear with the next level. In the northern hemisphere, warm air is to the right of the wind shear vector between levels in the atmosphere. The opposite is true in the southern hemisphere (see [thermal wind](wikipedia://en/1593644)). Using the example hodograph, the wind $\vec V_3$ from the southwest meets the right side of the wind shear vector which means warm [advection](wikipedia://en/166377) is occurring and thus the air is warming at that level.

## Distributed hodograph {#Distributed_hodograph}

It is a method of presenting the velocity field of a point in planar motion. The velocity vector, drawn at scale, is shown perpendicular rather than tangent to the point path, usually oriented away from the center of curvature of the path. [^o8]

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Hodograph method"}

## Hodograph transformation {#Hodograph_transformation}

Hodograph transformation is a technique used to transform nonlinear [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) into linear version. It consists of interchanging the dependent and independent variables in the equation to achieve linearity.[^o11]
