# Zhihong Xia

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zhihong Xia"}

[Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xia_%28surname%29) **Zhihong "Jeff" Xia** (; born 20 September 1962, in [Dongtai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongtai), [Jiangsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangsu), China) is a Chinese-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

*Image: Xia's 5-body configuration.png.* Xia's construction proving the Painlevé conjecture

Xia received, in 1982, from [Nanjing University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_University) a bachelor's degree in astronomy and in 1988, a PhD in mathematics from [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) with thesis advisor [Donald G. Saari](wikipedia://en/2409308), for his thesis, *The Existence of the Non-Collision Singularities*. From 1988 to 1990, Xia was an assistant professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and from 1990 to 1994, an associate professor at [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology) (and Institute Fellow). In 1994, he became a full professor at Northwestern University and since 2000, he has been the *Arthur and Gladys Pancoe Professor of Mathematics*.[^o4]

His research deals with celestial mechanics, dynamical systems, Hamiltonian dynamics, and ergodic theory. In his dissertation, he solved the [Painlevé conjecture](wikipedia://en/43825561), a long-standing problem posed in 1895 by [Paul Painlevé](wikipedia://en/402504). The problem concerns the existence of singularities of non-collision character in the $N$-body problem in three-dimensional space; Xia proved the existence for $N \geq 5$. For the existence proof, he constructed an example of five masses, of which four are separated into two pairs which revolve around each other in eccentric elliptical orbits about the [z-axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z-axis) of symmetry, and a fifth mass moves along the z-axis. For selected initial conditions, the fifth mass can be accelerated to an infinite velocity in a finite time interval (without any collision between the bodies involved in the example).[^o5] The case $N = 4$ was open until 2014,[^o6] when it was solved by Jinxin Xue.[^o7][^o8] For $N = 3$, Painlevé had proven that the singularities (points of the orbit in which accelerations become infinite in a finite time interval) must be of the collision type. However, Painlevé's proof did not extend to the case $N > 3$.

In 1993, Xia was the inaugural winner of the [Blumenthal Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blumenthal_Award) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). From 1989 to 1991, he was a [Sloan Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellow). From 1993 to 1998, he received the National Young Investigator Award from the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation). In 1995, he received the [Monroe H. Martin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_Martin_Prize) in Applied Mathematics from the University of Maryland.[^o9] In 1998, he was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o10]
