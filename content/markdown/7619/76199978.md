# John H. Wolfe

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John H. Wolfe"}

**John H. Wolfe** is the inventor of [model-based clustering](wikipedia://en/76187094) for continuous data.[^o7][^o8][^o6] Wolfe graduated with a B.A. in mathematics from [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech) and then went to graduate school in [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) to work with Robert Tryon.

:::

Around 1959, [Paul Lazarsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Lazarsfeld) visited Berkeley and gave a lecture on his [latent class analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_class_analysis), which fascinated Wolfe, and led him to start thinking about how one could do the same thing for continuous data. Wolfe's 1963 M.A. thesis[^o2] is a first, but ultimately failed attempt to do this. After graduating from Berkeley, Wolfe took a job with the [US Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Navy) in [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego) first as a computer programmer and then as an operations research analyst.

He continued his research on clustering and in 1965 he published the paper that invented model-based clustering.[^o3][^o6] He used the mixture of multivariate normal distributions model, estimated it by [maximum likelihood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood) using a [Newton-Raphson algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton-Raphson_algorithm) and gave the expression for the [posterior probabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posterior_probabilities) of membership in each cluster. This paper also contains the first publicly available software for estimating the model, called NORMIX. This was extended and published in a journal by Wolfe (1970).[^o4]

After 1970, Wolfe worked on other topics, but [model-based clustering](wikipedia://en/76187094) grew rapidly. Articles on [model-based clustering](wikipedia://en/76187094) have garnered over 20,000 citations in scientific publications,[^o9] while two of the most widely used software packages to implement it (the mclust and flexmix [R packages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_packages)) have been downloaded over 14 million times.[^o10]
