# Hidden line

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hidden line"}

In mathematics, a **hidden line** is a [geometric edge](wikipedia://en/12158034) line[^o2] that is not visible from an [observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation)'s view of a solid shape or object.

:::

A common practice is to draw the visible edges as solid lines and the hidden lines as dotted lines, dashed lines,[^o3] or thinner lines than the visible lines.

*Image: Hidden Line Cubes.jpg.* [Cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubes) with hidden lines: dotted(1), dashed(2), thin lines(3), a cube with hidden lines removed(4), and a cube with dotted lines(5) to give three dimensional reference.

*Image: Rhombic\_dodecahedron\_(green).png.* [Rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), a solid with hidden lines that thinner and a lighter shade of grey.

Hidden lines add geometric information about the unseen sides of an object. They are used to help a person visualize drawings of [geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) objects in [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853).[^o4]

*Image: Wire frame.svg.* [Wire-frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire-frame_model) cube, icosahedron, and sphere

A three-dimensional object drawn with solid visible and hidden lines is a [wire-frame model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire-frame_model) of the object.

## Geometry objects without hidden lines {#Geometry_objects_without_hidden_lines}

When a student is first introduced to a geometric [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), it's easier to start with something they are familiar such as a common box which is a cube without hidden lines. Drawing an object without hidden lines, matches an observer's view of the object. Omitting hidden lines makes an object easier to draw.[^o6]

To draw higher dimensional cubes, [hypercubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercubes), without hidden lines, make the [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) opaque. Then, the hidden lines are no longer visible, they are removed from the observer's view. This works with n-[cubes](wikipedia://en/6285) in [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853).

## Hidden lines in technical art {#Hidden_lines_in_technical_art}

Hidden lines represent edges of a physical object that are not visible from a specific viewpoint. Visible lines are viewable edges from a specified viewpoint. In a technical drawing hidden lines are either included or not included, depending on the viewer’s requirement.[^o8]

"Hidden lines are used in engineering drawings to represent features that cannot be seen in a particular view but are necessary to fully define the part or assembly. Hidden lines are represented by a series of short dashes, evenly spaced, with the first dash in contact with the visible line from which it starts and the last dash in contact with the visible line at which it ends."[^o9]

In a computer drawing application, the hidden line option may be referred to as a [wire frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire-frame_model) with hidden edges.[^o10] The wireframe hidden edges would be dashed lines.

*Image: Obj lineremoval.png.* A wire-frame image with [hidden-line removal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden-line_removal), applied.

[Removing hidden lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden-line_removal) is important in computer design and graphics applications. There are [algorithmic solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden-line_removal#Algorithms) to remove hidden lines or partially hidden lines during an object's rendering. "The general problem of efficiently and meaningfully displaying three-dimensional objects in two dimensions is central to computer graphics."[^o11][^o12]

*Image: Point of View Mapping of a 3D Surface to 2D.jpg.* Viewer's point of view: V, viewing the 2 dimensional plane: P, which is a mapping of the 3 dimensional surface or object: S. Plane: P, is the viewer's computer screen or engineering drawing.
