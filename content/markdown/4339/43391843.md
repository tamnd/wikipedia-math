# Continuous-time random walk

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Continuous-time random walk"}

In mathematics, a **continuous-time random walk** (**CTRW**) is a generalization of a [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) where the wandering particle waits for a random time between jumps. It is a [stochastic](wikipedia://en/292222) [jump process](wikipedia://en/2180750) with arbitrary distributions of jump lengths and waiting times.[^o2][^o3][^o4] More generally it can be seen to be a special case of a [Markov renewal process](wikipedia://en/5446890).

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

CTRW was introduced by [Montroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Waters_Montroll) and [Weiss](wikipedia://en/43489615)[^o6] as a generalization of physical diffusion processes to effectively describe [anomalous diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomalous_diffusion), i.e., the super- and sub-diffusive cases. An equivalent formulation of the CTRW is given by generalized [master equations](wikipedia://en/840106).[^o7] A connection between CTRWs and diffusion equations with [fractional time derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_derivative) has been established.[^o8] Similarly, [time-space fractional diffusion equations](wikipedia://en/229939#Time-space_fractional_diffusion_equation_models) can be considered as CTRWs with continuously distributed jumps or continuum approximations of CTRWs on lattices.[^o9]

::: {#o11 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

A simple formulation of a CTRW is to consider the stochastic process $X(t)$ defined by

$$X(t) = X_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{N(t)} \Delta X_i,$$

whose increments $\Delta X_i$ are [iid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iid) random variables taking values in a domain $\Omega$ and $N(t)$ is the number of jumps in the interval $(0,t)$. The probability for the process taking the value $X$ at time $t$ is then given by

$$P(X,t) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty P(n,t) P_n(X).$$

Here $P_n(X)$ is the probability for the process taking the value $X$ after $n$ jumps, and $P(n,t)$ is the probability of having $n$ jumps after time $t$.

:::

::: {#o13 .statement cue=named-heading name="Montroll–Weiss formula"}

## Montroll–Weiss formula {#Montroll–Weiss_formula}

We denote by $\tau$ the waiting time in between two jumps of $N(t)$ and by $\psi(\tau)$ its distribution. The [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) of $\psi(\tau)$ is defined by

$$\tilde{\psi}(s)=\int_0^{\infty} d\tau \, e^{-\tau s} \psi(\tau).$$

Similarly, the [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401) of the jump distribution $f(\Delta X)$ is given by its [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247):

$$\hat{f}(k)=\int_\Omega d(\Delta X) \, e^{i k\Delta X} f(\Delta X).$$

One can show that the Laplace–Fourier transform of the probability $P(X,t)$ is given by

$$\hat{\tilde{P}}(k,s) = \frac{1-\tilde{\psi}(s)}{s} \frac{1}{1-\tilde{\psi}(s)\hat{f}(k)}.$$

The above is called the [Montroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Waters_Montroll)–[Weiss](wikipedia://en/43489615) formula.

:::

::: {#o15 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [homogeneous Poisson point process](wikipedia://en/41597450) is a continuous time random walk with exponential holding times and with each increment deterministically equal to 1.
