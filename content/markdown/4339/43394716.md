# Robert Baldwin Hayward

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Baldwin Hayward"}

**Robert Baldwin Hayward** (7 March 1829 – 2 February 1903) was an English educator and mathematician.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born on 7 March 1829, at [Bocking, Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocking,_Essex), he was son of Robert Hayward by his wife Ann Baldwin; his father, from an old [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker) family, withdrew from the Quaker community on his marriage. Educated at [University College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_London), he entered [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), in 1846, graduating as fourth [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28tripos%29) in 1850. He was fellow from 30 March 1852 till 27 March 1860, and from 1852 till 1855 assistant tutor.[^o3]

From 1855 Baldwin was mathematical tutor and reader in natural philosophy at [Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University), leaving in 1859 to become a mathematical master at [Harrow School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow_School). Hayward remained at Harrow till 1893, a period of 35 years. He reformed mathematics teaching there. He was president (1878–89) of the Association for the Improvement of Geometrical Teaching (afterwards the [Mathematical Association](wikipedia://en/2742403)).[^o3]

Hayward was a mountain climber and an original member of the [Alpine Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_Club) from its foundation in 1858, withdrawing in 1865. He died at [Shanklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanklin), [Isle of Wight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Wight), on 2 February 1903.[^o3]

He was elected [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) on 1 June 1876, in recognition of his work on the method of [moving axes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moving_axes).[^o3][^o6]

### Unified angles {#Unified_angles}

Hayward called a hyperbola an *excircle* in his *Algebra of Coplanar Vectors and Trigonomety*. Chapter 6 considered "Excircular or hyperbolic trigonometry" where [hyperbolic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_function) are described. He was taken by the analogy of [circular sectors](wikipedia://en/1967249) and [hyperbolic sectors](wikipedia://en/1139926). Instead of cos u, sin u, etc. as functions of the angle IOp we might regard them as functions of the corresponding *sector* of the unit-circle, and then regarding cosh v, sinh v, etc. in like manner as functions of the excircular or hyperbolic sector IOP, the analogy between circular and excircular functions is complete. Introduction of trigonometric ratios by reference to hyperbolc sector area was also advocated by R. Levett and Charles Davison.[^o8] Such an approach to trigonometry was also taken by [Alexander Macfarlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Macfarlane) in his essay "Definitions of the Trigonometric Functions".[^o9]

## Family {#Family}

Hayward married in 1860 Marianne, daughter of Henry Rowe, of Cambridge; his wife's sister married [Henry William Watson](wikipedia://en/623067). He had issue two sons and four daughters, including Sir [Maurice Henry Weston Hayward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Hayward), K.C.S.I., colonial administrator in India.[^o3]
