# Benjamin Weiss

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Benjamin Weiss"}

**Benjamin Weiss** (בנימין ווייס; born 1941) is an American-Israeli mathematician known for his contributions to [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), [topological dynamics](wikipedia://en/16379713), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), and [descriptive set theory](wikipedia://en/396619). In 2026, he received the [Israel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Prize) for mathematics and computer science research.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Benjamin ("Benjy") Weiss was born in New York City. In 1962 he received B.A. from [Yeshiva University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_University) and M.A. from the Graduate School of Science, [Yeshiva University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_University). In 1965, he received his Ph.D. from [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) under the supervision of [William Feller](wikipedia://en/206644).

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Between 1965 and 1967, Weiss worked at the [IBM Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Research). In 1967, he joined the faculty of the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem); and since 1990 occupied the Miriam and Julius Vinik Chair in Mathematics (Emeritus since 2009). Weiss held visiting positions at [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford), [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute), and [IBM Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Research) Center.

Weiss published over 180 papers in [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), [topological dynamics](wikipedia://en/16379713), [orbit equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_equivalence), [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), [descriptive set theory](wikipedia://en/396619); with notable contributions including introduction of [Markov partitions](wikipedia://en/11491735) (with Roy Adler), development of ergodic theory of [amenable groups](wikipedia://en/657430) (with Don Ornstein), [mean dimension](wikipedia://en/47803999) (with Elon Lindenstrauss), introduction of [sofic subshifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofic_subshift) and [sofic groups](wikipedia://en/37690209). The [road coloring conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_coloring_problem) was also posed by Weiss with [Roy Adler](wikipedia://en/41177893).

One of Weiss's students is [Elon Lindenstrauss](wikipedia://en/28371257), a 2010 recipient of the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859).

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

Weiss gave an invited address at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) 1974, was twice the main speaker at a [Conference Board of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_Board_of_Mathematical_Sciences) (1979 and 1995), gave the M.B.Porter Distinguished Lecture Series at [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) (1998). In 2000 Weiss was elected as a Foreign Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences). In 2006 he was awarded the [Rothschild Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_Prize) in Mathematics. In 2012 Weiss was elected a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8] In 2026 Weiss received the [Israel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Prize) in Mathematics, Computer Science and Computer Engineering.[^o9]
