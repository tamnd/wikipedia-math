# Thomas M. Liggett

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas M. Liggett"}

**Thomas Milton Liggett** (March 29, 1944 – May 12, 2020) was a mathematician at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA). He worked in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), specializing in [interacting particle systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interacting_particle_systems).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Thomas Milton Liggett was born on March 29, 1944, in [Danville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danville,_Kentucky).[^o4] Liggett moved at the age of two with his missionary parents to Latin America, where he was educated in [Bueno Aires, Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires) and [San Juan, Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico). He graduated from [Oberlin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberlin_College) with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1965, where he was influenced towards [probability](wikipedia://en/23542) by Samuel Goldberg (b. 1925), an ex-student of [William Feller](wikipedia://en/206644). He moved to [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford), taking classes with [Kai Lai Chung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Lai_Chung), and writing his thesis, *Weak Convergence of Conditioned Sums of Independent Random Vectors*, in 1969 with advisor [Samuel Karlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Karlin) on problems associated with the [invariance principle](wikipedia://en/3828419). He graduated with a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in 1966 and a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1969.[^o4][^o13]

## Career {#Career}

Liggett joined the faculty at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) in 1969, where he spent his entire career. He became a professor in the mathematics department in 1976, and served as department chair from 1991 to 1994. He retired in 2011, but remained active within the department.[^o7] He was the advisor of [Norman Matloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Matloff)[^o5] and [Amber L. Puha](wikipedia://en/70551889).

Liggett had contributed to numerous areas of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), including subadditive [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), [random graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_graphs), [renewal theory](wikipedia://en/1945387), and was best known for his pioneering work on [interacting particle systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interacting_particle_systems), including the [contact process](wikipedia://en/5097569), the [voter model](wikipedia://en/35998199), and the [exclusion process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusion_process).[^o9][^o10] His two books in this field have been influential.[^o11][^o12]

Liggett was the managing editor of the [Annals of Probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) from 1985–1987. He held a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) from 1973–1977, and a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) from 1997–1998. He was the Wald Memorial Lecturer of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1996, and was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2008.[^o13][^o14] He had been elected to the American Academy of Arts & Sciences in 2012,[^o15] and in 2012 he also became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o16]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Liggett married Christina Marie Goodale on August 19, 1972. They had two children, Timothy and Amy.[^o4] Liggett died on May 12, 2020, in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles).[^o7][^o18]
