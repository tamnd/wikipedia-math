# Salva congruitate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Salva congruitate"}

***Salva congruitate***[^o2] is a [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) scholastic term in [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) which means "without becoming ill-formed",[^o3] *salva* meaning *rescue*, *salvation*, or *welfare* and *congruitate* meaning *combine*, *coincide*, or *agree*. The term is used in logic to mean that two terms may be [substituted](wikipedia://en/5438948) for each other while preserving [grammaticality](wikipedia://en/12569) in all contexts.[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Remarks on *salva congruitate* {#Remarks_on_salva_congruitate}

### Timothy C. Potts {#Timothy_C._Potts}

[Timothy C. Potts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_C._Potts) describes *salva congruitate* as a form of replacement in the context of meaning. It is a replacement which preserves [semantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic) coherence and should be distinguished from a replacement which preserves [syntactic](wikipedia://en/26860) coherence but may yield an expression to which no [meaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meaning_%28philosophy_of_language%29) has been given. This means that supposing an original expression is meaningful, the new expression obtained by the replacement will also be meaningful, though it will not necessarily have the same meaning as the original one, nor, if the expression in question happens to be a [proposition](wikipedia://en/81094), will the replacement necessarily preserve the truth value of the original.[^o8]

### Bob Hale {#Bob_Hale}

[Bob Hale](wikipedia://en/13931788) explains *salva congruitate*, as applied to singular terms, as [substantival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noun) expressions in natural language, which are able to replace singular terms without destructive effect on the [grammar](wikipedia://en/26860) of a sentence.[^o10] Thus the singular term 'Bob' may be replaced by the definite description 'the first man to swim the English Channel' *salva congruitate*. Such replacement may shift both meaning and reference, and so, if made in the context of a sentence, may cause a change in truth-value. Thus terms which may be interchanged *salva congruitate* may not be interchangeable *salva veritate* (preserving truth). More generally, expressions of any type are interchangeable *salva congruitate* if and only if they can replace one another preserving grammaticality or well-formedness.
