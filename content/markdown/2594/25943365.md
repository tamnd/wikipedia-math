# Serre's conjecture II

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

Must the [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) set of a [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879), [semisimple algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_algebraic_group) over a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088) of [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) at most 2 always equal zero?

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Serre's conjecture II"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Serre's conjecture II** is the statement that if *G* is a [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879), [semisimple algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_algebraic_group) over a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088) *F* of [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) at most 2, then the [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) set *H*<sup>1</sup>(*F*, *G*) is zero.[^o3][^o4] It was proposed by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) in 1962, as an higher-dimension equivalent of his conjecture I (which was later proven).[^o5]

:::

A converse of the conjecture holds: if the field *F* is perfect and if the cohomology set *H*<sup>1</sup>(*F*, *G*) is zero for every semisimple, simply connected algebraic group *G,* then the *p*-cohomological dimension of *F* is at most 2 for every [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*.[^o6] The conjecture has been proven for all groups over all perfect fields; however, it remains open for [anisotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropy) [E6](wikipedia://en/293725), [E7](wikipedia://en/649115) and [E8](wikipedia://en/647994) groups and [trialitarian](wikipedia://en/683109) [D4](wikipedia://en/41928) group over imperfect fields.

## Known cases {#Known_cases}

The conjecture holds in the case where *F* is a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) (such as [p-adic field](wikipedia://en/51423)), a [global field](wikipedia://en/288358) with no real [embeddings](wikipedia://en/52119) (such as **Q**($\sqrt{-1}$)) or a [totally imaginary number field](wikipedia://en/34962418).[^o8][^o9] This is a special case of the [Kneser](wikipedia://en/17620650)–[Harder](wikipedia://en/50792386)–Chernousov [Hasse principle](wikipedia://en/489707) for [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) over global fields. (Note that such fields do indeed have cohomological dimension at most 2.[^o4]) The conjecture holds when *F* is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) over [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) and has [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree) at most 2. The conjecture also holds for l-special fields, [complete valued fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_valued_field) and [function fields](wikipedia://en/2783843).[^o10]

The conjecture is known to hold for certain groups *G*. For [special linear groups](wikipedia://en/144823), it is a consequence of the [Merkurjev–Suslin theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkurjev–Suslin_theorem).[^o11] Building on this result, the conjecture holds if *G* is a [classical group](wikipedia://en/10172878) (type [A](wikipedia://en/4938898), B, [C](wikipedia://en/29529118) or D with no [triality](wikipedia://en/683109)); or a group of type [F4](wikipedia://en/292877) and [G2](wikipedia://en/292864) on a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088).[^o12] The conjecture also holds for classical groups over imperfect fields.[^o13] Over perfect fields, the conjecture also holds if *G* is an [isotropic](wikipedia://en/14865) or [quasi-split](wikipedia://en/34287621) [exceptional group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exceptional_Lie_group) (except for [E8](wikipedia://en/647994));[^o14][^o15] or a [pseudo-reductive group](wikipedia://en/36120746).[^o16]
