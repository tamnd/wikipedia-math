# Robin Gandy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robin Gandy"}

**Robin Oliver Gandy** (22 September 1919 – 20 November 1995) was a British mathematician and [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician).[^o3] He was a friend, student, and associate of [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208), having been supervised by Turing during his PhD at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge),[^o4] where they worked together.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

## Education and early life {#Education_and_early_life}

Robin Gandy was born in the village of [Rotherfield Peppard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotherfield_Peppard), Oxfordshire, England.[^o3] A great-great-grandson of the architect and artist [Joseph Gandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Gandy) (1771–1843), he was the son of Thomas Hall Gandy (1876–1948), a [general practitioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_practitioner), and [Ida Caroline née Hony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_Gandy) (1885–1977), a social worker and later an author.[^o9] His brother was the diplomat [Christopher Gandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Gandy) and his sister was the physician [Gillian Gandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillian_Gandy).[^o10]

Educated at [Abbotsholme School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbotsholme_School) in Derbyshire, Gandy took two years of the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos), at [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), before enlisting for military service in 1940. During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he worked on radio intercept equipment at [Hanslope Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanslope_Park), where [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208) was working on a speech [encipherment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encipherment) project, and he became one of Turing's lifelong friends and associates. In 1946, he completed Part III of the Mathematical Tripos, then began studying for a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) under Turing's supervision. He completed his thesis, *On axiomatic systems in mathematics and theories in Physics*, in 1952.[^o4] He was a member of the [Cambridge Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Apostles).[^o11]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Gandy held positions at the [University of Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leicester), the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds), and the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester). He was a visiting associate professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) from 1966 to 1967 and held a similar position at [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) in 1968. In 1969, he moved to [Wolfson College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfson_College,_Oxford), where he became [Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_%28academic_rank%29) in Mathematical Logic.

Gandy is known for his work in [recursion theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion_theory). His contributions include the Spector–Gandy theorem, the Gandy Stage Comparison theorem, and the Gandy Selection theorem. He also made a significant contribution to the [understanding of the Church–Turing thesis](wikipedia://en/13050911#Gandy:_"machine_computation",_discrete,_deterministic,_and_limited_to_"local_causation"_by_light_speed), and his generalisation of the [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403) is called a Gandy machine.[^o13]

Gandy died in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford), England on 20 November 1995.[^o3][^o14]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The **Robin Gandy Buildings**, a pair of accommodation blocks at [Wolfson College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfson_College,_Oxford), are named after Gandy.[^o17][^o18] A one-day centenary *Gandy Colloquium* was held on 22 February 2020 at the College in Gandy's honour, including contributions by some of his students;[^o19][^o20] the speakers were Marianna Antonutti Marfori (Munich), [Andrew Hodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Hodges) (Oxford), [Martin Hyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Hyland) (Cambridge), [Jeff Paris](wikipedia://en/11279983) (Manchester), Göran Sundholm (Leiden), Christine Tasson (Paris), and [Philip Welch](wikipedia://en/57452682) (Bristol).
