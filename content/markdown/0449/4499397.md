# Algebraic manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Algebraic manifold"}

\_\_notoc\_\_ In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **algebraic manifold** is an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) which is also a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). As such, algebraic manifolds are a generalisation of the concept of [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) [curves](wikipedia://en/89246) and [surfaces](wikipedia://en/27865) defined by [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000). An example is the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859), which can be defined as the [zero set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_set) of the polynomial *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> + *z*<sup>2</sup> – 1, and hence is an algebraic variety.

:::

For an algebraic manifold, the [ground field](wikipedia://en/27161748) will be the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers); in the case of the real numbers, the manifold of real points is sometimes called a [Nash manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_manifold).

Every sufficiently small local patch of an algebraic manifold is isomorphic to *k*<sup>*m*</sup> where *k* is the ground field. Equivalently the variety is [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) (free from [singular points](wikipedia://en/981915)). The [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799) is one example of a complex algebraic manifold, since it is the [complex projective line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_projective_line).
