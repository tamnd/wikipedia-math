# Logarithmic mean

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Logarithmic mean"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **logarithmic mean** is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) of two [non-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative) numbers which is equal to their [difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_%28mathematics%29) divided by the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) of their [quotient](wikipedia://en/332640). This calculation is applicable in [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) problems involving [heat](wikipedia://en/184726) and [mass transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transfer).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The logarithmic mean is defined by

$$L(x, y) = \left \{ \begin{array}{l l} x, & \text{if }x = y,\\ \dfrac{x - y}{\ln x - \ln y}, & \text{otherwise}, \end{array} \right .$$

for $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$, such that $x, y > 0$.

:::

## Inequalities {#Inequalities}

The logarithmic mean of two numbers is smaller than the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) and the [generalized mean](wikipedia://en/13143) with exponent greater than 1. However, it is larger than the [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) and the [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463), respectively. The inequalities are strict unless both numbers are equal.[^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8] More precisely, for $p, x, y \in \mathbb{R}$ with $x \neq y$ and $p > 1$, we have

$$\frac{2xy}{x + y} < \sqrt{x y} < \frac{x - y}{\ln x - \ln y} < \frac{x + y}{2} < \left(\frac{x^p + y^p}2\right)^{1/p},$$

where the expressions in the chain of inequalities are, in order: the harmonic mean, the geometric mean, the logarithmic mean, the arithmetic mean, and the generalized arithmetic mean with exponent $p$.

## Derivation {#Derivation}

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Mean value theorem of differential calculus"}

### Mean value theorem of differential calculus {#Mean_value_theorem_of_differential_calculus}

From the [mean value theorem](wikipedia://en/19662), [there exists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_exists) a value $\xi$ in the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) between $x$ and $y$ where the derivative $f\,'$ equals the slope of the [secant line](wikipedia://en/241886):

$$\exists \xi \in (x, y): \ f'(\xi) = \frac{f(x) - f(y)}{x - y}$$

The logarithmic mean is obtained as the value of $\xi$ by substituting $\ln$ for $f$ and similarly for its corresponding [derivative](wikipedia://en/21476#Derivative):

$$\frac{1}{\xi} = \frac{\ln x - \ln y}{x-y}$$

and solving for $\xi$:

$$\xi = \frac{x-y}{\ln x - \ln y}$$

:::

### Integration {#Integration}

The logarithmic mean is also given by the integral

$$L(x, y) = \int_0^1 x^{1-t} y^t\,\mathrm{d}t.$$

This interpretation allows the derivation of some properties of the logarithmic mean. Since the exponential function is [monotonic](wikipedia://en/48260), the integral over an interval of length 1 is bounded by $x$ and $y$.

Two other useful integral representations are

$${1 \over L(x,y)} = \int_0^1 {\operatorname{d}\!t \over t x + (1-t)y}$$

and

$${1 \over L(x,y)} = \int_0^\infty {\operatorname{d}\!t \over (t+x)\,(t+y)}.$$

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Mean value theorem of differential calculus"}

### Mean value theorem of differential calculus {#Mean_value_theorem_of_differential_calculus}

One can generalize the mean to $n + 1$ variables by considering the [mean value theorem for divided differences](wikipedia://en/3213853) for the $n$-th [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of the logarithm.

We obtain

$$L_\text{MV}(x_0,\, \dots,\, x_n) = \sqrt[-n]{(-1)^{n+1} n \ln\left(\left[x_0,\, \dots,\, x_n\right]\right)}$$

where $\ln\left(\left[x_0,\, \dots,\, x_n\right]\right)$ denotes a [divided difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divided_difference) of the logarithm. For $n = 2$ this leads to

$$L_\text{MV}(x, y, z) = \sqrt{\frac{(x-y)(y-z)(z-x)}{2 \bigl((y-z) \ln x + (z-x) \ln y + (x-y) \ln z \bigr)}}.$$

:::

### Integral {#Integral}

The integral interpretation can also be generalized to more variables, but it leads to a different result. Given the [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) $S$ with

$$S = \{\left(\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_n\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{n + 1}\,;\, \alpha_0 + \dots + \alpha_n = 1 \text{ and } \alpha_j \ge 0, \text{ for } j = 0, \dots, n\}$$

and an appropriate measure $\mathrm{d}\alpha$ which assigns the simplex a volume of 1, we obtain

$$L_\text{I}\left(x_0,\dots,x_n\right) = \int_S x_0^{\alpha_0} \cdots x_n^{\alpha_n}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha.$$

This can be expressed as the divided differences of the exponential function by

$$L_\text{I}\left(x_0,\dots,x_n\right) = n! \exp\left[\ln\left(x_0\right), \dots, \ln\left(x_n\right)\right].$$

In the case of $n = 2$, it is

$$L_\text{I}(x, y, z) = -2 \frac{x(\ln y - \ln z) + y(\ln z - \ln x) + z(\ln x - \ln y)} {(\ln x - \ln y)(\ln y - \ln z)(\ln z - \ln x)}.$$

:::

## Connection to other means {#Connection_to_other_means}

Some other means can be expressed in terms of the logarithmic mean.

*Other means expressed in terms of the logarithmic mean*

::: {#o19 .table cue=table name="Other means expressed in terms of the logarithmic mean"}

| Name | Mean | Expression |
|---|---|---|
| [Arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) | $\frac{x + y}{2}$ | $\frac{L\left(x^2, y^2\right)}{L(x, y)}$ |
| [Geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) | $\sqrt{x y}$ | $\sqrt{\frac{L\left(x, y\right)}{L\left( \frac{1}{x}, \frac{1}{y} \right)}}$ |
| [Harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463) | $\frac{2}{\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{y}}$ | $\frac{ L\left( \frac{1}{x}, \frac{1}{y} \right) }{L\left( \frac{1}{x^2}, \frac{1}{y^2} \right)}$ |

:::
