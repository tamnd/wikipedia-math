# Lagrangian system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lagrangian system"}

In mathematics, a **Lagrangian system** is a pair $(Y, L)$, consisting of a smooth [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) $Y \to X$ and a Lagrangian density $L$, which yields the Euler–Lagrange [differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886) acting on sections of $Y \to X$.

:::

In [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586), many [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) are Lagrangian systems. The configuration space of such a Lagrangian system is a fiber bundle $Q \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ over the time axis $\mathbb{R}$. In particular, $Q = \mathbb{R} \times M$ if a reference frame is fixed. In [classical field theory](wikipedia://en/1293340), all field systems are the Lagrangian ones.

## Lagrangians and Euler–Lagrange operators {#Lagrangians_and_Euler–Lagrange_operators}

A **Lagrangian density** $L$ (or, simply, a [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/40752010)) of order $r$ is defined as an [$n$-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_form), $n = \dim X$, on the $r$-order [jet manifold](wikipedia://en/928060) $J^{r}Y$ of $Y$.

A Lagrangian $L$ can be introduced as an element of the [variational bicomplex](wikipedia://en/23569508) of the [differential graded algebra](wikipedia://en/2415863) $O^{\ast}_{\infty}(Y)$ of [exterior forms](wikipedia://en/221519) on [jet manifolds](wikipedia://en/928060) of $Y \to X$. The [coboundary operator](wikipedia://en/194060) of this bicomplex contains the variational operator $\delta$ which, acting on $L$, defines the associated Euler–Lagrange operator $\delta L$.

### In coordinates {#In_coordinates}

Given bundle coordinates $x^{\lambda}, y^{i}$ on a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) $Y$ and the adapted coordinates $x^{\lambda}, y^{i}, y^{i}_{\Lambda}$, $(\Lambda = (\lambda_{1}, ...,\lambda_{k})$, $|\Lambda| = k \le r$) on jet manifolds $J^{r}Y$, a Lagrangian $L$ and its Euler–Lagrange operator read:

$$L=\mathcal{L}(x^\lambda,y^i,y^i_\Lambda) \, d^nx,$$

$$\delta L= \delta_i\mathcal{L} \, dy^i\wedge d^nx,\qquad \delta_i\mathcal{L} =\partial_i\mathcal{L} + \sum_{|\Lambda|}(-1)^{|\Lambda|} \, d_\Lambda \, \partial_i^\Lambda\mathcal{L},$$

where

$$d_\Lambda=d_{\lambda_1}\cdots d_{\lambda_k}, \qquad d_\lambda=\partial_\lambda + y^i_\lambda\partial_i +\cdots,$$

denote the total derivatives.

For instance, a first-order Lagrangian and its second-order Euler–Lagrange operator take the form

$$L=\mathcal{L}(x^\lambda,y^i,y^i_\lambda) \, d^nx,\qquad \delta_i L =\partial_i\mathcal{L} - d_\lambda \partial_i^\lambda\mathcal{L}.$$

### Euler–Lagrange equations {#Euler–Lagrange_equations}

The kernel of an Euler–Lagrange operator provides the [Euler–Lagrange equations](wikipedia://en/294995) $\delta L = 0$.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Cohomology and Noether's theorems"}

## Cohomology and Noether's theorems {#Cohomology_and_Noether's_theorems}

[Cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of the variational bicomplex leads to the so-called variational formula

$$dL=\delta L + d_H \Theta_L,$$

where

$$d_H\Theta_L=dx^\lambda\wedge d_\lambda\phi, \qquad \phi\in O^*_\infty(Y)$$

is the total differential and $\theta_{L}$ is a Lepage equivalent of $L$. [Noether's first theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noether's_first_theorem) and [Noether's second theorem](wikipedia://en/18021657) are corollaries of this variational formula.

:::

## Graded manifolds {#Graded_manifolds}

Extended to [graded manifolds](wikipedia://en/24519420), the [variational bicomplex](wikipedia://en/23569508) provides description of graded Lagrangian systems of even and odd variables.[^o8]

## Alternative formulations {#Alternative_formulations}

In a different way, Lagrangians, Euler–Lagrange operators and Euler–Lagrange equations are introduced in the framework of the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882).

## Classical mechanics {#Classical_mechanics}

In [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586), [equations of motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equations_of_motion) are first and second order [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) $M$ or various fiber bundles $Q$ over $\mathbb{R}$. A solution of the equations of motion is called a *motion*.[^o11][^o12]
