# Census in Armenia

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Census in Armenia"}

**Census in Armenia** is a population census conducted in [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia) about every 10 years with the purpose of capturing exact data on [demographics in the country](wikipedia://en/1092).

:::

## Demographic trends {#Demographic_trends}

While [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians) formed a consistent majority, [Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_people) were historically the second largest population in the republic under [Soviet rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_SSR) (forming about 2.5% in 1989[^o3]). However, due to hostilities with neighboring Azerbaijan over the disputed region of [Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh) virtually all Azeris emigrated from Armenia. Conversely, Armenia received a large influx of Armenian refugees from Azerbaijan, thus giving Armenia a more homogeneous character. This forceful population exchange also affected the Christian [Udi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udi_people) of Azerbaijan, many of whom were perceived as Armenians due to close cultural ties between both peoples.[^o4] The number of Udis residing in Armenia has increased from 19 in 1989[^o3] to about 200 by 2006.[^o4]

Additionally since independence, several other ethnic groups have emigrated especially [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians) (who decreased from 51,555 persons in 1989[^o3] to 14,660 in 2001[^o5]), [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians) (8,341 in 1989[^o3] to 1,633 in 2001[^o5]), [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks) (4,650 in 1989[^o3] to 1,176 in 2001[^o5]), and [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians) (1,061 in 1989[^o3] to 160 in 2001[^o6]). The numbers of [Yazidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidis), [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people), and [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) have remained consistent for the most part (though approximately 2,000 Assyrians have left Armenia between 1989[^o3] and 2001[^o5]). Although [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgians) were historically counted among the largest ethnic groups in modern Armenia, their numbers have dropped substantially since the 1989 Soviet census when they numbered 1,364 persons.[^o3]

## Republic of Armenia censuses {#Republic_of_Armenia_censuses}

section The first census in Armenia after the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union_%281985–1991%29#Yeltsin_and_the_dissolution_of_the_USSR) was conducted by the [Government of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Armenia)'s [National Statistical Service](wikipedia://en/24984172) during the period of October 10–19, 2001. The census night was October 10. The [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_%28Armenia%29) adopted the law "On Census" in 1999, but the government lacked the necessary funds to carry out the count immediately. According to Armenian law, a census must take place every 10 years.
