# Gladys Gregory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gladys Gregory"}

*Image: Gladys Gregory FamiltyPhoto1.jpg.* Gladys Gregory (1899–1981)

**Gladys Caroline Gregory** (19 May 1899 – 6 November 1981) was one of two women to share the accolade of first woman [actuary](wikipedia://en/43405) in the United Kingdom, the other being [Dorothy Spiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Spiers), née Davis. Gregory joined [Prudential Assurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudential_Assurance) in 1917, direct from school, and passed the actuarial exams at the [Institute of Actuaries](wikipedia://en/852871) in 1923.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Gladys Caroline Gregory was born on 19 May 1899 at 32 Mina Road, [Walworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walworth), London and was the eldest daughter of [shoemaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoemaker), Thomas Gregory (1871–1966), and his wife, Harriet Elizabeth Gregory, née Cobbett (1870–1958).[^o3] She attended the local council school in [Clapham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clapham) where she gained the school-leaving certificate.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Gregory joined Prudential Assurance in July 1917 when she was 18.[^o6][^o7] In November 1919 *The Institute of Actuaries* voted to admit women on the same conditions as men.[^o8] Gregory passed her first exam in 1920 and the subsequent exams in each year afterwards, qualifying in 1923, alongside Dorothy Davis.[^o9] Passing the exams was clearly a shared endeavour - Dorothy and Gladys “*coached*” and did their “*very hard swotting*” together, for “…*the ‘stiffest’ tests in mathematics*”, qualifying in less than the “*usual four years*”.[^o10][^o11] In 1923 Gregory was the more prominent of the pair: at 24 she was two years younger than her counterpart; her final examination was said to have been “*faultless*”, and she would have come overall first had the results been ranked.[^o10] Writing in *Good Housekeeping* in October 1923, she commended the profession to well-educated girls, prepared to work hard and give up their evenings to study. She also made the point that many insurance offices were overstaffed, and opportunities for girls were limited.[^o12]

## Personal and later life {#Personal_and_later_life}

Gladys resigned from Prudential in April 1927, shortly before she married John Brooker, and she formally resigned as a Fellow of the Institute of Actuaries in October 1927.[^o14][^o7] After their marriage, she became a housewife and had two daughters.[^o15] Gregory died on 16 November 1981 in [Wokingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wokingham), [Berkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire).[^o16][^o17]
