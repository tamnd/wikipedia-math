# Dyakis dodecahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dyakis dodecahedron"}

*Image: New dyakis dodecahedron.stl.* 3D model of a dyakis dodecahedron

In geometry, the **dyakis dodecahedron** /ˈdʌɪəkɪsˌdəʊdɪkəˈhiːdrən/[^o3] or **diploid** is a variant of the [deltoidal icositetrahedron](wikipedia://en/723339) with pyritohedral symmetry, transforming the kite faces into chiral [quadrilaterals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrilaterals). The name diploid derives from the Greek word διπλάσιος (*diplásios*), meaning twofold since it has 2-fold symmetry along its 6 octahedral vertices. It has the same number of faces, edges, and vertices as the deltoidal icositetrahedron as they are topologically identical.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The dyakis dodecahedron can be constructed by enlarging 24 of the 48 faces of the [disdyakis dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/724752) and is inscribed in the dyakis dodecahedron,[^o6] thus it exists as a hemihedral form of it with indices {hkl}.[^o7] It can be constructed into two non-regular pentagonal dodecahedra, the [pyritohedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyritohedron) and the [tetartoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetartoid). The transformation to the pyritohedron can be made by combining two adjacent trapezoids that share a long edge together into one [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) face. The short edges of the hexagon can then be combined to finally get the pentagon. The transformation to the tetartoid can be made by enlarging 12 of the dyakis dodecahedron's 24 faces.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Since the quadrilaterals are chiral and non-regular, the dyakis dodecahedron is a non-[uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223), a type of polyhedron that is not [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) and does not have regular polygon faces. It is an [isohedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isohedron),[^o10] meaning that it is [face transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_transitive).

The dual polyhedron of a dyakis dodecahedron is the [cantic snub octahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantic_snub_octahedron).

:::

## In crystallography {#In_crystallography}

The dyakis dodecahedron only exists in one crystal, [pyrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrite). Pyrite has other forms other than the dyakis dodecahedron, including [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606), [octahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedra), [cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubes) and [pyritohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyritohedra). Though the cube and octahedron are in the cubic crystal system, the dyakis dodecahedron and the pyritohedron are in the isometric crystal system and the tetrahedron is in the tetrahedral crystal system. Although the dyakis dodecahedron has 3-fold axes like the pyritohedron and cube, it doesn't have 4-fold axes, rather it has order-4 vertices, as when the dyakis dodecahedron is rotated 90 or 270° along an order-4 vertex, it is not the same as before, because the order-4 vertices act as 2-fold axes, as when they are rotated a full turn or 180°, the polyhedron looks the same as before.
