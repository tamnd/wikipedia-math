# Dionysius of Cyrene

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dionysius of Cyrene"}

**Dionysius** of [Cyrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrene,_Libya) (Διονύσιος ὁ Κυρηναῖος), lived c. 150 BC, was a [Stoic](wikipedia://en/19224834) philosopher and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

He was a pupil of [Diogenes of Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogenes_of_Babylon) and [Antipater of Tarsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipater_of_Tarsus).

He was famed as a mathematician, and he is probably the Dionysius whose arguments are attacked by [Philodemus](wikipedia://en/2625475) in his book *On Signs* (De Signis),[^o2] where Dionysius is reported as arguing that the Sun must be very large because it reappears slowly from behind an obstruction.[^o3]
