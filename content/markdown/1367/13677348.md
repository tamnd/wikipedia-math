# Brian Bowditch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brian Bowditch"}

**Brian Hayward Bowditch** (born 1961[^o2]) is a British mathematician known for his contributions to [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), particularly in the areas of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) and [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774). He is also known for solving[^o27] the [angel problem](wikipedia://en/1076026). Bowditch holds a chaired Professor appointment in Mathematics at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Brian Bowditch was born in 1961 in [Neath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neath), Wales. He obtained a B.A. degree from [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) in 1983.[^o2] He subsequently pursued doctoral studies in Mathematics at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) under the supervision of [David Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_B._A._Epstein) where he received a PhD in 1988.[^o4] Bowditch then had postdoctoral and visiting positions at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey), the University of Warwick, [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) at [Bures-sur-Yvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bures-sur-Yvette), the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne), and the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen).[^o2] In 1992 he received an appointment at the [University of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southampton) where he stayed until 2007. In 2007 Bowditch moved to the University of Warwick, where he received a chaired Professor appointment in Mathematics.

Bowditch was awarded a [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) by the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 1997 for his work in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) and [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732).[^o5][^o6] He gave an Invited address at the 2004 [European Congress of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13505846) in Stockholm.[^o7] Bowditch is a former member of the Editorial Board for the journal *[Annales de la Faculté des Sciences de Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annales_de_la_Faculté_des_Sciences_de_Toulouse)*[^o8] and a former Editorial Adviser for the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o9]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Early notable results of Bowditch include clarifying the classic notion of [geometric finiteness](wikipedia://en/31212031) for higher-dimensional [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652) in constant and variable negative curvature. In a 1993 paper[^o11] Bowditch proved that five standard characterisations of geometric finiteness for discrete groups of isometries of [hyperbolic 3-space](wikipedia://en/318742) and [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/241291), (including the definition in terms of having a finitely-sided fundamental polyhedron) remain equivalent for groups of isometries of [hyperbolic *n*-space](wikipedia://en/318742) where *n* ≥ 4. He showed, however, that in dimensions *n* ≥ 4 the condition of having a finitely-sided [Dirichlet domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_domain) is no longer equivalent to the standard notions of geometric finiteness. In a subsequent paper[^o12] Bowditch considered a similar problem for discrete groups of isometries of [Hadamard manifold](wikipedia://en/2850665) of pinched (but not necessarily constant) negative curvature and of arbitrary dimension *n* ≥ 2. He proved that four out of five equivalent definitions of geometric finiteness considered in his previous paper remain equivalent in this general set-up, but the condition of having a finitely-sided fundamental polyhedron is no longer equivalent to them.

Much of Bowditch's work in the 1990s concerned studying boundaries at infinity of [word-hyperbolic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-hyperbolic_group). He proved the *cut-point conjecture* which says that the boundary of a [one-ended](wikipedia://en/19196631) word-hyperbolic group does not have any global [cut-points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut-point). Bowditch first proved this conjecture in the main cases of a one-ended hyperbolic group that does not split over a two-ended [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011)[^o13] (that is, a subgroup containing [infinite cyclic subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_cyclic_subgroup) of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581)) and also for one-ended hyperbolic groups that are "strongly accessible".[^o14] The general case of the conjecture was finished shortly thereafter by G. Ananda Swarup[^o15] who characterised Bowditch's work as follows: "The most significant advances in this direction were carried out by Brian Bowditch in a brilliant series of papers (\[4\]-\[7\]). We draw heavily from his work". Soon after Swarup's paper Bowditch supplied an alternative proof of the cut-point conjecture in the general case.[^o16] Bowditch's work relied on extracting various discrete tree-like structures from the [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of a word-hyperbolic group on its boundary.

Bowditch also proved that (modulo a few exceptions) the boundary of a one-ended word-hyperbolic group *G* has local cut-points if and only if *G* admits an essential splitting, as an [amalgamated free product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_product_with_amalgamation) or an [HNN extension](wikipedia://en/2823319), over a virtually infinite cyclic group. This allowed Bowditch to produce[^o17] a theory of [JSJ decomposition](wikipedia://en/228599) for word-hyperbolic groups that was more canonical and more general (particularly because it covered groups with nontrivial torsion) than the original JSJ decomposition theory of [Zlil Sela](wikipedia://en/19309289).[^o18] One of the consequences of Bowditch's work is that for one-ended word-hyperbolic groups (with a few exceptions) having a nontrivial essential splitting over a virtually cyclic subgroup is a [quasi-isometry](wikipedia://en/12747259) invariant.

Bowditch also gave a topological characterisation of word-hyperbolic groups, thus solving a conjecture proposed by [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Leonidovich_Gromov). Namely, Bowditch proved[^o19] that a group *G* is word-hyperbolic if and only if *G* admits an [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) by [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) on a perfect metrisable compactum *M* as a "uniform convergence group", that is such that the diagonal action of *G* on the set of distinct triples from *M* is properly discontinuous and co-compact; moreover, in that case *M* is *G*-equivariantly homeomorphic to the boundary ∂*G* of *G*. Later, building up on this work, Bowditch's PhD student Yaman gave a topological characterisation of [relatively hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/21993809).[^o20]

Much of Bowditch's work in 2000s concerns the study of the [curve complex](wikipedia://en/51024643), with various applications to [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257), [mapping class groups](wikipedia://en/793295) and [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652). The [curve complex](wikipedia://en/51024643) *C*(*S*) of a finite type surface *S*, introduced by Harvey in the late 1970s,[^o21] has the set of free homotopy classes of essential simple closed curves on *S* as the set of vertices, where several distinct vertices span a simplex if the corresponding curves can be realised disjointly. The curve complex turned out to be a fundamental tool in the study of the geometry of the [Teichmüller space](wikipedia://en/1835661), of [mapping class groups](wikipedia://en/793295) and of [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652). In a 1999 paper[^o22] [Howard Masur](wikipedia://en/46960501) and [Yair Minsky](wikipedia://en/46905726) proved that for a finite type orientable surface *S* the curve complex *C*(*S*) is [Gromov-hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Δ-hyperbolic_space). This result was a key component in the subsequent proof of [Thurston's](wikipedia://en/39190) [Ending lamination conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ending_lamination_conjecture), a solution which was based on the combined work of Yair Minsky, Howard Masur, Jeffrey Brock, and [Richard Canary](wikipedia://en/46914909).[^o23] In 2006 Bowditch gave another proof[^o24] of hyperbolicity of the curve complex. Bowditch's proof is more combinatorial and rather different from the Masur-Minsky original argument. Bowditch's result also provides an estimate on the hyperbolicity constant of the curve complex which is logarithmic in complexity of the surface and also gives a description of geodesics in the curve complex in terms of the intersection numbers. A subsequent 2008 paper of Bowditch[^o25] pushed these ideas further and obtained new quantitative finiteness results regarding the so-called "tight geodesics" in the curve complex, a notion introduced by Masur and Minsky to combat the fact that the curve complex is not locally finite. As an application, Bowditch proved that, with a few exceptions of surfaces of small complexity, the action of the [mapping class group](wikipedia://en/793295) Mod(*S*) on *C*(*S*) is "acylindrical" and that the asymptotic translation lengths of [pseudo-Anosov](wikipedia://en/2327355) elements of Mod(*S*) on *C*(*S*) are rational numbers with bounded denominators.

A 2007 paper of Bowditch[^o27] produces a positive solution of the [angel problem](wikipedia://en/1076026) of [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807):[^o26] Bowditch proved[^o27] that a 4-angel has a winning strategy and can evade the devil in the "angel game". Independent solutions of the angel problem were produced at about the same time by András Máthé[^o28] and Oddvar Kloster.[^o29]
