# Shrikhande graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Shrikhande graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Shrikhande graph** is a [graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallery_of_named_graphs) discovered by [S. S. Shrikhande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._S._Shrikhande) in 1959.[^o4] It is a [strongly regular graph](wikipedia://en/1399856) with 16 [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and 48 [edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29), with each vertex having [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) 6. Every pair of nodes has exactly two other neighbors in common, whether or not the pair of nodes is connected.

:::

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The Shrikhande graph can be constructed as a [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277). The vertex set is $\mathbb{Z}_4 \times \mathbb{Z}_4$. Two vertices are adjacent if and only if the difference is in $\{\pm( 1,0),\pm(0,1),\pm (1,1)\}$.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

In the Shrikhande graph, any two distinct vertices *I* and *J* have two neighbors in common, which holds true whether or not *I* is adjacent to *J*. In other words, it is [strongly regular](wikipedia://en/1399856) and its parameters are {16, 6, 2, 2}, with $\lambda = \mu = 2$. This equality implies that the graph is associated with a [symmetric](wikipedia://en/53741) [BIBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BIBD). The Shrikhande graph shares these parameters with exactly one other graph, the 4×4 [rook's graph](wikipedia://en/7324959), i.e., the [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231) *L*(*K*<sub>4,4</sub>) of the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) *K*<sub>4,4</sub>. The latter graph is the only line graph *L*(*K<sub>n,n</sub>*) for which the strong regularity parameters do not determine that graph uniquely but are shared with a different graph.[^o4][^o9]

The Shrikhande graph is [locally hexagonal](wikipedia://en/7309251); that is, the neighbors of each vertex form a [cycle](wikipedia://en/878962) of six vertices. As with any locally cyclic graph, the Shrikhande graph is the [1-skeleton](wikipedia://en/2977910) of a [Whitney triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440976) of some surface; in the case of the Shrikhande graph, this surface is a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) in which each vertex is surrounded by six triangles.[^o10] Thus, the Shrikhande graph is a [toroidal graph](wikipedia://en/1633057). The embedding forms a [regular map](wikipedia://en/21533914) in the torus, with 32 triangular faces. The skeleton of the dual of this map (as embedded in the torus) is the [Dyck graph](wikipedia://en/24041180), a cubic symmetric graph.

The Shrikhande graph is not a [distance-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/7768222). It is the smallest [distance-regular graph](wikipedia://en/7768943) that is not distance-transitive.[^o11]

The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/15094186) of the Shrikhande graph is of order 192. It acts transitively on the vertices, on the edges and on the arcs of the graph. Therefore, the Shrikhande graph is a [symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/1577896).

The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the Shrikhande graph is $(x-6)(x-2)^6(x+2)^9$. Therefore, the Shrikhande graph is an [integral graph](wikipedia://en/24845923): its [spectrum](wikipedia://en/534914) consists entirely of integers.

It has [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 4 and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 3.[^o12]

The graph is not [1-planar](wikipedia://en/38249632).[^o13]
