# Hartry Field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hartry Field"}

**Hartry Hamlin Field** (born November 30, 1946) is an American philosopher. He is Silver Professor of Philosophy at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University); he is a notable contributor to [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science), [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439), [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology), and [philosophy of mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_mind).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hartry Hamlin Field was born on November 30, 1946, in [Boston, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston), to Adelaide (Anderson) and Donald Field.[^o5] Field earned a B.A. in mathematics from the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) in 1967 and an M.A. in philosophy from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1968.[^o6] He earned his Ph.D. in philosophy from Harvard in 1972 under the direction of [Hilary Putnam](wikipedia://en/75233) and [Richard Boyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Boyd).[^o7]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

He taught first at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), and then at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) and [City University of New York Graduate Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_New_York_Graduate_Center) before joining the [NYU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYU) faculty in 1997,[^o5][^o7] where he is currently Silver Professor of Philosophy.[^o9]

Field was elected Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2003 and is also a past winner of the [Lakatos Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakatos_Prize) in 1986.[^o5][^o10] He delivered the 2008 [John Locke Lectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke_Lectures) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o6][^o11] In 2012, he was appointed Distinguished Research Professor at the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham) in the UK.[^o12]

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

Field's first work was a commentary on [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920)'s theory of [truth](wikipedia://en/29920), which he has worked on since 1972. His current view on this matter is in favor of a [deflationary theory of truth](wikipedia://en/601131). His most influential work produced in this period is probably "Theory Change and the Indeterminacy of Reference" (*[Journal of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Philosophy)*, **70**(14): 462–481), in which he introduced the concept of partial [denotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denotation).

In the 1980s, Field started a project in the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439) in support of [mathematical fictionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_fictionalism), the doctrine that all mathematical statements are merely useful fictions, and should not be taken to be literally true. More precisely, Field aimed to produce reconstructions of science that would remove all reference to mathematical entities, hence showing that mathematics is dispensable to science in opposition to the [Quine–Putnam indispensability argument](wikipedia://en/58885745).[^o14]

Much of his current work is in semantic [paradoxes](wikipedia://en/24390). In 2008, he gave the John Locke Lectures, entitled "Logic, Normativity, and Rational Revisability".[^o15]
