# Split exact sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Split exact sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **split exact sequence** is a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) in which the middle term is built out of the two outer terms in the simplest possible way.

:::

## Equivalent characterizations {#Equivalent_characterizations}

A short exact sequence of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) or of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over a fixed [ring](wikipedia://en/48404), or more generally of objects in an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063)

$$0 \to A \mathrel{\stackrel{a}{\to}} B \mathrel{\stackrel{b}{\to}} C \to 0$$

is called split exact if it is isomorphic to the exact sequence where the middle term is the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of the outer ones:

$$0 \to A \mathrel{\stackrel{i}{\to}} A \oplus C \mathrel{\stackrel{p}{\to}} C \to 0$$

with $i: A \to A \oplus C$ being the natural inclusion of *A* into the direct sum, and $p: A \oplus C \to C$ denoting the natural projection of the direct sum onto the second summand. The requirement that the sequence is isomorphic means that there is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) $f : B \to A \oplus C$ such that the composite $f \circ a$ is the natural inclusion $i: A \to A \oplus C$ and such that the composite $p \circ f$ equals *b*. This can be summarized by a [commutative diagram](wikipedia://en/138484) as:

*Image: Commutative diagram for split exact sequence - fixed.svg.*

The [splitting lemma](wikipedia://en/138378) provides further equivalent characterizations of split exact sequences. The sequence

$$0 \to A \mathrel{\stackrel{a}{\to}} B \mathrel{\stackrel{b}{\to}} C \to 0$$

is split exact if and only if there exists $r : C \to B$ such that $b \circ r = 1_C$, which is the case if and only if there exists $s : B \to A$ such that $s \circ a = 1_A$.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

A trivial example of a split short exact sequence is

$$0 \to M_1 \mathrel{\stackrel{q}{\to}} M_1\oplus M_2 \mathrel{\stackrel{p}{\to}} M_2 \to 0$$

where $M_1, M_2$ are *R*-modules, $q$ is the canonical injection and $p$ is the canonical projection.

Any short exact sequence of [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) is split exact. This is a rephrasing of the fact that any [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent) vectors in a vector space can be extended to a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420).

The exact sequence $0 \to \mathbf{Z}\mathrel{\stackrel{2}{\to}} \mathbf{Z}\to \mathbf{Z}/ 2\mathbf{Z} \to 0$ (where the first map is multiplication by 2) is not split exact.

:::

## Related notions {#Related_notions}

[Pure exact sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_exact_sequence) can be characterized as the [filtered colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_colimit) of split exact sequences.[^o6]

## Terminology in more general contexts {#Terminology_in_more_general_contexts}

In the context of short exact sequences of (not necessarily abelian) groups or in other settings, the term **split exact sequence** is used in two different ways by different people. Some people mean a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) that right-splits (thus corresponding to a [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246)) and some people mean a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) that left-splits (which implies it right-splits, and corresponds to a [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015)).
