# E. H. Moore

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="E. H. Moore"}

**Eliakim Hastings Moore** (; January 26, 1862 – December 30, 1932), usually cited as **E. H. Moore** or **E. Hastings Moore**, was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Moore, the son of a Methodist minister and grandson of US Congressman [Eliakim H. Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliakim_H._Moore), discovered mathematics through a summer job at the [Cincinnati Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Observatory) while in high school. He subsequently studied mathematics at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), where he was a member of [Skull and Bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skull_and_Bones)[^o4] and obtained a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1883 and the [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in 1885 with a thesis supervised by [Hubert Anson Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Anson_Newton), on some work of [William Kingdon Clifford](wikipedia://en/250820) and [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034). Newton encouraged Moore to study in Germany, and thus he spent an academic year at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin), attending lectures by [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450) and [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459).

On his return to the United States, Moore taught at Yale and at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University). When the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) opened its doors in 1892, Moore was the first head of its mathematics department, a position he retained until his death in 1932. His first two colleagues were [Oskar Bolza](wikipedia://en/10217078) and [Heinrich Maschke](wikipedia://en/33372083). The resulting department was the second research-oriented mathematics department in American history, after [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University).

## Accomplishments {#Accomplishments}

Moore first worked in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), proving in 1893 the classification of the structure of [finite fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_fields) (also called [Galois fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_field)). Around 1900, he began working on the [foundations of geometry](wikipedia://en/21317755). He reformulated [Hilbert's axioms](wikipedia://en/838151) for geometry so that [points](wikipedia://en/593693) were the only [primitive notion](wikipedia://en/23706), thus turning [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)'s primitive lines and planes into defined notions. In 1902, he further showed that one of Hilbert's axioms for geometry was redundant. His work on axiom systems is considered one of the starting points for [metamathematics](wikipedia://en/183508) and [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858). After 1906, he turned to the foundations of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). The concept of a [closure operator](wikipedia://en/483120) first appeared in his 1910 *Introduction to a form of general analysis*.[^o6] He also wrote on [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and [integral equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_equations).[^o7]

At Chicago, Moore supervised 31 doctoral dissertations, including those of [George Birkhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Birkhoff), [Leonard Dickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Dickson), [Robert Lee Moore](wikipedia://en/1077133) (no relation), and [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen). Birkhoff and Veblen went on to lead departments at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), respectively. Dickson became the first great American algebraist and number theorist. Robert Moore founded American topology. According to the [Mathematics Genealogy Project](wikipedia://en/1152126), as of December 2023, E. H. Moore had 29,982 known "descendants."

Moore convinced the [New York Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mathematical_Society) to change its name to the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), whose Chicago branch he led. He presided over the AMS, 1901–02, and edited the *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)*, 1899–1907. He was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences), the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society). He was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1908 in Rome and in 1912 in Cambridge, England.

In 2002, the American Mathematical Society established the [E. H. Moore Research Article Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._H._Moore_Research_Article_Prize) in his honor.
