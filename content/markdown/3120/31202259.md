# Affine sphere

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Affine sphere"}

In [affine differential geometry](wikipedia://en/14222703), an **affine sphere** is a [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) for which the [affine normals](wikipedia://en/14222703#The_affine_normal_line) all intersect in a single point The term affine sphere is used because they play an analogous role in affine differential geometry to that of ordinary spheres in Euclidean differential geometry.

:::

An affine sphere is called **improper** or **parabolic** if all of the affine normals are constant.[^o2] In that case, the intersection point mentioned above lies on the [hyperplane at infinity](wikipedia://en/713946). If it is not improper, then it is **proper**. A proper sphere is **elliptic** iff its **mean affine curvature** $H > 0$, and **hyperbolic** iff $H < 0$.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- All [quadrics](wikipedia://en/145570) are affine spheres.
- Under technical assumptions, the only parabolic affine sphere is the elliptic paraboloid, and the only elliptic affine sphere is the ellipsoid.[^o5]
- In $\mathbb{R}^n$, the hyper surface $\prod_{i=1}^n x_i = 1$ is a hyperbolic affine sphere centered at the origin, even though it is not a quadric.
- If ƒ is a [smooth function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) on the plane and the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the [Hessian matrix](wikipedia://en/412108) is ±1 then the graph of ƒ in three-space is an improper affine sphere.[^o6]

:::

## Monge–Ampère equation {#Monge–Ampère_equation}

The graph of a locally strictly convex function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is a hypersurface $M \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ .

Then, $M$ is a affine sphere centered at the origin or infinity iff it solves

$$\det [ \partial^2_{ij} f ] = \begin{cases}(H f)^{-n-2}, & \text { if } H \neq 0 \\ 1, & \text { if } H=0\end{cases}$$

for some $H \geq 0$. If it is, then $H$ is the mean affine curvature of $M$. This equation is an elliptic [Monge–Ampère equation](wikipedia://en/4914066).[^o8] This produces a very strong constraint on affine spheres. By a result due to Jörgens, Calabi, and Pogorelov, (under some technical hypotheses) the only improper affine sphere is an elliptic paraboloid, and the only elliptic affine sphere is an ellipsoid.

### Hyperbolic case {#Hyperbolic_case}

Hyperbolic affine spheres are much more interesting and less well-understood. Similarly, hyperbolic Monge–Ampère equations are also less well-understood.

Most examples are known only implicitly, in the sense that they are proven to exist, without explicit formulas describing them.

The following theorem was conjectured by Calabi and proven by Cheng and Yau:[^o10]

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

Every [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesically_complete), *n*-dimensional affine sphere with mean curvature $H< 0$ is asymptotic to the boundary of a convex cone with vertex at the center. Every uniformly convex cone $K$ determines an affine sphere of hyperbolic type, which is asymptotic to the cone $K$, and uniquely determined by the mean curvature.

:::

In fact, Chern and Yau proved more, that these hyperbolic spheres come in families. Define a **sharp cone** in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ to be a closed subset $K \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ such that it is a union of rays leaving the origin, and such that it contains no full-line through the origin. Equivalently, it means that there exists a [supporting plane](wikipedia://en/28752383) of $K$ that touches $K$ only at the origin.

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

If $K \subset \mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ is a sharp and convex cone, then its interior has a unique foliation into $\cup_{r > 0} L_r$ such that each $L_r$ is a hyperbolic affine sphere of mean curvature $-r^2$.

Equivalently, consider the Monge–Ampère equation $\det [ \partial^2_{ij} f ] = e^{2f}$ for $f : \operatorname{int}(K) \to \mathbb{R}$, with conditions

$f \to +\infty$ as $x \to \partial K$;

$\partial_{ij}^2 f dx^i dx^j$ is a [complete](wikipedia://en/6875557) Riemannian metric.

Then it has a unique solution, whose level sets are the foliation $\cup_{r > 0} L_r$.

:::

The construction is implicit. Explicit representation is generally unknown, even in the case where $K$ is a [polyhedral cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_cone).[^o8]
