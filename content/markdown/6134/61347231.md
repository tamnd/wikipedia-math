# List of mathematician-politicians

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of mathematician-politicians"}

Historians of mathematics have noted the involvement of prominent mathematicians in politics at various times and places, notably in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) during the period of [unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_unification) at the end of the nineteenth century.[^o2]

:::

Those who become legislators attempt to use their mathematical skills to legitimise their political positions.[^o2] However, some parliamentary colleagues tend to view them as failing to connect with the real world.[^o2]

A maths columnist for *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* suggested in 2018 that mathematicians in politics would contribute strengths including problem-solving, creativity, overcoming challenges, and collaboration.[^o3]

[John Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Derbyshire) observed in 2003 that mathematicians have no dominant tendency; for example, [Cauchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy) was a [reactionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactionary) whereas [Galois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois) was a [radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_radicalism).[^o4] He opines that the most influential research mathematicians do not give much thought to politics.[^o5]
