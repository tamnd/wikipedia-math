# Poincaré space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Poincaré space"}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), a **Poincaré space** is an *n*-dimensional [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) with a distinguished element *μ* of its *n*th [homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) such that taking the [cap product](wikipedia://en/5230810) with an element of the *k*th [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) group yields an isomorphism to the (*n* − *k*)th homology group. The space is essentially one for which [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) is valid; more precisely, one whose singular chain complex forms a [Poincaré complex](wikipedia://en/28277010) with respect to the distinguished element *μ*.

:::

For example, any closed, orientable, connected manifold *M* is a Poincaré space, where the distinguished element is the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) $[M].$

Poincaré spaces are used in [surgery theory](wikipedia://en/5961115) to analyze and classify manifolds. Not every Poincaré space is a manifold, but the difference can be studied, first by having a [normal map](wikipedia://en/21298614) from a manifold, and then via [obstruction theory](wikipedia://en/2179639).

## Other uses {#Other_uses}

Sometimes,[^o4] *Poincaré space* means a [homology sphere](wikipedia://en/585388) with non-trivial [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004)—for instance, the Poincaré dodecahedral space in 3 dimensions.
