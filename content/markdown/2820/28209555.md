# R. Leonard Brooks

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="R. Leonard Brooks"}

**Rowland Leonard Brooks** (February 6, 1916 – June 18, 1993)[^o3] was an English mathematician, known for proving [Brooks's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooks's_theorem) on the relation between the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) and the [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) of [graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph). He was born in [Lincolnshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincolnshire), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), studied at [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University), and also worked with fellow Trinity students [W. T. Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021), [Cedric Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedric_Smith_%28statistician%29), and [Arthur Harold Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Harold_Stone) on the problem of "[Squaring the square](wikipedia://en/67903)" (partitioning [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048) and [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) into unequal squares), both under their own names and under the [pseudonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudonym) [Blanche Descartes](wikipedia://en/26959280).[^o4]

:::

After leaving Cambridge, he worked as a full-time tax inspector.[^o3]
