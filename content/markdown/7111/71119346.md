# Hidegorō Nakano

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hidegorō Nakano"}

**Hidegorō Nakano** (中野 秀五郎; 16 May 1909 – 11 March 1974) is a Japanese mathematician,[^o3] after whom Nakano Spaces are named.[^o4]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Nakano was born as the first son of Katsugoro Nakano and Kame Nakano, in Tokyo. After graduating from National First High School, a preparatory school for the [Imperial University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_University_of_Tokyo), he progressed to study mathematics in Tokyo Imperial University and graduated with a Bachelor of Science in 1933. Then he entered Graduate School at the same university under the supervision of Takuji Yoshie, and attained his doctoral degree in 1935. At that time, a doctorate was more commonly awarded to people over 50 years old.[^o6]

Nakano started teaching in The National First High School in 1935. At the same year he married Sumiko Yamamura (11 December 1913, Tokyo - 5 March 1999, Detroit). Then he held academic positions (1938-1952) in Tokyo Imperial University, before moving to [Hokkaido University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaido_University) and being appointed as a professor.[^o7]

In 1960, he left Japan and took a visit to [Queen's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston) in Canada for a year, under the invitation of [Canadian Mathematical Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Mathematical_Congress). He then took up professorship in [Wayne State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_State_University), Detroit, US, in 1961, and continued working there until his death in 1974.[^o7]

## Works {#Works}

### Nakano's name in mathematics {#Nakano's_name_in_mathematics}

Nakano is known for his research in [Functional Analysis](wikipedia://en/11180),[^o10] especially in [vector lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_lattice) and operator theory in [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932). He mainly made his name in his contribution to several mathematical subjects around [modulars](wikipedia://en/286000), [Riesz spaces](wikipedia://en/8099018), Orlicz-Nakano spaces and Nakano space.[^o6]
