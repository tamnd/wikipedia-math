# Continuous symmetry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Continuous symmetry"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **continuous symmetry** is an intuitive idea corresponding to the concept of viewing some [symmetries](wikipedia://en/2714149) as [motions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_%28physics%29), as opposed to [discrete symmetry](wikipedia://en/1372580), e.g. [reflection symmetry](wikipedia://en/1390873), which is invariant under a kind of flip from one state to another. However, a discrete symmetry can always be reinterpreted as a subset of some higher-dimensional continuous symmetry, e.g. reflection of a 2-dimensional object in 3-dimensional space can be achieved by continuously rotating that object 180 degrees across a non-parallel plane.

:::

## Formalization {#Formalization}

The notion of continuous symmetry has largely and successfully been formalised in the mathematical notions of a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315), [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) and [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29). For most practical purposes, continuous symmetry is modelled by a *group action* of a topological group that preserves some structure. Particularly, let $f:X\to Y$ be a function, and $G$ is a group that acts on $X$; then a subgroup $H\subseteq G$ is a symmetry of $f$ if $f(h\cdot x) = f(x)$ for all $h\in H$.

### One-parameter subgroups {#One-parameter_subgroups}

The simplest motions follow a [one-parameter subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-parameter_subgroup) of a Lie group, such as the [Euclidean group](wikipedia://en/652164) of [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853). For example [translation](wikipedia://en/204682) parallel to the *x*-axis by *u* units, as *u* varies, is a one-parameter group of motions. Rotation around the *z*-axis is also a one-parameter group.

## Noether's theorem {#Noether's_theorem}

Continuous symmetry has a basic role in [Noether's theorem](wikipedia://en/150159) in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), in the derivation of [conservation laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_law_%28physics%29) from symmetry principles, specifically for continuous symmetries. The search for continuous symmetries only intensified with the further developments of [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267).

:::
