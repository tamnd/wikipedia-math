# Phillip Griffiths

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Phillip Griffiths"}

**Phillip Augustus Griffiths IV** (born October 18, 1938) is an American mathematician, known for his work in the field of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), and in particular for the [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) approach to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). He is a major developer in particular of the theory of [variation of Hodge structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variation_of_Hodge_structure) in [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590) and [moduli theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_theory), which forms part of transcendental algebraic geometry and which also touches upon major and distant areas of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). He also worked on partial differential equations, coauthored with [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905), [Robert Bryant](wikipedia://en/24204031) and [Robert Gardner](wikipedia://en/47107406) on exterior differential systems.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Griffiths received his BS from [Wake Forest College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_College) in 1959.[^o4] He received his PhD from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1962 after completing a doctoral dissertation, titled "On certain homogeneous [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742)", under the supervision of [Donald Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_C._Spencer).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Afterwards, he held positions at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) (1962–1967) and Princeton University (1967–1972). Griffiths was a professor of mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) from 1972 to 1983.[^o7] He was then a Provost and James B. Duke Professor of Mathematics at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) from 1983 to 1991.[^o7] From 1991 to 2003, he was the Director of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (IAS) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey).[^o7] He remained as part of the Faculty of Mathematics at the IAS until June 2009, after which he has been emeritus at the IAS.[^o7] He has published on algebraic geometry, [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [geometric function theory](wikipedia://en/5458932), and the geometry of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations).

Griffiths serves as the Chair of the [Science Initiative Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Initiative_Group).[^o7] He is co-author, with [Joe Harris](wikipedia://en/3610251), of *Principles of Algebraic Geometry*, a well-regarded textbook on [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).[^o8]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Griffiths was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1979 and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1992.[^o10][^o11] In 2008, he was awarded the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) (jointly with [Deligne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deligne) and [Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594))[^o12] and the [Brouwer Medal](wikipedia://en/42578624).[^o13] In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o14] Moreover, in 2014 Griffiths was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Lifetime Achievement by the American Mathematical Society.[^o15][^o16] Also in 2014, Griffiths was awarded the [Chern Medal](wikipedia://en/26347082) for lifetime devotion to mathematics and outstanding achievements by the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868).[^o17]
