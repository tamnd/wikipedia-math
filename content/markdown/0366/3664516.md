# Jonathan Lear

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jonathan Lear"}

**Jonathan David Lear** (October 9, 1948 – September 22, 2025) was an American philosopher and psychoanalyst. He was the [John U. Nef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ulric_Nef_%28economic_historian%29) Distinguished Service Professor in the [Committee on Social Thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_on_Social_Thought) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) and served as the Roman Family Director of the [Neubauer Collegium for Culture and Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neubauer_Collegium_for_Culture_and_Society) from 2014 to 2022.[^o3]

:::

## Background and career {#Background_and_career}

Jonathan David Lear was born in New York City on October 9, 1948.[^o5] His father and [Norman Lear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Lear) were first cousins.[^o5] Lear was brought up in [West Hartford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Hartford,_Connecticut).[^o5]

Lear earned his B.A. (cum laude) in history at [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale) in 1970 and his B.A. in philosophy at [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1973.[^o5] He then received his Ph.D. in philosophy at [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University) with a dissertation on Aristotle's logic directed by [Saul Kripke](wikipedia://en/48039).[^o5] He also trained at the [Western New England Institute for Psychoanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_England_Institute_for_Psychoanalysis) in 1995. He subsequently won the Gradiva Award from the National Association for Psychoanalysis three times for work that advances psychoanalysis.

Before moving to Chicago permanently in 1996, Lear taught philosophy at Cambridge University (1979-1985), where he was a fellow and the director of studies in philosophy of [Clare College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge). He also taught philosophy at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) and was chair of the department of philosophy (1978–79, 1985–1996). He is a member of the [International Psychoanalytical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Psychoanalytical_Association). In 2009, he received the Mellon Distinguished Achievement Award in the Humanities.[^o6]

During his time as the Roman Family Director of the Neubauer Collegium for Culture and Society was able to work with the [Apsáalooke Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crow_people) and the [Field Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Museum_of_Natural_History) to sponsor the exhibit *Apsáalooke Women and Warriors*.[^o7]

In 2017, he was elected a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o8] He was elected a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 2019.[^o9]

Lear was first married to Cynthia Farrar, with whom he had a daughter before divorcing.[^o5] In 2003, he married fellow academic Gabriel Richardson, with whom he had a son.[^o5] Jonathan Lear died from [stomach cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach_cancer) at his home in Chicago on September 22, 2025, at the age of 76.[^o5][^o10]

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

Lear's early work focused on formal logic and ancient Greek philosophy. Much of his work involves the intersection of [psychoanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoanalysis) and philosophy. In addition to work involving [Sigmund Freud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmund_Freud), he also wrote widely on [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308), [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954), [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant), [Søren Kierkegaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Søren_Kierkegaard) and [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741), focusing on ideas of the human psyche. His most recent work explores the ethical task of managing to live with the fears and anxieties of world-catastrophe.[^o12][^o13]

Lear argues that mourning is a central human practice through which we confront transience, reclaim meaning after loss, and shape how we live ethically in a finite world.[^o14]
