# Sphere bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sphere bundle"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a **sphere bundle** is a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) in which the fibers are [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) $S^n$ of some dimension *n*.[^o2] Similarly, in a disk bundle, the fibers are [disks](wikipedia://en/329542) $D^n$. From a topological perspective, there is no difference between sphere bundles and disk bundles: this is a consequence of the [Alexander trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_trick), which implies $\operatorname{BTop}(D^{n+1}) \simeq \operatorname{BTop}(S^n).$

:::

An example of a sphere bundle is the torus, which is [orientable](wikipedia://en/187446) and has $S^1$ fibers over an $S^1$ base space. The non-orientable [Klein bottle](wikipedia://en/17412) also has $S^1$ fibers over an $S^1$ base space, but has a twist that produces a reversal of orientation as one follows the loop around the base space.[^o2]

A [circle bundle](wikipedia://en/2794417) is a special case of a sphere bundle.

## Orientation of a sphere bundle {#Orientation_of_a_sphere_bundle}

A sphere bundle that is a product space is orientable, as is any sphere bundle over a simply connected space.[^o2]

If *E* be a real vector bundle on a space *X* and if *E* is given an [orientation](wikipedia://en/44844703), then a sphere bundle formed from *E*, Sph(*E*), inherits the orientation of *E*.

## Spherical fibration {#Spherical_fibration}

A **spherical fibration**, a generalization of the concept of a sphere bundle, is a [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826) whose fibers are [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) to spheres. For example, the fibration

$$\operatorname{BTop}(\mathbb{R}^n) \to \operatorname{BTop}(S^n)$$

has fibers homotopy equivalent to *S*<sup>*n*</sup>.[^o5]
