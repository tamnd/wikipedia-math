# Joseph R. Shoenfield

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph R. Shoenfield"}

**Joseph Robert Shoenfield** (May 1, 1927, in [Detroit, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit,_Michigan) – November 15, 2000, in [Durham, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina)) was an American mathematical logician.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Shoenfield obtained his PhD in 1953 with [Raymond Louis Wilder](wikipedia://en/4297377) at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) (*Models of formal systems*).

## Career {#Career}

From 1952, he lectured at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University), where he remained until becoming emeritus in 1992. From 1970 to 1973 he was president of the mathematics faculty. In 1956/57 he was at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). Shoenfield worked on [recursion theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion_theory), [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858) and axiomatic [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553). His textbook on mathematical logic has become a classic.[^o6]

## Honors {#Honors}

From 1972 to 1976 he was president of the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438). He delivered the Gödel Lecture at the 1992 meeting of the ASL.[^o8]

## Hobbies {#Hobbies}

In his student days, Schoenfield was a [contract bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_bridge) player. He was an early member[^o10] of the [American Go Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Go_Association); the memorial tournament in North Carolina was founded in his memory.[^o11]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- Mathematical Logic, Addison Wesley 1967, 2nd edition, Association for Symbolic Logic, 2001[^o13]
- Degrees of unsolvability, North Holland Mathematical Studies 1971
- Recursion theory, Springer 1993[^o14]
