# Supporting line

*Image: Reuleaux supporting lines.svg.* Parallel supporting lines of a [Reuleaux triangle](wikipedia://en/580252)

*Image: 00-02 Tau - Square - Reuleaux Wikipedia.gif.* Animation of parallel supporting lines around a Reuleaux triangle.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Supporting line"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **supporting line** *L* of a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) *C* in the plane is a line that contains a point of *C*, but does not separate any two points of *C*.[^o2] In other words, *C* lies completely in one of the two [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [half-planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-plane) defined by *L* and has at least one point on *L*.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

There can be many supporting lines for a curve at a given point. When a [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) exists at a given point, then it is the unique supporting line at this point, if it does not separate the curve.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The notion of supporting line is also discussed for planar shapes. In this case a supporting line may be defined as a line which has common points with the boundary of the shape, but not with its interior.[^o7]

The notion of a supporting line to a planar curve or convex shape can be generalized to n dimension as a [supporting hyperplane](wikipedia://en/4165181).

:::

## Critical support lines {#Critical_support_lines}

If two bounded connected planar shapes have disjoint [convex hulls](wikipedia://en/40634) that are separated by a positive distance, then they necessarily have exactly four common lines of support, the [bitangents](wikipedia://en/2605212) of the two convex hulls. Two of these lines of support separate the two shapes, and are called **critical support lines**.[^o7] Without the assumption of convexity, there may be more or fewer than four lines of support, even if the shapes themselves are disjoint. For instance, if one shape is an [annulus](wikipedia://en/356158) that contains the other, then there are no common lines of support, while if each of two shapes consists of a pair of small disks at opposite corners of a square then there may be as many as 16 common lines of support.
