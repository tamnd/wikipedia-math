# Brown–Peterson cohomology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brown–Peterson cohomology"}

In mathematics, **Brown–Peterson cohomology** is a [generalized cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_cohomology_theory) introduced by (txt), depending on a choice of prime *p*. It is described in detail by (txt, Chapter 4). Its representing [spectrum](wikipedia://en/1033697) is denoted by $\text{BP}$.

:::

## Complex cobordism and Quillen's idempotent {#Complex_cobordism_and_Quillen's_idempotent}

Brown–Peterson cohomology $\text{BP}$ is a [summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summand) of MU<sub>($p$)</sub>, which is [complex cobordism](wikipedia://en/1033666) MU [localized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_space) at a prime $p$. In fact MU<sub>($p$)</sub> is a [wedge product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedge_product) of [suspensions](wikipedia://en/1251702) of $\text{BP}$.

For each prime $p$, [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519) showed there is a unique [idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent) map of [ring spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_spectra) ε from MUQ<sub>($p$)</sub> to itself, with the property that $\epsilon_*([CP^n])$ is \[CP<sup>$n$</sup>\] if $n+1$ is a power of $p$, and $0$ otherwise. The spectrum $\text{BP}$ is the image of this idempotent ε.

## Structure of $\text{BP}$ {#Structure_of_\text{BP}}

The coefficient ring $\pi_*(\text{BP})$ is a polynomial algebra over $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ on generators $v_n$ in degrees $2(p^n-1)$ for $n\ge 1$.

$\text{BP}_*(\text{BP})$ is isomorphic to the polynomial ring $\pi_*(\text{BP})[t_1, t_2, \ldots]$ over $\pi_*(\text{BP})$ with generators $t_i$ in $\text{BP}_{2 (p^i-1)}(\text{BP})$ of degrees $2 (p^i-1)$.

The cohomology of the [Hopf algebroid](wikipedia://en/31530123) $(\pi_*(\text{BP}), \text{BP}_*(\text{BP}))$ is the initial term of the [Adams–Novikov spectral sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams–Novikov_spectral_sequence) for calculating $p$-local [homotopy groups of spheres](wikipedia://en/1104697).

$\text{BP}$ is the universal example of a complex oriented cohomology theory whose associated formal group law is $p$-typical.
