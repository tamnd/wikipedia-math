# CC system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CC system"}

In [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), a **CC system** or **counterclockwise system** is a [ternary relation](wikipedia://en/3784565) $pqr$ introduced by [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095) to model the clockwise ordering of triples of points in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410).[^n1]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Axioms"}

## Axioms {#Axioms}

A CC system is required to satisfy the following axioms, for all distinct points *p*, *q*, *r*, *s*, and *t*:[^n2]

1. Cyclic symmetry: If $pqr$ then $qrp$.
1. Antisymmetry: If $pqr$ then not $prq$.
1. Nondegeneracy: Either $pqr$ or $prq$.
1. Interiority: If $tqr$ and $ptr$ and $pqt$, then $pqr$.
1. Transitivity: If $tsp$ and $tsq$ and $tsr$, and $tpq$ and $tqr$, then $tpr$.

Triples of points that are not distinct are not considered as part of the relation.

:::

## Construction from planar point sets {#Construction_from_planar_point_sets}

A CC system may be defined from any set of points in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), with no three of the points collinear, by including in the relation a triple $pqr$ of distinct points whenever the triple lists these three points in counterclockwise order around the triangle that they form. Using the [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate) of the points, the triple *pqr* is included in the relation exactly when[^n3]

$$\det\left( \begin{array}{ccc} x_p & y_p & 1 \\ x_q & y_q & 1 \\ x_r & y_r & 1 \end{array} \right) > 0.$$

The condition that the points are in general position is equivalent to the requirement that this matrix [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) is never zero for distinct points *p*, *q*, and *r*.

However, not every CC system comes from a Euclidean point set in this way.[^n4]

## Equivalent notions {#Equivalent_notions}

CC systems can also be defined from [pseudoline arrangements](wikipedia://en/864149), or from [sorting networks](wikipedia://en/562061) in which the compare-exchange operations only compare adjacent pairs of elements (as in for instance [bubble sort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_sort)), and every CC system can be defined in this way.[^n5] This relation is not one-to-one, but the numbers of nonisomorphic CC systems on *n* points, of pseudoline arrangements with *n* lines, and of sorting networks on *n* values, are within polynomial factors of each other.[^n6]

There exists a two-to-one correspondence between CC systems and uniform acyclic [oriented matroids](wikipedia://en/24338041) of [rank](wikipedia://en/37123010) 3.[^n7] These matroids in turn have a 1-1 correspondence to topological equivalence classes of pseudoline arrangements with one marked cell.[^n6]

## Algorithmic applications {#Algorithmic_applications}

The information given by a CC system is sufficient to define a notion of a [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) within a CC system. The convex hull is the set of ordered pairs *pq* of distinct points with the property that, for every third distinct point *r*, *pqr* belongs to the system. It forms a cycle, with the property that every three points of the cycle, in the same cyclic order, belong to the system.[^n8] By adding points one at a time to a CC system, and maintaining the convex hull of the points added so far in its cyclic order using a [binary search tree](wikipedia://en/4320), it is possible to construct the convex hull in time *O*(*n* log *n*), matching the known time bounds for [convex hull algorithms](wikipedia://en/11700432) for Euclidean points.[^n9]

It is also possible to find a single convex hull vertex, as well as the combinatorial equivalent of a bisecting line through a system of points, from a CC system in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time). The construction of an extreme vertex allows the [Graham scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_scan) algorithm for convex hulls to be generalized from point sets to CC systems, with a number of queries to the CC system that matches (to within lower-order terms) the number of comparisons needed in [comparison sorting](wikipedia://en/3189304).[^n10]

## Combinatorial enumeration {#Combinatorial_enumeration}

The number of non-isomorphic CC systems on *n* points is[^n6][^n11]

1, 1, 1, 2, 3, 20, 242, 6405, 316835, 28627261 ...

These numbers grow exponentially in *n*<sup>2</sup>;[^n12] in contrast, the number of realizable CC systems grows exponentially only in Θ(*n* log *n*).[^n7]

More precisely, the number *C<sub>n</sub>* of non-isomorphic CC systems on *n* points is at most[^n13]

$$3^{\binom{n}{2}}.$$

Knuth conjectures more strongly that these numbers obey the recursive inequality

$$C_n\le n2^{n-2} C_{n-1}.$$
