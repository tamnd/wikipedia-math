# Grace Andrews (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Grace Andrews"}

**Grace Andrews** (May 30, 1869 – July 27, 1951) was an American mathematician. She, along with [Charlotte Angas Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Angas_Scott), was one of only two women listed in the first edition of *[American Men of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Men_and_Women_of_Science)*, which appeared in 1906.[^o3]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Andrews was one of five children of [Edward Gayer Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Gayer_Andrews), a Methodist Episcopal bishop and school administrator; she was born in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), and moved frequently as a child, including stays in Ohio, Iowa, [Washington DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.), and Europe. She was a student at [Mount Vernon Seminary and College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon_Seminary_and_College), and obtained her undergraduate degree from [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College) in 1890, taking a five-year program at Wellesley that also included music.[^o7]

She went to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) for graduate study, earned an A.M. in 1899, and completed a Ph.D. in 1901. Her dissertation was *The Primitive Double Minimal Surface of the Seventh Class and its Conjugate*.

## Career {#Career}

She worked as an Assistant Professor of Mathematics for [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College) from 1900 to 1902. She then served as accountant to the Treasurer for [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University) from 1903 to 1926, working from her home in Brooklyn. She was also an executive in various capacities for the New York branch of the [Woman's Foreign Missionary Society of the Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman's_Foreign_Missionary_Society_of_the_Methodist_Episcopal_Church).[^o7]
