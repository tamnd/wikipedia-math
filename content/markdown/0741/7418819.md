# Thurston–Bennequin number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thurston–Bennequin number"}

In the [mathematical theory of knots](wikipedia://en/153008), the **Thurston–Bennequin number**, or **Bennequin number**, is an invariant associated with a [Legendrian knot](wikipedia://en/7404755) in a three dimensional [contact manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_manifold). It is named after [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190) and [Daniel Bennequin](wikipedia://en/48247312). The Thurston-Bennequin number measures the "twisting of the contact structure around the knot".[^o9] Together with the [rotation number](wikipedia://en/3928569), they are often referred as the "classical" invariants of [Legendrian knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legendrian_knots).

:::

The Thurston-Bennequin number of a Legendrian knot $K$ is usually denoted by $\mathrm{tb}(K)$. The **maximal Thurston–Bennequin number**, $\overline{\mathrm{tb}}(K)$, over all Legendrian representatives of a knot in $\mathbb R^3$ is a topological [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898).[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition and properties"}

## Definition and properties {#Definition_and_properties}

Let $K$ be a null-homologous oriented [Legendrian knot](wikipedia://en/7404755) in a co-oriented three-dimensional [contact manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_manifold) $(M^3,\xi)$ and fix a [Seifert surface](wikipedia://en/1343951) $\Sigma$ to $K$, that is an embedded connected, compact, orientable surface with boundary $\partial \Sigma = K$. The Thurston-Bennequin number of $K$ relative to $\Sigma$ is the defined as the signed [intersection number](wikipedia://en/384327) of the contact plane field $\xi$ with $\Sigma$.[^o5]

Let $K'$ be a small push-off of $K$ obtained by pushing along a vector field $v$ transverse to $\xi$. The Thurston-Bennequin number can also be defined as $\mathrm{lk}(K,K')$, where $\mathrm{lk}$ denotes the [linking number](wikipedia://en/451999).[^o5]

### The Euclidean case {#The_Euclidean_case}

We consider the case where $(M,\xi)=(\mathbb{R}^3,\xi_{\mathrm{std}})$ is [the standard contact structure](wikipedia://en/476398#The_standard_contact_structure) on $\mathbb{R}^3$. If we denote $(x,y,z)$ the coordinates in $\mathbb{R}^3$, the contact structure $\xi_{\mathrm{std}}$ is the kernel of the one-form $dz-ydx$. The applications $\Pi \colon \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2, (x,y,z) \mapsto (x,z)$ and $\pi_L \colon \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2, (x,y,z) \mapsto (x,y)$ denote respectively the **front projection** and the **Lagrangian projection**. The Thurston-Bennequin number can be computed easily from its front and Lagrangian projections.

#### Lagrangian projection description {#Lagrangian_projection_description}

The Thurston-Bennequin number of a Legendrian knot $K \subset \mathbb R^3$ is the [writhe](wikipedia://en/968591) of its Lagrangian projection $\pi_L(K)$.

#### Front projection description {#Front_projection_description}

For a Legendrian knot $K \subset \mathbb{R}^3$, its front projection $\Pi(K)\subset \mathbb R^2$ is called its **front diagram**. The front diagram of a Legendrian knot does not have vertical tangencies, however [cusps](wikipedia://en/6851758) can appear. Generically, the front diagram of a knot as no [tangency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangency) point, no triple intersection and standard [cusp](wikipedia://en/6851758) singularities. In this case the Thurston-Bennequin number is

$$\mathrm{tb}(K)=\textrm{writhe}(\Pi(K))-\dfrac{1}{2}\big(\# \text{number of cusps}\big),$$

where $\textrm{writhe}(\Pi(K))$ denotes the [writhe](wikipedia://en/968591) of the front diagram.[^o9]

The invariant can also be computed using a **grid diagram** corresponding to a particular Legendrian representative of a knot.[^o10][^o11] In this setting, the number can be computed as the writhe of the diagram minus the number of 'northwest' corners.

*Image: Grid-diagram-to-Legendrian-conversion.svg.* A grid diagram of the knot $8_{20}$ and an associated Legendrian representative of it.

By smoothing the 'northeast' and 'southwest' corners and rotating the diagram and switching all crossings, one can convert a grid diagram into the associated Legendrian knot.

:::

::: {#o13 .statement cue=named-heading name="The Bennequin inequality"}

## The Bennequin inequality {#The_Bennequin_inequality}

In his thesis [^o9], [Daniel Bennequin](wikipedia://en/48247312) proved an inequality involving the Thurston-Bennequin number. He proved that for all Legendrian knot $K$ in [the standard contact](wikipedia://en/476398#The_standard_contact_structure) $\mathbb R^3$ the following inequality is true:

$$\mathrm{tb}(K)+\vert \textrm{rot}(K) \vert \leq - \chi(\Sigma),$$

where $\chi(\Sigma)$ denotes the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of a [Seifert surface](wikipedia://en/1343951) $\Sigma$ of $K$ and $\textrm{rot}(K)$ denotes the [rotation number](wikipedia://en/3928569) of $K$.

In particular, the **maximal Thurston-Bennequin number** gives a lower bound on the [genus](wikipedia://en/1343951#Genus_of_a_knot) of a topological knot.
