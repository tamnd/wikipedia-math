# Jet group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jet group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **jet group** is a generalization of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) which applies to [Taylor polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_polynomial) instead of [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28mathematics%29) at a point. A jet group is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [jets](wikipedia://en/1521924) that describes how a Taylor polynomial transforms under changes of [coordinate systems](wikipedia://en/81931) (or, equivalently, [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564)).

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The *k*-th order **jet group** *G*<sup>*n*</sup><sub>*k*</sub> consists of [jets](wikipedia://en/1521924) of smooth diffeomorphisms φ: **R**<sup>*n*</sup> → **R**<sup>*n*</sup> such that φ(0)=0.[^o3]

The following is a more precise definition of the jet group.

Let *k* ≥ 2. The differential of a function *f:* **R**<sup>*k*</sup> → **R** can be interpreted as a section of the cotangent bundle of **R**<sup>*K*</sup> given by *df:* **R**<sup>*k*</sup> → *T\****R**<sup>*k*</sup>. Similarly, derivatives of order up to *m* are sections of the [jet bundle](wikipedia://en/928060) *J<sup>m</sup>*(**R**<sup>*k*</sup>) = **R**<sup>*k*</sup> × *W*, where

$$W = \mathbf R \times (\mathbf R^*)^k \times S^2( (\mathbf R^*)^k) \times \cdots \times S^{m} ( (\mathbf R^*)^k).$$

Here **R**\* is the [dual vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_vector_space) to **R**, and *S<sup>i</sup>* denotes the *i*-th [symmetric power](wikipedia://en/57047243). A smooth function *f:* **R**<sup>*k*</sup> → **R** has a prolongation *j<sup>m</sup>f*: **R**<sup>*k*</sup> → *J<sup>m</sup>*(**R**<sup>*k*</sup>) defined at each point *p* ∈ **R**<sup>*k*</sup> by placing the *i*-th partials of *f* at *p* in the *S<sup>i</sup>*((**R**\*)<sup>*k*</sup>) component of *W*.

Consider a point $p=(x,x')\in J^m(\mathbf R^n)$. There is a unique polynomial *f<sub>p</sub>* in *k* variables and of order *m* such that *p* is in the image of *j<sup>m</sup>f<sub>p</sub>*. That is, $j^k(f_p)(x)=x'$. The differential data *x′* may be transferred to lie over another point *y* ∈ **R**<sup>*n*</sup> as *j<sup>m</sup>f<sub>p</sub>(y)* , the partials of *f<sub>p</sub>* over *y*.

Provide *J<sup>m</sup>*(**R**<sup>*n*</sup>) with a group structure by taking

$$(x,x') * (y, y') = (x+y, j^mf_p(y) + y')$$

With this group structure, *J<sup>m</sup>*(**R**<sup>*n*</sup>) is a [Carnot group](wikipedia://en/32244169) of class *m* + 1.

Because of the properties of jets under [function composition](wikipedia://en/195947), *G*<sup>*n*</sup><sub>*k*</sub> is a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945). The jet group is a [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of the general linear group and a connected, simply connected [nilpotent Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilpotent_Lie_group). It is also in fact an [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924), since the composition involves only polynomial operations.
