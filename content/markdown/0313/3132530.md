# Nakai conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nakai conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Nakai conjecture** is an unproven characterization of [smooth algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_algebraic_variety), [conjectured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjectured) by Japanese mathematician Yoshikazu Nakai in 1961.[^o2] It states that if *V* is a [complex algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/39219687), such that its ring of [differential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_operators) is generated by the [derivations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28algebra%29) it contains, then *V* is a [smooth variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_algebraic_variety). The converse statement, that smooth algebraic varieties have rings of differential operators that are generated by their derivations, is a result of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042).[^o3]

:::

The Nakai conjecture is known to be true for [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260)[^o4] and [Stanley–Reisner rings](wikipedia://en/24394535).[^o5] A proof of the conjecture would also establish the **Zariski–Lipman conjecture**, for a complex variety *V* with [coordinate ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_ring) *R*. This conjecture states that if the derivations of *R* are a [free module](wikipedia://en/326454) over *R*, then *V* is smooth.[^o6]
