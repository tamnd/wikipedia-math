# Mihailo Petrović Alas

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mihailo Petrović Alas"}

**Mihailo Petrović Alas** (Михаило Петровић Алас; also **Michel Pétrovitch**; 6 May 1868 – 8 June 1943) was a [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia) [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematicians), inventor, university professor and academic. He was a distinguished professor at [Belgrade University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_University), an academic, [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), writer, publicist, musician, businessman, traveler and volunteer in the [Balkan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Wars), the First and Second World Wars and licensed [fisherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisherman) by trade – hence his nickname "Alas" ("fisherman on the rivers").

:::

He was a student of [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740), [Paul Painlevé](wikipedia://en/402504), [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422) and [Émile Picard](wikipedia://en/42000). Petrović contributed significantly to the study of [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations) and phenomenology,[^o3] founded [engineering mathematics](wikipedia://en/5381408) in Serbia,[^o4] and invented one of the first prototypes of a hydraulic [analog computer](wikipedia://en/2428).[^o5]

## Biography {#Biography}

Petrović was born on 6 May 1868, in [Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade), as the first child of Nikodim, a professor of theology, and Milica (née Lazarević). Petrović' father died when he was young, and his grandfather helped raise him.[^o7]

He finished the [First Belgrade Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Belgrade_Gymnasium) in 1885, and afterwards enrolled at the natural science-mathematical section of the Faculty of Philosophy in Belgrade. At the time when he finished his studies in Serbia in 1889, several Serbian mathematicians who had acquired their doctorate degrees abroad, like Dr. [Dimitrije Nešić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrije_Nešić) (at [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866) and [Karlsruhe Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Institute_of_Technology)), Dr. Dimitrije Danić (at [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jena), 1885) and [Bogdan Gavrilović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogdan_Gavrilović) (at [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest), 1887) were beginning to make a name for themselves.[^o7] Subsequently, in September 1889, he too went abroad, to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) to receive further education, and to prepare for the entrance exam to the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure).[^o7] He got a degree in mathematical sciences from [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) University in 1891. He worked on preparing his doctoral dissertation, and on 21 June 1894 he defended his PhD degree at the Sorbonne, and received a title *Docteur des sciences mathematiques* (doctor of mathematical sciences). His doctorate was in the field of [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations).[^o7]

*Image: Djoprdje and mika.png.* [George, Crown Prince of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George,_Crown_Prince_of_Serbia) and Alas posing beside a big catch

In 1894, Petrović became a professor of mathematics at the [Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade)'s [Velika škola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velika_škola) (which later became the [University of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Belgrade)). In those days, he was one of the greatest experts for differential equations. He held lectures until his retirement in 1938. [^o7]

In 1897, he became an associate member of the [Serbian Royal Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Royal_Academy) and associate member of the [Yugoslav Academy of Sciences and Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Arts) in [Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb). He became a full member of the Serbian Royal Academy in 1899, when he was only 31.[^o7]

In 1882, he became a fisherman apprentice, and in 1895 he took an exam to become a master fisherman. Mihailo Petrović got the nickname "Alas" (river fisher) because of his passion for fishery. He was not only an aficionado, but expert as well. He participated in legislative talks regarding the fishery convention with [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania), and in talks with Austria-Hungary about the protection of the fishery on [Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sava), [Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina_river) and [Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_river) rivers.[^o8] Alas published expert papers and reports on the fish-fauna found in the Macedonian lakes, such as Skadar Lake and Ohrid Lake.[^o8]

Mihailo Petrović Alas also constructed a hydrointegrator, and won the gold medal at the [World Exposition in Paris 1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_%281900%29). When in 1905 the [Velika škola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velika_škola) was transformed into the [University of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Belgrade) Petrović was among first eight regular professors, who elected other professors. He patented a total of 10 inventions, published 300 scientific works and a number of books and journals from his sea expeditions. These expeditions included trips to [Azores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores), [Newfoundland and Labrador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_and_Labrador), [Suez Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Canal), [Madagascar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagascar), [Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde), [Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands), [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland), [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland), [Bermuda Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Triangle), [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean) and others.[^o3] Petrovć also visited both the North and South poles, researched the culture of Eskimos and took part in whale hunting expeditions.[^o9]

He received numerous awards and acknowledgments and was a member of several foreign science academies ([Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague), [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest), [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków)) and scientific societies. In 1927, when [Jovan Cvijić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jovan_Cvijić) died, members of the Serbian academy proposed Mihailo Petrović as the new president of academy, but the authorities did not accept this proposal. Probable reason for this was that Mihailo Petrović Alas was first a private tutor and mentor and later a close friend of the prince [Đorđe P. Karađorđević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Đorđe_P._Karađorđević), the king's brother, who was arrested in 1925, and held in house arrest.[^o7]

*Image: Personal items Mihailo Petrović Alas.jpg.* Personal items of Mihailo Petrović

In 1931, members of the academy unanimously proposed Alas for the president of the academy, but authorities again dismissed this proposal. Mathematician and physicist [Bogdan Gavrilović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogdan_Gavrilović), a fellow professor, was nominated instead. In 1939, he became an honorary doctor at the University of Belgrade. In the same year, he received the order of [Saint Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sava), first class.[^o7]

He also founded the Belgrade School of Mathematics, which produced a number of mathematicians who continued Alas's work.

Science at the University of Belgrade had advanced andt the first doctorate in mathematical sciences was defended under the supervision of professor Petrović. In 1912, this doctorate in the field of differential equations was defended by Mladen Berić, a teacher at the First Belgrade Gymnasium and his assistant.[^o7] All doctoral dissertations defended on the Belgrade University since 1912 until the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) were under his mentorship.

Alas participated in the [Balkan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Wars) and in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) as an officer, and after the war he served as a reserve officer. He practised [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), and his cipher systems were used by the Yugoslav army even for some time after World War II.[^o10] When the Second World War broke out in Yugoslavia, he was again called into the army and the Germans captured him. After a while, he was released because of illness. On 8 June 1943, professor Petrović died in [his home in Kosančićev Venac Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mika_Alas's_House) in [Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade).[^o7]

## Private life {#Private_life}

He played violin, and in 1896, founded a musical society named Suz.[^o12]

Alas and fellow scientist [Milutin Milanković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milutin_Milanković) were close friends for several decades.[^o3]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

[Ninth Belgrade Gymnasium "Mihailo Petrović Alas"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_Belgrade_Gymnasium) and Primary School in John's Street is a high school in Belgrade, Serbia named after him. Alas and fellow scientist [Milutin Milanković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milutin_Milanković) were close friends for several decades.[^o3]

Due to his scientific work and results, Mihailo Petrović Alas is among the greatest Serbian mathematicians as well as one of the [100 most prominent Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_100_most_prominent_Serbs).

In the [Association for Culture, Art and International Cooperation "Adligat"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adligat) in Belgrade there is an extensive fund of documents from the legacy of Mihailo Petrović Alas, including his childhood photos, letters from his grandfather who educated him, diplomas, notes, a whole bundle of published and unpublished manuscripts, as well as numerous exam reports signed by him, among which is the report on the defense of the graduation exam jointly signed by Mihailo Petrović and [Milutin Milanković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milutin_Milanković).[^o14]
