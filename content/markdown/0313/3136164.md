# Principal ideal theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Principal ideal theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **principal ideal theorem** of [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695), a branch of [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), says that extending ideals gives a mapping on the [class group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_group) of an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) to the class group of its [Hilbert class field](wikipedia://en/3021036), which sends all ideal classes to the class of a principal ideal. The phenomenon has also been called [*principalization*](wikipedia://en/43103430), or sometimes *capitulation*.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Formal statement"}

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

For any [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) *K* and any [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) *I* of the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of *K*, if *L* is the [Hilbert class field](wikipedia://en/3021036) of *K*, then

$$IO_L\$$

is a [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126) α*O*<sub>*L*</sub>, for *O*<sub>*L*</sub> the ring of integers of *L* and some element α in it.

:::

## History {#History}

The principal ideal theorem was conjectured by (txt), and was the last remaining aspect of his program on class fields to be completed, in 1929.

(txt) reduced the principal ideal theorem to a question about finite abelian groups: he showed that it would follow if the [transfer](wikipedia://en/648166) from a finite group to its derived subgroup is trivial. This result was proved by [Philipp Furtwängler](wikipedia://en/895349) (1929).
