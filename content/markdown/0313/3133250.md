# Zariski geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zariski geometry"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Zariski geometry** consists of an abstract structure introduced by [Ehud Hrushovski](wikipedia://en/3132981) and [Boris Zilber](wikipedia://en/47992829), in order to give a characterisation of the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) on an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260), and all its powers. The Zariski topology on a product of [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties) is very rarely the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910), but richer in closed sets defined by equations that mix two sets of variables. The result described gives that a very definite meaning, applying to [projective curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_curve) and [compact Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Riemann_surface) in particular.

:::

## Definition {#Definition}

A **Zariski geometry** consists of a set *X* and a [topological structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_structure) on each of the sets

*X*, *X*<sup>2</sup>, *X*<sup>3</sup>, ...

satisfying certain axioms.

(N) Each of the *X*<sup>*n*</sup> is a [Noetherian topological space](wikipedia://en/2936080), of dimension at most *n*.

Some standard terminology for Noetherian spaces will now be assumed.

(A) In each *X*<sup>*n*</sup>, the subsets defined by equality in an *n*-[tuple](wikipedia://en/132729) are closed. The mappings

*X*<sup>*m*</sup> → *X*<sup>*n*</sup>

defined by projecting out certain coordinates and setting others as constants are all continuous.

(B) For a projection

*p*: *X*<sup>*m*</sup> → *X*<sup>*n*</sup>

and an [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_set) closed subset *Y* of *X*<sup>*m*</sup>, *p*(*Y*) lies between its closure *Z* and *Z* \\ *Z* where *Z* is a proper closed subset of *Z*. (This is [quantifier elimination](wikipedia://en/2150441), at an abstract level.)

(C) *X* is irreducible.

(D) There is a uniform bound on the number of elements of a fiber in a projection of any closed set in *X*<sup>*m*</sup>, other than the cases where the fiber is *X*.

(E) A closed irreducible subset of *X*<sup>*m*</sup>, of dimension *r*, when intersected with a diagonal subset in which *s* coordinates are set equal, has all components of dimension at least *r* − *s* + 1.

The further condition required is called *very ample* (cf. [very ample line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_ample_line_bundle)). It is assumed there is an irreducible closed subset *P* of some *X*<sup>*m*</sup>, and an irreducible closed subset *Q* of *P*× *X*<sup>2</sup>, with the following properties:

(I) Given pairs (*x*, *y*), (*x*, *y*) in *X*<sup>2</sup>, for some *t* in *P*, the set of (*t*, *u*, *v*) in *Q* includes (*t*, *x*, *y*) but not (*t*, *x*, *y*)

(J) For *t* outside a proper closed subset of *P*, the set of (*x*, *y*) in *X*<sup>2</sup>, (*t*, *x*, *y*) in *Q* is an irreducible closed set of dimension 1.

(K) For all pairs (*x*, *y*), (*x*, *y*) in *X*<sup>2</sup>, selected from outside a proper closed subset, there is some *t* in *P* such that the set of (*t*, *u*, *v*) in *Q* includes (*t*, *x*, *y*) and (*t*, *x*, *y*).

Geometrically this says there are enough curves to separate points (I), and to connect points (K); and that such curves can be taken from a single [parametric family](wikipedia://en/19653337).

Then Hrushovski and Zilber prove that under these conditions there is an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) *K*, and a [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) *C*, such that its Zariski geometry of powers and their [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) is isomorphic to the given one. In short, the geometry can be algebraized.
