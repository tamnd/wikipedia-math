# Fáry–Milnor theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fáry–Milnor theorem"}

In the [mathematical theory of knots](wikipedia://en/153008), the **Fáry–Milnor theorem**, named after [István Fáry](wikipedia://en/13183981) and [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632), states that three-dimensional [smooth curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_curve) with small [total curvature](wikipedia://en/19172363) must be [unknotted](wikipedia://en/60075). The theorem was proved independently by Fáry in 1949 and Milnor in 1950. It was later shown to follow from the existence of [quadrisecants](wikipedia://en/40087321) (Denne 2004).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

If *K* is any closed [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) that is sufficiently [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_curve) to define the [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770#Curvature_of_space_curves) κ at each of its points, and if the [total absolute curvature](wikipedia://en/48503145) is less than or equal to 4π, then *K* is an [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075), i.e.:

$$\text{If}\ \oint_K |\kappa(s)| \, \mathrm{d}s \le 4 \pi,\ \text{then}\ K\ \text{is an unknot}.$$

*Image: A worn-out baseball.JPG.* The seam of a [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_%28ball%29) follows an unknotted curve with total curvature roughly 4$\pi$. By making the curve more convoluted, unknots can be made to have arbitrarily large curvature.

The [contrapositive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrapositive) tells us that if *K* is not an unknot, i.e. *K* is not [isotopic](wikipedia://en/212250#Isotopy) to the circle, then the total curvature will be strictly greater than 4π. Notice that having the total curvature less than or equal to 4$\pi$ is merely a [sufficient condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficient_condition) for *K* to be an unknot; it is not a [necessary condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_condition). In other words, although all knots with total curvature less than or equal to 4π are the unknot, there exist unknots with curvature strictly greater than 4π.

:::

## Generalizations to non-smooth curves {#Generalizations_to_non-smooth_curves}

For closed polygonal chains the same result holds with the integral of curvature replaced by the sum of angles between adjacent segments of the chain. By approximating arbitrary curves by polygonal chains, one may extend the definition of total curvature to larger classes of curves, within which the Fáry–Milnor theorem also holds ((Milnor 1950), (Sullivan 2008)).
