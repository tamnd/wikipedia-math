# Ten-of-diamonds decahedron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ten-of-diamonds decahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **ten-of-diamonds decahedron** is a [space-filling polyhedron](wikipedia://en/58607583) with 10 faces, 2 opposite rhombi with orthogonal major axes, connected by 8 identical [isosceles triangle](wikipedia://en/315428) faces. Michael Goldberg named it after a [playing card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playing_card), as a 10-faced polyhedron with two opposite [rhombic](wikipedia://en/183884) (diamond-shaped) faces. He catalogued it in a 1982 paper as 10-II, the second in a list of 26 known space-filling decahedra.

:::

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

The ten-of-diamonds can be dissected in an [octagonal](wikipedia://en/314575) cross-section between the two rhombic faces. It is a decahedron with 12 vertices, 20 edges, and 10 faces (4 [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654), 4 [trapezoids](wikipedia://en/201826), 1 [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884), and 1 [isotoxal octagon](wikipedia://en/314575#Symmetry)). Michael Goldberg labels this polyhedron 10-XXV, the 25th in a list of space-filling decahedra.[^n1]

The ten-of-diamonds can be dissected as a half-model on a symmetry plane into a space-filling [heptahedron](wikipedia://en/9367140) with 6 vertices, 11 edges, and 7 faces (6 triangles and 1 trapezoid). Michael Goldberg identifies this polyhedron as a "triply truncated quadrilateral prism", type 7-XXIV, the 24th in a list of space-filling heptahedra.

It can be further dissected as a quarter-model by another symmetry plane into a space-filling [hexahedron](wikipedia://en/237209) with 6 vertices, 10 edges, and 6 faces (4 triangles, 2 right trapezoids). Michael Goldberg identifies this polyhedron as an "ungulated quadrilateral pyramid", type 6-X, the 10th in a list of space-filling hexahedra.[^o5]

*Image: Double-ten-of-diamonds-solid.png.* Rhombic bowtie

Pairs of *ten-of-diamonds* can be attached as a nonconvex *bow-tie* space-filler, called a **rhombic bowtie** for its cross-sectional appearance. The two right-most symmetric projections below show the rhombi edge-on on the top, bottom and a middle *neck* where the two halves are connected. It has 12 vertices, 28 edges, and 18 faces (16 triangles and 2 rhombi) within D<sub>2h</sub> symmetry. These paired-cells stack more easily as inter-locking elements. Long sequences of these can be stacked together in 3 axes to fill space.[^o6]
