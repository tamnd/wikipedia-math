# Alexander Merkurjev

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Merkurjev"}

**Aleksandr Sergeyevich Merkurjev** (Алекса́ндр Сергее́вич Мерку́рьев, born September 25, 1955[^o3]) is a Russian-American mathematician, who has made major contributions to the field of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923). Currently Merkurjev is a professor at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles).

:::

## Work {#Work}

Merkurjev's work focuses on [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924), [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478), [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315), [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) and [central simple algebras](wikipedia://en/404082). In the early 1980s Merkurjev proved a fundamental result about the structure of central simple algebras of [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_group) dividing 2, which relates the 2-torsion of the [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078) with [Milnor K-theory](wikipedia://en/2914128).[^o5] In subsequent work with [Suslin](wikipedia://en/7111820) this was extended to higher torsion as the [Merkurjev–Suslin theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkurjev–Suslin_theorem). The full statement of the [norm residue isomorphism theorem](wikipedia://en/27126857) (also known as the Bloch-Kato conjecture) was proven by [Voevodsky](wikipedia://en/104482).

In the late 1990s Merkurjev gave the most general approach to the notion of [essential dimension](wikipedia://en/16950168), introduced by [Buhler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Buhler) and [Reichstein](wikipedia://en/31869776), and made fundamental contributions to that field. In particular Merkurjev determined the essential p-dimension of central simple algebras of degree $p^2$ (for a prime p) and, in joint work with [Karpenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Karpenko), the essential dimension of finite *p*-groups.[^o6][^o7]

## Awards {#Awards}

Merkurjev won the Young Mathematician Prize of the [Petersburg Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petersburg_Mathematical_Society) for his work on algebraic K-theory.[^o9] In 1986 he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California), and his talk was entitled "Milnor K-theory and Galois cohomology".[^o10] In 1995 he won the [Humboldt Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Prize), an international prize awarded to renowned scholars. Merkurjev gave a plenary talk at the second [European Congress of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13505846) in [Budapest, Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest,_Hungary) in 1996.[^o11] In 2012 he won the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in Algebra for his work on the essential dimension of groups.[^o12]

In 2015 a special volume of *Documenta Mathematica* was published in honor of Merkurjev's sixtieth birthday.[^o13]
