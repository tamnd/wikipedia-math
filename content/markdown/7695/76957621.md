# Sallie Pero Mead

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Sallie Pero Mead"}

**Sallie P. Mead** (born **Sallie Eugena Pero**, October 1, 1893 – April 1981) was an American mathematician and engineer known for her contributions to [radar technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar) and the development of [waveguides](wikipedia://en/3854225) at the [American Telephone and Telegraph Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Telephone_and_Telegraph_Company)'s development and research arm (AT&T D&R).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Sallie P. Mead was born on October 1, 1893, in Manhattan, New York, to Robert R. Pero and Lillian M. Foggin. She began studying at [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College) in 1909 and graduated in 1913, earning the highest honors in mathematics and winning the Barnard's Kohn Mathematical Prize. She continued her studies at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), where she earned a master's degree in mathematics. Her master's essay, titled "Linear Transformations and the Theory of the Tetrahedron" focused on the application of [linear transformations](wikipedia://en/18102) and the [rotation group of the tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/2904472) to the solution of general [quartic equations](wikipedia://en/251591).

## Career {#Career}

Mead received a license to teach mathematics as a substitute in New York City schools, with her first assignment at [Evander Childs High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evander_Childs_Educational_Campus) in the Bronx. By June 1915, she had joined AT&T's Western Union building on Broadway, hired by [George Ashley Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Ashley_Campbell) as a "[computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_%28occupation%29)" to work on transmission probability problems.[^o5] In 1923, Campbell credited Mead in a technical paper for her meticulous work on the exponential [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144).[^o6]

### Transition to engineer {#Transition_to_engineer}

In 1919, Pero transitioned from "computer" to "engineer" at AT&T D&R's Transmission Engineering unit under supervisor [John Renshaw Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Renshaw_Carson). Mead developed expertise in the mathematics of transmission through cables, wires, and conducting tubes, especially those with circular cross sections. In 1924, she applied for a patent on a "distortion compensator," becoming the first woman at AT&T to hold a patent upon its granting in 1929.[^o8] Mead earned six patents throughout her career. She became the first woman to publish in the *[Bell System Technical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs_Technical_Journal)* and went on to author six *BSTJ* articles.[^o5]

### Contributions to waveguide development {#Contributions_to_waveguide_development}

In the early 1930s, Mead was assigned as an analyst to provide mathematical support to [George Southworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Southworth)'s development of waveguides, crucial for [microwave radar technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave) in World War II. [^o10] Along with [Sergei Schelkunoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Schelkunoff), Mead rediscovered [Lord Rayleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_William_Strutt,_3rd_Baron_Rayleigh)'s findings on waveguide cutoff frequencies, and their work went on to extend Rayleigh's results to include metal attenuation losses.[^n1] Southworth himself was unsure which of the two was first to describe the effect.[^o12] The work of Mead and Shelkunoff on the attenuation of the [TE01 mode](wikipedia://en/516150) led to significant advancements in microwave communications. Mead retired in 1958, remarking that she looked forward to having more time study mathematics and Russian.[^o5]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Mead married Charles Edwin Mead in 1924, who died in 1934. She married Chester E. Grant in 1938 but continued to use the name Sallie P. Mead professionally.[^o5]

## Later years and legacy {#Later_years_and_legacy}

During World War II, Mead contributed to the development of [fire-control systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-control_system). Post-war, she conducted probabilistic studies of communication traffic in the Traffic Department until her retirement in 1958. Mead died in April 1981 in New Jersey.[^o5]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- Barnard's Kohn Mathematical Prize (1913)
- First woman at AT&T to hold a patent (1929)[^o8]
- Member of the American Mathematical Society (1923)[^o10]
