# Hilbert modular variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hilbert modular variety"}

In mathematics, a **Hilbert modular surface** or **Hilbert–Blumenthal surface** is an [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311) obtained by taking a quotient of a product of two copies of the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826) by a [Hilbert modular group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_modular_group). More generally, a **Hilbert modular variety** is an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) obtained by taking a quotient of a product of multiple copies of the upper half-plane by a Hilbert modular group.

:::

Hilbert modular surfaces were first described by (txt) using some unpublished notes written by [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) about 10 years before.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

If *R* is the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of a real [quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781), then the Hilbert modular group SL<sub>2</sub>(*R*) [acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on the product *H*×*H* of two copies of the upper half plane *H*. There are several [birationally equivalent](wikipedia://en/382733) surfaces related to this action, any of which may be called [Hilbert modular surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_modular_surface):

- The surface *X* is the quotient of *H*×*H* by SL<sub>2</sub>(*R*); it is not compact and usually has quotient singularities coming from points with non-trivial isotropy groups.
- The surface *X*<sup>\*</sup> is obtained from *X* by adding a finite number of points corresponding to the [cusps](wikipedia://en/6851758) of the action. It is compact, and has not only the quotient singularities of *X*, but also singularities at its cusps.
- The surface *Y* is obtained from *X*<sup>\*</sup> by resolving the singularities in a minimal way. It is a compact smooth [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311), but is not in general minimal.
- The surface *Y*<sup>0</sup> is obtained from *Y* by blowing down certain exceptional −1-curves. It is smooth and compact, and is often (but not always) minimal.

There are several variations of this construction:

- The Hilbert modular group may be replaced by some subgroup of finite index, such as a [congruence subgroup](wikipedia://en/382694).
- One can extend the Hilbert modular group by a group of order 2, acting on the Hilbert modular group via the Galois action, and exchanging the two copies of the upper half plane.

:::

## Singularities {#Singularities}

(Hirzebruch 1953) showed how to resolve the quotient singularities, and (Hirzebruch 1971) showed how to resolve their cusp singularities.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Hilbert modular varieties cannot be [anabelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabelian).[^o10]

:::

## Classification of surfaces {#Classification_of_surfaces}

The papers (Hirzebruch 1971), (Hirzebruch Van de Ven 1974) and (Hirzebruch Zagier 1977) identified their type in the [classification of algebraic surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_algebraic_surfaces). Most of them are [surfaces of general type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfaces_of_general_type), but several are [rational surfaces](wikipedia://en/2380148) or blown up [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116) or [elliptic surfaces](wikipedia://en/3073462).

::: {#o16 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

(van der Geer 1988) gives a long table of examples.

The [Clebsch surface](wikipedia://en/23390462) blown up at its 10 Eckardt points is a Hilbert modular surface.

### Associated to a quadratic field extension {#Associated_to_a_quadratic_field_extension}

Given a [quadratic field extension](wikipedia://en/454781) $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})$ for $p = 4k + 1$ there is an associated Hilbert modular variety $Y(p)$ obtained from compactifying a certain quotient variety $X(p)$ and resolving its singularities. Let $\mathfrak{H}$ denote the upper half plane and let $SL(2,\mathcal{O}_K)/\{\pm \text{Id}_2\}$ act on $\mathfrak{H}\times \mathfrak{H}$ via

> $$\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} (z_1,z_2) = \left( \frac{ az_1 + bz_2 }{ cz_1 + dz_2 }, \frac{ a'z_1 + b'z_2 }{ c'z_1 + d'z_2 }\right)$$

where the $a',b',c',d'$ are the [Galois conjugates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_conjugate).[^o19] The associated quotient variety is denoted

> $$X(p) = G\backslash \mathfrak{H}\times\mathfrak{H}$$

and can be compactified to a variety $\overline{X}(p)$, called the **cusps**, which are in [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) with the [ideal classes](wikipedia://en/150907) in $\text{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_K)$. Resolving its singularities gives the variety $Y(p)$ called the **Hilbert modular variety of the field extension**. From the Bailey-Borel compactification theorem, there is an embedding of this surface into a [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135).[^o20]

:::
