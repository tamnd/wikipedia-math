# Jerzy Łoś

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jerzy Łoś"}

**Jerzy Maria Michał Łoś** (; born 22 March 1920 – 1 June 1998) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist), and [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher). He studied at the [University of Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv), and during his career held positions at several institutions including the [University of Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław), the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) and the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw).

:::

He is noted for his contributions to [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858) (particularly [Łoś's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łoś's_theorem)), [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), [Abelian group theory](wikipedia://en/2974), and [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255). In the late 1940s, he introduced some of the first systems of [temporal](wikipedia://en/321481) and [epistemic logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemic_logic).[^o3][^o4]

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Łoś was born on 22 March 1920 in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów) (now [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv), Ukraine) in the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic) as the son of Zygmunt, a Doctor of Law, and Zofia (Roztworowska), a landowner and social activist.[^o6] In 1937, he matriculated at the [Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kazimierz_University) (now the [University of Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv)), where he initially studied medicine before switching to philosophy and chemistry.[^o6]

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Łoś worked as an official at a sugar factory in [Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin). After the war, he pursued his studies in philosophy at the [Maria Curie-Skłodowska University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Curie-Skłodowska_University) in Lublin. In 1946, influenced by the writings of [Jan Śleszyński](wikipedia://en/3033762), he published *An Attempt to Axiomatise Traditional Logic* in which he examined [traditional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_logic), particularly the laws of [immediate inference](wikipedia://en/25379366) on the basis of relations between extensions of arguments. The following year, he received a master's degree on the basis of his work *Analiza metodologiczna kanonów Milla* (The Foundations of a Methodological Analysis of Mill's Method).[^o6]

In 1947, Łoś relocated to [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław), where he worked as an assistant at the Faculty of Physics and later the Faculty of Logic of the [University of Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław). Inspired by [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735) and [Edward Marczewski](wikipedia://en/1028149), he started developing a greater interest in mathematics. In 1949, Łoś obtained his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) based on his dissertation *O matrycach logicznych* (On Logical Matrices) written under the supervision of [Jerzy Słupecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Słupecki).[^o7] In the same year, he became a member of the Real Functions Group at the [Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/67042302) of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o6]

In 1952, Łoś started working at the [Nicolaus Copernicus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus_University_in_Toruń) in [Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toruń) and was appointed head of the Group of Algebra of the Institute of Mathematics.[^o8] He earned his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) there in 1955. In 1961, Łoś continued his academic career at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) where he shifted the focus of his research from [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358) and [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) to [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) and [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics).[^o6] He held positions in three institutes there: first the Institute of Philosophy, then the Institute of Mathematics and finally the Institute of Computer Science.[^o13] In 1967–1994, he sat on the editorial board of *[Fundamenta Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/1576701)*.[^o9] In 1969, he served as acting president of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791).[^o10]

In 1974, Łoś was awarded the Knight's Cross of the [Order of Polonia Restituta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta).[^o12] In January 1976, Łoś was among the signatories of the *Memorial 101*, a protest letter of Polish intellectuals to the Extraordinary Commission of the [Sejm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sejm) opposing the proposed amendments to the [Constitution of the Polish People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Polish_People's_Republic).[^o11] In 1983, he became a full member of the Polish Academy of Sciences, and in 1984, he joined the [Warsaw Scientific Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Scientific_Society).[^o12] In 1986, he received the Sierpiński Medal conferred jointly by the University of Warsaw and the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791). In 1995, he accepted an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate) from the [University of Hagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hagen), Germany.[^o6]

During his career, Łoś visited such institutions as [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley) (where he conducted a seminar on model theory with [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920)), [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University), [Institut Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/10513595), [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) and the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin).[^o6]

In 1996, Łoś suffered from a severe [brain stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke). He was thenceforward ill until his death in 1998 in Warsaw.[^o13]

### Contributions {#Contributions}

*Image: Jerzy Los caricature.png.* Jerzy Łoś caricature by Leon Jeśmanowicz, 1953

His 1947 master's thesis *Analiza metodologiczna kanonów Milla* (The Foundations of a Methodological Analysis of [Mill's Method](wikipedia://en/2041141)) is regarded as pioneering in the field of [temporal logic](wikipedia://en/321481).[^o15][^o16] [Arthur Prior](wikipedia://en/308764) references Łoś's work in his principal book on temporal logic, having learned about it through a review by [Henryk Hiż](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Hiż).[^o17]

In 1954, Jerzy Łoś and [Robert Lawson Vaught](wikipedia://en/3550212) independently established that a first-order theory having only infinite models is [complete](wikipedia://en/10086335) if it is [categorical](wikipedia://en/1096396) in some infinite cardinal greater than or equal to the cardinality of its language. It is now referred to as the [Łoś-Vaught test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łoś-Vaught_test) and its applications include demonstrating that the theory of closed [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983) formulas true on the [Rado graph](wikipedia://en/7785594) is complete.[^o18]

He is especially known for his work in [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), in particular for [Łoś's theorem](wikipedia://en/273642#Łoś's_theorem) (also known as the Fundamental Theorem on Ultraproducts), which states that any [first-order formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_formula) is true in an [ultraproduct](wikipedia://en/273642) if and only if it is true in "most" factors.[^o19] His conjecture on the [categoricity](wikipedia://en/1096396) of countable theories, later proved by [Michael D. Morley](wikipedia://en/2921675), and in its uncountable form by [Saharon Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211), played a crucial role in stimulating the development of [stability theory](wikipedia://en/5493795), which became a major branch of model theory.[^o9]

In model theory, he also proved many preservation theorems, including the [Łoś–Tarski preservation theorem](wikipedia://en/27151056), which asserts that the set of formulas preserved under taking [substructures](wikipedia://en/2057747) is exactly the set of *universal* formulas.[^o20]

He also gave significant contributions, as well, to [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358), [Abelian group theory](wikipedia://en/2974) and [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200). In the 60's he turned his attention to [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255), focusing mainly on production processes and dynamic decision processes.
