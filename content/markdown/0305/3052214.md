# Jakob Philipp Kulik

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jakob Philipp Kulik"}

*Image: Jakub Filip Kulik (1793-1863).jpg.* Jakob Philipp Kulik

**Jakob Philipp Kulik** (Jakub Filip Kulik; 20 April 1793 – 28 February 1863) was an Austrian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his construction of a massive [factor tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_table).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kulik was born in Lemberg, [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) (today [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv) in [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)). He studied there philosophy and law. In 1814–1816, he worked as a teacher of mathematics at a [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) in [Olomouc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olomouc), but then he went to [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz) and taught physics at the [University of Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Graz). In 1826, Kulik became a professor of mathematics at [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague). He died on 28 February 1863 in Prague.[^o3] Kulik is buried at [Vyšehrad Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyšehrad_Cemetery) in Prague.[^o4]

## Kulik's factor tables {#Kulik's_factor_tables}

In 1825, Kulik mentioned a table of factors up to 30 million, but this table does no longer seem to exist. It is also not clear if it had really been completed.

From about 1825 until 1863 Kulik produced a factor table of numbers up to 100330200 (except for numbers divisible by 2, 3, or 5). This table basically had the same format as the table to 30 million and it is therefore most likely that the work on the "Magnus canon divisorum" spanned from the mid-1820s to Kulik's death, at which time the tables were still unfinished. These tables fill eight volumes totaling 4212 pages, and are kept in the archives of the [Vienna Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Academy_of_Sciences). Volume II of the 8-volume set has been lost.
