# Gerald Sacks

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gerald Sacks"}

**Gerald Enoch Sacks** (1933 – October 4, 2019) was an American [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) whose most important contributions were in [recursion theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion_theory). Named after him is [Sacks forcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacks_forcing), a [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) notion based on [perfect sets](wikipedia://en/5075551)[^o3] and the Sacks Density Theorem, which asserts that the [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) of the [recursively enumerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursively_enumerable) [Turing degrees](wikipedia://en/764405) is [dense](wikipedia://en/6320997).[^o4] Sacks had a joint appointment as a professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) starting in 1972 and became emeritus at M.I.T. in 2006 and at Harvard in 2012.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

Sacks was born in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn) in 1933. He earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1961 from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) under the direction of [J. Barkley Rosser](wikipedia://en/679224), with his dissertation *On Suborderings of Degrees of Recursive Insolvability*. Among his notable students are [Lenore Blum](wikipedia://en/1884319), [Harvey Friedman](wikipedia://en/3038108), [Sy Friedman](wikipedia://en/17145259), [Leo Harrington](wikipedia://en/5693354), [Richard Shore](wikipedia://en/18435038), [Steve Simpson](wikipedia://en/50451674) and [Theodore Slaman](wikipedia://en/5693320).
