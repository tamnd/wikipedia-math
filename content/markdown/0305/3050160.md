# Static universe

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Static universe"}

In [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology), a **static universe** is a [cosmological model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmological_model) in which the [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe) is not assumed to be undergoing unlimited global [expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_of_the_universe). Such models can differ [spatially](wikipedia://en/27667) and [temporally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time), depending on the particular cosmology. A static infinite universe was first proposed by English astronomer [Thomas Digges](wikipedia://en/628083) (1546–1595).[^o2]

:::

In contrast to this model, [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) proposed a temporally infinite but spatially finite model - *static eternal universe* - as his preferred [cosmology](wikipedia://en/5378) during 1917, in his paper *Cosmological Considerations in the General Theory of Relativity*.

After the discovery of the [redshift-distance relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble's_law) (deduced by the inverse correlation of galactic brightness to redshift) by American astronomers [Vesto Slipher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesto_Slipher) and [Edwin Hubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Hubble), the Belgian astrophysicist and priest [Georges Lemaître](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Lemaître) interpreted the redshift as evidence of universal expansion and thus a [Big Bang](wikipedia://en/4116), whereas Swiss astronomer [Fritz Zwicky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Zwicky) proposed that the redshift was caused by the [photons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon) losing energy as they passed through the [matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter) and/or [forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_field_%28physics%29) in intergalactic space. Zwicky's proposal would come to be termed '[tired light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tired_light)'—a term invented by the major [Big Bang](wikipedia://en/4116) proponent [Richard Tolman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Tolman).

## The Einstein universe {#The_Einstein_universe}

During 1917, Albert Einstein added a positive [cosmological constant](wikipedia://en/38992) to his [equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_equation) of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) to counteract the attractive effects of [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity) on ordinary matter, which would otherwise cause a static, spatially finite universe to either [collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Crunch) or [expand forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_an_expanding_universe).[^o4][^o5][^o6] This model of the universe became known as the Einstein World or [Einstein's static universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein's_static_universe).

This motivation ended after the proposal by the astrophysicist and Roman Catholic priest [Georges Lemaître](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Lemaître) that the universe seems to be not static, but expanding. [Edwin Hubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Hubble) had researched data from the observations made by astronomer [Vesto Slipher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesto_Slipher) to confirm a relationship between [redshift and distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble's_law), which forms the basis for the modern [expansion paradigm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_expansion_of_space) that was introduced by Lemaître. According to [George Gamow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gamow) this caused Einstein to declare this cosmological model, and especially the introduction of the cosmological constant, his "biggest blunder".

Einstein's static universe is [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_universe) (i.e. has hyperspherical topology and positive spatial curvature), and contains uniform dust and a positive [cosmological constant](wikipedia://en/38992) with value precisely $\Lambda_E = 4\pi G\rho/c^2$, where $G$ is Newtonian gravitational constant, $\rho$ is the energy density of the matter in the universe and $c$ is the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light). The [radius of curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radius_of_curvature_%28mathematics%29) of space of the Einstein universe is equal to

$$R_E = \Lambda_E^{-1/2} = {c \over \sqrt{4\pi G\rho}}.$$

The Einstein universe is one of [Friedmann's solutions](wikipedia://en/343031) to Einstein's field equation for dust with density $\rho$, cosmological constant $\Lambda_E$, and radius of curvature $R_E$. It is the only non-trivial static solution to Friedmann's equations.

Because the Einstein universe soon was recognized to be inherently unstable, it was presently abandoned as a viable model for the universe. It is unstable in the sense that any slight change in either the value of the cosmological constant, the [matter density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter_density), or the [spatial curvature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_curvature) will result in a universe that either expands and accelerates forever or re-collapses to a singularity.

After Einstein renounced his cosmological constant, and embraced the Friedmann-LeMaitre model of an expanding universe,[^o7] most physicists of the twentieth century assumed that the cosmological constant is zero. If so (absent some other form of [dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_energy)), the expansion of the universe would be decelerating. However, after [Saul Perlmutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Perlmutter), [Brian P. Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_P._Schmidt), and [Adam G. Riess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_G._Riess) introduced the theory of an [accelerating universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_universe) during 1998, a positive cosmological constant has been revived as a simple explanation for [dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_energy).

In 1976 [Irving Segal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Segal) revived the static universe in his [chronometric cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronometric_cosmology). Similar to Zwicky, he ascribed the red shift of distant galaxies to curvature in the cosmos. Though he claimed vindication in astronomic data, others find the results to be inconclusive.[^o8]

## Requirements of a static infinite model {#Requirements_of_a_static_infinite_model}

In order for a static infinite universe model to be viable, it must explain three things:

First, it must explain the intergalactic [redshift](wikipedia://en/26262). Second, it must explain the [cosmic microwave background radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_microwave_background_radiation). Third, it must have a mechanism to re-create matter (particularly [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) atoms) from radiation or other sources in order to avoid a gradual 'running down' of the universe due to the conversion of matter into energy in [stellar processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_nucleosynthesis).[^o10][^o11] With the absence of such a mechanism, the universe would consist of dead objects such as [black holes](wikipedia://en/4650) and [black dwarfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_dwarf).
