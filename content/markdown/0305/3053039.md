# Bateman Manuscript Project

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bateman Manuscript Project"}

The **Bateman Manuscript Project** was a major effort at collation and encyclopedic compilation of the mathematical theory of [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110). It resulted in the eventual publication of five important reference volumes, under the editorship of [Arthur Erdélyi](wikipedia://en/4388684).

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The theory of special functions was a core activity of the field of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), from the middle of the nineteenth century to the advent of high-speed [electronic computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_computing). The intricate properties of [spherical harmonics](wikipedia://en/203056), [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939) and other staples of problem-solving in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) and right across the [physical sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_sciences), are not easy to document completely, absent a theory explaining the inter-relationships. [Mathematical tables](wikipedia://en/239112) to perform actual calculations needed to mesh with an adequate theory of how functions could be transformed into those already tabulated.

[Harry Bateman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Bateman), a distinguished applied mathematician, undertook the somewhat [quixotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quixotism) task of trying to collate the content of the very large literature. On his death in 1946, his papers on this project were still in a uniformly rough state. The publication of the edited version provided special functions texts more up-to-date than, for example, the classic [Whittaker & Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whittaker_&_Watson).

The volumes were out of print for many years, and copyright in the works reverted to the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), who renewed them in the early 1980s. In 2011, the California Institute of Technology gave permission for scans of the volumes to be made publicly available.

Other mathematicians involved in the project include [Wilhelm Magnus](wikipedia://en/8337666), Fritz Oberhettinger and [Francesco Tricomi](wikipedia://en/19830487).

## Askey–Bateman project {#Askey–Bateman_project}

In 2007, the *Askey–Bateman project* was announced by [Mourad Ismail](wikipedia://en/32809815) as a five- or six-volume encyclopedic book series on special functions, based on the works of [Harry Bateman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Bateman) and [Richard Askey](wikipedia://en/2836040).[^o16]

Starting in 2020, [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press) began publishing volumes 1 and 2 of this *Encyclopedia of Special Functions* with series editors Mourad Ismail and Walter Van Assche:[^o17]

- Volume 1: Univariate Orthogonal Polynomials (editor: Mourad Ismail)[^o17][^o18]
- Volume 2: Multivariable Special Functions (editors: [Tom H. Koornwinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_H._Koornwinder), [Jasper V. Stokman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasper_V._Stokman))[^o17][^o19]
- Volume 3: Hypergeometric and Basic Hypergeometric Functions (editor: Mourad Ismail)[^o17]

Further volumes were considered to include topics such as:[^o16]

- Continued fractions, number theory, and elliptic and theta functions
- Equations of mathematical physics, including continuous and discrete Painlevé, Lamé and Heun equations
- Transforms and related topics
