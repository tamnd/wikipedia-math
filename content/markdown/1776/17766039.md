# Lee distance

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lee distance"}

In [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869), the **Lee distance** is a [distance](wikipedia://en/39378) between two [strings](wikipedia://en/27701) $x_1 x_2 \dots x_n$ and $y_1 y_2 \dots y_n$ of equal length *n* over the *q*-ary [alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet) $\{0, 1, \ldots, q - 1$} of size $q \ge 2$. It is a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29)[^o5] defined as

$$\sum_{i=1}^n \min(|x_i - y_i|,\, q - |x_i - y_i|).$$

If $q = 2$ or $q = 3$ the Lee distance coincides with the [Hamming distance](wikipedia://en/41227), because both distances are 0 for two single equal symbols and 1 for two single non-equal symbols. For $q > 3$ this is not the case anymore; the Lee distance between single letters can become bigger than 1. However, there exists a [Gray isometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_isometry) (weight-preserving bijection) between $\mathbb{Z}_4$ with the Lee weight and $\mathbb{Z}_2^2$ with the [Hamming weight](wikipedia://en/1127884).[^o10]

:::

Considering the alphabet as the additive group [**Z**<sub>*q*</sub>](wikipedia://en/20087), the Lee distance between two single letters $x$ and $y$ is the length of shortest path in the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) (which is circular since the group is cyclic) between them.[^o2] More generally, the Lee distance between two strings of length $n$ is the length of the shortest path between them in the Cayley graph of $\mathbf{Z}_q^n$. This can also be thought of as the [quotient metric](wikipedia://en/20018#Quotient_metric_spaces) resulting from reducing $\mathbf{Z}^{n}$ with the [Manhattan distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_distance) modulo the [lattice](wikipedia://en/15782871) $q\mathbf{Z}^{n}$. The analogous quotient metric on a quotient of $\mathbf{Z}^{n}$ modulo an arbitrary lattice is known as a **Mannheim metric** or **Mannheim distance**.[^o3][^o4]

The [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) induced by the Lee distance is a discrete analog of the [elliptic space](wikipedia://en/236020).[^o5]

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

If $q = 6$, then the Lee distance between 3140 and 2543 is $1 + 2 + 0 + 3 = 6$.

:::

## History and application {#History_and_application}

The Lee distance is named after William Chi Yuan Lee (李始元). It is applied for phase [modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulation) while the Hamming distance is used in case of orthogonal modulation.

The [Berlekamp code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlekamp_code) is an example of code in the Lee metric.[^o9] Other significant examples are the [Preparata code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preparata_code) and [Kerdock code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerdock_code); these codes are non-linear when considered over a field, but are [linear over a ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring-linear_code).[^o10]
