# Invariant differential operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Invariant differential operator"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), an **invariant differential operator** is a kind of [mathematical map](wikipedia://en/516931) from some objects to an object of similar type. These objects are typically [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) on $\mathbb{R}^n$, functions on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470), [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometric%29) valued functions, [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641), or, more generally, [sections](wikipedia://en/10630303) of a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524).

:::

In an invariant differential operator $D$, the term *[differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886)* indicates that the value $Df$ of the map depends only on $f(x)$ and the [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921) of $f$ in $x$. The word *[invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638)* indicates that the operator contains some [symmetry](wikipedia://en/2714149). This means that there is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$ with a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on the functions (or other objects in question) and this action is preserved by the operator:

$$D(g\cdot f)=g\cdot (Df).$$

Usually, the action of the group has the meaning of a [change of coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change_of_coordinates) (change of observer) and the invariance means that the operator has the same expression in all admissible coordinates.

## Invariance on homogeneous spaces {#Invariance_on_homogeneous_spaces}

Let *M* = *G*/*H* be a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) for a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) G and a Lie subgroup H. Every [representation](wikipedia://en/6355948) $\rho:H\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{V})$ gives rise to a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524)

$$V=G\times_{H}\mathbb{V}\;\text{where}\;(gh,v)\sim(g,\rho(h)v)\;\forall\;g\in G,\;h\in H\;\text{and}\;v\in\mathbb{V}.$$

Sections $\varphi\in\Gamma(V)$ can be identified with

$$\Gamma(V)=\{\varphi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{V}\;:\;\varphi(gh)=\rho(h^{-1})\varphi(g)\;\forall\;g\in G,\; h\in H\}.$$

In this form the group *G* acts on sections via

$$(\ell_g \varphi)(g')=\varphi(g^{-1}g').$$

Now let *V* and *W* be two [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) over *M*. Then a differential operator

$$d:\Gamma(V)\rightarrow\Gamma(W)$$

that maps sections of *V* to sections of *W* is called invariant if

$$d(\ell_g \varphi) = \ell_g (d\varphi).$$

for all sections $\varphi$ in $\Gamma(V)$ and elements *g* in *G*. All linear invariant differential operators on homogeneous [parabolic geometries](wikipedia://en/30556018), i.e. when *G* is semi-simple and *H* is a [parabolic subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356), are given dually by homomorphisms of [generalized Verma modules](wikipedia://en/17114413).

## Invariance in terms of abstract indices {#Invariance_in_terms_of_abstract_indices}

Given two [connections](wikipedia://en/308815) $\nabla$ and $\hat{\nabla}$ and a one form $\omega$, we have

$$\nabla_{a}\omega_{b}=\hat{\nabla}_{a}\omega_{b}-Q_{ab}{}^{c}\omega_{c}$$

for some tensor $Q_{ab}{}^{c}$.[^o4] Given an [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) of connections $[\nabla]$, we say that an operator is invariant if the form of the operator does not change when we change from one connection in the equivalence class to another. For example, if we consider the equivalence class of all [torsion free](wikipedia://en/4342484) connections, then the tensor Q is symmetric in its lower indices, i.e. $Q_{ab}{}^{c}=Q_{(ab)}{}^{c}$. Therefore we can compute

$$\nabla_{[a}\omega_{b]}=\hat{\nabla}_{[a}\omega_{b]},$$

where brackets denote skew symmetrization. This shows the invariance of the exterior derivative when acting on one forms. Equivalence classes of connections arise naturally in differential geometry, for example:

- in [conformal geometry](wikipedia://en/295907) an equivalence class of connections is given by the Levi Civita connections of all [metrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) in the conformal class;
- in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) an equivalence class of connection is given by all connections that have the same [geodesics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesics);
- in [CR geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CR_geometry) an equivalence class of connections is given by the Tanaka-Webster connections for each choice of pseudohermitian structure

## Examples {#Examples}

1. The usual [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461) operator $\nabla$ acting on real valued functions on [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) is invariant with respect to all [Euclidean transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_transformation).
1. The [differential](wikipedia://en/220782) acting on functions on a manifold with values in [1-forms](wikipedia://en/314692#Differential_one-forms) (its expression is $d=\sum_j \partial_j \, dx_j$ in any local coordinates) is invariant with respect to all smooth transformations of the manifold (the action of the transformation on [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) is just the [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995)).
1. More generally, the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) $d:\Omega^n(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{n+1}(M)$ that acts on *n*-forms of any smooth manifold M is invariant with respect to all smooth transformations. It can be shown that the exterior derivative is the only linear invariant differential operator between those bundles.
1. The [Dirac operator](wikipedia://en/1079448) in physics is invariant with respect to the [Poincaré group](wikipedia://en/230487) (if we choose the proper [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of the [Poincaré group](wikipedia://en/230487) on [spinor](wikipedia://en/29276) valued functions. This is, however, a subtle question and if we want to make this mathematically rigorous, we should say that it is invariant with respect to a group which is a [double cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_covering_group) of the Poincaré group)
1. The [conformal Killing equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_Killing_equation) $X^a \mapsto \nabla_{(a}X_{b)}-\frac{1}{n}\nabla_c X^c g_{ab}$ is a conformally invariant linear differential operator between vector fields and symmetric trace-free tensors.

:::

## Conformal invariance {#Conformal_invariance}

Given a metric

$$g(x,y)=x_{1}y_{n+2}+x_{n+2}y_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{n+1}x_{i}y_{i}$$

on $\mathbb{R}^{n+2}$, we can write the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) $S^{n}$ as the space of generators of the [nil cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_cone)

$$S^{n}=\{[x]\in\mathbb{RP}_{n+1}\; :\; g(x,x)=0 \}.$$

In this way, the flat model of [conformal geometry](wikipedia://en/295907) is the sphere $S^{n}=G/P$ with $G=SO_{0}(n+1,1)$ and P the stabilizer of a point in $\mathbb{R}^{n+2}$. A classification of all linear conformally invariant differential operators on the sphere is known (Eastwood and Rice, 1987).[^o8]
