# Conoid

*Image: Conoid-circle.svg.* Right circular conoid: \#FF8080 blue

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conoid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) a **conoid** (el) is a [ruled surface](wikipedia://en/705158), whose rulings (lines) fulfill the additional conditions:

:::

**(1)** All rulings are parallel to a [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29), the *[directrix](wikipedia://en/24801595) plane*.

**(2)** All rulings intersect a fixed line, the *axis*.

The conoid is a [right conoid](wikipedia://en/23756785) if its axis is [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to its directrix plane. Hence all rulings are perpendicular to the axis.

Because of **(1)** any conoid is a [Catalan surface](wikipedia://en/23808629) and can be represented parametrically by

$$\mathbf x(u,v)= \mathbf c(u) + v\mathbf r(u)\$$

Any curve $\mathbf{x}(u_{0},v)$ with fixed parameter $u = u_{0}$ is a ruling, $\mathbf{c}(u)$ describes the *directrix* and the [vectors](wikipedia://en/32533) $\mathbf{r}(u)$ are all parallel to the directrix plane. The planarity of the vectors $\mathbf{r}(u)$ can be represented by

$$\det(\mathbf r,\mathbf \dot r,\mathbf \ddot r)=0.$$

If the directrix is a circle, the conoid is called a **circular conoid**.

The term *conoid* was already used by [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) in his treatise *[On Conoids and Spheroides](wikipedia://en/59532950)*.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Right circular conoid {#Right_circular_conoid}

The parametric representation

$$\mathbf x(u,v)=(\cos u,\sin u,0) + v (0,-\sin u,z_0) \ ,\ 0\le u <2\pi, v\in \mathbb{R}$$

describes a right circular conoid with the unit circle of the x-y-plane as directrix and a directrix plane, which is parallel to the y--z-plane. Its axis is the line $(x,0,z_0) \ x\in \mathbb{R} \ .$

*Special features*:

1. The intersection with a horizontal plane is an ellipse.
1. $(1-x^2)(z-z_0)^2-y^2z_0^2=0$ is an implicit representation. Hence the right circular conoid is a surface of degree 4.
1. [Kepler's rule](wikipedia://en/219962) gives for a right circular conoid with radius $r$ and height $h$ the exact volume: $V=\tfrac{\pi}{2}r^2h$.

The implicit representation is fulfilled by the points of the line $(x,0,z_0)$, too. For these points there exist no [tangent planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_plane). Such points are called *singular*.

### Parabolic conoid {#Parabolic_conoid}

*Image: Conoid-parabolic.svg.* parabolic conoid: directrix is a parabola

The parametric representation

$$\mathbf x(u,v)=\left(1,u,-u^2\right)+ v\left(-1,0,u^2\right)$$

$$=\left(1-v,u,-(1-v)u^2\right)\ , u,v \in \mathbb{R} \ ,$$

describes a *parabolic conoid* with the equation $z=-xy^2$. The conoid has a parabola as directrix, the y-axis as axis and a plane parallel to the x-z-plane as directrix plane. It is used by architects as roof surface (s. below).

The parabolic conoid has no singular points.

### Mathematics {#Mathematics}

There are a lot of conoids with singular points, which are investigated in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

### Architecture {#Architecture}

Like other ruled surfaces conoids are of high interest with architects, because they can be built using beams or bars. Right conoids can be manufactured easily: one threads bars onto an axis such that they can be rotated around this axis, only. Afterwards one deflects the bars by a directrix and generates a conoid (s. parabolic conoid).
