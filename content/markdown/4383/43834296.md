# Extension of a topological group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Extension of a topological group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically in [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315), **an extension of topological groups**, or a **topological extension**, is a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) $0\to H\stackrel{\imath}{\to} X \stackrel{\pi}{\to}G\to 0$ where $H, X$ and $G$ are topological groups and $i$ and $\pi$ are continuous homomorphisms which are also open onto their images.[^o2] Every extension of topological groups is therefore a [group extension](wikipedia://en/345807).

:::

## Classification of extensions of topological groups {#Classification_of_extensions_of_topological_groups}

We say that the topological extensions

$$0 \rightarrow H\stackrel{i}{\rightarrow} X\stackrel{\pi}{\rightarrow} G\rightarrow 0$$

and

$$0\to H\stackrel{i'}{\rightarrow} X'\stackrel{\pi'}{\rightarrow} G\rightarrow 0$$

are equivalent (or congruent) if there exists a topological isomorphism $T: X\to X'$ making [commutative](wikipedia://en/138484) the diagram of Figure 1.

*Image: Extensions groups2.png.* Figure 1

We say that the topological extension

$$0 \rightarrow H\stackrel{i}{\rightarrow} X\stackrel{\pi}{\rightarrow} G\rightarrow 0$$

is a *split extension* (or splits) if it is equivalent to the trivial extension

$$0 \rightarrow H\stackrel{i_H}{\rightarrow} H\times G\stackrel{\pi_G}{\rightarrow} G\rightarrow 0$$

where $i_H: H\to H\times G$ is the natural inclusion over the first factor and $\pi_G: H\times G\to G$ is the natural projection over the second factor.

It is easy to prove that the topological extension $0 \rightarrow H\stackrel{i}{\rightarrow} X\stackrel{\pi}{\rightarrow} G\rightarrow 0$ splits if and only if there is a continuous homomorphism $R: X \rightarrow H$ such that $R\circ i$ is the identity map on $H$

Note that the topological extension $0 \rightarrow H\stackrel{i}{\rightarrow} X\stackrel{\pi}{\rightarrow} G\rightarrow 0$ splits if and only if the subgroup $i(H)$ is a [topological direct summand](wikipedia://en/43854082) of $X$

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Take $\mathbb R$ the [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) and $\mathbb Z$ the [integer numbers](wikipedia://en/14563). Take $\imath$ the natural inclusion and $\pi$ the natural projection. Then

$$0\to \mathbb Z\stackrel{\imath}{\to} \mathbb R \stackrel{\pi}{\to}\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\to 0$$

is an extension of topological abelian groups. Indeed it is an example of a non-splitting extension.

:::

## Extensions of locally compact abelian groups (LCA) {#Extensions_of_locally_compact_abelian_groups_(LCA)}

An extension of topological abelian groups will be a short exact sequence $0\to H\stackrel{\imath}{\to} X \stackrel{\pi}{\to}G\to 0$ where $H, X$ and $G$ are [locally compact abelian groups](wikipedia://en/1651204) and $i$ and $\pi$ are relatively open continuous homomorphisms.[^o7]

- Let be an extension of locally compact abelian groups

$$0\to H\stackrel{\imath}{\to} X \stackrel{\pi}{\to}G\to 0.$$

Take $H^\wedge, X^\wedge$ and $G^\wedge$ the [Pontryagin duals](wikipedia://en/366136) of $H, X$ and $G$ and take $i^\wedge$ and $\pi^\wedge$ the dual maps of $i$ and $\pi$. Then the sequence

$$0\to G^\wedge\stackrel{\pi^\wedge}{\to} X^\wedge \stackrel{\imath^\wedge}{\to}H^\wedge\to 0$$

is an extension of locally compact abelian groups.

## Extensions of topological abelian groups by the unit circle {#Extensions_of_topological_abelian_groups_by_the_unit_circle}

A very special kind of topological extensions are the ones of the form $0 \rightarrow \mathbb T\stackrel{i}{\rightarrow} X\stackrel{\pi}{\rightarrow} G\rightarrow 0$ where $\mathbb T$ is the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) and $X$ and $G$ are topological abelian groups.[^o9]

### The class *S*(T) {#The_class_S(T)}

A topological abelian group $G$ belongs to the class $\mathcal S (\mathbb T)$ if and only if every topological extension of the form $0 \rightarrow \mathbb T\stackrel{i}{\rightarrow} X\stackrel{\pi}{\rightarrow} G\rightarrow 0$ splits

- Every [locally compact abelian group](wikipedia://en/1651204) belongs to $\mathcal S (\mathbb T)$. In other words every topological extension $0 \rightarrow \mathbb T\stackrel{i}{\rightarrow} X\stackrel{\pi}{\rightarrow} G\rightarrow 0$ where $G$ is a locally compact abelian group, splits.
- Every locally precompact abelian group belongs to $\mathcal S (\mathbb T)$.
- The [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) (and in particular [topological abelian group](wikipedia://en/584136)) $\ell^1$ does not belong to $\mathcal S (\mathbb T)$.
