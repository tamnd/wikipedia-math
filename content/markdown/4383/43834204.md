# K-space (functional analysis)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="K-space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a **K-space** is an [F-space](wikipedia://en/200440) $V$ such that every extension of F-spaces (or twisted sum) of the form

$$0 \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \rightarrow X \rightarrow V \rightarrow 0. \,\!$$

is equivalent to the trivial one[^o2]

$$0\rightarrow \mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \times V \rightarrow V \rightarrow 0. \,\!$$

where $\mathbb{R}$ is the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line).

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [$\ell^p$ spaces](wikipedia://en/45194) for $0< p < 1$ are K-spaces,[^o2] as are all finite dimensional [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989).

N. J. Kalton and N. P. Roberts proved that the Banach space $\ell^1$ is not a K-space.[^o2]
