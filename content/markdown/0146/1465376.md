# Alfred Gray (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alfred Gray"}

**Alfred Gray** (October 22, 1939 – October 27, 1998) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) whose main research interests were in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). He also made contributions in the fields of [complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variable) and [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations).

:::

## Short biography {#Short_biography}

Alfred Gray was born in [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas,_Texas) to Alfred James Gray and Eloise Evans and studied mathematics at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas). He received a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) in 1964. His thesis, entitled *Minimal Varieties and Kahler Submanifolds*, was written under the supervision of [Leo Sario](wikipedia://en/40944268) and [Barrett O'Neill](wikipedia://en/53631967).[^o4]

He worked for four years at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). From 1970 until his death he was a professor at the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park).

He died in [Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilbao) of a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction) while working with students in a computer lab at Colegio Mayor Miguel de Unamuno around 4 a.m., on October 27, 1998.[^o5][^o6]

In 2022 the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) has established the *Mary and Alfie Gray Award for Social Justice*, named after Alfred Gray and his wife [Mary W. Gray](wikipedia://en/17848969), in order to support mathematicians working for human rights.[^o7][^o8]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Gray made several contributions in the classification of various types of [geometric structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-structure), such as [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538) and [almost Hermitian manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_Hermitian_manifold). For instance, together with [Fernández](wikipedia://en/68933833) and Gotay, he provided the first example of a symplectic manifold which does not admit a compatible complex structure, hence cannot be Kähler.[^o10]

Among other results, Gray introduced the concept of a [nearly Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/30272017), gave topological obstructions to the existence of geometrical structures, and made several contributions in the computation of the volume of [tubes](wikipedia://en/3192042) and [balls](wikipedia://en/160556), curvature identities, etc.

He published a book on tubes[^o11] and is the author of two textbooks and over one hundred scientific articles. His books were translated into [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language).

Gray was a pioneer in the use of [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics) in teaching differential geometry (particularly the geometry of curves and surfaces) and of using electronic computation in teaching both differential geometry and [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753).[^o6]
