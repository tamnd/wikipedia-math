# Superadditivity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Superadditivity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f$ is **superadditive** if $f(x+y) \geq f(x) + f(y)$ for all $x$ and $y$ in the [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) of $f.$

:::

Similarly, a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) $a_1, a_2, \ldots$ is called **superadditive** if it satisfies the [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489)

$$a_{n+m} \geq a_n + a_m$$

for all $m$ and $n.$

The term "superadditive" is also applied to functions from a [boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/54476844) to the real numbers where $P(X \lor Y) \geq P(X) + P(Y),$ such as [lower probabilities](wikipedia://en/3011778).

## Examples of superadditive functions {#Examples_of_superadditive_functions}

- The map $f(x) = x^2$ is a superadditive function for nonnegative [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) because $f(x + y) = (x + y)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + 2 x y = f(x) + f(y) + 2 x y \ge f(x) + f(y).$
- The [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) is superadditive for nonnegative [Hermitian matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_matrices), that is, if $A, B \in \text{Mat}_n(\mathbb{C})$ are nonnegative Hermitian then $\det(A + B) \geq \det(A) + \det(B).$ This follows from the [Minkowski determinant theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_determinant_theorem), which more generally states that $\det(\cdot)^{1/n}$ is superadditive (equivalently, [concave](wikipedia://en/263321))[^o3] for nonnegative Hermitian matrices of size $n$: If $A, B \in \text{Mat}_n(\mathbb{C})$ are nonnegative Hermitian then $\det(A + B)^{1/n} \geq \det(A)^{1/n} + \det(B)^{1/n}.$
- Horst Alzer proved[^o4] that [Hadamard's gamma function](wikipedia://en/49425654) $H(x)$ is superadditive for all real numbers $x, y$ with $x, y \geq 1.5031.$
- [Mutual information](wikipedia://en/427282)

## Properties {#Properties}

If $f$ is a superadditive function whose domain contains $0,$ then $f(0) \leq 0.$ To see this, simply set $x=0$ and $y=0$ in the defining inequality.

The negative of a superadditive function is [subadditive](wikipedia://en/745789).

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Fekete's lemma"}

## Fekete's lemma {#Fekete's_lemma}

The major reason for the use of superadditive sequences is the following [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) due to [Michael Fekete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fekete).[^o10]

**Lemma:** (Fekete) For every superadditive sequence $a_1, a_2, \ldots,$ the [limit](wikipedia://en/285773) $\lim a_n/n$ is equal to the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) $\sup a_n/n.$ (The limit may be positive infinity, as is the case with the sequence $a_n = \log n!$ for example.)

The analogue of Fekete's lemma holds for [subadditive](wikipedia://en/745789) functions as well. There are extensions of Fekete's lemma that do not require the definition of superadditivity above to hold for all $m$ and $n.$ There are also results that allow one to deduce the rate of convergence to the limit whose existence is stated in Fekete's lemma if some kind of both superadditivity and subadditivity is present. A good exposition of this topic may be found in Steele (1997).[^o11][^o12]

:::
