# Variational inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Variational inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **variational inequality** is an [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) involving a [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948), which has to be [solved](wikipedia://en/89489#Solving_Inequalities) for all possible values of a given [variable](wikipedia://en/3728109), belonging usually to a [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292). The [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) [theory](wikipedia://en/30746) of variational inequalities was initially developed to deal with [equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_point) problems, precisely the [Signorini problem](wikipedia://en/22194510): in that model problem, the functional involved was obtained as the [first variation](wikipedia://en/3655074) of the involved [potential energy](wikipedia://en/22194510#The_potential_energy). Therefore, it has a [variational origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_of_variation), recalled by the name of the general abstract problem. The applicability of the theory has since been expanded to include problems from [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance), [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29) and [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924).

:::

## History {#History}

The first problem involving a variational inequality was the [Signorini problem](wikipedia://en/22194510), posed by [Antonio Signorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Signorini_%28physicist%29) in 1959 and solved by [Gaetano Fichera](wikipedia://en/22175811) in 1963, according to the references (Antman 1983, 282–284) and (Fichera 1995): the first papers of the theory were (Fichera 1963) and (Fichera 1964a), (Fichera 1964b). Later on, [Guido Stampacchia](wikipedia://en/4872667) proved his generalization to the [Lax–Milgram theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lax–Milgram_theorem) in (Stampacchia 1964) in order to study the [regularity problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regularity_problem) for [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [coined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin) the name "variational inequality" for all the problems involving [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489) of this kind. [Georges Duvaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Duvaut) encouraged his [graduate students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_student) to study and expand on Fichera's work, after attending a conference in [Brixen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brixen) on 1965 where Fichera presented his study of the Signorini problem, as (Antman 1983, 283) reports: thus the theory become widely known throughout [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France). Also in 1965, Stampacchia and [Jacques-Louis Lions](wikipedia://en/3735500) extended earlier results of (Stampacchia 1964), announcing them in the paper (Lions Stampacchia 1965): full proofs of their results appeared later in the paper (Lions Stampacchia 1967).

## Definition {#Definition}

Following (Antman 1983, 283), the definition of a variational inequality is the following one.

(Definition 1.) Given a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) $\boldsymbol{E}$, a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) $\boldsymbol{K}$ of $\boldsymbol{E}$, and a functional $F\colon \boldsymbol{K}\to \boldsymbol{E}^{\ast}$ from $\boldsymbol{K}$ to the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) $\boldsymbol{E}^{\ast}$ of the space $\boldsymbol{E}$, the variational inequality problem is the problem of [solving](wikipedia://en/89489#Solving_Inequalities) for the [variable](wikipedia://en/3728109) $x$ belonging to $\boldsymbol{K}$ the following [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489):

$$\langle F(x), y-x \rangle \geq 0\qquad\forall y \in \boldsymbol{K}$$

where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon \boldsymbol{E}^{\ast}\times\boldsymbol{E}\to \mathbb{R}$ is the [duality pairing](wikipedia://en/7988).

In general, the variational inequality problem can be formulated on any [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) – or [infinite](wikipedia://en/142679)-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989). The three obvious steps in the study of the problem are the following ones:

1. Prove the existence of a solution: this step implies the *mathematical correctness* of the problem, showing that there is at least a solution.
1. Prove the uniqueness of the given solution: this step implies the *physical correctness* of the problem, showing that the solution can be used to represent a physical phenomenon. It is a particularly important step since most of the problems modeled by variational inequalities are of physical origin.
1. Find the solution or prove its regularity.

## Examples {#Examples}

### The problem of finding the minimal value of a real-valued function of real variable {#The_problem_of_finding_the_minimal_value_of_a_real-valued_function_of_real_variable}

This is a standard example problem, reported by (Antman 1983, 283): consider the problem of finding the [minimal value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum) of a [differentiable function](wikipedia://en/330206) $f$ over a [closed interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_interval) $I = [a,b]$. Let $x^{\ast}$ be a point in $I$ where the minimum occurs. Three cases can occur:

1. if $a<x^{\ast}< b,$ then $f^{\prime}(x^{\ast}) = 0;$
1. if $x^{\ast}=a,$ then $f^{\prime}(x^{\ast}) \ge 0;$
1. if $x^{\ast}=b,$ then $f^{\prime}(x^{\ast}) \le 0.$

These necessary conditions can be summarized as the problem of finding $x^{\ast}\in I$ such that

$f^{\prime}(x^{\ast})(y-x^{\ast}) \geq 0\quad$ for $\quad\forall y \in I.$

The absolute minimum must be searched between the solutions (if more than one) of the preceding [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489): note that the solution is a [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438), therefore this is a finite [dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28mathematics%29) variational inequality.

::: {#o21 .statement cue=named-heading name="The general finite-dimensional variational inequality"}

### The general finite-dimensional variational inequality {#The_general_finite-dimensional_variational_inequality}

A formulation of the general problem in $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the following: given a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) $K$ of $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ and a [mapping](wikipedia://en/516931) $F\colon K\to\mathbb{R}$, the [finite](wikipedia://en/11742)-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) variational inequality problem associated with $K$ consist of finding a [$n$-dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [vector](wikipedia://en/32533) $x$ belonging to $K$ such that

$$\langle \nabla F(x), y-x \rangle \geq 0\qquad\forall y \in K$$

where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}$ is the standard [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) on the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $\mathbb{R}^{n}$.

### The variational inequality for the Signorini problem {#The_variational_inequality_for_the_Signorini_problem}

*Image: Classical Signorini problem.svg.* The classical [Signorini problem](wikipedia://en/22194510): what will be the [equilibrium](wikipedia://en/301429#Elastostatics) [configuration](wikipedia://en/5918#Formulation_of_models) of the orange spherically shaped [elastic body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_body) resting on the blue [rigid](wikipedia://en/455769) [frictionless](wikipedia://en/11062) [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29)?

In the historical survey (Fichera 1995), [Gaetano Fichera](wikipedia://en/22175811) describes the genesis of his solution to the [Signorini problem](wikipedia://en/22194510): the problem consist in finding the [elastic equilibrium](wikipedia://en/301429#Elastostatics) [configuration](wikipedia://en/5918#Formulation_of_models) $\boldsymbol{u}(\boldsymbol{x}) =\left(u_1(\boldsymbol{x}),u_2(\boldsymbol{x}),u_3(\boldsymbol{x})\right)$ of an [anisotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisotropy#Material_science_and_engineering) [non-homogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_media) [elastic body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_body) that lies in a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) $A$ of the three-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) whose [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) is $\partial A$, resting on a [rigid](wikipedia://en/455769) [frictionless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frictionless_plane) [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) and subject only to its [mass forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight). The solution **$u$** of the problem exists and is unique (under precise assumptions) in the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of **admissible displacements** $\mathcal{U}_\Sigma$ i.e. the set of [displacement vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_vector) satisfying the system of [ambiguous boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/22194510#The_ambiguous_boundary_conditions) if and only if

$$B(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v} - \boldsymbol{u}) - F(\boldsymbol{v} - \boldsymbol{u}) \geq 0 \qquad \forall \boldsymbol{v} \in \mathcal{U}_\Sigma$$

where $B(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})$ and $F(\boldsymbol{v})$ are the following [functionals](wikipedia://en/1374948), written using the [Einstein notation](wikipedia://en/195407)

$B(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}) = -\int_A \sigma_{ik}(\boldsymbol{u})\varepsilon_{ik}(\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x$, $F(\boldsymbol{v}) = \int_A v_i f_i\,\mathrm{d}x + \int_{\partial A\setminus\Sigma}\!\!\!\!\! v_i g_i \,\mathrm{d}\sigma$, $\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v} \in \mathcal{U}_\Sigma$

where, for all $\boldsymbol{x}\in A$,

- $\Sigma$ is the [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_%28mechanics%29) [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) (or more generally a contact [set](wikipedia://en/26691)),
- $\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}) = \left( f_1(\boldsymbol{x}), f_2(\boldsymbol{x}), f_3(\boldsymbol{x}) \right)$ is the *[body force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_force)* applied to the body,
- $\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{x})=\left(g_1(\boldsymbol{x}),g_2(\boldsymbol{x}),g_3(\boldsymbol{x})\right)$ is the [surface force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_force) applied to $\partial A\!\setminus\!\Sigma$,
- $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})=\left(\varepsilon_{ik}(\boldsymbol{u})\right)=\left(\frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_k} + \frac{\partial u_k}{\partial x_i} \right)\right)$ is the [infinitesimal strain tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_strain#Infinitesimal_strain_tensor),
- $\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\left(\sigma_{ik}\right)$ is the [Cauchy stress tensor](wikipedia://en/3323565), defined as

$$\sigma_{ik}= - \frac{\partial W}{\partial \varepsilon_{ik}} \qquad\forall i,k=1,2,3$$

where $W(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})=a_{ikjh}(\boldsymbol{x})\varepsilon_{ik}\varepsilon_{jh}$ is the [elastic potential energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastic_potential_energy) and $\boldsymbol{a}(\boldsymbol{x})=\left(a_{ikjh}(\boldsymbol{x})\right)$ is the [elasticity tensor](wikipedia://en/7739252).
