# Lawrence Rooke

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lawrence Rooke"}

**Lawrence Rooke** (also Laurence) (1622–26 June 1662) was an English [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was also one of the founders of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society), although he died as it was being formally constituted.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Deptford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deptford), and was a great-nephew through his mother of [Lancelot Andrewes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancelot_Andrewes).[^o3]

He was educated at [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College) and [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), where he graduated M.A. in 1647. He became a fellow commoner at [Wadham College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadham_College,_Oxford) in 1650, having dropped out of academia for a period because of bad health.[^o5][^o6] At Wadham he worked closely with [John Wilkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wilkins) and [Seth Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Ward_%28bishop%29).[^o3]

He became Professor of Astronomy at [Gresham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham_College) in 1652, and then [Professor of Geometry](wikipedia://en/4470052) there, in 1657, an appointment in which [Oliver Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell) took an interest.[^o5][^o7]

He was unpublished in his lifetime, but left papers on [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) and the [moons of Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moons_of_Jupiter) that were published posthumously. He also wrote at the Royal Society's request a set of directions for sailors, on the correct way to record meteorological and oceanographic observations on their travels. These appeared in volume 1 of the Royal Society's *Philosophical Transactions* as *Directions for Sea-men, bound for far Voyages* (Phil. Trans. 1665 1 140–143) [doi:10.1098/rstl.1665.0066](http://rstl.royalsocietypublishing.org/content/1/1-22/140.full.pdf+html).[^o8]

[Montes Rook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montes_Rook), a circular mountain range on the moon, is named after Lawrence Rooke.
