# Erich Leo Lehmann

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Erich Leo Lehmann"}

**Erich Leo Lehmann** (20 November 1917 – 12 September 2009) was a German-born American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661), who made a major contribution to [nonparametric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonparametric) [hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis_testing).[^o3] He is one of the [eponyms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym) of the [Lehmann–Scheffé theorem](wikipedia://en/342602) and of the [Hodges–Lehmann estimator](wikipedia://en/9622185).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Lehmann was born in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), [Alsace-Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace-Lorraine) in 1917 to a family of [Ashkenazi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jewish) ancestry. He grew up in [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main), Germany, until the [Machtergreifung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung) in 1933, when his family fled to [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) to escape the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi).[^o3] He graduated from high school in Zurich, and studied mathematics for two years at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). Following that, he emigrated to the United States, arriving in New York in late 1940. He enrolled in [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) as a post-graduate student—albeit without a prior degree—in 1941.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Lehmann obtained his [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in mathematics in 1942 and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) (under [Jerzy Neyman](wikipedia://en/2386106)) in 1946, at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he taught from 1942.[^o5] From August 1944 to August 1945 he worked as an operations analyst for the [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force) on [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam).[^o5] He taught at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) during 1950–51, and then during 1951–1952 he was a visiting associate professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).

He was an editor of the *[Annals of Mathematical Statistics](wikipedia://en/23772805)* and president of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics), and a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).

In 1977 he married another statistician, [Juliet Popper Shaffer](wikipedia://en/55815025), whom he had met four years earlier as the sponsor to her sabbatical visit to Berkeley. In the same year, Shaffer moved from being a psychology professor at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas) to a lecturer position in statistics at Berkeley.[^o7]

In 1997, on the occasion of his eightieth birthday, the department of statistics at the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley) created the Erich Lehmann Fund in Statistics[^o8] to support the students of the department.
