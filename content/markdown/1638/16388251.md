# Monomial group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monomial group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the area of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) studying the [character theory](wikipedia://en/691741) of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707), an **M-group** or **monomial group** is a finite [group](wikipedia://en/19447) whose complex irreducible [characters](wikipedia://en/691741) are all [monomial](wikipedia://en/2786447), that is, [induced](wikipedia://en/357452) from characters of degree 1.[^n1]

:::

In this section only finite groups are considered. A monomial group is [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012).[^o3] Every [supersolvable group](wikipedia://en/4966866)[^n2] and every solvable [A-group](wikipedia://en/4938898)[^n3] is a monomial group. Factor groups of monomial groups are monomial, but subgroups need not be, since every finite solvable group can be embedded in a monomial group.[^o6]

The [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_4$ is an example of a monomial group that is neither supersolvable nor an [A-group](wikipedia://en/4938898). The [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) $\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_3)$ is the smallest finite group that is not monomial: since the abelianization of this group has order three, its irreducible characters of degree two are not monomial.
