# Biquaternion algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Biquaternion algebra"}

In mathematics, a **biquaternion algebra** is a compound of [quaternion algebras](wikipedia://en/2776890) over a field.

:::

The [biquaternions](wikipedia://en/1207070) of [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817) (1844) and the related [split-biquaternions](wikipedia://en/4166765) and [dual quaternions](wikipedia://en/8730871) do not form biquaternion algebras in this sense.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *F* be a field of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) not equal to 2. A *biquaternion algebra* over *F* is a [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of two [quaternion algebras](wikipedia://en/2776890).[^n1][^n2]

A biquaternion algebra is a [central simple algebra](wikipedia://en/404082) of dimension 16 and [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_of_a_central_simple_algebra) 4 over the base field: it has exponent (order of its [Brauer class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brauer_class) in the [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078) of *F*)[^n3] equal to 1 or 2.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Albert's theorem"}

## Albert's theorem {#Albert's_theorem}

Let *A* = (*a*<sub>1</sub>,*a*<sub>2</sub>) and *B* = (*b*<sub>1</sub>,*b*<sub>2</sub>) be quaternion algebras over *F*.

The **[Albert form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_form)** for *A*, *B* is

$$q = \left\langle{ -a_1, -a_2, a_1a_2, b_1, b_2, -b_1b_2 }\right\rangle \ .$$

It can be regarded as the difference in the [Witt ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witt_ring_%28forms%29) of the ternary forms attached to the imaginary subspaces of *A* and *B*.[^n4] The quaternion algebras are **[linked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linked_quaternion_algebras)** if and only if the Albert form is [isotropic](wikipedia://en/10917170), otherwise **unlinked**.[^n5]

[Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Adrian_Albert)'s theorem states that the following are equivalent:

- *A* ⊗ *B* is a [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625);
- The Albert form is [anisotropic](wikipedia://en/10917170);
- *A*, *B* are division algebras and they do not have a common quadratic splitting field.[^n6][^n7]

In the case of linked algebras we can further classify the other possible structures for the tensor product in terms of the Albert form. If the form is [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/10917170), then the biquaternion algebra is isomorphic to the algebra M<sub>4</sub>(*F*) of 4×4 matrices over *F*: otherwise, it is isomorphic to the product M<sub>2</sub>(*F*) ⊗ *D* where *D* is a quaternion division algebra over *F*.[^n2] The [Schur index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur_index) of a biquaternion algebra is 4, 2 or 1 according as the [Witt index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witt_index) of the Albert form is 0, 1 or 3.[^n8][^n9]

:::

## Characterisation {#Characterisation}

A theorem of Albert states that every central simple algebra of degree 4 and exponent 2 is a biquaternion algebra.[^n8][^n10]
