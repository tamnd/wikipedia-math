# C. J. F. Williams

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="C. J. F. Williams"}

**Christopher John Fardo Williams** (31 December 1930 – 25 March 1997)[^o3][^o4] was a British philosopher. His areas of interest were [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939), on which topic he did most of his original work, and [ancient philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_philosophy), as an editor and translator.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Christopher Williams was born in Walsall[^o3] and was educated at [Shrewsbury School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury_School) and [Balliol College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College), [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) where he took a First in Greats and became a convert to Roman Catholicism, a faith to which he was openly devoted for the rest of his life. Planning to enter the [Benedictine Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine_Order), he became a novice at [Downside Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downside_Abbey), but shortly thereafter was affected by polio which left him paralysed from the waist down.[^o4]

Instead he became an academic philosopher, lecturing at the [University of Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hull) before moving to Bristol, where he taught in the philosophy department as lecturer, Reader and Professor until his retirement in 1996.[^o3] Despite his disability, Williams commuted between Bristol and his home in [Midsomer Norton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsomer_Norton) and attended many philosophical conferences around the world. He also edited the philosophy journal, *[Analysis](wikipedia://en/7579257)*.

## Thought {#Thought}

Believing that such fundamental concepts as existence, truth and identity had been widely misunderstood by the philosophical tradition, and obfuscated especially by [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), Williams attempted to show that they could be elucidated by a close analysis of the way those and related terms are actually used. Williams was not, however, an [ordinary language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_language) philosopher; rather, he produced painstaking analyses of the concepts couched in the terms of symbolic logic. In this approach, founded in the logical work of [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416), he was most immediately influenced by [Arthur Prior](wikipedia://en/308764) and by his friend and fellow [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic) philosopher [Peter Geach](wikipedia://en/302884).

He summarized his views in a trilogy of books, *What is Truth?* (1976), *What is Existence?* (1981) and *What is Identity?* (1989), and produced a more accessible overview, with less emphasis on symbolic logic, in a single volume, *Being, Truth and Identity* (1992).[^o7][^o8]
