# Leon Lichtenstein

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leon Lichtenstein"}

**Leon Lichtenstein** (16 May 1878 – 21 August 1933) was a Polish-German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who made contributions to the areas of [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), [conformal mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_mapping), and [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342). He was also interested in theoretical physics, publishing research in [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamics) and [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Leon Lichtenstein was born on 16 May 1878 to an [Ashkenazi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jew) family in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), then part of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). His cousin, [Leo Wiener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Wiener), was the father of MIT mathematician [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185). He studied in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), earning both a doctorate in [mechanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) and [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) at the [Technische Hochschule Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Institute_of_Technology) and a doctorate in mathematics at the [Friedrich Wilhelm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) with a thesis on [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) written under the supervision of [Hermann Schwarz](wikipedia://en/3165834) and [Friedrich Schottky](wikipedia://en/5872505). From 1902 he worked as an electrical engineer for [Siemens & Halske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_&_Halske); then, from 1910, he turned to the academic world by becoming privatdozent at the Berlin Technische Hochschule. Lichtenstein was one of the founders, in 1918, and the first editor of the journal *[Mathematische Zeitschrift](wikipedia://en/7457514)*. In 1920 he moved to a mathematics chair at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster) and in 1922 he joined the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig) where he would spend the rest of his career. At the University of Leipzig, he founded a mathematical school and his students, including [Ernst Hölder](wikipedia://en/31518948), [Erich Kähler](wikipedia://en/383501), [Aurel Wintner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurel_Wintner), [Hermann Boerner](wikipedia://en/44021499) and [Karl Maruhn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Maruhn), continued his research in mathematics and theoretical physics.

In 1933, as the [Nazi party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_party) came to power in Germany, Lichtenstein abandoned his position at the university and left to [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic), as he would have been dismissed anyway[^o4] for being Jewish. Shortly after, on 21 August 1933, he died of heart and kidney problems in [Zakopane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakopane), in Poland.
