# Giovanni Battista Rizza

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Giovanni Battista Rizza"}

**Giovanni Battista Rizza** (7 February 1924 – 15 October 2018), officially known as **Giambattista Rizza**,[^o5] was an Italian mathematician, working in the fields of [complex analysis of several variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables) and in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625): he is known for his contribution to [hypercomplex analysis](wikipedia://en/26666199), notably for extending [Cauchy's integral theorem](wikipedia://en/61190) and [Cauchy's integral formula](wikipedia://en/72827) to complex functions of a [hypercomplex variable](wikipedia://en/51438),[^o6] the theory of [pluriharmonic functions](wikipedia://en/2519049) and for the introduction of the now called [Rizza manifolds](wikipedia://en/34461947).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: 1965 International Algebraic Geometry Symposium.jpg.* The International Symposium on Algebraic Geometry held in Rome in 1965. [Enrico Bompiani](wikipedia://en/48385220) talking to Giovanni Battista Rizza and [Vittorio Dalla Volta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_Dalla_Volta).

### Life and academic career {#Life_and_academic_career}

Born in [Piazza Armerina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_Armerina), the son of Giovanni and Angioletta Bocciarelli, he graduated from the [Università degli Studi di Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Università_degli_Studi_di_Genova), earning his [laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea) degree in 1949 under the direction of [Enzo Martinelli](wikipedia://en/30265224).[^o9] In 1956 he was in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) at the [INdAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INdAM), having been awarded a scholarship for his early research activities.[^o10][^o11] A year later, in 1957, he was elected "*discepolo ricercatore*"[^o12] in the same institute.[^o13] During the same year,[^o14] he gave some lectures on topics belonging to the field of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables),[^o15] later included in the lecture notes (Severi 1958).[^o17] In Rome he also met [Lucilla Bassotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucilla_Bassotti), who eventually become his wife. In 1961, he won the competitive examination for the chair of "Geometria analitica con elementi di Geometria Proiettiva e Geometria Descrittiva con Disegno" of the [University of Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Parma),[^o18] scoring first out of the three finalists:[^o19] a year later, in 1962, he became [extraordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraordinary_professor),[^o20] and then, in 1965, [ordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_professor) to the same chair.[^o21] In 1979 he became ordinary professor of "*Geometria superiore*",[^o22] holding that chair uninterruptedly until 1994:[^o23] from 1994 up to his retirement in 1997, he was "*professore fuori ruolo*" in the same department of mathematics where he worked for more than 35 years.[^o24]

Apart from his research and teaching work, he was actively involved as a member of the editorial board of the "*[Rivista di Matematica della Università di Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivista_di_Matematica_della_Università_di_Parma)*", and served also as the journal director from 1992 to 1997.[^o25]

Rizza died in Parma on 15 October 2018, at the age of 94.[^o26][^o27]

### Honors {#Honors}

In 1954 he was awarded the [Ottorino Pomini prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottorino_Pomini_prize) by the [Unione Matematica Italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unione_Matematica_Italiana), jointly with [Gabriele Darbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriele_Darbo): the judging commission was composed by [Giovanni Sansone](wikipedia://en/34941945) (as the president), [Alessandro Terracini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Terracini), [Beniamino Segre](wikipedia://en/3534031), [Giuseppe Scorza-Dragoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Scorza-Dragoni), [Carlo Miranda](wikipedia://en/49974918), [Mario Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Villa) and [Enzo Martinelli](wikipedia://en/30265224) (as the secretary).[^o2]

In 1973 he was awarded the [golden medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_medal) "[Benemeriti della Scuola, della Cultura, dell'Arte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benemeriti_della_Scuola,_della_Cultura,_dell'Arte)" by the [President of the Italian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Italian_Republic),[^o3] as an acknowledgement his research and teaching and achievements as civil servant at the University of Parma.[^o29]

In 1995, to celebrate his 70th birthday, an international conference on differential geometry was organized in Parma: the [proceedings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings) were later published as a special issue of the "Rivista di Matematica della Università di Parma".[^o30] In 1999 the University of Parma, where he worked for more than 35 years, awarded him the title of [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus).[^o31]

Rizza was an honorary member of the [Balkan Society of Geometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Society_of_Geometers) and life member of the [Tensor Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_Society).[^o32]

### Personality traits {#Personality_traits}

[Enzo Martinelli](wikipedia://en/30265224) described Giovanni Battista Rizza as a passionate researcher with a "strong intellectual force",[^o34] and his scientific work as rich of [geometrical](wikipedia://en/18973446) ideas, denoting his strong [algorithmic](wikipedia://en/775) ability.[^o35] According to Martinelli, Rizza is also a skilled organizer:[^o36] his ability in organizational tasks is also acknowledged and praised by (Schreiber 1973, 1), who also alludes the positive opinions of colleagues and students alike about his involvement in research, teaching and administrative duties at the mathematics department of the [University of Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Parma).

## Work {#Work}

### Research activity {#Research_activity}

Giovanni Battista Rizza authored 53 research papers and 30 other scientific works, including research announcements, short notes, surveys and reports: he also wrote didactic notes and papers on historical topics, including commemorations of other scientists.[^o40] His main fields of research were the [theory of functions on algebras](wikipedia://en/26666199), the [theory of functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables), and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

#### Theory of functions on algebras {#Theory_of_functions_on_algebras}

The theory of functions on algebras, also referred to as [hypercomplex analysis](wikipedia://en/26666199), is the study of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) whose [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788).[^o42] The first works of Giovanni Battista Rizza belong to this field of research, and he was awarded the [Premio Ottorino Pomini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premio_Ottorino_Pomini) for his contributions.[^o6]

His first main result is the extension of [Cauchy's integral theorem](wikipedia://en/61190) to every [monogenic function](wikipedia://en/66390572) $F$ on a general [complex algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) $\boldsymbol{A}$,[^o43]

$$\int_{\Gamma_1} \mathrm{F}(\mathrm{X}) \mathrm{d}\mathrm{X}=0$$

where $\Gamma_{1}$ is a [1-dimensional cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_%28algebraic_topology%29) homologous to zero, and also satisfying other technical conditions.

Few years later, he extended [Cauchy's integral formula](wikipedia://en/72827) to every [monogenic function](wikipedia://en/66390572) $F$ on a [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29) [normed](wikipedia://en/1991870) [real algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) $\boldsymbol{A}^{*}$,[^o44] [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_%28algebra%29) to a given complex algebra $\boldsymbol{A}$:[^o45] precisely, he proves the formula

$$\int_{\Gamma_1}\frac{\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{X})}{\mathrm{X}-\Xi}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{X}=2\pi i\sum^k_{s=1}\mathrm{N}^{(s)}u^{(s)}\mathrm{F}(\Xi)$$

where

- $X \equiv x^{*} \equiv x$ identifies indifferently a [point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_%28mathematics%29) in the complex algebra $\boldsymbol{A}$ or in its isomorphic real algebra $\boldsymbol{A}^{*}$,
- $\Gamma_{1}$ is again a [1-dimensional cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_%28algebraic_topology%29) homologous to zero, and satisfying other technical conditions,
- $N^{(s)}$ is the [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113) of the cycle $\Gamma_{1}$ respect to the [zero divisor](wikipedia://en/51441) [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) for the considered algebra.

#### Theory of analytic functions of several complex variables {#Theory_of_analytic_functions_of_several_complex_variables}

Rizza published only three work in this field:[^o49] in the first one, the highly remarkable memoir (Rizza 1955),[^o51] he extends to pluriharmonic functions of $2n$ [real variables](wikipedia://en/1268958), $n > 2$, the methods introduced by Enzo Martinelli in order to give new proof of a result of [Luigi Amoroso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Amoroso) for pluriharmonic functions of four real variables.[^o52] Precisely, he proves the following formula

::: {#o53 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial \nu} = \frac{\vert\nabla \rho\vert^3}{Q(\rho)} E u \tag{1}$$

:::

where

- $u$ is a polyharmonic function defined on a [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258) [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) $\Omega$,
- $\rho$ is a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) defining the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of $\Omega$ by the equation

$$\partial\Omega = \{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}|\rho(x)=0\},$$

- $Q(\rho)$ is a linear combination of the [Levi forms](wikipedia://en/3131794#The_Levi_form) of $\rho$ relative to couples of [complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variable),
- $E$ is a linear [tangential operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangential_operator) defined on $\partial\Omega$.

Formula ((1)) express a condition the [normal derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_derivative) of the boundary value of a pluriharmonic function on domain with real analytic boundary must satisfy.[^o54] It can be used to construct an integral representation for pluriharmonic functions on such kind of domains, by using the [Green's formula](wikipedia://en/805700#Green's_third_identity) for the [Laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian),[^o55] and also to establish an [integro-differential equation](wikipedia://en/11095324) boundary values of pluriharmonic functions must satisfy.[^o56] Rizza's result motivated other works on the same topic by Gaetano Fichera, Paolo de Bartolomeis and [Giuseppe Tomassini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Tomassini).[^o57]

- Fichera, Gaetano. (1982a). "Atti del Convegno celebrativo dell'80° anniversario della nascita di Renato Calapso, Messina–Taormina, 1–4 aprile 1981". Libreria Eredi Virgilio Veschi. p. 127–152. mr:0698973. zbl:0958.32504. "Boundary value problems for pluriharmonic functions" (English translation of the title) deals with [boundary value problems](wikipedia://en/436779) for pluriharmonic functions: Fichera gives a [trace condition](wikipedia://en/11199199) for the solvability of the problem and extensively reviews its history, starting from its beginning in the work of [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) and analyzing several earlier results of Enzo Martinelli, Giovanni Battista Rizza and Francesco Severi, as well as works of [Aldo Andreotti](wikipedia://en/34935909) among the others.
- Fichera, Gaetano. (1982b). "Valori al contorno delle funzioni pluriarmoniche: estensione allo spazio di un teorema di L. Amoroso". *Rendiconti del Seminario Matematico e Fisico di Milano*. 52 (1). p. 23–34. doi:10.1007/BF02924996. mr:0802991. zbl:0569.31006. In this work Gaetano Fichera proves another trace condition for pluriharmonic functions and surveys other recent works in the fields, notably the one of (de Bartolomeis Tomassini 1981).
- Fuks, B. A. (1963). "Introduction to the Theory of Analytic Functions of Several Complex Variables". 8. American Mathematical Society. p. vi+374. isbn:9780821886441. mr:0168793. zbl:0138.30902. <https://books.google.com/books?id=OSlWYzf2FcwC>.
- Ichijyō, Yoshihiro. (1988). "Finsler metrics on almost complex manifolds". *Rivista di Matematica della Università di Parma*. 14\*. p. 1–28. mr:1045035. zbl:0885.53031. <http://rivista.math.unipr.it/fulltext/1988-14s/1988-14s-001.pdf>.
- Kobayashi, Shoshichi. (1975). "Negative vector bundles and complex Finsler structures". *Nagoya Mathematical Journal*. 57. p. 153–166. doi:10.1017/S0027763000016615. mr:0377126. zbl:0326.32016. In this paper, [Shoshichi Kobayashi](wikipedia://en/20695112) acknowledges Giovanni Battista Rizza as the first one to study [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742) with [Finsler structure](wikipedia://en/385510), now called [Rizza manifolds](wikipedia://en/34461947).
- Martinelli, Enzo. (1941). "Studio di alcune questioni della teoria delle funzioni biarmoniche e delle funzioni analitiche di due variabili complesse coll'ausilio del calcolo differenziale assoluto". *Atti della Reale Accademia d'Italia. Memorie della Classe di Scienze Fisiche, Matematiche e Naturali*. 12 (4). p. 143–167. mr:0017810. zbl:0025.40503. In this work Martinelli proves an earlier result of [Luigi Amoroso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Amoroso) on the boundary values of pluriharmonic function by using [tensor calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_calculus).
- Scharnhorst, K. (2001). "Angles in Complex Vector Spaces". *Acta Applicandae Mathematicae*. 69 (1). p. 95–103. doi:10.1023/A:1012692601098. arxiv:math/9904077. mr:1868915. zbl:0993.51010.
- Severi, Francesco. (1958). "Lezioni sulle funzioni analitiche di più variabili complesse – Tenute nel 1956–57 all'Istituto Nazionale di Alta Matematica in Roma". CEDAM – Casa Editrice Dott. Antonio Milani. p. XIV+255. zbl:0094.28002. A set of lecture notes from a course held by Francesco Severi at the [Istituto Nazionale di Alta Matematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Nazionale_di_Alta_Matematica), including appendices of Enzo Martinelli, Giovanni Battista Rizza and [Mario Benedicty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Benedicty).
