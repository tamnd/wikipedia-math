# Operator K-theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Operator K-theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **operator K-theory** is a [noncommutative](wikipedia://en/662624) analogue of [topological K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468) for [Banach algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_algebras) with most applications used for [C\*-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*-algebras).

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

Operator K-theory resembles topological K-theory more than [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500). In particular, a [Bott periodicity theorem](wikipedia://en/782099) holds. So there are only two K-groups, namely *K*<sub>0</sub>, which is equal to algebraic *K*<sub>0</sub>, and *K*<sub>1</sub>. As a consequence of the periodicity theorem, it satisfies [excision](wikipedia://en/1248603). This means that it associates to an [extension](wikipedia://en/2125) of [C\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/7184) to a [long exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_exact_sequence), which, by Bott periodicity, reduces to an exact cyclic 6-term-sequence.

Operator K-theory is a generalization of [topological K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468), defined by means of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) on [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/13637). Here, a vector bundle over a topological space *X* is associated to a projection in the C\* algebra of matrix-valued—that is, $M_n(\mathbb{C})$-valued—continuous functions over *X*. Also, it is known that isomorphism of vector bundles translates to Murray-von Neumann equivalence of the associated projection in *K* ⊗ *C*(*X*), where *K* is the compact operators on a separable Hilbert space.

Hence, the *K*<sub>0</sub> group of a (not necessarily commutative) C\*-algebra *A* is defined as [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790) generated by the Murray-von Neumann equivalence classes of projections in *K* ⊗ *C*(*X*). *K*<sub>0</sub> is a functor from the category of C\*-algebras and \*-homomorphisms, to the category of abelian groups and group homomorphisms. The higher K-functors are defined via a C\*-version of the suspension: *K*<sub>n</sub>(*A*) = *K*<sub>0</sub>(*S*<sup>*n*</sup>(*A*)), where *SA* = *C*<sub>0</sub>(0,1) ⊗ *A*.

However, by Bott periodicity, it turns out that *K*<sub>*n*+2</sub>(*A*) and *K*<sub>*n*</sub>(*A*) are isomorphic for each *n*, and thus the only groups produced by this construction are *K*<sub>0</sub> and *K*<sub>1</sub>.

The key reason for the introduction of K-theoretic methods into the study of C\*-algebras was the [Fredholm index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredholm_index): Given a bounded linear operator on a Hilbert space that has finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel, one can associate to it an integer, which, as it turns out, reflects the 'defect' on the operator - i.e. the extent to which it is not invertible. The Fredholm index map appears in the 6-term exact sequence given by the [Calkin algebra](wikipedia://en/945565). In the analysis on manifolds, this index and its generalizations played a crucial role in the [index theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiyah-Singer_index_theorem) of Atiyah and Singer, where the topological index of the manifold can be expressed via the index of elliptic operators on it. Later on, [Brown](wikipedia://en/48948022), [Douglas](wikipedia://en/14044625) and [Fillmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_A._Fillmore) observed that the Fredholm index was the missing ingredient in classifying [essentially normal operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essentially_normal_operator) up to certain natural equivalence. These ideas, together with [Elliott](wikipedia://en/33962087)'s classification of [AF C\*-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AF_C*-algebra) via K-theory led to a great deal of interest in adapting methods such as K-theory from algebraic topology into the study of operator algebras.

This, in turn, led to [K-homology](wikipedia://en/3021282), [Kasparov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennadi_Kasparov)'s bivariant [KK-theory](wikipedia://en/13314087), and, more recently, [Connes](wikipedia://en/340) and [Higson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Higson)'s [E-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-theory).
