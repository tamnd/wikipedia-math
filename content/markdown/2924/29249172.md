# Toida's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toida's conjecture"}

In [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) mathematics, **Toida's conjecture**, due to [Shunichi Toida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunichi_Toida) in 1977,[^o2] is a refinement of the disproven [Ádám's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ádám's_conjecture) from 1967.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Both [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) concern [circulant graphs](wikipedia://en/3498787). These are graphs defined from a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $n$ and a set $S$ of positive integers. Their vertices can be identified with the numbers from 0 to $n-1$, and two vertices $i$ and $j$ are connected by an edge whenever their difference [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) $n$ belongs to set $S$. Every [symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_automorphism) of the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of addition modulo $n$ gives rise to a [symmetry](wikipedia://en/15094186) of the $n$-vertex circulant graphs, and Ádám conjectured (incorrectly) that these are the only symmetries of the circulant graphs.

However, the known [counterexamples](wikipedia://en/143151) to Ádám's conjecture involve sets $S$ in which some elements share non-trivial [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) with $n$. Toida's conjecture states that, when every member of $S$ is [relatively prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_prime) to $n$, then the only symmetries of the circulant graph for $n$ and $S$ are symmetries coming from the underlying cyclic group.

:::

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proofs"}

## Proofs {#Proofs}

The conjecture was proven in the special case where *n* is a [prime power](wikipedia://en/1630483) by Klin and Poschel in 1978,[^o7] and by Golfand, Najmark, and Poschel in 1984.[^o8]

The conjecture was then fully proven by Muzychuk, Klin, and Poschel in 2001 by using [Schur algebra](wikipedia://en/26339669),[^o9] and simultaneously by Dobson and [Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_Morris) in 2002 by using the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445).[^o10]

:::
