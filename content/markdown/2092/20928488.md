# List of countries by past and projected future population

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of countries by past and projected future population"}

*Image: 2024 Our World In Data Population, 1800 to 2100 chart with future projections based on UN's medium-fertility scenario.png.* Population of the present-day top seven most-populous countries, 1800 to 2100. Future projections are based on the 2024 UN's medium-fertility scenario. Chart created by [Our World In Data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_World_in_Data) in 2024.

This article contains a **list of countries by past and projected future population**. This assumes that countries stay constant in the unforeseeable future, and does not take into account possible border changes.

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

All the figures shown here have been sourced from the International Database (IDB) Division of the [United States Census Bureau](wikipedia://en/57070). Every individual value has been rounded to the nearest thousand, to assure data coherence, particularly when adding up (sub)totals. Although data from specific statistical offices may be more accurate, the information provided here has the advantage of being homogeneous.

Population estimates, as long as they are based on recent censuses, can be more easily projected into the *near* future than many [macroeconomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomics) indicators, such as [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_domestic_product), which are much more sensitive to political and/or economic crises. This means that [demographic](wikipedia://en/45642) estimates for the next five (or even ten) years can be more accurate than the projected evolution of GDP over the same time period (which may also be distorted by [inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation)).

However, no projected population figures can be considered exact. As the IDB states, "figures beyond the years 2020–2025 should be taken with caution", as the "census way towards those years has yet to be paved". Thus projections can be said to be looking through a kind of "cloudy glass"[^o3] or a "misty window": realistically, the projections are "[guesstimates](wikipedia://en/2481546)".

To make matters more complicated, not all countries carry out censuses regularly, especially some of the poorer, faster-growing [sub-Saharan African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Saharan_Africa) nations (whose evolution may be more interesting, from a demographer's point of view, than the "stagnated" populations of countries like [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) or [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy)). The populations of many of these countries, as well as other primarily [conservative, Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_population_growth) nations like [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt), [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq), and [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan), are growing much faster due to their high [fertility rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate) than in the [aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_ageing) [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) nations or [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan).

On the other hand, some other countries, like the small [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia) state of [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan), have only recently had a thorough census for the first time: In Bhutan's case in particular, before its national 2005 population survey,[^o4][^o5][^o6] the IDB estimated its population at over 2 million; this was drastically reduced when the new census results proved to be 672,000.

Further, the IDB usually takes some time before including new data, as happened in the case of Indonesia. That country was reported by the IDB to have an inflated population of some 242 million by mid-2005, because it had not still processed the final results of the 2000 Indonesian census.[^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10] There was a similar discrepancy with the relatively recent [Ethiopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia) 2007 census,[^o11][^o12] which gave a preliminary result of "only" 73,918,505 inhabitants.

The largest absolute potential discrepancies are naturally related to the most populous nations. However, smaller states, such as [Tuvalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuvalu), can have large *relative* discrepancies. For instance, the 2002 census in that [Oceanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania) island, which gave a final population of 9,561[^o13] shows that IDB estimates can be significantly off.

## Preliminary notes {#Preliminary_notes}

The national 1 July, mid-year population estimates (usually based on past national censuses) supplied in these tables are given in thousands.

The retrospective figures use the present-day names and world political division: for example, the table gives data for each of the 15 republics of the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), as if they had already been independent in 1950. The opposite is the case for [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), which had been divided since the end of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) but was [reunified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) on October 3, 1990.

## Formulas used to calculate demographic growth {#Formulas_used_to_calculate_demographic_growth}

To the right of each year column (except for the initial 1950 one), a percentage figure is shown, which gives the *average annual* growth for the previous five-year period. Thus, the figures after the 1960 column show the percentage annual growth for the 1955-60 period; the figures after the 1980 column calculate the same value for 1975–80; and so on.

The formulas used for the annual growth rates are the standard ones, used both by the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) Statistics Division and by National Census Offices worldwide. They are [compound growth rates](wikipedia://en/434428), and have the general form:

$$\left ( \sqrt[y_f-y_i]{\frac{p_f}{p_i}} -1 \right ) \times 100$$

where $p_i$ and $p_f$ stand for the initial and final population, respectively, within a stated time period. Similarly $y_i$ and $y_f$ are the dates of the initial and final years.

In the calculations shown here, all periods are of five years, so2*y*<sub>*i*</sub> + 5 = *y*<sub>*f*</sub>2and the formula simplifies to:

$$\left ( \sqrt[5]{\frac{p_f}{p_i}} -1 \right ) \times 100.$$
