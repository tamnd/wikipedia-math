# Volterra lattice

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Volterra lattice"}

In mathematics, the **Volterra lattice**, also known as the **discrete KdV equation**, the **Kac–van Moerbeke lattice**, and the **Langmuir lattice**, is a system of ordinary differential equations with variables indexed by some of the points of a 1-dimensional [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246). It was introduced by (txt) and (txt) and is named after [Vito Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Volterra). The Volterra lattice is a special case of the [generalized Lotka–Volterra equation](wikipedia://en/24018375) describing predator–prey interactions, for a sequence of species with each species preying on the next in the sequence. The Volterra lattice also behaves like a discrete version of the [KdV equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KdV_equation). The Volterra lattice is an [integrable system](wikipedia://en/2573213), and is related to the [Toda lattice](wikipedia://en/3240033). It is also used as a model for [Langmuir waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langmuir_wave) in plasmas.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Volterra lattice is the set of ordinary differential equations for functions *a*<sub>*n*</sub>:

$$a_n'=a_n(a_{n+1}-a_{n-1})$$

where *n* is an integer. Usually one adds boundary conditions: for example, the functions *a*<sub>*n*</sub> could be periodic: *a*<sub>*n*</sub> = *a*<sub>*n*+*N*</sub> for some *N*, or could vanish for *n* ≤ 0 and *n* ≥ *N*.

The Volterra lattice was originally stated in terms of the variables *R*<sub>*n*</sub> = -log *a*<sub>*n*</sub> in which case the equations are

$$R_n'=e^{R_{n-1}}-e^{R_{n+1}}$$

:::
