# R. L. Hudson

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="R. L. Hudson"}

**Robin Lyth Hudson** (4 May 1940 – 12 January 2021) was a British mathematician notable for his contribution to [quantum probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_probability).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hudson received his Ph.D. from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) in 1966 under [John T. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_T._Lewis) with a thesis entitled *Generalised Translation-Invariant Mechanics*. He was appointed assistant lecturer at the University of Nottingham in 1964, promoted to a chair in 1985 and served as head of department from 1987 to 1990. He spent sabbatical semesters in Heidelberg (1978), [Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) (1983), and [Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder) (1996). After taking early retirement in 1997, he held part-time research posts at Nottingham Trent University (1997–2005), the Slovak Academy of Sciences (1997–2000) and Loughborough University (2005–21), and a visiting professorship at the University of Łódź (2002) which awarded him an honorary doctorate in 2013.

Hudson was a mathematical physicist who was one of the pioneers of [quantum probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_probability). An early result,[^o5] now known as Hudson's theorem in quantum optics, shows that the pure quantum states with positive [Wigner quasiprobability distribution](wikipedia://en/1884336) are the Gaussian ones. Together with PhD students, Hudson established one of the first quantum central limit theorems,[^o6] proved an early quantum de Finetti theorem,[^o7] and introduced quantum Brownian motion[^o8] as a non-commuting pair of families of unbounded operators, using the formalism of quantum field theory. He collaborated with [K. R. Parthasarathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._R._Parthasarathy_%28probabilist%29) first at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester), and later at [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham) and at [Loughborough University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughborough_University), on their seminal work in [quantum stochastic calculus](wikipedia://en/41263617).[^o9][^o10][^o11]

In later papers he developed a theory of quantum stochastic double product integrals and their application to the quantum [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810), the quantisation of [Lie bialgebras](wikipedia://en/4290894) and quantum Lévy area.[^o12]
