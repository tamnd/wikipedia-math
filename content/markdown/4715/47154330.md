# Siegmund Günther

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Siegmund Günther"}

**Adam Wilhelm Siegmund Günther** (6 February 1848 – 3 February 1923) was a German geographer, mathematician, historian of mathematics and natural scientist.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Born in 1848 to a German businessman, Günther would go on to attend several German universities including [Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlangen_University), [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University), [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University), [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_University), and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_University).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

In 1872 he began teaching at a school in Weissenburg, [Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria). He completed his [habilitation thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation_thesis) on [continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199) entitled *Darstellung der Näherungswerte der Kettenbrüche in independenter Form* in 1873. The next year he began teaching at [Munich Polytechnicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Polytechnicum). In 1876, he began teaching at a university in [Ansbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansbach) where he stayed for several years before moving to Munich and becoming a professor of geography until he retired; he served as the university's rector from 1911 to 1913.[^o5]

For some years, Günther was a member of the federal parliament, the [Reichstag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_%28German_Empire%29), and later the Bavarian parliament, representing liberal parties.[^o7]

His mathematical work[^o5] included works on the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468), [hyperbolic functions](wikipedia://en/56567), and *parabolic logarithms and trigonometry*.[^o8]
