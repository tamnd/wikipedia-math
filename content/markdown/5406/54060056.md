# 'Mamphono Khaketla

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="'Mamphono Khaketla"}

**'Mamphono Khaketla** (born 5 March 1960) is a [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senator) who served as [Minister of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_%28Lesotho%29) from March 2015 to June 2017.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Khaketla was born in [Maseru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maseru) on 5 March 1960 to Bennett Makalo and [Caroline Ntseliseng ’Masechele Khaketla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Ntseliseng_’Masechele_Khaketla).[^o4] Her father was a novelist, journalist, politician and former minister, as well as the major shareholder of Mohlabani Property Company, and left her a sizeable estate.[^o5][^o6] Her mother was a teacher and author, one of the first women published in Lesotho.[^o7][^o8]

Khaketla did her primary and secondary schooling Maseru, before receiving a [Bachelor of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Education) from the [National University of Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Lesotho) in 1980.[^o7] She has a master's degree in education and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471) from the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin) (1991).[^o7] Her thesis was titled "An analysis of the Lesotho Junior Certificate Mathematics Examination and its impact on instructions".[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Khaketla was a lecturer in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the National Teacher Training College from 1981 until 1995 and became assistant director of the college.[^o7] She worked at the Institute of Development Management in Lesotho and [Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana) from 1996 until 2001 before becoming the director of the Centre for Accounting Studies.

Khaketla was appointed as a senator by [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Prime_Ministers_of_Lesotho) [Pakalitha Mosisili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakalitha_Mosisili) in 2002 and served as Minister of Communications, Science and Technology from 2002 until 2004.[^o7] At the [2007 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Lesotho_general_election), she lost her seat but was elected to the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_%28Lesotho%29) as one of the [Lesotho Congress for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho_Congress_for_Democracy) members on a party list for [proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_representation) submitted by the National Independence Party.[^o7] She served as Minister of Education and Training from 2007 until 2012. In 2011, Khaketla was one of seven women ministers in the Cabinet, alongside: [Mannete Ramali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannete_Ramaili), [Maphoka Motoboli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maphoka_Motoboli), [Mathabiso Lepono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathabiso_Lepono), [Mphu Keneileo Ramatlapeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mphu_Keneileo_Ramatlapeng), [Mpeo Mahase-Moiloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpeo_Mahase-Moiloa) and [Pontso Suzan Matumelo Sekatle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontso_Suzan_Matumelo_Sekatle).[^o10] On 30 March 2015 she was appointed [Minister of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_%28Lesotho%29).

In November 2015, she presided over the 102nd session of the [African, Caribbean and Pacific Group of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African,_Caribbean_and_Pacific_Group_of_States) Council of Ministers.[^o11]

In July 2016, Khaketla was accused of soliciting a bribe for a major government contract in a case that was before the courts. She denied the allegation.[^o12][^o13]
