# Liouville–Bratu–Gelfand equation

*For Liouville's equation in differential geometry, see [Liouville's equation](wikipedia://en/12677528).*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Liouville–Bratu–Gelfand equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Liouville–Bratu–Gelfand equation** or **Liouville's equation** is a non-linear [Poisson equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_equation), named after the mathematicians [Joseph Liouville](wikipedia://en/341810),[^o2] Gheorghe Bratu[^o3] and [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643).[^o4] The equation reads

:::

$$\nabla^2 \psi + \lambda e^\psi = 0$$

The equation appears in [thermal runaway](wikipedia://en/2350918) as [Frank-Kamenetskii theory](wikipedia://en/54055676), [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics) for example, [Emden–Chandrasekhar equation](wikipedia://en/54056040). This equation also describes [space charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_charge) of electricity around a glowing wire[^o5] and describes [planetary nebula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_nebula).

## Liouville's solution {#Liouville's_solution}

Source:[^o7]

In two dimension with [Cartesian Coordinates](wikipedia://en/7706) $(x,y)$, [Joseph Liouville](wikipedia://en/341810) proposed a solution in 1853 as

$$\lambda e^\psi (u^2 + v^2 + 1) ^2 = 2 \left[\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)^2\right]$$

where $f(z)=u + i v$ is an arbitrary [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) with $z=x+iy$. In 1915, G.W. Walker[^o8] found a solution by assuming a form for $f(z)$. If $r^2=x^2+y^2$, then Walker's solution is

$$8 e^{-\psi} = \lambda \left[\left(\frac{r}{a}\right)^n + \left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^n\right]^2$$

where $a$ is some finite radius. This solution decays at infinity for any $n$, but becomes infinite at the origin for $n<1$ , becomes finite at the origin for $n=1$ and becomes zero at the origin for $n>1$. Walker also proposed two more solutions in his 1915 paper.

## Radially symmetric forms {#Radially_symmetric_forms}

If the system to be studied is radially symmetric, then the equation in $n$ dimension becomes

$$\psi'' + \frac{n-1}{r}\psi' + \lambda e^\psi=0$$

where $r$ is the distance from the origin. With the boundary conditions

$$\psi'(0)=0, \quad \psi(1) = 0$$

and for $\lambda\geq 0$, a real solution exists only for $\lambda \in [0,\lambda_c]$, where $\lambda_c$ is the critical parameter called as [**Frank-Kamenetskii parameter**](wikipedia://en/54055676). The critical parameter is $\lambda_c=0.8785$ for $n=1$, $\lambda_c=2$ for $n=2$ and $\lambda_c=3.32$ for $n=3$. For $n=1, \ 2$, two solution exists and for $3\leq n\leq 9$ infinitely many solution exists with solutions oscillating about the point $\lambda=2(n-2)$. For $n\geq 10$, the solution is unique and in these cases the critical parameter is given by $\lambda_c=2(n-2)$. Multiplicity of solution for $n=3$ was discovered by [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643) in 1963 and in later 1973 generalized for all $n$ by [Daniel D. Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_D._Joseph) and [Thomas S. Lundgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_S._Lundgren).[^o10]

The solution for $n=1$ that is valid in the range $\lambda \in [0,0.8785]$ is given by

$$\psi = -2 \ln \left[e^{-\psi_m/2}\cosh \left(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\sqrt 2}e^{-\psi_m/2}r\right)\right]$$

where $\psi_m=\psi(0)$ is related to $\lambda$ as

$$e^{\psi_m/2} = \cosh \left(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\sqrt 2}e^{-\psi_m/2}\right).$$

The solution for $n=2$ that is valid in the range $\lambda \in [0,2]$ is given by

$$\psi = \ln \left[\frac{64e^{\psi_m}}{(\lambda e^{\psi_m}r^2+8)^2}\right]$$

where $\psi_m=\psi(0)$ is related to $\lambda$ as

$$(\lambda e^{\psi_m}+8)^2 - 64 e^{\psi_m} =0.$$
