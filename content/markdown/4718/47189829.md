# J. F. Cameron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="J. F. Cameron"}

**John Forbes Cameron** (July 1873 – 21 March 1952) was a Scottish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), academic and [academic administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_administrator). He was [Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_%28college%29) of [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge) from 1928 to 1948 and was [Vice-Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Chancellor) of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) from 1933 to 1935.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Cameron was born in July 1873 in [Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley,_Perthshire), [Perthshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perthshire), Scotland.[^o4][^o5] He was educated at [Perth Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Academy), a [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school#United_Kingdom) [high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school#United_Kingdom) in [Perth, Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth,_Scotland).[^o4] He studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy) at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh).[^o6] He was awarded a Ferguson Scholarship, available to graduates of Scottish universities, to attend the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). He studied the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) at [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge) from 1895 to 1898.[^o6] He was tutored by [R. R Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rumsey_Webb) and completed his degree as [second wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29).[^o4]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

In 1899, Cameron was elected a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge). The following year, in 1900, he was appointed a [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) in mathematics and in 1909 was appointed a tutor.[^o6]

During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), from 1914 to 1918, he left the college to work at the [Ministry of Munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Munitions).[^o5][^o6]

After the war, he returned to Gonville and Caius College and was appointed senior tutor in 1919. Between 1921 and 1928, he served as the college [bursar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursar).[^o6] On 23 November 1928, he was elected [Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_%28college%29) of Gonville and Caius College.[^o8] From 1933 to 1935, he additionally served as [Vice-Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Chancellor) of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). In 1943, he reached the usual retirement age for a college head, 70 years, but his [tenure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenure) was extended. He finally retired in 1948, after 20 years as Master.[^o6]
