# Affiliated operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Affiliated operator"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **affiliated operators** were introduced by [Murray](wikipedia://en/1732264) and [von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) in the theory of [von Neumann algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_algebras) as a technique for using [unbounded operators](wikipedia://en/1422584) to study modules generated by a single vector. Later [Atiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Francis_Atiyah) and [Singer](wikipedia://en/324564) showed that [index theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiyah-Singer_index_theorem) for [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137) on [closed manifolds](wikipedia://en/669475) with infinite [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) could naturally be phrased in terms of unbounded operators affiliated with the von Neumann algebra of the group. Algebraic properties of affiliated operators have proved important in [L<sup>2</sup> cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L2_cohomology), an area between [analysis](wikipedia://en/1134) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) that evolved from the study of such index theorems.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *M* be a [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) acting on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) *H*. A [closed](wikipedia://en/64713671) and densely defined operator *A* is said to be **affiliated** with *M* if *A* commutes with every [unitary operator](wikipedia://en/225999) *U* in the [commutant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutant) of *M*. Equivalent conditions are that:

- each unitary *U* in *M**should leave invariant the graph of*****A*defined by $G(A)=\{(x,Ax):x\in D(A)\} \subseteq H\oplus H$.***
- the projection onto *G*(*A*) should lie in *M*<sub>2</sub>(*M*).
- each unitary *U* in *M**should carry*****D*(*A*), the [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) of*A*, onto itself and satisfy*UAU\* = A*there.***
- each unitary *U* in *M**should commute with both operators in the [polar decomposition](wikipedia://en/1369241) of*****A*.***

The last condition follows by uniqueness of the polar decomposition. If *A* has a polar decomposition

$$A=V|A|, \,$$

it says that the [partial isometry](wikipedia://en/1348079) *V* should lie in *M* and that the positive [self-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint) operator *\|A|* should be affiliated with *M*. However, by the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495), a positive self-adjoint operator commutes with a unitary operator if and only if each of its spectral projections $E([0,N])$ does. This gives another equivalent condition:

- each spectral projection of |*A*| and the partial isometry in the polar decomposition of *A* lies in *M*.

:::

## Measurable operators {#Measurable_operators}

In general the operators affiliated with a von Neumann algebra *M* need not necessarily be well-behaved under either addition or composition. However in the presence of a faithful semi-finite normal trace τ and the standard [Gelfand&ndash;Naimark&ndash;Segal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfand&ndash;Naimark&ndash;Segal) action of *M* on *H* = *L*<sup>2</sup>(*M*, τ), [Edward Nelson](wikipedia://en/387561) proved that the **measurable** affiliated operators do form a [\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/293426) with nice properties: these are operators such that τ(*I* − *E*(\[0,*N*\])) \< ∞ for *N* sufficiently large. This algebra of unbounded operators is complete for a natural topology, generalising the notion of [convergence in measure](wikipedia://en/7996261). It contains all the non-commutative *L*<sup>*p*</sup> spaces defined by the trace and was introduced to facilitate their study.

This theory can be applied when the von Neumann algebra *M* is **type I** or **type II**. When *M* = *B*(*H*) acting on the Hilbert space *L*<sup>2</sup>(*H*) of [Hilbert–Schmidt operators](wikipedia://en/976673), it gives the well-known theory of non-commutative *L*<sup>*p*</sup> spaces *L*<sup>*p*</sup> (*H*) due to [Schatten](wikipedia://en/25778498) and [von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942).

When *M* is in addition a **finite** von Neumann algebra, for example a type II<sub>1</sub> factor, then every affiliated operator is automatically measurable, so the affiliated operators form a [\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/293426), as originally observed in the first paper of [Murray](wikipedia://en/1732264) and von Neumann. In this case *M* is a [von Neumann regular ring](wikipedia://en/907524): for on the closure of its image *\|A|* has a measurable inverse *B* and then *T* = *BV*<sup>\*</sup> defines a measurable operator with *ATA* = *A*. Of course in the classical case when *X* is a probability space and *M* = *L*<sup>∞</sup> (*X*), we simply recover the \*-algebra of measurable functions on *X*.

If however *M* is **type III**, the theory takes a quite different form. Indeed in this case, thanks to the [Tomita–Takesaki theory](wikipedia://en/2898625), it is known that the non-commutative *L*<sup>*p*</sup> spaces are no longer realised by operators affiliated with the von Neumann algebra. As [Connes](wikipedia://en/340) showed, these spaces can be realised as unbounded operators only by using a certain positive power of the reference modular operator. Instead of being characterised by the simple affiliation relation *UAU*<sup>\*</sup> = *A*, there is a more complicated bimodule relation involving the analytic continuation of the modular automorphism group.
