# Hereditary ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hereditary ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the area of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) known as [module theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_theory), a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* is called **hereditary** if all [submodules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) of [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488) over *R* are again projective. If this is required only for [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) submodules, it is called **semihereditary**.

:::

For a [noncommutative ring](wikipedia://en/24062516) *R*, the terms **left hereditary** and **left semihereditary** and their right hand versions are used to distinguish the property on a single side of the ring. To be left (semi-)hereditary, all (finitely generated) submodules of projective *left* *R*-modules must be projective, and similarly to be right (semi-)hereditary all (finitely generated) submodules of projective *right* *R*-modules must be projective. It is possible for a ring to be left (semi-)hereditary but not right (semi-)hereditary and vice versa.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Equivalent definitions"}

## Equivalent definitions {#Equivalent_definitions}

- The ring *R* is left (semi-)hereditary [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) all ([finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430)) [left ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_ideal) of *R* are projective modules.[^o4][^o5]
- The ring *R* is left hereditary if and only if all left [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) have [projective resolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_resolution) of length at most 1. This is equivalent to saying that the left [global dimension](wikipedia://en/2291739) is at most 1. Hence the usual [derived functors](wikipedia://en/433875) such as $\mathrm{Ext}_R^i$ and $\mathrm{Tor}_i^R$ are trivial for $i>1$.

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- [Semisimple rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring) are left and right hereditary via the equivalent definitions: all left and right ideals are summands of *R*, and hence are projective. By a similar token, in a [von Neumann regular ring](wikipedia://en/907524) every finitely generated left and right ideal is a direct summand of *R*, and so von Neumann regular rings are left and right semihereditary.
- For any nonzero element *x* in a [domain](wikipedia://en/487627) *R*, $R\cong xR$ via the map $r\mapsto xr$. Hence in any domain, a [principal](wikipedia://en/145126) right ideal is [free](wikipedia://en/326454), hence projective. This reflects the fact that domains are right [Rickart rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickart_ring). It follows that if *R* is a right [Bézout domain](wikipedia://en/3131407), so that finitely generated right ideals are principal, then *R* has all finitely generated right ideals projective, and hence *R* is right semihereditary. Finally if *R* is assumed to be a [principal right ideal domain](wikipedia://en/6961771), then all right ideals are projective, and *R* is right hereditary.
- A commutative hereditary [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) is called a *[Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132)*. A commutative semi-hereditary integral domain is called a *[Prüfer domain](wikipedia://en/12367835)*.
- An important example of a (left) hereditary ring is the [path algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_algebra) of a [quiver](wikipedia://en/355100). This is a consequence of the existence of the standard resolution (which is of length 1) for modules over a path algebra.
- The [triangular matrix ring](wikipedia://en/30536078) $\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb Z& \mathbb Q \\0& \mathbb Q \end{bmatrix}$ is right hereditary and left semi-hereditary but not left hereditary.
- If *S* is a von Neumann regular ring with an ideal *I* that is not a direct summand, then the triangular matrix ring $\begin{bmatrix}S/I&S/I \\0&S \end{bmatrix}$ is left semi-hereditary but not right semi-hereditary.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- For a left hereditary ring *R*, every submodule of a [free](wikipedia://en/326454) left *R*-module is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the direct sum of left ideals of *R* and hence is projective.[^o5]

:::
