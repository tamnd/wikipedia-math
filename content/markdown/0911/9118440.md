# Dvoretzky's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dvoretzky's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Dvoretzky's theorem** is an important structural theorem about [normed vector spaces](wikipedia://en/21538) proved by [Aryeh Dvoretzky](wikipedia://en/19901447) in the early 1960s,[^o2] answering a question of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042). In essence, it says that every sufficiently high-dimensional normed vector space will have low-dimensional subspaces that are approximately [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9697). Equivalently, every high-dimensional bounded symmetric [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292) has low-dimensional sections that are approximately [ellipsoids](wikipedia://en/145381).

:::

A new proof found by [Vitali Milman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitali_Milman) in the 1970s[^o3] was one of the starting points for the development of [asymptotic geometric analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_geometric_analysis) (also called *asymptotic [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180)* or the *local theory of Banach spaces*).[^o4]

## Original formulations {#Original_formulations}

For every natural number *k* ∈ **N** and every *ε* > 0 there exists a natural number *N*(*k*, *ε*) ∈ **N** such that if (*X*, ‖·‖) is any normed space of dimension *N*(*k*, *ε*), there exists a subspace *E* ⊂ *X* of dimension *k* and a positive definite [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) *Q* on *E* such that the corresponding Euclidean norm

$$| \cdot | = \sqrt{Q(\cdot)}$$

on *E* satisfies:

$$|x| \leq \|x\| \leq (1+\varepsilon)|x| \quad \text{for every} \ x \in E.$$

In terms of the [multiplicative Banach-Mazur distance *d*](wikipedia://en/20021256) the theorem's conclusion can be formulated as:

$$d(E,\ \ell_k^2)\leq 1+\varepsilon$$

where $\ell_k^2$ denotes the standard *k*-dimensional Euclidean space.

Since the [unit ball](wikipedia://en/27033090) of every normed vector space is a bounded, symmetric, convex set and the unit ball of every Euclidean space is an ellipsoid, the theorem may also be formulated as a statement about ellipsoid sections of convex sets.

As a consequence, we have the following statement. For any $\epsilon > 0$, we call a $\epsilon$-sphere a convex body $K$ such that there exists a ball $B$, such that $B \subset K \subset (1 + \epsilon) B$. Then, for any integer $n$ and any $\epsilon \in (0, 1)$, for all large enough $N$, and any $N$-dimensional centrally symmetric body, there exists an $n$-dimensional subspace $V \subset \mathbb{R}^N$, such that $K \cap V$ is an $\epsilon$-sphere.[^o6]

## Further developments {#Further_developments}

In 1971, [Vitali Milman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitali_Milman) gave a new proof of Dvoretzky's theorem, making use of the [concentration of measure](wikipedia://en/788497) on the sphere to show that a random *k*-dimensional subspace satisfies the above inequality with probability very close to 1. The proof gives the sharp dependence on *k*:

$$N(k,\varepsilon)\leq\exp(C(\varepsilon)k)$$

where the constant *C*(*ε*) only depends on *ε*.

We can thus state: for every *ε* > 0 there exists a constant C(ε) > 0 such that for every normed space (*X*, ‖·‖) of dimension *N*, there exists a subspace *E* ⊂ *X* of dimension *k* ≥ *C*(*ε*) log *N* and a Euclidean norm |⋅| on *E* such that

$$|x| \leq \|x\| \leq (1+\varepsilon)|x| \quad \text{for every} \ x \in E.$$

More precisely, let *S*<sup>*N* − 1</sup> denote the unit sphere with respect to some Euclidean structure *Q* on *X*, and let *σ* be the invariant probability measure on *S*<sup>*N* − 1</sup>. Then:

- there exists such a subspace *E* with

$$k = \dim E \geq C(\varepsilon) \, \left(\frac{\int_{S^{N-1}} \| \xi \| \, d\sigma(\xi)}{\max_{\xi \in S^{N-1}} \| \xi \|}\right)^2 \, N.$$

- For any *X* one may choose *Q* so that the term in the brackets will be at most

$$c_1 \sqrt{\frac{\log N}{N}}.$$

Here *c*<sub>1</sub> is a universal constant. For given *X* and *ε*, the largest possible *k* is denoted *k*<sub>\*</sub>(*X*) and called the [Dvoretzky dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvoretzky_dimension) of *X*.

The dependence on *ε* was studied by [Yehoram Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehoram_Gordon),[^o8][^o9] who showed that *k*<sub>\*</sub>(*X*) ≥ *c*<sub>2</sub> *ε*<sup>2</sup> log *N*. Another proof of this result was given by [Gideon Schechtman](wikipedia://en/60040433).[^o10]

[Noga Alon](wikipedia://en/4939651) and [Vitali Milman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitali_Milman) showed that the logarithmic bound on the dimension of the subspace in Dvoretzky's theorem can be significantly improved, if one is willing to accept a subspace that is close either to a Euclidean space or to a [Chebyshev space](wikipedia://en/1503963). Specifically, for some constant *c*, every *n*-dimensional space has a subspace of dimension *k* ≥ exp(*c*$\sqrt{\log N}$) that is close either to *ℓ*${}^{k}_{2}$ or to *ℓ*${}^{k}_{\infty}$.[^o11]

Important related results were proved by [Tadeusz Figiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Figiel), [Joram Lindenstrauss](wikipedia://en/20653945) and Milman.[^o12]
