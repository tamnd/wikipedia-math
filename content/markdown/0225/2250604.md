# L(R)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="L(R)"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), **L(R)** (pronounced *L of R*) is the smallest [transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_class) [inner model](wikipedia://en/1118686) of [ZF](wikipedia://en/152214) containing all the [ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932) and all the [reals](wikipedia://en/20646438).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

L(R) can be constructed in a manner analogous to the construction of [Gödel's constructible universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_constructible_universe), L, by adding in all the reals at the start, and then iterating the definable powerset operation through all the ordinals.

:::

## Assumptions {#Assumptions}

In general, the study of L(R) assumes a wide array of [large cardinal](wikipedia://en/383644) axioms, since without these axioms one cannot show even that L(R) is distinct from L. But given that sufficient large cardinals exist, L(R) does not satisfy the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840), but rather the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300). However, L(R) will still satisfy the [axiom of dependent choice](wikipedia://en/441950), given only that the [von Neumann universe](wikipedia://en/445980), V, also satisfies that axiom.

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Results"}

## Results {#Results}

Under the assumption of sufficiently strong large cardinal axioms, some additional results of the theory are:

- L(R) satisfies [ZF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zermelo-Fraenkel_set_theory) + [AD<sup>\+</sup>](wikipedia://en/2818935). (In particular, it satisfies the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300).)
- Every [projective set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_set) of reals – and therefore every [analytic set](wikipedia://en/634261) and every [Borel set](wikipedia://en/43327) of reals – is an element of L(R).
- Every set of reals in L(R) is [Lebesgue measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_measurable) (in fact, [universally measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universally_measurable)) and has the [property of Baire](wikipedia://en/2213768) and the [perfect set property](wikipedia://en/2221109).
- L(R) does *not* satisfy the [axiom of uniformization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_of_uniformization) or the [axiom of real determinacy](wikipedia://en/1121647).
- R<sup>\#</sup>, the [sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_%28set_theory%29) of the set of all reals, has the smallest [Wadge degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadge_degree) of any set of reals *not* contained in L(R).
- While not every [relation](wikipedia://en/3931) on the reals in L(R) has a [uniformization](wikipedia://en/2148548) in L(R), every such relation *does* have a uniformization in L(R<sup>\#</sup>).
- Given any (set-size) [generic extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_extension) V\[G\] of V, L(R) is an [elementary submodel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_submodel) of L(R) as calculated in V\[G\]. Thus the theory of L(R) cannot be changed by [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205).

:::
