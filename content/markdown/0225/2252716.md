# Michel Chasles

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Michel Chasles"}

**Michel Floréal Chasles** (; 15 November 1793 – 18 December 1880) was a [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born at [Épernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Épernon) in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) and studied at the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) under [Siméon Denis Poisson](wikipedia://en/44670). In the [War of the Sixth Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Sixth_Coalition) he was drafted to fight in the defence of Paris in 1814. After the war, he gave up on a career as an engineer or stockbroker in order to pursue his mathematical studies.

## Career {#Career}

In 1837 he published the book *Aperçu historique sur l'origine et le développement des méthodes en géométrie* ("Historical view of the origin and development of methods in geometry"), a study of the method of reciprocal [polars](wikipedia://en/19451201) in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849). The work gained him considerable fame and respect; he was appointed Professor at the École Polytechnique in 1841 and was awarded a chair at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) in 1846. A second edition of this book was published in 1875. In 1839, Ludwig Adolph Sohncke (the father of [Leonhard Sohncke](wikipedia://en/37572822)) translated the original into German as *Geschichte der geometrie, hauptsachlich mit bezug auf die neueren methoden*.[^o6][^o7][^o8] Shortly thereafter, in 1841, [Charles Graves](wikipedia://en/17582874) published an English version as *Two Geometrical Memoirs on the General Properties of Cones of the Second Degree and on the Spherical Conics*, adding a significant amount of original material.

[Jakob Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445) had proposed [Steiner's conic problem](wikipedia://en/50076509) of enumerating the number of conic sections tangent to each of five given conics and had answered it incorrectly. Chasles developed a theory of characteristics that enabled the correct enumeration of the conics (there are 3264) (see [enumerative geometry](wikipedia://en/3069092)). He established several important theorems (all called [Chasles's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chasles's_theorem_%28disambiguation%29)). In [kinematics](wikipedia://en/65914), Chasles's description of a Euclidean motion in space [as screw displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chasles's_theorem_%28kinematics%29) was seminal to the development of the theories of [dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamics_%28physics%29) of [rigid bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_bodies). Chasles was elected a Foreign Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1864.[^o9] In 1865 he was awarded the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal).

As described in *A Treasury of Deception*, by Michael Farquhar ([Penguin Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin_Books), 2005), between 1861 and 1869 Chasles purchased some of the 27,000 forged letters from Frenchman [Denis Vrain-Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Vrain-Lucas). Included in this trove were letters from [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) to [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308), from [Cleopatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleopatra) to [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar), and from [Mary Magdalene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Magdalene) to a revived [Lazarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazarus_of_Bethany), all in a fake medieval French. In 2004, the journal *[Critical Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_Inquiry)* published a recently "discovered" 1871 letter written by Vrain-Lucas (from prison) to Chasles, conveying Vrain-Lucas's perspective on these events,[^o10] itself an invention.[^o11]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

In 1986, Alexander Jones published a commentary on Book 7 of the *Collection* of [Pappus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/509300), which Chasles had referred to in his history of geometric methods. Jones makes these comments about Chasles, Pappus and Euclid:[^o13]

Chasles's contribution to our comprehension of the *Porisms* tends to be obscured by the inherent unreasonableness of his claim to have restored substantially the contents of Euclid's book on the basis of the meagre data of Pappus and Proclus...one still turns to Chasles for the first appreciation of the interest in the *Porisms* from the point of view of modern geometry. Above all, he was the first to notice the recurrence of [cross-ratios](wikipedia://en/401767) and harmonic ratios in the lemmas, and because these concepts suffuse most of his restoration, it is probable that many of his inventions coincide with some of [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s, even if we cannot now tell which they are.

Chasles's name is one of the [72 names inscribed on the Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_72_names_on_the_Eiffel_Tower).
