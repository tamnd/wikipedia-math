# Richard Proto

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Proto"}

**Richard C. Proto** (February 9, 1940 – July 27, 2008)[^o2][^o3] was a noted American [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer) and former Director of Research and Advanced Technology at the [United States National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Security_Agency). He was elected to [NSA Hall of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA_Hall_of_Honor) in 2013.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Proto was born and Fair Haven section of [New Haven, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut) and graduated from [Wilbur Cross High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilbur_Cross_High_School) in 1958, where he was a member of 1958 team that won the New England High School basketball championship in the Boston Garden. Proto then received his bachelor's degree in mathematics from [Fairfield University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield_University) in 1962, and his master's degree in mathematics from [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College) in 1964.[^o5]

## Cryptography career {#Cryptography_career}

Proto joined the [United States National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Security_Agency) ("NSA") in 1964 as a cryptologic mathematician in the Communications Security Research & Development organization. He then dedicated the next thirty-five years to the NSA where he served in key roles in the research organization and the operational side of [information security](wikipedia://en/15036), he worked in security component design, security system architectures, vulnerability finding, and risk management.[^o7] Proto eventually served as the NSA Director of Research and Advanced Technology from 1994 to 1999.[^o7]

Proto is credited with creating the NSA Applied Mathematics Program to train mathematicians to apply their knowledge to a range of problems beyond the traditional cryptologic mission. He also created the Laboratory for Telecommunications Sciences to focus resources on mastering network technology.[^o7] Proto authored 28 technical papers at NSA and was responsible for the invention of several information security devices, two of which were patented in his name.[^o8]

## Awards {#Awards}

During his career, Proto received the [Presidential Rank Award for Distinguished Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Rank_Awards) and the [National Intelligence Distinguished Service Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Intelligence_Distinguished_Service_Medal). In 2009, Proto was recognized by the NSA with the naming of the *Richard C. Proto Symposium Center* within the NSA compound at [Fort George G. Meade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_George_G._Meade) in Maryland. It is only the second time the NSA has formally named one of its facilities.[^o5] In 2013, Proto was elected to [NSA Hall of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA_Hall_of_Honor) in 2013 for distinguished service to American cryptology.[^o7]
