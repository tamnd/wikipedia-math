# Kauffman polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kauffman polynomial"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **Kauffman polynomial** is a 2-variable [knot polynomial](wikipedia://en/313735) due to [Louis Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kauffman).[^o2] It is initially defined on a [link](wikipedia://en/1161784) diagram as

:::

$$F(K)(a,z)=a^{-w(K)}L(K)\,,$$

where $w(K)$ is the [writhe](wikipedia://en/968591) of the link diagram and $L(K)$ is a polynomial in *a* and *z* defined on link diagrams by the following properties:

- $L(O) = 1$ (O is the unknot).
- $$L(s_r)=aL(s), \qquad L(s_\ell)=a^{-1}L(s).$$
- *L* is unchanged under type II and III [Reidemeister moves](wikipedia://en/1152374).

Here $s$ is a strand and $s_r$ (resp. $s_\ell$) is the same strand with a right-handed (resp. left-handed) curl added (using a type I Reidemeister move).

Additionally *L* must satisfy Kauffman's [skein relation](wikipedia://en/314902):

*Image: Kauffman poly.png.*

The pictures represent the *L* polynomial of the diagrams which differ inside a disc as shown but are identical outside.

Kauffman showed that *L* exists and is a [regular isotopy](wikipedia://en/7404505) invariant of unoriented links. It follows easily that *F* is an [ambient isotopy](wikipedia://en/7404467) invariant of oriented links.

The [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642) is a special case of the Kauffman polynomial, as the *L* polynomial specializes to the [bracket polynomial](wikipedia://en/3774360). The Kauffman polynomial is related to [Chern–Simons gauge theories](wikipedia://en/391908) for SO(N) in the same way that the [HOMFLY polynomial](wikipedia://en/1039260) is related to Chern–Simons gauge theories for SU(N).[^o3]
