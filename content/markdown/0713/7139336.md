# Runge–Kutta method (SDE)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Runge–Kutta method"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) of stochastic systems, the **Runge–Kutta method** is a technique for the approximate [numerical solution](wikipedia://en/21506) of a [stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/1361454). It is a generalisation of the [Runge–Kutta method](wikipedia://en/213671) for [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753) to stochastic differential equations (SDEs). Importantly, the method does not involve knowing derivatives of the coefficient functions in the SDEs.

:::

## Most basic scheme {#Most_basic_scheme}

Consider the [Itō diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itō_diffusion) $X$ satisfying the following Itō stochastic differential equation

$$dX_t = a(X_t) \, dt + b(X_t) \, dW_t,$$

with [initial condition](wikipedia://en/1186804) $X_0=x_0$, where $W_t$ stands for the [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984), and suppose that we wish to solve this SDE on some interval of time $[0,T]$. Then the basic **Runge–Kutta approximation** to the true solution $X$ is the [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) $Y$ defined as follows:[^o3]

- partition the interval $[0,T]$ into $N$ subintervals of width $\delta=T/N > 0$: $$0 = \tau_{0} < \tau_{1} < \dots < \tau_{N} = T;$$
- set $Y_0 := x_0$;
- recursively compute $Y_n$ for $1\leq n\leq N$ by $$Y_{n + 1} := Y_{n} + a(Y_{n}) \delta + b(Y_{n}) \Delta W_{n} + \frac{1}{2} \left( b(\hat{\Upsilon}_{n}) - b(Y_{n}) \right) \left( (\Delta W_{n})^{2} - \delta \right) \delta^{-1/2},$$ where $\Delta W_{n} = W_{\tau_{n + 1}} - W_{\tau_{n}}$ and $\hat{\Upsilon}_{n} = Y_{n} + a(Y_n) \delta + b(Y_{n}) \delta^{1/2}.$

The [random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variables) $\Delta W_{n}$ are [independent and identically distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_and_identically_distributed) [normal random variables](wikipedia://en/21462) with [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) zero and [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) $\delta$.

This scheme has strong order 1, meaning that the [approximation error](wikipedia://en/640422) of the actual solution at a fixed time scales with the time step $\delta$. It has also weak order 1, meaning that the error on the statistics of the solution scales with the time step $\delta$. See the references for complete and exact statements.

The functions $a$ and $b$ can be time-varying without any complication. The method can be generalized to the case of several coupled equations; the principle is the same but the equations become longer.

## Variation of the Improved Euler is flexible {#Variation_of_the_Improved_Euler_is_flexible}

A newer Runge—Kutta scheme also of strong order 1 straightforwardly reduces to the [improved Euler](wikipedia://en/11754125) scheme for deterministic ODEs.[^o5] Consider the vector stochastic process $\vec X(t)\in \mathbb R^n$ that satisfies the general Ito SDE

$$d\vec X=\vec a(t,\vec X)\,dt+\vec b(t,\vec X)\,dW,$$

where drift $\vec a$ and volatility $\vec b$ are sufficiently smooth functions of their arguments. Given time step $h$, and given the value $\vec X(t_k) = \vec X_k$, estimate $\vec X(t_{k+1})$ by $\vec X_{k+1}$ for time $t_{k+1}=t_k+h$ via

$$\begin{array}{l} \vec K_1=h\vec a(t_k,\vec X_k)+(\Delta W_k-S_k\sqrt h)\vec b(t_k,\vec X_k), \\ \vec K_2=h\vec a(t_{k+1},\vec X_k+\vec K_1)+(\Delta W_k+S_k\sqrt h)\vec b(t_{k+1},\vec X_k+\vec K_1), \\ \vec X_{k+1}=\vec X_k+\frac12(\vec K_1+\vec K_2), \end{array}$$

- where $\Delta W_k=\sqrt hZ_k$ for normal random $Z_k\sim N(0,1)$;
- and where $S_k=\pm1$, each alternative chosen with probability $1/2$.

The above describes only one time step. Repeat this time step $(t_m-t_0)/h$ times in order to integrate the SDE from time $t=t_0$ to $t=t_m$.

The scheme integrates Stratonovich SDEs to $O(h)$ provided one sets $S_k=0$ throughout (instead of choosing $\pm 1$).

## Higher order Runge-Kutta schemes {#Higher_order_Runge-Kutta_schemes}

Higher-order schemes also exist, but become increasingly complex. Rößler developed many schemes for Ito SDEs,[^o7][^o8] whereas Komori developed schemes for Stratonovich SDEs.[^o9][^o10][^o11] Rackauckas extended these schemes to allow for adaptive-time stepping via [Rejection Sampling](wikipedia://en/502022) with Memory (RSwM), resulting in orders of magnitude efficiency increases in practical biological models,[^o12] along with coefficient optimization for improved stability.[^o13]
