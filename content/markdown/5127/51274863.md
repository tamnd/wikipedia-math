# Carlo Catassi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carlo Catassi"}

**Carlo Catassi** (born 19 April 1953) is an Italian [pediatric gastroenterologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pediatric_gastroenterology) and [epidemiologist](wikipedia://en/66997). He is a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at the [Università Politecnica delle Marche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Università_Politecnica_delle_Marche) in [Ancona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancona), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy), where he leads the Department of Pediatrics and directs the Pediatric Residency Program.[^o3] He is also a visiting scientist at [Massachusetts General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_General_Hospital) in [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston), [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States).[^o4]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Catassi has held a full professorship at the [Università Politecnica delle Marche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Università_Politecnica_delle_Marche) since 1993. From 2013 to 2016, he served as president of the Italian Society of Pediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition (SIGENP).[^o6]

In the 1990s, he coordinated a multicenter screening in Italy that helped shape the "celiac iceberg" model, illustrating how many individuals with [celiac disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celiac_disease) remain undiagnosed.[^o7] He later led one of the first population-based screening studies in the United States, which estimated the disease affects roughly 1% of the population.[^o8]

His research extended globally, including studies in [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil)[^o9] and [Middle Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern) populations, where he contributed to early data on disease prevalence.[^o10]

## Research {#Research}

Catassi’s research focuses on the epidemiology and clinical management of gluten-related disorders. He conducted one of the first clinical trials to determine how much [gluten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluten) can safely be tolerated by individuals with celiac disease. These results helped set the international regulatory limit of 20 ppm for gluten-free products.[^o12]

He has also examined high-prevalence populations, including the [Saharawi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharawis), among whom celiac disease rates were observed to be around 6%.[^o13]

In collaboration with international experts, Catassi co-developed the Salerno criteria for diagnosing [non-celiac gluten sensitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-celiac_gluten_sensitivity) (NCGS), aiming to improve diagnostic consistency.[^o14]

His studies also examined environmental triggers of celiac disease in children, showing that neither the timing of gluten introduction nor breastfeeding significantly impacts disease risk.[^o15]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Catassi is married to Dr. Eleonora Bove, a lawyer. They have two children.
