# Janusz Brzozowski (computer scientist)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Janusz Brzozowski"}

**Janusz (John) Antoni Brzozowski** (May 10, 1935 – October 24, 2019) was a Polish-Canadian [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) and Distinguished Professor Emeritus[^o3] at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo)'s [David R. Cheriton School of Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_R._Cheriton_School_of_Computer_Science).[^o4]

:::

In 1962, Brzozowski earned his PhD in the field of [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) under [Edward J. McCluskey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_J._McCluskey). The topic of the thesis was *Regular Expression Techniques for Sequential Circuits*. From 1967 to 1996 he was Professor at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo). He is known for his contributions to [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), [circuit theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_theory), and [automata theory](wikipedia://en/103356).

## Achievements in research {#Achievements_in_research}

Brzozowski worked on [regular expressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_expressions) and on [syntactic semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntactic_semigroup) of [formal languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_languages).[^o6] The result was *Characterizations of locally testable events* written together with [Imre Simon](wikipedia://en/23579726), which had a similar impact[^o7] on the development of the algebraic theory of formal languages as [Marcel-Paul Schützenberger](wikipedia://en/2817337)'s characterization of the [star-free languages](wikipedia://en/4922792).

In the area, today at least four concepts bear Brzozowski's name in honour of his contributions: The first is the *Brzozowski's conjecture*[^o8] about the regularity of noncounting classes. Second, *[Brzozowski's algorithm](wikipedia://en/17447039#Brzozowski's_algorithm)*,[^o9] a conceptually simple algorithm for performing [DFA minimization](wikipedia://en/17447039). Third, the *[Brzozowski derivative](wikipedia://en/42785451)* of a formal language or of a generalised [regular expression](wikipedia://en/25717). Fourth, [Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501)'s reference work on automata theory has a chapter devoted to the so-called *Brzozowski hierarchy*[^o10] inside the [star-free languages](wikipedia://en/4922792), also known as *dot-depth hierarchy*. Notably, Brzozowski was not only co-author of the paper that defined the [dot-depth hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot-depth_hierarchy) and raised the question whether this hierarchy is strict,[^o11] he later also was co-author of the paper resolving that problem after roughly ten years.[^o12] The Brzozowski hierarchy gained further importance after Wolfgang Thomas discovered a relation between the algebraic concept of dot-depth and the alternation depth of quantifiers in [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983) via [Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games](wikipedia://en/1040475).[^o13]

He received the following academic awards and honours:

- NSERC Scientific Exchange Award to France (1974–1975)
- Japan Society for the Promotion of Science Research Fellowship (1984)
- [Computing Research Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing_Research_Association) Certificate of Appreciation for outstanding contributions and service as a member of the CRA Board of Directors (1992)
- Distinguished Professor Emeritus, [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo), Canada (1996)[^o14]
- Medal of Merit, Catholic University of Lublin, Poland (2001)
- IBM Canada Canadian Pioneer in Computing (2005)[^o15]
- The Role of Theory in Computer Science, a one-day conference in honour of John Brzozowski's 80th birthday (2015)[^o16]
- The Role of Theory in Computer Science: Essays Dedicated to Janusz Brzozowski, World Scientific (2017)[^o17]
- Lifetime Achievement Award, Computer Science Canada/Informatique Canada (CS-CAN/INFO-CAN) (2016)[^o18]
- CIAA 2017 Sheng Yu Award for Best Paper for *Complexity of Proper Prefix-Convex Regular Languages* by J. Brzozowski and C. Sinnamon[^o19]
- CIAA 2018 Sheng Yu Award for Best Paper for *State Complexity of Overlap Assembly* by J. Brzozowski, [L. Kar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lila_Kari)i, B. Li, M. Szykula[^o20]
