# Michael Schemper

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Schemper"}

**Michael Schemper** is a biostatistician and an academic, serving as professor emeritus at the [Medical University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_University_of_Vienna).

:::

Schemper's research focuses on nonparametric estimation and testing methods, [survival analysis](wikipedia://en/419259), and particularly [Cox regression models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox_proportional_hazards_model), [logistic regression](wikipedia://en/226631) models, and explained variation in statistical models. He has been awarded Lifetime Honorary Memberships by both the International Society for Clinical Biostatistics (ISCB) and the Austro-Swiss Region (ROeS) of the [International Biometric Society (IBS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Biometric_Society). According to [Google Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Scholar), his work has been cited more than 21,000 times.

## Education {#Education}

Schemper studied statistics at the [Vienna University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) from 1972 to 1977, earning an [M.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) degree in 1976 and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1977. He later completed his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in [Medical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Statistics) and Documentation at Vienna University in 1985.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Schemper's academic career started as a biostatistician in 1977 and as an associate professor from 1985 at the (former) Medical Faculty of Vienna University. He was a visiting associate professor at the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas) in [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston), where he remained from 1987 to 1988. In 1991, he became a professor of clinical biostatistics at the Medical University of Vienna,[^o4] and later became an emeritus professor there.[^o6] In 1991, he founded the Institute of Clinical Biometrics at the Medical University of Vienna and remained its head until 2015.[^o7]

## Research {#Research}

Schemper has authored more than 300 publications, which have collectively received over 21,000 citations. His work spans both the application of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) in medical research and the development of biostatistical methods.[^o9] His research has focused on quantifying the variation in outcomes explained by prognostic factors,[^o10] contributing conceptually to understanding the degrees of necessity and sufficiency of such factors in outcome modeling.[^o11] His methodological work has addressed the analysis of survival data under non-proportional hazards[^o12] and the development of solutions to the monotone likelihood problem in risk (Cox) regression.[^o13] He has also examined residuals in survival analysis[^o14] and the quantification of follow-up in studies of failure time.[^o15] In addition, his work includes the development of statistical methods for assessing the correlation between bivariate failure times under censoring,[^o16] as well as contributions to the treatment of missing data in [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997)[^o17] and to nonparametric estimation and testing in survival analysis.[^o18]
