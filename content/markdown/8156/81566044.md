# John Stuart Hunter

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Stuart Hunter"}

**John Stuart Hunter** (born June 3, 1923) is an American statistician and engineer whose career has spanned academia and industry. He is considered a pioneer of the application of statistical methods, especially [experimental design](wikipedia://en/9541) and [quality control](wikipedia://en/41613), to [industrial engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_engineering). He is an elected fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), the [American Society for Quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Quality), and the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Hunter earned his BSc (1947) in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering), his MS (1949) in [engineering mathematics](wikipedia://en/5381408), and his PhD (1954) in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) from [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University). His PhD thesis, *Multi-factor experimental designs*, was written under the tutelage of Robert John Hader.

## Career {#Career}

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Hunter was [called up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription) and joined the [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army). He was initially assigned to the [Army Specialized Training Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Specialized_Training_Program) at NC State and later served in [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines). After earning his PhD he worked at [American Cyanamid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Cyanamid). He joined [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1961, where he is now professor [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus), School of Engineering and Applied Science.

Hunter was the founding editor of the statistical journal [Technometrics](wikipedia://en/5854084) from 1959 to 1963.[^n1] He was [president of the American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/14833025) in 1992.

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

*Image: John\_Stuart\_Hunter\_Wilks\_Award\_Letter\_1987.jpg.* In 1987, Hunter was awarded the US Army's Wilks Memorial Medal and Award

Hunter has been widely recognized for his contributions to statistical methodology and industrial engineering.

- 1959 and 1985, recipient of the ASQ's Brumbaugh Award
- 1961, elected [fellow of the American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/51140705)
- 1965, elected fellow of the American Association for the Advancement of Science
- 1970, recipient of the [Shewhart Medal](wikipedia://en/831964)
- 1985, recipient of the ASQ's Deming Medal
- 1987, recipient of the United States Army Samuel S. Wilks Memorial Medal and Award
- 1995, recipient of the [American Statistical Association Founders Award](wikipedia://en/81174224)
- 1998, elected honorary member of the [American Society for Quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Quality) (ASQ)
- 2004, named Distinguished Alumnus for the College of Physical and Mathematical Sciences, North Carolina State University
- 2005, elected member of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering)
- 2006, awarded an [honorary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) DSc from North Carolina State University
- 2008, awarded an honorary DSc from [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University)
- 2015, recipient of the [William G. Hunter Award](wikipedia://en/16658629) presented by the Statistics Division of the American Society for Quality
