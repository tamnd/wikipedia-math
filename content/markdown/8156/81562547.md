# Alexandru D. Ionescu

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexandru D. Ionescu"}

**Alexandru Dan Ionescu** is a mathematician and academic who serves as a professor of mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).

:::

Ionescu's research primarily focuses on [partial differential equations (PDEs)](wikipedia://en/52564) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). He is a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), a member of the American Academy of Sciences and Letters, and was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 2022.

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Ionescu earned his [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in mathematics from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1995. He then obtained both his [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._A.) in mathematics in 1997 and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics in 1999 from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o4]

Ionescu held postdoctoral appointments at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) and the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). In 2002, he joined the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) as an assistant professor, was promoted to associate professor in 2005, and became a full professor in 2008. Since 2010, he has served as a professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o4]

## Works {#Works}

Ionescu's research spans analysis and partial differential equations, with applications to mathematical physics. His work has addressed questions of global [well-posedness](wikipedia://en/176673) for nonlinear dispersive equations, including the [KP-I equation](wikipedia://en/4211219),[^o6] [Schrödinger maps](wikipedia://en/59874),[^o7] the [Benjamin–Ono equation](wikipedia://en/16697638),[^o8] and [water waves](wikipedia://en/1744650)[^o9]

Ionescu has also made contributions to [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), particularly in [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), where he proved extensions of the Furstenberg–Bergelson–Leibman conjecture to the [nilpotent setting](wikipedia://en/252235).[^o10] His research further encompasses kinetic and [plasma models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_modeling), including stability[^o11] and scattering results for the [Vlasov–Poisson system](wikipedia://en/1991528).[^o12]

In addition, his work in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) includes proving [rigidity](wikipedia://en/1458875) results for [Kerr black-hole solutions](wikipedia://en/456715) to the [Einstein vacuum equations](wikipedia://en/424540) under specific geometric and analytic assumption.[^o13]
