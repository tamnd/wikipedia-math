# List of probabilistic proofs of non-probabilistic theorems

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of probabilistic proofs of non-probabilistic theorems"}

[Probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) routinely uses results from other fields of mathematics (mostly, analysis). The opposite cases, collected below, are relatively rare; however, probability theory is used systematically in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) via the [probabilistic method](wikipedia://en/173525). They are particularly used for [non-constructive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-constructive) proofs.

:::

## Analysis {#Analysis}

::: {#o3 .entry cue=list-entry name="Normal number"}

- [Normal numbers](wikipedia://en/204277) exist. Moreover, [computable](wikipedia://en/6206) normal numbers [exist](wikipedia://en/204277#Properties_and_examples). These non-probabilistic existence theorems follow from probabilistic results: (a) a number chosen at random (uniformly on (0,1)) is normal almost surely (which follows easily from the [strong law of large numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_law_of_large_numbers)); (b) some probabilistic inequalities behind the strong law. The existence of a normal number follows from (a) immediately. The proof of the existence of computable normal numbers, based on (b), involves additional arguments. All known proofs use probabilistic arguments.

:::

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dvoretzky's theorem"}

- [Dvoretzky's theorem](wikipedia://en/9118440) which states that high-dimensional convex bodies have ball-like slices is proved probabilistically. No deterministic construction is known, even for many specific bodies.

:::

::: {#o5 .entry cue=list-entry name="Banach–Mazur compactum"}

- The diameter of the [Banach–Mazur compactum](wikipedia://en/20021256) was calculated using a probabilistic construction. No deterministic construction is known.

:::

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hausdorff–Young inequality"}

- The original proof that the [Hausdorff–Young inequality](wikipedia://en/22386332) cannot be extended to $p > 2$ is probabilistic. The proof of the de Leeuw–Kahane–Katznelson theorem (which is a stronger claim) is partially probabilistic.[^o7]

:::

- The first construction of a Salem set was probabilistic.[^o8] Only in 1981 did Kaufman give a deterministic construction.[^o9]

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="Weierstrass approximation theorem"}

- Every continuous function on a compact interval can be uniformly approximated by polynomials, which is the [Weierstrass approximation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_approximation_theorem). A [probabilistic proof](wikipedia://en/340136#Proof) uses the [weak law of large numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_law_of_large_numbers). Non-probabilistic proofs were available earlier.

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="Wiener process"}

- Existence of a nowhere differentiable continuous function follows easily from properties of [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984). A [non-probabilistic proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_function) was available earlier.

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=list-entry name="Stirling's formula"}

- [Stirling's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirling's_formula) was first discovered by [Abraham de Moivre](wikipedia://en/57327) in his \`[The Doctrine of Chances](wikipedia://en/161715)' (with a constant identified later by Stirling) in order to be used in probability theory. Several probabilistic proofs of Stirling's formula (and related results) were found in the 20th century.[^o13][^o14]

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=list-entry name="Liouville's theorem"}

- The only bounded harmonic functions defined on the whole plane are constant functions by [Liouville's theorem](wikipedia://en/55762#Liouville's_theorem). A probabilistic proof via n-dimensional [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) is well known.[^o16] Non-probabilistic proofs were available earlier.

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=list-entry name="analytic"}

- Non-tangential boundary values[^o18] of an [analytic](wikipedia://en/14110) or [harmonic](wikipedia://en/55762) function exist at almost all boundary points of non-tangential boundedness. This result ([Privalov](wikipedia://en/6111554)'s theorem), and several results of this kind, are deduced from [martingale convergence](wikipedia://en/12261058).[^o19] Non-probabilistic proofs were available earlier.

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=list-entry name="Boundary Harnack principle"}

- The [boundary Harnack principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Harnack_principle) is proved using Brownian motion[^o21] (see also[^o22]). Non-probabilistic proofs were available earlier.

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler's Basel sum"}

- [Euler's Basel sum](wikipedia://en/490303), $\qquad \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6},$ can be demonstrated by considering the expected exit time of planar Brownian motion from an infinite strip. A number of other less well-known identities can be deduced in a similar manner.[^o24]

:::

::: {#o25 .entry cue=list-entry name="Picard theorem"}

- The [Picard theorem](wikipedia://en/674050) can be proved using the winding properties of planar Brownian motion.[^o26][^o27]

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="Lipschitz function"}

- The fact that every [Lipschitz function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_function) on the real line is differentiable almost everywhere can be proved using [martingale convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martingale_convergence).

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=list-entry name="Weak law of large numbers"}

- Multidimensional Fourier inversion formula can be established by the [weak law of large numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_law_of_large_numbers) and some elementary results from complex analysis.[^o30]

:::

::: {#o31 .entry cue=list-entry name="Abért"}

- [Abért](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miklós_Abért) and [Weiss](wikipedia://en/40423267) proved, via a probabilistic construction, that [Bernoulli shifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_shift) are weakly contained (in the sense of [Kechris](wikipedia://en/2291355)) in any free [measure-preserving action](wikipedia://en/398931) action of a discrete, countable group on a standard probability space.[^o32]

:::

## Combinatorics {#Combinatorics}

::: {#o34 .entry cue=list-entry name="Probabilistic method"}

- A number of theorems stating existence of graphs (and other discrete structures) with desired properties are proved by the [probabilistic method](wikipedia://en/173525). Non-probabilistic proofs are available for a few of them.

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=list-entry name="Maximum-minimums identity"}

- The [maximum-minimums identity](wikipedia://en/19167177) admits a probabilistic proof.

:::

::: {#o36 .entry cue=list-entry name="Crossing number inequality"}

- [Crossing number inequality](wikipedia://en/50834770) which is a lower bound on the number of crossing for any drawing of a graph as a function of the number of vertices, edges the graph has.

:::

## Algebra {#Algebra}

::: {#o38 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fundamental theorem of algebra"}

- The [fundamental theorem of algebra](wikipedia://en/51414) can be proved using two-dimensional Brownian motion.[^o16] Non-probabilistic proofs were available earlier.

:::

::: {#o39 .entry cue=list-entry name="index theorem for elliptic complexes"}

- The [index theorem for elliptic complexes](wikipedia://en/324752#Generalizations) is proved using probabilistic methods[^o40] (rather than heat equation methods). A non-probabilistic proof was available earlier.

:::

## Topology and geometry {#Topology_and_geometry}

::: {#o42 .entry cue=list-entry name="boundary"}

- A smooth [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) is evidently two-sided, but a non-smooth (especially, fractal) boundary can be quite complicated. It was conjectured to be two-sided in the sense that the natural projection of the [Martin boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_boundary)[^o43] to the topological boundary is at most 2 to 1 almost everywhere.[^o44] This conjecture is proved using [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/149984), [local time](wikipedia://en/5678101), [stochastic integration](wikipedia://en/228001), [coupling](wikipedia://en/887545), hypercontractivity etc.[^o45] (see also[^o46]). A non-probabilistic proof is found 18 years later.[^o47]

:::

::: {#o48 .entry cue=list-entry name="Loewner's torus inequality"}

- The [Loewner's torus inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduction_to_systolic_geometry#Loewner's_torus_inequality) relates the area of a [compact surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_surface) (topologically, a torus) to its [systole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduction_to_systolic_geometry#Notion_of_systole). It can be [proved most easily](wikipedia://en/12003118#Proof_of_Loewner's_torus_inequality) by using the probabilistic notion of [variance](wikipedia://en/32344).[^o49] A non-probabilistic proof was available earlier.

:::

::: {#o50 .entry cue=list-entry name="Minimal surface"}

- The weak halfspace theorem for [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734) states that any complete minimal surface of bounded curvature which is not a plane is not contained in any halfspace. This theorem is proved using a [coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling) between Brownian motions on minimal surfaces.[^o51] A non-probabilistic proof was available earlier.

:::

## Number theory {#Number_theory}

::: {#o53 .entry cue=list-entry name="Normal number"}

- The [normal number](wikipedia://en/204277) theorem (1909), due to [Émile Borel](wikipedia://en/356161), could be one of the first examples of the [probabilistic method](wikipedia://en/173525), providing the first proof of existence of normal numbers, with the help of the first version of the strong [law of large numbers](wikipedia://en/157055#Strong_law) (see also the first item of the section Analysis).

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=list-entry name="Rogers–Ramanujan identities"}

- The [Rogers–Ramanujan identities](wikipedia://en/2324711) are proved using [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876).[^o55] A non-probabilistic proof was available earlier.

:::

## Quantum theory {#Quantum_theory}

::: {#o57 .entry cue=list-entry name="Von Neumann algebra"}

- Non-commutative dynamics (called also quantum dynamics) is formulated in terms of [Von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185) and continuous [tensor products of Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/2728973).[^o58] Several results (for example, a continuum of mutually non-isomorphic models) are obtained by probabilistic means ([random compact sets](wikipedia://en/5308916) and [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/149984)).[^o59][^o60] One part of this theory (so-called type III systems) is translated into the analytic language[^o61] and is developing analytically;[^o62] the other part (so-called type II systems) exists still in the probabilistic language only.

:::

::: {#o63 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bell inequalities"}

- Tripartite quantum states can lead to arbitrary large violations of [Bell inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_inequalities)[^o64] (in sharp contrast to the bipartite case). The proof uses random unitary matrices. No other proof is available.

:::

## Information theory {#Information_theory}

::: {#o66 .entry cue=list-entry name="Shannon"}

- The proof of [Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693)'s [channel coding theorem](wikipedia://en/3474289) uses random coding to show the existence of a code that achieves [channel capacity](wikipedia://en/231204).

:::
