# SC (complexity)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="SC"}

In [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), **SC** (Steve's Class, named after [Stephen Cook](wikipedia://en/39432))[^o2] is the [complexity class](wikipedia://en/502426) of problems solvable by a [deterministic Turing machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic_Turing_machine) in polynomial time (class **[P](wikipedia://en/658550)**) and polylogarithmic space (class **[PolyL](wikipedia://en/26953472)**) (that is, [O](wikipedia://en/44578)((log *n*)<sup>k</sup>) space for some constant *k*). It may also be called **DTISP(poly, polylog)**, where **DTISP** stands for *deterministic time and space*. The definition of **SC** differs from **P** ∩ **PolyL**, since for the former, it is required that a single algorithm runs in both polynomial time and polylogarithmic space; while for the latter, two separate algorithms will suffice: one that runs in polynomial time, and another that runs in polylogarithmic space. It is unknown whether **SC** and **P** ∩ **PolyL** are equivalent.

:::

**[DCFL](wikipedia://en/7962589)**, the strict subset of [context-free languages](wikipedia://en/6867) recognized by [deterministic pushdown automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic_pushdown_automaton), is contained in **SC**, as shown by Cook in 1979.[^o3] It is open if all context-free languages can be recognized in **SC**, although they are known be in **P** ∩ **PolyL**.[^o4]

It is open whether directed [st-connectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St-connectivity) is in **SC**, although it is known to be in **P** ∩ **PolyL**: [depth first search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_first_search) solves it in polynomial time and [Savitch's theorem](wikipedia://en/658501) provides a solution on space $O(\log^2 n)$. This question is equivalent to **[NL](wikipedia://en/1145955)** ⊆ **SC**.[^o5]

**[RL](wikipedia://en/1176581)** and **[BPL](wikipedia://en/18449708)** are classes of problems acceptable by [probabilistic Turing machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_Turing_machines) in logarithmic space and polynomial time. [Noam Nisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Nisan) showed in 1992 the weak [derandomization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derandomization) result that both are contained in **SC**.[^o6] In other words, given *polylogarithmic* space, a deterministic machine can simulate *logarithmic* space probabilistic algorithms.
