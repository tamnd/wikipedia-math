# Symmetric inverse semigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Symmetric inverse semigroup"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of all [partial bijections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_bijection) on a set *X* ( one-to-one partial transformations) forms an [inverse semigroup](wikipedia://en/1757814), called the **symmetric inverse semigroup**[^o2] (actually a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652)) on *X*. The conventional notation for the symmetric inverse semigroup on a set *X* is $\mathcal{I}_X$[^o3] or $\mathcal{IS}_X$.[^o4] In general $\mathcal{I}_X$ is not [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_semigroup).

:::

Details about the origin of the symmetric inverse semigroup are available in the discussion on the [origins of the inverse semigroup](wikipedia://en/1757814#Origins).

## Finite symmetric inverse semigroups {#Finite_symmetric_inverse_semigroups}

When *X* is a finite set {1, ..., *n*}, the inverse semigroup of one-to-one partial transformations is denoted by *C*<sub>*n*</sub> and its elements are called **charts** or **partial symmetries**.[^o6] The notion of chart generalizes the notion of [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027). A (famous) example of (sets of) charts are the hypomorphic mapping sets from the [reconstruction conjecture](wikipedia://en/255594) in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401).[^o7]

The [cycle notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_notation) of classical, group-based permutations generalizes to symmetric inverse semigroups by the addition of a notion called a *path*, which (unlike a cycle) ends when it reaches the ["undefined" element](wikipedia://en/23577#In_category_theory); the notation thus extended is called *path notation*.[^o7]
