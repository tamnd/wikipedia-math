# Apeirophobia

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Apeirophobia"}

**Apeirophobia** ( ; from ᾰ̓́πειρος) is the [specific phobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_phobia) of [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824), [eternity](wikipedia://en/217472), endlessness, or the uncountable and is also known as the **fear of infinity**, the **fear of eternity**, or the **fear of endlessness**, causing discomfort and sometimes [panic attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_attacks) from intrusive thoughts of the [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824).[^o3] It normally starts in [adolescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolescence) or earlier and it is currently not known how it normally develops over time. Apeirophobia may be caused by existential dread about eternal life or oblivion following death. Due to this, it is often connected with [thanatophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanatophobia) (the phobia of death), [chronophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronophobia) (the phobia of time or the passage of time), nihilophobia, and other existential fears.[^o4][^o5] Sufferers commonly report feelings of [derealization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derealization) which may cause the perception of a dreamlike or distorted reality. [Existential OCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_OCD) may sometimes be the cause of obsessive thoughts about [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824) or [eternity](wikipedia://en/217472), which can lead to or trigger apeirophobia. Like other phobias, apeirophobia may be tied to mental health conditions such as [anxiety disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety_disorders) or [obsessive-compulsive disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsessive-compulsive_disorder).[^o5] Martin Wiener, a neuroscience professor at [George Mason University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_University), hypothesized that apeirophobia is a manifestation of the fear of an unknown future, similarly to [the fear of aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerascophobia).[^o6]

:::

There is very little research on this phobia. Despite not being recognized specifically by the [American Psychiatric Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychiatric_Association) in the [DSM-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSM-5), it does meet their criteria for a [specific phobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_phobia), which is a type of anxiety disorder.[^o4] There are no known treatment methods that are specifically designed to treat apeirophobia,[^o7] although some apeirophobes have benefited from [anti-anxiety medications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiolytic) or [cognitive behavioral therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_behavioral_therapy).[^o6]
