# William Frederick Eberlein

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Frederick Eberlein"}

**William Frederick Eberlein** (June 25, 1917, [Shawano, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawano,_Wisconsin) – 1986, [Rochester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York)) was an American mathematician, specializing in mathematical analysis and mathematical physics.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Eberlein studied from 1936 to 1942 at the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin) and at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he received in 1942 a PhD for the thesis *Closure, Convexity, and Linearity in Banach Spaces* under the direction of [Marshall Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Harvey_Stone).

He was married twice—to Mary Bernarda Barry and Patricia Ramsay James. He had four children with Mary Barry, including Patrick Barry Eberlein, another renowned mathematician. Patricia Ramsay James was a mathematician who moved into computer science as the field opened up; their one child is Kristen James Eberlein, the chair of the OASIS [Darwin Information Typing Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_Information_Typing_Architecture) Technical Committee.

## Work {#Work}

Eberlein had academic positions at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (1947–1948), at the University of Wisconsin (1948–1955), at [Wayne State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_State_University) (1955–1956), and from 1957 at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester), where he remained for the rest of his career.[^o5] His doctoral students include [William F. Donoghue, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Donoghue,_Jr.)[^o6] and [A. Wayne Wymore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._Wayne_Wymore).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

He worked on [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), mean value theorems, and [numerical integration](wikipedia://en/170089). Eberlein also worked on spacetime models, internal symmetries in [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155), and [spinors](wikipedia://en/29276).[^o5] His name is attached to the [Eberlein–Šmulian theorem](wikipedia://en/17446349) in functional analysis[^o8], the [Eberlein compacta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eberlein_compacta) in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954)[^o9], and the Eberlein mean ergodic theorem[^o10].
