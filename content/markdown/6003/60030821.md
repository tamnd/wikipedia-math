# Serguei Barannikov

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Serguei Barannikov"}

**Serguei Barannikov** (Сергей Александрович Баранников; born April 16, 1972) is a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his works in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Barannikov graduated with honors from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) in 1994.

In 1995–1999, Barannikov received his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree (Ph.D.) in Mathematics from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). Simultaneously, he was an invited researcher at [Institut des Hautes Etudes Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Etudes_Scientifiques) in France.

During 1999–2010, he worked as a researcher at [Ecole Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecole_Normale_Supérieure) in Paris. Since 2010, he works as a researcher at [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University).

## Scientific work {#Scientific_work}

At the age of 20, Barannikov wrote a paper[^o6] on algebraic topology, in which he introduced the "canonical forms" invariants of filtered complexes, later also called "Barannikov modules".[^o7][^o8] Ten years later, these invariants became widely used in applied mathematics in the field of [topological data analysis](wikipedia://en/17740009#Early_history) under the name of ["persistence bar-codes"](wikipedia://en/31069689#Definition) and ["persistence diagrams"](wikipedia://en/31069689#Definition).[^o8][^o9]

Barannikov is known for his work on [mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/644671), [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560), and [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590). In mirror symmetry, he is a co-author of construction of Frobenius manifold, mirror symmetric to genus zero Gromov–Witten invariants.[^o12]

He is one of authors of hypothesis of homological mirror symmetry for Fano manifolds.[^o10] In the theory of exponential integrals, Barannikov is a co-author of the theorem on the degeneration of analogue of Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence.[^o13]

In the theory of noncommutative varieties, Barannikov is the author of the theory of noncommutative Hodge structures.[^o11]

Barannikov is known for: Barannikov–Morse complexes,[^o7] Barannikov modules,[^o8] Barannikov–Kontsevich construction,[^o12] and Barannikov–Kontsevich theorem.[^o13]
