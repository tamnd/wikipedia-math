# Muhammad Rafique (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Muhammad Rafique"}

**Muhammad Rafique** (2 January 1940 — 16 June 1996) was a Pakistani mathematician and professor of mathematics at the Punjab University. He was a versatile scholar who authored [textbooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_textbook) on [computer language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_language) and [special relativity](wikipedia://en/26962). He was the co-author of textbook *Group Theory for High Energy Physicists*, which was eventually published years after his death in 2016.

:::

## Biographical overview {#Biographical_overview}

Rafique was born in [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore), [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_British_India) in [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Empire) on 2 January 1940 in Lahore, Punjab in India and was educated at the [Punjab University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Punjab) where he graduated with [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) with [first-class honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_honours) in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in 1960.[^o4] He served in the Faculty of Mathematics at the Punjab University where he graduated with [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in Mathematics in 1964, and earned a scholarship to study mathematics in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom).[^o5] He attended the [University of North Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Wales) where he graduated with a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in Mathematics in 1967.[^o5]

Upon returning to Pakistan, he joined the Punjab University and taught there until 1971 when he joined the [International Center for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Center_for_Theoretical_Physics) in Italy as a post-doctoral scholar.[^o6] From 1972 to 1977, Rafique worked at the [Institute of Nuclear Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Institute_of_Nuclear_Science_and_Technology) where he contributed his work on [fast neutron calculations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_neutron_calculations) for [atomic weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapon) which built his interests in the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001) and [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics).[^o7]

From 1977 to 1982, he served on the faculty of [University of Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tripoli) in Libya, and served as the Head of Department of Mathematics at the Punjab University from 1983 until 1992 when he went to teach mathematics at the [King Fahd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Fahd_University_of_Petroleum_and_Minerals) in [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia).[^o4] His tenure at the King Fahd University was short-lived. He died due to cardiac arrest in June 1996.[^o4] Although a mathematician, Rafique was a prolific author on physics, was writing a college text on [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890)'s applications on [high energy physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_energy_physics) with [Mohammad Saleem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Saleem_%28physicist%29) at the time of his death in 1992. The college book was eventually published in 2015–2016 by British publisher [Taylor & Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_&_Francis).
