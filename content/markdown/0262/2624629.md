# Minimum degree spanning tree

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minimum degree spanning tree"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **minimum degree spanning tree** is a subset of the edges of a [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) graph that connects all the [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) together, without any [cycles](wikipedia://en/168609), and its maximum degree of its vertices as small as possible. That is, it is a [spanning tree](wikipedia://en/455770) whose maximum degree is minimal.

:::

The decision problem is: Given a graph *G* and an integer *k*, does *G* have a spanning tree such that no vertex has degree greater than *k*? This is also known as the [degree-constrained spanning tree](wikipedia://en/3037035) problem.

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Algorithms"}

## Algorithms {#Algorithms}

Finding the minimum degree spanning tree of an undirected graph is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard). This can be shown by constructing a reduction from the [Hamiltonian path problem](wikipedia://en/149646). For directed graphs, finding the minimum degree spanning tree is also NP-hard. [^o4]

R. Krishman and B. Raghavachari (2001) have a [quasi-polynomial time](wikipedia://en/25269049) approximation algorithm to solve the problem for directed graphs.[^o4]

M. Haque, Md. R. Uddin, and Md. A. Kashem (2007) found a [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time) algorithm that can find the minimum degree spanning tree of [series-parallel graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series-parallel_graph) with small degrees.[^o5]

G. Yao, D. Zhu, H. Li, and S. Ma (2008) found a [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) algorithm that can find the minimum degree spanning tree of [directed acyclic graphs](wikipedia://en/204002).[^o6]
