# Charles Bossut

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Bossut"}

**Charles Bossut** (11 August 1730 – 14 January 1814) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and *confrère* of the [Encyclopaedists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopaedists).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Bossut was born in 1730 in [Tartaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartaras), [Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loire_%28department%29) to Barthélemy Bossut and Jeanne Thonnerine.[^o4] He lost his father early in life and was raised by his uncle.[^o4] He received his education at the Jesuit College of Lyon from age fourteen, under the influence of Père Béraud, who also mentored [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) such as [Jean-Étienne Montucla](wikipedia://en/2157781) and [Jérôme Lalande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jérôme_Lalande).[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

After completing his studies, Bossut followed a path in the Church, becoming known as Abbé Charles Bossut.[^o4] He delved into mathematical research, collaborating with contemporaries such as [Jean le Rond d'Alembert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_le_Rond_d'Alembert), [Alexis Clairaut](wikipedia://en/196273), and [Charles Étienne Louis Camus](wikipedia://en/2977925).[^o4] In 1753, he became a correspondant at the French Academy of Sciences.[^o4]

By the age of 21, he was appointed professor of mathematics at the [École du Génie in Mézières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_royale_du_génie_de_Mézières), where he enhanced the course quality and taught future mathematicians, including [Jean-Charles de Borda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Charles_de_Borda) and [Charles-Augustin de Coulomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles-Augustin_de_Coulomb).[^o4]

Bossut's research in [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics) and resistance to [planetary motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_motion) earned recognition from the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences), resulting in multiple Grand Prix awards.[^o4] He played a pivotal role in guiding [Gaspard Monge](wikipedia://en/77840)'s career, who later succeeded him at Mézières.[^o4]

In the 1760s, Bossut authored textbooks, notably *Traité élémentaire de mécanique* (1763) and *Cours complet de mathématiques* (1765).[^o4] He also served as an examiner at Mézières and later at the École Polytechnique.[^o4]

In 1774, Bossut was appointed to a newly established chair of [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamics) at the Louvre, a position he held until 1780.[^o4] During this time, he conducted [fluid resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_%28physics%29) experiments and edited an edition of Pascal's works.[^o4]

Bossut's later works include contributing to Diderot's *Encyclopédie méthodique* and publishing *Mécanique en général* (1792) and *Essai sur l'histoire générale des mathématiques* (1802).[^o4] In his later years, Bossut became reclusive and remained unmarried.[^o4] Nonetheless, he received accolades from several scientific academies, including those in Lyon, Toulouse, St Petersburg, Turin, and Bologna, for his contributions to mathematics.[^o4]
