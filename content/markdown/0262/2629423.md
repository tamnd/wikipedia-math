# Normal scheme

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Normal scheme"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), an [algebraic variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties) or [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X* is **normal** if it is normal at every point, meaning that the [local ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_ring_at_a_point) at the point is an [integrally closed domain](wikipedia://en/21420209).[^o2] An [affine variety](wikipedia://en/320468) *X* (understood to be irreducible) is normal if and only if the ring *O*(*X*) of [regular functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_function) on *X* is an integrally closed domain. A variety *X* over a field is normal if and only if every [finite](wikipedia://en/2139357) [birational morphism](wikipedia://en/382733) from any variety *Y* to *X* is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828).

:::

Normal varieties were introduced by [Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352).[^o3]

## Geometric and algebraic interpretations of normality {#Geometric_and_algebraic_interpretations_of_normality}

A morphism of varieties is finite if the inverse image of every point is finite and the morphism is [proper](wikipedia://en/603782). A morphism of varieties is birational if it restricts to an isomorphism between dense open subsets. So, for example, the [cuspidal cubic curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuspidal_cubic_curve) *X* in the affine plane *A*<sup>2</sup> defined by *x*<sup>2</sup> = *y*<sup>3</sup> is not normal, because there is a finite birational morphism *A*<sup>1</sup> → *X* (namely, *t* maps to (*t*<sup>3</sup>, *t*<sup>2</sup>)) which is not an isomorphism. By contrast, the affine line *A*<sup>1</sup> is normal: it cannot be simplified any further by finite birational morphisms.

A normal complex variety *X* has the property, when viewed as a [stratified space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically_stratified_space) using the classical topology, that every link is connected. Equivalently, every complex point *x* has arbitrarily small neighborhoods *U* such that *U* minus the singular set of *X* is connected. For example, it follows that the nodal cubic curve *X* in the figure, defined by *y*<sup>2</sup> = *x*<sup>2</sup>(*x* + 1), is not normal. This also follows from the definition of normality, since there is a finite birational morphism from *A*<sup>1</sup> to *X* which is not an isomorphism; it sends two points of *A*<sup>1</sup> to the same point in *X*.

*Image: Newtonsche Knoten.png.* Curve *y*<sup>2</sup> = *x*<sup>2</sup>(*x* + 1)

More generally, a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X* is **normal** if each of its [local rings](wikipedia://en/153391)

*O*<sub>*X,x*</sub>

is an [integrally closed domain](wikipedia://en/21420209). That is, each of these rings is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) *R*, and every ring *S* with *R* ⊆ *S* ⊆ Frac(*R*) such that *S* is finitely generated as an *R*-module is equal to *R*. (Here Frac(*R*) denotes the [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) of *R*.) This is a direct translation, in terms of local rings, of the geometric condition that every finite birational morphism to *X* is an isomorphism. For instance, in the case of the nodal cubic *X* in the figure, the local ring $A = \left(k[x,y]/(y^2 - x^2(x+1))\right)_{(x,y)}$ is not integrally closed in its field of fractions, since *y/x* is integral over *A* but is not in *A*. Therefore *X* is not normal at the point (0,0).[^o5]

An older notion is that a subvariety *X* of projective space is [linearly normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_normal#Projective_normality) if the [linear system](wikipedia://en/1186886) giving the embedding is complete. Equivalently, *X* ⊆ **P**<sup>n</sup> is not the linear projection of an embedding *X* ⊆ **P**<sup>n+1</sup> (unless *X* is contained in a hyperplane **P**<sup>n</sup>). This is the meaning of "normal" in the phrases [rational normal curve](wikipedia://en/2380117) and [rational normal scroll](wikipedia://en/34956805).

Every [regular scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#regular) is normal. Conversely, [Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352) showed that every normal variety is regular outside a subset of codimension at least 2, and a similar result is true for schemes.[^o6][^o7] So, for example, every normal [curve](wikipedia://en/253260) is regular.

## The normalization {#The_normalization}

Any [reduced scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_scheme) *X* has a unique **normalization**: a normal scheme *Y* with an integral birational morphism *Y* → *X*. (For *X* a variety over a field, the morphism *Y* → *X* is finite, which is stronger than "integral".[^o9]) The normalization of a scheme of dimension 1 is regular, and the normalization of a scheme of dimension 2 has only isolated singularities. Normalization is not usually used for [resolution of singularities](wikipedia://en/9914115) for schemes of higher dimension.

To define the normalization, first suppose that *X* is an [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#irreducible) reduced scheme *X*. Every affine open subset of *X* has the form Spec *R* with *R* an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462). Write *X* as a union of affine open subsets Spec *A*<sub>i</sub>. Let *B*<sub>i</sub> be the [integral closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_closure) of *A*<sub>i</sub> in its fraction field. Then the normalization of *X* is defined by gluing together the affine schemes Spec *B*<sub>i</sub>.

If the initial scheme is not irreducible, the normalization is defined to be the disjoint union of the normalizations of the irreducible components.

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

#### Normalization of a cusp {#Normalization_of_a_cusp}

Consider the affine curve

> $$C = \text{Spec} \left( \frac{ k[x,y] }{ y^2 - x^5 } \right)$$

with the [cusp singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cusp_singularity) at the origin. Its normalization can be given by the map

> $$\text{Spec}(k[t]) \to C$$

induced from the algebra map

> $$x \mapsto t^2, y \mapsto t^5$$

#### Normalization of axes in affine plane {#Normalization_of_axes_in_affine_plane}

For example,

> $$X=\text{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(xy))$$

is not an irreducible scheme since it has two components. Its normalization is given by the scheme morphism

> $$\text{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(x)\times\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(y)) \to \text{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(xy))$$

induced from the two quotient maps

> $$\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(xy) \to \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(x,xy) = \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(x)$$

> $$\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(xy) \to \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(y,xy) = \mathbb{C}[x,y]/(y)$$

#### Normalization of reducible projective variety {#Normalization_of_reducible_projective_variety}

Similarly, for homogeneous irreducible polynomials $f_1,\ldots,f_k$ in a UFD, the normalization of

> $$\text{Proj}\left( \frac{k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]}{(f_1\cdots f_k,g)} \right)$$

is given by the morphism

> $$\text{Proj}\left(\prod \frac{k[x_0\ldots, x_n]}{(f_i,g)} \right) \to \text{Proj}\left( \frac{k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]}{(f_1\cdots f_k,g)} \right)$$

:::
