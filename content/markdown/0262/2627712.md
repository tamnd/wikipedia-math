# Yuri Matiyasevich

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Yuri Matiyasevich"}

**Yuri Vladimirovich Matiyasevich** (Ю́рий Влади́мирович Матиясе́вич; born 2 March 1947 in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad)) is a Russian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist). He is best known for his negative solution of [Hilbert's tenth problem](wikipedia://en/101851) ([Matiyasevich's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matiyasevich's_theorem)), which was presented in his 1972 [doctoral thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_thesis) at [LOMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOMI) (the Leningrad Department of the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494)). He continued to work at that institute, becoming a professor there in 1995.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early years and education {#Early_years_and_education}

Yuri Matiyasevich was born in Leningrad on March 2, 1947. The first few classes he studied at school No. 255 with Sofia G. Generson, thanks to whom he became interested in mathematics. In 1961 he began to participate in all-Russian olympiads. From 1962 to 1963 he studied at Leningrad [physical and mathematical school No. 239](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Lyceum_239). Also from 7th to 9th grade he was involved in the mathematical circle of the Leningrad Palace of Pioneers. In 1963-1964 he completed 10th grade at the [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) physics and mathematics boarding school No. 18 named after [A. N. Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161).[^n1][^n2]

In 1964, he won a gold medal at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208)[^o7] and was enrolled in the Mathematics and Mechanics Department of [St. Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_State_University) without exams. He took his high school diploma exams as a first-year student.[^n3][^o9]

Being a second-year student, he released two papers in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) that were published in the *[Proceedings of the USSR Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_USSR_Academy_of_Sciences)*. He presented these works at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1966.[^n2]

After graduation, he enrolled in graduate school at [St. Petersburg Department of Steklov Mathematical Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences](wikipedia://en/16031996) (POMI). In 1970, under the guidance of Sergei Maslov (mathematician), he defended his thesis for the degree of [Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences) in Physics and Mathematics.[^n4]

In 1972, at the age of 25, he defended his doctoral dissertation on the unsolvability of Hilbert's tenth problem.[^n5] Using [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_numbers), he managed to show that solutions to [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109) may grow exponentially. Earlier work by [Julia Robinson](wikipedia://en/952987), [Martin Davis](wikipedia://en/953041) and [Hilary Putnam](wikipedia://en/75233) had shown that this suffices to prove that every [computably enumerable set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursively_enumerable_set) is Diophantine, a result which solves Hilbert's tenth problem and is now known as the MRDP theorem.

From 1974 Matiyasevich worked in scientific positions at LOMI, first as a senior researcher, in 1980 he headed the Laboratory of Mathematical Logic. In 1995, Matiyasevich became a professor at POMI, initially at the chair of [software engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_engineering), later at the chair of [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^n6]

In 1997, he was elected as a corresponding member of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences). Since 1998, Yuri Matiyasevich has been a vice-president of the [St. Petersburg Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Mathematical_Society). Since 2002, he has been a head of the [St.Petersburg City Mathematical Olympiad](http://www.pdmi.ras.ru/~olymp/).

Since 2003, Matiyasevich has been a co-director of an annual German–Russian student school [JASS](http://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/jass07/).

In 2008, he was elected as a full member of [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o14]

He was a member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438); and also of the editorial boards for the journals [Discrete Mathematics and Applications](https://web.archive.org/web/20070405101041/http://www.vsppub.com/journals/jn-DisMatApp.html) and [Computer Instruments in Education](http://www.ipo.spb.ru/journal/). As a teacher, he mentored [Eldar Musayev](https://web.archive.org/web/20070112070241/http://www.eldar.com/eldar/eldar.htm), [Maxim Vsemirnov](http://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~vsemir/), [Alexei Pastor](http://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~pastor/), [Dmitri Karpov](http://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~dvk/).[^o15]

A [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) related to the colorings of a [triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440976) of a sphere was named after Matiyasevich; see [The Matiyasevich polynomial, four colour theorem and weight systems](http://www.pdmi.ras.ru/~duzhin/papers/mati-pol.ps.gz).

## Selected works {#Selected_works}

- At the age of 22, he came up with a negative solution [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) for [Hilbert's tenth problem](wikipedia://en/101851) ([Matiyasevich's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matiyasevich's_theorem)), which was presented in his [doctoral thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_thesis) at [LOMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOMI) (the Leningrad Department of the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494)).[^n7]
- In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), he answered [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya)'s question of 1927 regarding an infinite system of inequalities linking the Taylor coefficients of the [Riemann $\zeta$-function](wikipedia://en/25809). He proved that all these inequalities are a consequence of a single functional inequality linking the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of a $\zeta$-function and its derivatives.[^n8]
- In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), he found an unexpected connection between the [four color theorem](wikipedia://en/10949) and divisibility of [binomial coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_coefficients), and gave a probabilistic interpretation of the four color theorem.[^n8]
- He discovered a number of new interesting qualities of the zeros of the Riemann $\zeta$-function.[^o15]
