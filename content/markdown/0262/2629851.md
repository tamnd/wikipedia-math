# Rational singularity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rational singularity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more particularly in the field of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) $X$ has **rational singularities**, if it is [normal](wikipedia://en/2629423), of finite type over a field of [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_of_a_ring) zero, and there exists a [proper](wikipedia://en/603782) [birational map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birational_map)

:::

$$f \colon Y \rightarrow X$$

from a [regular scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#Properties_of_schemes) $Y$ such that the [higher direct images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_direct_image) of $f_*$ applied to $\mathcal{O}_Y$ are trivial. That is,

$R^i f_* \mathcal{O}_Y = 0$ for $i > 0$.

If there is one such resolution, then it follows that all resolutions share this property, since any two resolutions of singularities can be dominated by a third.

For surfaces, rational singularities were defined by (Artin 1966).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formulations"}

## Formulations {#Formulations}

Alternately, one can say that $X$ has rational singularities if and only if the natural map in the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897)

$$\mathcal{O}_X \rightarrow R f_* \mathcal{O}_Y$$

is a [quasi-isomorphism](wikipedia://en/3653039). Notice that this includes the statement that $\mathcal{O}_X \simeq f_* \mathcal{O}_Y$ and hence the assumption that $X$ is normal.

There are related notions in positive and mixed [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_of_a_ring) of

- [pseudo-rational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-rational_%28mathematics%29)

and

- [F-rational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-rational_%28mathematics%29)

Rational singularities are in particular [Cohen-Macaulay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen-Macaulay_ring), [normal](wikipedia://en/2629423) and [Du Bois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Bois_singularities). They need not be [Gorenstein](wikipedia://en/48497362) or even [Q-Gorenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-Gorenstein).

[Log terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log_terminal) singularities are rational.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

An example of a rational singularity is the singular point of the [quadric cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadric_cone)

$$x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 0. \,$$

Artin[^o8] showed that the rational [double points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_point) of [algebraic surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311) are the [Du Val singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Val_singularities).
