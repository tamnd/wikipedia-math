# Sphericity

sphericity in statistics

*Image: Rounding & sphericity EN.svg.* Schematic representation of difference in grain shape. Two parameters are shown: sphericity (vertical) and [rounding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundness_%28geology%29) (horizontal).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sphericity"}

**Sphericity** is a measure of how closely the shape of a [physical object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_object) resembles that of a perfect [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859). For example, the sphericity of the [balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_%28bearing%29) inside a [ball bearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_bearing) determines the [quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_%28business%29) of the bearing, such as the load it can bear or the speed at which it can turn without failing. Sphericity is a specific example of a [compactness measure](wikipedia://en/4662632) of a shape.

:::

Sphericity applies in [three dimensions](wikipedia://en/3054853); its analogue in [two dimensions](wikipedia://en/19469852), such as the [cross sectional](wikipedia://en/1337370) circles along a [cylindrical](wikipedia://en/859283) object such as a [shaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaft_%28mechanical_engineering%29), is called [*roundness*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundness_%28object%29).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Defined by Wadell in 1935,[^o4] the sphericity, $\Psi$, of an object is the ratio of the [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) of a sphere with the same volume to the object's surface area:

$$\Psi = \frac{\pi^{\frac{1}{3}}(6V_p)^{\frac{2}{3}}}{A_p}$$

where $V_p$ is volume of the object and $A_p$ is the surface area. The sphericity of a sphere is [unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29) by definition and, by the [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182), any shape which is not a sphere will have sphericity less than 1.

:::

## Ellipsoidal objects {#Ellipsoidal_objects}

The sphericity, $\Psi$, of an [oblate spheroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblate_spheroid) (similar to the shape of the planet [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228)) is:

$$\Psi = \frac{\pi^{\frac{1}{3}}(6V_p)^{\frac{2}{3}}}{A_p} = \frac{2\sqrt[3]{ab^2}}{a+\frac{b^2}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}\ln{\left(\frac{a+\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}b\right)}},$$

where *a* and *b* are the [semi-major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-major_axis) and [semi-minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-minor_axis) axes respectively.

## Derivation {#Derivation}

Hakon Wadell defined sphericity as the surface area of a sphere of the same volume as the object divided by the actual surface area of the object.

First we need to write surface area of the sphere, $A_s$ in terms of the volume of the object being measured, $V_p$

$$A_{s}^3 = \left(4 \pi r^2\right)^3 = 4^3 \pi^3 r^6 = 4 \pi \left(4^2 \pi^2 r^6\right) = 4 \pi \cdot 3^2 \left(\frac{4^2 \pi^2}{3^2} r^6\right) = 36 \pi \left(\frac{4 \pi}{3} r^3\right)^2 = 36\,\pi V_{p}^2$$

therefore

$$A_{s} = \left(36\,\pi V_{p}^2\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} = 36^{\frac{1}{3}} \pi^{\frac{1}{3}} V_{p}^{\frac{2}{3}} = 6^{\frac{2}{3}} \pi^{\frac{1}{3}} V_{p}^{\frac{2}{3}} = \pi^{\frac{1}{3}} \left(6V_{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}$$

hence we define $\Psi$ as:

$$\Psi = \frac{A_s}{A_p} = \frac{ \pi^{\frac{1}{3}} \left(6V_{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} }{A_{p}}$$
