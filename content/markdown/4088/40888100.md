# Isotypical representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isotypical representation"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), an **isotypical**, **primary** or **factor representation**[^o2] of a group G is a [unitary representation](wikipedia://en/292788) $\pi : G \longrightarrow \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ such that any two [subrepresentations](wikipedia://en/55943952) have equivalent sub-subrepresentations.[^o3] This is related to the notion of a primary or [factor representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_representation) of a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184), or to the factor for a [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185): the representation $\pi$ of G is isotypical iff $\pi(G)^{''}$ is a factor.

:::

This term more generally used in the context of [semisimple modules](wikipedia://en/1001361).

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Property"}

## Property {#Property}

One of the interesting property of this notion lies in the fact that two isotypical representations are either quasi-equivalent or disjoint (in analogy with the fact that [irreducible representations](wikipedia://en/385982) are either unitarily equivalent or disjoint).

This can be understood through the correspondence between factor representations and minimal [central projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_projection) (in a von Neumann algebra).[^o6] Two minimal central projections are then either equal or orthogonal.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Let G be a compact group. A corollary of the [Peter–Weyl theorem](wikipedia://en/294474) has that any unitary representation $\pi : G \longrightarrow \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ on a [separable Hilbert space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_Hilbert_space) $\mathcal{H}$ is a possibly infinite [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of finite dimensional irreducible representations. An isotypical representation is any direct sum of equivalent irreducible representations that appear (typically multiple times) in $\mathcal{H}$.

:::
