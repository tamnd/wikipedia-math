# Ornstein isomorphism theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ornstein isomorphism theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Ornstein isomorphism theorem** is a deep result in [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986). It states that if two [Bernoulli schemes](wikipedia://en/2580555) have the same [Kolmogorov entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov_entropy), then they are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828).[^o2][^o3] The result, given by [Donald Ornstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Ornstein) in 1970, is important because it states that many systems previously believed to be unrelated are in fact isomorphic; these include many finite [stationary stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_stochastic_process), including [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876) and [subshifts of finite type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subshifts_of_finite_type), [Anosov flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anosov_flow) and [Sinai's billiards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai's_billiards), [ergodic automorphisms of the *n*-torus](wikipedia://en/2217712), and the [continued fraction transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GKW_operator).

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

The theorem is actually a collection of related theorems. The first theorem states that if two different [Bernoulli shifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_shift) have the same [Kolmogorov entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov_entropy), then they are [isomorphic as dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_of_dynamical_systems). The third theorem extends this result to [flows](wikipedia://en/2619735): namely, that there exists a flow $T_t$ such that $T_1$ is a Bernoulli shift. The fourth theorem states that, for a given fixed entropy, this flow is unique, up to a constant rescaling of time. The fifth theorem states that there is a single, unique flow (up to a constant rescaling of time) that has infinite entropy. The phrase "up to a constant rescaling of time" means simply that if $T_t$ and $S_t$ are two Bernoulli flows with the same entropy, then $S_t = T_{ct}$ for some constant *c*. The developments also included proofs that factors of Bernoulli shifts are isomorphic to Bernoulli shifts, and gave criteria for a given measure-preserving dynamical system to be isomorphic to a Bernoulli shift.

A corollary of these results is a solution to the root problem for Bernoulli shifts: So, for example, given a shift *T*, there is another shift $\sqrt{T}$ that is isomorphic to it.

## History {#History}

The question of isomorphism dates to [von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), who asked if the two [Bernoulli schemes](wikipedia://en/2580555) BS(1/2, 1/2) and BS(1/3, 1/3, 1/3) were isomorphic or not. In 1959, [Ya. Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya._Sinai) and [Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov) replied in the negative, showing that two different schemes cannot be isomorphic if they do not have the same entropy. Specifically, they showed that the entropy of a Bernoulli scheme BS(*p*<sub>1</sub>, *p*<sub>2</sub>,..., *p*<sub>*n*</sub>) is given by[^o6][^o7]

$$H = -\sum_{i=1}^N p_i \log p_i .$$

The Ornstein isomorphism theorem, proved by [Donald Ornstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Ornstein) in 1970, states that two Bernoulli schemes with the same entropy are [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_of_dynamical_systems). The result is sharp,[^o8] in that very similar, non-scheme systems do not have this property; specifically, there exist [Kolmogorov systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov_system) with the same entropy that are not isomorphic. Ornstein received the [Bôcher prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bôcher_prize) for this work.

A simplified proof of the isomorphism theorem for symbolic Bernoulli schemes was given by Michael S. Keane and M. Smorodinsky in 1979.[^o9][^o10]
