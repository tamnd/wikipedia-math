# Edmund Hlawka

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Edmund Hlawka"}

**Edmund Hlawka** (5 November 1916, [Bruck an der Mur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruck_an_der_Mur), [Styria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styria) – 19 February 2009) was an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was a leading [number theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theorist). Hlawka did most of his work at the [Vienna University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_University_of_Technology). He was also a visiting professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). Hlawka died on 19 February 2009 in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hlawka studied at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) from 1934 to 1938, when he gained his doctorate under [Nikolaus Hofreiter](wikipedia://en/22209837). Among his PhD students were [Rainer Burkard](wikipedia://en/27415322), later to become president of the Austrian Society for Operations Research, graph theorist [Gert Sabidussi](wikipedia://en/31554519), [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) winner [Wolfgang M. Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_M._Schmidt), [Walter Knödel](wikipedia://en/41885639) who became one of the first German [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) professors, and [Hermann Maurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Maurer), also a computer scientist. Through these and other students, Hlawka has over 3000 [academic descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_genealogy). Hlawka was awarded the [Decoration for Services to the Republic of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decoration_for_Services_to_the_Republic_of_Austria) in 2007.
