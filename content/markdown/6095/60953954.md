# Schlüsselgerät 41

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schlüsselgerät 41"}

*Image: Hitlermühle IMG 1481.JPG.* *Schlüsselgerät* 41 at the [Fort Reuenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Reuenthal) Museum

The ***Schlüsselgerät* 41** ("Cipher Machine 41"), also known as the **SG-41** or **Hitler mill**, was a [rotor cipher machine](wikipedia://en/196223), first produced in 1941 in [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany), that was designed as a potential successor for the [Enigma machine](wikipedia://en/9256). It saw limited use by the *[Abwehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abwehr)* (German Army intelligence) towards the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).[^o2][^o3][^o7]

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Schlüsselgerät 41.jpg.* An SG-41 ready to encipher text

The SG-41 was created under order of the *[Heereswaffenamt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waffenamt)* ([Inspectorate 7/VI organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_der_Nachrichtenaufklärung#Inspectorate_7/VI_organisation))[^o5] as a collaboration between German cryptographer [Fritz Menzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Menzer) and [Wanderer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanderer_%28company%29), a leading typewriter manufacturer.[^o6] The machine also acquired the nickname "Hitler mill" because of the large crank attached to the side of the unit. Instead of using a lampboard like the Enigma, the SG-41 printed both the plaintext and ciphertext of the message onto two paper tapes. Due to wartime shortages of [light metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_metal) such as [aluminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium) and [magnesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium), the SG-41 weighed approximately \13.5 kg,[^o5] which made it unsuitable for the front lines.[^o2]

Menzer intended for the SG-41 to fully replace Enigma, which he considered to no longer be secure;[^o5] the *[Luftwaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftwaffe)* and *[Heer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_%281935–1945%29)* ordered around 11,000 units.[^o6]

A total of 1,000 units were produced. Various sources have reported production figures as low as 500 units due to [materiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materiel) shortages,[^o7][^o8] but production was halted after 1,000 units, as it was considered too heavy for use on the front. In December 1943, General [Fritz Thiele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Thiele) ordered production to cease by the end of 1944.[^o5] Beginning on 12 October 1944, the first deliveries to the *[Abwehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abwehr)* began.[^o9] In the final months of the war, the SG-41 was used instead of the *Abwehr* Enigma.[^o2]

## Function {#Function}

*Image: Six Cipher Wheels of SG-41.jpg.* The SG-41 had six cipher rotors.

*Image: Hitlermühle IMG 1481 excerpt.jpg.* The "J" key was red and was used to switch between letters and numbers; "JRQJ" would represent "41".

Functionally, the machine had greater similarities with the [Boris Hagelin](wikipedia://en/578033) [C-Series](wikipedia://en/743513). The SG-41 had six encryption rotors, compared to the Enigma, which had either three or four, in addition to a number of advanced features,[^o11] making it much more resistant to cryptanalysis than the Enigma or other contemporary Hagelin machines.[^o12] While the Enigma rotors advanced by one for each letter enciphered, the SG-41's wheels interacted with each other and moved irregularly. Similar functionality was not adopted in a mass-produced cipher machine until 1952 with the advent of the Hagelin [CX-52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CX-52).[^o13]

## Cryptanalysis {#Cryptanalysis}

The Allied codebreakers in [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037) considered the device a "mystery".[^o6] Only a handful of messages were able to be deciphered during the war, namely when two messages were "[in depth](wikipedia://en/5715#Depth)" i.e. encrypted with the same key. The inner workings of the device were unclear until after the war, so it was not possible to perform a systematic cryptanalysis on the messages. Allied codebreakers referred to it as a "remarkable machine".[^o9]

## SG-41Z {#SG-41Z}

In the final months of the war, an additional 550 units were built, which are referred to as the **SG-41Z**.[^o5][^o16] This model only allowed the numbers 0–9 to be enciphered and was used by the *[Luftwaffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftwaffe)* for weather reports.[^o9]

## Find near Aying {#Find_near_Aying}

On 5 May 2017, two hobbyist [treasure hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_hunter) found an SG-41 using a metal detector in a forest near the [Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria) city of [Aying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aying), buried approximately 40 cm deep.[^o18] The hobbyists donated their find to the [Deutsches Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Museum) in Munich instead of selling it privately.[^o19] The museum intends to [conserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_and_restoration_of_cultural_heritage) it in its current condition and display it in a new permanent exhibit, *Bild – Schrift – Codes*.[^o20]
