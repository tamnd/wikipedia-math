# Helen Popova Alderson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helen Popova Alderson"}

**Helen Popova Alderson** (1924–1972) was a Soviet and British mathematician and mathematics translator known for her research on [quasigroups](wikipedia://en/25223) and on higher [reciprocity laws](wikipedia://en/247622).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Alderson was born on 14 May 1924 in [Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku), then part of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), to a family of two academics from [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow). Her father, a [neurophysiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurophysiologist), had been a student of [Ivan Pavlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Pavlov). She began studying mathematics at [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_University) in 1937, when she was only 13. She had to break off her studies because of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), moving to Paris as a refugee with her family.

After the war, she returned to study at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh). She completed a Ph.D. there in 1951; her dissertation was *Logarithmetics of Non-Associative Algebras*.

After leaving mathematical research to raise two children in Cambridge, she was funded by the [Calouste Gulbenkian Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calouste_Gulbenkian_Foundation) with a Fellowship at [Lucy Cavendish College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Cavendish_College,_Cambridge), beginning in the late 1960s. At Cambridge, she worked with [J. W. S. Cassels](wikipedia://en/1709206).

She died on 5 November 1972, from complications of kidney disease.

## Research {#Research}

In the theory of higher [reciprocity laws](wikipedia://en/247622), Alderson published necessary and sufficient conditions for 2 and 3 to be [seventh powers](wikipedia://en/54558969), in [modular arithmetic](wikipedia://en/20087) modulo a given [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$.7X

According to (Smith 1976), "plain quasigroups were first studied by Helen Popova-Alderson, in a series of papers dating back to the early fifties". Smith cites in particular a posthumous paper (Alderson 1974)FPQ and its references. In this context, a [quasigroup](wikipedia://en/25223) is a mathematical structure consisting of a set of elements and a [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) that does not necessarily obey the [associative law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_law), but where (like a [group](wikipedia://en/19447)) this operation can be inverted. Being plain involves having only a finite number of elements and no non-trivial subalgebras.

## Translation {#Translation}

As well as Russian, English, and French, Alderson spoke Polish, Czech, and some German. She became the English translator of *Elementary Number Theory*, a textbook originally published in Russian in 1937 by B. A. Venkov. Her translation was published by Wolters-Noordhoff of Groningen in 1970. As well as the original text, it includes footnotes by Alderson updating the material with new developments in number theory.ENT
