# Beilinson–Bernstein localization

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Beilinson–Bernstein localization"}

In mathematics, especially in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **Beilinson–Bernstein localization** theorem relates [D-modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-modules) on [flag varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_variety) *G*/*B* to representations of the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak g$ attached to a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) *G*. It was introduced by (Beilinson Bernstein 1981).

:::

Extensions of this theorem include the case of partial flag varieties *G*/*P*, where *P* is a [parabolic subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) in (Holland Polo 1996) and a theorem relating *D*-modules on the [affine Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/16896827) to representations of the [Kac–Moody algebra](wikipedia://en/1146292) $\widehat \mathfrak g$ in (Frenkel Gaitsgory 2009).

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *G* be a reductive group over the complex numbers, and *B* a Borel subgroup. Then there is an equivalence of categories[^o7]

$$\mathcal{D}\text{-Mod}(G/B)\ \simeq\ \left(U(\mathfrak{g})/\ker\chi\right) \text{-Mod}.$$

On the left is the category of [D-modules](wikipedia://en/3440755) on *G/B*. On the right *χ* is a homomorphism *χ : Z(U(g)) → **C*** from the centre of the universal enveloping algebra,

$$Z(U(\mathfrak{g}))\ \simeq\ \text{Sym}(\mathfrak{t})^{W,\rho},$$

corresponding to the [weight](wikipedia://en/277179) *\-ρ ∈ t<sup>\*</sup>* given by minus half the sum over the positive [roots](wikipedia://en/277087) of *g*. The above action of *W* on *t<sup>\*</sup> = Spec Sym(t)* is shifted so as to fix *\-ρ*.

### Twisted version {#Twisted_version}

There is an equivalence of categories[^o9]

$$\mathcal{D}_\lambda\text{-Mod}(G/B)\ \simeq\ \left(U(\mathfrak{g})/\ker\chi_\lambda\right) \text{-Mod}.$$

for any *λ ∈ t<sup>\*</sup>* such that *λ-ρ* does not pair with any positive root *α* to give a nonpositive integer (it is "regular dominant"):

$$(\lambda-\rho, \alpha)\ \in\ \mathbf{C}-\mathbf{Z}_{\le 0}.$$

Here *χ* is the central character corresponding to *λ-ρ*, and *D<sub>λ</sub>* is the sheaf of rings on *G/B* formed by taking the [\*-pushforward](wikipedia://en/3022744) of *D<sub>G/U</sub>* along the *T*-bundle *G/U → G/B*, a sheaf of rings whose center is the constant sheaf of algebras *U(t)*, and taking the quotient by the central character determined by *λ* (not *λ-ρ*).

:::

## Example: *SL<sub>2</sub>* {#Example:_SL2}

The Lie algebra of [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641) on the [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578) **P**<sup>1</sup> is identified with *sl<sub>2</sub>*, and

$$U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)/\Omega\ \simeq \ \mathcal{D}(\mathbf{P}^1)$$

via

$$(e,h,f) \ \mapsto \ (\partial_z, -2z\partial_z, z^2\partial_z)$$

It can be checked linear combinations of three vector fields **C** ⊂ **P**<sup>1</sup> are the only vector fields extending to ∞ ∈ **P**<sup>1</sup>. Here,

$$\Omega\ =\ ef+fe+\frac{1}{2}h^2$$

is sent to zero.

The only finite dimensional *sl<sub>2</sub>* representation on which *Ω* acts by zero is the [trivial representation](wikipedia://en/450573) *k*, which is sent to the constant sheaf, i.e. the ring of functions *O ∈ D-Mod*. The Verma module of weight 0 is sent to the D-Module *δ* supported at *0* ∈ **P**<sup>1</sup>.

Each finite dimensional representation corresponds to a different twist.
