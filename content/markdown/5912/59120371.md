# Jerzy Baksalary

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jerzy Baksalary"}

**Jerzy Kazimierz Baksalary** (25 June 1944 – 8 March 2005) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specialized in [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) and [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422).[^o3] In 1990 he was appointed professor of mathematical sciences. He authored over 170 academic papers published and won one of the Ministry of National Education awards.[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education (1944 – 1988) {#Early_life_and_education_(1944_–_1988)}

Baksalary was born in [Poznań, Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań,_Poland) on 25 June 1944.[^o3] From 1969 to 1988, he worked at the [Agricultural University of Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_University_of_Poznań).[^o3]

In 1975, Baksalary received a PhD degree from [Adam Mickiewicz University in Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Mickiewicz_University_in_Poznań); his thesis on [linear statistical models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_statistical_models) was supervised by [Tadeusz Caliński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Caliński).[^o3][^o7] He received a [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1984, also from Adam Mickiewicz University, where his *Habilitationsschrift* was also on linear statistical models.[^o3]

### Career (1988 – 2005) {#Career_(1988_–_2005)}

In 1988, Baksalary joined the [Tadeusz Kotarbiński Pedagogical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Kotarbiński_Pedagogical_University) in [Zielona Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zielona_Góra), Poland, being the university's rector from 1990 to 1996.[^o3] In 1990, he became a "Professor of Mathematical Sciences", a title received from the [President of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Poland).[^o3] For the 1989–1990 academic year, he moved to the [University of Tampere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tampere) in Finland.[^o3] Later on, he joined the [University of Zielona Góra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zielona_Góra).[^o3]

### 2005 death and legacy {#2005_death_and_legacy}

Baksalary died in Poznań on 8 March 2005.[^o3][^o7] His funeral was held there on 15 March 2005.[^o3][^o7] There, Caliński praised Baksalary for his "contributions to the Poznań school of mathematical statistics and [biometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometry)".[^o3]

Memorial events in honor of Baksalary were also held at two conferences after his death:[^o3]

- The 14th International Workshop on Matrices and Statistics, held at [Massey University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massey_University) in New Zealand from 29 March to 1 April 2005.
- The Southern Ontario Matrices and Statistics Days, held at the [University of Windsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Windsor)[^o10] in Canada from 9 to 10 June 2005.

## Research {#Research}

In 1979, Baksalary and [Radosław Kala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radosław_Kala) proved that the [matrix equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_equation) $AX - YB = C$ has a solution for some matrices *X* and *Y* if and only if $(I - A^-A)C(I - B^-B) = 0$.[^o12] (Here, $A^-$ denotes some [g-inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-inverse) of the matrix *A*.) This is equivalent to a 1952 result by [W. E. Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._E._Roth) on the same equation, which states that the equation has a solution iff the ranks of the [block matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_matrices) $\begin{bmatrix} A & C\\ 0 & B\\ \end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix} A & 0\\ 0 & B\\ \end{bmatrix}$ are equal.[^o12]

In 1980, he and Kala extended this result to the matrix equation $AXB + CYD = E$, proving that it can be solved if and only if $K_GK_AE = 0, K_AER_D = 0, K_CER_B = 0, ER_BR_H = 0$, where $G := K_AC$ and $H := DR_B$.[^o13]p. 146 (Here, the notation $K_M := I - MM^-$, $R_M := I - M^-M$ is adopted for any matrix *M*.[^o13]p. 146)

In 1981, Baksalary and Kala proved that for a [Gauss-Markov model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss-Markov_model) $\{y, X\beta, V\}$, where the vector-valued variable has expectation $X\beta$ and variance *V* (a [dispersion matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispersion_matrix)), then for any function *F*, a [best linear unbiased estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_linear_unbiased_estimator) of $X\beta$ which is a function of $Fy$ exists iff $C(X)\subset C(TF')$. The condition is equivalent to stating that $r(X\vdots TF') = r(X)$, where $r(\cdot)$ denotes the [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) of the respective matrix.[^o14]

In 1995, Baksalary and [Sujit Kumar Mitra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sujit_Kumar_Mitra) introduced the "left-star" and "right-star" [partial orderings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_orderings) on the set of [complex matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_matrices), which are defined as follows. The matrix *A* is below the matrix *B* in the left-star ordering, written $A ~*< B$, iff $A^*A = A^*B$ and $\mathcal{M}(A)\subseteq \mathcal{M}(B)$, where $\mathcal{M}(\cdot)$ denotes the [column span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_span) and $A^*$ denotes the [conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548).[^o15]p. 76 Similarly, *A* is below *B* in the right-star ordering, written $A <*~ B$, iff $AA^* = BA^*$ and $\mathcal{M}(A^*) \subseteq \mathcal{M}(B^*)$.[^o15]p. 76

In 2000, Jerzy Baksalary and [Oskar Maria Baksalary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Maria_Baksalary) characterized all situations when a [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) $P = c_1P_1 + c_2P_2$ of two [idempotent matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent_matrices) can itself be idempotent.[^o16] These include three previously known cases $P = P_1 + P_2$, $P = P_1 - P_2$, or $P = P_2 - P_1$, previously found by Rao and Mitra (1971); and one additional case where $c_2 = 1 - c_1$ and $(P_1 - P_2)^2 = 0$.[^o16]
