# J-2 ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="J-2 ring"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), a **J-0 ring** is a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $R$ such that the set of regular points, that is, points $p$ of the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) at which the localization $R_p$ is a regular local ring, contains a non-empty open subset, a **J-1 ring** is a ring such that the set of regular points is an [open subset](wikipedia://en/39358), and a **J-2 ring** is a ring such that any [finitely generated algebra](wikipedia://en/11985661) over the ring is a J-1 ring.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Most rings that occur in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) or [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) are J-2 rings, and in fact it is not trivial to construct any examples of rings that are not. In particular all [excellent rings](wikipedia://en/10016360) are J-2 rings; in fact this is part of the definition of an excellent ring.

All [Dedekind domains](wikipedia://en/145132) of characteristic 0 and all local [Noetherian rings](wikipedia://en/56129) of dimension at most 1 are J-2 rings. The family of J-2 rings is closed under taking [localizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) and finitely generated algebras.

For an example of a [Noetherian domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noetherian_domain) that is not a J-0 ring, take *R* to be the subring of the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) *k*\[*x*<sub>1</sub>,*x*<sub>2</sub>,...\] in infinitely many generators generated by the squares and cubes of all generators, and form the ring *S* from *R* by adjoining inverses to all elements not in any of the ideals generated by some *x*<sub>*n*</sub>. Then *S* is a 1-dimensional Noetherian domain that is not a J-0 ring. More precisely *S* has a cusp singularity at every [closed point](wikipedia://en/33086408), so the set of non-singular points consists of just the ideal (0) and contains no nonempty open sets.
