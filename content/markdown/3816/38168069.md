# Exalcomm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exalcomm"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), **Exalcomm** is a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) classifying the extensions of a [commutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29) by a [module](wikipedia://en/276410). More precisely, the elements of Exalcomm<sub>*k*</sub>(*R*,*M*) are [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of commutative [*k*-algebras](wikipedia://en/191788) *E* with a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) [onto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) the *k*-algebra *R* whose [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) is the *R*-module *M* (with all pairs of elements in *M* having product 0). Note that some authors use **Exal** as the same functor. There are similar functors **Exal** and **Exan** for [non-commutative rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative_ring) and algebras, and functors **Exaltop**, **Exantop**, and **Exalcotop** that take a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) into account.

:::

"Exalcomm" is an abbreviation for "COMMutative ALgebra EXtension" (or rather for the corresponding French phrase). It was introduced by (Grothendieck Dieudonné 1964, 18.4.2).

Exalcomm is one of the [André–Quillen cohomology](wikipedia://en/23974317) [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) and one of the [Lichtenbaum–Schlessinger functors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichtenbaum–Schlessinger_functor).

Given homomorphisms of [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346) *A* → *B* → *C* and a *C*-module *L* there is an [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215) of *A*-modules (Grothendieck Dieudonné 1964, 20.2.3.1)

$$\begin{aligned} 0 \rightarrow\; &\operatorname{Der}_B(C,L)\rightarrow \operatorname{Der}_A(C,L)\rightarrow \operatorname{Der}_A(B,L) \rightarrow \\ &\operatorname{Exalcomm}_B(C,L)\rightarrow \operatorname{Exalcomm}_A(C,L)\rightarrow \operatorname{Exalcomm}_A(B,L) \end{aligned}$$

where Der<sub>*A*</sub>(*B*,*L*) is the module of derivations of the *A*-algebra *B* with values in *L*. This sequence can be extended further to the right using [André–Quillen cohomology](wikipedia://en/23974317).

## Square-zero extensions {#Square-zero_extensions}

In order to understand the construction of Exal, the notion of square-zero extensions must be defined. Fix a [topos](wikipedia://en/21391464) $T$ and let all algebras be algebras over it. Note that the topos of a point gives the special case of commutative rings, so the topos hypothesis can be ignored on a first reading.

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

### Definition {#Definition}

In order to define the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $\underline{\text{Exal}}$ we need to define what a square-zero extension actually is. Given a [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) morphism of $A$-algebras $p: E \to B$ it is called a **square-zero extension** if the kernel $I$ of $p$ has the property $I^2 = (0)$ is the zero [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977).

#### Remark {#Remark}

Note that the kernel can be equipped with a $B$-module structure as follows: since $p$ is surjective, any $b \in B$ has a lift to a $x\in E$, so $b \cdot m := x\cdot m$ for $m \in I$. Since any lift differs by an element $k \in I$ in the kernel, and

$$(x + k)\cdot m = x\cdot m + k\cdot m = x\cdot m$$

because the ideal is square-zero, this module structure is well-defined.

:::

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

#### From deformations over the dual numbers {#From_deformations_over_the_dual_numbers}

Square-zero extensions are a generalization of deformations over the [dual numbers](wikipedia://en/42169). For example, a deformation over the dual numbers

> $$\begin{matrix} \text{Spec}\left( \frac{k[x,y]}{(y^2 - x^3 )} \right) & \to & \text{Spec}\left( \frac{k[x,y][\varepsilon]}{(y^2 - x^3 + \varepsilon)} \right) \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ \text{Spec}(k) & \to & \text{Spec}(k[\varepsilon]) \end{matrix}$$

has the associated square-zero extension

> $$0 \to (\varepsilon) \to \frac{k[x,y][\varepsilon]}{(y^2 - x^3 + \varepsilon)} \to \frac{k[x,y]}{(y^2 - x^3 )} \to 0$$

of $k$-algebras.

#### From more general deformations {#From_more_general_deformations}

But, because the idea of square zero-extensions is more general, deformations over $k[\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2]$ where $\varepsilon_1\cdot \varepsilon_2 = 0$ will give examples of square-zero extensions.

#### Trivial square-zero extension {#Trivial_square-zero_extension}

For a $B$-module $M$, there is a trivial square-zero extension given by $B \oplus M$ where the product structure is given by

$$(b,m)\cdot (b',m') = (bb',bm' + b'm)$$

hence the associated square-zero extension is

$$0 \to M \to B\oplus M \to B \to 0$$

where the surjection is the projection map forgetting $M$.

:::

::: {#o14 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The general abstract construction of Exal[^o15] follows from first defining a category of extensions $\underline{\text{Exal}}$ over a topos $T$ (or just the category of commutative rings), then extracting a [subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959) where a base ring $A$ $\underline{\text{Exal}}_A$ is fixed, and then using a functor $\pi:\underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,-) \to \text{B-Mod}$ to get the module of commutative algebra extensions $\text{Exal}_A(B,M)$ for a fixed $M \in \text{Ob}(\text{B-Mod})$.

### General Exal {#General_Exal}

For this fixed topos, let $\underline{\text{Exal}}$ be the category of pairs $(A, p:E \to B)$ where $p:E\to B$ is a surjective morphism of $A$-algebras such that the kernel $I$ is square-zero, where morphisms are defined as [commutative diagrams](wikipedia://en/138484) between $(A, p:E \to B) \to (A', p':E' \to B')$. There is a functor

$$\pi: \underline{\text{Exal}} \to \text{Algmod}$$

sending a pair $(A, p:E \to B)$ to a pair $(A\to B, I)$ where $I$ is a $B$-module.

### Exal<sub>*A*,</sub> Exal<sub>*A*</sub>(*B*, –) {#ExalA,_ExalA(B,_–)}

Then, there is an overcategory denoted $\underline{\text{Exal}}_A$ (meaning there is a functor $\underline{\text{Exal}}_A \to {\displaystyle {\underline {\text{Exal}}}}$) where the objects are pairs $(A, p:E \to B)$, but the first ring $A$ is fixed, so morphisms are of the form

$$(A, p:E \to B) \to (A, p':E' \to B')$$

There is a further reduction to another overcategory $\underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,-)$ where morphisms are of the form

$$(A, p:E \to B) \to (A, p':E' \to B)$$

### Exal<sub>*A*</sub>(*B*,*I*) {#ExalA(B,I)}

Finally, the category $\underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,I)$ has a fixed kernel of the square-zero extensions. Note that in $\text{Algmod}$, for a fixed $A,B$, there is the subcategory $(A\to B, I)$ where $I$ is a $B$-module, so it is equivalent to $\text{B-Mod}$. Hence, the image of $\underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,I)$ under the functor $\pi$ lives in $\text{B-Mod}$.

The isomorphism classes of objects has the structure of a $B$-module since $\underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,I)$ is a Picard stack, so the category can be turned into a module $\text{Exal}_A(B,I)$.

:::

## Structure of Exal<sub>*A*</sub>(*B*, *I*) {#Structure_of_ExalA(B,_I)}

There are a few results on the structure of $\underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,I)$ and $\text{Exal}_A(B,I)$ which are useful.

### Automorphisms {#Automorphisms}

The [group of automorphisms](wikipedia://en/56661772) of an object $X \in \text{Ob}(\underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,I) )$ can be identified with the [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) of the trivial extension $B\oplus M$ (explicitly, we mean automorphisms $B\oplus M \to B\oplus M$ compatible with both the inclusion $M\to B \oplus M$ and projection $B\oplus M \to B$). These are classified by the derivations module $\text{Der}_A(B,M)$. Hence, the category $\underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,I)$ is a torsor. In fact, this could also be interpreted as a [Gerbe](wikipedia://en/822043) since this is a group acting on a stack.

### Composition of extensions {#Composition_of_extensions}

There is another useful result about the categories $\underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,-)$ describing the extensions of $I\oplus J$, there is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828)

> $$\underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,I\oplus J) \cong \underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,I)\times \underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,J)$$

It can be interpreted as saying the square-zero extension from a deformation in two directions can be decomposed into a pair of square-zero extensions, each in the direction of one of the deformations.

::: {#o23 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

#### Application {#Application}

For example, the deformations given by infinitesimals $\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2$ where $\varepsilon_1^2 = \varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2 = \varepsilon_2^2 = 0$ gives the isomorphism

> $$\underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,(\varepsilon_1) \oplus (\varepsilon_2)) \cong \underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,(\varepsilon_1))\times \underline{\text{Exal}}_A(B,(\varepsilon_2))$$

where $I$ is the module of these two infinitesimals. In particular, when relating this to Kodaira-Spencer theory, and using the comparison with the [cotangent complex](wikipedia://en/17346860) (given below) this means all such deformations are classified by

> $$H^1(X,T_X)\times H^1(X,T_X)$$

hence they are just a pair of first order deformations paired together.

:::

## Relation with the cotangent complex {#Relation_with_the_cotangent_complex}

The [cotangent complex](wikipedia://en/17346860) contains all of the information about a deformation problem, and it is a fundamental theorem that given a morphism of rings $A \to B$ over a topos $T$ (note taking $T$ as the point topos shows this generalizes the construction for general rings), there is a functorial isomorphism

> $\text{Exal}_A(B,M) \xrightarrow{\simeq} \text{Ext}_B^1(\mathbf{L}_{B/A}, M)$[^o15]<sup>(theorem III.1.2.3)</sup>

So, given a commutative square of ring morphisms

> $$\begin{matrix} A' & \to & B' \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ A & \to & B \end{matrix}$$

over $T$ there is a square

> $$\begin{matrix} \text{Exal}_A(B,M) & \to & \text{Ext}^1_B(\mathbf{L}_{B/A}, M) \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ \text{Exal}_{A'}(B',M) & \to & \text{Ext}^1_{B'}(\mathbf{L}_{B'/A'}, M) \end{matrix}$$

whose horizontal arrows are isomorphisms and $M$ has the structure of a $B'$-module from the ring morphism.
