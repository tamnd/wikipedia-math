# Piers Bohl

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Piers Bohl"}

**Piers Bohl** (23 October 1865 – 25 December 1921) was a [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who worked in [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [quasiperiodic functions](wikipedia://en/2657905).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in 1865 in [Walk, Livonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walk,_Livonia), in the family of a poor [Baltic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_German) merchant. In 1884, after graduating from a German school in [Viljandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viljandi), he entered the faculty of physics and mathematics at the [University of Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tartu). In 1893 Bohl was awarded his Master's degree. This was for an investigation of quasi-periodic functions. The notion of quasi-periodic functions was generalised still further by [Harald Bohr](wikipedia://en/638305) when he introduced [almost periodic functions](wikipedia://en/405512). He has been the first to prove the three-dimensional case of the [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/4101), but his work was not noticed at the time.[^o4]

## Polynomial result on trinomial equations {#Polynomial_result_on_trinomial_equations}

In 1908, Bohl established a general theorem for locating the roots of complex [trinomials](wikipedia://en/23000) of the form $P(z) = z^k + a\,z^\ell + b$, where $k$ and $\ell$ are positive integers with $k>\ell$, and $a$ and $b$ are nonzero [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). Rather than relying on heavy algebraic manipulations, he employed an elementary geometric construction: by interpreting the magnitudes of the coefficients $|a|$, $|b|$ and the chosen radius (for instance, the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071)) as the sides of a triangle, one can associate two angles that, together with the [arguments](wikipedia://en/14699765) of $a$ and $b$, yield explicit bounds. These bounds determine exactly how many roots lie inside the circle, either by simple inequalities when one coefficient dominates, or by counting the integers in a specific interval when all three lengths can form a triangle.[^o7]

Bohl's result not only unifies numerous special‐case criteria (such as those later attributed to [Schur](wikipedia://en/617522), [Cohn](wikipedia://en/23000#Root‑location_algorithms) or [Jury](wikipedia://en/13119919)) but also provides direct formulas that apply regardless of the relative sizes or orientations of the coefficients. Although his work went largely unnoticed for many decades, it anticipates modern applications in the [stability](wikipedia://en/5493795) analysis of [differential](wikipedia://en/1424309) and [difference equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_equation), where knowing whether all characteristic roots lie within the unit circle is essential for determining [asymptotic behaviour](wikipedia://en/641995).[^o7]
