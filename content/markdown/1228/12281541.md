# Lumer–Phillips theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lumer–Phillips theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Lumer–Phillips theorem,** named after [Günter Lumer](wikipedia://en/47675313) and [Ralph Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Phillips_%28mathematician%29), is a result in the theory of [strongly continuous semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C0_semigroup) that gives a necessary and sufficient condition for a [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) in a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) to generate a [contraction semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraction_semigroup).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *A* be a [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) defined on a linear subspace *D*(*A*) of the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *X*. Then *A* generates a [contraction semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraction_semigroup) if and only if[^o4]

1. *D*(*A*) is [dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_%28topology%29) in *X*,
1. *A* is [dissipative](wikipedia://en/12281671), and
1. *A* − *λ*<sub>0</sub>*I* is [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) for some *λ*<sub>0</sub>> 0, where *I* denotes the [identity operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_operator).

An operator satisfying the last two conditions is called maximally dissipative.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Variants of the theorem"}

## Variants of the theorem {#Variants_of_the_theorem}

### Reflexive spaces {#Reflexive_spaces}

Let *A* be a [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) defined on a linear subspace *D*(*A*) of the [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091) [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *X*. Then *A* generates a [contraction semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraction_semigroup) if and only if[^o8]

1. *A* is [dissipative](wikipedia://en/12281671), and
1. *A* − *λ*<sub>0</sub>*I* is [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) for some *λ*<sub>0</sub>> *0*, where *I* denotes the [identity operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_operator).

Note that the condition that *D*(*A*) is dense is dropped in comparison to the non-reflexive case. This is because in the reflexive case it follows from the other two conditions.

### Dissipativity of the adjoint {#Dissipativity_of_the_adjoint}

Let *A* be a [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) defined on a [dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_%28topology%29) linear subspace *D*(*A*) of the [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091) [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *X*. Then *A* generates a [contraction semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraction_semigroup) if and only if[^o10]

- *A* is [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_operator) and both *A* and its [adjoint operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_operator) *A<sup>∗</sup>* are [dissipative](wikipedia://en/12281671).

In case that *X* is not reflexive, then this condition for *A* to generate a contraction semigroup is still sufficient, but not necessary.[^o11]

### Quasicontraction semigroups {#Quasicontraction_semigroups}

Let *A* be a [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) defined on a linear subspace *D*(*A*) of the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *X*. Then *A* generates a [quasi contraction semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraction_semigroup) if and only if

1. *D*(*A*) is [dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_%28topology%29) in *X*,
1. *A* is [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_operator),
1. *A* is quasidissipative, i.e. there exists a *ω* ≥ 0 such that *A* − *ωI* is [dissipative](wikipedia://en/12281671), and
1. *A* − *λ*<sub>0</sub>*I* is [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) for some *λ*<sub>0</sub> > *ω*, where *I* denotes the [identity operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_operator).

:::

::: {#o14 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Consider *X* = *L*<sup>2</sup>(\[0, 1\]; **R**) with its usual inner product, and let *Au* = *u*′ with domain *D*(*A*) equal to those functions *u* in the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) *H*<sup>1</sup>(\[0, 1\]; **R**) with *u*(1) = 0. *D*(*A*) is dense. Moreover, for every *u* in *D*(*A*),

$$\langle u, A u \rangle = \int_0^1 u(x) u'(x) \, \mathrm{d} x = - \frac1{2} u(0)^2 \leq 0,$$

so that *A* is dissipative. The ordinary differential equation *u'* − *λu* = *f*, *u*(1) = 0 has a unique solution u in *H*<sup>1</sup>(\[0, 1\]; **R**) for any *f* in *L*<sup>2</sup>(\[0, 1\]; **R**), namely

$$u(x)={\rm e}^{\lambda x}\int_1^x {\rm e}^{-\lambda t}f(t)\,dt$$

so that the surjectivity condition is satisfied. Hence, by the Lumer–Phillips theorem *A* generates a contraction semigroup.

There are many more examples where a direct application of the Lumer–Phillips theorem gives the desired result.

In conjunction with translation, scaling and perturbation theory the Lumer–Phillips theorem is the main tool for showing that certain operators generate [strongly continuous semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C0_semigroup). The following is an example in point.

- A [normal operator](wikipedia://en/190167) (an operator that commutes with its adjoint) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) generates a strongly continuous semigroup if and only if its [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137) is bounded from above.[^o15]

:::
