# Graph of groups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph of groups"}

In [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), a **graph of groups** is an object consisting of a collection of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) indexed by the vertices and edges of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806), together with a family of [monomorphisms](wikipedia://en/59538) of the edge groups into the vertex groups. There is a unique group, called the **fundamental group**, canonically associated to each finite [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) graph of groups. It admits an orientation-preserving [action](wikipedia://en/12781) on a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560): the original graph of groups can be recovered from the [quotient graph](wikipedia://en/46261775) and the [stabilizer subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_subgroup). This theory, commonly referred to as [Bass–Serre theory](wikipedia://en/18673708), is due to the work of [Hyman Bass](wikipedia://en/7085910) and [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **graph of groups** over a graph $Y$ is an assignment to each vertex $x$ of $Y$ of a group $G_{x}$ and to each edge $y$ of $Y$ of a group $G_{y}$ as well as monomorphisms $\varphi_{y,0}$ and $\varphi_{y,1}$ mapping $G_{y}$ into the groups assigned to the vertices at its ends.

:::

## Fundamental group {#Fundamental_group}

Let $T$ be a [spanning tree](wikipedia://en/455770) for $Y$ and define the **fundamental group** $\Gamma$ to be the group generated by the vertex groups $G_{x}$ and elements $y$ for each edge of $Y$ with the following relations:

- $\overline{y} = y^{-1}$ if $\overline{y}$ is the edge $y$ with the reverse orientation.
- $y \varphi_{y,0}(x) y^{-1} = \varphi_{y,1}(x)$ for all $x$ in $G_{y}$.
- $y = 1$ if $y$ is an edge in $T$.

This definition is independent of the choice of $T$.

The benefit in defining the fundamental [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) of a graph of groups, as shown by (Higgins 1976), is that it is defined independently of base point or tree. In the 1976 Higgins paper, there is also a proof establishing a [normal form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_form_%28mathematics%29) for the elements of the fundamental groupoid. This includes normal form theorems for a [free product with amalgamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_product_with_amalgamation) and for an [HNN extension](wikipedia://en/2823319) (Bass 1993).

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Structure theorem"}

## Structure theorem {#Structure_theorem}

Let $\Gamma$ be the fundamental group corresponding to the spanning tree $T$. For every vertex $x$ and edge $y$, $G_{x}$ and $G_{y}$ can be identified with their images in $\Gamma$. It is possible to define a graph with vertices and edges the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of all [coset](wikipedia://en/98759) spaces $\Gamma/G_{x}$ and $\Gamma/G_{y}$ respectively. This graph is a tree, called the **universal covering tree**, on which $\Gamma$ acts. It admits the graph $Y$ as [fundamental domain](wikipedia://en/469365). The graph of groups given by the stabilizer subgroups on the fundamental domain corresponds to the original graph of groups.

:::

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A graph of groups on a graph with one edge and two vertices corresponds to a [free product with amalgamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_product_with_amalgamation).
- A graph of groups on a single vertex with a [loop](wikipedia://en/1449083) corresponds to an [HNN extension](wikipedia://en/2823319).

:::

## Generalisations {#Generalisations}

The simplest possible generalisation of a graph of groups is a 2-dimensional [complex of groups](wikipedia://en/293504#Complexes_of_groups). These are modeled on [orbifolds](wikipedia://en/293504) arising from [cocompact](wikipedia://en/5358554) [properly discontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Properly_discontinuous) actions of discrete groups on 2-dimensional [simplicial complexes](wikipedia://en/252686) that have the structure of [CAT(0) spaces](wikipedia://en/12941912). The quotient of the simplicial complex has finite stabilizer groups attached to vertices, edges and triangles together with monomorphisms for every inclusion of simplices. A complex of groups is said to be **developable** if it arises as the quotient of a CAT(0) simplicial complex. Developability is a non-positive curvature condition on the complex of groups: it can be verified locally by checking that all [circuits](wikipedia://en/168609) occurring in the [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_link) of vertices have length at least six. Such complexes of groups originally arose in the theory of 2-dimensional [Bruhat–Tits buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruhat–Tits_building); their general definition and continued study have been inspired by the ideas of [Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29).
