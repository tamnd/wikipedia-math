# Weakly dependent random variables

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weakly dependent random variables"}

In probability, **weak dependence of random variables** is a generalization of [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_random_variables) that is weaker than the concept of a [martingale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martingale_%28probability%29). A (time) [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) is weakly dependent if distinct portions of the sequence have a [covariance](wikipedia://en/157059) that [asymptotically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotically) decreases to 0 as the blocks are further separated in time. Weak dependence primarily appears as a technical condition in various [probabilistic limit theorems](wikipedia://en/39406).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Fix a set $S$, a sequence of sets of [measurable functions](wikipedia://en/44055) $\{\mathcal{F}_d\}_{d=1}^{\infty}\in\prod_{d=1}^{\infty}{\left(S^d\to\mathbb{R}\right)}$, a decreasing sequence $\{\theta_{\delta}\}_{\delta=1}^{\infty}\to0$, and a function $\psi\in\mathcal{F}^2\times(\mathbb{Z}^+)^2\to\mathbb{R}^+$. A sequence $\{X_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ of random variables is $(\{\mathcal{F}_d\}_{d=1}^{\infty},\{\theta_{\delta}\}_{\delta},\psi)$-weakly dependent iff, for all $j_1\leq j_2\leq\dots\leq j_d<j_d+\delta\leq k_1\leq k_2\leq\dots\leq k_e$, for all $\phi\in\mathcal{F}_d$, and $\theta\in\mathcal{F}_e$, we have[^o4]

$$|\operatorname{Cov}{(\phi(X_{j_1},\dots,X_{j_d}), \theta(X_{k_1},\dots,X_{k_e}))}|\leq\psi(\phi,\theta,d,e)\theta_{\delta}$$

Note that the covariance does *not* decay to $0$ uniformly in $d$ and $e$.[^o5]

:::

## Common applications {#Common_applications}

Weak dependence is a sufficient weak condition that many natural instances of stochastic processes exhibit it.[^o5] In particular, weak dependence is a natural condition for the ergodic theory of random functions.[^o7]

A sufficient substitute for independence in the [Lindeberg–Lévy central limit theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindeberg–Lévy_central_limit_theorem) is weak dependence.[^o4] For this reason, specializations often appear in the probability literature on limit theorems.[^o5] These include Withers' condition for strong mixing,[^o4][^o8] Tran's "absolute regularity in the locally transitive sense,"[^o9] and Birkel's "asymptotic quadrant independence."[^o10]

Weak dependence also functions as a substitute for [strong mixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_mixing).[^o11] Again, generalizations of the latter are specializations of the former; an example is [Rosenblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Rosenblatt)'s mixing condition.[^o12]

Other uses include a generalization of the [Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequality](wikipedia://en/22583255) and [Rosenthal inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosenthal_inequalities).[^o4]

[Martingales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martingale_%28probability%29) are weakly dependent , so many results about martingales also hold true for weakly dependent sequences. An example is [Bernstein's bound on higher moments](wikipedia://en/9075610), which can be relaxed to only require[^o13][^o14]

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{E} \left [ X_i \mid X_1, \dots, X_{i-1} \right ] &= 0, \\ \operatorname{E} \left [ X_i^2 \mid X_1, \dots, X_{i-1} \right ] &\leq R_i \operatorname{E} \left [ X_i^2 \right ], \\ \operatorname{E} \left [ X_i^k \mid X_1, \dots, X_{i-1} \right ] &\leq \tfrac{1}{2} \operatorname{E} \left[ X_i^2 \mid X_1, \dots, X_{i-1} \right ] L^{k-2} k! \end{aligned}$$
