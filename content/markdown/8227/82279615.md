# Chien-Pai Han

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Chien-Pai Han"}

**Chien-Pai Han** (December 17, 1936 - September 1, 2020) was an American statistician known for his contributions to statistical inference, multivariate analysis, discriminant analysis, and sample survey methodology.[^o3] Han held faculty positions at [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University) (1967-1982) and the [University of Texas at Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Arlington) (1982-2017).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Han was born in Hunan, China on December 17, 1936 and grew up in Taiwan.[^o3] He earned his Ph.D. in Statistics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1967,[^o5] where he studied under the supervision of [Arthur P. Dempster](wikipedia://en/7040761).[^o5] He completed his Ph.D. in Statistics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1967,[^o5] where his doctoral advisor was [Arthur P. Dempster](wikipedia://en/7040761).[^o5] His dissertation was titled *Testing the Homogeneity of a Set of Correlated Variances*.[^o5]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Han began his academic career at [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University) in 1967 as an associate professor in the Department of Statistics,[^o7] where he contributed to research and teaching in mathematical statistics.[^o3] During his tenure at Iowa State, he collaborated extensively with [T. A. Bancroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._A._Bancroft).[^o14] He remained at Iowa State University for 15 years.[^o3]

Han joined the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Texas at Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Arlington) (UTA) in 1982,[^o3] where he became a central figure in developing the statistics program.[^o3] Over the course of his 35-year tenure at UTA, he supervised 20 Ph.D. students and 36 M.S. students,[^o3] and he was known for his mentorship of junior faculty and graduate students.[^o3] Han retired in 2017 and was named Professor Emeritus.[^o3][^o8]

## Research contributions {#Research_contributions}

Han's research contributions spanned multiple areas of statistical theory and methodology. His early work focused on discriminant analysis, particularly the distribution of discriminant functions when covariance matrices have special structures.[^o10] His work in this area provided theoretical foundations for classification problems in multivariate analysis.

Han made contributions to survey sampling theory, including work on double sampling with partial information on auxiliary variables[^o7] and the efficiency of ratio estimators under different sampling schemes.[^o18] His research also addressed shrinkage estimation,[^o19] analysis of variance for incomplete models,[^o11] estimation in random effects models,[^o20] and central limit theorems for nonlinear statistics in repeated sampling from finite populations.[^o16]

Later in his career, Han worked on quality control and process capability indices,[^o3] computational statistics,[^o21] and applications of statistical methods to practical problems

Han was elected a Fellow of the American Statistical Association in 1998.[^o12]

## Death {#Death}

Han died on September 1, 2020, following a long illness.[^o3][^o8] He was 83 years old.[^o24]
