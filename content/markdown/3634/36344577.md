# Terrence L. Fine

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Terrence L. Fine"}

**Terrence L. Fine** (March 9, 1939 – January 31, 2021)[^o2] was an American scientist, engineer and philosopher. He is known[^o3][^o4][^o5] especially for his contributions to the defense and development of alternatives to the classical calculus for [probabilistic](wikipedia://en/22934) modeling and decision-making. Other contributions include [Fine's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine's_theorem),[^o6] the [Fine numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_number)[^o7] and the [Fine–McMillan quantizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine–McMillan_quantizer).[^o8] He was the recipient of the first patent awarded in the area of statistical [delta modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_modulation).[^o9]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Fine was born in New York City on March 9, 1939. His academic degrees were from the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York) ([B.E.E.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Electrical_Engineering)) and from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) (S.M., Ph.D.). He was awarded a Miller Research Fellowship at the [University of California Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_Berkeley) for the period 1964 to 1966, after which he joined the faculty of [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in Ithaca, New York, where he remained until his retirement in 2010. There he served as Professor in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering; concurrently, as Professor in the Department of Statistical Sciences; and from 1999 to 2004, as Director of the university's multidisciplinary Center for Applied Mathematics. He was a Professor Emeritus at Cornell. He was a Life Fellow and Third Millennium Medalist of the IEEE ([Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers)).[^o11]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- *Theories of Probability: An Examination of Foundations*, Academic Press, 1973. A study of mathematical and interpretive alternatives to the standard framework for mathematical probability.
- *Feedforward Neural Network Methodology*, Series on Statistics for Engineering and Information Science, Springer-Verlag, 1999.
- *Probability and Probabilistic Reasoning for Electrical Engineering*, Pearson/Prentice-Hall, 2006.
- "An argument for comparative probability", in R. Butts, [J. Hintikka](wikipedia://en/1710903), eds., *Basic Problems in Methodology and Linguistics III*, Univ. Western Ontario Ser. Philosophy of Science, **11**, D. Reidel, Dordrecht, 105–119, 1977.
- "On the apparent convergence of relative frequency and its implications", *[IEEE Transactions on Information Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Transactions_on_Information_Theory)*, **IT-16**, 251–257, 1970. Source of Fine's Theorem.
- "Extrapolation when very little is known about the source", *[Information and Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_Control)*, **16**, 331–359, 1970. A non-statistical approach to extrapolation, and source of the Fine numbers.
