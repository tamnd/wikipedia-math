# K-convexity in Rn

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="K-convexity in Rn"}

**K-convexity in Rn** is a mathematical concept.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formula"}

## Formula {#Formula}

Let *$\mathrm{K}$*= (*K<sub>0</sub>*,*K<sub>1</sub>*,...,*K<sub>n</sub>*) to be a vector of (n+1) nonnegative constants and define a function $\mathrm{K}$(<sup>.</sup>): $\Re_+^n$ → $\Re_+^1$ as follows:

$\mathrm{K}$($x$) = *K<sub>0</sub>*$\delta$(*e*$x$) + $\sum_{i=1}^n$*K<sub>i</sub>*$\delta$($x_i$),

where *e* = (1,1,...,1) ∈ $\Re^n$, $\Re_+^n = \{x \in \Re^n | x \geq 0 \}$, $\delta$(0) = 0 and $\delta(z)$= 1 for all $z$ > 0.[^o4]

The concept of **K**-convexity generalizes *K*[\-convexity](wikipedia://en/47070537) introduced by [Scarf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Scarf) (1960)[^o5] to higher dimensional spaces and is useful in multiproduct inventory problems with fixed setup costs. Scarf used *K*[\-convexity](wikipedia://en/47070537) to prove the optimality of the (s, S) policy in the single product case. Several papers are devoted to obtaining optimal policies for multiple product problems with fixed ordering costs.[^o4][^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13]

This definition introduced by Gallego and [Sethi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suresh_P._Sethi) (2005)[^o4] is motivated by the joint replenishment problem when we incur a setup cost *K<sub>0</sub>*, whenever we order an item or items and an individual setup cost *K<sub>i</sub>* for each item $i$ we order. There are some important special cases:

(i) The simplest is the case of one product or *n* = 1, where *K<sub>0</sub> + K<sub>1</sub>* can be considered to be the setup cost.

(ii) The joint setup cost arises when *K<sub>i</sub>* = 0*, $i$* = 1*,* 2*, . . . , n,* and a setup cost of $\mathrm{K}_0$ is incurred whenever any one or more of the items are ordered. In this case, *$\mathrm{K}$* = *(K<sub>0</sub>,* 0*,* 0*, . . . ,* 0*)* and $\mathrm{K}$($x$) = *K<sub>0</sub>$\delta$*(*e*$x$).

(iii) When there is no joint setup cost, i.e., *K<sub>0</sub>* = 0, and there are only individual setups, we have *$\mathrm{K}$($x$)* = $\sum_{i=1}^n$*K<sub>i</sub>*$\delta$($x_i$)*.*

:::
