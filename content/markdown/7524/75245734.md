# Shai Haran

Academics

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Shai Haran"}

**Shai Haran** (שי הרן; born 1958) is an Israeli mathematician and professor at the [Technion – Israel Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology).[^o3] He is known for his work in [p-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327), [p-adic quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_quantum_mechanics), and [non-additive geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-additive_geometry), including the [field with one element](wikipedia://en/13798040), in relation to strategies for proving the [Riemann Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_Hypothesis).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in Jerusalem on October 8, 1958, Haran graduated from the [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University) in 1979, and, in 1983, received his PhD in mathematics from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT) on "p-Adic L-functions for Elliptic Curves over CM Fields"[^o5] under his advisor [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), and his mentors [Michael Artin](wikipedia://en/1519821) and [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519) from MIT.

Haran is a professor at the [Technion – Israel Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology). He was a frequent visitor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), MIT, Harvard and [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University),[^o6] the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques), [Max-Planck Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck_Institute), [Kyushu University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu_University)[^o7] and the [Tokyo Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Institute_of_Technology), among other institutions.

## Work {#Work}

His early work was in the construction of [p-adic L-functions](wikipedia://en/22059815) for [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) on GL(2) over any number field.[^o9] He gave a formula for the explicit sums of arithmetic functions expressing in a uniform way the contribution of a prime, finite or real, as the derivative at $\alpha=0$ of the [Riesz potential](wikipedia://en/6779393) of order $\alpha$.[^o10] This formula is one of the inspirations[^o11][^o12] for the non-commutative geometry approach to the Riemann Hypothesis of [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340). He then developed potential theory[^o13] and quantum mechanics over the p-adic numbers,[^o14] and is currently an editor of the journal "*p-Adic Numbers, Ultrametric Analysis and Applications"* [^o15]*.*

Haran also studied the tree structure of the [p-adic integers](wikipedia://en/51423) within the real and complex numbers and showed that it is given by the theory of classic [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718).[^o16] He constructed [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876) over the p-adic, real, and complex numbers, giving finite approximations to the harmonic beta measure. In particular, he showed that there is a [q-analogue](wikipedia://en/1619396) theory that interpolates between the p-adic theory and the real and complex theory. With his students Uri Onn and Uri Badder, he developed the higher rank theory for GL(n).[^o17]

His recent work is focused on the development of mathematical foundations for non-additive geometry, a geometric theory that is not based on commutative rings.[^o18] In this theory, the field with one element $\mathbb{F}$ is defined as the category of finite sets with partial bijections, or equivalently, of finite [pointed sets](wikipedia://en/1242444) with maps that preserve the distinguished points. The non-additive geometry is then developed using two languages, $\mathbb{F}-\mathcal{R}\text{ings}$ and "generalized rings", to replace commutative rings in usual algebraic geometry. In this theory, it is possible to consider the [compactification](wikipedia://en/6617) of the spectrum of $\mathbb{Z}$ and a model for the arithmetic plane that does not reduce to the diagonal $\mathbb{Z}$.[^o19]
