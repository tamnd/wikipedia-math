# Willmore conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Willmore conjecture"}

::: {#o2 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Willmore conjecture"}

**Willmore conjecture.**

:::

*Image: Willmore conjecture - perfect doughnut.png.* The torus with minimal Willmore energy, with major radius $\sqrt{2}$ and minor radius 1[^o9]

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **Willmore conjecture** is a [lower bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_bound) on the [Willmore energy](wikipedia://en/2072431) of a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800). It is named after the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Tom Willmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Willmore), who [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) it in 1965.[^o3] A proof by [Fernando Codá Marques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Codá_Marques) and [André Neves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Neves) was announced in 2012 and published in 2014.[^o9][^o8]

:::

## Background {#Background}

Let *v* : *M* → **R**<sup>3</sup> be a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) [immersion](wikipedia://en/6993953) of a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [orientable surface](wikipedia://en/187446). Giving *M* the [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) induced by *v*, let *H* : *M* → **R** be the [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210) (the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) of the [principal curvatures](wikipedia://en/2072472) *κ*<sub>1</sub> and *κ*<sub>2</sub> at each point) and let *K* be the [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623). In this notation, the *Willmore energy* *W*(*M*) of *M* is given by

$$W(M) = \int_M H^2 \, dA - \int_M K \, dA.$$

It is not hard to prove that the Willmore energy satisfies *W*(*M*) ≥ 0, with equality [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *M* is an embedded round [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859).

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Calculation of *W*(*M*) for a few examples suggests that there should be a better bound than *W*(*M*) ≥ 4*π* for surfaces with [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_%28topology%29) *g*(*M*) > 0. In particular, calculation of *W*(*M*) for tori with various symmetries led Willmore to propose in 1965 the following conjecture, which now bears his name

For every smooth immersed torus *M* in **R**<sup>3</sup>, *W*(*M*) ≥ 2*π*<sup>2</sup>.

In 1982, [Peter Wai-Kwong Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Wai-Kwong_Li) and [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137) proved the conjecture in the non-embedded case, showing that if $f:\Sigma\to S^3$ is an immersion of a compact surface, which is *not* an embedding, then *W*(*M*) is at least 8*π*.[^o7]

In 2012, [Fernando Codá Marques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Codá_Marques) and [André Neves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Neves) proved the conjecture in the embedded case, using the [Almgren–Pitts min-max theory of minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/46587298).[^o8][^o9] Prior to the proof of Marques and Neves, the Willmore conjecture had already been proved for many special cases, such as [tube tori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tube_torus) (by Willmore himself), and for [tori](wikipedia://en/74800) of [revolution](wikipedia://en/329549) (by Langer & Singer).[^o10]
