# Localization of a topological space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Localization of a topological space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), well-behaved [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) can be **localized** at primes, in a similar way to the [localization of a ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) at a prime. This construction was described by [Dennis Sullivan](wikipedia://en/7349608) in 1970 lecture notes that were finally published in (Sullivan 2005).

:::

The reason to do this was in line with an idea of making [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), more precisely [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), more geometric. Localization of a space *X* is a geometric form of the algebraic device of choosing 'coefficients' in order to simplify the algebra, in a given problem. Instead of that, the localization can be applied to the space *X*, directly, giving a second space *Y*.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

We let *A* be a [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), and let *X* be a [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022). Then there is a simply connected CW complex *Y* together with a map from *X* to *Y* such that

- *Y* is *A*-local; this means that all its [homology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) are modules over *A*
- The map from *X* to *Y* is universal for (homotopy classes of) maps from *X* to *A*-local CW complexes.

This space *Y* is unique up to [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence), and is called the **localization** of *X* at *A*.

If *A* is the localization of **Z** at a prime *p*, then the space *Y* is called the **localization** of *X* at *p*.

The map from *X* to *Y* induces [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828) from the *A*-localizations of the homology and homotopy groups of *X* to the homology and homotopy groups of *Y*.
