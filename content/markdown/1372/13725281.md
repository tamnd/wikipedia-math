# Differential inclusion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differential inclusion"}

In mathematics, **differential inclusions** are a generalization of the concept of [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753) of the form

:::

$$\frac{dx}{dt}(t)\in F(t,x(t)),$$

where *F* is a [multivalued map](wikipedia://en/222367), i.e. *F*(*t*, *x*) is a *set* rather than a single point in $\mathbb{R}^d$. Differential inclusions arise in many situations including [differential variational inequalities](wikipedia://en/5353216), [projected dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/5779282), Moreau's sweeping process, linear and nonlinear complementarity dynamical systems, discontinuous ordinary differential equations, switching dynamical systems, and [fuzzy set](wikipedia://en/56601) arithmetic.[^o2]

For example, the basic rule for Coulomb friction is that the friction force has magnitude *μN* in the direction opposite to the direction of slip, where *N* is the normal force and *μ* is a constant (the friction coefficient). However, if the slip is zero, the friction force can be *any* force in the correct plane with magnitude smaller than or equal to *μN*. Thus, writing the friction force as a function of position and velocity leads to a [set-valued function](wikipedia://en/12506030).

In differential inclusion, we not only take a set-valued map at the right hand side but also we can take a subset of a Euclidean space $\mathbb R^N$ for some $N\in \mathbb N$ as following way. Let $n\in \mathbb N$ and $E\subset \mathbb R^{n\times n}\setminus \{0\}.$ Our main purpose is to find a $W^{1,\infty}_{0}(\Omega, \mathbb R^n)$ function $u$ satisfying the differential inclusion $Du \in E$ a.e. in $\Omega,$ where $\Omega\subset \mathbb R^n$ is an open bounded set.

## Theory {#Theory}

Existence theory usually assumes that *F*(*t*, *x*) is an [upper hemicontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemicontinuous) function of *x*, measurable in *t*, and that *F*(*t*, *x*) is a closed, convex set for all *t* and *x*. Existence of solutions for the initial value problem

$$\frac{dx}{dt}(t)\in F(t,x(t)), \quad x(t_0)=x_0$$

for a sufficiently small time interval \[*t*<sub>0</sub>, *t*<sub>0</sub> + *ε*), *ε* > 0 then follows. Global existence can be shown provided *F* does not allow "blow-up" ($\scriptstyle \Vert x(t)\Vert\,\to\,\infty$ as $\scriptstyle t\,\to\, t^*$ for a finite $\scriptstyle t^*$).

Existence theory for differential inclusions with non-convex *F*(*t*, *x*) is an active area of research.

Uniqueness of solutions usually requires other conditions. For example, suppose $F(t,x)$ satisfies a [one-sided Lipschitz condition](wikipedia://en/51731#One-sided_Lipschitz):

$$(x_1-x_2)^T(F(t,x_1)-F(t,x_2))\leq C\Vert x_1-x_2\Vert^2$$

for some *C* for all *x*<sub>1</sub> and *x*<sub>2</sub>. Then the initial value problem

$$\frac{dx}{dt}(t)\in F(t,x(t)), \quad x(t_0)=x_0$$

has a unique solution.

This is closely related to the theory of **maximal monotone operators**, as developed by Minty and [Haïm Brezis](wikipedia://en/18329570).

[Filippov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksei_Fedorovich_Filippov)'s theory only allows for discontinuities in the derivative $\frac{dx}{dt}(t)$, but allows no discontinuities in the state, i.e. $x(t)$ need be continuous. [Schatzman](wikipedia://en/54187001) and later [Moreau](wikipedia://en/34674927) (who gave it the currently accepted name) extended the notion to *measure differential inclusion* (MDI) in which the inclusion is evaluated by taking the [limit from above](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_from_above) for $x(t)$.[^o4][^o5]

## Applications {#Applications}

Differential inclusions can be used to understand and suitably interpret discontinuous ordinary differential equations, such as arise for [Coulomb friction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulomb_friction) in mechanical systems and ideal switches in power electronics. An important contribution has been made by [A. F. Filippov](wikipedia://en/37660194), who studied regularizations of discontinuous equations. Further, the technique of regularization was used by [N.N. Krasovskii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Nikolaevich_Krasovsky) in the theory of [differential games](wikipedia://en/17507367).

Differential inclusions are also found at the foundation of [non-smooth dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-smooth_dynamical_systems) (NSDS) analysis,[^o8] which is used in the *analog* study of switching electrical circuits using idealized component equations (for example using idealized, straight vertical lines for the sharply exponential forward and breakdown conduction regions of a diode characteristic)[^o9] and in the study of certain [non-smooth mechanical system](wikipedia://en/15947157) such as [stick-slip oscillations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stick-slip_phenomenon) in systems with [dry friction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_friction) or the dynamics of [impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_%28mechanics%29) phenomena.[^o10] Software that solves NSDS systems exists, such as [INRIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INRIA)'s [Siconos](wikipedia://en/34624069).

In continuous function when [Fuzzy concept](wikipedia://en/1973470) is used in differential inclusion a new concept comes as [Fuzzy differential inclusion](wikipedia://en/72003352) which has application in [Atmospheric dispersion modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_dispersion_modeling) and [Cybernetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybernetics) in [Medical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging).
