# Exterior calculus identities

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exterior calculus identities"}

This article summarizes several [identities](wikipedia://en/298428) in [exterior calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_calculus), a mathematical [calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_%28disambiguation%29) used in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

The following notation is used in this article.

### Manifold {#Manifold}

$M$, $N$ are $n$-dimensional smooth manifolds, where $n\in \mathbb{N}$. That is, [differentiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219) that can be differentiated enough times for the purposes on this page.

$p \in M$, $q \in N$ denote one point on each of the manifolds.

The boundary of a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) $M$ is a manifold $\partial M$, which has dimension $n - 1$. An orientation on $M$ induces an orientation on $\partial M$.

We usually denote a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) by $\Sigma \subset M$.

### Tangent and cotangent bundles {#Tangent_and_cotangent_bundles}

$TM$, $T^{*}M$ denote the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) and [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600), respectively, of the smooth manifold $M$.

$T_p M$, $T_q N$ denote the [tangent spaces](wikipedia://en/30807) of $M$, $N$ at the points $p$, $q$, respectively. $T^{*}_p M$ denotes the [cotangent space](wikipedia://en/6620) of $M$ at the point $p$.

[Sections](wikipedia://en/367621) of the tangent bundles, also known as [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641), are typically denoted as $X, Y, Z \in \Gamma(TM)$ such that at a point $p \in M$ we have $X|_p, Y|_p, Z|_p \in T_p M$. Sections of the cotangent bundle, also known as [differential 1-forms](wikipedia://en/221519) (or [covector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covector) fields), are typically denoted as $\alpha, \beta \in \Gamma(T^{*}M)$ such that at a point $p \in M$ we have $\alpha|_p, \beta|_p \in T^{*}_p M$. An alternative notation for $\Gamma(T^{*}M)$ is $\Omega^1(M)$.

### Differential *k*-forms {#Differential_k-forms}

Differential $k$-forms, which we refer to simply as $k$-forms here, are [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) defined on $TM$. We denote the set of all $k$-forms as $\Omega^k(M)$. For $0\leq k,\ l,\ m\leq n$ we usually write $\alpha\in\Omega^k(M)$, $\beta\in\Omega^l(M)$, $\gamma\in\Omega^m(M)$.

$0$-forms $f\in\Omega^0(M)$ are just scalar functions $C^{\infty}(M)$ on $M$. $\mathbf{1}\in\Omega^0(M)$ denotes the constant $0$-form equal to $1$ everywhere.

### Omitted elements of a sequence {#Omitted_elements_of_a_sequence}

When we are given $(k+1)$ inputs $X_0,\ldots,X_k$ and a $k$-form $\alpha\in\Omega^k(M)$ we denote omission of the $i$th entry by writing

$$\alpha(X_0,\ldots,\hat{X}_i,\ldots,X_k):=\alpha(X_0,\ldots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\ldots,X_k) .$$

### Exterior product {#Exterior_product}

The [exterior product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_product) is also known as the *wedge product*. It is denoted by $\wedge : \Omega^k(M) \times \Omega^l(M) \rightarrow \Omega^{k+l}(M)$. The exterior product of a $k$-form $\alpha\in\Omega^k(M)$ and an $l$-form $\beta\in\Omega^l(M)$ produce a $(k+l)$-form $\alpha\wedge\beta \in\Omega^{k+l}(M)$. It can be written using the set $S(k,k+l)$ of all permutations $\sigma$ of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ such that $\sigma(1)<\ldots <\sigma(k), \ \sigma(k+1)<\ldots <\sigma(k+l)$ as

$$(\alpha\wedge\beta)(X_1,\ldots,X_{k+l})=\sum_{\sigma\in S(k,k+l)}\text{sign}(\sigma)\alpha(X_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,X_{\sigma(k)})\otimes\beta(X_{\sigma(k+1)},\ldots,X_{\sigma(k+l)}) .$$

### Directional derivative {#Directional_derivative}

The [directional derivative](wikipedia://en/487859) of a 0-form $f\in\Omega^0(M)$ along a section $X\in\Gamma(TM)$ is a 0-form denoted $\partial_X f .$

### Exterior derivative {#Exterior_derivative}

The [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) $d_k : \Omega^k(M) \rightarrow \Omega^{k+1}(M)$ is defined for all $0 \leq k\leq n$. We generally omit the subscript when it is clear from the context.

For a $0$-form $f\in\Omega^0(M)$ we have $d_0f\in\Omega^1(M)$ as the $1$-form that gives the directional derivative, i.e., for the section $X\in \Gamma(TM)$ we have $(d_0f)(X) = \partial_X f$, the [directional derivative](wikipedia://en/487859) of $f$ along $X$.[^o16]

For $0 < k\leq n$,[^o16]

$$(d_k\omega)(X_0,\ldots,X_k)=\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}(-1)^jd_{0}(\omega(X_0,\ldots,\hat{X}_j,\ldots,X_k))(X_j) + \sum_{0\leq i < j\leq k}(-1)^{i+j}\omega([X_i,X_j],X_0,\ldots,\hat{X}_i,\ldots,\hat{X}_j,\ldots,X_k) .$$

### Lie bracket {#Lie_bracket}

The [Lie bracket](wikipedia://en/10282799) of sections $X,Y \in \Gamma(TM)$ is defined as the unique section $[X,Y] \in \Gamma(TM)$ that satisfies

$$\forall f\in\Omega^0(M) \Rightarrow \partial_{[X,Y]}f = \partial_X \partial_Y f - \partial_Y \partial_X f .$$

### Tangent maps {#Tangent_maps}

If $\phi : M \rightarrow N$ is a [smooth map](wikipedia://en/1408000), then $d\phi|_p:T_pM\rightarrow T_{\phi(p)}N$ defines a tangent map from $M$ to $N$. It is defined through curves $\gamma$ on $M$ with derivative $\gamma'(0)=X\in T_pM$ such that

$$d\phi(X):=(\phi\circ\gamma)' .$$

Note that $\phi$ is a $0$-form with values in $N$.

### Pull-back {#Pull-back}

If $\phi : M \rightarrow N$ is a smooth map, then the [pull-back](wikipedia://en/494995) of a $k$-form $\alpha\in \Omega^k(N)$ is defined such that for any $k$-dimensional submanifold $\Sigma\subset M$

$$\int_{\Sigma} \phi^*\alpha = \int_{\phi(\Sigma)} \alpha .$$

The pull-back can also be expressed as

$$(\phi^*\alpha)(X_1,\ldots,X_k)=\alpha(d\phi(X_1),\ldots,d\phi(X_k)) .$$

### Interior product {#Interior_product}

Also known as the interior derivative, the [interior product](wikipedia://en/2154963) given a section $Y\in \Gamma(TM)$ is a map $\iota_Y:\Omega^{k+1}(M) \rightarrow \Omega^k(M)$ that effectively substitutes the first input of a $(k+1)$-form with $Y$. If $\alpha\in\Omega^{k+1}(M)$ and $X_i\in \Gamma(TM)$ then

$$(\iota_Y\alpha)(X_1,\ldots,X_k) = \alpha(Y,X_1,\ldots,X_k) .$$

### Metric tensor {#Metric_tensor}

Given a [nondegenerate bilinear form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate_bilinear_form) $g_p( \cdot , \cdot )$ on each $T_p M$ that is continuous on $M$, the manifold becomes a [pseudo-Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/287555). We denote the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) $g$, defined pointwise by $g( X , Y )|_p = g_p( X|_p , Y|_p )$. We call $s=\operatorname{sign}(g)$ the [signature](wikipedia://en/310914#Duality) of the metric. A [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) has $s=1$, whereas [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space) has $s=-1$.

### Musical isomorphisms {#Musical_isomorphisms}

The [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) $g(\cdot,\cdot)$ induces duality mappings between vector fields and one-forms: these are the [musical isomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_isomorphisms) flat $\flat$ and sharp $\sharp$. A section $A \in \Gamma(TM)$ corresponds to the unique one-form $A^{\flat}\in\Omega^1(M)$ such that for all sections $X \in \Gamma(TM)$, we have:

$$A^{\flat}(X) = g(A,X) .$$

A one-form $\alpha\in\Omega^1(M)$ corresponds to the unique vector field $\alpha^{\sharp}\in \Gamma(TM)$ such that for all $X \in \Gamma(TM)$, we have:

$$\alpha(X) = g(\alpha^\sharp,X) .$$

These mappings extend via multilinearity to mappings from $k$-vector fields to $k$-forms and $k$-forms to $k$-vector fields through

$$\begin{aligned} (A_1 \wedge A_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge A_k)^{\flat} &= A_1^{\flat} \wedge A_2^{\flat} \wedge \cdots \wedge A_k^{\flat} \\ (\alpha_1 \wedge \alpha_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha_k)^{\sharp} &= \alpha_1^{\sharp} \wedge \alpha_2^{\sharp} \wedge \cdots \wedge \alpha_k^{\sharp}. \end{aligned}$$

### Hodge star {#Hodge_star}

For an *n*-manifold *M*, the [Hodge star operator](wikipedia://en/310914) ${\star}:\Omega^k(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{n-k}(M)$ is a duality mapping taking a $k$-form $\alpha \in \Omega^k(M)$ to an $(n{-}k)$-form $({\star}\alpha) \in \Omega^{n-k}(M)$.

It can be defined in terms of an oriented frame $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ for $TM$, orthonormal with respect to the given metric tensor $g$:

$$({\star}\alpha)(X_1,\ldots,X_{n-k})=\alpha(X_{n-k+1},\ldots,X_n) .$$

### Co-differential operator {#Co-differential_operator}

The [co-differential operator](wikipedia://en/310914#Codifferential) $\delta:\Omega^k(M)\rightarrow\Omega^{k-1}(M)$ on an $n$ dimensional manifold $M$ is defined by

$$\delta := (-1)^{k} {\star}^{-1} d {\star} = (-1)^{nk+n+1}{\star} d {\star} .$$

The [Hodge–Dirac operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge–Dirac_operator), $d+\delta$, is a [Dirac operator](wikipedia://en/1079448) studied in [Clifford analysis](wikipedia://en/22424754).

### Oriented manifold {#Oriented_manifold}

An $n$-dimensional [orientable manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable_manifold) $M$ is a manifold that can be equipped with a choice of an $n$-form $\mu\in\Omega^n(M)$ that is continuous and nonzero everywhere on $M$.

### Volume form {#Volume_form}

On an orientable manifold $M$ the canonical choice of a [volume form](wikipedia://en/1855811) given a metric tensor $g$ and an [orientation](wikipedia://en/1391942#Multilinear_algebra) is $\mathbf{det}:=\sqrt{|\det g|}\;dX_1^{\flat}\wedge\ldots\wedge dX_n^{\flat}$ for any basis $dX_1,\ldots, dX_n$ ordered to match the orientation.

### Area form {#Area_form}

Given a volume form $\mathbf{det}$ and a unit normal vector $N$ we can also define an area form $\sigma:=\iota_N\textbf{det}$ on the boundary $\partial M.$

### Bilinear form on *k*-forms {#Bilinear_form_on_k-forms}

A generalization of the metric tensor, the [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375) between two $k$-forms $\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^k(M)$, is defined [pointwise](wikipedia://en/4786797) on $M$ by

$$\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle|_p := {\star}(\alpha\wedge {\star}\beta )|_p .$$

The $L^2$-bilinear form for the space of $k$-forms $\Omega^k(M)$ is defined by

$$\langle\!\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\!\rangle:= \int_M\alpha\wedge {\star}\beta .$$

In the case of a Riemannian manifold, each is an [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) (i.e. is positive-definite).

### Lie derivative {#Lie_derivative}

We define the [Lie derivative](wikipedia://en/294437) $\mathcal{L}:\Omega^k(M)\rightarrow\Omega^k(M)$ through [Cartan's magic formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan's_magic_formula) for a given section $X\in \Gamma(TM)$ as

$$\mathcal{L}_X = d \circ \iota_X + \iota_X \circ d .$$

It describes the change of a $k$-form along a [flow](wikipedia://en/2619735) $\phi_t$ associated to the section $X$.

### Laplace–Beltrami operator {#Laplace–Beltrami_operator}

The [Laplacian](wikipedia://en/174706) $\Delta:\Omega^k(M) \rightarrow \Omega^k(M)$ is defined as $\Delta = -(d\delta + \delta d)$.

## Important definitions {#Important_definitions}

### Definitions on Ω<sup>*k*</sup>(*M*) {#Definitions_on_Ωk(M)}

$\alpha\in\Omega^k(M)$ is called...

- *closed* if $d\alpha=0$
- *exact* if $\alpha = d\beta$ for some $\beta\in\Omega^{k-1}$
- *coclosed* if $\delta\alpha=0$
- *coexact* if $\alpha = \delta\beta$ for some $\beta\in\Omega^{k+1}$
- *harmonic* if *closed* and *coclosed*

### Cohomology {#Cohomology}

The $k$-th [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of a manifold $M$ and its exterior derivative operators $d_0,\ldots,d_{n-1}$ is given by

$$H^k(M):=\frac{\text{ker}(d_{k})}{\text{im}(d_{k-1})}$$

Two closed $k$-forms $\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^k(M)$ are in the same cohomology class if their difference is an exact form i.e.

$$[\alpha]=[\beta] \ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \alpha{-}\beta = d\eta \ \text{ for some } \eta\in\Omega^{k-1}(M)$$

A closed surface of genus $g$ will have $2g$ generators which are harmonic.

### Dirichlet energy {#Dirichlet_energy}

Given $\alpha\in\Omega^k(M)$, its [Dirichlet energy](wikipedia://en/14377820) is

$$\mathcal{E}_\text{D}(\alpha):= \dfrac{1}{2}\langle\!\langle d\alpha,d\alpha\rangle\!\rangle + \dfrac{1}{2}\langle\!\langle \delta\alpha,\delta\alpha\rangle\!\rangle$$

## Properties {#Properties}

### Exterior derivative properties {#Exterior_derivative_properties}

- $\int_{\Sigma} d\alpha = \int_{\partial\Sigma} \alpha$ ( *[Stokes' theorem](wikipedia://en/25441497)* )
- $d \circ d = 0$ ( *[cochain complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochain_complex)* )
- $d(\alpha \wedge \beta ) = d\alpha\wedge \beta +(-1)^k\alpha\wedge d\beta$ for $\alpha\in\Omega^k(M), \ \beta\in\Omega^l(M)$ ( *Leibniz rule* )
- $df(X) = \partial_X f$ for $f\in\Omega^0(M), \ X\in \Gamma(TM)$ ( *[directional derivative](wikipedia://en/487859)* )
- $d\alpha = 0$ for $\alpha \in \Omega^n(M), \ \text{dim}(M)=n$

### Exterior product properties {#Exterior_product_properties}

- $\alpha \wedge \beta = (-1)^{kl}\beta \wedge \alpha$ for $\alpha\in\Omega^k(M), \ \beta\in\Omega^l(M)$ ( *[alternating](wikipedia://en/2497875)* )
- $(\alpha \wedge \beta)\wedge\gamma = \alpha \wedge (\beta\wedge\gamma)$ ( *associativity* )
- $(\lambda\alpha) \wedge \beta = \lambda (\alpha \wedge \beta)$ for $\lambda\in\mathbb{R}$ ( *compatibility of scalar multiplication* )
- $\alpha \wedge ( \beta_1 + \beta_2 ) = \alpha \wedge \beta_1 + \alpha \wedge \beta_2$ ( *distributivity over addition* )
- $\alpha \wedge \alpha = 0$ for $\alpha\in\Omega^k(M)$ when $k$ is odd or $\operatorname{rank} \alpha \le 1$. The [rank of a $k$-form](wikipedia://en/221537#Rank_of_a_k-vector) $\alpha$ means the minimum number of monomial terms (exterior products of one-forms) that must be summed to produce $\alpha$.

### Pull-back properties {#Pull-back_properties}

- $d(\phi^*\alpha) = \phi^*(d\alpha)$ ( *commutative with $d$* )
- $\phi^*(\alpha\wedge\beta) = (\phi^*\alpha)\wedge(\phi^*\beta)$ ( *distributes over $\wedge$* )
- $(\phi_1\circ\phi_2)^* = \phi_2^*\phi_1^*$ ( *contravariant* )
- $\phi^*f=f\circ\phi$ for $f\in\Omega^0(N)$ ( *function composition* )

### Musical isomorphism properties {#Musical_isomorphism_properties}

- $$(X^{\flat})^{\sharp}=X$$
- $$(\alpha^{\sharp})^{\flat}=\alpha$$

### Interior product properties {#Interior_product_properties}

- $\iota_X \circ \iota_X = 0$ ( *nilpotent* )
- $$\iota_X \circ \iota_Y = - \iota_Y \circ \iota_X$$
- $\iota_X (\alpha \wedge \beta ) = (\iota_X\alpha)\wedge\beta + (-1)^k\alpha\wedge(\iota_X \beta )$ for $\alpha\in\Omega^k(M), \ \beta\in\Omega^l(M)$ ( *Leibniz rule* )
- $\iota_X\alpha = \alpha(X)$ for $\alpha\in\Omega^1(M)$
- $\iota_X f = 0$ for $f \in \Omega^0(M)$
- $\iota_X(f\alpha) = f \iota_X\alpha$ for $f \in \Omega^0(M)$

### Hodge star properties {#Hodge_star_properties}

- ${\star}(\lambda_1\alpha + \lambda_2\beta) = \lambda_1({\star}\alpha) + \lambda_2({\star}\beta)$ for $\lambda_1,\lambda_2\in\mathbb{R}$ ( *linearity* )
- ${\star}{\star}\alpha = s(-1)^{k(n-k)}\alpha$ for $\alpha\in \Omega^k(M)$, $n=\dim(M)$, and $s = \operatorname{sign}(g)$ the sign of the metric
- ${\star}^{(-1)} = s(-1)^{k(n-k)}{\star}$ ( *inversion* )
- ${\star}(f\alpha)=f({\star}\alpha)$ for $f\in\Omega^0(M)$ ( *commutative with $0$-forms* )
- $\langle\!\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle\!\rangle = \langle\!\langle{\star}\alpha,{\star}\alpha\rangle\!\rangle$ for $\alpha\in\Omega^1(M)$ ( *Hodge star preserves $1$-form norm* )
- ${\star} \mathbf{1} = \mathbf{det}$ ( *Hodge dual of constant function 1 is the volume form* )

### Co-differential operator properties {#Co-differential_operator_properties}

- $\delta\circ\delta = 0$ ( *nilpotent* )
- ${\star}\delta=(-1)^kd{\star}$ and ${\star} d = (-1)^{k+1}\delta{\star}$ ( *Hodge adjoint to $d$* )
- $\langle\!\langle d\alpha,\beta\rangle\!\rangle = \langle\!\langle \alpha,\delta\beta\rangle\!\rangle$ if $\partial M=0$ ( *$\delta$ adjoint to $d$* )
- In general, $\int_M d\alpha \wedge \star \beta = \int_{\partial M} \alpha \wedge \star \beta + \int_M \alpha\wedge\star\delta\beta$
- $\delta f = 0$ for $f \in \Omega^0(M)$

### Lie derivative properties {#Lie_derivative_properties}

- $d\circ\mathcal{L}_X = \mathcal{L}_X\circ d$ ( *commutative with $d$* )
- $\iota_X \circ\mathcal{L}_X = \mathcal{L}_X\circ \iota_X$ ( *commutative with $\iota_X$* )
- $$\mathcal{L}_X(\iota_Y\alpha) = \iota_{[X,Y]}\alpha + \iota_Y\mathcal{L}_X\alpha$$
- $\mathcal{L}_X(\alpha\wedge\beta) = (\mathcal{L}_X\alpha)\wedge\beta + \alpha\wedge(\mathcal{L}_X\beta)$ ( *Leibniz rule* )

:::

## Exterior calculus identities {#Exterior_calculus_identities}

- $$\iota_X({\star}\mathbf{1}) = {\star} X^{\flat}$$
- $\iota_X({\star}\alpha) = (-1)^k{\star}(X^{\flat}\wedge\alpha)$ if $\alpha\in\Omega^k(M)$
- $$\iota_X(\phi^*\alpha)=\phi^*(\iota_{d\phi(X)}\alpha)$$
- $$\nu,\mu\in\Omega^n(M), \mu \text{ non-zero } \ \Rightarrow \ \exists \ f\in\Omega^0(M): \ \nu=f\mu$$
- $X^{\flat}\wedge{\star} Y^{\flat} = g(X,Y)( {\star} \mathbf{1})$ ( *bilinear form* )
- $[X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]] = 0$ ( *[Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370)* )

### Dimensions {#Dimensions}

If $n=\dim M$

- $\dim\Omega^k(M) = \binom{n}{k}$ for $0\leq k\leq n$
- $\dim\Omega^k(M) = 0$ for $k < 0, \ k > n$

If $X_1,\ldots,X_n\in \Gamma(TM)$ is a basis, then a basis of $\Omega^k(M)$ is

$$\{X_{\sigma(1)}^{\flat}\wedge\ldots\wedge X_{\sigma(k)}^{\flat} \ : \ \sigma\in S(k,n)\}$$

### Exterior products {#Exterior_products}

Let $\alpha, \beta, \gamma,\alpha_i\in \Omega^1(M)$ and $X,Y,Z,X_i$ be vector fields.

1. $$\alpha(X) = \det \begin{bmatrix} \alpha(X) \\ \end{bmatrix}$$
1. $$(\alpha\wedge\beta)(X,Y) = \det \begin{bmatrix} \alpha(X) & \alpha(Y) \\ \beta(X) & \beta(Y) \\ \end{bmatrix}$$
1. $$(\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\gamma)(X,Y,Z) = \det \begin{bmatrix} \alpha(X) & \alpha(Y) & \alpha(Z) \\ \beta(X) & \beta(Y) & \beta(Z) \\ \gamma(X) & \gamma(Y) & \gamma(Z) \end{bmatrix}$$
1. $$(\alpha_1\wedge\ldots\wedge\alpha_l)(X_1,\ldots,X_l) = \det \begin{bmatrix} \alpha_1(X_1) & \alpha_1(X_2) & \dots & \alpha_1(X_l) \\ \alpha_2(X_1) & \alpha_2(X_2) & \dots & \alpha_2(X_l) \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \alpha_l(X_1) & \alpha_l(X_2) & \dots & \alpha_l(X_l) \end{bmatrix}$$

### Projection and rejection {#Projection_and_rejection}

- $(-1)^k\iota_X{\star}\alpha = {\star}(X^{\flat}\wedge\alpha)$ ( *interior product $\iota_X{\star}$ dual to wedge $X^{\flat}\wedge$* )
- $(\iota_X\alpha)\wedge{\star}\beta =\alpha\wedge{\star}(X^{\flat}\wedge\beta)$ for $\alpha\in\Omega^{k+1}(M),\beta\in\Omega^k(M)$

If $|X|=1, \ \alpha\in\Omega^k(M)$, then

- $\iota_X\circ (X^{\flat}\wedge ):\Omega^k(M)\rightarrow\Omega^k(M)$ is the *projection* of $\alpha$ onto the [orthogonal complement](wikipedia://en/1058833) of $X$.
- $(X^{\flat}\wedge )\circ \iota_X:\Omega^k(M)\rightarrow\Omega^k(M)$ is the *rejection* of $\alpha$, the remainder of the projection.
- thus $\iota_X \circ (X^{\flat}\wedge ) + (X^{\flat}\wedge)\circ\iota_X = \text{id}$ ( *projection–rejection decomposition* )

Given the boundary $\partial M$ with unit normal vector $N$

- $\mathbf{t}:=\iota_N\circ (N^{\flat}\wedge )$ extracts the *tangential component* of the boundary.
- $\mathbf{n}:=(\text{id}-\mathbf{t})$ extracts the *normal component* of the boundary.

### Sum expressions {#Sum_expressions}

- $$(d\alpha)(X_0,\ldots,X_k)=\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}(-1)^jd(\alpha(X_0,\ldots,\hat{X}_j,\ldots,X_k))(X_j) + \sum_{0\leq i < j\leq k}(-1)^{i+j}\alpha([X_i,X_j],X_0,\ldots,\hat{X}_i,\ldots,\hat{X}_j,\ldots,X_k)$$
- $$(d\alpha)(X_1,\ldots,X_k) =\sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i+1}(\nabla_{X_i}\alpha)(X_1,\ldots,\hat{X}_i,\ldots,X_k)$$
- $(\delta\alpha)(X_1,\ldots,X_{k-1})=-\sum_{i=1}^n(\iota_{E_i}(\nabla_{E_i}\alpha))(X_1,\ldots,\hat{X}_i,\ldots,X_k)$ given a positively oriented orthonormal frame $E_1,\ldots,E_n$.
- $$(\mathcal{L}_Y\alpha)(X_1,\ldots,X_k) =(\nabla_Y\alpha)(X_1,\ldots,X_k) - \sum_{i=1}^k\alpha(X_1,\ldots,\nabla_{X_i}Y,\ldots,X_k)$$

### Hodge decomposition {#Hodge_decomposition}

If $\partial M =\emptyset$, $\omega\in\Omega^k(M) \Rightarrow \exists \alpha\in\Omega^{k-1}, \ \beta\in\Omega^{k+1}, \ \gamma\in\Omega^k(M), \ d\gamma=0, \ \delta\gamma = 0$ such that

$$\omega = d\alpha + \delta\beta + \gamma$$

::: {#o52 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Poincaré lemma"}

### [Poincaré lemma](wikipedia://en/600030) {#Poincaré_lemma}

If a boundaryless manifold $M$ has trivial cohomology $H^k(M)=\{0\}$, then any closed $\omega\in\Omega^k(M)$ is exact. This is the case if *M* is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414).

:::

## Relations to vector calculus {#Relations_to_vector_calculus}

### Identities in Euclidean 3-space {#Identities_in_Euclidean_3-space}

Let [Euclidean metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_metric) $g(X,Y):=\langle X,Y\rangle = X\cdot Y$.

We use $\nabla = \left( {\partial \over \partial x}, {\partial \over \partial y}, {\partial \over \partial z} \right)$ [differential operator](wikipedia://en/151925) $\mathbb{R}^3$

- $\iota_X\alpha = g(X,\alpha^{\sharp}) = X\cdot \alpha^{\sharp}$ for $\alpha\in\Omega^1(M)$.
- $\mathbf{det}(X,Y,Z)=\langle X,Y\times Z\rangle = \langle X\times Y,Z\rangle$ ( *[scalar triple product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_triple_product)* )
- $X\times Y = ({\star}(X^{\flat}\wedge Y^{\flat}))^{\sharp}$ ( *[cross product](wikipedia://en/157092)* )
- $\iota_X\alpha=-(X\times A)^{\flat}$ if $\alpha\in\Omega^2(M),\ A=({\star}\alpha)^{\sharp}$
- $X\cdot Y = {\star}(X^{\flat}\wedge {\star} Y^{\flat})$ ( *[scalar product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_product)* )
- $\nabla f=(df)^{\sharp}$ ( *[gradient](wikipedia://en/12461)* )
- $X\cdot\nabla f=df(X)$ ( *[directional derivative](wikipedia://en/487859)* )
- $\nabla\cdot X = {\star} d {\star} X^{\flat} = -\delta X^{\flat}$ ( *[divergence](wikipedia://en/8328)* )
- $\nabla\times X = ({\star} d X^{\flat})^{\sharp}$ ( *[curl](wikipedia://en/6123)* )
- $\langle X,N\rangle\sigma = {\star} X^\flat$ where $N$ is the unit normal vector of $\partial M$ and $\sigma=\iota_{N}\mathbf{det}$ is the area form on $\partial M$.
- $\int_{\Sigma} d{\star} X^{\flat} = \int_{\partial\Sigma}{\star} X^{\flat} = \int_{\partial\Sigma}\langle X,N\rangle\sigma$ ( *[divergence theorem](wikipedia://en/151864)* )

### Lie derivatives {#Lie_derivatives}

- $\mathcal{L}_X f =X\cdot \nabla f$ ( *$0$-forms* )
- $\mathcal{L}_X \alpha = (\nabla_X\alpha^{\sharp})^{\flat} +g(\alpha^{\sharp},\nabla X)$ ( *$1$-forms* )
- ${\star}\mathcal{L}_X\beta = \left( \nabla_XB - \nabla_BX + (\text{div}X)B \right)^{\flat}$ if $B=({\star}\beta)^{\sharp}$ ( *$2$-forms on $3$-manifolds* )
- ${\star}\mathcal{L}_X\rho = dq(X)+(\text{div}X)q$ if $\rho={\star} q \in \Omega^0(M)$ ( *$n$-forms* )
- $$\mathcal{L}_X(\mathbf{det})=(\text{div}(X))\mathbf{det}$$
