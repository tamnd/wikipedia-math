# Lichnerowicz formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lichnerowicz formula"}

The **Lichnerowicz formula** (also known as the **Lichnerowicz–Weitzenböck formula**) is a fundamental equation in the analysis of [spinors](wikipedia://en/29276) on [pseudo-Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/287555). In dimension 4, it forms a piece of [Seiberg–Witten theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiberg–Witten_gauge_theory) and other aspects of [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155). It is named after noted mathematicians [André Lichnerowicz](wikipedia://en/2389188) who proved it in 1963, and [Roland Weitzenböck](wikipedia://en/35767598). The formula gives a relationship between the [Dirac operator](wikipedia://en/1079448) and the [Laplace–Beltrami operator](wikipedia://en/1763356) acting on spinors, in which the [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) appears in a natural way. The result is significant because it provides an interface between results from the study of [elliptic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/662787), results concerning the scalar curvature, and results on spinors and spin structures.

:::

Given a [spin structure](wikipedia://en/2844303) on a pseudo-Riemannian manifold *M* and a [spinor bundle](wikipedia://en/348085) *S*, the Lichnerowicz formula states that on a [section](wikipedia://en/367621) ψ of *S*,

$$D^2\psi = \nabla^*\nabla\psi + \frac{1}{4}\operatorname{Sc}\psi$$

where Sc denotes the [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) and $\nabla^*\nabla$ is the [connection Laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichnerowicz_Laplacian#Connection_Laplacian). More generally, given a [complex spin structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_spin_structure) on a pseudo-Riemannian manifold *M*, a [spinor bundle](wikipedia://en/348085) *W*<sup>±</sup> with section $\phi$, and a connection *A* on its [determinant line bundle](wikipedia://en/984307) *L*, the Lichnerowicz formula is

$$D_{A}^{*}D_{A}\phi=\nabla _A^{*}\nabla_{A}\phi+\frac{1}{4}R\phi+\frac{1}{2}\langle F_{A}^{+},\phi\rangle.$$

Here, $D_A$ is the [Dirac operator](wikipedia://en/1079448) $D_A:\Gamma (W^+)\to \Gamma (W^-),$ and $\nabla _A$ is the [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848) associated with the [connection](wikipedia://en/391816) A, $\nabla _A: \Gamma (W^+)\to \Gamma(W^+\otimes T_M^*)$. $R$ is the usual scalar curvature (a contraction of the [Ricci tensor](wikipedia://en/276582)) and $F_A^+$ is the [self-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual) part of the curvature of A. The asterisks denote the adjoint of the quantity and the brackets $\langle , \rangle$ denote the [Clifford action](wikipedia://en/45305).
