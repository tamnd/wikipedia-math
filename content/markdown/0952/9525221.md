# James Arthur (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Arthur"}

**James Greig Arthur** (born May 18, 1944)[^o3] is a Canadian mathematician working on [automorphic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_forms), and former President of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). He is a Mossman Chair and University Professor Emeritus at the [University of Toronto Department of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto_Department_of_Mathematics).[^o4] He won the [Wolf Prize](wikipedia://en/714278) in 2015 "for his monumental work on the [trace formula](wikipedia://en/20419621) and his fundamental contributions to the theory of [automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation) of [reductive groups](wikipedia://en/1088386)".[^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Born in [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), Arthur graduated from [Upper Canada College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Canada_College) in 1962,[^o7] received a BSc from the University of Toronto in 1966, and a MSc from the same institution in 1967. He received his PhD from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1970. He was a student of [Robert Langlands](wikipedia://en/25986); his dissertation was *Analysis of Tempered Distributions on Semisimple Lie Groups of Real Rank One*.

Arthur taught at Yale from 1970 until 1976. He joined the faculty of [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) in 1976. He has been a professor at the University of Toronto since 1978.[^o3] He was four times a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) between 1976 and 2002.[^o9]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Arthur is known for the [Arthur–Selberg trace formula](wikipedia://en/20419621), generalizing the [Selberg trace formula](wikipedia://en/981694) from the rank-one case (due to Selberg himself) to general [reductive groups](wikipedia://en/1088386), one of the most important tools for research on the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905). He also introduced the [Arthur conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_conjectures). His seminal 2013 book *The endoscopic classification of representations—orthogonal and symplectic groups* uses the trace formula to establish important special cases of the Langlands Functoriality Principle.[^o11]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Arthur was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Canada) in 1981 and a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1992.[^o13][^o14] In 1998 he was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o15] He was elected a Foreign Honorary Member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2003.[^o16] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o17] He was elected as a fellow of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) in 2019.[^o18]
