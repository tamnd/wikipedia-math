# Elliptic Gauss sum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elliptic Gauss sum"}

In mathematics, an **elliptic Gauss sum** is an analog of a [Gauss sum](wikipedia://en/6378204) depending on an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) with complex multiplication. The [quadratic residue](wikipedia://en/200091) symbol in a Gauss sum is replaced by a higher residue symbol such as a cubic or quartic residue symbol, and the exponential function in a Gauss sum is replaced by an [elliptic function](wikipedia://en/69939). They were introduced by (txt), at least in the lemniscate case when the elliptic curve has complex multiplication by $i$, but seem to have been forgotten or ignored until the paper (Pinch 1988).

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

(Lemmermeyer 2000, 9.3) gives the following example of an elliptic Gauss sum, for the case of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by $i$.

$$-\sum_t\chi(t)\varphi\left ( \frac{t}{\pi} \right )^\frac{p-1}{m}$$

where

- The sum is over residues mod $P$ whose representatives are Gaussian integers
- $n$ is a positive integer
- $m$ is a positive integer dividing $4n$
- $p = 4n + 1$ is a rational prime congruent to 1 mod 4
- $\varphi(z) = \operatorname{sl}((1 - i)\omega z)$ where $\operatorname{sl}$ is the [sine lemniscate function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine_lemniscate_function), an elliptic function.
- $\chi$ is the $m$th power residue symbol in $K$ with respect to the prime $P$ of $K$
- $K$ is the field $k[\zeta]$
- $k$ is the field $\mathbb{Q}[i]$
- $\zeta$ is a primitive $4n$th root of 1
- $\pi$ is a primary prime in the Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ with norm $p$
- $P$ is a prime in the ring of integers of $K$ lying above $\pi$ with inertia degree 1

:::
