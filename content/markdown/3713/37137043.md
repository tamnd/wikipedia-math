# Toothpick sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toothpick sequence"}

*Image: Margolus toothpick animated.gif.* The first three steps of the toothpick sequence and its emulation by a [cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/54342) with the [Margolus neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margolus_neighborhood)

*Image: Toothpick 89.svg.* The 89th stage of the sequence, one of the stages at which *T*(*n*)/*n*<sup>2</sup> is near its minimum

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **toothpick sequence** is a sequence of 2-dimensional patterns which can be formed by repeatedly adding line segments ("toothpicks") to the previous pattern in the sequence.

:::

The first stage of the design is a single "toothpick", or line segment. Each stage after the first is formed by taking the previous design and, for every exposed toothpick end, placing another toothpick centered at a right angle on that end.[^o2]

This process results in a pattern of growth in which the number of segments at stage $n$ oscillates with a [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) pattern between $0.45n^{2}$ and $0.67n^{2}$. If $T(n)$ denotes the number of segments at stage $n$, then values of $n$ for which $T(n)/n^{2}$ is near its maximum occur when $n$ is near a power of two, while the values for which it is near its minimum occur near numbers that are approximately $1.43$ times a power of two.[^o3] The structure of stages in the toothpick sequence often resembles the [T-square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-square_%28fractal%29) fractal, or the arrangement of cells in the [Ulam–Warburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulam–Warburton_automaton) [cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/54342).[^o2]

All of the bounded regions surrounded by toothpicks in the pattern, but not themselves crossed by toothpicks, must be squares or rectangles.[^o2] It has been [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) that every open rectangle in the toothpick pattern (that is, a rectangle that is completely surrounded by toothpicks, but has no toothpick crossing its interior) has side lengths and areas that are [powers of two](wikipedia://en/376948), with one of the side lengths being at most two.
