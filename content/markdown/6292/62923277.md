# Arnold Oberschelp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arnold Oberschelp"}

*Image: Arnold Oberschelp.jpg.* Oberschelp in 1978

**Arnold Oberschelp** (5 February 1932 – 31 August 2024) was a German mathematician and [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician). He was a professor of logic and science in [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Oberschelp studied mathematics and physics at the universities of [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) and [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster). In Münster he received in December 1957 his doctorate in mathematical logic under [Hans Hermes](wikipedia://en/41139401).[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6] In 1958 he was a research assistant at the Mathematical Institute of the Technical College of Hannover (now [Leibniz University Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_University_Hannover)) where he habilitated in mathematics in 1961.[^o3][^o7] In 1968, he accepted an appointment as full professor of logic and science at the [University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel). Oberschelp has been emeritus professor since 1997.[^o8]

Arnold Oberschelp developed a general class logic in which arbitrary classes can be formed without the contradictions of naive [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553). Additional axioms result in the [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214), which is much more handy in his class-logical representation than in the usual [predicate logical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_logic) representation.[^o9]

In 1962 he gave a lecture as an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Stockholm on classes as "primal elements" in set theory.

From 1970 to 1976 he was chairman of the Deutsche Vereinigung für mathematische Logik und für Grundlagenforschung der exakten Wissenschaften,[^o10] on whose board he served from 1965 to 1978.

In September 2019, he received the [German Institute for Standardization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Institute_for_Standardization)'s Beuth Memorial Coin in recognition of his services to standardization in mathematics and technical foundations.[^o11]

Oberschelp died on 31 August 2024, at the age of 92.[^o12]
