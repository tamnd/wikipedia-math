# Jim Coykendall

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jim Coykendall"}

**James "Jim" Barker Coykendall IV** is an American mathematician.

:::

## Education and Career {#Education_and_Career}

Coykendall was born in 1967. Coykendall earned his bachelor's degree from the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1989 and completed a doctorate at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1995.[^o3][^o7] His thesis, titled *Normsets and Rings of Algebraic Integers*, was overseen by [Shankar Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shankar_Sen).[^o4] After earning his doctorate, Coykendall was the C. C. Hsiung Visiting Professor of Mathematics at [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University) until 1996. Coykendall's teaching career continued at [North Dakota State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_State_University), where he was named James A. Meier Professor in 2003.[^o6][^o7] Coykendall joined the faculty of [Clemson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clemson_University) in 2013 and served as the Department Chair of the Mathematical Sciences Department from 2013 to 2015.[^o8]

Coykendall and [Hal Schenck](wikipedia://en/67786208) have served as editors of the *[Journal of Commutative Algebra](wikipedia://en/56125679)* since the publication's first issue in 2009.[^o9] Coykendall also serves on the editorial boards of the *Rocky Mountain Journal of Mathematics* and the *International Journal of Commutative Rings*.[^o10]

Coykendall has made significant contributions to research in commutative algebra, particularly in the study of factorization properties of rings and ideals. He presented his work on *Normsets and Factorization Properties of Rings of Integers* at the 1996 Spring Southeastern Sectional Meeting of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in Baton Rouge.[^o11] His research has been widely published, and he has co-authored articles such as *Sets with Few Intersection Numbers from Singer Subgroup Orbits*, which appeared in the *European Journal of Combinatorics*.[^o12]

According to the [Mathematics Genealogy Project](wikipedia://en/1152126), Coykendall has supervised 17 doctoral students, as of January 2026.[^o13]
