# Sums of powers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sums of powers"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **sums of powers** occur in a number of contexts:

:::

- [Sums of squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum_of_squares_%28disambiguation%29) arise in many contexts. For example, in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034) involves the sum of two squares; in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), there are [Legendre's three-square theorem](wikipedia://en/40708124) and [Jacobi's four-square theorem](wikipedia://en/18808748); and in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634) involves summing the squares of quantities.
- There are only finitely many positive integers that are not sums of *distinct* squares. The largest one is 128. The same applies for sums of distinct cubes (largest one is 12,758), distinct fourth powers (largest is 5,134,240), etc. See [^o2] for a generalization to sums of polynomials.
- [Faulhaber's formula](wikipedia://en/6738151) expresses $1^k + 2^k + 3^k + \cdots + n^k$ as a polynomial in $n$, or alternatively [in terms of a Bernoulli polynomial](wikipedia://en/228161#Sums_of_pth_powers).
- The [triangular numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_numbers) are the sums of the first powers of the first several positive integers. The [squares of the triangular numbers](wikipedia://en/4924488) are the sums of the third powers of the first several positive integers. In formulas, these are

$$1^1 + 2^1 + 3^1 + \cdots + n^1 = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$$

and

$$1^3 + 2^3 + 3^3 + \cdots + n^3 = \left(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right)^2.$$

- [Fermat's right triangle theorem](wikipedia://en/29559494) states that there is no solution in positive integers for $a^2=b^4+c^4$ and $a^4=b^4+c^2$.
- [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) states that $x^k+y^k=z^k$ is impossible in positive integers with $k > 2$.
- The equation of a [superellipse](wikipedia://en/52211) is $|x/a|^k+|y/b|^k=1$. The [squircle](wikipedia://en/5565333) is the case $k = 4$, $a = b$.
- [Euler's sum of powers conjecture](wikipedia://en/9660) (disproved) concerns situations in which the sum of $n$ integers, each a $k$<sup>th</sup> power of an integer, equals another $k$<sup>th</sup> power.
- The [Fermat-Catalan conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat-Catalan_conjecture) asks whether there are an infinitude of examples in which the sum of two coprime integers, each a power of an integer, with the powers not necessarily equal, can equal another integer that is a power, with the reciprocals of the three powers summing to less than 1.
- [Beal's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beal's_conjecture) concerns the question of whether the sum of two coprime integers, each a power greater than 2 of an integer, with the powers not necessarily equal, can equal another integer that is a power greater than 2.
- The [Jacobi–Madden equation](wikipedia://en/16870573) is $a^4 + b^4 + c^4 + d^4 = (a + b + c + d)^4$ in integers.
- The [Prouhet–Tarry–Escott problem](wikipedia://en/16400356) considers sums of two sets of $k$<sup>th</sup> powers of integers that are equal for multiple values of $k$.
- A [taxicab number](wikipedia://en/513117) is the smallest integer that can be expressed as a sum of two positive third powers in $n$ distinct ways.
- The [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) is the [sum of reciprocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum_of_reciprocals) of the positive integers each raised to the power $s$, where $s$ is a [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) whose real part is greater than 1.
- The [Lander, Parkin, and Selfridge conjecture](wikipedia://en/40052960) concerns the minimal value of $m + n$ in $\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i^k = \sum_{j=1}^{m} b_j^k.$
- [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569) asks whether for every natural number $k$ there exists an associated positive integer $s$ such that every natural number is the sum of at most $s$ $k$<sup>th</sup> powers of natural numbers.
- The successive powers of the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386#Relationship_to_Fibonacci_and_Lucas_numbers) *φ* obey the Fibonacci recurrence: $$\varphi^{n+1} = \varphi^n + \varphi^{n-1}.$$
- [Newton's identities](wikipedia://en/2468892) express the sum of the $k$<sup>th</sup> powers of all the roots of a polynomial in terms of the coefficients in the polynomial.
- The [sum of cubes of numbers in arithmetic progression](wikipedia://en/451445#Sum_of_cubes_of_numbers_in_arithmetic_progression) is sometimes another cube.
- The [Fermat cubic](wikipedia://en/4058828), in which the sum of three cubes equals another cube, has a general solution.
- The [power sum symmetric polynomial](wikipedia://en/10195749) is a building block for symmetric polynomials.
- The [sum of the reciprocals of all perfect powers](wikipedia://en/4023591#Examples_and_sums) including duplicates (but not including 1) equals 1.
- The [Erdős–Moser equation](wikipedia://en/53551948), $1^k+2^k+\cdots+m^k=(m+1)^k$ where $m$ and $k$ are positive integers, is conjectured to have no solutions other than 1<sup>1</sup> + 2<sup>1</sup> = 3<sup>1</sup>.
- The [sums of three cubes](wikipedia://en/60187666) cannot equal 4 or 5 modulo 9, but it is unknown whether all remaining integers can be expressed in this form.
- The sum of the terms in the [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630) is $\sum_{i=k}^{n} z^i = \frac{z^{k}-z^{n+1}}{1-z}.$
- The sum of powers can be expressed as such : $\sum_{i=1}^{n} i^k = \sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=i}^{n} j^{k-a}(i^a-(i-1)^a)$ for n greater than 1, a greater than 1 and all k. This one is useful to help determine subsequent sums of power based on known previous sums of power.
