# Parallelogram

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Parallelogram"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), a **parallelogram** is a [chiral](wikipedia://en/1170160) [simple](wikipedia://en/1059530) (non-[self-intersecting](wikipedia://en/50055439)) [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) with two pairs of [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497) sides. The opposite or facing sides of a parallelogram are of equal length and the opposite angles of a parallelogram are of equal measure. The [congruence](wikipedia://en/39330) of opposite sides and opposite angles is a direct consequence of the Euclidean [parallel postulate](wikipedia://en/33731493) and neither condition can be proven without appealing to the Euclidean parallel postulate or one of its equivalent formulations.

:::

By comparison, a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides is a [trapezoid](wikipedia://en/201826) in American English or a trapezium in British English.

The three-dimensional counterpart of a parallelogram is a [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975).

The word "parallelogram" comes from the Greek παραλληλό-γραμμον, *parallēló-grammon*, which means "a shape of parallel lines".

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

- [Rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) – A parallelogram with four right angles.
- [Rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) – A parallelogram with four sides of equal length.
- [Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) – A parallelogram with four sides of equal length and four right angles.
- Rhomboid – A parallelogram with adjacent sides that are of unequal lengths and [non-right angles](wikipedia://en/1196#Types_of_angles) (and thus is neither a rectangle nor a rhombus). This term is not used in modern mathematics but it does survive in some contexts in biology in names like the [rhomboid muscles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhomboid_muscles) or [rhomboid leaf shapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_leaf_morphology#Leaf_and_leaflet_shapes).[^o5]

:::

## Characterizations {#Characterizations}

A [simple](wikipedia://en/1059530) (non-self-intersecting) [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) is a parallelogram [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) any one of the following statements is true:[^o8][^o9]

- Two pairs of opposite sides are parallel (by definition).
- Two pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
- Two pairs of opposite angles are equal in measure.
- The [diagonals](wikipedia://en/349251) bisect each other.
- One pair of opposite sides is [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497) and equal in length.
- [Adjacent angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjacent_angles) are [supplementary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplementary_angles).
- Each diagonal divides the quadrilateral into two [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654).
- The sum of the [squares](wikipedia://en/213919) of the sides equals the sum of the squares of the diagonals. (This is the [parallelogram law](wikipedia://en/362953).)
- It has [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096) of order 2.
- The sum of the distances from any interior point to the sides is independent of the location of the point.[^o10] (This is an extension of [Viviani's theorem](wikipedia://en/10460511).)
- There is a point *X* in the plane of the quadrilateral with the property that every straight line through *X* divides the quadrilateral into two regions of equal area.[^o11]

Thus, all parallelograms have all the properties listed above, and [conversely](wikipedia://en/155941), if just any one of these statements is true in a simple quadrilateral, then it is considered a parallelogram.

:::

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

- Opposite sides of a parallelogram are parallel (by definition) and so will never intersect.
- The area of a parallelogram is twice the area of a triangle created by one of its diagonals.
- The area of a parallelogram is also equal to the magnitude of the [vector cross product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_cross_product) of two [adjacent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjacent_side_%28polygon%29) sides.
- Any line through the midpoint of a parallelogram bisects the area.[^o13]
- Any non-degenerate [affine transformation](wikipedia://en/38449) takes a parallelogram to another parallelogram.
- A parallelogram has [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096) of order 2 (through 180°) (or order 4 if a square). If it also has exactly two lines of [reflectional symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflectional_symmetry) then it must be a rhombus or an oblong (a non-square rectangle). If it has four lines of reflectional symmetry, it is a [square](wikipedia://en/659939).
- The perimeter of a parallelogram is 2(*a* + *b*) where *a* and *b* are the lengths of adjacent sides.
- Unlike any other convex polygon, a parallelogram cannot be inscribed in any triangle with less than twice its area.[^o14]
- The centers of four squares all constructed either internally or externally on the sides of a parallelogram are the vertices of a square.[^o15]
- If two lines parallel to sides of a parallelogram are constructed [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157) to a diagonal, then the parallelograms formed on opposite sides of that diagonal are equal in area.[^o15]
- The diagonals of a parallelogram divide it into four triangles of equal area.

## Area formula {#Area_formula}

*Image: ParallelogramArea.svg.* A parallelogram can be rearranged into a rectangle with the same area.

*Image: Parallelogram area animated.gif.* Animation for the area formula $K = bh$.

All of the [area formulas for general convex quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/25278#Area_of_a_convex_quadrilateral) apply to parallelograms. Further formulas are specific to parallelograms:

A parallelogram with base *b* and height *h* can be divided into a [trapezoid](wikipedia://en/201826) and a [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420), and rearranged into a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048), as shown in the figure to the left. This means that the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a parallelogram is the same as that of a rectangle with the same base and height:

$$K = bh.$$

*Image: Parallelogram area.svg.* The area of the parallelogram is the area of the blue region, which is the interior of the parallelogram

The base × height area formula can also be derived using the figure to the right. The area *K* of the parallelogram to the right (the blue area) is the total area of the rectangle less the area of the two orange triangles. The area of the rectangle is

$$K_\text{rect} = (B+A) \times H\,$$

and the area of a single triangle is

$$K_\text{tri} = \frac{A}{2} \times H. \,$$

Therefore, the area of the parallelogram is

$$K = K_\text{rect} - 2 \times K_\text{tri} = ( (B+A) \times H) - ( A \times H) = B \times H.$$

Another area formula, for two sides *B* and *C* and angle θ, is

$$K = B \cdot C \cdot \sin \theta.\,$$

Provided that the parallelogram is a rhombus, the area can be expressed using sides *B* and *C* and angle $\gamma$ at the intersection of the diagonals:[^o18]

$$K = \frac{|\tan \gamma|}{2} \cdot \left| B^2 - C^2 \right|.$$

When the parallelogram is specified from the lengths *B* and *C* of two adjacent sides together with the length *D*<sub>1</sub> of either diagonal, then the area can be found from [Heron's formula](wikipedia://en/194123). Specifically it is

$$K=2\sqrt{S(S-B)(S-C)(S-D_1)}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(B+C+D_1)(-B+C+D_1)(B-C+D_1)(B+C-D_1)},$$

where $S=(B+C+D_1)/2$ and the leading factor 2 comes from the fact that the chosen diagonal divides the parallelogram into *two* congruent triangles.

### From vertex coordinates {#From_vertex_coordinates}

Let vectors $\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^2$ and let $V = \begin{bmatrix} a_1 & a_2 \\ b_1 & b_2 \end{bmatrix} \in\mathbb{R}^{2 \times 2}$ denote the matrix with elements of **a** and **b**. Then the area of the parallelogram generated by **a** and **b** is equal to $|\det(V)| = |a_1b_2 - a_2b_1|\,$.

Let vectors $\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^n$ and let $V = \begin{bmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & \dots & a_n \\ b_1 & b_2 & \dots & b_n \end{bmatrix} \in\mathbb{R}^{2 \times n}$. Then the area of the parallelogram generated by **a** and **b** is equal to $\sqrt{\det(V V^\mathrm{T})}$.

Let points $a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}^2$. Then the [signed area](wikipedia://en/39433938) of the parallelogram with vertices at *a*, *b* and *c* is equivalent to the determinant of a matrix built using *a*, *b* and *c* as rows with the last column padded using ones as follows:

$$K = \left| \begin{matrix} a_1 & a_2 & 1 \\ b_1 & b_2 & 1 \\ c_1 & c_2 & 1 \end{matrix} \right|.$$

## Proof that diagonals bisect each other {#Proof_that_diagonals_bisect_each_other}

*Image: Parallelogram1.svg.* Parallelogram ABCD

To prove that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, we will use [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654#Basic_facts):

$\angle ABE \cong \angle CDE$ *(alternate interior angles are equal in measure)*

$\angle BAE \cong \angle DCE$ *(alternate interior angles are equal in measure)*.

(since these are angles that a transversal makes with [parallel lines](wikipedia://en/664497) *AB* and *DC*).

Also, side *AB* is equal in length to side *DC*, since opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal in length.

Therefore, triangles *ABE* and *CDE* are congruent (ASA postulate, *two corresponding angles and the included side*).

Therefore,

$$AE = CE$$

$$BE = DE.$$

Since the diagonals *AC* and *BD* divide each other into segments of equal length, the diagonals bisect each other.

Separately, since the diagonals *AC* and *BD* bisect each other at point *E*, point *E* is the midpoint of each diagonal.

:::

## Lattice of parallelograms {#Lattice_of_parallelograms}

Parallelograms can tile the plane by translation. If edges are equal, or angles are right, the symmetry of the lattice is higher. These represent the four [Bravais lattices in 2 dimensions](wikipedia://en/661808#In_2_dimensions).

## Parallelograms arising from other figures {#Parallelograms_arising_from_other_figures}

### Automedian triangle {#Automedian_triangle}

An [automedian triangle](wikipedia://en/33531848) is one whose [medians](wikipedia://en/1072006) are in the same proportions as its sides (though in a different order). If *ABC* is an automedian triangle in which vertex *A* stands opposite the side *a*, *G* is the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) (where the three medians of *ABC* intersect), and *AL* is one of the extended medians of *ABC* with *L* lying on the circumcircle of *ABC*, then *BGCL* is a parallelogram.

### Varignon parallelogram {#Varignon_parallelogram}

*Image: varignon\_parallelogram.svg.* Proof without words of Varignon's theorem

[Varignon's theorem](wikipedia://en/17006564) holds that the [midpoints](wikipedia://en/437979) of the sides of an arbitrary quadrilateral are the vertices of a parallelogram, called its *Varignon parallelogram*. If the quadrilateral is [convex](wikipedia://en/660019) or [concave](wikipedia://en/605156) (that is, not self-intersecting), then the area of the Varignon parallelogram is half the area of the quadrilateral.

[Proof without words](wikipedia://en/18045517) (see figure):

1. An arbitrary quadrilateral and its diagonals.
1. Bases of similar triangles are parallel to the blue diagonal.
1. Ditto for the red diagonal.
1. The base pairs form a parallelogram with half the area of the quadrilateral, *A<sub>q</sub>*, as the sum of the areas of the four large triangles, *A<sub>l</sub>* is 2 *A<sub>q</sub>* (each of the two pairs reconstructs the quadrilateral) while that of the small triangles, *A<sub>s</sub>* is a quarter of *A<sub>l</sub>* (half linear dimensions yields quarter area), and the area of the parallelogram is *A<sub>q</sub>* minus *A<sub>s</sub>*.

### Tangent parallelogram of an ellipse {#Tangent_parallelogram_of_an_ellipse}

For an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277), two diameters are said to be [conjugate](wikipedia://en/24531598) if and only if the [tangent line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_line) to the ellipse at an endpoint of one diameter is parallel to the other diameter. Each pair of conjugate diameters of an ellipse has a corresponding [tangent parallelogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_parallelogram), sometimes called a bounding parallelogram, formed by the tangent lines to the ellipse at the four endpoints of the conjugate diameters. All tangent parallelograms for a given ellipse have the same area.

It is possible to [reconstruct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge_constructions) an ellipse from any pair of conjugate diameters, or from any tangent parallelogram.

### Faces of a parallelepiped {#Faces_of_a_parallelepiped}

A [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975) is a three-dimensional figure whose six [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) are parallelograms.
