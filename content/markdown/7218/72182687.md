# Kees Vuik

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kees Vuik"}

Cornelis (Kees) Vuik ([Capelle aan den IJssel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capelle_aan_den_IJssel), Jan. 25, 1959) is a Dutch [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and emeritus professor. In 1982 he received his master's degree in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) from [Delft University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology) in [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands). He worked at [Philips Natuurkundig Laboratorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philips_Natuurkundig_Laboratorium) for six months. He completed his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University) in 1988. His research[^o2] focused on moving-boundary problems (Stefan problems) and he was supervised by Prof. dr. E.M.J. Bertin and Prof. dr. A. van der Sluis.[^o3] Vuik then worked at [TU Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology), successively as assistant professor, associate professor, and eventually full professor of [Numerical Analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) (2007-2026) in the Faculty of Electrical Engineering, Mathematics and Computer Science.[^o4] From 2022-2025, he served as chair of the Delft Institute of Applied Mathematics (DIAM) department.[^o5]

:::

## Research {#Research}

Vuik's primary research interests are in [numerical linear algebra](wikipedia://en/7330660). He also works on simulators for energy networks and high-performance computing.[^o7] Several dozen Ph.D. students have been supervised by Vuik.[^o3] His research has led to many publications in international journals.[^o8][^o9] He was the founder of the TU Delft Institute for Computational Science and Engineering (DCSE), of which he has been the director from 2007 to 2025[^o10]. The institute connects TU Delft researchers from various disciplines that make use of computational methods. Thanks to his efforts, a High-Performance Computing facility was created within [TU Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology),[^o11] to which he has been a scientific director from 2020 to 2023.[^o12] From 2012 to 2019, Vuik also served as scientific director of the 4TU.Applied Mathematics Institute[^o13] (4TU.AMI) of [TU Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology), [TU Eindhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology), the [University of Twente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Twente) and [Wageningen University & Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wageningen_University_&_Research). Under his leadership, the institute has grown into an important player within the Dutch mathematical community, with connections to similar institutes such as Matheon in Berlin.[^o14] 4TU.AMI has also contributed to the innovation of mathematics education.[^o15] In 2013, Vuik participated in a Flemish-Dutch economic mission to Texas as a representative of (at the time) the 3TU federation.[^o16][^o17]

## Teaching {#Teaching}

Vuik is active in developing mathematical courses, BSc. and MSc. curricula, and educational innovations. He founded the minor Computational Science and Engineering.[^o19] He is the coordinator of the international program Computer Simulation for Science and Engineering (COSSE), established in cooperation with [TU Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Berlin) and [KTH Royal Institute of Technology Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTH_Royal_Institute_of_Technology). Vuik was also closely involved in the Massive Open Online Course (MOOC) Mathematical Modeling Basics.[^o20] Tens of thousands of students have already participated in this online course. Vuik is a faculty advisor of the SIAM Student Chapter Delft of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM). He is further involved in the development and application of Multi-Media Math Education (MUMIE), an open-source e-learning platform in mathematics.

## Awards {#Awards}

Vuik has won several awards throughout his career, including:

\- 2026: [SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376) Fellow; [^o22]

\- 2022: Professor of Excellence Award of the Delft University Fund;[^o23]

\- 2021: Officer in the Order of [Orange Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Orange-Nassau);[^o24]

\- 2017: Bronze medal of the [TU Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology).[^o25]

## Ancillary positions {#Ancillary_positions}

Some of Vuik's ancillary positions are:

\- Jury member of the [ASML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASML_Holding) graduation prize for mathematics at the [Royal Holland Society of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Holland_Society_of_Sciences_and_Humanities) (KHMW);

\- Participant in the mathematics council of the Dutch Platform for Mathematics (PWN);[^o27]

\- President-director of the Batavian Society of Experimental Philosophy;[^o28][^o29]

\- Treasurer at EU-Maths-IN;[^o30]

\- Chairman of the local organizing committee of ICIAM 2027;[^o31][^o32]

\- Member of the organizing committee of the SIAM Conference on Computational Science and Engineering in 2021[^o33] and 2023.[^o34]
