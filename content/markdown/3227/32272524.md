# John Brownlee (statistician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Brownlee"}

**John Brownlee** (1868–1927) was a British physician and [medical statistician](wikipedia://en/11082896) who became the first director of the Statistics Department of the [UK's Medical Research Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_%28UK%29).[^o2]

:::

## Life {#Life}

The son of a [Church of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Scotland) minister, he studied at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow), obtaining degrees first in mathematics and [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy) and then in medicine. He became in 1900 physician-superintendent to the [City of Glasgow Fever Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Glasgow_Fever_and_Smallpox_Hospitals,_Belvidere) and the new [Ruchill Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruchill_Hospital) from 1908.[^o4][^o5] In 1914 he became the founding director of the statistics department of the [UK Medical Research Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_%28UK%29) and held the post until his sudden death from [bronchopneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchopneumonia) in 1927.[^o6]

## Works {#Works}

Brownlee was influenced by [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695)'s mathematical approach to statistics, and applied the [Pearson family of distributions](wikipedia://en/1804023) to [epidemics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic). In the view of fellow epidemiologist and statistician [Major Greenwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_Greenwood), Brownlee took these techniques further than any of his contemporaries.[^o8] His studies included the epidemiology of [phthisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis) and [measles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measles).[^o4]
