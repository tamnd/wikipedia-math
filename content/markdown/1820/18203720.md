# Topological degree theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Topological degree theory"}

In mathematics, **topological degree theory** is a generalization of the [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113) of a curve in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628). It can be used to estimate the number of solutions of an equation, and is closely connected to [fixed-point theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_point_theorem). When one solution of an equation is easily found, degree theory can often be used to prove existence of a second, nontrivial, solution. There are different types of degree for different types of maps: e.g. for maps between [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_spaces) there is the [Brouwer degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brouwer_degree) in **R**<sup>*n*</sup>, the [Leray-Schauder degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leray-Schauder_degree) for [compact mappings](wikipedia://en/6042) in [normed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_spaces), the [coincidence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coincidence_degree) and various other types. There is also a degree for [continuous maps between manifolds](wikipedia://en/3094450).

:::

Topological degree theory has applications in [complementarity problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complementarity_problem), [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), [differential inclusions](wikipedia://en/13725281) and [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087).
