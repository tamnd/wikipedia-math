# John Colson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="John Colson"}

**John Colson** (1680 – 20 January 1760) was an English clergyman, mathematician, and the [Lucasian Professor of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/147921) at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University).

:::

## Life {#Life}

John Colson was educated at Lichfield School before becoming an undergraduate at [Christ Church, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford), though he did not complete a degree there. He became a schoolmaster at [Sir Joseph Williamson's Mathematical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Joseph_Williamson's_Mathematical_School) in Rochester, and was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1713. He was Vicar of [Chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk,_Kent), Kent from 1724 to 1740. He relocated to Cambridge and lectured at [Sidney Sussex College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Sussex_College,_Cambridge).[^n1] From 1739 to 1760, he was Lucasian Professor of Mathematics. He was also Rector of [Lockington, Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockington,_Yorkshire).

## Works {#Works}

In 1726 he published his "Negativo-Affirmativo Arithmetik", which advocated a modified decimal system of numeration.[^o7] He proposed "reduction \[to\] small figures" by "throwing all the large figures $9, 8, 7, 6$ out of a given number, and introducing in their room the equivalent small figures $1\bar{1}, 1\bar{2}, 1\bar{3}, 1\bar{4}$ respectively". This method of signifying numbers is now called [signed-digit representation](wikipedia://en/1181818).[^o8]

John Colson translated several of [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627)'s works into English, including *De Methodis Serierum et Fluxionum* in 1736.[^n1]
