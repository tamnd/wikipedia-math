# Distinction without a difference

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Distinction without a difference"}

A **distinction without a difference** is an [informal fallacy](wikipedia://en/1655450) where an author or speaker attempts to describe a distinction between two things where no discernible difference exists.[^o2] It is particularly used when a word or phrase has [connotations](wikipedia://en/55671) associated with it that one party to an argument prefers to avoid.

:::

For example, a person might say "I did not lie; I merely stretched the truth a little bit."[^o3]

## Form of the fallacy {#Form_of_the_fallacy}

- Claim X is made where the truth of the claim requires a distinct difference between A and B.
- There is no distinct difference between A and B.
- Therefore, claim X is incorrectly claimed to be true.

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

In the following conversation:

- Sergio: There is no way I would ever even consider taking dancing lessons.
- Kitty: How about I ask my friend from work to teach you?
- Sergio: If you know someone who is willing to teach me how to dance, then I am willing to learn, sure.[^o7]

Sergio's fallacy is thinking that being taught by someone is different from taking a lesson.

:::
