# Llewellyn Thomas

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Llewellyn Thomas"}

**Llewellyn Hilleth Thomas** (21 October 1903 – 20 April 1992) was a British [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) and [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/24295969).[^o3] He is best known for his contributions to atomic and molecular physics and solid-state physics. His key achievements include calculating relativistic effects on the [spin-orbit interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin-orbit_interaction) in a [hydrogenic atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogenic_atom) ([Thomas precession](wikipedia://en/1868229)), creating an approximate theory of $N$-body quantum systems ([Thomas-Fermi theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas-Fermi_theory)), and devising an efficient method for solving tridiagonal system of linear equations ([Thomas algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_algorithm)).

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Born in London, he studied at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), receiving his [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts), [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD), and [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) degrees in 1924, 1927 and 1928 respectively. While on a Traveling Fellowship for the academic year 1925–1926 at [Bohr's Institute in Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr_Institute), he proposed Thomas precession in 1926, to explain the difference between predictions made by spin-orbit coupling theory and experimental observations.

In 1929 he obtained a job as a professor of physics at the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University), where he stayed until 1943. He married Naomi Estelle Frech in 1933.[^o5] In 1935 he was the master's thesis advisor for [Leonard Schiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_I._Schiff), whose thesis was published with Thomas as coauthor.[^o6] From 1943 until 1945 Thomas worked on ballistics at the [Aberdeen Proving Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Proving_Ground) in Maryland. In 1946 he became a member of the staff of the [Watson Scientific Computing Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_J._Watson_Research_Center#Overview) at Columbia University, remaining there until 1968. In 1958 he was elected as a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences). In 1963, Thomas was appointed as IBM's First Fellow in the Watson Research Center.[^o7] He was appointed professor at [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University) in 1968, retiring from this position in 1976.[^o5] In 1982 he received the [Davisson-Germer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davisson–Germer_Prize_in_Atomic_or_Surface_Physics).[^o8] He died in Raleigh, North Carolina.[^o5][^o9]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Thomas was responsible for multiple advances in physics. The Thomas precession is a correction to the atomic [spin-orbit interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin-orbit_interaction) in [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), which takes into account the [relativistic](wikipedia://en/26962) [time dilation](wikipedia://en/297839) between the [electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron) and the [atomic nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus). The Thomas–Fermi model is a statistical model for electron-[ion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion) interactions, which later formed the basis of [density functional theory](wikipedia://en/209874). The Thomas collapse is effect in [few-body physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Few-body_systems), which corresponds to infinite value of the three body [binding energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binding_energy) for zero-range potentials.

In mathematics, his name is frequently attached to an efficient [Gaussian elimination](wikipedia://en/13035) method for [tridiagonal matrices](wikipedia://en/519218)—the Thomas algorithm.
