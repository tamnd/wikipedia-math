# Parshin's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Parshin's conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **Parshin's conjecture** (also referred to as the **Beilinson–Parshin conjecture**) states that for any [smooth](wikipedia://en/7837041) [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) *X* defined over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), the higher [algebraic K-groups](wikipedia://en/598500) vanish up to torsion:[^o2]

:::

$$K_i(X) \otimes \mathbf Q = 0, \ \, i > 0.$$

It is named after [Aleksei Nikolaevich Parshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksei_Nikolaevich_Parshin) and [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613).

## Finite fields {#Finite_fields}

The conjecture holds if $dim\ X = 0$ by Quillen's computation of the K-groups of finite fields,[^o4] showing in particular that they are finite groups.

## Curves {#Curves}

The conjecture holds if $dim\ X = 1$ by the proof of Corollary 3.2.3 of Harder.[^o6] Additionally, by [Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519)'s finite generation result[^o7] (proving the [Bass conjecture](wikipedia://en/32485057) for the *K*-groups in this case) it follows that the *K*-groups are finite if $dim\ X = 1$.
