# Next-generation matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Next-generation matrix"}

In [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997), the **next-generation matrix** is used to derive the [basic reproduction number](wikipedia://en/917273), for a [compartmental model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compartmental_models_in_epidemiology) of the spread of [infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease). In [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466) it is used to compute the basic reproduction number for structured population models.[^o2] It is also used in multi-type branching models for analogous computations.[^o3]

:::

The method to compute the basic reproduction ratio using the next-generation matrix is given by Diekmann *et al.* (1990)[^o4] and van den Driessche and Watmough (2002).[^o5] To calculate the basic reproduction number by using a next-generation matrix, the whole population is divided into $n$ compartments in which there are $m<n$ infected compartments. Let $x_i, i=1,2,3,\ldots,m$ be the numbers of infected individuals in the $i^{th}$ infected compartment at time *t*. Now, the [epidemic model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic_model) is

$\frac{\mathrm{d} x_i}{\mathrm{d}t}= F_i (x)-V_i(x)$, where $V_i(x)= [V^-_i(x)-V^+_i(x)]$

In the above equations, $F_i(x)$ represents the rate of appearance of new infections in compartment $i$. $V^+_i$ represents the rate of transfer of individuals into compartment $i$ by all other means, and $V^-_i (x)$ represents the rate of transfer of individuals out of compartment $i$. The above model can also be written as

$$\frac{\mathrm{d} x}{\mathrm{d}t}= F(x)-V(x)$$

where

$$F(x) = \begin{pmatrix} F_1(x), & F_2(x), & \ldots, & F_m(x) \end{pmatrix}^T$$

and

$$V(x) = \begin{pmatrix} V_1(x), & V_2 (x), & \ldots, & V_m(x) \end{pmatrix}^T.$$

Let $x_0$ be the disease-free equilibrium. The values of the parts of the [Jacobian matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_matrix) $F(x)$ and $V(x)$ are:

$$DF(x_0) = \begin{pmatrix} F & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

and

$$DV(x_0) = \begin{pmatrix} V & 0 \\ J_3 & J_4 \end{pmatrix}$$

respectively.

Here, $F$ and $V$ are *m* × *m* matrices, defined as $F= \frac{\partial F_i}{\partial x_j}(x_0)$ and $V=\frac{\partial V_i}{\partial x_j}(x_0)$.

Now, the matrix $FV^{-1}$ is known as the next-generation matrix. The basic reproduction number of the model is then given by the [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429) of $FV^{-1}$ with the largest absolute value (the [spectral radius](wikipedia://en/386169) of $FV^{-1}$). Next generation matrices can be computationally evaluated from observational data, which is often the most productive approach where there are large numbers of compartments.[^o6]
