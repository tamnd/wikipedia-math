# Commuting probability

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Commuting probability"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and more precisely in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **commuting probability** (also called **degree of commutativity** or **commutativity degree**) of a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) is the [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) that two randomly chosen elements [commute](wikipedia://en/294390).[^o2][^o3] It can be used to measure how close to [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) a finite group is. It can be generalized to infinite [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) equipped with a suitable [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437),[^o15] and can also be generalized to other [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364) such as [rings](wikipedia://en/48404).[^o13]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $G$ be a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707). We define $p(G)$ as the averaged number of pairs of elements of $G$ which commute:

$$p(G) := \frac{1}{\# G^2} \#\!\left\{ (x,y) \in G^2 \mid xy=yx \right\}$$

where $\# X$ denotes the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of a finite set $X$.

If one considers the [uniform distribution](wikipedia://en/1699214) on $G^2$, $p(G)$ is the probability that two randomly chosen elements of $G$ commute. That is why $p(G)$ is called the **commuting probability** of $G$.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement cue=heading name="Results"}

## Results {#Results}

- The finite group $G$ is abelian [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $p(G) = 1$.
- One has

$$p(G) = \frac{k(G)}{\# G}$$

where $k(G)$ is the number of [conjugacy classes](wikipedia://en/49176) of $G$.

- If $G$ is not abelian then $p(G) \leq 5/8$ (this result is sometimes called the 5/8 theorem[^o8]) and this upper bound is sharp: there are infinitely many finite groups $G$ such that $p(G) = 5/8$, the smallest one being the [dihedral group of order 8](wikipedia://en/100267).
- There is no uniform lower bound on $p(G)$. In fact, for every positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $n$ there exists a finite group $G$ such that $p(G) = 1/n$.
- If $G$ is not abelian but [simple](wikipedia://en/47600), then $p(G) \leq 1/12$ (this upper bound is attained by $\mathfrak{A}_5$, the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) of degree 5).
- The set of commuting probabilities of finite groups is reverse-well-ordered, and the reverse of its order type is $\omega^\omega$.[^o9][^o10]

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

- The commuting probability can be defined for other [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364) such as [finite rings](wikipedia://en/23706953).[^o13] The 5/8 theorem also applies to finite rings.[^o14]
- The commuting probability can be defined for infinite [compact groups](wikipedia://en/750326); the probability measure is then, after a renormalisation, the [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645).[^o15]

:::
