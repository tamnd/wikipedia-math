# James A. Morrow

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James A. Morrow"}

**James Allen Morrow** (September 14, 1941 – April 2, 2024) was an American mathematician. His research interests shifted from [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) to [discrete inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_inverse) problems in the middle of his career.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Morrow was born in [Little Rock, Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rock,_Arkansas) and attended high school in [Dallas, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas,_Texas). In 1963, Morrow received a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.S.) degree from [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) (Caltech) in [Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena,_California), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California). In 1967, Morrow received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in [Palo Alto, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto,_California). His dissertation "The Topological Type of Non-Singular Deformations of Singular Surfaces" was written under the supervision of [Kunihiko Kodaira](wikipedia://en/646326). After teaching at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) for two years, Morrow joined the faculty of the University of Washington in [Seattle, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle,_Washington) as an assistant professor in 1969. He was promoted to associate professor in 1973 and to professor in 1978.

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

In 2005, Morrow received the Pacific Institute for the Mathematical Sciences (PIMS) Education Prize, which recognizes mathematicians who have "played a major role in encouraging activities which enhance public awareness and appreciation of mathematics, as well as fostering communication amongst the various groups and organizations concerned". In that same year he was awarded a University of Washington College on Arts and Sciences Alumni Distinguished Professor.

In 2006, Morrow received the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA) Distinguished Teaching Award of the Pacific Northwest Section of the MAA.

In 2008, Morrow received one of the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Awards for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics) from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).

In 2013, Morrow received the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) (AWM) M. Gweneth Humphreys Award for Mentorship of Undergraduate Women for his "outstanding achievements in inspiring undergraduate women to discover and pursue their passion for mathematics."

In 2018, Morrow was elected a Fellow of the Association for Women in Mathematics in the inaugural class.
