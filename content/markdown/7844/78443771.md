# Stable Yang–Mills–Higgs pair

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stable Yang–Mills–Higgs pair"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and especially [Yang–Mills theory](wikipedia://en/672202), a **(weakly) stable Yang–Mills–Higgs (YMH) pair** is a Yang–Mills–Higgs pair around which the Yang–Mills–Higgs action functional is positively or even strictly positively curved. Yang–Mills–Higgs pairs are solutions of the [Yang–Mills–Higgs equations](wikipedia://en/25723035) following from them being [local extrema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_extrema) of the [curvature](wikipedia://en/489440) of both fields, hence [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424) of the Yang–Mills-Higgs action functional, which are determined by a vanishing first [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of a [variation](wikipedia://en/171882). (Weakly) stable Yang–Mills-Higgs pairs furthermore have a positive or even strictly positive curved neighborhood and hence are determined by a positive or even strictly positive second derivative of a variation.

:::

(Weakly) stable Yang–Mills–Higgs pairs are named after [Yang Chen-Ning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Chen-Ning), [Robert Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mills_%28physicist%29) and [Peter Higgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Higgs).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $G$ be a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) with [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak{g}$ and $E\twoheadrightarrow B$ be a [principal $G$-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) with a compact [orientable](wikipedia://en/187446) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $B$ having a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) $g$ and a [volume form](wikipedia://en/1855811) $\operatorname{vol}_g$. Let $\operatorname{Ad}(E) :=E\times_G\mathfrak{g}$ be its [adjoint bundle](wikipedia://en/9943932). $\mathcal{A} =\Omega_{\operatorname{Ad}}^1(E,\mathfrak{g})$, an [affine vector space](wikipedia://en/298834) (not canonically) isomorphic to $\Omega^1(B,\operatorname{Ad}(E))$, is the space of [connections](wikipedia://en/470632).[^o4] These are under the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) $\operatorname{Ad}$ invariant $\mathfrak{g}$-valued ([Lie algebra–valued](wikipedia://en/2677220)) [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) on $E$ and through pullback along smooth sections $B\hookrightarrow E$ differ by $\operatorname{Ad}(E)$-valued ([vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524)–valued) differential forms on $B$.

The Yang–Mills–Higgs action functional is given by:[^o5]

$$\operatorname{YMH}\colon \Omega_{\operatorname{Ad}}^1(E,\mathfrak{g})\times\Gamma^\infty(B,\operatorname{Ad}(E))\rightarrow\mathbb{R}, \operatorname{YMH}(A,\Phi) :=\int_B\|F_A\|^2+\|\mathrm{d}_A\Phi\|^2\mathrm{d}\operatorname{vol}_g.$$

A Yang–Mills–Higgs pair $A\in\mathcal{A} =\Omega_{\operatorname{Ad}}^1(E,\mathfrak{g})$ and $\Phi\in\Gamma^\infty(B,\operatorname{Ad}(E))$, hence which fulfill the [Yang–Mills–Higgs equations](wikipedia://en/25723035), is called stable if:[^o6][^o7][^o8]

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}t^2}\operatorname{YMH}(\alpha(t),\varphi(t))\vert_{t=0}>0$$

for every smooth family $\alpha\colon (-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\rightarrow\mathcal{A} =\Omega_{\operatorname{Ad}}^1(E,\mathfrak{g})$ with $\alpha(0)=A$ and $\varphi\colon (-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\rightarrow\Gamma^\infty(B,\operatorname{Ad}(E))$ with $\varphi(0)=\Phi$. It is called weakly stable if only $\geq 0$ holds. A Yang–Mills–Higgs pair, which is not weakly stable, is called instable. For comparison, the condition to be a Yang–Mills–Higgs pair is:

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\operatorname{YMH}(\alpha(t),\varphi(t))\vert_{t=0}=0.$$

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Let $(A,\Phi)$ be a weakly stable Yang–Mills–Higgs pair on $S^n$, then the following claims hold:[^o8]
  - If $n=4$, then $A$ is a Yang–Mills connection ($\mathrm{d}_A\star F_A=0$) as well as $\mathrm{d}_A\Phi=0$ and $\|\Phi\|=1$.
  - If $n\geq 5$, then $A$ is flat ($F_A=0$) as well as $\mathrm{d}_A\Phi=0$ and $\|\Phi\|=1$.
