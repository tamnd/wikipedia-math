# Bundle metric

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bundle metric"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the notion of a [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) can be extended to an arbitrary [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524), and to some [principal fiber bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_fiber_bundle). This metric is often called a **bundle metric**, or **fibre metric**.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If *M* is a [topological manifold](wikipedia://en/2119193) and $\pi$ : *E* → *M* a vector bundle on *M*, then a metric on *E* is a [bundle map](wikipedia://en/7220589) *k* : *E* ×<sub>*M*</sub> *E* → *M* × **R** from the [fiber product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_product) of *E* with itself to the [trivial bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_bundle) with fiber **R** such that the restriction of *k* to each fibre over *M* is a [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate) [bilinear map](wikipedia://en/4365) of [vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_spaces).[^o4] Roughly speaking, *k* gives a kind of [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) (not necessarily symmetric or positive definite) on the vector space above each point of *M*, and these products vary smoothly over *M*.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Every vector bundle with paracompact base space can be equipped with a bundle metric.[^o4] For a vector bundle of rank *n*, this follows from the [bundle charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_trivialization) $\phi:\pi^{-1}(U)\to U\times\mathbb{R}^n$: the bundle metric can be taken as the pullback of the [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) of a metric on $\mathbb{R}^n$; for example, the orthonormal charts of Euclidean space. The [structure group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_group) of such a metric is the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) *O*(*n*).

:::

## Example: Riemann metric {#Example:_Riemann_metric}

If *M* is a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652), and *E* is its [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) T*M*, then the [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) gives a bundle metric, and vice versa.[^o4]

## Example: on vertical bundles {#Example:_on_vertical_bundles}

If the bundle $\pi$:*P* → *M* is a [principal fiber bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_fiber_bundle) with group *G*, and *G* is a [compact Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Lie_group), then there exists an Ad(*G*)-invariant inner product *k* on the fibers, taken from the inner product on the corresponding [compact Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/25262324). More precisely, there is a [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) *k* defined on the [vertical bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_bundle) E = V*P* such that *k* is invariant under left-multiplication:

$$k(L_{g*}X, L_{g*}Y)=k(X,Y)$$

for vertical vectors *X*, *Y* and *L*<sub>*g*</sub> is left-multiplication by *g* along the fiber, and *L*<sub>*g\**</sub> is the [pushforward](wikipedia://en/1558510). That is, *E* is the vector bundle that consists of the vertical subspace of the tangent of the principal bundle.

More generally, whenever one has a compact group with [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) μ, and an arbitrary [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) *h(X,Y)* defined at the tangent space of some point in *G*, one can define an invariant metric simply by averaging over the entire group, i.e. by defining

$$k(X,Y)=\int_G h(L_{g*} X, L_{g*} Y) d\mu_g$$

as the average.

The above notion can be extended to the [associated bundle](wikipedia://en/398874) $P\times_G V$ where *V* is a vector space transforming covariantly under some [representation](wikipedia://en/318712) of *G*.

## In relation to Kaluza–Klein theory {#In_relation_to_Kaluza–Klein_theory}

If the base space *M* is also a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018), with metric *g*, and the principal bundle is endowed with a [connection form](wikipedia://en/706412) ω, then $\pi$<sup>\*</sup>g+kω is a metric defined on the entire [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) *E* = T*P*.[^o10]

More precisely, one writes $\pi$<sup>\*</sup>g(*X*,*Y*) = *g*($\pi$<sub>\*</sub>*X*, $\pi$<sub>\*</sub>*Y*) where $\pi$<sub>\*</sub> is the [pushforward](wikipedia://en/1558510) of the projection $\pi$, and *g* is the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) on the base space *M*. The expression *kω* should be understood as (*kω*)(*X*,*Y*) = *k*(*ω*(*X*),*ω*(*Y*)), with *k* the metric tensor on each fiber. Here, *X* and *Y* are elements of the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) T*P*.

Observe that the lift $\pi$<sup>\*</sup>g vanishes on the [vertical subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_subspace) T*V* (since $\pi$<sub>\*</sub> vanishes on vertical vectors), while kω vanishes on the horizontal subspace T*H* (since the horizontal subspace is defined as that part of the tangent space T*P* on which the connection ω vanishes). Since the total tangent space of the bundle is a direct sum of the vertical and horizontal subspaces (that is, T*P* = T*V* ⊕ T*H*), this metric is well-defined on the entire bundle.

This bundle metric underpins the generalized form of [Kaluza–Klein theory](wikipedia://en/17070) due to several interesting properties that it possesses. The [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) derived from this metric is constant on each fiber,[^o10] this follows from the Ad(*G*) invariance of the fiber metric *k*. The scalar curvature on the bundle can be decomposed into three distinct pieces:

*R*<sub>*E*</sub> = *R*<sub>M</sub>(*g*) + *L*(*g*, ω) + *R*<sub>*G*</sub>(*k*)

where *R*<sub>*E*</sub> is the scalar curvature on the bundle as a whole (obtained from the metric $\pi$<sup>\*</sup>g+kω above), and *R*<sub>M</sub>(*g*) is the scalar curvature on the base manifold *M* (the [Lagrangian density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_density) of the [Einstein–Hilbert action](wikipedia://en/855776)), and *L*(*g*, ω) is the Lagrangian density for the [Yang–Mills action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang–Mills_action), and *R*<sub>*G*</sub>(*k*) is the scalar curvature on each fibre (obtained from the fiber metric *k*, and constant, due to the Ad(*G*)-invariance of the metric *k*). The arguments denote that *R*<sub>M</sub>(*g*) only depends on the metric *g* on the base manifold, but not ω or *k*, and likewise, that *R*<sub>*G*</sub>(*k*) only depends on *k*, and not on *g* or ω, and so-on.
