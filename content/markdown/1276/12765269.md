# Decision Linear assumption

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Decision Linear assumption"}

The **Decision Linear assumption** (**DLIN assumption**) is a [computational hardness assumption](wikipedia://en/6158383) used in [elliptic curve cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_cryptography). In particular, the DLIN assumption is useful in settings where the [decisional Diffie&ndash;Hellman assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decisional_Diffie&ndash;Hellman_assumption) does not hold (as is often the case in [pairing-based cryptography](wikipedia://en/8605953)). The Decision Linear assumption was introduced by [Boneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Boneh), Boyen, and Shacham.[^o2]

:::

Informally the DLIN assumption states that given $(u, \, v, \, h, \, u^x, \, v^y)$, with $u, \, v, \, h$ random group elements and $x, \, y$ random exponents, it is hard to distinguish $h^{x+y}$ from an independent random group element $\eta$.

## Motivation {#Motivation}

In symmetric [pairing-based cryptography](wikipedia://en/8605953) the group $G$ is equipped with a pairing $e :G \times G \to T$ which is [bilinear](wikipedia://en/4365). This map gives an efficient algorithm to solve the [decisional Diffie-Hellman](wikipedia://en/1198015) problem.[^o4] Given input $(g, \, g^a, \, g^b, \, h)$, it is easy to check if $h$ is equal to $g^{ab}$. This follows by using the pairing: note that

$$e(g^a, g^b) = e(g,g)^{ab} = e(g,g^{ab}).$$

Thus, if $h = g^{ab}$, then the values $e(g^a, g^b)$ and $e(g,h)$ will be equal.

Since this cryptographic assumption, essential to building [ElGamal encryption](wikipedia://en/59458) and [signatures](wikipedia://en/2033473), does not hold in this case, new assumptions are needed to build cryptography in symmetric bilinear groups. The DLIN assumption is a modification of Diffie-Hellman type assumptions to thwart the above attack.

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $G$ be a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) [order](wikipedia://en/493527) $p$. Let $u$, $v$, and $h$ be uniformly random [generators](wikipedia://en/99945) of $G$. Let $a,b$ be uniformly random elements of $\{1, \, 2, \, \dots, \, p-1\}$. Define a distribution

$$D_1 = (u, \, v, \, h, \, u^a, \, v^b, \, h^{a+b}).$$

Let $\eta$ be another uniformly random element of $G$. Define another distribution

$$D_2 = (u, \, v, \, h, \, u^a, \, v^b, \, \eta).$$

The Decision Linear assumption states that $D_1$ and $D_2$ are [computationally indistinguishable](wikipedia://en/1467815).

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

### Linear encryption {#Linear_encryption}

Boneh, Boyen, and Shacham define a [public key encryption](wikipedia://en/24222) scheme by analogy to ElGamal encryption.[^o2] In this scheme, a public key is the generators $u,v,h$. The private key is two exponents such that $u^x = v^y = h$. Encryption combines a message $m \in G$ with the public key to create a ciphertext

$$c := (c_1, \, c_2, \, c_3) = (u^a, \, v^b, \, m \cdot h^{a+b}).$$

To decrypt the ciphertext, the private key can be used to compute

$$m' := c_3 \cdot (c_1^x \cdot c_2^y)^{-1}.$$

To check that this encryption scheme is [correct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correctness_%28computer_science%29), i.e. $m' = m$ when both parties follow the protocol, note that

$$m' = c_3 \cdot (c_1^x \cdot c_2^y)^{-1} = m \cdot h^{a+b} \cdot ((u^a)^x \cdot (v^b)^y)^{-1} = m \cdot h^{a+b} \cdot ((u^x)^a \cdot (v^y)^b)^{-1}.$$

Then using the fact that $u^x = v^y = h$ yields

$$m' = m \cdot h^{a+b} \cdot (h^a \cdot h^b)^{-1} = m \cdot (h^{a+b} \cdot h^{-a-b}) = m.$$

Further, this scheme is [IND-CPA](wikipedia://en/2187252) [secure](wikipedia://en/1182348) assuming that the DLIN assumption holds.

### Short group signatures {#Short_group_signatures}

Boneh, Boyen, and Shacham also use DLIN in a scheme for [group signatures](wikipedia://en/3347478).[^o2] The signatures are called "short group signatures" because, with a standard [security level](wikipedia://en/52729212), they can be represented in only 250 [bytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byte).

Their protocol first uses linear encryption in order to define a special type of [zero-knowledge proof](wikipedia://en/450541). Then the [Fiat–Shamir heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat–Shamir_heuristic) is applied to transform the proof system into a [digital signature](wikipedia://en/59644). They prove this signature fulfills the additional requirements of unforgeability, anonymity, and traceability required of a group signature.

Their proof relies on not only the DLIN assumption but also another assumption called the [$q$-strong Diffie–Hellman assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_Diffie–Hellman_assumption). It is proven in the [random oracle model](wikipedia://en/451286).

### Other applications {#Other_applications}

Since its definition in 2004, the Decision Linear assumption has seen a variety of other applications. These include the construction of a [pseudorandom function](wikipedia://en/3454616) that generalizes the [Naor–Reingold construction](wikipedia://en/30578523),[^o12] an [attribute-based encryption](wikipedia://en/38366406) scheme,[^o13] and a special class of [non-interactive zero-knowledge proofs](wikipedia://en/11589052).[^o14]
