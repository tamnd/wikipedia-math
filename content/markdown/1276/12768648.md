# Apeirotope

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Apeirotope"}

*Image: Tiling 3 alternating dual.svg.* The regular hexagonal tiling is an example of a 3-dimensional apeirotope

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **apeirotope** or **infinite polytope** is a generalized [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) which has infinitely many [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Abstract apeirotope"}

### Abstract apeirotope {#Abstract_apeirotope}

An [abstract *n*-polytope](wikipedia://en/1211056) is a [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) *P* (whose elements are called *faces*) such that *P* contains a least face and a greatest face, each maximal totally ordered subset (called a *flag*) contains exactly *n* + 2 faces, *P* is strongly connected, and there are exactly two faces that lie strictly between *a* and *b* are two faces whose ranks differ by two.[^n1][^n2] An abstract polytope is called an **abstract apeirotope** if it has infinitely many faces.[^n3]

An abstract polytope is called *regular* if its automorphism group Γ(*P*) acts transitively on all of the flags of *P*.[^n4]

:::

:::

## Classification {#Classification}

There are two main geometric classes of apeirotope:[^n5]

- [honeycombs](wikipedia://en/6008654) in *n* dimensions, which completely fill an [*n*-dimensional space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-dimensional_space).
- skew apeirotopes, comprising an *n*-dimensional manifold in a higher space

### Honeycombs {#Honeycombs}

In general, a honeycomb in *n* dimensions is an infinite example of a polytope in *n* + 1 dimensions.

Tilings of the plane and close-packed space-fillings of polyhedra are examples of honeycombs in two and three dimensions respectively.

A line divided into infinitely many finite segments is an example of an [apeirogon](wikipedia://en/5658261).

::: {#o14 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Skew apeirotope"}

### Skew apeirotopes {#Skew_apeirotopes}

#### Skew apeirogons {#Skew_apeirogons}

A skew apeirogon in two dimensions forms a zig-zag line in the plane. If the zig-zag is even and symmetrical, then the apeirogon is regular.

Skew apeirogons can be constructed in any number of dimensions. In three dimensions, a regular [skew apeirogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skew_apeirogon#Helical_apeirogons_in_3-dimensions) traces out a helical spiral and may be either [left- or right-handed](wikipedia://en/32703814).

#### Infinite skew polyhedra {#Infinite_skew_polyhedra}

There are three regular skew apeirohedra, which look rather like polyhedral sponges:

- 6 squares around each vertex, Coxeter symbol {4,6|4}
- 4 hexagons around each vertex, Coxeter symbol {6,4|4}
- 6 hexagons around each vertex, Coxeter symbol {6,6|3}

There are thirty regular apeirohedra in Euclidean space.[^o17] These include those listed above, as well as (in the plane) polytopes of type: {∞,3}, {∞,4}, {∞,6} and in 3-dimensional space, blends of these with either an apeirogon or a line segment, and the "pure" 3-dimensional apeirohedra (12 in number)

:::
