# Robinson's joint consistency theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robinson's joint consistency theorem"}

**Robinson's joint consistency theorem** is an important theorem of [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). It is related to [Craig interpolation](wikipedia://en/2056790) and [Beth definability](wikipedia://en/12923821).

:::

The classical formulation of Robinson's joint [consistency](wikipedia://en/75802) theorem is as follows:

Let $T_1$ and $T_2$ be [first-order](wikipedia://en/10983) [theories](wikipedia://en/3512103). If $T_1$ and $T_2$ are [consistent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistent) and the intersection $T_1 \cap T_2$ is [complete](wikipedia://en/10086335) (in the common language of $T_1$ and $T_2$), then the union $T_1 \cup T_2$ is consistent. A theory $T$ is called [complete](wikipedia://en/10086335) if it decides every formula, meaning that for every [sentence](wikipedia://en/2129527) $\varphi,$ the theory contains the sentence or its negation but not both (that is, either $T \vdash \varphi$ or $T \vdash \neg \varphi$).

Since the completeness assumption is quite hard to fulfill, there is a variant of the theorem:

Let $T_1$ and $T_2$ be [first-order](wikipedia://en/10983) theories. If $T_1$ and $T_2$ are consistent and if there is no formula $\varphi$ in the common language of $T_1$ and $T_2$ such that $T_1 \vdash \varphi$ and $T_2 \vdash \neg \varphi,$ then the union $T_1\cup T_2$ is consistent.

::: {#o3 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

For a first-order language $L$ and a set $S$, let $L_S$ denote the language obtained from $L$ by adding 0-ary operations bijectively corresponding to the set $S$. For a first-order language $L$, an $L$-structure $M$ and a subset $S\subseteq M$, let $M_S$ denote the $L_S$-structure obtained from $M$ by interpreting each 0-ary operation $s\in S$ as $s$ itself. For a first-order language $L$, its expansion $L'$ and an $L'$-structure $M$, let $M\upharpoonright L$ denote the reduct of $M$ in $L$.

Let $L$ be a first-order language and $L_1$ and $L_2$ its expansions such that $L_1\cap L_2=L$. Let $T_1$ be a consistent theory in $L_1$ and $T_2$ a consistent theory in $L_2$. Suppose that $T_1\cap T_2$ is a complete theory in $L$. We shall show that $T_1\cup T_2$ is a consistent theory.

Using the [compactness theorem](wikipedia://en/152207), it is not hard to show that, for every $L_1$-structure $M$ and an $L_2$-structure $N$, if $M\upharpoonright L$ is [elementarily equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementarily_equivalent) to $N\upharpoonright L$, then the theory

$$\operatorname{Th}(M_M)\cup\operatorname{Th}((N\upharpoonright L)_N)$$

is [satisfiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satisfiable_theory).

For an $L_1$-structure $M$ and an $L_2$-structure $N$, if $M\upharpoonright L$ is elementarily equivalent to $N\upharpoonright L$, then for some $L_1$-structure $M'$, there exist an $L_1$-[elementary embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_embedding) $M\hookrightarrow M'$ and an $L$-elementary embedding $N\upharpoonright L\hookrightarrow M'\upharpoonright L$. Indeed, take an $(L_1)_{M,N}$-model

$$M''\models\operatorname{Th}(M_M)\cup\operatorname{Th}((N\upharpoonright L)_N)$$

and put $M'=M''\upharpoonright L_1$.

Now take a model $M_0\models T_1$ and a model $N_0\models T_2$. Since $T_1\cap T_2$ is an $L$-complete theory, we have $T_1\cap T_2=\operatorname{Th}(M_0\upharpoonright L)=\operatorname{Th}(N_0\upharpoonright L)$. Therefore, there exist an $L_2$-structure $N_1$, an $L_2$-elementary embedding $N_0\hookrightarrow N_1$ and an $L$-elementary embedding $M_0\upharpoonright L\hookrightarrow N_1\upharpoonright L$. Again, since $(L_1)_{M_0}\cap(L_2)_{M_0}=L_{M_0}$ and $(M_0\upharpoonright L)_{M_0}$ is elementarily equivalent to $(N_1\upharpoonright L)_{M_0}$, there exist an $(L_1)_{M_0}$-structure $(M_1)_{M_0}$, $(L_1)_{M_0}$-elementary embedding $(M_0)_{M_0}\hookrightarrow(M_1)_{M_0}$ and an $L_{M_0}$-elementary embedding $(N_1\upharpoonright L)_{M_0}\hookrightarrow(M_1\upharpoonright L)_{M_0}$. In particular, there exist an $L_2$-structure $M_1$, an $L_1$-elementary embedding $M_0\hookrightarrow M_1$ and an $L$-elementary embedding $N_1\upharpoonright L\hookrightarrow M_1\upharpoonright L$.

By induction, there exist $L_1$-structures $M_0,M_1,\dots$, $L_2$-structures $N_0,N_1,\dots$ and elementary embeddings

$$M_i\hookrightarrow M_{i+1}$$

$$N_i\hookrightarrow N_{i+1}$$

$$M_i\upharpoonright L\hookrightarrow N_{i+1}\upharpoonright L$$

$$N_{i+1}\upharpoonright L\hookrightarrow M_{i+1}\upharpoonright L$$

which make the following diagram commute.

$$\begin{matrix} M_0 & \to & M_1 & \to & M_2 & \to & \cdots \\ & \searrow & \uparrow & \searrow & \uparrow & \searrow & \cdots \\ N_0 & \to & N_1 & \to & N_2 & \to & \cdots \end{matrix}$$

(In the above diagram, the horizontal arrows are elementary embeddings in $L_1$ or $L_2$, and the vertical and diagonal arrows are elementary embeddings in $L$.) Finally, the [colimit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit) of the above diagram is easily seen to be a model of $T_1\cup T_2$.

:::
