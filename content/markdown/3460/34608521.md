# Vivid designator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vivid designator"}

In [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) and the [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language), a **vivid designator** is a term which is *believed* to designate the same thing in all [possible worlds](wikipedia://en/780566)[^o2] and nothing else where such an object does not exist in a possible world. It is the analogue, in the sense of believing, of a [rigid designator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_designator),[^o3] which *is* ([refers to](wikipedia://en/20110874)) the same in all possible worlds, rather than just *believed* to be so.

:::

## Willard Van Orman Quine {#Willard_Van_Orman_Quine}

[Willard Van Orman Quine](wikipedia://en/33833) credits [David Kaplan](wikipedia://en/1479115) (who in turn credits [Montgomery Furth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_Furth)) for the term "vivid designator" in his 1977 paper "Intensions Revisited". He examines the separation between [*de re* and *de dicto*](wikipedia://en/530256) and does away with *de re* statements, because *de re* statements can only work for names that are used [referentially](wikipedia://en/20110874).[^o5] In fact, both [rigid designators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_designators) and vivid designators are similarly dependent on context and empty otherwise. The same is true of the whole [quantified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantification_%28logic%29) [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) of necessity because it collapses if [essence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essence) is withdrawn.[^o6]
