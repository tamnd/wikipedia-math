# Numerical error

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Numerical error"}

**Numerical error** is the error that arises in [numerical computations](wikipedia://en/21506) due to limitations in representing real numbers and performing arithmetic operation on computers. These errors commonly appear in [software engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_engineering) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).

Parameter m= 2.0, initial point is random.

:::

## Types {#Types}

Numerical error in [computations](wikipedia://en/5926) generally arises from several sources. The most common sources are [round-off error](wikipedia://en/432450) and [truncation error](wikipedia://en/9366097).[^o3]

It can be the combined effect of two kinds of error in a calculation. The first is referred to as [round-off error](wikipedia://en/432450) and is caused by the finite [precision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_%28computer_science%29) of computations involving [floating-point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating-point) numbers. The second, usually called [truncation error](wikipedia://en/9366097), is the difference between the exact mathematical solution and the approximate solution obtained when simplifications are made to the mathematical equations to make them more amenable to calculation.

## Measure {#Measure}

Floating-point numerical error is often measured in ULP ([unit in the last place](wikipedia://en/1498076)), which represents the distance between two adjacent [floating-point](wikipedia://en/11376) numbers.[^o5]
