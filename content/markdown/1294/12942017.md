# CAT(0) group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CAT(0) group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **CAT(0) group** is a [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [group](wikipedia://en/19447) with a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on a [CAT(0) space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_space) that is geometrically proper, [cocompact](wikipedia://en/12746615), and [isometric](wikipedia://en/254777). They form a possible notion of non-positively curved group in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $G$ be a group. Then $G$ is said to be a **CAT(0) group** if there exists a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) $X$ and an [action](wikipedia://en/12781) of $G$ on $X$ such that:

1. $X$ is a [CAT(0) metric space](wikipedia://en/12941912)
1. The action of $G$ on $X$ is by [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777), i.e. it is a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) $G \longrightarrow \mathrm{Isom}(X)$
1. The action of $G$ on $X$ is geometrically proper (see below)
1. The action is **cocompact**: there exists a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) subset $K\subset X$ whose translates under $G$ together cover $X$, i.e. $X = G\cdot K = \bigcup_{g\in G} g\cdot K$

An group action on a metric space satisfying conditions 2 - 4 is sometimes called *geometric*.

This definition is analogous to one of the many possible definitions of a [Gromov-hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/3172817), where the condition that $X$ is CAT(0) is replaced with [Gromov-hyperbolicity](wikipedia://en/1285375) of $X$. However, contrarily to hyperbolicity, CAT(0)-ness of a space is not a quasi-isometry invariant, which makes the theory of CAT(0) groups a lot harder.

### CAT(0) space {#CAT(0)_space}

### Metric properness {#Metric_properness}

The suitable notion of properness for actions by isometries on metric spaces differs slightly from that of a [properly discontinuous action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Properly_discontinuous_action) in topology.[^o6] An isometric action of a group $G$ on a metric space $X$ is said to be **geometrically proper** if, for every $\ce{x\in X}$, there exists $r > 0$ such that $\{g\in G | B(x, r)\cap g\cdot B(x, r) \neq \emptyset\}$is finite.

Since a compact subset $K$ of $X$ can be covered by finitely many balls $B(x_i, r_i)$ such that $B(x_i, 2r_i)$ has the above property, metric properness implies proper discontinuity. However, metric properness is a stronger condition in general. The two notions coincide for [proper metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_metric_space).

If a group $G$ acts (geometrically) properly and cocompactly by isometries on a [length space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Length_space) $X$, then $X$ is actually a proper [geodesic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_space) space (see [metric Hopf-Rinow theorem](wikipedia://en/681265#Variations_and_generalizations)), and $G$ is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) (see [Švarc-Milnor lemma](wikipedia://en/51457085)). In particular, CAT(0) groups are finitely generated, and the space $X$ involved in the definition is actually proper.

## Examples {#Examples}

### CAT(0) groups {#CAT(0)_groups}

- Finite groups are trivially CAT(0), and [finitely generated abelian groups](wikipedia://en/58527) are CAT(0) by acting on euclidean spaces.
- [Crystallographic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallographic_group)
- [Fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004) of compact [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) having non-positive [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) are CAT(0) thanks to their action on the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover), which is a [Cartan-Hadamard manifold](wikipedia://en/12471936).
- More generally, fundamental groups of compact, locally CAT(0) metric spaces are CAT(0) groups, as a consequence of the [metric Cartan-Hadamard theorem](wikipedia://en/11939373#Metric_geometry). This includes groups whose Dehn complex can wear a piecewise-euclidean metric of non-positive curvature. Examples of these are provided by presentations satisfying [small cancellation](wikipedia://en/19882883) conditions.[^o10]
- Any finitely presented group is a [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) of a CAT(0) group (in fact, of a fundamental group of a 2-dimensional CAT(-1) complex) with finitely generated kernel.[^o10]
- [Free products](wikipedia://en/360036) of CAT(0) groups and [free amalgamated products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_products_with_amalgamation) of CAT(0) groups over finite or infinite cyclic subgroups are CAT(0).[^o11]
- [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004) are CAT(0), and act properly cocompactly on CAT(0) [cube complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_complex).[^o12]
- [Fundamental groups of hyperbolic knot complements](wikipedia://en/1164126).[^o10]
- $\mathrm{Aut}(F_2)$, the [automorphism group of the free group](wikipedia://en/31079483) of rank 2, is CAT(0).[^o13]
- The [braid groups](wikipedia://en/550138) $B_n$, for $n\le 6$, are known to be CAT(0). It is conjectured that all braid groups are CAT(0).[^o14]
- [Limit groups](wikipedia://en/78938535) over free groups are CAT(0) with isolated flats.[^o15]

### Non-CAT(0) groups {#Non-CAT(0)_groups}

- [Mapping class groups](wikipedia://en/31060586) of closed surfaces with genus $\ge 3$, or surfaces with genus $\ge 2$ and nonempty boundary or at least two punctures, are not CAT(0).[^o17]
- Some [free-by-cyclic](wikipedia://en/10377468) groups cannot act properly by isometries on a CAT(0) space,[^o18] although they have quadratic isoperimetric inequality.[^o19]
- [Automorphism groups of free groups](wikipedia://en/31079483) of rank $\ge 3$ have exponential Dehn function, and hence (see below) are not CAT(0).[^o20]

## Properties {#Properties}

### Properties of the group {#Properties_of_the_group}

Let $G$ be a CAT(0) group. Then:

- There are finitely many [conjugacy classes](wikipedia://en/49176#Conjugacy_of_subgroups_and_general_subsets) of finite subgroups in $G$.[^o24] In particular, there is a bound for [cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_%28mathematics%29) of finite subgroups of $G$.
- The solvable subgroup theorem: any [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012) subgroup of $G$ is finitely generated and [virtually](wikipedia://en/10377971) [free abelian](wikipedia://en/247296). Moreover, there is a finite bound on the [rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rank_of_a_free_module) of free abelian subgroups of $G$.[^o17]
- If $G$ is infinite, then $G$ contains an element of infinite order.[^o25]
- If $A$ is a free abelian subgroup of $G$ and $C$ is a finitely generated subgroup of $G$ containing $A$ in its [center](wikipedia://en/7125), then a finite index subgroup $D$ of $C$ splits as a direct product $D \cong A\times B$.[^o26]
- The [Dehn function](wikipedia://en/20017026) of $G$ is at most quadratic.[^o27]
- $G$ has a [finite presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group) with solvable [word problem](wikipedia://en/33563) and [conjugacy problem](wikipedia://en/4942384).[^o27]

### Properties of the action {#Properties_of_the_action}

Let $G$ be a group acting properly cocompactly by isometries on a CAT(0) space $X$.

- Any finite subgroup of $G$ fixes a nonempty closed convex set.
- For any infinite order element $g\in G$, the set $\min(g)$ of elements $x\in X$ such that $d(g\cdot x, x) > 0$ is minimal is a nonempty, closed, convex, $g$-invariant subset of $X$, called the *minimal set* of $g$. Moreover, it splits isometrically as a (l²) [direct product](wikipedia://en/6812823) $\min(g) = A\times \mathbb{R}$ of a closed convex set $A\subset X$ and a geodesic line, in such a way that $g$ acts trivially on the $A$ factor and by translation on the $\mathbb{R}$ factor. A geodesic line on which $g$ acts by translation is always of the form $\{a\}\times \mathbb{R}$, $a\in A$, and is called an *axis* of $g$. Such an element is called *hyperbolic*.
- The flat torus theorem: any free abelian subgroup $\mathbb{Z}^n \cong A \subset G$ leaves invariant a subspace $F\subset X$ isometric to $\mathbb{R}^n$, and $A$ acts cocompactly on $F$ (hence the quotient $F/A$ is a [flat torus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_torus)).[^o17]
- In certain situations, a splitting of $G \cong G_1\times G_2$ as a cartesian product induces a splitting of the space $X\cong X_1\times X_2$ and of the action.[^o26]
