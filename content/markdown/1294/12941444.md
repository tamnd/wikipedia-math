# Local flatness

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local flatness"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **local flatness** is a smoothness condition that can be imposed on topological [submanifolds](wikipedia://en/525095). In the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of topological manifolds, locally flat submanifolds play a role similar to that of [embedded submanifolds](wikipedia://en/525095#Embedded_submanifolds) in the category of [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifolds). Violations of local flatness describe ridge networks and [crumpled structures](wikipedia://en/53133381), with applications to materials processing and [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Suppose a *d* dimensional manifold *N* is embedded into an *n* dimensional manifold *M* (where *d* \< *n*). If $x \in N,$ we say *N* is **locally flat** at *x* if there is a neighborhood $U \subset M$ of *x* such that the [topological pair](wikipedia://en/19067071) $(U, U\cap N)$ is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the pair $(\mathbb{R}^n,\mathbb{R}^d)$, with the standard inclusion of $\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^n.$ That is, there exists a homeomorphism $U\to \mathbb{R}^n$ such that the [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of $U\cap N$ coincides with $\mathbb{R}^d$. In diagrammatic terms, the following [square must commute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuting_square):

*Image: Locally flat.svg.*

We call *N* **locally flat** in *M* if *N* is locally flat at every point. Similarly, a map $\chi\colon N\to M$ is called **locally flat**, even if it is not an embedding, if every *x* in *N* has a neighborhood *U* whose image $\chi(U)$ is locally flat in *M*.

### In manifolds with boundary {#In_manifolds_with_boundary}

The above definition assumes that, if *M* has a [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824), *x* is not a boundary point of *M*. If *x* is a point on the boundary of *M* then the definition is modified as follows. We say that *N* is **locally flat** at a boundary point *x* of *M* if there is a neighborhood $U\subset M$ of *x* such that the topological pair $(U, U\cap N)$ is homeomorphic to the pair $(\mathbb{R}^n_+,\mathbb{R}^d)$, where $\mathbb{R}^n_+$ is a standard [half-space](wikipedia://en/647297) and $\mathbb{R}^d$ is included as a standard subspace of its boundary.

:::

## Consequences {#Consequences}

Local flatness of an embedding implies strong properties not shared by all embeddings. Brown (1962) proved that if *d* = *n* − 1, then *N* is collared; that is, it has a neighborhood which is homeomorphic to *N* × \[0,1\] with *N* itself corresponding to *N* × 1/2 (if *N* is in the interior of *M*) or *N* × 0 (if *N* is in the boundary of *M*).

::: {#o7 .example kind=example/counterexample cue=heading name="Non-example"}

## Non-example {#Non-example}

Let $K$ be a non-trivial knot in $S^3$; that is, a connected, locally flat one-dimensional submanifold of $S^3$ such that the pair $(S^3, K)$ is not homeomorphic to $(S^3, S^1)$. Then the cone on $K$ from the center $\underline{0}$ of $D^4$ is a submanifold of $D^4$, but it is not locally flat at $\underline{0}$.[^o8]

:::
