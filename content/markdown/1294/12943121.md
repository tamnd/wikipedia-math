# Hindu units of time

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hindu units of time"}

**Hindu units of time** are described in [Hindu texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_texts) ranging from microseconds to trillions of years, including cycles of cosmic time that repeat general events in [Hindu cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_cosmology).[^o2][^o3] Time ([kāla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaal)) is described as eternal.[^n1] Various fragments of time are described in the [Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas), *[Manusmriti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manusmriti)*, *[Bhagavata Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavata_Purana)*, *[Vishnu Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu_Purana)*, *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata)*, *[Surya Siddhanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surya_Siddhanta)* etc.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

## Sidereal metrics {#Sidereal_metrics}

HinduMeasurements.svg

Sidereal astrology maintains the alignment between signs and [constellations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation) via corrective systems of [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) ([Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas))-origin known as [ayanamsas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayanamsa) (Sanskrit: *'ayana'* "movement" + *'aṃśa**"component"), to allow for the observed [precession of equinoxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precession_of_equinoxes), whereas tropical astrology ignores precession.[^o9] This has caused the two systems, which were aligned around 2,000 years ago, to drift apart over the centuries.[^o9][^o10]***

Ayanamsa systems used in Hindu astrology (also known as [Vedic astrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas)) include the Lahiriayanamsa and the Raman ayanamsa.[^o11] The Fagan-Bradley ayanamsa is an example of an ayanamsa system used in Western sidereal astrology.[^o11] As of 2020, zodiacal signs calculated using the Sri Yukteswar ayanamsa were around 23 degrees behind tropical zodiacal signs.[^o9] Per these calculations, persons born between March 12 - April 12, for instance, would have the sun sign of Pisces.[^o9] By contrast, persons born between March 21 - April 19 would have the sun sign of Aries per tropical calculations.[^o12]

## Lunar metrics {#Lunar_metrics}

The traditional lunar calendar system measures time based on the Moon's phases and its relation to the Sun. Unlike solar calendars, it uses units such as tithi (lunar day), pakṣa (lunar fortnight), māsa (lunar month), ṛitu (season), ayanam (half-year), and varsha (lunar year) to structure the year.[^o26] This system was integral to ancient cultures for tracking time, planning festivals, and guiding agricultural practices. The following section provides an overview of these key time units and their relationships:[^n5][^o28]

::: {#o29 .table cue=table}

| [pakṣa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paksha) ([lunar fortnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_fortnight)) | 15 *tithis* | ≈ 15 days |
| māsa ([lunar month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_month)) | 2 pakṣas: *gaura* (bright) or śukla pakṣa during [waxing moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_phase#Waxing_and_waning); kṛṣṇa (dark) pakṣa during [waning moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_phase#Waxing_and_waning) | ≈ 30 days (29.5 days) |

## Tropical metrics {#Tropical_metrics}

Tropical metrics are time units used to measure intervals based on the tropical year and related cycles. This system includes units such as ghaṭi (base unit), yāma (a period of 7.5 ghaṭis), and ahorātram (a full day comprising 8 yāmas).[^o35] The following section explains these units and their approximate durations, offering insight into how time is organized in relation to the tropical year:[^n6]

::: {#o37 .table cue=table}

| Unit | Definition | Equivalence |
|---|---|---|
| ghaṭi (घटि) | [base unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_unit_%28measurement%29) | ≈ 24 minutes |
| yāma (याम) | \7.5 ghaṭis | ≈ 3 hours |
| ahorātram (अहोरात्रम्) | 8 yāmas | ≈ 24 hours (tropical day starting at sunrise) |

### Lifespans {#Lifespans}

[Hindu texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_texts) define lifespans differently for [humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human), [Pitris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitrs) (forefathers), [Devas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deva_%28Hinduism%29) (gods), [Manus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manu_%28Hinduism%29) (progenitors of mankind), and [Brahma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma) (creator god). The division of a year for each is twelve 30-day months or 360 days, where a day is divided into a 12-hour day proper and 12-hour night.[^o49] A 30-day month amounts to four 7-day weeks with an extra 8th day every two weeks (48-week year). A traditional human year is measured by the sun's northern (*[uttarayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttarayana)*) and southern (*[dakshinayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshinayana)*) movements in the sky, where the new year commences only when the sun returns to the same starting point and a pause on the commencement otherwise. Ebenezer Burgess postulates an intercalary month was inserted every five years to anciently maintain the correspondence of the 360-day years with the true solar years (~365.24-day years).[^n7] For this reason, a traditional 360-day year is equivalent to a modern ~365.24-day solar or [tropical year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_year).

### Cosmic date {#Cosmic_date}

According to [Puranic sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purana), [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna)'s departure marks the end of the human age of *[Dvapara-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvapara_Yuga)* and the start of *[Kali-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Yuga)*, which is dated to midnight ([Ujjain meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_India#Ancient_India)) 17/18 February 3102 [BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era) ([proleptic Julian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proleptic_Julian_calendar)), corresponding to 22/23 January ([proleptic Gregorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proleptic_Gregorian_calendar)). We are currently halfway through [Brahma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma)'s life (*maha-kalpa*), whose lifespan is equal to the duration of the manifested material elements, from which Brahma manifests his universe in *[kalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpa_%28time%29)* cycles:[^o82][^n8][^n9][^o54][^o55]

- 51st year of 100 (2nd half or *parardha*)
- 1st month of 12
- 1st *[kalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpa_%28time%29)* (*Shveta-Varaha Kalpa*) of 30
- 7th *[manvantara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manvantara)* ([Vaivasvatha Manu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shraddhadeva_Manu)) of 14
- 28th *[chatur-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_Cycle)* of 71
- 4th *[yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga)* (*[Kali-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Yuga)*) of 4

A *maha-kalpa* is followed by a *[maha-pralaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pralaya)* (full dissolution) of equal length. Each *kalpa* (day of Brahma) is followed by a *[pralaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pralaya)* (night of Brahma or partial dissolution) of equal length. Preceding the first and following each *manvantara* is a *manvantara-sandhya* (connection period), each with a length of *Krita-yuga* ( *[Satya-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satya_Yuga)*).[^o82][^n8]

[Hindu texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_texts) specify that the start and end of each of the *yugas* are marked by astronomical alignments. This cycle's *[Treta-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treta_Yuga)* began with 5 planets residing in the "Aries" constellation. This cycle's *[Dvapara-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvapara_Yuga)* ended with the "Saptarshi" constellation (Ursa major) residing in the "Magha" constellation. The current *[Kali-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Yuga)* will end with the Sun, Moon and Jupiter residing in the "Pushya" sector.[^o57]

### Human {#Human}

The history of humanity is divided up into [four *yugas*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_Cycle) ( *[dharmic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharma#Hinduism)* ages or world ages)—Kṛta-yuga (pronounced *Krita-yuga*; *[Satya-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satya_Yuga)*), [Tretā-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treta_Yuga), [Dvāpara-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvapara_Yuga) and *[Kali-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Yuga)*—each with a decline in *dharmic* practices and length, giving proportions (caraṇas; pronounced *charanas*) of 4:3:2:1 (e.g. *Satya*: 100% start; *Kali*: 25% start, 0% end), indicating a [de-evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution_%28biology%29) in spiritual consciousness and an evolution in material consciousness. *Kali-yuga* is followed by *Satya-yuga* of the next cycle, where a cycle is called a [catur-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_Cycle) (pronounced *chatur-yuga*; *mahā-yuga*). Each *yuga* is divided into a main period ( *yuga* proper) and two *yuga-sandhis* ( yuga-sandhyās; connecting periods)⁠—yuga-sandhyā (dawn) and yuga-sandhyāṃśa ( yuga-sandhyānśa; dusk)⁠—where each *yuga-sandhi* lasts for 10% of the main period. Lengths are given in divine years ( celestial or [Deva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deva_%28Hinduism%29) years), where a divine year lasts for 360 [solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_year) (human) years. A *chatur-yuga* lasts for 4.32 million solar (12,000 divine) years with 1,728,000 years of *Krita-yuga*, 1,296,000 years of *Treta-yuga*, 864,000 years of *Dvapara-yuga*, and 432,000 years of *Kali-yuga*.[^n10][^o60][^o61][^o62][^o63]

### Pitri {#Pitri}

The lifespan of the [Pitris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitrs) (forefathers) lasts for 100 of their years.[^n6]

- 24 hours (1 day & night) of Pitris = 1 solar month (*masa*; Moon's two 15-day fortnights: bright and dark)
- 30 days (1 month) of Pitris = 30 solar months (2.5 solar years)
- 12 months (1 year) of Pitris = 30 solar years (1 month of [Devas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deva_%28Hinduism%29))
- 100 years (lifespan) of Pitris = 3,000 solar years

### Deva {#Deva}

The lifespan of the [Devas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deva_%28Hinduism%29) (gods) lasts for 100 of their years.[^n6]

- 24 hours (1 day & night) of Devas = 1 solar year (Sun's two 180-day motions: northern and southern)
- 30 days (1 month) of Devas = 30 solar years (1 year of [Pitris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitrs))
- 12 months (1 year) of Devas = 360 solar years
- 100 years (lifespan) of Devas = 36,000 solar years

### Manu {#Manu}

The lifespan of the [Manus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manu_%28Hinduism%29) (progenitors of mankind) lasts for 100 of their years. Each Manu reigns over a period called a *[manvantara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manvantara)*, each lasting for 71 *[chatur-yugas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_Cycle)* (306.72 million years). A total of 14 Manus reign successively in one *[kalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpa_%28time%29)* (day of [Brahma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma)). Preceding the first and following each *manvantara* is a *sandhyā* (connection period), each lasting the duration of *[Satya-yuga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satya_Yuga)* (1.728 million years). During each *manvantara-sandhyā* ( *manvantara-sandhi*), Earth (*[Bhu-loka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loka#Hinduism)*) is submerged in water.[^o82][^n10][^o84][^o85]

- 24 hours (1 day & night) of Manu = 8,520 solar years
- 30 days (1 month) of Manu = 255,600 solar years
- 12 months (1 year) of Manu = 3,067,200 solar years
- 100 years (lifespan) of Manu = 306,720,000 solar years (71 *[chatur-yugas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_Cycle)*)

### Brahma {#Brahma}

The lifespan of [Brahma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma) (creator god) lasts for 100 of his years. His 12-hour day or *[kalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpa_%28time%29)* ( day of Brahma) is followed by a 12-hour night or *[pralaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pralaya)* ( night of Brahma) of equal length, each lasting for 4.32 billion years. A *kalpa* lasts for 1,000 *[chatur-yugas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_Cycle)* and has 14 *[manvantaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manvantara)* and 15 *manvantara-sandhyas* occurring in it. At the start of Brahma's days, he is re-born and creates the planets and the first living entities. At the end of his days, he and his creations are unmanifest (partial dissolution). His 100-year life (311.04 trillion years) is called a mahā-kalpa, which is followed by a mahā-pralaya (full dissolution) of equal length, where the bases of the universe, *[prakriti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prakṛti)*, is manifest at the start and unmanifest at the end of a *maha-kalpa*. His 100-year life is divided into two 50-year periods, each called a parārdha.[^n8][^o85][^o89] In 100 360-day years (*maha-kalpa*), there are a total of 36,000 full days: 36,000 *kalpas* (days proper) and 36,000 *pralayas* (nights).

- 12 hours (1 day proper: *kalpa*) of Brahma = 4.32 billion solar years (1,000 *[chatur-yugas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuga_Cycle)*; 14 *[manvantaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manvantara)* + 15 *manvantara-sandhyas*)
- 24 hours (1 day & night: *kalpa* + *pralaya*) of Brahma = 8.64 billion solar years

## Hindu texts {#Hindu_texts}

### *Mahabharata* {#Mahabharata}

The *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata)* (12.231.12–31) describes units of time from a wink of the eye (*nimesha*) up to the days (*[kalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpa_%28time%29)*) and nights (*[pralaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pralaya)*) of [Brahma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma).[^o94]

(12–13) The Rishis, measuring time, have given particular names to particular portions. Five and ten winks of the eye make what is called a Kashtha. Thirty Kashthas make what is called a Kala. Thirty Kalas, with the tenth part of a Kala, make a Muhurta. Thirty Muhurtas make up one day and night. Thirty days and nights form a month, and twelve months form a year.

(14) Persons well-read in mathematical science say that a year is made up of two solar motions, viz, the northern and the southern.

(15) The sun makes the day and the night for men. The night is for the sleep of all living creatures and the day is for work.

(16) A month of human beings is equal to a day and night of the departed manes. That division consists in this the light half of the month is their day which is for work, and the dark fortnight is their night for sleep.

(17) A year (of men) is equal to a day and night of the gods. This division consists in this the half year for which the sun travels from the vernal to the autumnal equinox is the day of the gods, and the half year for which the sun moves from the latter to the former is their night.

(18) Calculating by the days and nights of human beings about which I have told you I shall speak of the day and night of Brahman \[(Brahma)\] and his years also.

(19) I shall, in their order, tell you the number of years that are for different purposes calculated differently, in the Krita, the Treta, the Dwapara, and the Kali yugas.

(20) Four thousand celestial years is the duration of the first or Krita age. The morning of that cycle consists of four hundred years and its evening is of four hundred years.

(21) Regarding the other cycles, the duration of each gradually decreases by a quarter in respect of both the principal period with the minor portion and the conjoining portion itself.

(29) The learned say that these twelve thousand celestial years form what is called a cycle. A thousand such cycles form a single day of Brahman \[(Brahma)\].

(30) The same is the duration of Brahman's \[(Brahma's)\] night. With the beginning of Brahman's \[(Brahma's)\] day the universe begins to come into being. During the period of universal dissolution the Creator sleeps in Yoga meditation. When the period of sleep expires, He awakes.

(31) What is Brahman's \[(Brahma's)\] day covers a thousand such cycles. His night also covers a thousand similar cycles. They who know this are said to know the day and the night.

### *Manusmriti* {#Manusmriti}

The *[Manusmriti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manusmriti)* ( *Laws of Manu*; 1.64–80) describes units of time from a twinkling of the eye (*nimesha*) up to the days (*[kalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalpa_%28time%29)*) and nights (*[pralaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pralaya)*) of [Brahma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma).[^o96][^o98][^o97]

निमेषा दश चाष्टौ च काष्ठा त्रिंशत् तु ताः कला । त्रिंशत् कला मुहूर्तः स्यादहोरात्रं तु तावतः ॥ ६४ ॥ sa (64) Eighteen nimeshas (twinklings of the eye, are one kashtha), thirty kashthas one kala, thirty kalas one muhurta, and as many (muhurtas) one day and night.

अहोरात्रे विभजते सूर्यो मानुषदैविके । रात्रिः स्वप्नाय भूतानां चेष्टायै कर्मणामहः ॥ ६५ ॥ sa (65) The sun divides days and nights, both human and divine, the night (being intended) for the repose of created beings and the day for exertion.

पित्र्ये रात्र्यहनी मासः प्रविभागस्तु पक्षयोः । कर्मचेष्टास्वहः कृष्णः शुक्लः स्वप्नाय शर्वरी ॥ ६६ ॥ sa (66) A month is a day and night of the manes, but the division is according to fortnights. The dark (fortnight) is their day for active exertion, the bright (fortnight) their night for sleep.

दैवे रात्र्यहनी वर्षं प्रविभागस्तयोः पुनः । अहस्तत्रोदगयनं रात्रिः स्याद् दक्षिणायनम् ॥ ६७ ॥ sa (67) A year is a day and a night of the gods; their division is (as follows): the half year during which the sun progresses to the north will be the day, that during which it goes southwards the night.

ब्राह्मस्य तु क्षपाहस्य यत् प्रमाणं समासतः । एकैकशो युगानां तु क्रमशस्तन्निबोधत ॥ ६८ ॥ sa (68) But hear now the brief (description of) the duration of a night and a day of Brahman \[(Brahma)\] and of the several ages (of the world, yuga) according to their order.

चत्वार्याहुः सहस्राणि वर्षाणां तत् कृतं युगम् । तस्य तावत्शती सन्ध्या सन्ध्यांशश्च तथाविधः ॥ ६९ ॥ sa (69) They declare that the Krita age (consists of) four thousand years (of the gods); the twilight preceding it consists of as many hundreds, and the twilight following it of the same number.

इतरेषु ससन्ध्येषु ससन्ध्यांशेषु च त्रिषु । एकापायेन वर्तन्ते सहस्राणि शतानि च ॥ ७० ॥ sa (70) In the other three ages with their twilights preceding and following, the thousands and hundreds are diminished by one (in each).

यदेतत् परिसङ्ख्यातमादावेव चतुर्युगम् । एतद् द्वादशसाहस्रं देवानां युगमुच्यते ॥ ७१ ॥ sa (71) These twelve thousand (years) which thus have been just mentioned as the total of four (human) ages, are called one age of the gods.

दैविकानां युगानां तु सहस्रं परिसङ्ख्यया । ब्राह्ममेकमहर्ज्ञेयं तावतीं रात्रिमेव च ॥ ७२ ॥ sa (72) But know that the sum of one thousand ages of the gods (makes) one day of Brahman \[(Brahma)\], and that his night has the same length.

तद् वै युगसहस्रान्तं ब्राह्मं पुण्यमहर्विदुः । रात्रिं च तावतीमेव तेऽहोरात्रविदो जनाः ॥ ७३ ॥ sa (73) Those (only, who) know that the holy day of Brahman \[(Brahma)\], indeed, ends after (the completion of) one thousand ages (of the gods) and that his night lasts as long, (are really) men acquainted with (the length of) days and nights.

यद् प्राग् द्वादशसाहस्रमुदितं दैविकं युगम् । तदेकसप्ततिगुणं मन्वन्तरमिहोच्यते ॥ ७९ ॥ sa (79) The before-mentioned age of the gods, (or) twelve thousand (of their years), being multiplied by seventy-one, (constitutes what) is here named the period of a Manu (Manvantara).

मन्वन्तराण्यसङ्ख्यानि सर्गः संहार एव च । क्रीडन्निवैतत् कुरुते परमेष्ठी पुनः पुनः ॥ ८० ॥ sa (80) The Manvantaras, the creations and destructions (of the world, are) numberless; sporting, as it were, Brahman \[(Brahma)\] repeats this again and again.

According to [Patrick Olivelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Olivelle), most scholars take the table of contents (1.111–118) to be an addition, but for him the account of time and cosmology (1.61–86) to the aforementioned (1.118) are out of place redactions. He feels the narrative should have ended when the initial command to "listen" (1.4) was repeated (1.60), then transition to "learn" (2.1).[^n13]

[Georg Bühler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Bühler), whose translation has remained the standard for over a century according to Olivelle, translated 1.71 as 12,000 years in a four-aged period, same as [Sir William Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Jones_%28philologist%29)'s translation, both based on Kulluka Bhatta's commentary. [Medhatithi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medhātithi) translated it as 12,000 four-aged periods in an age of the gods. Kulluka and Olivelle reject Medhatithi's interpretation based on 1.79 mentioning 12,000 without a qualifier ("*etat*" or "this") and must be assumed as years.[^o100]

### *Surya Siddhanta* {#Surya_Siddhanta}

The *[Surya Siddhanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surya_Siddhanta)* (1.10–21) describes units of time from a respiration (*prana*)[^n14] up to the 100-year lifespan of [Brahma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma) (*maha-kalpa*).[^o103]

sa (10) Time is the destroyer of the worlds; another Time has for its nature to bring to pass. This latter, according as it is gross or minute, is called by two names, real (murta) and unreal (amurta).

sa (11) That which begins with respirations (prana) is called real; that which begins with atoms (truti) is called unreal. Six respirations make a vinadi, sixty of these a nadi;

sa (12) And sixty nadis make a sidereal day and night. Of thirty of these sidereal days is composed a month; a civil (savana) month consists of as many sunrises;

sa (13) A lunar month, of as many lunar days (tithi); a solar (saura) month is determined by the entrance of the sun into a sign of the zodiac : twelve months make a year. This is called a day of the gods.

sa (14) The day and night of the gods and of the demons are mutually opposed to one another. Six times sixty \[360\] of them are a year of the gods, and likewise of the demons.

sa (15) Twelve thousand of these divine years are denominated a Quadruple Age (caturyuga); of ten thousand times four hundred and thirty-two \[4,320,000\] solar years

sa (16) Is composed that Quadruple Age, with its dawn and twilight. The difference of the Golden and the other Ages, as measured by the difference in the number of the feet of Virtue in each, is as follows:

sa (17) The tenth part of an Age, multiplied successively by four, three, two, and one, gives the length of the Golden and the other Ages, in order : the sixth part of each belongs to its dawn and twilight.

sa (18) One and seventy \[71\] Ages are styled here a Patriarchate (manvantara); at its end is said to be a twilight which has the number of years of a Golden Age, and which is a deluge.

sa (19) In an Æon (kalpa) are reckoned fourteen such Patriarchs (manu) with their respective twilights; at the commencement of the Æon is a fifteenth dawn, having the length of a Golden Age.

sa (20) The Æon, thus composed of a thousand Ages, and which brings about the destruction of all that exists, is styled a day of Brahma; his night is of the same length.

sa (21) His extreme age is a hundred, according to this valuation of a day and a night ...
