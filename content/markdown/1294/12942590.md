# Gromov product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gromov product"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Gromov product** is a concept in the theory of [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018) named after the mathematician [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29). The Gromov product can also be used to define [*δ*-hyperbolic metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Δ-hyperbolic_space) in the sense of Gromov.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let (*X*, *d*) be a metric space and let *x*, *y*, *z* ∈ *X*. Then the **Gromov product** of *y* and *z* at *x*, denoted (*y*, *z*)<sub>*x*</sub>, is defined by

$$(y, z)_{x} = \frac1{2} \big( d(x, y) + d(x, z) - d(y, z) \big).$$

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

*Image: Inkreis mit Strecken.svg.*

Given three points *x*, *y*, *z* in the metric space *X*, by the triangle inequality there exist non-negative numbers *a*, *b*, *c* such that $d(x,y) = a + b, \ d(x,z) = a + c, \ d(y,z) = b + c$. Then the Gromov products are $(y,z)_x = a, \ (x,z)_y = b, \ (x,y)_z = c$. In the case that the points *x*, *y*, *z* are the outer nodes of a [tripod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripod_%28mathematics%29) then these Gromov products are the lengths of the edges.

In the hyperbolic, spherical or euclidean plane, the Gromov product (*A*, *B*)<sub>*C*</sub> equals the distance *p* between *C* and the point where the [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle) of the geodesic triangle *ABC* touches the edge *CB* or *CA*. Indeed from the diagram $c = (a - p) + (b - p)$, so that $p = (a + b - c)/2 = (A,B)_{C}$. Thus for any metric space, a geometric interpretation of (*A*, *B*)<sub>*C*</sub> is obtained by isometrically embedding (A, B, C) into the euclidean plane.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The Gromov product is symmetric: (*y*, *z*)<sub>*x*</sub> = (*z*, *y*)<sub>*x*</sub>.
- The Gromov product degenerates at the endpoints: (*y*, *z*)<sub>*y*</sub> = (*y*, *z*)<sub>*z*</sub> = 0.
- For any points *p*, *q*, *x*, *y* and *z*,

$$d(x, y) = (x, z)_{y} + (y, z)_{x},$$

$$0 \leq (y, z)_{x} \leq \min \big\{ d(y, x), d(z, x) \big\},$$

$$\big| (y, z)_{p} - (y, z)_{q} \big| \leq d(p, q),$$

$$\big| (x, y)_{p} - (x, z)_{p} \big| \leq d(y, z).$$

:::

## Points at infinity {#Points_at_infinity}

Consider [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742) **H**<sup>*n*</sup>. Fix a base point *p* and let $x_\infty$ and $y_\infty$ be two distinct points at infinity. Then the limit

$$\liminf_{x \to x_\infty \atop y \to y_\infty} (x,y)_p$$

exists and is finite, and therefore can be considered as a generalized Gromov product. It is actually given by the formula

$$(x_\infty, y_\infty)_{p} = \log \csc (\theta/2),$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) rays $px_\infty$ and $py_\infty$.[^o9]

## δ-hyperbolic spaces and divergence of geodesics {#δ-hyperbolic_spaces_and_divergence_of_geodesics}

The Gromov product can be used to define [*δ*-hyperbolic spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Δ-hyperbolic_space) in the sense of Gromov.: (*X*, *d*) is said to be ***δ*-hyperbolic** if, for all *p*, *x*, *y* and *z* in *X*,

$$(x, z)_{p} \geq \min \big\{ (x, y)_{p}, (y, z)_{p} \big\} - \delta.$$

In this case. Gromov product measures how long geodesics remain close together. Namely, if *x*, *y* and *z* are three points of a *δ*-hyperbolic metric space then the initial segments of length (*y*, *z*)<sub>*x*</sub> of geodesics from *x* to *y* and *x* to *z* are no further than 2*δ* apart (in the sense of the [Hausdorff distance](wikipedia://en/429296) between closed sets).
