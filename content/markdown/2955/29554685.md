# Converse theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Converse theorem"}

In the mathematical theory of [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146), a **converse theorem** gives sufficient conditions for a [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) to be the [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297) of a [modular form](wikipedia://en/286000). More generally a converse theorem states that a representation of an [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) over the [adeles](wikipedia://en/247978) is automorphic whenever the L-functions of various twists of it are well-behaved.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Weil's converse theorem"}

## Weil's converse theorem {#Weil's_converse_theorem}

The first converse theorems were proved by (txt) who characterized the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) by its functional equation, and by (Hecke 1936) who showed that if a Dirichlet series satisfied a certain [functional equation](wikipedia://en/364377) and some growth conditions then it was the [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297) of a modular form of level 1. (Weil 1967) found an extension to modular forms of higher level, which was described by (Ogg 1969, chapter V). Weil's extension states that if not only the Dirichlet series

$$L(s)=\sum\frac{a_n}{n^s}$$

but also its twists

$$L_\chi(s)=\sum\frac{\chi(n)a_n}{n^s}$$

by some [Dirichlet characters](wikipedia://en/174515) χ, satisfy suitable functional equations relating values at *s* and 1−*s*, then the Dirichlet series is essentially the Mellin transform of a modular form of some level.

:::

## Higher dimensions {#Higher_dimensions}

J. W. Cogdell, H. Jacquet, I. I. [Piatetski-Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piatetski-Shapiro) and J. Shalika have extended the converse theorem to automorphic forms on some higher-dimensional groups, in particular GL<sub>*n*</sub> and GL<sub>*m*</sub>×GL<sub>*n*</sub>, in a long series of papers.
