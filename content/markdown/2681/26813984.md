# Robert F. Coleman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert F. Coleman"}

**Robert Frederick Coleman** (November 22 1954 – March 24, 2014) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), and professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

After graduating from [Nova High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_High_School), he completed his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1976 and subsequently attended [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) for [Part III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_III_of_the_Mathematical_Tripos) of the [mathematical tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Mathematical_Tripos). While there [John H. Coates](wikipedia://en/230705) provided him with a problem for his doctoral thesis ("Division Values in Local Fields"), which he completed at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1979 under the advising of [Kenkichi Iwasawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenkichi_Iwasawa). He then had a one-year postdoctoral appointment at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) and then taught at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) for three years. In 1983, he moved to [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). In 1985, he was struck with a severe case of [multiple sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_sclerosis), in which he lost the use of his legs. Despite this, he remained an active faculty member until his retirement in 2013. He was awarded a [MacArthur fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_fellowship) in 1987.[^o5]

Coleman died on March 24, 2014.[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

He worked primarily in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), with specific interests in [p-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327) and [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177). In particular, he developed a theory of [p-adic integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integration) analogous to the classical complex theory of abelian integrals. Applications of Coleman integration include an effective version of Chabauty's theorem concerning rational points on curves and a new proof of the [Manin-Mumford conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manin-Mumford_conjecture), originally proved by [Michel Raynaud](wikipedia://en/4760928). Coleman is also known for introducing p-adic Banach spaces into the study of [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_forms) and discovering important classicality criteria for overconvergent p-adic modular forms. With [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679), he introduced the [eigencurve](wikipedia://en/37629519) and established some of its fundamental properties. In 1990, Coleman found a gap in Manin's proof of the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture) over function fields and managed to fill it in. With [José Felipe Voloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Felipe_Voloch), Coleman established an important unchecked compatibility in [Benedict Gross](wikipedia://en/4326863)'s theory of companion forms.[^o8]

Coleman's effective version of Chabauty's method only applies to curves that satisfy Chabauty's condition. In 2004 [Minhyong Kim](wikipedia://en/30994594) published a far-reaching generalization of Chabauty's method.[^o9][^o10]
