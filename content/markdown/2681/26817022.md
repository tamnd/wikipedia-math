# Maxwell Rosenlicht

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maxwell Rosenlicht"}

**Maxwell Alexander Rosenlicht** (April 15, 1924 – January 22, 1999) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for works in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924), and [differential algebra](wikipedia://en/1816587).

:::

Rosenlicht attended high school in Brooklyn ([Erasmus High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Hall_High_School)) and studied at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) (B.A. 1947) and at Harvard University, where he worked under [Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352). He was a [Putnam fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_fellow) twice, in 1946 and 1947.[^o3] He was awarded his doctorate on algebraic curve equivalence concepts in 1950. In 1952, he moved to [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University). From 1958 until his retirement in 1991, he was a professor at the University of California, Berkeley. He was also a visiting professor in Mexico City, [IHÉS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques), Rome, Leiden, and at Harvard University.

In 1960, he shared the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in algebra with [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991) for his work on [generalized Jacobian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Jacobian_variety).[^o4][^o5] He also studied the algorithmic algebraic theory of integration.

Rosenlicht was a Fulbright Fellow and 1954 Guggenheim Fellow.

He died of neurological disease on a trip to Hawaii. Rosenlicht married in 1954 and had four children.
