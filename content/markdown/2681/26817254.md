# Diagonalizable group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Diagonalizable group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an [affine algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_algebraic_group) is said to be **diagonalizable** if it is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of **D**<sub>*n*</sub>, the group of [diagonal matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_matrices). A diagonalizable group defined over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *k* is said to *split over* *k* or *k*-*split* if the isomorphism is defined over *k*. This coincides with the usual notion of [split](wikipedia://en/138378) for an algebraic group. Every diagonalizable group splits over the [separable closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_closure) *k*<sub>s</sub> of *k*. Any closed subgroup and image of diagonalizable groups are diagonalizable. The [torsion subgroup](wikipedia://en/144052) of a diagonalizable group is dense.

:::

The [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of diagonalizable groups defined over *k* is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the category of [finitely generated abelian groups](wikipedia://en/58527) with Gal(*k*<sub>s</sub>/*k*)-equivariant morphisms without *p*-torsion, if *k* is of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p*. This is an analog of [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) and motivated the terminology.

A diagonalizable *k*-group is said to be *anisotropic* if it has no nontrivial *k*-valued [character](wikipedia://en/293418).

The so-called "rigidity" states that the identity component of the [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) of a diagonalizable group coincides with the identity component of the [normalizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalizer) of the group. The fact plays a crucial role in the structure theory of [solvable groups](wikipedia://en/105012).

A connected diagonalizable group is called an [algebraic torus](wikipedia://en/504697) (which is not necessarily compact, in contrast to a [complex torus](wikipedia://en/3055042)). A *k*-torus is a torus defined over *k*. The centralizer of a maximal torus is called a [Cartan subgroup](wikipedia://en/4838153).
