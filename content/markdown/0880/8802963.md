The **triangular prismatic honeycomb** or **triangular prismatic cellulation** is a space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in [Euclidean 3-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_3-space). It is composed entirely of [triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/2859584).

:::

It is constructed from a [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858) extruded into prisms.

It is one of 28 [convex uniform honeycombs](wikipedia://en/2961).

It consists of 1 + 6 + 1 = 8 edges meeting at a vertex, There are 6 triangular prism cells meeting at an edge and faces are shared between 2 cells.

The **hexagonal prismatic honeycomb** or **hexagonal prismatic cellulation** is a space-filling tessellation (or honeycomb) in Euclidean 3-space made up of hexagonal prisms.

It is constructed from a hexagonal tiling extruded into prisms.

*Image: Hexagonal prismatic honeycomb.png.*

It is one of 28 convex uniform honeycombs.

This honeycomb can be [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) into the [gyrated tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrated_tetrahedral-octahedral_honeycomb), with pairs of tetrahedra existing in the alternated gaps (instead of a triangular bipyramid).

There are 1 + 3 + 1 = 5 edges meeting at a vertex, 3 [hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859668) cells meeting at an edge, and faces are shared between 2 cells.

### Gyrated triangular prismatic honeycomb {#Gyrated_triangular_prismatic_honeycomb}

The **gyrated triangular prismatic honeycomb** or **parasquare fastigial cellulation** is a space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in [Euclidean 3-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_3-space) made up of [triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/2859584). It is vertex-uniform with 12 triangular prisms per vertex.

*Image: Gyrated triangular prismatic honeycomb.png.*

*Image: Gyrated triangular prismatic tiling.png.*

It can be seen as parallel planes of [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809) with alternating offsets caused by layers of paired triangular prisms. The prisms in each layer are rotated by a right angle to those in the next layer.

It is one of 28 [convex uniform honeycombs](wikipedia://en/2961).

Pairs of triangular prisms can be combined to create [gyrobifastigium](wikipedia://en/1177963) cells. The resulting honeycomb is closely related but not equivalent: it has the same vertices and edges, but different two-dimensional faces and three-dimensional cells.

The **gyroelongated triangular prismatic honeycomb** or **elongated parasquare fastigial cellulation** is a uniform space-filling [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) (or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654)) in Euclidean 3-space. It is composed of [cubes](wikipedia://en/6285) and [triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/2859584) in a ratio of 1:2.

*Image: Gyroelongated triangular prismatic honeycomb.png.*

*Image: Gyroelongated triangular prismatic tiling.png.*

It is created by alternating layers of cubes and triangular prisms, with the prisms alternating in orientation by 90 degrees.

It is related to the [elongated triangular prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongated_triangular_prismatic_honeycomb) which has the triangular prisms with the same orientation.

This is related to a space-filling polyhedron, [elongated gyrobifastigium](wikipedia://en/53773496), where [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) and two opposite triangular prisms are augmented together as a single polyhedron:
