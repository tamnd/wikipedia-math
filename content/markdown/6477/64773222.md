# Maria Deijfen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maria Deijfen"}

**Maria Deijfen** (born 1975) is a Swedish mathematician known for her research on [random graphs](wikipedia://en/358477) and [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895) on graphs, including the [Reed–Frost model](wikipedia://en/24246411) of [epidemics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic). She is a professor of mathematics at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Deijfen was educated at Stockholm University, earning a licenciate in 2001, a doctorate in 2004, and a [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 2008. Her doctoral dissertation, *Stochastic Models for Spatial Growth and Competition*, was supervised by [Olle Häggström](wikipedia://en/52600654).

After completing her doctorate, she became a postdoctoral researcher at the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452), [Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrije_Universiteit_Amsterdam), [Chalmers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalmers_University), and [Delft University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology) before returning to Stockholm as a junior faculty member in 2006. She was promoted to full professor in [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) in 2015.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Deijfen was one of the 2018 recipients of the [Paul R. Halmos – Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_R._Halmos_–_Lester_R._Ford_Award) of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for her paper with Alexander E. Holroyd and James B. Martin, "Friendly Frogs, Stable Marriage, and the Magic of Invariance", using [combinatorial game theory](wikipedia://en/292231) to analyze the [stable marriage problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_marriage_problem).
