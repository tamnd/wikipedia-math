# Bhatia–Davis inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bhatia–Davis inequality"}

In mathematics, the **Bhatia–Davis inequality**, named after [Rajendra Bhatia](wikipedia://en/39347256) and [Chandler Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandler_Davis), is an [upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) on the [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) *σ*<sup>2</sup> of any bounded [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) on the real line.

:::

Despite its name, the inequality had previously been found by Muilwijk,[^o2] based on Murthy and Sethi,[^o3] 34 years before publication of the better-known article by Bhatia and Davis.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *m* and M be the lower and upper bounds, respectively, for a set of real numbers *a<sub>1</sub>*, ..., *a<sub>n</sub> ,* with a particular [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543). Let *μ* be the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of this distribution.

Then the Bhatia–Davis inequality states:

$$\sigma^2 \le (M - \mu)(\mu - m). \,$$

Equality holds if and only if every *a<sub>j</sub>* in the set of values is equal either to *M* or to *m*.[^o2][^o4]

:::

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Since $m \leq A \leq M$,

$$0 \leq \mathbb{E}[(M - A)(A - m)] = -\mathbb{E}[A^2] - m M + (m+M)\mu.$$

Thus,

$$\sigma^2 = \mathbb{E}[A^2] - \mu^2 \leq - m M + (m+M)\mu - \mu^2 = (M - \mu) (\mu - m).$$

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Extensions of the Bhatia–Davis inequality"}

## Extensions of the Bhatia–Davis inequality {#Extensions_of_the_Bhatia–Davis_inequality}

If $\Phi$ is a positive and unital linear mapping of a *C\* -*algebra $\mathcal{A}$ into a *C\** -algebra $\mathcal{B}$, and *A* is a self-adjoint element of $\mathcal{A}$ satisfying *m* $\leq$ *A* $\leq$ *M*, then:

$$\Phi (A^2)-(\Phi A)^2\leq (M-\Phi A)(\Phi A - m).$$

If $\mathit{X}$ is a discrete random variable such that

$P (X=x_i)=p_i,$ where $i = 1, ..., n$, then:

$$s_p^2=\sum_{1}^n p_ix_i^2-(\sum_{1}^n p_ix_i)^2\leq(M-\sum_{1}^n p_ix_i)(\sum_{1}^n p_ix_i-m),$$

where $0\leq p_i \leq1$ and $\sum_{1}^n p_i=1$.

:::

## Comparisons to other inequalities {#Comparisons_to_other_inequalities}

The Bhatia–Davis inequality is stronger than [Popoviciu's inequality on variances](wikipedia://en/24107159) (note, however, that Popoviciu's inequality does not require knowledge of the expectation or mean), as can be seen from the conditions for equality. Equality holds in Popoviciu's inequality if and only if half of the *a<sub>j</sub>* are equal to the upper bounds and half of the *a<sub>j</sub>* are equal to the lower bounds. Additionally, Sharma[^o12] has made further refinements on the Bhatia–Davis inequality.
