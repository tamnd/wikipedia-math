# Katugampola fractional operators

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Katugampola fractional operators"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Katugampola fractional operators** are [integral operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_operators) that generalize the *Riemann–Liouville* and the *Hadamard* fractional operators into a unique form.[^o2][^o3][^o17][^o25] The **Katugampola fractional integral** generalizes both the [Riemann–Liouville fractional integral](wikipedia://en/229939#Riemann–Liouville_fractional_integral) and the [Hadamard fractional integral](wikipedia://en/229939#Hadamard_fractional_integral) into a single form and It is also closely related to the [Erdelyi–Kober](wikipedia://en/32798255)[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7] operator that generalizes the Riemann–Liouville fractional integral. **Katugampola fractional derivative**[^o3][^o17][^o25] has been defined using the [Katugampola fractional integral](wikipedia://en/229939#Further_generalizations)[^o17] and as with any other [fractional differential operator](wikipedia://en/229939), it also extends the possibility of taking [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) powers or [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) powers of the integral and [differential operators](wikipedia://en/7921).

:::

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

These operators have been defined on the following extended-Lebesgue space..

Let $\textit{X}^p_c(a,b), \; c\in \mathbb{R}, \, 1 \leq p \leq \infty$ be the space of those Lebesgue [measurable functions](wikipedia://en/44055) $f$ on $[a, b]$ for which $\|f\|_{\textit{X}^p_c} < \infty$, where the norm is defined by [^o2]

$$\begin{aligned} \|f\|_{\textit{X}^p_c} =\left(\int^b_a |t^c f(t)|^p \frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/p} < \infty, \end{aligned}$$

for $1 \leq p < \infty,\, c \in \mathbb{R}$ and for the case $p=\infty$

$$\begin{aligned} \|f\|_{\textit{X}^\infty_c} = \text{ess sup}_{a \leq t \leq b} [t^c|f(t)|], \quad ( c \in \mathbb{R}). \end{aligned}$$

:::

## Katugampola fractional integral {#Katugampola_fractional_integral}

It is defined via the following integrals [^o2][^o3][^o11][^o12][^o13]

::: {#o14 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$({}^\rho \mathcal{I}^\alpha_{a+}f)(x) = \frac{\rho^{1- \alpha }}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \int^x_a \frac{\tau^{\rho-1} f(\tau) }{(x^\rho - \tau^\rho)^{1-\alpha}}\, d\tau, \tag{1}$$

:::

for $x > a$ and $\operatorname{Re}(\alpha) > 0.$ This integral is called the *left-sided* fractional integral. Similarly, the *right-sided* fractional integral is defined by,

::: {#o15 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$({}^\rho \mathcal{I}^\alpha_{b-}f)(x) = \frac{\rho^{1- \alpha }}{\Gamma({\alpha})} \int^b_x \frac{\tau^{\rho-1} f(\tau) }{(\tau^\rho - x^\rho)^{1-\alpha}}\, d\tau. \tag{2}$$

:::

for $\textstyle x < b$ and $\textstyle\operatorname{Re}(\alpha) > 0$.

These are the fractional generalizations of the $n$-fold left- and right-integrals of the form

$$\int_a^x t_1^{\rho-1} \, dt_1 \int_a^{t_1} t_2^{\rho-1} \,dt_2 \cdots \int_a^{t_{n -1}} t_n^{\rho-1} f(t_n)\,dt_n$$

and

$\int_x^b t_1^{\rho-1} \,dt_1 \int^b_{t_1} t_2^{\rho-1} \,dt_2 \cdots \int^b_{t_{n -1}} t_n^{\rho-1} f(t_n) \, dt_n$ for $\textstyle n \in \mathbb{N},$

respectively. Even though the integral operators in question are close resemblance of the famous [Erdélyi–Kober operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdélyi–Kober_operator), it is not possible to obtain the Hadamard fractional integrals as a direct consequence of the Erdélyi–Kober operators. Also, there is a corresponding fractional derivative, which generalizes the *Riemann–Liouville* and the *Hadamard fractional derivatives*. As with the case of fractional integrals, the same is not true for the Erdélyi–Kober operator.

## Katugampola fractional derivative {#Katugampola_fractional_derivative}

As with the case of other fractional derivatives, it is defined via the Katugampola fractional integral.[^o17][^o11][^o12][^o13]

Let $\alpha \in \mathbb{C},\ \operatorname{Re}(\alpha) \geq 0, n=[\operatorname{Re}(\alpha)]+1$ and $\rho >0.$ The generalized fractional derivatives, corresponding to the generalized fractional integrals ((1)) and ((2)) are defined, respectively, for $0 \leq a < x < b \leq \infty$, by

*Image: Fig1-Katugampola.jpg.* The half-derivative of the function $f(x) = x^{0.5}$ for the Katugampola fractional derivative.

*Image: Fig2-Katugampola.jpg.* The half derivative of the function $f(x) = x^\nu$ for the Katugampola fractional derivative for $\alpha = 0.5$ and $\rho = 2$.

$$\begin{aligned} \big({}^\rho \mathcal{D}^\alpha_{a+}f\big)(x)&= \bigg(x^{1-\rho} \,\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^n\,\, \big({}^\rho \mathcal{I}^{n-\alpha}_{a+}f\big)(x)\\ &= \frac{\rho^{\alpha-n+1 }}{\Gamma({n-\alpha})} \, \bigg(x^{1-\rho} \,\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^n \int^x_a \frac{\tau^{\rho-1} f(\tau) }{(x^\rho - \tau^\rho)^{\alpha-n+1}}\, d\tau, \end{aligned}$$

and

$$\begin{aligned} \big({}^\rho \mathcal{D}^\alpha_{b-}f\big)(x) &= \bigg(-x^{1-\rho} \,\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^n\,\, \big({}^\rho \mathcal{I}^{n-\alpha}_{b-}f\big)(x)\\ &= \frac{\rho^{\alpha-n+1 }}{\Gamma({n-\alpha})}\bigg(-x^{1-\rho}\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^n \int^b_x\frac{\tau^{\rho-1} f(\tau) }{(\tau^\rho - x^\rho)^{\alpha-n+1}}\, d\tau, \end{aligned}$$

respectively, if the integrals exist.

These operators generalize the Riemann–Liouville and Hadamard fractional derivatives into a single form, while the Erdelyi–Kober fractional is a generalization of the Riemann–Liouville fractional derivative.[^o17] When, $b=\infty$, the fractional derivatives are referred to as [Weyl-type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_integral) derivatives.

### Caputo–Katugampola fractional derivative {#Caputo–Katugampola_fractional_derivative}

There is a Caputo-type modification of the Katugampola derivative that is now known as the Caputo–Katugampola fractional derivative.[^o19][^o20] Let $f \in L^1[a, b], \alpha \in (0, 1]$ and $\rho$. The C-K fractional derivative of order $\alpha$ of the function $f:[a,b] \rightarrow \mathbb{R},$ with respect to parameter $\rho$ can be expressed as

$${}^C\mathcal{D}^{\alpha, \rho}_{a+}f(t)=\frac{\rho^\alpha t^{1-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\frac{d}{dt}\int^t_a\frac{s^{\rho-1}}{(t^\rho-s^\rho)^\alpha}\big[f(s)-f(a)\big]\,ds.$$

It satisfies the following result. Assume that $f \in C^1[a, b]$, then the C-K derivative has the following equivalent form

$${}^C\mathcal{D}^{\alpha, \rho}_{a+}f(t)=\frac{\rho^\alpha }{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int^t_a \frac{f^\prime(s)}{(t^\rho-s^\rho)^\alpha}ds.$$

### Hilfer–Katugampola fractional derivative {#Hilfer–Katugampola_fractional_derivative}

Another recent generalization is the *Hilfer-Katugampola* fractional derivative.[^o22][^o23] Let order $0<\alpha<1$ and type $0\leq{\beta}\leq{1}$. The fractional derivative (left-sided/right-sided), with respect to $x$, with $\rho>0$, is defined by

$$\begin{aligned} ({^{\rho}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha,\beta}_{a\pm}}\varphi)(x)&=\left(\pm\,{^{\rho}\mathcal{J}_{a\pm}^{\beta(1-\alpha)}}\left(t^{\rho-1}\frac{d}{dt}\right){^{\rho}\mathcal{J}_{a\pm}^{(1-\beta)(1-\alpha)}}\varphi\right)(x)\\ &=\left(\pm\,{^{\rho}\mathcal{J}_{a\pm}^{\beta(1-\alpha)}}\delta_{\rho}\,{^{\rho}\mathcal{J}_{a\pm}^{(1-\beta)(1-\alpha)}}\varphi\right)(x), \end{aligned}$$

where $\delta_{\rho}= t^{\rho-1}\frac{d}{dt}$, for functions $\varphi$ in which the expression on the right hand side exists, where $\mathcal{J}$ is the generalized fractional integral given in ((1)).

## Mellin transform {#Mellin_transform}

As in the case of [Laplace transforms](wikipedia://en/18610), [Mellin transforms](wikipedia://en/387297) will be used specially when solving [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). The Mellin transforms of the *left-sided* and *right-sided* versions of Katugampola Integral operators are given by [^o3][^o25]

::: {#o27 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

### Theorem {#Theorem}

Let $\alpha \in \mathcal{C},\ \operatorname{Re}(\alpha) > 0,$ and $\rho >0.$ Then,

$$\begin{aligned} & \mathcal{M}\bigg({}^\rho \mathcal{I}^\alpha_{a+}f\bigg)(s) = \frac{\Gamma\big(1-\frac{s}{\rho}-\alpha\big)}{\Gamma\big(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\big)\,\rho^\alpha}\, \mathcal{M}f(s + \alpha\rho), \quad \operatorname{Re}(s/\rho + \alpha) < 1, \, x > a, \\ & \mathcal{M}\bigg({}^\rho \mathcal{I}^\alpha_{b-}f\bigg)(s) = \frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{s}{\rho}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{s}{\rho} + \alpha\big)\,\rho^\alpha}\, \mathcal{M}f(s + \alpha\rho), \quad \operatorname{Re}(s/\rho) > 0, \, x < b, \end{aligned}$$

for $f \in \textit{X}^1_{s + \alpha\rho}(\mathbb{R}^+)$, if $\mathcal{M} f(s + \alpha\rho)$ exists for $s \in \mathbb{C}$.

:::

## Hermite-Hadamard type inequalities {#Hermite-Hadamard_type_inequalities}

Katugampola operators satisfy the following Hermite-Hadamard type inequalities:[^o29]

::: {#o31 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

### Theorem {#Theorem}

Let $\alpha > 0$ and $\rho > 0$. If $f$ is a [convex function](wikipedia://en/245568) on $[a, b]$, then

$$f\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right) \leq \frac{\rho^\alpha \Gamma(\alpha +1)}{4(b^\alpha -a^\alpha)^\alpha}\left[{}^\rho \mathcal{I}^\alpha_{a+}F(b)+{}^\rho \mathcal{I}^\alpha_{b-}F(a)\right] \leq \frac{f(a)+f(b)}{2},$$

where $F(x) = f(x) + f(a+b-x), \; x \in [a, b]$.

When $\rho \rightarrow 0^+$, in the above result, the following Hadamard type inequality holds:[^o29]

:::

::: {#o33 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=named-heading name="Corollary"}

### Corollary {#Corollary}

Let $\alpha > 0$. If $f$ is a convex function on $[a, b]$, then

$$f\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right) \leq \frac{\Gamma(\alpha +1)}{4\left(\ln \frac{b}{a}\right)^\alpha}\left[ \mathbf{I}^\alpha_{a+}F(b)+ \mathbf{I}^\alpha_{b-}F(a)\right] \leq \frac{f(a)+f(b)}{2},$$

where $\mathbf{I}^\alpha_{a+}$ and $\mathbf{I}^\alpha_{b-}$ are left- and right-sided [Hadamard fractional integrals](wikipedia://en/229939#Hadamard_fractional_integral).

:::
