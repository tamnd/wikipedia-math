# Dmitry Mirimanoff

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Dmitry Mirimanoff"}

**Dmitry Semionovitch Mirimanoff** (Дми́трий Семёнович Мирима́нов; 13 September 1861, [Pereslavl-Zalessky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pereslavl-Zalessky), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) – 5 January 1945, [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland)) was a member of the [Moscow Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/9098251) in 1897.[^o3] And later became a doctor of [mathematical sciences](wikipedia://en/25750436) in 1900, in Geneva, and taught at the universities of [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva) and [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne).

:::

Mirimanoff made notable contributions to [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory) and to number theory (relating specifically to [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953), on which he corresponded with [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) before the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I)[^o4]). In 1917, he introduced, though not as explicitly as [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) later, the [cumulative hierarchy](wikipedia://en/7393195) of sets and the notion of [von Neumann ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932#Von_Neumann_definition_of_ordinals); although he introduced a notion of regular (and [well-founded set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-founded_set)) he did not consider [regularity as an axiom](wikipedia://en/2113), but also explored what is now called [non-well-founded set theory](wikipedia://en/1091767) and had an emergent idea of what is now called [bisimulation](wikipedia://en/397571).[^o5][^o6]

## Life {#Life}

Dmitry Semionovitch was born in [Pereslavl-Zalessky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pereslavl-Zalessky), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), on 13 September 1861. His parents were Semion Mirimanovitch Mirimanoff (Семён Мирима́нович Мирима́нов) and Maria Dmitrievna Rudakova (Мари́я Дми́триевна Рудакова).

Around 1885, Dmitry Mirimanoff met a [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people) woman, Malvina Geneviève Valentine Adriansen in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice). Geneviève Adriansen learnt [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) and accepted [Russian Orthodox Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church). They married in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) on 25 October 1897 and had two sons: Alexander (later Alexandre) Dmitrievitch Mirimanoff (Алекса́ндр Дми́триевич Мирима́нов), born in Oranienbaum (now [Lomonossov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov,_Russia)) in 1898, and Andreï (later André) Dmitrievitch Mirimanoff (Андрей Дми́триевич Мирима́нов), born in Geneva in 1902.

The family lived in [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (first, in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), then in [St Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg)) until 1900 when they moved to Geneva (in search of a better climate for Dmitry Mirimanoff's bad health). After the [1917 revolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution) they never visited Russia, although Dmitry's sisters Sophia and Lydia remained there. Dmitry Mirimanoff became a [Swiss citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_citizen) on 17 September 1926. He later died on 5 January 1945 in Geneva.

## Work {#Work}

### Set theory {#Set_theory}

Mirimanoff in a 1917 paper introduced the concept of well-founded set and the notion of rank of a set.[^o10] Mirimanoff called a set *x* "regular" (French: "ordinaire") if every descending chain *x* ∋ *x<sub>1</sub>* ∋ *x<sub>2</sub>* ∋ ... is finite. Mirimanoff however did not consider his notion of regularity (well-foundedness) as an axiom to be observed by all sets;[^o6] in later papers Mirimanoff also explored what are now called [non-well-founded sets](wikipedia://en/1091767) ("extraordinaire" in Mirimanoff's terminology).[^o5]

### Reflection method {#Reflection_method}

In 2008, [Marc Renault](http://webspace.ship.edu/msrenault/) published an article[^o12] in which he pointed out that it is Dmitry Mirimanoff who should be credited for creating "[the reflection method](wikipedia://en/1043036#The_reflection_method)" for solving [Bertrand's ballot problem](wikipedia://en/1043036), not [Désiré André](wikipedia://en/38149596) to whom it had been long credited. Therefore, [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095), who has read Renault's article, will credit Mirimanoff instead of André in future printings of Volume 1 of his monograph [The Art of Computer Programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Art_of_Computer_Programming).[^o13]
