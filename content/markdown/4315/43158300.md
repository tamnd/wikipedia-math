# Alexander Murray MacBeath

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Murray MacBeath"}

**Alexander Murray MacBeath** (30 June 1923 [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow) – 14 May 2014 [Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick))[^o3][^o4][^o5] was a Scottish mathematician who worked on [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181). [MacBeath surfaces](wikipedia://en/12838118) and [MacBeath regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacBeath_regions) are named after him.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

MacBeath was the son of Alexander MacBeath, a philosopher and logician who took a position at [Queen's University Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_Belfast) in 1925,[^o7] soon after Murray was born. Murray also studied at Queen's University, earning a B.A. with honours in 1943.[^o3]

During World War II, he worked in [Hut 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hut_7) of the [Government Code and Cypher School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Communications_Headquarters#Government_Code_and_Cypher_School_%28GC&CS%29) at [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037), breaking ciphers used for military communications by the Japanese navy and, later, the army.[^o8]

He earned an M.A. (again with honours) from Clare College, Cambridge in 1948. With a Commonwealth Fund fellowship, he then attended [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University),[^o3] where he earned his Ph.D. on "The Geometry of Non-Homogeneous Lattices" in 1950 under the supervision of [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351).

## Career {#Career}

He taught at [Keele University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keele_University) and the [University of Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dundee) before moving to the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham) in 1963 where he stayed until 1979 as Mason Professor,[^o5] then moved back to the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) in the United States until he reached their statutory retirement age of 60.[^o3]

He subsequently took up a position at the [University of Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dundee) where he remained for a number of years, before moving to Warwickshire where at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) he held the position of emeritus professor of mathematics.

## Death {#Death}

Professor MacBeath died on 14 May 2014 in Warwick, England.
