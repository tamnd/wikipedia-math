# Euclides Danicus

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Euclides Danicus"}

***Euclides Danicus*** (Danish Euclid) is one of three books of mathematics written by [Georg Mohr](wikipedia://en/83128).[^o3] It was published in 1672 simultaneously in [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen) and [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam), in [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language) and [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) respectively. It contains the first proof of the [Mohr–Mascheroni theorem](wikipedia://en/1196909), which states that every geometric construction that can be performed using a [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge) can also be done with compass alone.[^o4]

:::

## Contents {#Contents}

The book is divided into two parts. In the first part, Mohr shows how to perform all of the constructions of [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s [*Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107) using a compass alone. In the second part, he includes some other specific constructions, including some related to the mathematics of the [sundial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundial).[^o6]

## 1928 rediscovery of the book {#1928_rediscovery_of_the_book}

*Euclides Danicus* languished in obscurity, possibly caused by its choice of language, until its rediscovery in 1928 in a bookshop in Copenhagen. Until then, the Mohr–Mascheroni theorem had been credited to [Lorenzo Mascheroni](wikipedia://en/88116), who published a proof in 1797, independently of Mohr's work.[^o4][^o8] Soon after the rediscovery of Mohr's book, publications about it by [Florian Cajori](wikipedia://en/542738) and [Nathan Altshiller Court](wikipedia://en/37312015) made its existence much more widely known.[^o4][^o8][^o9] The Danish version was republished in facsimile in 1928 by the [Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters), with a foreword by [Johannes Hjelmslev](wikipedia://en/30961758),[^o4][^o8][^o10][^o11] and a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) translation was published in 1929.[^o4]

## Surviving copies {#Surviving_copies}

Only eight copies of the original publication of the book are known to survive. In 2005, one of these original copies was sold at auction, to [Fry's Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fry's_Electronics), for what [Gerald L. Alexanderson](wikipedia://en/43156665) calls a "ridiculously low price": US\$13,000.[^o4]
