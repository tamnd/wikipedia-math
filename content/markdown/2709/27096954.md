# Lobb number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lobb number"}

In [combinatorial mathematics](wikipedia://en/5170), the **Lobb number** *L*<sub>*m*,*n*</sub> counts the ways that *n* + *m* open parentheses and *n* − *m* close parentheses can be arranged to form the start of a valid sequence of [balanced parentheses](wikipedia://en/1852078).[^o2]

:::

Lobb numbers form a natural generalization of the [Catalan numbers](wikipedia://en/216488), which count the complete strings of balanced parentheses of a given length. Thus, the *n*th Catalan number equals the Lobb number *L*<sub>0,*n*</sub>.[^o3] They are named after Andrew Lobb, who used them to give a simple [inductive proof](wikipedia://en/18881) of the formula for the *n*<sup>th</sup> Catalan number.[^o4]

The Lobb numbers are parameterized by two non-negative [integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integers) *m* and *n* with *n* ≥ *m* ≥ 0. The (*m*, *n*)<sup>th</sup> Lobb number *L*<sub>*m*,*n*</sub> is given in terms of [binomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/4668) by the formula

$$L_{m,n} = \frac{2m+1}{m+n+1}\binom{2n}{m+n} \qquad\text{ for }n \ge m \ge 0.$$

An alternative expression for Lobb number *L*<sub>*m*,*n*</sub> is:

$$L_{m,n} = \binom{2n}{m+n} - \binom{2n}{m+n+1} .$$

The triangle of these numbers starts as

$$\begin{array}{rrrrrr} 1 \\ 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 5 & 9 & 5 & 1\\ 14 & 28 & 20 & 7 & 1\\ 42 & 90 & 75 &35 & 9 & 1\\ \end{array}$$

where the diagonal is

$$L_{n,n}=1,$$

and the left column are the Catalan Numbers

$$L_{0,n}= \frac{1}{1+n}\binom{2n}{n}.$$

As well as counting sequences of parentheses, the Lobb numbers also count the ways in which *n* + *m* copies of the value +1 and *n* − *m* copies of the value −1 may be arranged into a sequence such that all of the [partial sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_sum) of the sequence are non-negative.

## Ballot counting {#Ballot_counting}

The combinatorics of parentheses is replaced with counting ballots in an election with two candidates in [Bertrand's ballot theorem](wikipedia://en/1043036), first published by [William Allen Whitworth](wikipedia://en/38807454) in 1878. The theorem states the [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) that winning candidate is ahead in the count, given known final tallies for each candidate.
