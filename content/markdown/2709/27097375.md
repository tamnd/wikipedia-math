# Brownian excursion

*Image: BrownExcursion1D.svg.* A realization of Brownian Excursion.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brownian excursion"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) a **Brownian excursion process** (**BPE**) is a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) that is closely related to a [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984) (or [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436)). Realisations of Brownian excursion processes are essentially just realizations of a Wiener process selected to satisfy certain conditions. In particular, a Brownian excursion process is a Wiener process [conditioned](wikipedia://en/24104134) to be positive and to take the value 0 at time 1. Alternatively, it is a [Brownian bridge](wikipedia://en/1843777) process conditioned to be positive. BEPs are important because, among other reasons, they naturally arise as the limit process of a number of conditional functional central limit theorems.[^o2]

:::

## Constructions {#Constructions}

*Image: Brownian bridge as a union of excursions.svg.* Brownian bridge as a union of excursions.

A Brownian excursion process, $e$, is a [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984) (or [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436)) [conditioned](wikipedia://en/24104134) to be positive and to take the value 0 at time 1. Alternatively, it is a [Brownian bridge](wikipedia://en/1843777) process conditioned to be positive. Although note that, since the probability for an unrestricted Brownian bridge to be positive is zero, the conditioning requires care.

Another representation of a Brownian excursion $e$ in terms of a Brownian motion process *W* (due to [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462) and noted by [Kiyosi Itô](wikipedia://en/1329240) and [Henry P. McKean, Jr.](wikipedia://en/35051081)[^o5]) is in terms of the last time $\tau_{-}$ that *W* hits zero before time 1 and the first time $\tau_{+}$ that Brownian motion $W$ hits zero after time 1:[^o5]

$$\{ e(t) : \ {0 \le t \le 1} \} \ \stackrel{d}{=} \ \left \{ \frac{|W((1-t) \tau_{-} + t \tau_{+} )|}{\sqrt{\tau_+ - \tau_{-}}} : \ 0 \le t \le 1 \right \},$$

where the square root is due to the square root self-similarity of Wiener process. That is, $\sqrt a W_{t/a}$ is a Wiener process for any fixed constant $a > 0$.

*Image: Vervaat's transformation.svg.* Vervaat's transformation.

Let $\tau_m$ be the time that a Brownian bridge process $W_0$ achieves its minimum on \[0, 1\]. Vervaat (1979) shows that

$$\{ e(t) : \ {0\le t \le 1} \} \ \stackrel{d}{=} \ \left \{ W_0 ( \tau_m + t \bmod 1) - W_0 (\tau_m ): \ 0 \le t \le 1 \right \} .$$

This is sometimes called Vervaat's transformation.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Vervaat's representation of a Brownian excursion has several consequences for various functions of $e$. In particular:

$$M_{+} \equiv \sup_{0 \le t \le 1} e(t) \ \stackrel{d}{=} \ \sup_{0 \le t \le 1} W_0 (t) - \inf_{0 \le t \le 1} W_0 (t) ,$$

(this can also be derived by explicit calculations[^o8][^o9]) and

$$\int_0^1 e(t) \, dt \ \stackrel{d}{=} \ \int_0^1 W_0 (t) \, dt - \inf_{0 \le t \le 1} W_0 (t) .$$

The following result holds:[^o10]

$$E M_+ = \sqrt{\pi/2} \approx 1.25331 \ldots, \,$$

and the following values for the second moment and variance can be calculated by the exact form of the distribution and density:[^o10]

$$E M_+^2 \approx 1.64493 \ldots \ , \ \ \operatorname{Var}(M_+) \approx 0.0741337 \ldots.$$

Groeneboom (1989), Lemma 4.2 gives an expression for the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) of (the density) of $\int_0^1 e(t) \, dt$. A formula for a certain double transform of the distribution of this area integral is given by Louchard (1984).

Groeneboom (1983) and Pitman (1983) give decompositions of [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/149984) $W$ in terms of i.i.d Brownian excursions and the least concave majorant (or greatest convex minorant) of $W$.

For an introduction to [Itô's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiyoshi_Itō) general theory of Brownian excursions and the [Itô](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiyoshi_Itō) [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) of excursions, see Revuz and Yor (1994), chapter XII.

:::

## Connections and applications {#Connections_and_applications}

With probability 1, a Wiener process is continuous, which means the set on which it is non-zero is an open subset of the real line, thus it is the union of countably many Brownian excursions.

The Brownian excursion area

$$A_+ \equiv \int_0^1 e(t) \, dt$$

arises in connection with the enumeration of connected graphs, many other problems in combinatorial theory; see e.g.[^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16] and the limit distribution of the Betti numbers of certain varieties in cohomology theory.[^o17] Takacs (1991a) shows that $A_+$ has density

$$f_{A_+} (x) = \frac{2 \sqrt{6}}{x^2} \sum_{j=1}^\infty v_j^{2/3} e^{-v_j} U\left ( - \frac{5}{6} , \frac{4}{3}; v_j \right ) \ \ \text{ with } \ \ v_j = \frac{2 |a_j|^3}{27x^2}$$

where $a_j$ are the zeros of the Airy function and $U$ is the [confluent hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/1516095). [Janson](wikipedia://en/27867508) and Louchard (2007) show that

$$f_{A_+} (x) \sim \frac{72 \sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{\pi}} x^2 e^{- 6 x^2} \ \ \text{ as } \ \ x \rightarrow \infty,$$

and

$$P(A_+ > x) \sim \frac{6 \sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{\pi}} x e^{- 6x^2} \ \ \text{ as } \ \ x \rightarrow \infty.$$

They also give higher-order expansions in both cases.

Janson (2007) gives moments of $A_+$ and many other area functionals. In particular,

$$E (A_+) = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}, \ \ E(A_+^2) = \frac{5}{12} \approx 0.416666 \ldots, \ \ \operatorname{Var}(A_+) = \frac{5}{12} - \frac{\pi}{8} \approx .0239675 \ldots \ .$$

Brownian excursions also arise in connection with queuing problems,[^o18] railway traffic,[^o19][^o20] and the heights of random rooted binary trees.[^o21]
