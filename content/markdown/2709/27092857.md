# Robert Breusch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Breusch"}

**Robert Hermann Breusch** (April 2, 1907 – March 29, 1995) was an American [number theorist](wikipedia://en/21527), the William J. Walker Professor of Mathematics at [Amherst College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst_College).[^o2][^o3]

:::

Breusch was born in [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), and studied mathematics both at the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg) and the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin).[^o2][^o4] Unable to secure a university position after receiving his doctorate, Breusch became a schoolteacher near Freiburg, where he met his future wife, Kate Dreyfuss; Breusch was Protestant, but Dreyfuss was Jewish, and the two of them left Nazi Germany for [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile) in the mid-1930s.[^o2][^o5] They married there, and Breusch found a faculty position at [Federico Santa María Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Santa_María_Technical_University) in [Valparaíso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valparaíso).[^o2] In 1939, they left Chile for the United States, inviting [Robert Frucht](wikipedia://en/27069553) to take Breusch's place at Santa María;[^o6] after some years working again as a schoolteacher, Breusch found a position at Amherst College in 1943. He became the Walker professor in 1970, and retired to become an emeritus professor in 1973.[^o2][^o3] The Robert H. Breusch Prize in Mathematics, for the best senior thesis from an Amherst student, was endowed in his memory.[^o2]

As a mathematician, Breusch was known for his new proof of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692)[^o2][^o7] and for the many solutions he provided to problems posed in the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)*.[^o2] His thesis work combined [Bertrand's postulate](wikipedia://en/232526) with [Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/101453) by showing that each of the progressions 3*i* + 1, 3*i* + 2, 4*i* + 1, and 4*i* + 3 (for *i* = 0, 1, 2, ...) contains a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) between *x* and 2*x* for every *x* ≥ 7.[^o4][^o8][^o9] For instance, he proved that for n > 47 there is at least one prime between n and (9/8)n. He also wrote a [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) textbook, *Calculus and Analytic Geometry with Applications* (Prindle, Weber & Schmidt, 1969) with [C. Stanley Ogilvy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Stanley_Ogilvy).
