# Weak equivalence (homotopy theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weak equivalence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **weak equivalence** is a notion from [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) that in some sense identifies objects that have the same "shape". This notion is formalized in the [axiomatic](wikipedia://en/928) definition of a [model category](wikipedia://en/2895304).

:::

A model category is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) with classes of [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) called weak equivalences, [fibrations](wikipedia://en/316826), and [cofibrations](wikipedia://en/1697216), satisfying several axioms. The associated [homotopy category](wikipedia://en/2040756) of a model category has the same objects, but the morphisms are changed in order to make the weak equivalences into [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828). It is a useful observation that the associated homotopy category depends only on the weak equivalences, not on the fibrations and cofibrations.

## Topological spaces {#Topological_spaces}

Model categories were defined by [Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519) as an axiomatization of homotopy theory that applies to [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450), but also to many other categories in [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). The example that started the subject is the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051) with [Serre fibrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_fibration) as fibrations and **weak homotopy equivalences** as weak equivalences (the cofibrations for this model structure can be described as the [retracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retract_%28topology%29) of relative cell complexes *X* ⊆ *Y*[^o3]). By definition, a [continuous mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_mapping) *f*: *X* → *Y* of spaces is called a weak homotopy equivalence if the induced function on sets of [path components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_component)

$$f_*\colon \pi_0(X) \to \pi_0(Y)$$

is [bijective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijective), and for every point *x* in *X* and every *n* ≥ 1, the induced [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396)

$$f_*\colon \pi_n(X,x) \to \pi_n(Y,f(x))$$

on [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) is bijective. (For *X* and *Y* [path-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path-connected), the first condition is automatic, and it suffices to state the second condition for a single point *x* in *X*.)

[Whitehead theorem](wikipedia://en/976793) implies that weak homotopy equivalence between CW-complexes actually is a homotopy equivalence. For [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) topological spaces *X* and *Y*, a map *f*: *X* → *Y* is a weak homotopy equivalence if and only if the induced homomorphism *f*<sub>\*</sub>: *H*<sub>*n*</sub>(*X*,**Z**) → *H*<sub>*n*</sub>(*Y*,**Z**) on [singular homology](wikipedia://en/431041) groups is bijective for all *n*.[^o4] Likewise, for simply connected spaces *X* and *Y*, a map *f*: *X* → *Y* is a weak homotopy equivalence if and only if the pullback homomorphism *f*\*: *H*<sup>*n*</sup>(*Y*,**Z**) → *H*<sup>*n*</sup>(*X*,**Z**) on [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology) is bijective for all *n*.[^o5]

Example: Let *X* be the set of natural numbers {0, 1, 2, ...} and let *Y* be the set {0} ∪ {1, 1/2, 1/3, ...}, both with the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) from the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line). Define *f*: *X* → *Y* by mapping 0 to 0 and *n* to 1/*n* for positive integers *n*. Then *f* is continuous, and in fact a weak homotopy equivalence, but it is not a [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence).

The homotopy category of topological spaces (obtained by inverting the weak homotopy equivalences) greatly simplifies the category of topological spaces. Indeed, this homotopy category is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the category of [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022) with morphisms being [homotopy classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_class) of continuous maps.

Many other model structures on the category of topological spaces have also been considered. For example, in the Strøm model structure on topological spaces, the fibrations are the [Hurewicz fibrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurewicz_fibration) and the weak equivalences are the homotopy equivalences.[^o6]

## Chain complexes {#Chain_complexes}

Some other important model categories involve [chain complexes](wikipedia://en/138404). Let *A* be a [Grothendieck abelian category](wikipedia://en/30051538), for example the category of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) or the category of [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) on a topological space. Define a category *C*(*A*) with objects the complexes *X* of objects in *A*,

$$\cdots\to X_1\to X_0\to X_{-1}\to\cdots,$$

and morphisms the [chain maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_map). (It is equivalent to consider "cochain complexes" of objects of *A*, where the numbering is written as

$$\cdots\to X^{-1}\to X^0\to X^1\to\cdots,$$

simply by defining *X*<sup>*i*</sup> = *X*<sub>−*i*</sub>.)

The category *C*(*A*) has a model structure in which the cofibrations are the [monomorphisms](wikipedia://en/59538) and the weak equivalences are the **[quasi-isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/3653039)**.[^o8] By definition, a chain map *f*: *X* → *Y* is a quasi-isomorphism if the induced homomorphism

$$f_*\colon H_n(X) \to H_n(Y)$$

on [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) is an isomorphism for all integers *n*. (Here *H*<sub>*n*</sub>(*X*) is the object of *A* defined as the [kernel](wikipedia://en/142616) of *X*<sub>*n*</sub> → *X*<sub>*n*−1</sub> modulo the [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of *X*<sub>*n*+1</sub> → *X*<sub>*n*</sub>.) The resulting homotopy category is called the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) *D*(*A*).

## Trivial fibrations and trivial cofibrations {#Trivial_fibrations_and_trivial_cofibrations}

In any model category, a fibration that is also a weak equivalence is called a **trivial** (or **acyclic**) **fibration**. A cofibration that is also a weak equivalence is called a **trivial** (or **acyclic**) **cofibration**.
