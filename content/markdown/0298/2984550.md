# Roger Heath-Brown

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Roger Heath-Brown"}

**David Rodney**"**Roger**"**Heath-Brown** (born 12 October 1952) is a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in the field of [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513).[^o3]

:::

## Education {#Education}

He was an undergraduate and graduate student of [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge); his research supervisor was [Alan Baker](wikipedia://en/18950800).[^o5]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

In 1979 he moved to the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), where from 1999 he held a [professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professorship) in [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210).[^o8] He retired in 2016.[^o9]

Heath-Brown is known for many striking results. He [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that there are infinitely many [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) of the form *x*<sup>3</sup> + 2*y*<sup>3</sup>.[^o10] In collaboration with [S. J. Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._J._Patterson) in 1978 he proved the [Kummer conjecture on cubic Gauss sums](wikipedia://en/6006062) in its equidistribution form. He has applied [Burgess's method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgess's_method) on [character sums](wikipedia://en/2986034) to the [ranks](wikipedia://en/51244926) of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) in families. He proved that every non-singular cubic form over the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) in at least ten variables represents 0.[^o11] Heath-Brown also showed that [Linnik's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linnik's_constant) is less than or equal to 5.5.[^o12] More recently, Heath-Brown is known for his pioneering work on the so-called determinant method. Using this method he was able to prove a conjecture of [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483)[^o13] in the four variable case in 2002.[^o14] This particular conjecture of Serre, on the number of rational points on an integral algebraic variety, was later dubbed the "dimension growth conjecture" and was almost completely solved by various works of Browning, Heath-Brown, and Salberger by 2009.[^o15]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

The [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) has awarded Heath-Brown the Junior [Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prize) (1981), the Senior Berwick Prize (1996),[^o17] and the [Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya_Prize_%28LMS%29) (2009). He was made a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1993,[^o8] and a corresponding member of the [Göttingen Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1999.[^o18]

He was an invited speaker at [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1983 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) and in 2010 in [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad,_India) on the topic of "*Number Theory.*"[^o19]

In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o20] In 2022 the Royal Society awarded him the [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal) "for his many important contributions to the study of prime numbers and solutions to equations in integers".[^o21]

Heath-Brown was appointed [Officer of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (OBE) in the [2024 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_New_Year_Honours) for services to mathematics and mathematical research.

## Other {#Other}

In September 2007, he co-authored (along with [Joseph H. Silverman](wikipedia://en/2288703)) the preface to the Oxford University Sixth Edition of the classic text *[An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers](wikipedia://en/31962097)* by [G.H. Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.H._Hardy) and [E.M. Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.M._Wright).
