# Character sum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Character sum"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **character sum** is a sum $\sum \chi(n)$ of values of a [Dirichlet character](wikipedia://en/174515) χ *[modulo](wikipedia://en/20087)* *N*, taken over a given range of values of *n*. Such sums are basic in a number of questions, for example in the distribution of [quadratic residues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_residues), and in particular in the classical question of finding an upper bound for the [least quadratic non-residue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_quadratic_non-residue) *modulo* *N*. Character sums are often closely linked to [exponential sums](wikipedia://en/468937) by the [Gauss sums](wikipedia://en/6378204) (this is like a finite [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297)).

:::

Assume χ is a non-principal Dirichlet character to the modulus *N*.

## Sums over ranges {#Sums_over_ranges}

The sum taken over all residue classes mod *N* is then zero. This means that the cases of interest will be sums $\Sigma$ over relatively short ranges, of length *R* \< *N* say,

$$M \le n < M + R.$$

A fundamental improvement on the trivial estimate $\Sigma = O(N)$ is the [Pólya–Vinogradov inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_residues#The_P.C3.B3lya.E2.80.93Vinogradov_inequality), established independently by [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) and [I. M. Vinogradov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._M._Vinogradov) in 1918,[^n1][^n2] stating in [big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578) that

$$\Sigma = O(\sqrt{N}\log N).$$

Assuming the [generalized Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/152567), [Hugh Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Montgomery_%28mathematician%29) and [R. C. Vaughan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Charles_Vaughan_%28mathematician%29) have shown[^n3] that there is the further improvement

$$\Sigma = O(\sqrt{N}\log\log N).$$

## Summing polynomials {#Summing_polynomials}

Another significant type of character sum is that formed by

$$\sum \chi(F(n))$$

for some function *F*, generally a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000). A classical result is the case of a quadratic, for example,

$$F(n) = n(n + 1)$$

and χ a [Legendre symbol](wikipedia://en/18567). Here the sum can be evaluated (as −1), a result that is connected to the [local zeta-function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_zeta-function) of a [conic section](wikipedia://en/19008673).

More generally, such sums for the [Jacobi symbol](wikipedia://en/97313) relate to local zeta-functions of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) and [hyperelliptic curves](wikipedia://en/499361); this means that by means of [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019)'s results, for *N* = *p* a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666), there are non-trivial bounds

$$O(\sqrt{p}).$$

The constant implicit in the notation is [linear](wikipedia://en/152111) in the [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) of the curve in question, and so (Legendre symbol or hyperelliptic case) can be taken as the degree of *F*. (More general results, for other values of *N*, can be obtained starting from there.)

Weil's results also led to the *[Burgess bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgess_bound)*,[^n4] applying to give non-trivial results beyond Pólya–Vinogradov, for *R* a power of *N* greater than 1/4.

Assume the modulus *N* is a prime.

$$\begin{aligned} \Sigma & \ll p^{1/2} \log p , \\[6pt] \Sigma & \ll 2 R^{1/2} p^{3/16} \log p , \\[6pt] \Sigma & \ll r R^{1-1/r} p^{(r+1)/4r^2} (\log p)^{1/2r} \end{aligned}$$

for any integer *r* ≥ 3.[^n5]
