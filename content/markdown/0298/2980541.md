# Clifford's theorem on special divisors

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clifford's theorem on special divisors"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Clifford's theorem on special divisors** is a result of (txt) on [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260), showing the constraints on [special linear systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_linear_system) on a curve *C*.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

A [divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor_on_an_algebraic_curve) on a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) *C* is a [formal sum](wikipedia://en/498958) $\textstyle D = \sum_P m_P P$ of points *P* on *C* with integer coefficients. One considers a divisor as a set of constraints on [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857) in the [function field](wikipedia://en/2783843) of *C,* defining $L(D)$ as the vector space of functions having poles only at points of *D* with positive coefficient, *at most as bad* as the coefficient indicates, and having zeros at points of *D* with negative coefficient, with *at least* that multiplicity. The dimension of $L(D)$ is finite, and denoted $\ell(D)$. The [linear system of divisors](wikipedia://en/1186886) attached to *D* is the corresponding [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) of dimension $\ell(D)-1$.

The other significant invariant of *D* is its degree *d*, which is the sum of all its coefficients.

A divisor is called *[special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_divisor)* if *ℓ*(*K* − *D*) > 0, where *K* is the [canonical divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_divisor).[^o5]

**Clifford's theorem** states that for an effective [special divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_divisor) *D*, one has:

$$2(\ell(D)- 1) \le d,$$

and that equality holds only if *D* is zero or a canonical divisor, or if *C* is a [hyperelliptic curve](wikipedia://en/499361) and *D* linearly equivalent to an integral multiple of a hyperelliptic divisor.

The **Clifford index** of *C* is then defined as the minimum of $d - 2(\ell(D) - 1)$ taken over all special divisors (except canonical and trivial), and Clifford's theorem states this is non-negative. It can be shown that the Clifford index for a *generic* curve of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) *g* is equal to the [floor function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floor_function) $\lfloor\tfrac{g-1}{2}\rfloor.$

The Clifford index measures how far the curve is from being hyperelliptic. It may be thought of as a refinement of the [gonality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonality): in many cases the Clifford index is equal to the gonality minus 2.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Green's conjecture"}

## Green's conjecture {#Green's_conjecture}

A conjecture of [Mark Green](wikipedia://en/56936978) states that the Clifford index for a curve over the complex numbers that is not hyperelliptic should be determined by the extent to which *C* as [canonical curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_curve) has linear syzygies. In detail, one defines the invariant *a*(*C*) in terms of the minimal [free resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_resolution) of the [homogeneous coordinate ring](wikipedia://en/23833909) of *C* in its canonical embedding, as the largest index *i* for which the [graded Betti number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_Betti_number) β<sub>*i*, *i* + 2</sub> is zero. Green and [Robert Lazarsfeld](wikipedia://en/29022720) showed that *a*(*C*) + 1 is a lower bound for the Clifford index, and **Green's conjecture** states that equality always holds. There are numerous partial results.[^o9]

[Claire Voisin](wikipedia://en/5980475) was awarded the [Ruth Lyttle Satter Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/37554140) for her solution of the generic case of Green's conjecture in two papers.[^o10][^o11] The case of Green's conjecture for *generic* curves had attracted a huge amount of effort by algebraic geometers over twenty years before finally being laid to rest by Voisin.[^o12] The conjecture for *arbitrary* curves remains open.

:::
