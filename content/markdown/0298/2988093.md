# Kodaira vanishing theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kodaira vanishing theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Kodaira vanishing theorem** is a basic result of [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) theory and complex [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), describing general conditions under which [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) groups with indices *q* > 0 are automatically zero. The implications for the group with index *q* = 0 is usually that its dimension — the number of independent [global sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_section) — coincides with a [holomorphic Euler characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_Euler_characteristic) that can be computed using the [Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/2892412).

:::

## The complex analytic case {#The_complex_analytic_case}

The statement of [Kunihiko Kodaira](wikipedia://en/646326)'s result is that if *M* is a compact [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) of complex dimension *n*, *L* any [holomorphic line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_line_bundle) on *M* that is [positive](wikipedia://en/10592733), and *K<sub>M</sub>* is the [canonical line bundle](wikipedia://en/734893), then

$$H^q(M, K_M\otimes L) = 0$$

for *q* > 0. Here $K_M\otimes L$ stands for the [tensor product of line bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_product_of_line_bundles). By means of [Serre duality](wikipedia://en/359175), one also obtains the vanishing of $H^q(M, L^{\otimes-1})$ for *q* \< *n*. There is a generalisation, the [Kodaira–Nakano vanishing theorem](wikipedia://en/44745308), in which $K_M\otimes L\cong\Omega^n(L)$, where Ω<sup>*n*</sup>(*L*) denotes the sheaf of [holomorphic (*n*,0)-forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolbeault_complex) on *M* with values on *L*, is replaced by Ω<sup>*r*</sup>(*L*), the sheaf of holomorphic (*r*,0)-forms with values on *L*. Then the cohomology group H<sup>*q*</sup>(*M*, Ω<sup>*r*</sup>(*L*)) vanishes whenever *q* + *r* > *n*.

## The algebraic case {#The_algebraic_case}

The Kodaira vanishing theorem can be formulated within the language of algebraic geometry without any reference to *transcendental* methods such as Kähler metrics. Positivity of the line bundle *L* translates into the corresponding [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859) being [ample](wikipedia://en/1186896) (i.e., some tensor power gives a projective embedding). The algebraic Kodaira–Akizuki–Nakano vanishing theorem is the following statement:

If *k* is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero, *X* is a [smooth](wikipedia://en/7837041) and [projective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_morphism) *k*-[scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) of dimension *d*, and *L* is an ample invertible sheaf on *X*, then[^o4]

$$H^q(X,L\otimes\Omega^p_{X/k}) = 0 \text{ for } p+q>d,$$

where Ω<sup>p</sup> denotes the [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of relative (algebraic) [differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_forms) (see [Kähler differential](wikipedia://en/603916)).

(Raynaud 1978) showed that this result does not always hold over fields of characteristic *p* > 0, and in particular fails for [Raynaud surfaces](wikipedia://en/22712664). Later (Sommese 1986) give a counterexample for singular varieties with non-log canonical singularities,[^o7] and also,(Lauritzen Rao 1997) gave elementary counterexamples inspired by proper [homogeneous spaces](wikipedia://en/363325) with non-reduced stabilizers.

Until 1987 the only known proof in characteristic zero was however based on the complex analytic proof and the [GAGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAGA) comparison theorems. However, in 1987 [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) and [Luc Illusie](wikipedia://en/27206965) gave a purely algebraic proof of the vanishing theorem in (Deligne Illusie 1987). Their proof is based on showing that the [Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/43662331) for [algebraic de Rham cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_de_Rham_cohomology) degenerates in degree 1. This is shown by lifting a corresponding more specific result from characteristic *p* > 0 — the positive-characteristic result does not hold without limitations but can be lifted to provide the full result.

## Consequences and applications {#Consequences_and_applications}

Historically, the [Kodaira embedding theorem](wikipedia://en/2986151) was derived with the help of the vanishing theorem. With application of Serre duality, the vanishing of various sheaf cohomology groups (usually related to the canonical line bundle) of curves and surfaces help with the classification of complex manifolds, e.g. [Enriques–Kodaira classification](wikipedia://en/3029260).
