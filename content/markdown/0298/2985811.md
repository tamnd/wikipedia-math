# Signed measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Signed measure"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **signed measure** is a generalization of the concept of (positive) [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) by allowing the set function to take [negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_and_positive_numbers) values, i.e., to acquire [sign](wikipedia://en/7951270).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

There are two slightly different concepts of a signed measure, depending on whether or not one allows it to take infinite values. Signed measures are usually only allowed to take finite [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) values, while some textbooks allow them to take infinite values. To avoid confusion, this article will call these two cases "finite signed measures" and "extended signed measures".

Given a [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333) $(X, \Sigma)$ (that is, a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $X$ with a [σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/29586) $\Sigma$ on it), an **extended signed measure** is a [set function](wikipedia://en/25911217)

$$\mu : \Sigma \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty,-\infty\}$$

such that $\mu(\varnothing) = 0$ and $\mu$ is [σ-additive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_additivity) – that is, it satisfies the equality

$$\mu\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\right) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \mu(A_n)$$

for any [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_n, \ldots$ of [disjoint sets](wikipedia://en/39359) in $\Sigma.$ The series on the right must [converge absolutely](wikipedia://en/61532) when the value of the left-hand side is finite. One consequence is that an extended signed measure can take $+\infty$ or $-\infty$ as a value, but not both. The expression $\infty - \infty$ is undefined[^o4] and must be avoided.

A **finite signed measure** (a.k.a. **real measure**) is defined in the same way, except that it is only allowed to take real values. That is, it cannot take $+\infty$ or $-\infty.$

Finite signed measures form a real [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), while extended signed measures do not because they are not closed under addition. On the other hand, measures are extended signed measures, but are not in general finite signed measures.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Consider a [non-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative) measure $\nu$ on the space (*X*, Σ) and a [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) *f*: *X* → **R** such that

$$\int_X \! |f(x)| \, d\nu (x) < \infty.$$

Then, a finite signed measure is given by

$$\mu (A) = \int_A \! f(x) \, d\nu (x)$$

for all *A* in Σ.

This signed measure takes only finite values. To allow it to take +∞ as a value, one needs to replace the assumption about *f* being absolutely integrable with the more relaxed condition

$$\int_X \! f^-(x) \, d\nu (x) < \infty,$$

where *f*<sup>−</sup>(*x*) = max(−*f*(*x*), 0) is the [negative part](wikipedia://en/3344921) of *f*.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

What follows are two results which will imply that an extended signed measure is the difference of two non-negative measures, and a finite signed measure is the difference of two finite non-negative measures.

The [Hahn decomposition theorem](wikipedia://en/2985858) states that given a signed measure *μ*, there exist two measurable sets *P* and *N* such that:

1. *P*∪*N* = *X* and *P*∩*N* = ∅;
1. *μ*(*E*) ≥ 0 for each *E* in Σ such that *E* ⊆ *P* — in other words, *P* is a [positive set](wikipedia://en/3375352);
1. *μ*(*E*) ≤ 0 for each *E* in Σ such that *E* ⊆ *N* — that is, *N* is a negative set.

Moreover, this decomposition is unique [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) adding to/subtracting *μ*-[null sets](wikipedia://en/21520) from *P* and *N*.

Consider then two non-negative measures *μ*<sup>\+</sup> and *μ*<sup>−</sup> defined by

$$\mu^+(E) = \mu(P\cap E)$$

and

$$\mu^-(E)=-\mu(N\cap E)$$

for all measurable sets *E*, that is, *E* in Σ.

One can check that both *μ*<sup>\+</sup> and *μ*<sup>−</sup> are non-negative measures, with one taking only finite values, and are called the *positive part* and *negative part* of *μ*, respectively. One has that *μ* = *μ*<sup>\+</sup> − μ<sup>−</sup>. The measure |*μ*| = *μ*<sup>\+</sup> + *μ*<sup>−</sup> is called the *variation* of *μ*, and its maximum possible value, ||*μ*|| = |*μ*|(*X*), is called the *[total variation](wikipedia://en/683561)* of *μ*.

This consequence of the Hahn decomposition theorem is called the *Jordan decomposition*. The measures *μ*<sup>\+</sup>, *μ*<sup>−</sup> and |*μ*| are independent of the choice of *P* and *N* in the Hahn decomposition theorem.

:::

## The space of signed measures {#The_space_of_signed_measures}

The sum of two finite signed measures is a finite signed measure, as is the product of a finite signed measure by a real number – that is, they are closed under [linear combinations](wikipedia://en/55632). It follows that the set of finite signed measures on a measurable space (*X*, Σ) is a real [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370); this is in contrast to positive measures, which are only closed under [conical combinations](wikipedia://en/19634578), and thus form a [convex cone](wikipedia://en/3558266) but not a vector space. Furthermore, the [total variation](wikipedia://en/683561) defines a [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) in respect to which the space of finite signed measures becomes a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989). This space has even more structure, in that it can be shown to be a [Dedekind complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind_complete) [Banach lattice](wikipedia://en/8099018) and in so doing the [Radon–Nikodym theorem](wikipedia://en/338746) can be shown to be a special case of the [Freudenthal spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/34121965).

If *X* is a compact separable space, then the space of finite signed Baire measures is the dual of the real Banach space of all [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) real-valued functions on *X*, by the [Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem](wikipedia://en/38948504).
