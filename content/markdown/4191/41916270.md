# Hadley Wickham

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hadley Wickham"}

**Hadley Alexander Wickham** (born 14 October 1979) is a [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand) [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) known for his work on [open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) for the [R statistical programming environment](wikipedia://en/376707). He is the [chief scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_scientific_officer) at [Posit PBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posit_PBC) and an adjunct professor of statistics at the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University). His work includes the [data visualisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_visualisation) [system](wikipedia://en/80435299) [ggplot2](wikipedia://en/32380922) and the [tidyverse](wikipedia://en/59164935), a collection of [R packages](wikipedia://en/65740838) for [data science](wikipedia://en/35458904) based on the concept of [tidy data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidy_data).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Wickham was born in [Hamilton, New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_New_Zealand). He received a bachelor's degree in [human biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_biology) and a master's degree in statistics at the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland) in 1999–2004 and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) at [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University) in 2008, supervised by [Di Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Di_Cook) and [Heike Hofmann](wikipedia://en/52167372).[^o5]

His sister, Charlotte Wickham, is also a statistician, data scientist and educator. She taught in the Statistics Department at [Oregon State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University) between 2011 and 2022,[^o6] and currently works for Posit PBC on the developer relations team.[^o7] She holds a first-class honours bachelor of science degree in Statistics from University of Auckland and a PhD in statistics from [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

Wickham is the [chief scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_scientific_officer) at [Posit PBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posit_PBC) (formerly RStudio PBC)[^o10] and an adjunct professor of statistics at the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University).[^o11][^o12][^o13]

He is a prominent and active member of the [R](wikipedia://en/376707) user community, and has developed several notable and widely used packages including [ggplot2](wikipedia://en/32380922), plyr, [dplyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dplyr) and reshape2.[^o13][^o14] Wickham's data analysis packages for R are collectively known as the [tidyverse](wikipedia://en/59164935).[^o15] According to Wickham's [tidy data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidy_data) approach, each [variable](wikipedia://en/3728109) should be a [column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_%28database%29), each observation should be a [row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Row_%28database%29), and each type of observational unit should be a [table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_%28database%29).[^o16]

### Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

In 2006 he was awarded the [John Chambers](wikipedia://en/16745085) Award for Statistical Computing for his work developing tools for data reshaping and visualisation.[^o18] Wickham was named a Fellow by the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 2015 for "pivotal contributions to statistical practice through innovative and pioneering research in statistical graphics and computing".[^o19] Wickham was awarded the international [COPSS Presidents' Award](wikipedia://en/11690483) in 2019 for "influential work in statistical computing, visualisation, graphics, and data analysis" including "making statistical thinking and computing accessible to a large audience".[^o20]
