# Groupoid object

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Groupoid object"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **groupoid object** is both a generalization of a [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) which is built on richer structures than sets, and a generalization of a [group objects](wikipedia://en/53507) when the multiplication is only [partially defined](wikipedia://en/23577).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **groupoid object** in a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) **C** admitting finite [fiber products](wikipedia://en/841689) consists of a pair of [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) $R, U$ together with five [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891)

$$s, t: R \to U, \ e: U \to R, \ m: R \times_{U, t, s} R \to R, \ i: R \to R$$

satisfying the following groupoid axioms

1. $s \circ e = t \circ e = 1_U, \, s \circ m = s \circ p_1, t \circ m = t \circ p_2$ where the $p_i: R \times_{U, t, s} R \to R$ are the two projections,
1. (associativity) $m \circ (1_R \times m) = m \circ (m \times 1_R),$
1. (unit) $m \circ (e \circ s, 1_R) = m \circ (1_R, e \circ t) = 1_R,$
1. (inverse) $i \circ i = 1_R$, $s \circ i = t, \, t \circ i = s$, $m \circ (1_R, i) = e \circ s, \, m \circ (i, 1_R) = e \circ t$.[^o4]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

### Group objects {#Group_objects}

A [group object](wikipedia://en/53507) is a special case of a groupoid object, where $R = U$ and $s = t$. One recovers therefore [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315) by taking the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051), or [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) by taking the [category of manifolds](wikipedia://en/10059981), etc.

### Groupoids {#Groupoids}

A groupoid object in the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) is precisely a [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) in the usual sense: a category in which every morphism is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828). Indeed, given such a category **C**, take *U* to be the set of all objects in **C**, *R* the set of all morphisms in **C**, the five morphisms given by $s(x \to y) = x, \, t(x \to y) = y$, $m(f, g) = g \circ f$, $e(x) = 1_x$ and $i(f) = f^{-1}$. When the term "groupoid" can naturally refer to a groupoid object in some particular category in mind, the term **groupoid set** is used to refer to a groupoid object in the category of sets.

However, unlike in the previous example with Lie groups, a groupoid object in the category of manifolds is not necessarily a [Lie groupoid](wikipedia://en/310923), since the maps *s* and *t* fail to satisfy further requirements (they are not necessarily [submersions](wikipedia://en/356413)).

### Groupoid schemes {#Groupoid_schemes}

A **groupoid *S*-scheme** is a groupoid object in the category of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) over some fixed base scheme *S*. If $U = S$, then a groupoid scheme (where $s = t$ are necessarily the structure map) is the same as a [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523). A groupoid scheme is also called an **algebraic groupoid**,[^n1] to convey the idea it is a generalization of [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) and their actions.

For example, suppose an algebraic group *G* [acts](wikipedia://en/56600253) from the right on a scheme *U*. Then take $R = U \times G$, *s* the projection, *t* the given action. This determines a groupoid scheme.

## Constructions {#Constructions}

Given a groupoid object (*R*, *U*), the [equalizer](wikipedia://en/310767) of $R \,\overset{s}\underset{t}\rightrightarrows\, U$, if any, is a group object called the **inertia group** of the groupoid. The [coequalizer](wikipedia://en/431940) of the same diagram, if any, is the quotient of the groupoid.

Each groupoid object in a category **C** (if any) may be thought of as a [contravariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) from **C** to the category of groupoids. This way, each groupoid object determines a [prestack](wikipedia://en/39547490) in groupoids. This prestack is not a [stack](wikipedia://en/13185596) but it can be [stackified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stackification) to yield a stack.

The main use of the notion is that it provides an [atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_%28stack%29) for a stack. More specifically, let $[R \rightrightarrows U]$ be the category of [$(R \rightrightarrows U)$-torsors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsor_under_a_groupoid). Then it is a [category fibered in groupoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_fibered_in_groupoids); in fact (in a nice case), a [Deligne–Mumford stack](wikipedia://en/40639179). Conversely, any DM stack is of this form.
