# Clebsch graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Clebsch graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Clebsch graph** is either of two [complementary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complement_%28graph_theory%29) graphs on 16 vertices, a 5-[regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) with 40 edges and a 10-regular graph with 80 edges. The 80-edge graph is the dimension-5 [halved cube graph](wikipedia://en/39329236); it was called the Clebsch graph by Seidel (1968)[^o3] because of its relation to the configuration of 16 lines on the quartic surface discovered in 1868 by the German mathematician [Alfred Clebsch](wikipedia://en/1369957). The 40-edge version is the dimension-5 [folded cube graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folded_cube_graph); it is also known as the **Greenwood–Gleason graph** after the work of (txt), who used it to evaluate the [Ramsey number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_number) *R*(3,3,3) = 17.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The dimension-5 [folded cube graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folded_cube_graph) (the 5-regular Clebsch graph) may be constructed by adding edges between opposite pairs of vertices in a 4-dimensional hypercube graph. (In an *n*-dimensional hypercube, a pair of vertices are *opposite* if the shortest path between them has *n* edges.) Alternatively, it can be formed from a 5-dimensional hypercube graph by [identifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_identification) together (or contracting) every opposite pair of vertices.

Another construction, leading to the same graph, is to create a vertex for each element of the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) GF(16), and connect two vertices by an edge whenever the difference between the corresponding two field elements is a [perfect cube](wikipedia://en/451445).[^o10]

The dimension-5 [halved cube graph](wikipedia://en/39329236) (the 10-regular Clebsch graph) is the [complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complement_%28graph_theory%29) of the 5-regular graph. It may also be constructed from the vertices of a 5-dimensional hypercube, by connecting pairs of vertices whose [Hamming distance](wikipedia://en/41227) is exactly two. This construction is an instance of the construction of [Frankl–Rödl graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankl–Rödl_graph). It produces two subsets of 16 vertices that are disconnected from each other; both of these [half-squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-square) of the hypercube are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) to the 10-regular Clebsch graph. Two copies of the 5-regular Clebsch graph can be produced in the same way from a 5-dimensional hypercube, by connecting pairs of vertices whose Hamming distance is exactly four.

## Properties {#Properties}

The 5-regular Clebsch graph is a [strongly regular graph](wikipedia://en/1399856) of degree 5 with parameters $(v,k,\lambda,\mu) = (16, 5, 0, 2)$.[^o13][^o14] Its complement, the 10-regular Clebsch graph, is therefore also a strongly regular graph,[^o15][^o6] with parameters $(16, 10, 6, 6)$.

The 5-regular Clebsch graph is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph), [non planar](wikipedia://en/24314) and [non Eulerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eulerian_graph). It is also both 5-[vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) and 5-[edge-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-edge-connected_graph). The [subgraph that is induced](wikipedia://en/1655188) by the ten non-neighbors of any vertex in this graph forms an [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) copy of the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926).

It has [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 4 and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 3.[^o16]

*Image: K\_16 partitioned into three Clebsch graphs.svg.* K<sub>16</sub> 3-coloured as three Clebsch graphs.

The edges of the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K*<sub>16</sub> may be partitioned into three disjoint copies of the 5-regular Clebsch graph. Because the Clebsch graph is a [triangle-free graph](wikipedia://en/7667996), this shows that there is a triangle-free three-coloring of the edges of *K*<sub>16</sub>; that is, that the [Ramsey number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_number) *R*(3,3,3) describing the minimum number of vertices in a complete graph without a triangle-free three-coloring is at least 17. (Greenwood Gleason 1955) used this construction as part of their proof that *R*(3,3,3) = 17.[^o7][^o18]

The 5-regular Clebsch graph may be [colored](wikipedia://en/426743) with four colors, but not three: its largest [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) has five vertices, not enough to partition the graph into three independent color classes. It contains as an [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) the [Grötzsch graph](wikipedia://en/7685783), the smallest [triangle-free](wikipedia://en/7667996) four-chromatic graph, and every four-chromatic induced subgraph of the Clebsch graph is a supergraph of the Grötzsch graph. More strongly, every triangle-free four-chromatic graph with no [induced path](wikipedia://en/7714430) of length six or more is an induced subgraph of the Clebsch graph and an induced supergraph of the Grötzsch graph.[^o19]

The 5-regular Clebsch graph is the [Keller graph](wikipedia://en/33297462) of dimension two, part of a family of graphs used to find tilings of high-dimensional [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697) by [hypercubes](wikipedia://en/39783) no two of which meet face-to-face.

The 5-regular Clebsch graph can be embedded as a [regular map](wikipedia://en/21533914) in the orientable manifold of genus 5, forming pentagonal faces; and in the non-orientable surface of genus 6, forming tetragonal faces.

::: {#o21 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Algebraic properties"}

### Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the 5-regular Clebsch graph is $(x+3)^5(x-1)^{10}(x-5)$. Because this polynomial can be completely factored into linear terms with integer coefficients, the Clebsch graph is an [integral graph](wikipedia://en/24845923): its [spectrum](wikipedia://en/534914) consists entirely of integers.[^o6] The Clebsch graph is the only graph with this characteristic polynomial, making it a graph determined by its spectrum.

The 5-regular Clebsch graph is a [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) with an automorphism group of order 1920, isomorphic to the [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) $D_5$. As a Cayley graph, its automorphism group acts transitively on its vertices, making it [vertex transitive](wikipedia://en/581175). In fact, it is [arc transitive](wikipedia://en/1577896), hence [edge transitive](wikipedia://en/584406) and [distance transitive](wikipedia://en/7768222). It is also [connected-homogeneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_graph), meaning that every isomorphism between two connected induced subgraphs can be extended to an automorphism of the whole graph.
