# Nathan Fine

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nathan Fine"}

**Nathan Jacob Fine** (22 October 1916 in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia,_Pennsylvania) – 18 November 1994 in [Deerfield Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deerfield_Beach), [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida)) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked on [basic hypergeometric series](wikipedia://en/2233526).

:::

## Сareer {#Сareer}

He is best known for his lecture notes on the subject which for four decades served as an inspiration to experts in the field until they were finally published as a book. He solved the [Jeep problem](wikipedia://en/19749717) in 1946.

Nathan Fine retired in 1978, as a professor at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University). Prior to that he had been on the faculties of the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) and [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). For a brief period (1946–1947) he also worked at the Operations Evaluation Group, affiliated with the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). Beside the book he published about 40 papers in several fields of mathematics. He is known for the Rogers-Fine identity.[^o4]

Nathan Fine received his Ph.D. in 1946 from [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), where he was a student of [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477). Fine was at the Institute for Advanced Study for the three academic years 1953–1954, 1958–1959, and 1959–1960.[^o5] Fine's doctoral students include [J. J. Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Jesse_Price).

He wrote the book *Basic Hypergeometric Series and Applications* .[^o6][^o7]
