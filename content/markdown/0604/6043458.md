# Cuspidal representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cuspidal representation"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **cuspidal representations** are certain [representations](wikipedia://en/12695) of [algebraic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_groups) that occur discretely in $L^2$ spaces.[^n1] The term *cuspidal* is derived, at a certain distance, from the [cusp forms](wikipedia://en/610165) of classical [modular form](wikipedia://en/286000) theory. In the contemporary formulation of [automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation), representations take the place of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110); these representations may be of [adelic algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/1168608).

:::

When the group is the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) $\operatorname{GL}_2$, the cuspidal representations are directly related to cusp forms and [Maass forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maass_form). For the case of cusp forms, each [Hecke eigenform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecke_eigenform) ([newform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newform)) corresponds to a cuspidal representation.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Let $G$ be a [reductive algebraic group](wikipedia://en/1088386) over a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) $K$ and let $\mathbb{A}$ denote the [adeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adele_group) of $K$. The group $G(K)$ embeds diagonally in the group $G(\mathbb{A})$ by sending $g$ in $G(K)$ to the tuple $(g_p)_p$ in $G(\mathbb{A})$ with $g=g_p$ for all (finite and infinite) primes $p$.

Let $Z$ denote the [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_a_group) of $G$ and let $\omega$ be a [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_%28mathematics%29) [unitary character](wikipedia://en/293418) from $Z(K)\setminus Z(\mathbb{A})^\times$ to $\mathbb{C}^\times$. Fix a [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) on $G(\mathbb{A})$ and let $L^2_0(G(K)\setminus G(\mathbb{A}),\omega)$ denote the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) of [complex](wikipedia://en/5826)-valued [measurable functions](wikipedia://en/44055) $f$ on $G(\mathbb{A})$ satisfying

1. $f(\gamma g)=f(g)$ for all $\gamma\in G(K)$,
1. $f(gz)=f(g)\omega(z)$ for all $z\in Z(\mathbb{A})$,
1. $$\textstyle\int_{Z(\mathbb{A})G(K)\,\setminus\, G(\mathbb{A})} |f(g)|^2\,dg < \infty,$$
1. $\textstyle \int_{U(K)\,\setminus \,U(\mathbb{A})}f(ug)\,du = 0$ for all [unipotent radicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unipotent_radical) $U$ of all proper [parabolic subgroups](wikipedia://en/588356) of $G(\mathbb{A})$ and $g\in G(\mathbb{A})$.

The [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $L^2_0(G(K)\setminus G(\mathbb{A}),\omega)$ is called the **space of cusp forms with central character $\omega$** on $G(\mathbb{A})$. A function appearing in such a space is called a **cuspidal function**.

A cuspidal function generates a [unitary representation](wikipedia://en/292788) of the group $G(\mathbb{A})$ on the complex Hilbert space $V_f$ generated by the right translates of $f$. Here the [action](wikipedia://en/12781) of $g\in G(\mathbb{A})$ on $V_f$ is given by

$$(g \cdot u)(x) = u(xg), \qquad u(x) = \sum_j c_j f(xg_j) \in V_f.$$

The space of cusp forms with central character $\omega$ decomposes into a [direct sum of Hilbert spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_Hilbert_spaces)

$$L^2_0(G(K)\setminus G(\mathbb{A}),\omega) = \widehat{\bigoplus}_{(\pi,V_\pi)} m_\pi V_\pi$$

where the sum is over [irreducible](wikipedia://en/385982) [subrepresentations](wikipedia://en/55943952) of $L^2_0(G(K)\setminus G(\mathbb{A}),\omega)$ and the $m_\pi$ are positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) (i.e. each irreducible subrepresentation occurs with *finite* multiplicity). A **cuspidal representation of $G($**$\mathbb{A}$**$)$** is such a subrepresentation $(\pi, V_\pi)$ for some $\omega$.

The groups for which the multiplicities $m_\pi$ all equal $1$ are said to have the [multiplicity-one property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicity-one_property).
