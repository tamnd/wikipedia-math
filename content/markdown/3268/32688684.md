# Graph energy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph energy"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **energy** of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) is the sum of the [absolute values](wikipedia://en/991) of the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of the graph. This quantity is studied in the context of [spectral graph theory](wikipedia://en/534914).

:::

More precisely, let *G* be a graph with *n* [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899). It is assumed that *G* is a [simple graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph), that is, it does not contain loops or parallel edges. Let *A* be the [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of *G* and let $\lambda_i$, $i = 1 , \ldots , n$, be the eigenvalues of *A*. Then the energy of the graph is defined as:

$$E(G) = \sum_{i=1}^n|\lambda_i|.$$
