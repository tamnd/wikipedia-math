# Gino Loria

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gino Loria"}

*Image: Gino Loria.jpg.* Photograph of Gino Loria

*Image: Gino-Loria.jpg.* Portrait of Gino Loria in pencil on paper

**Gino Benedetto Loria** (19 May 1862, [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantua) – 30 January 1954, [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa)) was a [Jewish-Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish-Italian) mathematician and [historian of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian_of_mathematics).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Loria studied mathematics in [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantua), [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), and [Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavia) and received his doctorate in 1883 from the [University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin) under the direction of [Enrico D'Ovidio](wikipedia://en/55588432). For several years, he was D'Ovidio's assistant in Turin. Starting in 1886, he became, as a result of winning a then-customary competition, Professor for Algebra and Analytic Geometry at the [University of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genoa), where he stayed for the remainder of his career.

Loria did research on [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), special curves and [rational transformations in algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/382733), and [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939). At the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) he was an invited speaker in 1897 in Zürich, 1904 in Heidelberg,[^o4][^o5] in 1908 in Rome, in 1912 in Cambridge, UK, in 1924 in Toronto, in 1928 in Bologna, and in 1932 in Zürich.[^o6]

In 1897, he became editor of *Bolletino di Bibliografia e Storia delle Science Matematiche*. In 1916, he published a guide to the study of the history of mathematics.[^o7] A reviewer noted

The amount of information given is really remarkable, and it is well up to date; the author, too, has not shrunk from the disagreeable duty of pointing out works ... which must be used with caution.[^o8]

Loria wrote a history of mathematics and was especially concerned with the history of mathematics in Italy and among the ancient Greeks.

After the Germans seized control of Italy in World War II, [Waldensians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldensians) helped Loria (endangered as a Jew) hide in [Torre Pellice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_Pellice). Loria was elected to the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) and the Turin Academy of Sciences. An asteroid ([27056 Ginoloria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/27056_Ginoloria)) is named after him.
