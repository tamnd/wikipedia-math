# Singular cardinals hypothesis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Singular cardinals hypothesis"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), the **singular cardinals hypothesis** (**SCH**) arose from the question of whether the least [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173) for which the [generalized continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705#The_generalized_continuum_hypothesis) (GCH) might fail could be a [singular cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cardinal).

:::

According to Mitchell (1992), the singular cardinals hypothesis is:

If *κ* is any singular [strong limit cardinal](wikipedia://en/374002), then 2<sup>*κ*</sup> = *κ*<sup>\+</sup>.

Here, *κ*<sup>\+</sup> denotes the [successor cardinal](wikipedia://en/375210) of *κ*.

Since SCH is a consequence of GCH, which is known to be [consistent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistent) with [ZFC](wikipedia://en/152214), SCH is consistent with ZFC. The negation of SCH has also been shown to be consistent with ZFC, if one assumes the existence of a sufficiently large cardinal number. In fact, by results of [Moti Gitik](wikipedia://en/25533079), ZFC + ¬SCH is equiconsistent with ZFC + the existence of a [measurable cardinal](wikipedia://en/248102) *κ* of [Mitchell order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchell_order) *κ*<sup>\++</sup>.

Another form of the SCH is the following statement:

2<sup>cf(*κ*)</sup> \< *κ* implies *κ*<sup>cf(*κ*)</sup> = *κ*<sup>\+</sup>,

where cf denotes the [cofinality](wikipedia://en/6693) function. Note that *κ*<sup>cf(*κ*)</sup>= 2<sup>*κ*</sup> for all singular strong limit cardinals *κ*. The second formulation of SCH is strictly stronger than the first version, since the first one only mentions strong limits. From a [model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_%28logic%29) in which the first version of SCH fails at ℵ<sub>ω</sub> and GCH holds above ℵ<sub>ω+2</sub>, we can construct a model in which the first version of SCH holds but the second version of SCH fails, by adding ℵ<sub>ω</sub> Cohen subsets to ℵ<sub>*n*</sub> for some *n*.

[Jack Silver](wikipedia://en/21626512) proved that if *κ* is singular with uncountable cofinality and 2<sup>*λ*</sup> = *λ*<sup>\+</sup> for all infinite cardinals *λ* \< *κ*, then 2<sup>*κ*</sup> = *κ*<sup>\+</sup>. Silver's original proof used [generic ultrapowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_ultrapowers). The following important fact follows from Silver's theorem: if the singular cardinals hypothesis holds for all singular cardinals of countable cofinality, then it holds for all singular cardinals. In particular, then, if $\kappa$ is the least counterexample to the singular cardinals hypothesis, then $\mathrm{cf}(\kappa) = \mathrm{\omega}$.

The negation of the singular cardinals hypothesis is intimately related to violating the GCH at a measurable cardinal. A well-known result of [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875) is that if the GCH holds below a measurable cardinal $\kappa$ on a set of measure one—i.e., there is normal $\kappa$-complete [ultrafilter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafilter_%28set_theory%29) *D* on $\mathcal{P}(\kappa)$ such that $\{\alpha < \kappa\mid 2^{\alpha} = \alpha^+\}\in D$, then $2^\kappa = \kappa^+$. Starting with $\kappa$ a [supercompact cardinal](wikipedia://en/248116), Silver was able to produce a model of set theory in which $\kappa$ is measurable and in which $2^\kappa > \kappa^+$. Then, by applying [Prikry forcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prikry_forcing) to the measurable $\kappa$, one gets a model of set theory in which $\kappa$ is a strong limit cardinal of countable cofinality and in which $2^\kappa > \kappa^+$—a violation of the SCH. [Gitik](wikipedia://en/25533079), building on work of [Woodin](wikipedia://en/2275071), was able to replace the *supercompact* in Silver's proof with *measurable of Mitchell order $\kappa^{++}$*. That established an upper bound for the consistency strength of the failure of the SCH. Gitik again, using results of [inner model theory](wikipedia://en/3796621), was able to show that a measurable cardinal of Mitchell order $\kappa^{++}$ is also the lower bound for the consistency strength of the failure of SCH.

A wide variety of propositions imply SCH. As was noted above, GCH implies SCH. On the other hand, the [proper forcing axiom](wikipedia://en/6250799), which implies $2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_2$ and hence is incompatible with GCH also implies SCH. [Solovay](wikipedia://en/3160110) showed that large cardinals almost imply SCH—in particular, if $\kappa$ is [strongly compact cardinal](wikipedia://en/1957057), then the SCH holds above $\kappa$. On the other hand, the non-existence of (inner models for) various large cardinals (below a measurable cardinal of Mitchell order $\kappa^{++}$) also imply SCH.
