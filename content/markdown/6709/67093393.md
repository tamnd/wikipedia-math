# Ruth Britto

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ruth Britto"}

**Ruth Alexandra Britto-Pacumio** is an American [mathematical physicist](wikipedia://en/173416) whose research topics include [black holes](wikipedia://en/4650), [Yang–Mills theory](wikipedia://en/672202), and the theory of [Feynman integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_integral); with [Freddy Cachazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freddy_Cachazo), Bo Feng, and [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) she is one of the namesakes of the [BCFW recursion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCFW_recursion) relations for computing [scattering amplitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_amplitude). She is an associate professor in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) at [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin), and is also affiliated with the [Institut de physique théorique - IPhT Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_de_physique_théorique_-_IPhT_Saclay).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Britto is originally from [Binghamton, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binghamton,_New_York), where her father, Ronald Britto, was a professor of [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) at [Binghamton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binghamton_University). As an undergraduate mathematics student at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), she was the 1994 winner of the Elizabeth Lowell Putnam Prize for the best performance by a female student in the [William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition), and the 1995 winner of the [Alice T. Schafer Prize for Excellence in Mathematics by an Undergraduate Woman](wikipedia://en/57181252), awarded by the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698).

She completed her Ph.D. in physics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 2002. Her dissertation, *Bound states of supersymmetric black holes*, was supervised by [Andrew Strominger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Strominger). She was a researcher at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), [Fermilab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermilab), and [CEA Paris-Saclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEA_Paris-Saclay) before joining the Trinity College staff in 2014.
