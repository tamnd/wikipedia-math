# Dilip Madan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dilip Madan"}

**Dilip B. Madan** is an American financial economist, mathematician, academic, and author. He is [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) of finance at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland).[^o3]

:::

Madan is most known for his work on the [variance gamma model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variance_gamma_model), the [fast Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/11512) method for option pricing, and the development of Conic Finance.[^o4] Madan is a recipient of the 2006 [Humboldt Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Research_Award). He has authored several books, including *Applied Conic Finance* and *Nonlinear Valuation and Non-Gaussian Risks in Finance*.[^o5]

## Education {#Education}

Madan completed his [Bachelor of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Commerce) in Accounting from the [University of Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bombay) in 1967. In 1972, he obtained a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in Economics from the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland), followed by another PhD in mathematics in 1975 from the same university.[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

Madan began his academic career in 1972 as an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) of economics at the University of Maryland. In 1976, he joined the [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney) and held various positions, including lecturer in [economic statistics](wikipedia://en/340363) from 1976 to 1979 and senior lecturer in [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390) from 1980 to 1988. Subsequently, he rejoined the University of Maryland, where he was appointed as assistant professor of finance between 1989 and 1992, served as an [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor) of finance between 1992 and 1997, and held an appointment as a professor of finance between 1997 and 2019. Currently, he is professor emeritus of finance at the University of Maryland since 2019.[^o3]

Madan has been a director and treasurer of the Scientific Association of Mathematical Finance since 2021.[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

Madan's [quantitative finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_finance) research has won him the 2021 Northfield Financial Engineer of the Year Award from the International Association for Quantitative Finance.[^o10] He has authored numerous publications spanning the areas of [financial markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_markets), [general equilibrium theory](wikipedia://en/45938), and [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680) including books and articles in peer-reviewed journals.[^o4]

### Valuation model {#Valuation_model}

Madan's valuation model research has contributed to the improvement and development of valuation models in various fields including business and finance.[^o12] In his analysis of the impact of model risk on the valuation of barrier options, he highlighted the divergent pricing outcomes of up-and-out call options resulting from the use of different stochastic processes to calibrate the underlying vanilla options surface.[^o13] He conducted pricing comparisons between Sato processes and conventional models, revealing that Sato processes exhibit relatively higher pricing for cliquets, while effectively preserving the value of long-dated out-of-the-money realized variance options.[^o14] Focusing his research efforts on credit value adjustments, his study proposed a theory of capital requirements to address the problem of cross-default exposures.[^o15] Furthermore, he presented a Markov chain-based method for valuing structured financial products, offering financial institutions a tool to assess locally capped and floored cliquets, as well as unhedged and hedged variance swap contracts.[^o16] He also introduced a conic finance-based nonlinear equity valuation model, which integrated risk charges contingent upon measure distortions.[^o17] More recently in 2016 and 2022, he co-authored with Wim Schoutens two books titled Applied Conic Finance and Nonlinear valuation and non-Gaussian risks in finance, which provided an overview of the newly established conic finance theory, including its theoretical framework and various applications.[^o5]

### Options pricing {#Options_pricing}

Madan's options pricing research has focused on conducting empirical studies to test the performance of various option pricing models using real-world data.[^o19][^o20] While exploring the valuation of European call options employing the Vasicek-Gaussian [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895), his research proposed an approach to approximate and determine the equilibrium change of measure in [incomplete markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incomplete_markets), using log return mean, variance, and kurtosis.[^o21] In a collaborative study with Robert A. Jarrow, he demonstrated the application of term-structure-associated financial instruments in formulating dynamic portfolio management tactics, specifically aimed at mitigating distinct systematic jump hazards inherent in asset returns.[^o22] In his early works, he introduced the variance gamma process, a stochastic model for log stock price dynamics, highlighting its symmetric statistical density with some kurtosis and negatively skewed risk-neutral density with higher kurtosis.[^o23] His study further proposed using Markov Chains and homogeneous Levy processes, specifically the variance gamma process, as a robust modeling approach for financial asset prices, thereby facilitating the computation of option and series prices.[^o24] His research work on pricing European options involved exploring self-similar risk-neutral processes and proposing two parameter stability-based models, highlighting their usefulness in studying the time variation of option prices.[^o25] Concentrating his research on risk premia in options markets, he used the variance gamma model for density synthesis, revealing mean reversion and predictability in premia, with particular emphasis on short-term market crashes and long-term market rallies.[^o26] His recent work in 2021 has contributed to the understanding of risk-neutral densities and jump arrival tails by introducing theoretical examples and practical models based on quasi-infinitely divisible distributions.[^o27]

### Asset pricing {#Asset_pricing}

Madan's contributions to asset pricing research have resulted in the development of asset pricing models.[^o29] His early research examined the minimum variance estimator to achieve a singular optimal power and provided approximations for estimating the scalar diffusion coefficient through the application of Ito calculus and Milstein methods.[^o30] He also addressed the paradox posed by Artzner and Heath, offering a solution determining that completeness pertains to the topology of the cash flow space and is associated with the singular nature of the price functional in the topological dual space.[^o31] In 2001, he proposed a modeling approach for asset price processes[^o32] and illustrated that asset price dynamics are more suitably represented by pure jump processes, devoid of any continuous martingale component.[^o33] In his analysis of equilibrium asset pricing, his work established that factor prices are influenced by exponentially tilted prices due to non-Gaussian factor risk exposures, which can be determined from the univariate probability distribution of the factor exposure.[^o34] Moreover, with Wim Schoutens, he co-developed a technique that uses historical data to establish upper and lower valuations, leading to enhanced risk evaluation in the stock market through the integration of risk attributes into required returns.[^o35]
