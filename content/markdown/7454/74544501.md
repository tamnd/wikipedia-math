# Boris Davison

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Boris Davison"}

**Boris Davison** (7 October 1908 – 24 January 1961) was a Russian-born [mathematical physicist](wikipedia://en/173416).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Boris Borisovich Davison was born 7 October 1908 in [Vasilsursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasilsursk), [Gorky Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorky_Oblast), Russia.[^o4] He attended [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_State_University), graduating in 1931.[^o5][^o4] He then worked at the [State Hydrological Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Hydrological_Institute).

Davison's grandfather had been British, and in 1938 Davison was given a choice – either renounce his British nationality or leave the Soviet Union. He chose to emigrate to the United Kingdom.[^o5][^o4] He then briefly worked with [Louis Rosenhead](wikipedia://en/39261792) at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool) but withdrew from work due to illness.[^o4][^o5]

In 1942 he joined the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham)'s atomic energy research team working under [Rudolf Peierls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Peierls), and in 1944 the university awarded him a PhD.[^o5][^o4]

In 1943 he moved to Canada to work under [George Placzek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Placzek) at the [Montreal Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Laboratory) of the joint British-Canadian atomic energy project.[^o5][^o4][^o6] In October 1945 he briefly joined the [British Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_contribution_to_the_Manhattan_Project) at [Los Alamos Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_Laboratory) in [New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico), part of the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project) which had just developed the [atom bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom_Bomb).[^o7] In 1946 Davison married Olga Hansen.[^o8]

He worked at [Chalk River Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk_River_Laboratory) in [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario) before returning to the UK in 1947 to work at the [Atomic Energy Research Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_Energy_Research_Establishment) at [Harwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harwell,_Oxfordshire), [Oxfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxfordshire).[^o5][^o4]

In 1953 his [security clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_clearance) was revoked by the British government because his parents still lived in the Soviet Union, potentially putting Davison at risk of blackmail.[^o9] He was given a year's [leave of absence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leave_of_absence) working at the University of Birmingham.[^o10][^o7] Davison then emigrated to Canada in 1954, where he took up a position at the computation centre at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto).[^o6][^o7]

In 1957 he authored the book *Neutron Transport Theory*.[^o5]

Davison died suddenly at his home in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto) on 24 January 1961 at the age of 52.[^o8][^o5][^o11]
