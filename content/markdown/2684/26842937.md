::: {#o8 .subject cue=lead name="5 21 honeycomb"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **5<sub>21</sub> honeycomb** is a [uniform tessellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_honeycomb) of 8-dimensional Euclidean space. The symbol 5<sub>21</sub> is from [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter), named for the length of the 3 branches of its Coxeter-Dynkin diagram.[^o9]

:::

By putting spheres at its vertices one obtains the densest-possible packing of spheres in 8 dimensions. This was proven by [Maryna Viazovska](wikipedia://en/50004967) in 2016 using the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000). Viazovska was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) for this work in 2022.

This honeycomb was first studied by Gosset who called it a *9-ic semi-regular figure*[^o10] (Gosset regarded honeycombs in *n* dimensions as degenerate *n*+1 polytopes).

Each vertex of the 5<sub>21</sub> honeycomb is surrounded by 2160 [8-orthoplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-orthoplex) and 17280 [8-simplices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-simplex).

The [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of Gosset's honeycomb is the semiregular [4<sub>21</sub> polytope](wikipedia://en/19236362). It is the final figure in the [k<sub>21</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiregular_k_21_polytope) family.

This honeycomb is highly regular in the sense that its symmetry group (the affine ${\tilde{E}}_8$ Weyl group) acts transitively on the [*k*-faces](wikipedia://en/149926) for *k* ≤ 6. All of the *k*-faces for *k* ≤ 7 are simplices.

## Construction {#Construction}

It is created by a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) upon a set of 9 [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) mirrors in 8-dimensional space.

The facet information can be extracted from its [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram).

Removing the node on the end of the 2-length branch leaves the [8-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-orthoplex), 6<sub>11</sub>.

Removing the node on the end of the 1-length branch leaves the [8-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-simplex).

The [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is determined by removing the ringed node and ringing the neighboring node. This makes the [4<sub>21</sub> polytope](wikipedia://en/19218705).

The [edge figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_figure) is determined from the vertex figure by removing the ringed node and ringing the neighboring node. This makes the [3<sub>21</sub> polytope](wikipedia://en/19236353).

The [face figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_figure) is determined from the edge figure by removing the ringed node and ringing the neighboring node. This makes the [2<sub>21</sub> polytope](wikipedia://en/19239704).

The cell figure is determined from the face figure by removing the ringed node and ringing the neighboring node. This makes the [1<sub>21</sub> polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demipenteract).

:::

## Kissing number {#Kissing_number}

Each vertex of this tessellation is the center of a 7-sphere in the densest [packing](wikipedia://en/368621) in 8 dimensions; its [kissing number](wikipedia://en/408555) is 240, represented by the vertices of its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) [4<sub>21</sub>](wikipedia://en/19236362).

## Regular complex honeycomb {#Regular_complex_honeycomb}

Using a [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) coordinate system, it can also be constructed as a $\mathbb{C}^4$ [regular complex polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_complex_polytope), given the symbol 3{3}3{3}3{3}3{3}3, and [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) . Its elements are in relative proportion as 1 vertex, 80 3-edges, 270 <sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub> faces, 80 <sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub> cells and 1 <sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub> [Witting polytope](wikipedia://en/28363938) cells.[^o20]

## Related polytopes and honeycombs {#Related_polytopes_and_honeycombs}

The 5<sub>21</sub> is seventh in a dimensional series of [semiregular polytopes](wikipedia://en/11684875), identified in 1900 by [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset). Each [member of the sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_k21_polytope) has the previous member as its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719). All facets of these polytopes are [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693), namely [simplexes](wikipedia://en/39781) and [orthoplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex).
